An animated, cited guide for students, people living with borderline personality disorder, and their families and carers. Educational only, not medical advice. Your place is saved on this device.

Not medical adviceThe BPD Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. It does not replace a psychiatrist, psychologist, nurse or other clinician, and the criteria and questionnaires in it are teaching aids, not self-tests. No medication is approved for BPD itself; drug findings are reported as published. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

Candidate geneA gene studied because of a biological hunch; 5-HTTLPR and other serotonin candidates showed no association with BPD 7,83.
Carer burdenThe practical and emotional load of caring; about half an SD higher in carers of people with BPD than of people with other serious mental illnesses 23.

Complex PTSDA trauma diagnosis with a stably negative self-concept; BPD is separated by abandonment fears, unstable self, unstable relationships and impulsiveness 105,172.
Consultation team (DBT)The weekly therapists' meeting that keeps a compassionate stance and reduces burnout 99.
CortisolThe main stress hormone; continuous output is elevated but the response to social stress blunted in BPD 134.

Crisis resolution and home treatment teamA community team NICE CG78 asks to be offered before an acute admission 379.
CyberballA virtual ball-tossing game that studies social exclusion; people with BPD feel excluded even when included 430,498.

MBT-AMBT for adolescents; it reduced self-harm and depression more than usual care 438.

Negative affectivityThe ICD-11 trait domain of frequent, intense negative emotions out of proportion to the situation 21.

NeuroticismA normal personality trait; BPD's genetic liability is shared with high neuroticism and low agreeableness 125.

NICE NG225The 2022 English self-harm guideline; it advises against risk scales for predicting suicide 380.

PsilocybinA psychedelic; one open-label study of 9 adults lowered depression but not BPD scores (Hypothesis-generating) 186.
Psychic equivalenceTreating inner experience as if it were outer reality 102.
PsychoeducationLearning about BPD; a workshop within a week of diagnosis reduced impulsivity and stormy relationships 583.
Psychosocial assessmentA needs-focused assessment after self-harm, not delayed by medical treatment or alcohol levels 380.
Punitive parentA harsh, self-critical schema mode characteristic of BPD 14.

Reflective functioningA measure of mentalizing; very large deficit in BPD (d = 1.68) 67.

Respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA)Heart-rate variability with breathing, a marker of regulation; low at rest in BPD 289.

SplittingA primitive defence in Kernberg's model, seeing people as all good or all bad 203,259.
Standardised mortality ratio (SMR)Observed over expected deaths; 8.3 for hospital-treated BPD in Denmark 264.
Standardised mean difference (SMD)An effect size in SD units; −0.52 for psychotherapy on BPD severity in Cochrane 2020 514.
STEPPSSystems Training for Emotional Predictability and Problem Solving, a 20-week add-on group involving the support network 60.
Stepped careStarting brief and stepping up as needed; a whole-service model cut bed days per admission from 13.46 to 4.28 189.
StigmaUnfair negative views; nearly half of 706 clinicians preferred to avoid patients with BPD 56.

Structured clinical management (SCM)A generalist comparator; both SCM and MBT improved substantially in Bateman 2009 37.
Substance use disorderPresent in about half of people with BPD; its absence best predicts remission 536,600.

BPD Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on borderline personality disorder, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people with BPD, their families and carers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should change a treatment without your own clinician. Criteria, assessment tools and guideline statements are quoted as published and are for learning, not self-diagnosis. No medication is approved for BPD itself; drug evidence is reported as published.
