Part 1 · Understanding BPD

Brain & stress systems

Brain-imaging and stress-hormone studies find group differences in borderline personality disorder: in threat responses, emotion regulation, stress hormones and pain. They are averages from research groups. They describe processes, not a fixed "BPD brain", and some shift after therapy. [[Schulze2016,Marek2022,Goodman2014]]

How sure are we? A replication board

Each card is one finding, with its sample, its effect size where one was published, and a confidence meter. The meter is our editorial reading of sample size, replication and agreement between studies, not a published score. Across neuroscience, small samples inflate effects; brain–behaviour links usually need thousands of participants to replicate. [[Button2013,Marek2022]]

Minds, not only brains

Established

Mentalizing and hypermentalizing

Mentalizing means understanding behaviour in terms of thoughts and feelings. In a meta-analysis of 34 studies (1,448 people with BPD, 2,006 controls), reflective functioning showed a very large deficit (d = 1.68) but theory of mind only a modest one (d = 0.36); over-interpreting others' minds ("hyper-ToM" errors, d = 0.60) was among the most robust findings. Hypermentalizing is linked to psychopathology in general, not more to BPD (r = 0.26 vs 0.24). Some people with BPD even read neutral eyes better than controls. The mentalizing model itself is a hypothesis. See Mentalization-based treatment. [[Bora2021,McLaren2022,Fertuck2009,Fonagy2008]]

Established

Dissociation and stress-related paranoia

DSM-5-TR criterion 9 is transient, stress-related paranoid ideation or severe dissociative symptoms. On the Dissociative Experiences Scale, 26% of 290 people with BPD scored high, against 3% of other personality-disorder controls. Hourly monitoring shows dissociation rising with stress, more steeply in BPD, and higher baseline dissociation predicted less improvement in inpatient DBT. About 20–50% of people with BPD report psychotic symptoms, mostly in stressful moments; the labels "pseudo-psychotic" and "quasi-psychotic" are discouraged. [[APA2022,Zanarini2000Dissociation,Stiglmayr2008,Kleindienst2011,Schroeder2013,DAgostino2019]]

Established

Why scans cannot diagnose BPD

Early BPD imaging studies had 6 to 15 people per group. In about 50,000 people, brain-wide links between brain measures and mental-health traits were much smaller than thought, and studies at the median sample of about 25 were underpowered and failed to replicate. Limbic overactivity, prefrontal underactivity and smaller grey matter also appear in PTSD, depression or bipolar disorder. Diagnosis rests on a clinical interview: see Assessment & interviews. [[Herpertz2001,Donegan2003,Marek2022,Schulze2019,Yu2019BJP,Lou2021]]

Early evidence

The brain changes with therapy

After 12 months of standard DBT in unmedicated people with BPD (11 per group), amygdala activation fell and better amygdala habituation tracked better emotion regulation. After 12 weeks of residential DBT, pain-related amygdala deactivation and amygdala–cingulate coupling were reduced compared with usual treatment. Samples were small. See Dialectical behaviour therapy. [[Goodman2014,Niedtfeld2017]]

Established

Not specific to BPD

Affective instability, limbic hyperactivity, hypermentalizing and low vagal tone all appear in other conditions too: limbic hyperactivation in PTSD as well as BPD, for example, while the amygdala response is blunted in major depression. These are processes, which is why the same skills-based treatments can help across diagnoses. [[Santangelo2014,Schulze2019,McLaren2022,Koenig2016]]

Medicines from these pathways?

Single-dose oxytocin and opioid studies are experiments, not treatment trials. In current reviews, drug treatment in BPD is reserved for co-occurring conditions or crises when psychosocial help is not enough. See Medication: the evidence and Research toward better care. [[Bohus2021,Schmahl2012]]
