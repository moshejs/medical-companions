What the big reviews found

The 2020 Cochrane review pooled 75 randomised trials with 4507 participants, mostly women aged 14.8 to 45.7, testing more than 16 kinds of psychotherapy; about a third of the trials were of DBT and 7 of MBT. Against usual care, BPD-tailored psychotherapy reduced BPD severity by an SMD of −0.52, about 3.6 points on the 0–36 ZAN-BPD scale, which is above the 3.0-point minimal clinically relevant difference. Only that outcome reached the clinically meaningful cut-off, and subgroup analysis found no clear difference between types of therapy. [[Storebo2020]]

Other reviews agree on direction and disagree on size. Oud 2018 found a medium effect of the specialised therapies (DBT, MBT, TFP, schema therapy) on BPD severity (SMD −0.59). Cristea 2017 found small effects (stand-alone g 0.32, add-on g 0.40) and warned that they are "inflated by risk of bias and publication bias". A 2024 review puts the effect on core severity at 0.50–0.65 and notes that "almost half of the patients do not respond sufficiently". Established structured psychotherapy helps; Early evidence on who benefits most. [[Oud2018,Cristea2017,Leichsenring2024WP,PereiraRibeiro2026]]

Dropout, guidelines and the limits of the evidence

Dropout

About 1 in 4 stop early

Pooled dropout was 22.3% across all studies and 28.2% in outpatient trials (up to 29.9% after adjusting for publication bias), mostly in the first half of treatment. Reasons included dissatisfaction, being discharged by the service and low motivation. Schema therapy had the lowest dropout overall, MBT in the first two quarters; individual formats lower than group. [[Iliakis2021,Arntz2023Dropout]]

Guideline

APA 2024

"APA recommends (1B) that a patient with borderline personality disorder be treated with a structured approach to psychotherapy that has support in the literature and targets the core features of the disorder." It also recommends (1C) a collaborative discussion of the diagnosis that includes psychoeducation. [[APA2024Guideline]]

Guideline

NICE CG78 (2009)

For women for whom reducing recurrent self-harm is a priority, NICE says to "consider a comprehensive dialectical behaviour therapy programme". It advises against brief BPD-specific psychological interventions (under 3 months) outside a structured service with "an explicit and integrated theoretical approach". [[NICE2009]]

Guideline

Australia's NHMRC and others

National guidelines including the German AWMF, Australian NHMRC and UK NICE guidelines are reported to recommend treating BPD in adolescents from age 12 or 14. The NHMRC's own wording could not be checked for this guide, so it is not quoted. [[Kaess2025]]

Limits

Small trials, low certainty

Apart from BPD severity, the Cochrane outcomes are low certainty; BPD-specific psychotherapy did not reduce attrition compared with usual care, and adverse effects were unclear because too few data were reported. The 2012 version concluded that "none of the treatments has a very robust evidence base". [[Storebo2020,Stoffers2012]]

Limits

Usual care is not nothing

In the usual-care arms of 16 BPD trials, BPD symptoms improved by a small-to-moderate amount (g 0.371), though self-harm and suicidality did not change (g 0.003). The size of a "versus usual care" effect depends on how good the usual care is. [[Finch2019TAU]]

Early evidence

Who does best with which?

In individual-participant data from 19 trials, co-occurring depression and sexual trauma predicted less benefit from psychotherapy, while co-occurring anxiety or alcohol use disorder predicted more. These are averages, not a way to choose for one person yet. [[PereiraRibeiro2026]]

Plain words

Treatable, and no single best

Talking therapies built for BPD help most people feel and function better, and many people recover over time. DBT, MBT, schema therapy and TFP have all worked in trials, and well-organised general care also helps. [[Storebo2020,Gunderson2018,McMain2009]]
