Course & recovery

BPD is not a life sentence. In the longest studies almost everyone reached a lasting remission of symptoms, and long remissions seldom ended. Getting work, study and relationships back on track takes longer, and that is where much of the remaining effort goes. [[Zanarini2012,Gunderson2011]]

Two words that mean different things

Remission

The symptoms settle

Symptomatic remission means the person no longer has enough symptoms for the diagnosis. MSAD counted remissions by how long they lasted, from 2 to 8 years. By 16 years, 99% had a 2-year remission and 78% an 8-year one. [[Zanarini2012]]

Recovery

Symptoms settle and life works

Recovery adds good social and vocational functioning over the previous 2 years to remission. By 16 years, 60% had a 2-year recovery and 40% an 8-year one. "Excellent recovery", which also needs full-time work or study and no impairing other disorder, was reached by 39% of people with BPD by 20 years. [[Zanarini2010Recovery,Zanarini2012,Zanarini2018Excellent]]

What else changes over time

Functioning lags

Work is the sticking point

Over 90% of poor psychosocial functioning over 10 years came from poor vocational, not social, performance. [[Zanarini2010Psychosocial]] In CLPS, social functioning stayed severely impaired with only modest gains, even as criteria fell; fewer criteria did predict later improvement in overall functioning. [[Gunderson2011]] Higher IQ, a good childhood and adult work record, lower neuroticism and higher agreeableness predicted excellent recovery. [[Zanarini2018Excellent]]

Comorbidity

Other conditions improve too

People whose BPD remitted had a substantial fall in all co-occurring disorders; those whose BPD did not remit kept stable rates. [[Zanarini2004AxisI]] More than 90% of people with a substance use disorder at baseline remitted from it within 10 years. [[Zanarini2011SUD]] Not having a substance use disorder was the strongest comorbidity predictor of BPD remission and of recovery (relative risk ratio 2.53). [[Zanarini2004AxisI,Zanarini2024Comorbid]] BPD and major depression slow each other's remission; BPD and bipolar disorder run largely independent courses. [[Gunderson2014Mood]]

Who remits sooner

Predictors, not a verdict

Seven baseline factors predicted earlier remission: younger age, no childhood sexual abuse, no family history of substance use disorder, a good work record, no anxious-cluster personality disorder, low neuroticism and high agreeableness. [[Zanarini2006Prediction]] Hypothesis These are candidate guides for planning care, not a validated tool for any one person. [[Zanarini2006Prediction]]

Physical health

Recovery shows in the body

At 6 years, people whose BPD had not remitted more often had obesity, diabetes, high blood pressure, osteoarthritis, back pain and "syndrome-like" conditions such as chronic fatigue. [[Frankenburg2004Medical]] Over the next decade, people who never recovered had more medical illness and used more emergency care and hospital admissions. [[Keuroghlian2013Medical]] Physical inactivity stayed stable over 12 years in people who did not recover. [[Glass2024Inactivity]]

Population statistics

Early death, and why recovery matters

Over 24 years in MSAD, 5.9% of people with BPD died by suicide and 14.0% died of other causes, against 1.4% and 5.5% of comparison subjects. Most of those who died had not recovered before death. [[Temes2019]] In a Danish register of hospital-treated BPD, mortality was 8.3 times that of the general population. [[Kjaer2020]] These are group figures from specific cohorts; self-harm and suicide attempts fall sharply for most people over time. [[Zanarini2008SelfHarm]]

Need help now?If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

How far it generalises

Who these numbers describe

MSAD recruited severely ill inpatients at one hospital in 1992–1995: 77.1% female, 87% white, mean age 27, mean GAF 39.8. [[Zanarini2012]] Most received many treatments, so the course is that of treated BPD. [[Zanarini2003Course]] A meta-analysis of 11 prospective studies in 9 countries found long-term remission in 50–70%, more often in younger people. [[AlvarezTomas2019Meta]] Whether treatment changes the long-term course is still unproven. [[AlvarezTomas2019Meta]]

The message of hope, stated carefully

Across the large cohorts, remission is the usual outcome, and recurrence after a long remission is uncommon. [[Zanarini2012,Gunderson2011,Ng2016Recovery]] Evidence-based psychosocial treatment can speed remission, though many people still need help with social and working life. [[Gunderson2018]] NICE asks clinicians to work "in an atmosphere of hope and optimism", explaining that recovery is "possible and attainable". [[NICE2009]] People with lived experience describe recovery as non-linear, built on hope, active engagement, treatment, and meaningful activities and relationships. [[Ng2019LivedRecovery]]

These are averages across groups. They cannot promise any one person's path, and they do not mean anyone has to wait years for help: treatment options are in Dialectical behaviour therapy, Therapies compared and Living with BPD.
