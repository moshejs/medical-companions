Perspective

Schematic. An illustrative conversation, not a recording or a measured result. The wave heights are drawn to show a pattern, and no reply guarantees an outcome. Nobody here is to blame: the transactional model describes how both people's responses shape each other. [[Fruzzetti2005,Crowell2009]]

What validation is, and what it is not

Validation means communicating that another person's feelings make sense from where they stand. It does not mean agreeing with everything, approving of a behaviour or giving up your own limits. DBT family skills training aims to build a mutually validating environment, with skills for every family member, not just the person with the diagnosis. [[Hoffman1999]] In a co-produced study, both people with BPD and carers described helpful language as "connecting, validating and accepting". [[vanSchie2024]]

The crisis principles in the NICE guideline describe the same moves for clinicians: stay calm, try to understand the crisis from the person's point of view, ask open questions, use validating statements, avoid minimising the stated reasons, and do not offer solutions before the problem is clear. [[NICE2009]]

Often heard as invalidating
  • Dismisses"You're overreacting."
  • Minimises"It's not a big deal."
  • Jumps to fixing"Just do X and it'll be fine."
  • Labels the person"You're being dramatic again."

Illustrative phrases. Minimising the stated reasons and offering solutions before the problem is clear are what NICE advises against. [[NICE2009]]

Often heard as validating
  • Listens"Tell me what happened."
  • Reflects"That sounds really lonely."
  • Makes sense of it"Given the week you've had, it makes sense you're upset."
  • Asks before fixing"Do you want ideas, or just to talk?"

Illustrative phrases built on the same principles: understand first, use validating statements, then problem-solve. [[NICE2009,Hoffman1999]]

Family Connections: what the studies found

Family Connections is a 12-week manualised programme for relatives, led by trained family members. It is based on DBT and DBT for families, and covers information about BPD, coping skills, family skills and building a support network. [[Hoffman2005]] Pick a study to see which outcomes moved.

Study

Bar lengths show direction, not size. The study abstracts report which outcomes changed and how significant the change was, not comparable scores, so every "before" bar is drawn the same length. Lower burden, grief and depression, and higher mastery, are improvements.

Taken together: Family Connections has reduced burden and grief in uncontrolled and controlled studies, but in the one randomised trial against an active comparison of similar length it was not statistically superior on burden. Promising, not yet proven superior. [[Hoffman2005,Flynn2017,Guillen2024]] Early evidence

Carer wellbeing: a checklist for you

Tick what is already in place. Nothing is scored: each item opens a short note about why it matters.

  • Carers in one survey of 287 reported symptoms consistent with mood, anxiety and posttraumatic stress disorders. Your own health is part of the picture, not a luxury. [[Bailey2014]]

  • NICE asks services to tell families about local carer support groups. Carers in interviews valued support from carer peer workers with lived experience. [[NICE2009,Barr2020]]

  • A 10-week carer group improved empowerment and lowered expressed emotion for at least 12 months, and Family Connections lowered burden and grief in several studies. [[Grenyer2019,Flynn2017]]

  • Family skills programmes teach everyone's emotion regulation and interpersonal skills, so limits can be held without blame. Validating a feeling and holding a limit can happen in the same sentence. [[Hoffman1999,Hoffman2005]]

  • In one small study, families who knew more about BPD reported more burden and hostility, and the authors questioned the accuracy of the information they had received. Good information matters more than lots of it. [[Hoffman2003]] Early evidence

For families and carers

Common

What families commonly feel

In a review of 465 carers, burden was about half a standard deviation higher than for carers of people with other serious mental illnesses, with grief, low empowerment, depression and anxiety. [[Bailey2013]] Carers described grieving for the change in their lives. [[Bailey2014]] Carers of young people with BPD features reported adversity similar to or greater than carers of young people with eating disorders, cancer or psychosis. [[Seigerman2020]] These are common reactions, not failures.

Language

What not to say

Every participant in a co-produced study had met stigmatising language, and carers felt frustrated when they felt blamed or their needs went unacknowledged. [[vanSchie2024]] High criticism (82.9%) and emotional overinvolvement (69.6%) were common among 280 carers, and overinvolvement went with more burden. [[Bailey2015]] Avoid labels for the person; describe the behaviour and the feeling.

Skills

Setting limits kindly

A limit is about what you will do, said calmly and ahead of time where possible: "I want to hear this. I can't talk while we're shouting, so I'll step out for ten minutes and come back." Family skills training covers everyone's emotion regulation and interpersonal skills, so limits sit alongside validation, not instead of it. [[Hoffman1999]] The transactional model blames neither the person with BPD nor the parents. [[Fruzzetti2005]] Hypothesis

Children

Siblings and children

Reviews link BPD in mothers with less sensitive, more intrusive or hostile parenting and with emotional and behavioural difficulties in children; the authors call for parenting support, and prospective studies are still needed. [[Eyden2016,Petfield2015,Steele2019]] Early evidence A 25-week Mother–Infant DBT group improved mothers' depression, anxiety and BPD symptoms. [[SvedWilliams2021]] Brothers and sisters are relatives too: Family Connections is open to family members, not only parents. [[Hoffman2005]] More in Living with BPD.

Urgent help

When and how to seek urgent help

If someone is in immediate danger, contact emergency services. A crisis plan agreed in advance with the care team lists warning signs, what helps and who to call. [[NICE2009]] Even when a person has not consented to family involvement, families and carers can still give information to staff after self-harm. [[NICE2022SelfHarm]] See Suicide & self-harm safety.

Need help now?If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

Support

Where support comes from

Kinds of support, without endorsing any organisation: family-led skills programmes such as Family Connections, run by trained family members [[Hoffman2005]]; local carer support groups, which NICE asks services to tell families about [[NICE2009]]; and carer peer workers with lived experience [[Barr2020]]. Ask the care team what exists locally. NICE asks clinicians to check whether the person wants family involved and to make sure involvement never reduces the person's access to services. [[NICE2009]]

Recovery is common. Over 16 years in the McLean study, 78–99% of people with BPD reached remission, depending on how long it had to last; recovery that includes steady work or study took longer (40–60%). These are population figures, not a forecast for one person. See Course & recovery. [[Zanarini2012]]
