Medication: the evidence

No medicine is approved for BPD itself, yet most people with BPD are prescribed several. Here is what the trials show, how a pile of prescriptions builds up, and what guidelines say about reviewing it.

No medication is approved for borderline personality disorder. An openFDA search of every US drug label's indications for "borderline personality" returned no matches, and reviews state that "no medications have been approved by regulatory agencies for the treatment of BPD"; all use for BPD is off-label. The European Medicines Agency was not searched directly for this guide. [[FDA-LabelSearch2026,Gartlehner2021,StoffersWinterling2022]]

What the guidelines say

GuidelineStatement (quoted as published)
NICE CG78 1.3.5.1"Drug treatment should not be used specifically for borderline personality disorder or for the individual symptoms or behaviour associated with the disorder (for example, repeated self-harm, marked emotional instability, risk-taking behaviour and transient psychotic symptoms)." [[NICE2009]]
NICE CG78 1.3.5.2"Antipsychotic drugs should not be used for the medium- and long-term treatment of borderline personality disorder." [[NICE2009]]
NICE CG78 1.3.5.3–4Drug treatment "may be considered in the overall treatment of comorbid conditions". Short-term sedative medication may be considered cautiously in a crisis, "no longer than 1 week". [[NICE2009]]
APA 2024, statement 6 (1C)A review of co-occurring disorders, prior psychotherapies, other non-pharmacological treatments, past medication trials and current medications "before initiating any new medication". [[APA2024Guideline]]
APA 2024, statement 7 (2C)"APA suggests (2C) that any psychotropic medication treatment of borderline personality disorder be time-limited, aimed at addressing a specific measurable target symptom, and adjunctive to psychotherapy." [[APA2024Guideline]]
APA 2024, statement 8 (1C)"A review and reconciliation of their medications at least every 6 months to assess the effectiveness of treatment and identify medications that warrant tapering or discontinuation." [[APA2024Guideline]]
NICE NG225 1.11.10"Do not offer drug treatment as a specific intervention to reduce self-harm." [[NICE2022NG225]]

NICE is firmer than APA: NICE says drugs should not be used for BPD or its symptoms, while APA allows time-limited, symptom-targeted use alongside psychotherapy. Both agree medicines are not first-line. NICE states it is reviewing CG78 after the ICD-11 change. [[NICE2009,APA2024Guideline]]

Safety

Overdose and access to medicines

APA 2024 says decisions "should also consider potential risks of toxicity in overdose or potential for misuse". NICE NG225 asks prescribers to consider toxicity in overdose and to use shared decision making about limiting the quantity supplied, for example weekly prescriptions, and returning unwanted medicines. [[APA2024Guideline,NICE2022NG225]]

Pregnancy

Valproate

The US boxed warning: valproate "can cause major congenital malformations, particularly neural tube defects", and decreased IQ after exposure in the womb. In the EURAP registry, 9.9% of valproate-exposed pregnancies had a major malformation vs 3.1% with lamotrigine; in NEAD, mean IQ at age 6 was 97 vs 108. People who could become pregnant should discuss this with their doctor before starting or stopping it. [[FDA-Depakote,Battino2024EURAP,Meador2013NEAD]]

Caution

Benzodiazepines

APA: "not generally recommended because of potential for greater impulsivity or disinhibition as well as potential for misuse or development of dependence". In a Swedish within-person cohort of 22,601 people with BPD, the hazard of attempted or completed suicide was higher on than off benzodiazepines (HR 1.61; 1.45–1.78); this is observational and cannot rule out that medicines were started when people were already worse. Early evidence [[APA2024Guideline,Lieslehto2023Suicide]]

Labels

Boxed warnings worth knowing

Lamotrigine: serious rash including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (about 0.08–0.3% of adults). Antidepressants: more suicidal thoughts and behaviour in children, adolescents and young adults in short-term studies. Lithium: toxicity "can occur at doses close to therapeutic levels". Clozapine: severe neutropenia, seizures, myocarditis and more. [[FDA-Lamictal,FDA-Prozac,FDA-Lithobid,FDA-Clozaril]]

Ask

Questions for a prescriber

  • What is this medicine for: a co-occurring condition, or a specific target symptom?
  • How will we know if it works, and when will we review it? (APA: at least every 6 months.)
  • What is the evidence for it in BPD, and what are the harms? (NICE asks for written information.)
  • Which of my current medicines could be reduced or stopped?

[[APA2024Guideline,NICE2009]]

Plain words

Therapy first

No medicine is approved to treat BPD itself; talking therapies are the main treatment. Medicines can still help with conditions that often come with BPD, such as depression, or for a short time in a crisis. Tell your care team about side effects such as weight gain, sleepiness or restlessness. See Therapies compared and Suicide & self-harm safety. [[Bohus2021,NICE2009]]
