Part 1 · Understanding BPD

What BPD is

Borderline personality disorder (BPD) is a long-lasting pattern of unstable emotions, relationships and sense of self, with impulsive and sometimes self-endangering behaviour. It is common, it usually starts in adolescence, and most people improve a great deal over time. [[Leichsenring2024WP,Bohus2021,Zanarini2012]]

Why the community estimates disagree

The same country can give a figure of 1.4% or 5.9%, depending on who is sampled, which interview is used, whether there is a second clinical stage, and above all whether each criterion has to cause distress or impairment before it counts. BPD in plain words

Core features

What people notice

  • Intense, volatile emotions and extreme sensitivity to perceived slights. [[Gunderson2018]]
  • Strong fears of being abandoned, and unstable relationships. [[Leichsenring2024WP]]
  • An unstable sense of who you are, and feelings of emptiness. [[Leichsenring2024WP,Gunderson2018]]
  • Impulsive acts, intense anger, and self-harm or suicidal behaviour. [[Leichsenring2024WP]]
  • Under heavy stress, short spells of feeling unreal or numb, or of suspicion. Dissociation rises more steeply with stress in BPD than in other groups. [[Stiglmayr2008,Leichsenring2024WP]]

About 20–50% of people with BPD report psychotic symptoms, mostly hallucinations; a review advises against calling them "pseudo-psychotic". [[Schroeder2013]] The formal rules are in DSM-5-TR criteria and ICD-11.

Rarely alone

Other conditions are the rule

In NESARC, 84.8% of people with BPD had a lifetime anxiety disorder, 82.7% a mood disorder and 78.2% a substance use disorder. [[Tomko2014]] Depression, PTSD, ADHD, bipolar disorder and bulimia nervosa are all linked with BPD. [[Leichsenring2024WP]] Telling them apart is covered in BPD or something else?

Impact

School, work, relationships, money

BPD is linked with lower educational and work attainment, fewer long-term relationships, low social support and more use of services. [[Bohus2021]] Danish young people diagnosed before 19 were about 22 times more likely to be unemployed at 20 (OR 21.7) and nearly 15 times more likely to be on disability pension. [[Hastrup2022]] In Germany, specialist outpatients cost society €31,130 a year each (2015 prices), 45% of it indirect costs. [[Wagner2022Cost]] Evidence-based psychotherapy saved a mean USD 2,988 per patient a year across 30 economic studies. [[Meuldijk2017]]

Physical health

The body matters too

BPD is linked with heart and blood-vessel disease, liver and gut disease and arthritis, even after adjustment (adjusted ORs 1.46–2.80). [[ElGabalawy2010]] People whose BPD did not remit had more obesity, diabetes, osteoarthritis and back pain than people whose BPD remitted. [[Frankenburg2004Medical]] In MSAD, deaths from causes other than suicide (14.0%) were more common over 24 years than suicides (5.9%). [[Temes2019]]

Population statistics

Suicide risk is real, and help works

Suicidal behaviour is common in BPD. Older long-term clinical cohorts reported that up to about 10% died by suicide; in the 24-year McLean study it was 5.9%, against 1.4% in a comparison group. [[Paris2019Suicidality,Paris2001,Temes2019]] These are averages for groups, not predictions for anyone. Repeated suicide attempts should never be read as a sign of little wish to die. [[Black2004Suicide]] Specifically designed psychotherapies have the strongest evidence, and self-harm and suicide attempts fall sharply over time for most people. [[Paris2019Suicidality,Zanarini2008SelfHarm]] More in Suicide & self-harm safety.

Need help now?If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

Outlook

Most people get better

93%

of 290 inpatients with BPD in the McLean study reached a symptomatic remission lasting 2 years or more within 10 years; half also reached good social and work functioning. [[Zanarini2010Recovery]] BPD "was previously regarded as untreatable"; earlier diagnosis and better treatment have improved outcomes. [[Bohus2021]] The long view is in Course & recovery.

Words matter

Stigma attached to BPD may exceed that of other mental illnesses, and it shapes care: in one survey of 706 clinicians, nearly half preferred to avoid patients with BPD, while those who had treated more people with BPD rated themselves higher on empathy and optimism. [[Aviram2006,Black2011Attitudes]] People with BPD and carers describe language that is "connecting, validating and accepting" as helpful. [[vanSchie2024]] Tap or press a card to turn it over.

The front of each card shows a phrase the stigma research warns against, quoted so it can be recognised; the back gives a person-first alternative and the evidence. [[Lewis1988,Markham2003,Sheehan2016,vanSchie2024]]
