Perspectives and practicalities

Lived experience

What recovery feels like from inside

Lived-experience research describes recovery as non-linear: from being stuck, through diagnosis, to an improving experience, driven by hope, active engagement, treatment and meaningful activities and relationships. [[Ng2019LivedRecovery]] Peer workers with their own lived experience can offer hope, connection and validation, with supervision and clear role boundaries. [[Barr2020]] Peer support fits personal recovery goals such as self-acceptance, hope and meaningful roles, though formal evaluations are few. [[Turner2024,Dufour2025]] Early evidence

Work

Disclosure at work or college

In an integrative review of 11 studies, barriers to work and study included symptoms, self-stigma and non-disclosure; enablers included boldness and honesty, and employment itself appeared to improve symptoms. [[Larcombe2022]] There is no single right answer about telling an employer. Some people tell a manager or occupational health only what they need (for example, "I have a health condition that affects stress; regular check-ins help"), without naming the diagnosis.

Rights

Rights and reasonable adjustments

NICE states that people with BPD "should not be excluded from any health or social care service because of their diagnosis or because they have self-harmed". [[NICE2009]] Care plans should include long-term goals such as employment and occupation, linked to short-term treatment aims. [[NICE2009]] Workplace rights and adjustments depend on the country's law; an occupational health service, union or advice service can explain what applies locally.

Stigma

Self-stigma

Women with BPD showed more self-stigma than women with social phobia, and self-stigma predicted lower self-esteem after accounting for depression and shame. [[Rusch2006]] A review of 30 studies found stigma themes including perceived untreatability and low BPD health literacy among patients and clinicians alike. [[Ring2019]] Combining recovery messages with biological explanations looks most promising for reducing public stigma. [[Ociskova2023]] Early evidence Recovery is possible and attainable, as NICE asks clinicians to explain. [[NICE2009]]

Most people improve. In the McLean study, 93% had a remission lasting at least 2 years within 10 years and 50% reached full recovery, which also needs good work and social functioning; in the CLPS study, 85% remitted over 10 years. These are group figures. See Course & recovery and Families & carers. [[Zanarini2010Recovery,Gunderson2011]]
