Nursing care

Nurses are often the first and most frequent contact for people with BPD in crisis. Follow one journey from the emergency department to the ward and home, practise replies that validate while holding boundaries, and see what training and ward culture can change.

Need help now?If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

Practise: validate and hold the boundary

Validation means communicating that a person's feelings make sense from where they stand. It does not mean agreeing with everything or giving up the plan. NICE asks for a calm, non-threatening attitude, empathic open questions and validating statements, without minimising the person's reasons. [[NICE2009]] Pick a situation, then choose a reply.

Situation

Attitudes, training and ward culture

A review of 40 studies found that mental health nurses' attitudes toward people with BPD were poorer than those of other professionals and poorer than toward other diagnoses; nurses asked for a coherent therapeutic framework, and care seemed better where one existed. [[Dickens2016]] Training helps, mostly with what nurses know and think. [[Dickens2016Interventions]]

Key points for practice

Relationship

The therapeutic relationship

People with BPD cared for by community mental health nurses named three helpful parts of the relationship: learning about the disorder, building trust, and being empowered through validation. [[RomeuLabayen2020]] On acute wards, people valued being listened to, time to talk and feeling safe, and found negative attitudes, little staff contact and poor discharge planning harmful. [[Stapleton2019]]

Team

Consistency across the team

Community teams should manage risk as a whole multidisciplinary team, with good supervision, especially for less experienced staff. [[NICE2009]] Expect endings and transitions to evoke strong emotions: discuss changes beforehand, phase them, and keep access to crisis services. [[NICE2009]] One shared plan means every nurse responds the same way.

Least restrictive

Observation and de-escalation

Decide on close observation case by case, taking the person's views into account, by trained staff, reviewed regularly. [[NICE2022SelfHarm]] De-escalation combines communication, self-regulation, assessment, actions and safety maintenance, aiming to reduce aggression while minimising coercion. [[Hallett2017]] Safewards cut conflict by 15% and containment by 23.2% on 31 wards. [[Bowers2015]]

Medicines

Medication safety

NICE CG78: no drug treatment specifically for BPD or its individual symptoms; crisis sedation cautiously, for no longer than 1 week, and "prescribe fewer tablets more frequently if there is a significant risk of overdose", with one named primary prescriber. [[NICE2009]] NICE NG225 adds shared decisions about limited quantities and returning unwanted medicines. [[NICE2022NG225]]

Records

Documentation and crisis plans

A care plan should include a crisis plan with triggers, self-management strategies and how to reach services, shared with the GP and the person, and updated with them after a crisis. [[NICE2009]] In 41 joint crisis plans, people asked to be treated with dignity and respect; some recorded refusals of particular medicines or of involuntary treatment. [[Borschmann2014]] Measures help document change: the BSL-23 (self-rated) and ZAN-BPD (clinician-rated, past week, 0–36). [[Bohus2009,Zanarini2003ZAN]]

Staff care

Supervision and burnout

Inpatient nurses start with strong motivation, then unexpected demands lead to emotional exhaustion and compassion fatigue. [[Nordkamp2024]] NICE asks for routine supervision and staff support [[NICE2009]], and for regular, high-quality supervision with reflective practice for staff working with self-harm. [[NICE2022SelfHarm]] DBT skills-coaching training for 22 nursing staff reduced burnout and stigma Early evidence [[Haynos2016]]

Groups

Groups nurses can deliver

STEPPS is a 20-week manualised group that includes the person's support network. In an RCT of 124 people it improved ZAN-BPD scores, impulsivity, mood and functioning more than usual care, but not suicide attempts, self-harm or hospitalisation. [[Blum2008STEPPS]] A 1-day STEPPS workshop improved 271 clinicians' empathy and attitudes. [[Shanks2011]]

Exam hooks

For nursing students

  • NICE NG225: no risk scales or low/medium/high stratification to predict suicide or decide treatment; risk formulation instead. [[NICE2022SelfHarm]]
  • Do not delay the psychosocial assessment until medical care is finished or because of alcohol levels. [[NICE2022SelfHarm]]
  • Crisis or home treatment team before admission; agree length and purpose of any admission. [[NICE2009]]
  • Training effects are largest for knowledge, smaller for feelings. [[Dickens2016Interventions]]
  • Safewards: conflict −15%, containment −23.2%. [[Bowers2015]]
