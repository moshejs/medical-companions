Suicide & self-harm safety

Thoughts of suicide and self-harm are common in BPD, and they are treatable: they usually become less frequent over the years, and specific therapies and safety planning reduce them. This module explains how a safety plan works, why risk is assessed by understanding a person rather than by a score, and what helps in a crisis.

Need help now?If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123). Both are free and open around the clock. [[SAMHSA988,Samaritans116123]]

This page follows safe-messaging guidance: it describes no methods or means, and it focuses on help-seeking, coping and recovery. Stories of people getting through suicidal crises without acting on them have been linked to fewer suicides Early evidence [[Niederkrotenthaler2010,SamaritansMedia]]

Chronic and acute risk

Many people with BPD live with long-standing suicidal thoughts. Paris distinguishes this chronic suicidality, which in outpatient therapy "can be best understood as a way of communicating distress", from acute risk, the periods when danger rises above a person's usual level. [[Paris2002Chronic]] Both need a caring response; they call for different kinds of help.

What the evidence says about care

Self-harm

Why self-harm happens

Across 18 studies, the best-supported function of self-injury is regulating emotion: overwhelming feelings come first and short-term relief follows Established [[Klonsky2007]] In 101 women with BPD, most named several motives, and relief from negative emotion was "almost always involved". [[Kleindienst2008]] It is estimated to occur in about 65–80% of people with BPD. [[Brickman2014]] Therapy can teach other ways to get through those feelings: in Cochrane 2020, DBT reduced self-harm compared with usual care (SMD −0.28) and MBT reduced it too (RR 0.62). [[Storebo2020]]

NICE NG225

Why risk scales should not predict

NICE NG225: "Do not use risk assessment tools and scales to predict future suicide or repetition of self-harm", and do not use a global low, medium or high rating to decide who gets treatment or who is discharged. Instead, a risk formulation belongs in every psychosocial assessment. [[NICE2022NG225]] Pooled, risk scales had a positive predictive value of only 5.5% for suicide, and "No 'high-risk' classification was clinically useful". [[Carter2017PPV]] Sensitivity was 56% and specificity 79%, with no improvement in newer studies. [[Large2016]]

Brief admission

Brief admission by self-referral

In Brief Admission Skåne, people agree a contract in advance and can admit themselves at times of rising risk, for up to 3 days and at most 3 times a month. [[Liljedahl2017BA]] In its RCT (125 participants), brief admission did not reduce hospital days at 12 months, though only that group showed within-group falls in compulsory admission days and self-injury. [[Westling2019BA,NCT02985047]] Cochrane 2022 found no clear benefit of crisis interventions over usual care, with low-certainty evidence. [[MonkCunliffe2022]]

Emergency department

What helps in the ED

  • A calm, non-threatening attitude, trying to understand the crisis from the person's view, and validating statements. [[NICE2009]]
  • The psychosocial assessment should not wait until medical treatment is finished, nor be delayed because of alcohol levels. [[NICE2022NG225]]
  • A safety plan with at least two follow-up calls was followed by suicidal behaviour in 3.03% vs 5.29% over 6 months. [[Stanley2018ED]] Across 6 studies, safety planning cut suicidal behaviour (RR 0.57; NNT 16) Established [[Nuij2021]]
  • Nobody should be excluded from a service because of their diagnosis or because they have self-harmed. [[NICE2009]]
Friends & family

What friends can say

Stay calm, listen without judging, and try to understand what the crisis feels like from their side; that is what NICE asks of clinicians too. [[NICE2009]] Saying "That sounds unbearable right now. I'm here, and I want to help you get through tonight" validates without minimising. Ask whether they have a safety plan and help them use it, including the people and crisis lines on it. If someone is in immediate danger, contact emergency services.

Medicines

Medicines in a crisis

NICE CG78: short-term drug treatment "may be helpful" in a crisis, using a single drug with "relative safety in overdose", prescribing "fewer tablets more frequently" where needed, and usually stopping within 1 week. [[NICE2009]] NICE NG225 advises against drug treatment as a specific intervention to reduce self-harm. [[NICE2022NG225]]

Recovery is common. Self-harm and proneness to crisis "can decrease over time", and most people with BPD gradually enter remission Established [[Gunderson2018]]
