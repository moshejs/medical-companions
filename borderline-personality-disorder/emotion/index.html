Part 1 · Understanding BPD

The emotion wave

Many people with BPD describe emotions that start fast, run high and take a long time to settle. Scrub through one schematic wave, from a calm baseline through an interpersonal trigger to the peak and the return, then see the cycle that can follow. Each phase says what the research does and does not support.

Curves

Schematic, not measured. No study gives minute-by-minute values for this curve. The shape follows the published directions: a higher starting level in BPD, a rise roughly parallel to comparison groups in the laboratory, and a slower return in some contexts such as shame after criticism. The "skills" curve shows the idea of regulation, not a trial result. 73,187,238,428

Need help now?If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123). Urges build and then pass; reaching out to someone during the peak matters. See Suicide & self-harm safety.

Three parts of emotion vulnerability, and what tests show

Linehan's biosocial account describes emotion vulnerability in BPD as three parts: high sensitivity (reacting to small cues), high intensity or reactivity, and a slow return to baseline. As a theory it is a hypothesis, and the tests do not all agree. 289,428 Carpenter and Trull add a fourth angle: too few helpful regulation strategies and too many unhelpful ones. 85

Measure it yourself: an e-diary day

Studies that ask people to rate their mood on a phone many times a day show the shifts clearly. 455,534 The preferred instability index is the mean squared successive difference (MSSD): the average of the squared jumps between one rating and the next. Unlike the plain variance, it depends on the order of the ratings, so it tells sudden swings apart from slow drift. 239 Drag any point (or pick a prompt and use the slider), then press Sort the same readings: the mean and variance stay the same, the MSSD falls.

Riding on the wave

Trigger

Rejection sensitivity

On the Rejection Sensitivity Questionnaire, people with BPD scored highest of all groups tested, above people with social anxiety disorder. 497 In 21 days of daily life, rejection was followed by hostility and sadness more strongly in BPD than in community adults. 217 In 30 inpatients, stronger reactions appeared after disappointment in someone else, not after general appraisals. 233

Exam hookCyberball: people with BPD feel excluded even when they are included. 430,498
Criterion 7

Chronic emptiness

DSM-5-TR lists chronic feelings of emptiness as one of the nine criteria. 11 In 1,870 outpatients, emptiness was the only single feature that marked impairment on every index of psychosocial functioning. 143 It is one of the slower symptoms to settle: by 16 years in the McLean study, 71% had a 2-year remission of chronic loneliness or emptiness, against 96% for affective instability. 596

Exam hookAffective symptoms (anger, emptiness) are the most persistent; impulsive ones remit fastest. 582
Criterion 9

Dissociation under stress

DSM-5-TR criterion 9 is transient, stress-related paranoid ideation or severe dissociative symptoms. 11 In hourly monitoring over 48 hours, dissociation rose with stress in every group, more steeply in BPD. 510 On the Dissociative Experiences Scale, 26% of 290 people with BPD scored high, against 3% of controls. 571 Aversive tension correlated strongly with dissociation. 509

Exam hookHigher baseline dissociation predicted less improvement in a 3-month inpatient DBT programme. 268
Shame

Shame keeps the wave going

60 women with BPD reported more shame-proneness and state shame than women with social phobia or healthy women, and shame-proneness went with lower self-esteem and quality of life. 444 After criticism in the laboratory, shame stayed raised in BPD through the recovery period. 187

Established

Not only BPD

Affective instability is also high in PTSD and bulimia nervosa, and similar in BPD and ADHD; the authors advise against using emotion dysregulation alone to tell them apart. 354,455 See BPD or something else?

Early evidence

Reactivity, or a high baseline?

Linehan's model predicts high reactivity and slow recovery; laboratory studies mostly find a high baseline of negative emotion and little extra physiological reactivity. Reactivity tied to specific contexts, such as rejection, criticism and shame, is the best-supported middle position. The question is unresolved. 73,187,217,238,289

The wave changes over time. In the McLean study, 96% of people had a 2-year remission of affective instability by 16 years, and e-diary instability is lower in older people with BPD. These are population figures, not a forecast for one person. 456,596 More in Course & recovery, and on how therapy targets the wave in Dialectical behaviour therapy.
