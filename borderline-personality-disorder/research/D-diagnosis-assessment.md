# D · Diagnosis: DSM-5-TR, ICD-11, assessment and differential

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: criteria, icd, assess, differential.

This pass covers how borderline personality disorder (BPD) is defined and diagnosed: the DSM-5-TR Section II criteria and general personality disorder criteria, the arithmetic of criterion combinations, how often each criterion occurs and how well it predicts the diagnosis, the ICD-11 dimensional model (severity, trait domains, borderline pattern), the DSM-5-TR Alternative Model (AMPD), diagnosis in adolescents, assessment tools with their published cut-offs and accuracy, disclosure of the diagnosis, and the differential diagnosis and comorbidity matrix (bipolar disorder, complex PTSD, ADHD, depression, autism, substance use, eating disorders, psychosis, other personality disorders). Every number was read in the PubMed abstract or, where stated, in the open-access full text (PMC or Europe PMC).

## Findings

### criteria · DSM-5-TR Section II: the nine criteria
- DSM-5-TR defines BPD as a pervasive pattern of instability of interpersonal relationships, self-image and affects, and marked impulsivity. It begins by early adulthood, is present in a variety of contexts, and is shown by 5 (or more) of 9 criteria [[APA2022,Kaess2025]].
- Criterion 1, abandonment: frantic efforts to avoid real or imagined abandonment. Suicidal or self-harming behaviour is not counted here because it belongs to criterion 5 [[APA2022,Kaess2025]].
- Criterion 2, relationships: a pattern of unstable and intense relationships that alternate between extremes of idealization and devaluation [[APA2022,Kaess2025]].
- Criterion 3, identity: identity disturbance, a markedly and persistently unstable self-image or sense of self [[APA2022,Kaess2025]].
- Criterion 4, impulsivity: impulsivity in at least two potentially self-damaging areas (for example spending, sex, substance use, reckless driving, binge eating). Suicidal or self-harming behaviour is again excluded [[APA2022,Kaess2025]].
- Criterion 5, suicidal or self-harming behaviour: recurrent suicidal behaviour, gestures or threats, or self-mutilating behaviour [[APA2022,Kaess2025]].
- Criterion 6, affective instability: affective instability due to marked reactivity of mood, for example intense episodic dysphoria, irritability or anxiety "usually lasting a few hours and only rarely more than a few days" [[APA2022,Kaess2025]].
- Criterion 7, emptiness: chronic feelings of emptiness [[APA2022,Kaess2025]].
- Criterion 8, anger: inappropriate, intense anger or difficulty controlling anger (for example frequent displays of temper, constant anger, recurrent physical fights) [[APA2022,Kaess2025]].
- Criterion 9, stress-related paranoia or dissociation: transient, stress-related paranoid ideation or severe dissociative symptoms [[APA2022,Kaess2025]].
- The defining criteria have not changed in any major way from DSM-III (1980) to DSM-5. BPD can be diagnosed reliably, and distinguished from other mental disorders, with semi-structured interviews (Established) [[Leichsenring2024]].
- DSM-5-TR general personality disorder criteria (short form): an enduring pattern of inner experience and behaviour that deviates markedly from cultural expectations in at least two of cognition, affectivity, interpersonal functioning and impulse control. The pattern must be inflexible and pervasive, cause clinically significant distress or impairment, be stable and of long duration with onset traceable to adolescence or early adulthood, not be better explained by another mental disorder, and not be attributable to a substance or a medical condition [[APA2022]].

### criteria · Heterogeneity: the arithmetic of 5 of 9
- The polythetic rule (any 5 of 9) can be met in 256 different combinations. That total is 126 combinations of exactly 5 criteria, 84 of 6, 36 of 7, 9 of 8 and 1 of all 9 [[Mulay2019]].
- Arithmetic consequence: two people can both meet the threshold yet share only one criterion. For example, one person meets criteria 1–5 and another meets 5–9 [[Mulay2019]].
- Despite this heterogeneity, the nine criteria form a statistically coherent construct. In 668 mainly treatment-seeking CLPS participants, a one-factor model fitted well. A three-factor model (disturbed relatedness, behavioural dysregulation, affective dysregulation) fitted significantly better and replicated at 2 years in 498 participants (Established) [[Sanislow2002]].
- Across DSM-IV personality disorders in CLPS (n = 668), Cronbach's alpha ranged from 0.47 to 0.87 (median 0.71). Median correlations among criteria within a disorder (0.23) were higher than between disorders (0.08) [[Grilo2001]].
- In a bifactor analysis of 966 inpatients assessed with the SCID-II, the BPD criteria loaded only on the general personality-pathology factor ("g"). The other personality disorders loaded on both the general factor and a specific factor, or mainly on a specific factor (Early evidence) [[Sharp2015JAP]].
- In network analyses of three samples (CLPS n = 731; MIDAS outpatients n = 3,651; adolescents n = 1,012), affective instability and identity disturbance were the most central symptoms across all three. Relationship difficulties were also central in the two adult networks [[Peters2023]].
- The proportion meeting full BPD criteria differed by sample: 46% in CLPS, 10% in MIDAS outpatients and 26.2% in the adolescent clinical sample. The mean number of criteria was 3.41, 1.74 and 2.78 respectively [[Peters2023]].
- In 506 help-seeking adolescents aged 12–17, latent class analysis found a "borderline group" with high probabilities on all criteria (86.8%, mostly female) and an "impulsive group" high only on impulsivity and anger (13.2%, mostly male). These resemble the ICD-10 borderline and impulsive subtypes of emotionally unstable personality disorder [[Cavelti2021]].

### criteria · How common each criterion is and how well it predicts BPD
- Among 3,674 psychiatric outpatients interviewed with a semi-structured interview, affective instability had the highest sensitivity of the nine criteria (92.8%) and a negative predictive value of 99%. The authors recommend asking about affective instability as a one-question screen [[Zimmerman2017BJP]].
- In 53 people with BPD compared with 83 with bipolar disorder (comorbid cases excluded), affective instability was the most common criterion in BPD (92.5%) and inappropriate anger the least common (49%) [[Bayes2020]].
- In the same comparison, abandonment fears had the highest specificity and the highest positive predictive value (0.9). Affective instability had the lowest specificity. Unstable relationships had the highest negative predictive value (0.91), and identity disturbance and abandonment fears gave the highest accuracy (85% each) [[Bayes2020]].
- People with BPD met a mean of 6.6 criteria (SD 1.0), compared with 1.9 (SD 1.3) in people with bipolar disorder [[Bayes2020]].
- Over 2 years in CLPS (n = 550, blinded follow-up), suicidal or self-injurious behaviour and unstable relationships were the criteria that best predicted a later BPD diagnosis [[Grilo2007]].
- In inpatients (123 adolescents, 106 adults), BPD and each criterion were about equally common in the two age groups. Abandonment fears were the best inclusion criterion in adolescents, while in adults all criteria were about equivalent. The best exclusion criterion was uncontrolled anger in adolescents and impulsiveness in adults [[Becker2002]].
- Below the threshold, features still matter. Among 1,976 outpatients with 0 or 1 BPD criterion, people with 1 criterion had more current Axis I disorders, suicide attempts, current suicidal ideation, hospitalisations and time off work than people with none. Rating 0 vs 1 criterion was reliable (kappa 0.70) [[Zimmerman2012JCP]].
- In 1,870 outpatients, meeting only one criterion (impulsivity, affective instability, emptiness or anger) was linked to more dysfunction than meeting none. Only emptiness on its own marked impairment on every index of psychosocial morbidity [[Ellison2016]].
- In 499 help-seeking youth aged 15–25, the number of BPD criteria was the best predictor of poor functioning, even below the diagnostic threshold [[Thompson2020]].

### icd · ICD-11: one personality disorder, graded by severity
- ICD-11 was approved by the World Health Assembly in May 2019, with health reporting from 1 January 2022. Dimensional approaches were built in, especially for personality disorders [[Reed2019]].
- ICD-11 removes all named personality disorder types except a general personality disorder (6D10). This is graded as mild (6D10.0), moderate (6D10.1) or severe (6D10.2) [[Sharan2023,Mulder2021,WHO2022ICD11]].
- General requirement: an enduring disturbance in the functioning of aspects of the self (for example identity, self-worth, accuracy of self-view, self-direction) and/or interpersonal dysfunction. It has persisted for an extended period (more than 2 years), shows as maladaptive patterns of cognition, emotion and behaviour, and appears across a range of personal and social situations [[Bach2018BMC]].
- Below the disorder threshold, "Personality Difficulty" (QE50.7) is not classified as a mental disorder. It is listed among factors influencing health status and covers relatively stable difficulties (at least 2 years) that appear intermittently or at low intensity [[Sharan2023,Bach2018BMC]].
- Harm marks the severity bands: mild personality disorder is "typically not", moderate "sometimes" and severe "often" associated with harm to self or others [[Bach2018BMC]].
- Functional impairment also scales with severity: limited to circumscribed areas in mild, marked in moderate (although functioning in some areas may be kept), and severe in all or nearly all areas in severe personality disorder [[Bach2018BMC]].
- Under stress, mild personality disorder shows some distortion of appraisals with intact reality testing. Moderate shows marked distortions, possibly with mild dissociative states or psychotic-like beliefs. Severe often shows dissociative states or psychotic-like beliefs, for example extreme paranoid reactions [[Bach2018BMC]].
- Five optional trait-domain specifiers describe the style of the disturbance: Negative Affectivity (6D11.0), Detachment (6D11.1), Dissociality (6D11.2), Disinhibition (6D11.3) and Anankastia (6D11.4). As many as needed may be recorded [[Sharan2023,Bach2018BMC]].
- Negative Affectivity is defined as a tendency to experience a broad range of negative emotions with frequency and intensity out of proportion to the situation. Disinhibition is a tendency to act rashly on immediate stimuli without considering negative consequences [[Bach2018BMC]].
- The Borderline pattern specifier (6D11.5) was added "to enhance the clinical utility" of the classification, including identifying people who may respond to certain psychotherapies [[Sharan2023]].
- The Borderline pattern is the only ICD-11 personality specifier defined polythetically: at least 5 of 9 features adapted from the DSM-5 BPD criteria [[Bach2018BMC]].
- ICD-11's borderline pattern describes emotional instability as mood states that "typically last for a few hours but may last for up to several days". It lists further features that may or may not be present: a view of the self as inadequate or bad, profound alienation and loneliness, and rejection hypersensitivity with frequent misreading of social signals [[Bach2018BMC]].
- A full ICD-11 description can combine every layer, for example "Moderate Personality Disorder, with Borderline Pattern, with Negative Affectivity, Disinhibition and Dissociality" [[Bach2018BMC]].
- Mapping to DSM: in 606 patients from depression trials, BPD symptoms were associated with greater ICD-11 severity (p < 0.0001). They mapped mainly onto the Negative Affective, Dissocial and Disinhibited domains, with only weak links to Anankastia and Detachment [[Mulder2020]].
- The ICD-11 and DSM-5 trait models share four comparable domains (Negative Affectivity, Detachment, Antagonism/Dissociality, Disinhibition). ICD-11 adds Anankastia and DSM-5 adds Psychoticism. In 226 outpatients, both trait models captured much of the information in the categorical personality disorders [[Bach2018ANZJP]].
- The ICD-11 domains can be scored from 16 PID-5 trait facets. The five-domain structure was replicated in a Danish sample (n = 1,541) and a US sample (n = 637) [[Bach2017]].
- ICD-11 places schizotypal features in the psychotic disorders chapter, so there is no personality code for schizotypy or psychoticism [[Bach2018BMC]].
- ICD-11 states that personality disorders are only "relatively" stable after young adulthood and may lessen by middle age, or emerge later in life [[Bach2018BMC]].
- Rationale: there is no clear threshold between types and degrees of personality dysfunction, and pathology is best classified on a single dimension from normal personality to severe personality disorder (Established as the ICD-11 rationale; contested, see Controversies) [[Tyrer2015]].

### icd · DSM-5-TR Alternative Model (AMPD, Section III)
- AMPD Criterion A for BPD: moderate or greater impairment in personality functioning, shown as characteristic difficulties in 2 or more of 4 areas: identity, self-direction, empathy and intimacy [[APA2022,Kaess2025]].
- AMPD Criterion B for BPD: 4 or more of 7 pathological traits, at least one of which must be impulsivity, risk taking or hostility. The 7 traits are emotional lability, anxiousness, separation insecurity, depressivity, impulsivity, risk taking and hostility [[APA2022,Kaess2025]].
- The AMPD was proposed by the DSM-5 work group and endorsed by the DSM-5 Task Force, but the APA Board of Trustees placed it in Section III ("Emerging Measures and Models") [[Morey2013]].
- In a national sample of 337 patients rated by their clinicians, decision rules could be built that matched DSM-IV-TR and AMPD diagnoses better than DSM-III matched DSM-III-R [[Morey2013]].
- When 20 expert raters translated each DSM criterion into AMPD terms, 19 of 20 (95%) rated the Level of Personality Functioning Scale as impaired whenever 5 or more BPD criteria were present. The shared core was severity plus anxiousness, depressivity, emotional lability and impulsivity [[Mulay2019]].
- In 285 undergraduates, the Section III BPD trait score was associated with every external criterion tested. Disinhibition traits tracked externalising problems and negative-affect traits tracked internalising problems [[Anderson2015]].
- ICD-11 aligns closely with the AMPD [[Mulder2021]]. The SCID-AMPD can be cross-walked into an ICD-11 severity grade and trait domains [[Bach2018BMC]].

### criteria · Adolescents
- DSM-5-TR uses the same criteria for adolescents, but in people under 18 the features must have been present for at least 1 year [[APA2022,Kaess2025]].
- A coherent BPD syndrome typically begins in adolescence (after age 12) [[Bohus2021]]. Evidence supports diagnosing BPD from age 12, and validated measures exist for this age group (Established) [[Kaess2025]].
- BPD is as reliable and valid in adolescents as in adults, and it is recognised for adolescents in classification systems and national guidelines [[Kaess2014]].
- BPD has its onset in adolescence but is typically first diagnosed in young adulthood. The ESCAP expert review concludes there is "no justification for failing to make an early diagnosis" [[Fonagy2015]].
- Major classification systems now legitimise the BPD diagnosis in adolescents. Early, active assessment is presented as the way to reduce lifelong suffering [[Sharp2015JCPP]]. Prevention and early intervention are framed as a public-health priority [[Chanen2017]].
- In clinical settings, youth with BPD make up about 11% of outpatients and up to 50% of inpatients. One study reports BPD in 76% of adolescents attending the emergency department for suicidal behaviour [[Kaess2025]].
- Community samples show a roughly balanced sex ratio, while youth clinical samples are about 3 women to 1 man [[Kaess2025]].
- Several national guidelines (German AWMF, Australian NHMRC, UK NICE) recommend treating BPD in adolescents from age 12 or 14 [[Kaess2025]].
- NICE recommends that a person under 18 who presents in primary care with repeated self-harm, persistent risk-taking or marked emotional instability is referred to child and adolescent mental health services (CAMHS) for assessment. Young people should have the full range of recommended treatments within CAMHS [[NICE2009]].
- Interviews and self-report tools validated in youth include the CI-BPD, the DIB-R, the BPDSI-IV adolescent version, the SCID-II, the BPFSC (self- and parent-report), the BPQ, the MSI-BPD and the BSL [[Kaess2025]].
- The BPD symptom network is broadly the same in adolescents and adults, which supports the validity of the diagnosis across development [[Peters2023]].
- Clinicians remain reluctant to diagnose BPD in adolescents. More training in early diagnosis is recommended [[Kaess2025,Peters2023]].

### assess · Screening and diagnostic instruments
- Recommended route: a structured or semi-structured interview for diagnosis, and self-report questionnaires for screening or to rate severity [[Kaess2025,Leichsenring2024]].
- MSI-BPD (McLean Screening Instrument): 10 yes/no self-report items. In 200 people with treatment histories (139 with interview-confirmed BPD), a cut-off of 7 or more gave sensitivity 0.81 and specificity 0.85 [[Zanarini2003MSI]].
- MSI-BPD in younger adults: among 63 participants aged 25 or under, the same cut-off of 7 gave sensitivity 0.90 and specificity 0.93 [[Zanarini2003MSI]].
- MSI-BPD, Dutch version (159 women, SCID-II reference): alpha 0.90, AUC 0.96. A cut-off of 7 gave specificity 0.96 and sensitivity 0.71. A cut-off of 5 gave sensitivity 0.94 and specificity 0.86 [[Andre2015]].
- A review of MSI-BPD studies found that most derived the cut-off with a case-finding approach. For screening, where high sensitivity matters, the cut-off may need to be below 7 [[Zimmerman2021MSI]].
- Youth screening (101 outpatients aged 15–25, SCID-II reference): the Borderline Personality Questionnaire (BPQ) had sensitivity 0.68, specificity 0.90, NPV 0.91, PPV 0.65 and accuracy 0.85. It had test-retest ICC 0.92 and alpha 0.92, and outperformed the MSI on ROC analysis (p = 0.05). Screening "is not a replacement for clinical diagnosis" [[Chanen2008]].
- One-question screen: asking about affective instability identifies about 93% of outpatients with BPD (sensitivity 92.8%), and its absence nearly rules BPD out (NPV 99%) [[Zimmerman2017BJP]].
- PAI-BOR (Personality Assessment Inventory Borderline Features scale): in outpatients, a BOR-Total T-score of 70 or more classified the presence or absence of borderline pathology correctly in 73% [[Stein2007PAI]].
- BSL-23 (Borderline Symptom List, short form): 23 self-rated items drawn from the 95-item BSL. It was developed in 379 patients with BPD and tested in 659. It correlates 0.958–0.963 with the BSL-95 and has Cronbach's alpha 0.935–0.969 [[Bohus2009]].
- The BSL-23 separated BPD from Axis I disorders (mean effect size 1.13). It was sensitive to change: d = 0.47 after 3 months of DBT, compared with d = 0.38 for the BSL-95 [[Bohus2009]].
- BSL-23 severity grades (mean item score, n = 1,090 adults): none or low 0–0.3; mild 0.3–0.7; moderate 0.7–1.7; high 1.7–2.7; very high 2.7–3.5; extremely high 3.5–4 [[Kleindienst2020]].
- BSL-23 grades of high or above were seen in 70.0% of treatment-seeking people with BPD, 17.6% of clinical controls and 0.0% of healthy controls. The best separating score was 1.50 for BPD vs clinical controls and 0.64 for BPD vs healthy controls [[Kleindienst2020]].
- ZAN-BPD (Zanarini Rating Scale): a clinician-rated scale of change over a 1-week window. Each of the 9 criteria is rated 0–4, giving a total of 0–36 [[Zanarini2003ZAN]].
- In 200 patients, ZAN-BPD internal consistency was 0.85. Inter-rater (32 conjoint interviews) and same-day test-retest (40 patients) intraclass correlations were good to excellent, and score changes tracked SCL-90 changes over 7–10 days [[Zanarini2003ZAN]].
- DIB-R (Revised Diagnostic Interview for Borderlines): kappas above 0.75 for the BPD diagnosis in inter-rater, test-retest and longitudinal reliability substudies. Test-retest kappas for individual symptoms were excellent for one-third and fair to good (0.57–0.73) for two-thirds [[Zanarini2002DIBR]].
- IPDE (International Personality Disorder Examination): a semi-structured interview compatible with ICD-10 and DSM. In the WHO field trial (716 patients, 14 centres, 11 countries, 58 clinicians), its inter-rater reliability and temporal stability were roughly similar to instruments for psychosis, mood, anxiety and substance use disorders [[Loranger1994]].
- SCID-II: in 151 inpatients, outpatients and controls, most personality disorders showed excellent inter-rater agreement [[Lobbestael2011]].
- SCID-5-PD (Persian version, 250 patients): agreement with psychiatrists' DSM-5 diagnosis was acceptable (kappa > 0.4) for BPD. Inter-rater and test-retest reliability were good for BPD (Early evidence; single-country study) [[Gharraee2022]].
- Interview vs self-report: in CLPS, self-report gave higher rates of criterion endorsement. Interviews were not more valid for predicting outcome, and using both methods together identified BPD criteria best [[Hopwood2008]].
- NICE assessment in community mental health services covers psychosocial and occupational functioning, coping, strengths and vulnerabilities, comorbid mental disorders and social problems, the need for psychological treatment, social care and occupational rehabilitation, and the needs of any dependent children [[NICE2009]].
- Pre-test probability matters for any screen. BPD affects about 1.7% of the general population and 15–28% of patients in psychiatric clinics or hospitals [[Gunderson2018]]. Other estimates are a median point prevalence of about 1%, 10–12% in outpatient clinics and 20–22% in inpatient clinics [[Ellison2018]].
- Worked example (calculated from Zanarini2003MSI sensitivity 0.81 and specificity 0.85; population estimate, not a measured value): the PPV of an MSI-BPD score of 7+ would be about 5% at 1% prevalence, about 38% at 10% and about 57% at 20%. The NPV at 10% prevalence would be about 98% [[Zanarini2003MSI,Ellison2018]].

### assess · Telling people the diagnosis
- In a survey of 134 US psychiatrists, 57% said they had at some point not disclosed a BPD diagnosis and 37% had not documented it. Stigma or diagnostic uncertainty played a role for most of them [[Sisti2016]].
- Arguments for disclosure include patient autonomy, the chance for psychoeducation and collaboration on specific and effective therapies, and patients' access to diagnostic information from other sources [[Lequesne2004]].
- Withholding a psychiatric diagnosis to protect the patient (therapeutic privilege) is now generally seen as unethical. Non-disclosure of BPD also harms integrated care [[Sims2022]].
- Psychoeducation right after disclosure: 50 young women met DIB-R and DSM-IV criteria and were all told the diagnosis. The 30 randomised to a workshop within a week had a significantly greater fall in general impulsivity and in the "storminess" of close relationships over 12 weeks than the 20 waitlisted. Psychosocial functioning did not improve significantly (Early evidence) [[Zanarini2008Psychoed]].
- NICE asks clinicians to explore treatment options "in an atmosphere of hope and optimism, explaining that recovery is possible and attainable". People with BPD should not be excluded from any service because of their diagnosis or because they have self-harmed [[NICE2009]].
- The evidence behind that message: in 290 people with severe BPD followed prospectively, 34.5% were in remission at 2 years, 49.4% at 4 years and 68.6% at 6 years. Only 5.9% of remissions were followed by recurrence [[Zanarini2003Course]].
- By 16 years, 78%–99% had reached sustained symptomatic remission (population statistics from one inpatient cohort). Recovery that also included good social and vocational functioning was reached by 40%–60% [[Zanarini2012]].
- Impulsive symptoms resolved fastest, affective symptoms were the most persistent, and cognitive and interpersonal symptoms were in between [[Zanarini2003Course]].
- Missing the diagnosis leads to misleading drug treatment that rarely succeeds. Clinicians are urged to ask actively about relationships and unstable identity [[Gunderson2018]].

### differential · Bipolar I and II disorder
- The core difference: mood shifts in BPD are transient and occur in response to interpersonal stressors, while bipolar disorder involves sustained mood changes. Misdiagnosis can deprive a person of effective psychotherapy for BPD, or of effective medication for bipolar disorder [[Paris2015]].
- DSM criterion 6 describes mood episodes in BPD "usually lasting a few hours and only rarely more than a few days" [[APA2022,Kaess2025]].
- An Indian Psychiatric Society guideline describes BPD mood oscillations as frequent and short, lasting hours to a few days, and reactive to perceived rejection. Bipolar oscillations are described as longer and able to occur regardless of external events [[Sharan2023]].
- Across studies, about 10% of people with BPD had bipolar I and another 10% bipolar II. About 20% of people with bipolar II and 10% with bipolar I had BPD. In 80%–90% of cases each disorder occurs without the other [[Zimmerman2013DCN]].
- Meta-analysis (42 papers): the prevalence of BPD among 5,273 people with bipolar disorder was 21.6% (95% CI 17.0–27.1). It was 37.7% (21.9–56.6) in bipolar II. The prevalence of bipolar disorder among 1,814 people with BPD was 18.5% (12.7–26.1) [[Fornaro2016]].
- Major depression, substance use disorders and PTSD are each more common in BPD than bipolar disorder is [[Zimmerman2013DCN]].
- The two conditions share mood lability and impulsivity, but differ on the other validators: childhood sexual abuse history, family history of bipolar disorder, parasuicidal self-harm, treatment response and neurobiology [[Ghaemi2014]].
- The most consistent predictors of BPD over bipolar disorder were childhood sexual abuse, childhood depersonalisation, personality features of relationship difficulty and sensitivity to criticism, and no family history of bipolar disorder [[Bayes2016]].
- Personality items alone classified 81%–84% of participants correctly. Classification rose to 88% with the other study variables and to 92%–95% when hypo/manic features were added [[Bayes2016]].
- Key differentiators for bipolar II vs BPD are family history, developmental antecedents, illness course, the phenomenology of mood states, personality style and relationship factors. Impulsivity, neuropsychological profile, sex distribution, comorbidity and treatment response separate them less well [[Bayes2019]].
- Among criteria, abandonment fears and identity disturbance distinguish BPD from bipolar disorder best. Affective instability is transdiagnostic and less useful for this decision [[Bayes2020]].
- Overdiagnosis of bipolar disorder: among 610 outpatients, BPD significantly raised the odds of a past bipolar misdiagnosis, and no single BPD criterion was unique in predicting it [[Ruggero2010]].
- Of 82 outpatients whose earlier bipolar diagnosis was not confirmed by SCID, 24.4% had BPD, compared with 6.1% of 528 never so diagnosed (p < .001) [[Zimmerman2010JCP]].
- In the US national NESARC Wave 2 sample (n = 34,653), the association of BPD with bipolar disorder stayed strong after other comorbidity was controlled (odds ratio ≥ 4.3, shared with schizotypal and narcissistic personality disorders) [[Grant2008]].

### differential · Complex PTSD and PTSD
- ICD-11 complex PTSD has the three core PTSD symptom clusters plus chronic, pervasive disturbances in emotion regulation, identity (self-concept) and relationships. It usually follows sustained or multiple trauma exposure. Population prevalence is 1%–8%, and up to 50% in mental health facilities [[Maercker2022]].
- Latent class analysis of 280 women seeking treatment for childhood abuse found four distinct classes: low symptom, PTSD, complex PTSD and BPD [[Cloitre2014]].
- Four BPD symptoms greatly increased the odds of being in the BPD class rather than the complex PTSD class: frantic efforts to avoid abandonment, unstable sense of self, unstable and intense relationships, and impulsiveness [[Cloitre2014]].
- Bifactor modelling in 617 adults: a negative self-concept was the most distinctive feature of complex PTSD, while an alternating, unstable self-concept was the most distinctive feature of BPD. BPD symptoms were harder to separate from the general vulnerability factor [[Frost2020]].
- Reviews conclude that PTSD, complex PTSD and BPD are potentially comorbid but distinct syndromes. The authors put forward a continuum model, linked to the stress response and with dissociation in each, as a hypothesis (Hypothesis) [[Ford2021]].
- In the US population (NESARC, n = 34,653), lifetime prevalence was 6.6% for PTSD and 5.9% for BPD. Of people with BPD, 30.2% also had PTSD, and 24.2% of people with PTSD had BPD [[Pagura2010]].
- Having both PTSD and BPD was linked to poorer quality of life, more comorbidity, higher odds of a lifetime suicide attempt and more repeated childhood trauma than either alone [[Pagura2010]].
- In inpatients, PTSD was "common but not universal" in BPD. That argues against the view that BPD is simply chronic PTSD [[Zanarini1998AxisI]].

### differential · ADHD
- Among 118 women seeking treatment for BPD, 41.5% had childhood ADHD and 16.1% had adult ADHD. Childhood ADHD was associated with childhood emotional abuse and more severe adult BPD symptoms [[Philipsen2008]].
- In another adult BPD sample, 69 (38.1%) had comorbid adult ADHD. The BPD + ADHD group had more substance use disorders (59.4% vs 38.4%) and antisocial personality disorder (7.2% vs 0.9%). The BPD-only group had more mood disorders (62.5% vs 37.7%) and panic disorder (54.5% vs 23.1%) [[Ferrer2010]].
- Lifetime ADHD was found in 27.9% of adult inpatients and 22.1% of adolescent inpatients with BPD, with no significant age difference [[Zanarini2021Comorbidity]].
- Meta-analysis of adults with ADHD (11 studies, 2,120 participants): BPD co-occurred in 21.9%. Heterogeneity was very high (I² > 90%), and structured interviews gave higher rates than routine clinical assessment [[Adamis2026]].
- Distinguishing features: impulsivity in BPD is stress-dependent and linked to poor use of context cues for inhibition, while ADHD shows more motor impulsivity. Emotion dysregulation increases from ADHD to BPD to the two combined [[Ditrich2021]].

### differential · Depression
- Major depression is the most common lifetime comorbidity: 86.6% of adult and 75.0% of adolescent inpatients with BPD. Dysthymia affects 44.8% and 26.0% [[Zanarini2021Comorbidity]].
- Clinical cues described in one guideline: sudden anger is rare in depression; and in BPD, mistrust is accompanied by anger rather than resignation [[Sharan2023]].
- BPD treatments and natural course often leave persistent social disability related to depression or self-harm, even after symptomatic remission [[Gunderson2018]].
- Pooled across clinical studies, depressive disorders, substance use disorders, PTSD, ADHD, bipolar disorder, bulimia nervosa and other personality disorders are all significantly associated with BPD [[Leichsenring2024]].

### differential · Autism
- Meta-analysis (19 studies, 12 pooled, 11 at high risk of bias): pooled prevalence of BPD in autism was 4% (95% CI 0–9%) and of autism in BPD 3% (1–8%). Both are within population estimates, and the data could not show whether one is misdiagnosed as the other [[May2021]].
- On self-report trait measures (624 autistic, 23 BPD, 16 comorbid, 2,081 neurotypical controls), autistic-trait scores ran comorbid > autism > BPD > controls. Empathy Quotient scores in BPD did not differ from controls, while autistic groups scored lower [[Dudas2017]].
- In women and people assigned female at birth (51 autistic, 51 with BPD), identity disruption was the largest difference, higher in BPD (d = -1.36, 95% CI -1.79 to -0.92). Sensory processing differences were the largest feature favouring autism (d = 1.19, 95% CI 0.76 to 1.60) [[Barnicot2026]].
- In that study, social cognition, social camouflaging, identity disruption, impulsivity and coping with being alone together classified 95.1% of participants correctly (AUC 0.98) [[Barnicot2026]].
- Autistic girls and women without intellectual disability are diagnosed later and may be misdiagnosed with BPD. The authors attribute this to camouflaging (masking) and to assessments that look only at current presentation. They recommend a full neurodevelopmental history when BPD is suspected (Hypothesis-level conceptual analysis) [[McQuaid2024]].

### differential · Substance use
- Review of 70 studies (2000–2017): among people with BPD, a weighted 45.46% had a current substance use disorder and 75.28% a lifetime one. Current alcohol use disorder was 46.39% and current drug use disorder 39.24% [[Trull2018]].
- Among people with a substance use disorder, 22.1% met BPD criteria. The figure was 16.99% with alcohol use disorder, 22.03% with cocaine dependence and 33.80% with opioid dependence [[Trull2018]].
- Substance use disorders were more common in men with BPD, and eating disorders more common in women with BPD [[Zanarini1998AxisI]].
- Over 6 years, not having a substance use disorder predicted remission from BPD far more strongly than not having PTSD, mood, anxiety or eating disorders [[Zanarini2004Comorbidity]].
- Lifetime substance use disorders affected 62.1% of adult and 35.6% of adolescent inpatients with BPD [[Zanarini2021Comorbidity]].

### differential · Eating disorders
- Meta-analysis of 87 studies: 52% of people with an eating disorder had a personality disorder, compared with 9% of healthy controls. Borderline and avoidant were the most prevalent, with similar profiles in anorexia and bulimia [[Martinussen2017]].
- Lifetime eating disorders were found in 53.8% of adult inpatients with BPD (anorexia 21.7%, bulimia 24.1%, eating disorder not otherwise specified 28.3%) and 30.8% of adolescents [[Zanarini2021Comorbidity]].
- Over 10 years, more than 90% of people with BPD who had anorexia, bulimia or EDNOS at baseline reached stable remission. Shifting from one eating disorder to another was common [[Zanarini2010ED]].
- EDNOS was the most persistent: recurrences 52% and new onsets 43%. The comparable figures were 28% and 4% for anorexia, and 29% and 11% for bulimia [[Zanarini2010ED]].

### differential · Psychotic symptoms and other personality disorders
- In 324 outpatients with BPD, the point prevalence of hallucinations was 43%, with 8%–21% in individual sensory modalities. Auditory verbal hallucinations were mostly verbal abuse and distressing [[Niemantsverdriet2017]].
- Hallucination severity tracked delusions and unusual thought content, but not negative symptoms or disorganisation. This helps distinguish them from primary psychotic disorders [[Niemantsverdriet2017]].
- Hallucinations correlated with PTSD, the number of comorbid disorders, childhood emotional abuse and adult stressors. The authors advise treating PTSD and hallucinations when they are present [[Niemantsverdriet2017]].
- Among 379 inpatients with BPD and 125 with other personality disorders, paranoid, avoidant and dependent personality disorders discriminated BPD best [[Zanarini1998AxisII]].
- Men with BPD were more likely than women with BPD to meet criteria for paranoid, passive-aggressive, narcissistic, sadistic and antisocial personality disorders [[Zanarini1998AxisII]].

### differential · The overall comorbidity pattern
- "Complex comorbidity", meaning a lifetime disorder of affect plus a disorder of impulse, had strong positive predictive power for the BPD diagnosis, with high sensitivity and specificity [[Zanarini1998AxisI]].
- Complex comorbidity was present in 76.6% of adult and 40.4% of adolescent inpatients with BPD (OR 4.21) [[Zanarini2021Comorbidity]].
- Anxiety disorders were almost as common as mood disorders in BPD and discriminated it from other personality disorders better [[Zanarini1998AxisI]]. Lifetime anxiety disorders affected 89.0% of adults and 60.6% of adolescents [[Zanarini2021Comorbidity]].
- Lifetime PTSD is reported as 53.3% in adult inpatients with BPD (n = 169 of 290 in the same table, which would be 58.3%) and 22.1% in adolescents [[Zanarini2021Comorbidity]].
- Bipolar II was uncommon in these inpatient samples: 5.5% of adults and 3.8% of adolescents [[Zanarini2021Comorbidity]].
- Over follow-up, rates of comorbid disorders fell substantially in people whose BPD remitted but stayed stable in those whose BPD did not remit [[Zanarini2004Comorbidity,Shah2018]].

### Exam hooks
- BPD needs 5 or more of 9 DSM-5-TR criteria, which gives 256 possible combinations (126 with exactly 5) [[APA2022,Mulay2019]].
- Criterion 6 mood episodes usually last hours, rarely more than a few days. Sustained mood episodes point to bipolar disorder [[Kaess2025,Paris2015]].
- Suicidal or self-harming behaviour is scored only under criterion 5, never under criteria 1 or 4 [[APA2022,Kaess2025]].
- Affective instability has the highest sensitivity (92.8%) and NPV (99%), so its absence nearly rules BPD out [[Zimmerman2017BJP]].
- Abandonment fears and identity disturbance best separate BPD from bipolar disorder. Affective instability is transdiagnostic [[Bayes2020]].
- MSI-BPD: 10 items, cut-off 7, sensitivity 0.81, specificity 0.85 [[Zanarini2003MSI]].
- ZAN-BPD measures change: 9 items rated 0–4, total 0–36, over a 1-week window [[Zanarini2003ZAN]].
- BSL-23 is a self-rated severity measure with six grades of mean score. A mean of 1.50 best separated BPD from other patients [[Kleindienst2020]].
- ICD-11 code map: 6D10 personality disorder (.0 mild, .1 moderate, .2 severe), 6D11.0–6D11.4 trait domains, 6D11.5 borderline pattern, QE50.7 personality difficulty [[Sharan2023]].
- The five ICD-11 trait domains are Negative Affectivity, Detachment, Dissociality, Disinhibition and Anankastia. There is no psychoticism domain [[Bach2018BMC,Bach2018ANZJP]].
- AMPD BPD: Criterion A, impairment in 2 or more of identity, self-direction, empathy and intimacy. Criterion B, 4 or more of 7 traits, at least one of impulsivity, risk taking or hostility [[Kaess2025]].
- Under 18, the DSM requires BPD features to have lasted at least 1 year [[APA2022,Kaess2025]].
- Cloitre's four discriminators of BPD from complex PTSD are abandonment fears, unstable self, unstable relationships and impulsiveness [[Cloitre2014]].
- In complex PTSD the self-concept is stably negative. In BPD it alternates [[Frost2020]].
- About half of people with BPD have a current substance use disorder, and about a quarter of people with a substance use disorder have BPD [[Trull2018]].
- About 1 in 5: BPD is found in 21.6% of people with bipolar disorder and bipolar disorder in 18.5% of people with BPD [[Fornaro2016]].
- Hallucinations occur in about 43% of outpatients with BPD, without the negative symptoms or disorganisation typical of primary psychosis [[Niemantsverdriet2017]].
- "Complex comorbidity" (a disorder of affect plus a disorder of impulse) is a strong marker of BPD [[Zanarini1998AxisI]].

### In plain words
- BPD is diagnosed when at least 5 of 9 typical difficulties have been part of someone's life for a long time [[APA2022]].
- Because only 5 of 9 are needed, two people with BPD can look very different from each other [[Mulay2019]].
- Mood swings in BPD usually last hours, often after a hurt in a relationship. Mood episodes in bipolar disorder last days to weeks [[Paris2015,Kaess2025]].
- A short questionnaire can flag who should have a full assessment, but only a careful interview gives a diagnosis [[Chanen2008,Zanarini2003MSI]].
- Teenagers can be diagnosed too. Early diagnosis means earlier help [[Kaess2025,Fonagy2015]].
- Newer systems (ICD-11) first ask how much a person's personality difficulties affect their life (mild, moderate or severe), then describe their style [[Bach2018BMC]].
- Many people with BPD also have depression, anxiety, trauma symptoms, substance problems or eating problems. Each of these needs attention [[Zanarini2021Comorbidity]].
- Hearing voices can happen in BPD, especially under stress, and it is not the same as schizophrenia [[Niemantsverdriet2017]].
- BPD and autism can look alike from the outside, but they are different. A good assessment asks about childhood development [[McQuaid2024,Barnicot2026]].
- You have the right to know your diagnosis. Learning about BPD soon after hearing the diagnosis can help [[Sims2022,Zanarini2008Psychoed]].
- Recovery is common: in a large long-term study, most people with BPD no longer met the criteria after some years [[Zanarini2003Course,Zanarini2012]].
- If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

## Open questions and controversies
- Should BPD remain a named category? Position A: BPD is "a spurious condition unsupported by science that should be abandoned" for the ICD-11 severity-plus-traits model [[Mulder2023,Tyrer2015]]. The bifactor finding that BPD criteria load only on general personality pathology is read by some as support for this [[Sharp2015JAP]]. Position B: BPD can be diagnosed reliably with semi-structured interviews, its criteria form a coherent construct, and it links to effective specific treatments [[Leichsenring2024,Sanislow2002]]. Status: unresolved. DSM-5-TR keeps the category, and ICD-11 keeps it only as an optional specifier.
- Is the ICD-11 borderline pattern specifier redundant? Position A: its features are already captured by severity plus the Negative Affectivity, Dissociality and Disinhibition domains [[Mulder2020]]. Position B: it gives a familiar guide to choosing manualised psychotherapies [[Bach2018BMC,Sharan2023]]. Status: no outcome studies yet comparing care guided by the specifier with care guided by severity and domains alone.
- Is BPD part of the bipolar spectrum? Position A: about 1 in 5 people with one disorder has the other, so comorbidity is common [[Fornaro2016]]. Position B: the disorders differ on almost every validator apart from mood lability and impulsivity, and each usually occurs without the other [[Ghaemi2014,Zimmerman2013DCN,Paris2015]]. Status: most current reviews treat them as distinct but often comorbid. Overdiagnosis of bipolar disorder in BPD is documented [[Ruggero2010,Zimmerman2010JCP]].
- Complex PTSD vs BPD: separate disorders or points on a continuum? Latent class and bifactor studies support separate constructs, distinguished by abandonment fears, identity instability, relationship instability, impulsivity and the type of self-concept [[Cloitre2014,Frost2020]]. Ford and Courtois propose a stress-response continuum with dissociation in each (Hypothesis) [[Ford2021]]. Status: open, and both can be diagnosed together.
- What cut-off should the MSI-BPD use? The original cut-off is 7 [[Zanarini2003MSI]]. Dutch data give an optimal cut-off of 5 [[Andre2015]], and a review argues for a lower cut-off when the aim is screening rather than case-finding [[Zimmerman2021MSI]]. Status: depends on setting and purpose. Report sensitivity and specificity for each cut-off.
- Diagnosing BPD in adolescents: evidence supports diagnosis from age 12 and the symptom network matches adults [[Kaess2025,Peters2023]], but clinicians' reluctance persists [[Kaess2025]]. Status: guidelines favour diagnosis and early intervention, and practice lags behind.
- Autism misdiagnosed as BPD in women: a conceptual case is made for sex- and gender-related bias and masking [[McQuaid2024]]. The meta-analysis could not assess misdiagnosis, and its pooled overlap rates are low [[May2021]]. A new controlled comparison finds the groups separable on measures, with identity disruption favouring BPD and sensory processing favouring autism [[Barnicot2026]]. Status: open. A developmental history is recommended.
- Disclosure: most psychiatrists surveyed had withheld the diagnosis at some time [[Sisti2016]], while ethics and psychoeducation evidence favour open disclosure [[Sims2022,Zanarini2008Psychoed]]. Status: guidance favours disclosure. The psychoeducation evidence comes from one small trial.
- How the ICD-11 and DSM-5 systems relate: ICD-11 aligns with the DSM-5 AMPD [[Mulder2021]], but the AMPD sits in Section III while Section II categories remain the DSM standard [[Morey2013]]. Status: two parallel systems, and NICE CG78 is under review because of the ICD-11 change [[NICE2009]].

## Provenance
- Validation: 84 sources were checked against PubMed E-utilities esummary records (authors, title, journal, year, volume, pages, PMID, PMCID, DOI), and the source lines above were generated from those records. APA2022 was checked against its Crossref record (doi:10.1176/appi.books.9780890425787, APA Publishing, issued 2022-03-18). One source (WHO2022ICD11) is unverified because icd.who.int could not be read by the tools; its codes and wording were cross-checked against Sharan2023 and Bach2018BMC.
- Abstracts were read for every PubMed source. Open-access full text (PMC or Europe PMC) was read for Zanarini2021Comorbidity (Table 1), Trull2018 (weighted co-occurrence rates), Mulay2019 (256-combination footnote), Bach2018BMC (ICD-11 Tables 1–6), Sharan2023 (ICD-11 codes and differential table), Kaess2025 (DSM-5 Section II and AMPD criteria tables, adolescent prevalence, instruments), Peters2023 (Table 1 symptom means), Cavelti2021 (class sizes) and Kleindienst2020.
- Not accessible: the DSM-5-TR text (BPD and AMPD criteria were checked against the reproductions in Kaess2025; the general personality disorder criteria were not checked against a reproduction), the APA 2024 practice guideline full text (not cited), Biskin & Paris 2012 CMAJ and Oldham 2015 (no readable text; not cited), Grilo 2007 full text (only its abstract-level conclusion is used), and the full texts of Leichsenring2024 and Grant2008 (abstract-level facts only).
- One internal inconsistency was found in a source: Zanarini2021Comorbidity Table 1 gives adult lifetime PTSD as 53.3% with n = 169 of 290, which would be 58.3%. Both figures are reported, and the published percentage is used in the animation row.
- Calculated values (MSI-BPD positive predictive values at 1%, 10% and 20% prevalence) are derived with Bayes' rule from the published sensitivity and specificity. They are labelled "calculated" and are not reported by any source.
- Correction to the brief: there are 256 combinations of 5–9 criteria, not 636.
- Dropped for lack of a readable, validated source: per-criterion PPVs from Grilo 2007, Trull's PAI-BOR raw-score cut-off, the APA 2024 guideline statements on assessment, and Gunderson's "Good Psychiatric Management" disclosure script (its book source could not be validated). The Gunderson 2010 DSM-V proposal and First 2021 (ICD-11 vs DSM-5 comparison) were validated but not used, so they are not listed.
