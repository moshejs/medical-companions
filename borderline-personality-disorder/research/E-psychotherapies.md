# E · Psychological treatments: DBT, MBT, schema therapy, TFP, GPM and their evidence

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: dbt, mbt, schema, gpm, compare.

This pass covers the structured psychotherapies for borderline personality disorder (BPD): how dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT), mentalization-based treatment (MBT), schema therapy (ST), transference-focused psychotherapy (TFP) and generalist models (general psychiatric management / good psychiatric management, GPM; structured clinical management, SCM; STEPPS; stepped care) are built, their pivotal trials, the meta-analyses, dropout, and the guideline wording. Trial and meta-analysis numbers come from PubMed or Europe PMC abstracts, quoted as the abstracts state them. Outcome figures are group averages from trials and populations, not predictions for any one person.

## Findings

### dbt · What DBT is and how it is built
- DBT was developed from early efforts to apply standard behaviour therapy to people who were highly suicidal; its development was "trial and error", driven mainly by clinical experience [[LinehanWilks2015]].
- DBT is described as "a modular and hierarchical treatment" combining individual psychotherapy, group skills training, telephone coaching and a therapist consultation team [[LinehanWilks2015]].
- The standard package: weekly individual therapy (about 1 hour), a weekly group skills-training session (about 1.5–2.5 hours) and a therapist consultation-team meeting (about 1–2 hours) [[Chapman2006DBT]].
- Any DBT adaptation must serve five functions: (1) enhancing capabilities, (2) generalising capabilities, (3) improving motivation and reducing dysfunctional behaviours, (4) enhancing and maintaining therapist capabilities and motivation, (5) structuring the environment [[Chapman2006DBT]].
- The four skills areas taught in group: emotion regulation, mindfulness (attention to the present moment), interpersonal effectiveness, and distress tolerance (surviving crises without making things worse) [[Chapman2006DBT]].
- Skills groups usually have about 4 to 10 members and include teaching, active practice, discussion and homework [[Chapman2006DBT]].
- Phone coaching: the individual therapist is available by phone between sessions to help the person apply skills when most needed, for example in a crisis; this serves the "generalisation" function [[Chapman2006DBT]].
- Diary card: each week the person completes a self-monitoring form (the "diary card") tracking treatment targets such as self-harm, suicide attempts and emotional misery; the therapist uses it to set the session agenda [[Chapman2006DBT]].
- Target hierarchy: session time goes first to behaviours that threaten life (suicidal or self-injurious behaviour), then to behaviours that interfere with therapy (absence, lateness, non-collaboration), then to behaviours that interfere with quality of life [[Chapman2006DBT]].
- Chain analysis: after picking the target, therapist and patient work out what led up to the behaviour and which consequences may be maintaining it, then find more skilful alternatives [[Chapman2006DBT,Lynch2006DBT]].
- DBT's risk-management toolkit is described as the diary card, chain analysis, solution analysis, phone coaching and consultation team, with early orientation and commitment to reducing life-threatening behaviour [[Alba2022DBT]].
- The consultation team meets weekly for about 1–2 hours to support therapists, keep a compassionate, non-judgemental stance, and reduce burnout [[Chapman2006DBT]].
- Essential DBT elements beyond the format: the biosocial theory and a focus on emotions, a dialectical philosophy, and mindfulness and acceptance-oriented interventions [[Chapman2006DBT]].
- Proposed mechanisms of change in DBT can be distilled to "the reduction of ineffective action tendencies linked with dysregulated emotions", working through mindfulness, validation, targeting and chain analysis, and dialectics (Hypothesis) [[Lynch2006DBT]].

### dbt · Pivotal trials in adults
- Linehan 1991: the first RCT of DBT, 1 year of DBT vs community treatment as usual in chronically parasuicidal women with BPD, assessed every 4 months [[Linehan1991]].
- Linehan 1991 result: DBT recipients had fewer and less medically severe parasuicidal acts, stayed in individual therapy more, and had fewer inpatient psychiatric days; there were no between-group differences in depression, hopelessness, suicidal ideation or reasons for living [[Linehan1991]].
- Linehan 2006: 101 women with recent suicidal and self-injurious behaviour randomised to 1 year of DBT or community treatment by non-behavioural experts, followed for a further year [[Linehan2006]].
- Linehan 2006: people receiving DBT were "half as likely to make a suicide attempt" (hazard ratio 2.66; P = .005) over the 2-year treatment and follow-up period [[Linehan2006]].
- Linehan 2006: DBT also meant less hospitalisation for suicidal ideation, lower medical risk across suicide attempts and self-injury, fewer psychiatric hospitalisations and psychiatric emergency visits, and lower dropout (hazard ratio 3.2; P < .001) [[Linehan2006]].
- Linehan 2006's control arm was designed to match therapist sex, availability, expertise, allegiance, training, consultation and institutional prestige, so the effect "cannot reasonably be attributed to general factors associated with expert psychotherapy" [[Linehan2006]].
- Verheul 2003 (Netherlands): 58 women with BPD, 12 months of DBT vs usual treatment; DBT gave better retention and greater reductions in self-mutilating and self-damaging impulsive behaviour, especially in those with frequent self-mutilation [[Verheul2003]].
- Linehan 2015 component trial: 99 women with BPD and recent suicide attempts and self-injury were randomised to standard DBT, DBT skills training plus case management (DBT-S), or DBT individual therapy plus an activities group without skills (DBT-I), with 1 year of treatment and 1 year of follow-up [[Linehan2015]].
- Linehan 2015: all three arms improved similarly on suicide attempts, suicidal ideation, crisis-service use and reasons for living [[Linehan2015]].
- Linehan 2015: arms containing skills training reduced self-injury (NSSI) more than DBT-I, and standard DBT had lower treatment dropout than DBT-I (8 patients, 24% vs 16 patients, 48%; P = .04) [[Linehan2015]].
- Linehan 2015 conclusion: "Interventions that include DBT skills training are more effective than DBT without skills training" [[Linehan2015]].
- McMain 2017: 84 suicidal outpatients with BPD randomised to 20 weeks of DBT skills group (n = 42) or a waitlist (n = 42); the skills group reduced suicidal and NSSI episodes more by 32 weeks (P < 0.0001) and improved anger, distress tolerance and emotion regulation [[McMain2017]].
- DBT for PTSD (DBT-PTSD) vs cognitive processing therapy: 193 women with childhood-abuse PTSD and at least 3 BPD criteria; both groups improved (d 1.35 vs 0.98), with a small advantage for DBT-PTSD (difference 4.82 CAPS-5 points; 95% CI 0.67–8.96; d 0.33) [[Bohus2020DBTPTSD]].
- In the same trial, DBT-PTSD had less early dropout (25.5% vs 39.0%) and more symptomatic remission (58.4% vs 40.7%) than cognitive processing therapy [[Bohus2020DBTPTSD]].

### dbt · How long does DBT need to be?
- McMain 2022: 240 suicidal or self-harming people with BPD (mean age 28; 79% female) randomised to 6 months (DBT-6) or 12 months (DBT-12) of comprehensive DBT, with assessments to month 24 [[McMain2022]].
- McMain 2022: 6 months was non-inferior to 12 months for total self-harm at 6, 12 and 24 months (for example at 24 months, mean difference 0.12 vs margin −1.25; 95% CI −0.02 to 0.36) [[McMain2022]].
- McMain 2022: DBT-6 also showed faster reductions in BPD symptoms and general psychopathology, and dropout did not differ between arms [[McMain2022]].

### dbt · DBT for adolescents (DBT-A)
- Mehlum 2014: 77 adolescents with recent, repeated self-harm were randomised to 19 weeks of DBT-A or enhanced usual care; DBT-A was better at reducing self-harm, suicidal ideation and depressive symptoms [[Mehlum2014]].
- Mehlum 2016 (1-year follow-up, 75 of 77 retained): DBT-A stayed superior for self-harm frequency; other differences narrowed because the usual-care group caught up [[Mehlum2016]].
- Mehlum 2026 (12.4-year follow-up, 61 of 77 at mean age 28): self-harm episodes were more than twice as common after usual care as after DBT-A (mean difference 90 episodes per person), although the difference at the final follow-up was not statistically significant [[Mehlum2026]].
- McCauley 2018: 173 adolescents aged 12–18 at high suicide risk were randomised to 6 months of DBT or individual and group supportive therapy (IGST) at 4 US sites [[McCauley2018]].
- McCauley 2018 after treatment: no suicide attempts in 90.3% with DBT vs 78.9% with IGST (OR 0.30; 95% CI 0.10–0.91); no NSSI in 56.9% vs 40.0% (OR 0.32); no self-harm in 54.2% vs 36.9% (OR 0.33; 95% CI 0.14–0.78) [[McCauley2018]].
- McCauley 2018: the DBT advantage shrank between 6 and 12 months (OR 0.65; 95% CI 0.12–3.36), and treatment completion was higher with DBT (75.6% vs 55.2%) [[McCauley2018]].
- Kothgassner 2021 meta-analysis (21 studies, 1673 adolescents): vs controls, DBT-A reduced self-harm (g −0.44; 95% CI −0.81 to −0.07) and suicidal ideation (g −0.31); a reduction in BPD symptoms was seen only in pre–post studies (g −0.97) [[Kothgassner2021]].

### dbt · DBT meta-analyses
- Kliem 2010 (16 studies, 8 RCTs): moderate global effect and moderate effect on suicidal and self-injurious behaviour; dropout averaged 27.3% from pre- to post-treatment [[Kliem2010]].
- DeCou 2019 (18 controlled trials): DBT reduced self-directed violence (d −0.324; 95% CI −0.471 to −0.176) and psychiatric crisis-service use (d −0.379), with no significant pooled effect on suicidal ideation (d −0.229; 95% CI −0.473 to 0.016) [[DeCou2019]].

### gpm · General psychiatric management (GPM) and structured clinical management (SCM)
- McMain 2009: 180 people with BPD and at least 2 suicidal or non-suicidal self-injurious episodes in 5 years were randomised to 1 year of DBT or general psychiatric management (psychodynamically informed therapy plus symptom-targeted medication management following APA guidance) [[McMain2009]].
- McMain 2009: both groups improved, with fewer and less severe self-harm episodes, fewer emergency visits and hospital days, and better BPD symptoms, depression, anger and interpersonal functioning; "No significant differences across any outcomes were found between groups" [[McMain2009]].
- McMain 2012 (2 years after treatment): gains held in both groups with no loss on any outcome; about two-thirds reached diagnostic remission, but 53% were neither employed nor in school and 39% were receiving psychiatric disability support at 36 months [[McMain2012]].
- GPM is described as "a once-weekly, generalist model that medicalizes the disorder, emphasizes psychoeducation, and focuses on social adaptation"; its authors propose it as the primary intervention, with more intensive therapies kept for people who do not respond (Hypothesis) [[Gunderson2018GPM]].
- Brief GPM (10 sessions over 4 months) vs usual treatment, 76 patients: no difference in total ZAN-BPD at 4 months (d 0.04); GPM did better on relationship problems (d 0.52), impulsivity, social problems and retention [[Kramer2024GPM]].
- At 12 months after the same brief treatment, the GPM group showed significantly greater ZAN-BPD symptom reduction than usual treatment (Early evidence) [[BlancoMachinea2026]].
- Structured clinical management (SCM) was the active comparator in Bateman 2009: 18 months of outpatient MBT vs SCM in 134 patients; "Substantial improvements were observed in both conditions" [[Bateman2009]].
- A 2026 Korean pilot RCT (64 outpatients, 12 months) found both MBT and SCM cut suicide attempts with no between-group difference on that primary outcome (Early evidence) [[Shin2026]].
- A meta-analysis of the treatment-as-usual arms of BPD trials (16 studies) found small-to-moderate improvement in BPD symptoms under usual care (g 0.371; 95% CI 0.246–0.495) but no change in self-harm or suicidality (g 0.003), which the authors say questions the idea that usual care is harmful [[Finch2019TAU]].

### gpm · STEPPS, emotion-regulation groups and psychoeducation
- STEPPS (Systems Training for Emotional Predictability and Problem Solving) is a 20-week, manual-based group programme that combines cognitive-behavioural elements and skills training with a "systems" component, added to usual care [[Blum2008STEPPS]].
- Blum 2008 (124 analysed; STEPPS + TAU n = 65 vs TAU n = 59): STEPPS improved ZAN-BPD total and all four domains, impulsivity, mood and global functioning with moderate to large effects; no difference in suicide attempts, self-harm or hospitalisation; dropout was high in both arms [[Blum2008STEPPS]].
- Emotion regulation group therapy (14 weeks, added to ongoing therapy) in 61 women with BPD and recurrent self-harm reduced self-harm, emotion dysregulation, BPD symptoms, depression and stress, with gains kept at 9 months [[Gratz2014ERGT]].
- Psychoeducation soon after diagnosis (50 young women; workshop within a week of being told the diagnosis) gave a greater fall in general impulsivity and stormy relationships than waitlist, but did not improve psychosocial functioning [[Zanarini2008Psychoed]].
- Web-based psychoeducation (80 women): by the 12-month maintenance phase the psychoeducation group showed greater declines in all 5 areas of BPD psychopathology than the control website [[Zanarini2018Web]].

### gpm · Stepped care and early intervention
- Laporte 2018 (Montreal, naturalistic): 479 patients in a 12-week short-term clinic and 145 in extended care (up to 24 months) all improved; those in extended care for 18–24 months improved no more than those staying 6–12 months; the authors call brief treatment a reasonable first step for most (Early evidence) [[Laporte2018Stepped]].
- Grenyer 2018 cluster RCT (642 inpatients with a primary personality disorder, Australia): a cost-neutral whole-of-service stepped-care model cut average bed days per admission from 13.46 to 4.28, made people 1.3 times less likely to re-present to emergency, and saved an estimated USD 2,720 per patient per year [[Grenyer2018Stepped]].
- MOBY trial (139 people aged 15–25 with first-presentation BPD): with a dedicated youth BPD service (HYPE) and case management, adding cognitive analytic therapy did not improve psychosocial functioning at 12 months; all arms improved by 19.3%–23.8% on primary outcomes [[Chanen2022MOBY]].
- In MOBY, the HYPE service model had higher treatment completion than a general youth mental health service (47.8% vs 19.2%) [[Chanen2022MOBY]].

### mbt · What MBT is
- Mentalizing is "the capacity to understand others and oneself in terms of internal mental states", described along four dimensions: automatic–controlled, internal–external focus, self–other, and cognitive–affective [[Luyten2020]].
- In the mentalizing model, people with BPD show reduced capacity to mentalize, which leads to problems with emotion regulation and impulsivity, especially in close relationships (Hypothesis) [[Bateman2010WP]].
- MBT is "a time-limited treatment which structures interventions that promote the further development of mentalizing", and was found effective when delivered by mental health professionals with limited extra training and moderate supervision [[Bateman2010WP]].
- Three low-mentalizing ("pre-mentalizing") modes [[Chung2026]]:
  - Psychic equivalence: treating inner experience as if it were outer reality.
  - Pretend mode: appearing to mentalize while cut off from real inner states and reality.
  - Teleological thinking: focusing on physical, goal-directed actions as the only proof of what someone feels or intends.
- In a Korean sample of 90 people with BPD and 47 controls, psychic equivalence was linked both to having the diagnosis and to symptom severity, and mentalizing difficulties partly mediated the link between early life stress and BPD (Early evidence) [[Chung2026]].

### mbt · MBT trials
- Bateman 1999: 38 patients with BPD randomised to psychoanalytically oriented partial hospitalisation (individual and group psychotherapy, up to 18 months) or standard psychiatric care [[Bateman1999]].
- Bateman 1999: the partial-hospital group improved on all measures (less self-harm and fewer suicide attempts, fewer inpatient days, less depression, better social function), with change beginning at 6 months; controls showed limited change or got worse [[Bateman1999]].
- Bateman 2001 (18 months after treatment ended, 44 patients): partial-hospital patients kept their gains and kept improving on most measures [[Bateman2001]].
- Bateman 2008 (8 years after entry, 5 years after MBT ended, 41 patients): MBT by partial hospitalisation vs treatment as usual [[Bateman2008]]:
  - suicidality: 23% vs 74%
  - still meeting the BPD diagnosis: 13% vs 87%
  - global functioning above 60: 45% vs 10%
  - employed or in education: 3.2 vs 1.2 years
  - three or more medications: 0.02 vs 1.90 years
- Bateman 2008 caveat: "their general social function remains impaired" [[Bateman2008]].
- Bateman 2009: outpatient MBT (18 months) vs SCM in 134 patients; both improved, but MBT gave a steeper decline in suicide attempts and hospitalisation [[Bateman2009]].
- Bateman 2021 (8 years after the start of the 2009 trial; 98 of 134 seen): more patients still met the recovery criteria for the primary outcome after MBT (74% vs 51%), and the MBT group was more often in purposeful activity [[Bateman2021]].
- Jørgensen 2013 (Denmark, 85 patients): 2 years of twice-weekly combined MBT vs fortnightly supportive group therapy; both improved strongly (pre–post effect sizes 0.5–2.1), and only global functioning (GAF) was significantly better with MBT [[Jorgensen2013MBT]].
- Laurenssen 2018 (Netherlands, 95 patients): day-hospital MBT was not superior to specialist treatment as usual at 18 months, but early dropout was lower (9% vs 34%) [[Laurenssen2018]].
- Juul 2023 (166 outpatients): 5 months vs 14 months of MBT; no difference in BPD symptoms at 16 months (MD 0.99; 95% CI −1.06 to 3.03) or other outcomes [[Juul2023]].
- Juul 2025 (24 months): still no difference in BPD symptoms (MD −0.56; 95% CI −2.67 to 1.54); the long-term group had better quality of life, but 40% of that data was missing [[Juul2025]].
- Other researchers published a critique arguing the short- vs long-term MBT trial was flawed in design [[Philips2024]].

### mbt · MBT for adolescents
- MBT-A (80 adolescents who self-harmed, with depression): MBT-A reduced self-harm and depression more than usual treatment over 12 months; the benefit was explained by better mentalizing and less attachment avoidance [[Rossouw2012MBTA]].
- MBT in groups for adolescents (MBT-G; 112 adolescents, 1 year): no difference from usual care on the primary outcome (adjusted difference 0.4 BPFS-C units; 95% CI −6.3 to 7.1); 29% in both groups remitted [[Beck2020MBTG]].
- In MBT-G, 29% attended less than half their sessions, vs 7% of the control group [[Beck2020MBTG]].
- MBT-G follow-up, 2 years after entry: remission was 35% with MBT-G vs 39% with usual care, with no significant differences [[Jorgensen2021MBTG]].

### schema · What schema therapy is
- Young's schema-mode model proposes that people with BPD flip between 4 maladaptive modes [[Arntz2005Modes]]:
  - Detached Protector
  - Punitive Parent
  - Abused/Abandoned Child (the "vulnerable child")
  - Angry/Impulsive Child
- In an experimental test, people with BPD scored highest on the 4 maladaptive modes and lowest on the Healthy Adult mode; a stress film raised the Detached Protector mode more in BPD than in either control group [[Arntz2005Modes]].
- The same 4 modes were found in BPD in a second study; the Healthy Adult mode was low in BPD and high in non-patients [[Lobbestael2005]].
- In 489 participants, mode profiles matched the hypothesised links with personality disorders, but many correlations raised doubts about how specific the model is [[Lobbestael2008]].
- A comprehensive review found support for many parts of Young's schema model but "mixed results and some empirical blanks", and called schema therapy promising and implementable at reasonable cost [[Sempertegui2013]].

### schema · Schema therapy trials
- Giesen-Bloo 2006: 88 patients at 4 community mental health centres received 3 years of schema-focused therapy (SFT) or TFP, twice a week [[GiesenBloo2006]].
- Giesen-Bloo 2006: both therapies helped on all measures; after 3 years more SFT patients had recovered (relative risk 2.18; P = .04) or reliably improved (relative risk 2.33; P = .009), and TFP had a higher dropout risk (P = .01) [[GiesenBloo2006]].
- Cost-effectiveness of the same trial: 52% vs 29% recovered (SFT vs TFP); mean 4-year costs were EUR 37,826 vs EUR 46,795, so SFT was "dominant" for recovery [[vanAsselt2008]].
- Nadort 2009 (62 patients in regular care): after 1.5 years of schema therapy, 42% had recovered from BPD; adding therapist phone availability outside office hours added nothing [[Nadort2009]].
- Arntz 2022: 495 outpatients at 15 sites in 5 countries were randomised to mainly group schema therapy (PGST), combined individual and group schema therapy (IGST), or optimal usual care, over 2 years [[Arntz2022GST]].
- Arntz 2022: both schema formats together beat usual care on BPD severity (Cohen d 0.73; 95% CI 0.29–1.18); IGST beat usual care (d 1.14) and PGST (d 0.84), while PGST alone did not beat usual care (d 0.30; 95% CI −0.29 to 0.89) [[Arntz2022GST]].
- Arntz 2022 retention at 2 years: IGST 0.74, PGST 0.62, usual care 0.64 [[Arntz2022GST]].

### schema · TFP trials
- Clarkin 2007: 90 patients randomised to a year of TFP, DBT or dynamic supportive therapy; all three improved depression, anxiety, global functioning and social adjustment; TFP and DBT both improved suicidality, and only TFP changed irritability and verbal and direct assault [[Clarkin2007]].
- Doering 2010: 104 women, 1 year of TFP vs experienced community psychotherapists; TFP had less dropout (38.5% vs 67.3%), fewer suicide attempts (d 0.8), and better borderline symptoms (d 1.6), psychosocial functioning (d 1.0) and fewer inpatient admissions (d 0.5) [[Doering2010]].
- Doering 2010: self-harm did not change in either group [[Doering2010]].

### compare · Head-to-head trials
- DBT vs GPM: no difference on any outcome at 1 year [[McMain2009]] or 2 years after treatment [[McMain2012]].
- Schema therapy vs TFP: schema therapy did better on recovery and retention over 3 years [[GiesenBloo2006]].
- DBT vs TFP vs supportive therapy: all improved, with different patterns of change [[Clarkin2007]].
- BOOTS 2026: 204 outpatients at 9 Dutch centres randomised to 2 years of DBT or schema therapy (both group plus individual); no significant difference in BPD severity at 3 years (mean difference −1.09 on BPDSI-5; Cohen d 0.15; 95% CI −0.17 to 0.47), with large improvements in both [[Wibbelink2026BOOTS]].
- BOOTS dropout: DBT 34% at 1 year and 52% at 2 years; schema therapy 29% and 46%; not significantly different [[Wibbelink2026BOOTS]].

### compare · Meta-analyses
- Cochrane 2020 (Storebø): 75 RCTs, 4507 participants, mostly women, ages 14.8 to 45.7 years, more than 16 kinds of psychotherapy; DBT was the subject of about one-third of trials, MBT of 7 [[Storebo2020]].
- Cochrane 2020, psychotherapy vs usual care [[Storebo2020]]:
  - BPD severity: SMD −0.52 (95% CI −0.70 to −0.33; 22 trials, 1244 participants; moderate certainty), about −3.6 points on the 0–36 ZAN-BPD, above the 3.0-point minimal clinically relevant difference.
  - Self-harm: SMD −0.32 (95% CI −0.49 to −0.14; 13 trials; low certainty).
  - Suicide-related outcomes: SMD −0.34 (95% CI −0.57 to −0.11; 13 trials; low certainty).
  - Psychosocial functioning: SMD −0.45 (95% CI −0.68 to −0.22; 22 trials; low certainty), about −2.8 GAF points.
- Cochrane 2020: "only the outcome of BPD severity reached the MIREDIF-defined cut-off for a clinically meaningful improvement", and subgroup analysis found no clear difference between types of therapy vs usual care [[Storebo2020]].
- Cochrane 2020, single therapies vs usual care (all low-certainty) [[Storebo2020]]:
  - DBT: BPD severity SMD −0.60 (3 trials), self-harm SMD −0.28 (7 trials), psychosocial functioning SMD −0.36 (6 trials).
  - MBT: self-harm RR 0.62 (95% CI 0.49–0.80; 3 trials) and suicidality RR 0.10 (95% CI 0.04–0.30; 3 trials).
- Cochrane 2020: BPD-specific psychotherapy did not reduce attrition compared with usual care, and adverse effects were unclear because of too few data [[Storebo2020]].
- The earlier Cochrane review (2012; 28 studies, 1804 participants) concluded that "none of the treatments has a very robust evidence base" [[Stoffers2012]].
- Focused 2022 meta-analysis (31 RCTs, 1870 adults) [[StoffersWinterling2022BJP]]:
  - Standalone DBT: self-harm SMD −0.54, psychosocial functioning SMD −0.51 (low certainty).
  - Standalone MBT: self-harm RR 0.51, suicide-related outcomes RR 0.10 (low certainty).
  - Add-on DBT skills training: BPD severity SMD −0.66 (moderate certainty).
  - Add-on STEPPS: BPD severity SMD −0.48.
- Cristea 2017 (33 RCTs, 2256 participants): psychotherapies beat controls on BPD-relevant outcomes with small effects (stand-alone g 0.32; add-on g 0.40); only DBT (g 0.34) and psychodynamic approaches (g 0.41) beat controls on their own [[Cristea2017]].
- Cristea 2017 warns that effects are "inflated by risk of bias and publication bias, and particularly unstable at follow-up" [[Cristea2017]].
- Oud 2018 (20 RCTs, 1375 participants): specialised therapies (DBT, MBT, TFP, schema therapy) vs usual care or community experts gave a medium effect on BPD severity (SMD −0.59; 95% CI −0.90 to −0.28; moderate quality); DBT vs usual care reduced self-injury (SMD −0.40) [[Oud2018]].
- Rameckers 2021 (87 studies, 5881 people, all designs): schema therapy, MBT and reduced DBT had higher-than-average effect sizes; general severity and affective instability improved most, while dissociation, anger, impulsivity and suicidality/self-injury improved least [[Rameckers2021]].
- Individual-participant meta-analysis (19 trials, 1104 participants): psychotherapy reduced BPD severity vs usual care or clinical management (β −0.25; 95% CI −0.43 to −0.07); co-occurring depression and sexual trauma predicted less benefit, while co-occurring anxiety or alcohol use disorder predicted more [[PereiraRibeiro2026]].
- A 2024 review puts the effect of psychotherapy vs usual care on core BPD severity at 0.50–0.65, notes that "almost half of the patients do not respond sufficiently", and finds no approach superior to the others [[Leichsenring2024]].
- The Lancet seminar calls psychotherapy the main treatment for BPD, with medication only for co-occurring conditions or in a crisis when psychosocial care is not enough [[Bohus2021]].
- The Nature Reviews primer notes that most patients gradually reach symptom remission, and evidence-based psychosocial treatment can speed it up, though many keep persistent social disability [[Gunderson2018]].

### compare · Dropout
- Pooled dropout from BPD psychotherapy studies was 22.3% across all studies and 28.2% in outpatient RCTs, and up to 29.9% after adjusting for publication bias; most dropout happened in the first half of treatment [[Iliakis2021]].
- Reasons for dropout included dissatisfaction with treatment, being discharged by the service ("expulsion"), and low motivation [[Iliakis2021]].
- A survival meta-analysis (111 studies, 159 arms, N = 9100): dropout was highest in the first quarter of treatment; schema therapy had the lowest dropout overall, MBT in the first two quarters, and community treatment by experts the highest [[Arntz2023Dropout]].
- In the same analysis, individual therapy had the lowest dropout, group therapy the highest, and combined formats were in between [[Arntz2023Dropout]].

### compare · Costs, access and new formats
- Dutch modelling found specialised outpatient psychotherapy for BPD likely cost-effective, with scale-up costing about EUR 2.367 million per 1,000 extra patients [[Wetzelaer2017]].
- Fewer than 25% of patients with BPD receive guideline-adherent psychotherapy, according to the authors of a digital-therapeutic trial [[Assmann2025]].
- That trial (580 adults, Germany) tested an unguided schema-therapy-based app plus usual care vs usual care: BSL-23 improved more at 3 months (d 0.24; 95% CI 0.07–0.42), with fewer suicide attempts (7 vs 21; incidence rate ratio 0.34) (Early evidence) [[Assmann2025]].
- A review describes a move toward generalist care models and "pared down" forms of intensive therapies, for example case management plus DBT skills groups [[ChoiKain2017]].

### compare · Guideline wording
- APA 2024, the single psychotherapy statement: "APA recommends (1B) that a patient with borderline personality disorder be treated with a structured approach to psychotherapy that has support in the literature and targets the core features of the disorder." [[APA2024Guideline]]
- APA 2024 also recommends (1C) "a collaborative discussion about their diagnosis and treatment, which includes psychoeducation related to the disorder" [[APA2024Guideline]].
- APA 2024 suggests (2C) that "any psychotropic medication treatment of borderline personality disorder be time-limited, aimed at addressing a specific measurable target symptom, and adjunctive to psychotherapy" [[APA2024Guideline]].
- The APA's news release states the guideline has eight statements in all; its chair said several structured psychotherapies were effective and "No therapy emerged as a 'gold standard'" [[APA2024Guideline]].
- NICE CG78 1.3.4.4: "Do not use brief psychological interventions (of less than 3 months' duration) specifically for borderline personality disorder or for the individual symptoms of the disorder, outside a service that has the characteristics outlined in recommendation 1.3.4.3." [[NICE2009]]
- NICE CG78 1.3.4.3 service characteristics: "an explicit and integrated theoretical approach used by both the treatment team and the therapist, which is shared with the service user", structured care and therapist supervision; "twice-weekly sessions may be considered" [[NICE2009]].
- NICE CG78 1.3.4.5: "For women with borderline personality disorder for whom reducing recurrent self-harm is a priority, consider a comprehensive dialectical behaviour therapy programme." [[NICE2009]]
- NICE CG78 1.3.5.1: "Drug treatment should not be used specifically for borderline personality disorder or for the individual symptoms or behaviour associated with the disorder" [[NICE2009]].
- NICE CG78 1.3.4.7 asks services to monitor the effect of treatment on a broad range of outcomes, including personal functioning, drug and alcohol use, self-harm, depression and BPD symptoms [[NICE2009]].

### Exam hooks
- DBT's four skills modules: mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, interpersonal effectiveness [[Chapman2006DBT]].
- Standard DBT has four parts: individual therapy, skills group, between-session phone coaching, and a therapist consultation team [[LinehanWilks2015]].
- DBT target priority: life-threatening behaviour, then therapy-interfering behaviour, then quality-of-life-interfering behaviour [[Chapman2006DBT]].
- Linehan 2006: DBT halved the likelihood of a suicide attempt vs expert community therapy (HR 2.66) [[Linehan2006]].
- Linehan 2015 component trial: the skills-training component drives the extra drop in non-suicidal self-injury [[Linehan2015]].
- McMain 2009: DBT and general psychiatric management gave equal outcomes over 1 year [[McMain2009]].
- McMain 2022: 6 months of DBT was non-inferior to 12 months for self-harm [[McMain2022]].
- McCauley 2018: DBT reduced suicide attempts in high-risk adolescents vs supportive therapy (OR 0.30) [[McCauley2018]].
- MBT's three pre-mentalizing modes: psychic equivalence, pretend mode, teleological mode [[Chung2026]].
- Bateman 2008: 8 years after entry, 13% vs 87% still met BPD criteria after MBT partial hospitalisation vs usual care [[Bateman2008]].
- Schema modes in BPD: detached protector, punitive parent, abandoned/abused (vulnerable) child, angry/impulsive child; healthy adult is weak [[Arntz2005Modes]].
- Giesen-Bloo 2006: schema therapy beat TFP on recovery over 3 years (RR 2.18) and had less dropout [[GiesenBloo2006]].
- Arntz 2022: combined individual and group schema therapy beat both usual care and mainly-group schema therapy [[Arntz2022GST]].
- Doering 2010: TFP beat experienced community therapists on BPD symptoms (d 1.6) and dropout (38.5% vs 67.3%) [[Doering2010]].
- Cochrane 2020: only BPD severity reached the clinically meaningful threshold (SMD −0.52; moderate certainty) [[Storebo2020]].
- No psychotherapy has been shown superior to another; APA 2024 recommends (1B) a structured, evidence-supported psychotherapy [[APA2024Guideline,Leichsenring2024]].
- NICE CG78: do not use brief (<3 months) BPD-specific psychological interventions outside a structured service [[NICE2009]].
- STEPPS is a 20-week add-on group programme with a "systems" component that involves the person's support network [[Blum2008STEPPS]].
- Dropout from BPD psychotherapy is about 1 in 4 (22.3–28.2%) and peaks early in treatment [[Iliakis2021,Arntz2023Dropout]].

### In plain words
- BPD is treatable. Talking therapies built for BPD help most people feel and function better, and many people recover over time [[Storebo2020,Gunderson2018]].
- There is no single "best" therapy. DBT, MBT, schema therapy and TFP have all worked in trials, and well-organised general care also helps [[Leichsenring2024,McMain2009]].
- DBT teaches practical skills in four areas: staying present, getting through a crisis, handling strong feelings, and asking for what you need in relationships [[Chapman2006DBT]].
- In DBT you fill in a weekly diary card, and you can phone your therapist for skills coaching between sessions [[Chapman2006DBT]].
- MBT helps you notice what is going on in your own mind and in other people's minds, especially when emotions run high [[Bateman2010WP,Luyten2020]].
- Schema therapy helps you understand the "modes" you switch into, such as a harsh inner critic or a shut-down protector, and build a strong "healthy adult" side [[Arntz2005Modes]].
- Shorter therapy can work as well as longer therapy for some people: 6 months of DBT did as well as 12 months on self-harm [[McMain2022]].
- Learning about BPD itself, soon after diagnosis, can help [[Zanarini2008Psychoed]].
- Many people stop therapy early, often in the first months. If therapy feels hard, saying so to your therapist is part of the work [[Iliakis2021,Arntz2023Dropout]].
- Medicines are not the main treatment for BPD. Guidelines put structured psychotherapy first [[APA2024Guideline,NICE2009]].
- These figures are averages from groups of people in studies. They cannot predict how any one person will do.
- If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

## Open questions and controversies
- Is any one therapy better? Specialised therapies beat usual care on BPD severity [[Oud2018,Storebo2020]], but head-to-head trials mostly find no difference: DBT vs GPM [[McMain2009]], DBT vs schema therapy [[Wibbelink2026BOOTS]]. Schema therapy did beat TFP [[GiesenBloo2006]]. Status: most guidelines and reviews say no therapy is superior [[APA2024Guideline,Leichsenring2024]].
- How big are the effects really? Cochrane rates the BPD-severity effect moderate-certainty and clinically relevant, but other outcomes low-certainty [[Storebo2020]]; Cristea finds effects small and inflated by bias [[Cristea2017]]; about half of patients do not respond enough [[Leichsenring2024]]. Status: unresolved; larger, lower-bias trials needed.
- How long should therapy be? 6 months of DBT matched 12 [[McMain2022]], 5 months of MBT matched 14 [[Juul2023,Juul2025]], and brief stepped care looks reasonable for many [[Laporte2018Stepped]]. Critics question the MBT trial design [[Philips2024]], and NICE still warns against BPD-specific interventions under 3 months outside a structured service [[NICE2009]]. Status: open; matching dose to need is the working hypothesis.
- Generalist vs specialist care: GPM matched DBT [[McMain2009]] and its authors propose it as first-line [[Gunderson2018GPM]]; brief 4-month GPM did not beat usual care at 4 months [[Kramer2024GPM]] but did at 1-year follow-up [[BlancoMachinea2026]]. Status: Early evidence for brief GPM.
- Adolescents: DBT-A reduces self-harm in two trials [[Mehlum2014,McCauley2018]], but MBT in groups did not beat usual care [[Beck2020MBTG]], and adding psychotherapy to a youth-focused service added nothing in MOBY [[Chanen2022MOBY]]. Status: service structure may matter as much as the specific therapy.
- Functional recovery lags symptom remission: even after successful therapy many people remain out of work or study [[McMain2012,Bateman2008]]. Status: rehabilitation add-ons are untested.
- Who benefits from which therapy? Individual-participant data suggest depression and sexual trauma predict less benefit, and anxiety and alcohol use disorder more [[PereiraRibeiro2026]]; the BOOTS project aims to personalise selection [[Wibbelink2026BOOTS]]. Status: Early evidence.
- Digital therapy: an unguided schema-based app showed a small benefit and fewer suicide attempts in one trial [[Assmann2025]]. Status: Early evidence; needs replication.

## Provenance
- Validation: every source except NICE2009 was matched against its Europe PMC core record (authors, title, journal, year, volume, issue, pages, PMID, PMCID, DOI), and the Sources lines were generated directly from those records. Twenty-two records were also seen in PubMed esummary or efetch output and are labelled (validated: PubMed). The other 53 are labelled (validated: Europe PMC). APA2024Guideline was also checked on Crossref.
- PubMed E-utilities returned "API rate limit exceeded" for many calls because of parallel use, so Europe PMC was the main route. Abstracts truncated at "<" by Europe PMC were re-read from PubMed efetch: Arntz 2022, Linehan 2015, Linehan 2006, Giesen-Bloo 2006.
- Full texts read: Chapman 2006 (open access, PMC page) for DBT structure, and Chung 2026 (PMC efetch XML) for the mentalizing modes. pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov later showed a browser check; this was not bypassed. Bateman & Fonagy 2010 is PDF-only and was used for its abstract only.
- Guidelines:
  - APA 2024: the journal full text returned 403, so the statements were quoted from the APA news release, which reproduces all eight verbatim.
  - NICE CG78: quoted from nice.org.uk.
  - NHMRC 2012 (Australia): web page and PDF timed out, so the guideline is not cited.
- Not used:
  - "45.5% vs 23.8%" recovery for Giesen-Bloo 2006, which is not in the abstract (the full text is paywalled).
  - Linehan 1991 sample sizes and event counts, which are not in the abstract.
  - Bateman 2009 numeric results, which are not in the abstract.
  - Grenyer 2013 MJA and Kendall 2009 BMJ, which have no abstracts.
- Philips 2024 is a letter with no abstract; it is cited only for its stated position, taken from its title.
- No fact is marked (unverified) in Findings. The only unverified source line is NICE2009, kept because it is the essential UK guideline and its text was read on the official site.
