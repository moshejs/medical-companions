# F · Medication evidence, crisis care & suicide and self-harm safety

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: meds, crisis.

This pass covers what medicines can and cannot do for borderline personality disorder (BPD), the guideline positions on prescribing, the main randomised trials and reviews, how often medicines are prescribed in practice, their harms, and real-world data on medicines and suicidal behaviour. The crisis half covers how common suicide, suicide attempts and non-suicidal self-injury (NSSI) are in people with BPD, chronic versus acute risk, why risk scales should not be used to predict suicide, safety planning, emergency department (ED) care, crisis plans, brief admission and safe-messaging rules for this content. It reports what sources say; nothing here is advice to a reader. Doses and guideline statements are quoted as published, not as instructions. All suicide and mortality figures are population statistics from specific cohorts.

Safe-messaging note for builders: this file deliberately gives no information about methods or means of self-harm or suicide. Every page that uses it should carry the crisis line text: "If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123)."

## Findings

### meds · Regulatory status: no medicine is approved for BPD
- No US prescription drug label lists borderline personality disorder in its indications: an openFDA search of the indications section for "borderline personality" returned no matches on 2026-10-25 [[FDA-LabelSearch2026]].
- A 2021 systematic review states that "no medications have been approved by regulatory agencies for the treatment of BPD", yet "up to 96%" of people with BPD receive at least one psychotropic medicine [[Gartlehner2021]].
- The 2024 brexpiprazole trial report opens: "No drugs are currently approved for the treatment of borderline personality disorder (BPD)" [[Rothman2024Brexpiprazole]].
- The 2022 Cochrane review describes all medication use for BPD as "off-label", despite high prescription rates and frequent use of several drugs at once for long periods [[StoffersWinterling2022]].
- Medicines that are used (for example quetiapine, olanzapine, lamotrigine, valproate, clozapine) carry US indications for other conditions such as schizophrenia, bipolar disorder or epilepsy, not BPD [[FDA-Seroquel,FDA-Depakote,FDA-Lamictal,FDA-Clozaril]].
- Clozapine's US label includes "reducing the risk of recurrent suicidal behavior" only in schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder, not in BPD [[FDA-Clozaril]].

### meds · Guideline positions on medicines
- NICE CG78 (2009): "Drug treatment should not be used specifically for borderline personality disorder or for the individual symptoms or behaviour associated with the disorder (for example, repeated self-harm, marked emotional instability, risk-taking behaviour and transient psychotic symptoms)" (recommendation 1.3.5.1) [[NICE2009]].
- NICE CG78: "Antipsychotic drugs should not be used for the medium- and long-term treatment of borderline personality disorder" (1.3.5.2) [[NICE2009]].
- NICE CG78: drug treatment "may be considered in the overall treatment of comorbid conditions" (1.3.5.3) [[NICE2009]].
- NICE CG78: "Short-term use of sedative medication may be considered cautiously as part of the overall treatment plan for people with borderline personality disorder in a crisis", with duration agreed and "no longer than 1 week" (1.3.5.4) [[NICE2009]].
- NICE CG78 asks services to review people with BPD and no comorbid illness who are taking medicines, "with the aim of reducing and stopping unnecessary drug treatment" (1.3.5.6) [[NICE2009]].
- NICE CG78 asks that a person considering any drug be given written information on its evidence for BPD and for any comorbid condition, and on potential harm (1.3.5.5) [[NICE2009]].
- APA 2024 Statement 6: "APA recommends (1C) that a patient with borderline personality disorder have a review of co-occurring disorders, prior psychotherapies, other non-pharmacological treatments, past medication trials, and current medications before initiating any new medication" [[APA2024Guideline]].
- APA 2024 Statement 7: "APA suggests (2C) that any psychotropic medication treatment of borderline personality disorder be time-limited, aimed at addressing a specific measurable target symptom, and adjunctive to psychotherapy" [[APA2024Guideline]].
- APA 2024 Statement 8: "APA recommends (1C) that a patient with borderline personality disorder have a review and reconciliation of their medications at least every 6 months to assess the effectiveness of treatment and identify medications that warrant tapering or discontinuation" [[APA2024Guideline]].
- APA 2024 Statement 5 (context for medicines): "APA recommends (1B)" a structured approach to psychotherapy that has support in the literature and targets the core features of the disorder; a "1" is a recommendation and a "2" a suggestion, and C means low-strength evidence [[APA2024Guideline]].
- APA 2024 implementation text: medicines "would not be expected to affect core features of BPD", and "Frequent dose escalation or medication changes in response to crises or transient mood states are also problematic and rarely effective" [[APA2024Guideline]].
- APA 2024 implementation text: benzodiazepines are "not generally recommended because of potential for greater impulsivity or disinhibition as well as potential for misuse or development of dependence" [[APA2024Guideline]].
- APA 2024 implementation text: a low dose of a second-generation antipsychotic "may be used" for psychosis, high impulsivity or agitation, and clozapine "may be considered" for extremely ill hospitalized patients, based on case reports, naturalistic data and a small trial [[APA2024Guideline]].
- The Lancet seminar: "Psychotherapy is the main treatment for BPD; drug treatment is only indicated for comorbid conditions that require medication, or during a crisis if psychosocial interventions are insufficient" [[Bohus2021]].
- The Nature Reviews primer warns that failure to diagnose BPD correctly "leads to misleading pharmacological interventions that rarely succeed" [[Gunderson2018]].
- NICE's self-harm guideline NG225 (2022): "Do not offer drug treatment as a specific intervention to reduce self-harm" (1.11.10) [[NICE2022NG225]].

### meds · What the systematic reviews found
- Cochrane 2022 included 46 randomised trials (2769 participants; 45 trials and 2752 participants in the meta-analyses), 18 more trials than the 2010 version, testing 29 different medicines [[StoffersWinterling2022]].
- Cochrane 2022: compared with placebo, "no difference in effects were observed on any of the primary outcomes at the end of treatment for any medication" (BPD severity, self-harm, suicide-related outcomes, psychosocial functioning) [[StoffersWinterling2022]].
- Cochrane 2022, BPD symptom severity versus placebo (very low certainty): antipsychotics SMD -0.18 (95% CI -0.45 to 0.08; 8 trials, 951 people); antidepressants SMD -0.27 (-0.65 to 1.18; 2 trials, 87); mood stabilisers SMD -0.07 (-0.43 to 0.57; 4 trials, 265) [[StoffersWinterling2022]].
- Cochrane 2022, self-harm versus placebo (very low certainty): antipsychotics RR 0.66 (0.15 to 2.84; 2 trials, 76 people); mood stabilisers RR 1.08 (0.79 to 1.48; 1 trial, 276 people) [[StoffersWinterling2022]].
- Cochrane 2022, suicide-related outcomes versus placebo (very low certainty): antipsychotics SMD 0.05 (-0.18 to 0.29; 7 trials, 854 people) [[StoffersWinterling2022]].
- Cochrane 2022, psychosocial functioning (very low certainty): antipsychotics SMD -0.16 (-0.33 to 0.00; 7 trials, 904 people) [[StoffersWinterling2022]].
- Cochrane 2022, the one signal: low-certainty evidence that antipsychotics may slightly reduce interpersonal problems (SMD -0.21, -0.34 to -0.08; 8 trials, 907 people) and mood stabilisers may reduce them (SMD -0.58, -1.14 to -0.02; 4 trials, 300 people) [[StoffersWinterling2022]].
- Cochrane 2022: 17 of 46 trials were fully or partly industry-funded; reporting of adverse events was poor and mostly non-standardised [[StoffersWinterling2022]].
- Cochrane 2022 conclusion: the review "supports the continued understanding that no pharmacological therapy seems effective in specifically treating BPD pathology" [[StoffersWinterling2022]].
- A 2025 secondary analysis of the Cochrane data (22 trials) found only low or very low certainty evidence on co-occurring symptoms and concluded the findings "do not support the use of pharmacological interventions in people with BPD to target co-occurring psychopathology" [[PereiraRibeiro2025]].
- Gartlehner 2021 (21 RCTs, 1768 participants; studies existed for only 9 of 87 medicines used in practice): second-generation antipsychotics, anticonvulsants and antidepressants "were not able to consistently reduce the severity of BPD" [[Gartlehner2021]].
- Gartlehner 2021: low-certainty evidence that anticonvulsants may improve anger, aggression and affective lability, "mostly limited to single studies"; second-generation antipsychotics improved general psychiatric symptoms more than BPD-specific ones [[Gartlehner2021]].
- The earlier Cochrane review (2010; 27 trials) found most benefit for topiramate, lamotrigine, valproate semisodium, aripiprazole and olanzapine, but "the robustness of findings is low, since they are based mostly on single, small studies", and no effect on overall BPD severity [[Lieb2010Cochrane]].
- Ingenhoven 2010 (21 placebo-controlled trials to 2007) reported large pooled effects for mood stabilisers on impulsive-behavioural dyscontrol (SMD 1.51) and anger (SMD 1.33); these early small trials were later not confirmed by the larger LABILE trial (Early evidence, superseded) [[Ingenhoven2010,Crawford2018LABILE]].
- Contrast with psychotherapy: in Cochrane 2020 (75 RCTs, 4507 participants), BPD-tailored psychotherapy reduced BPD severity versus treatment as usual (SMD -0.52, -0.70 to -0.33; 22 trials; moderate-quality evidence) (Established) [[Storebo2020]].

### meds · Key trials: mood stabilisers
- LABILE (UK, 276 adults, lamotrigine up to 400 mg/day vs placebo, 52 weeks): mean ZAN-BPD 11.3 (SD 6.6) on lamotrigine vs 11.5 (SD 7.7) on placebo, adjusted difference 0.1 (95% CI -1.8 to 2.0) [[Crawford2018LABILE]].
- LABILE found no difference in any secondary outcome, including self-harm and depression, and similar costs; the authors concluded lamotrigine "is not a clinically effective or cost-effective use of resources" [[Crawford2018LABILE]].
- LABILE excluded people with bipolar disorder or psychosis and "women at risk of pregnancy"; at 52 weeks only 36% (lamotrigine) and 42% (placebo) were still taking study medicine [[Crawford2018LABILE]].
- An earlier small lamotrigine trial (24 women, 8 weeks, randomised 2:1) reported large reductions in self-rated anger, a typical early small-trial result [[Tritt2005Lamotrigine]].
- Divalproex pilot (16 outpatients, 12 drug vs 4 placebo, 10 weeks): a "high dropout rate precluded finding significant differences" in intention-to-treat analyses [[Hollander2001Divalproex]].
- Divalproex trial (52 outpatients, 12 weeks): divalproex was superior to placebo for impulsive aggression, especially in people with higher baseline impulsivity and aggression [[Hollander2005Divalproex]].
- Topiramate trial (56 women, up to 200 mg/day, 10 weeks) reported improvements on several symptom scales and weight loss [[Loew2006Topiramate]].

### meds · Key trials: antipsychotics
- Quetiapine XR trial (95 adults, 8 weeks): 150 mg/day improved ZAN-BPD scores versus placebo; time to response hazard ratio 2.54 (150 mg) and 2.37 (300 mg) [[Black2014Quetiapine]].
- Quetiapine XR: among completers, responders were 82% (150 mg), 74% (300 mg) and 48% (placebo); completion was 67%, 58% and 79%, and people with sedation were more likely to drop out [[Black2014Quetiapine]].
- Olanzapine dose trial (451 outpatients, 12 weeks, 9 countries): only 5-10 mg/day beat placebo on ZAN-BPD change (-8.5 vs -6.8; effect size 0.29, 95% CI 0.06-0.52), which the authors called "a clinically modest advantage" [[Zanarini2011Olanzapine]].
- Olanzapine dose trial: response rates 73.6% (5-10 mg), 60.1% (2.5 mg) and 57.8% (placebo) [[Zanarini2011Olanzapine]].
- Olanzapine variable-dose trial (314 people, 2.5-20 mg/day, 12 weeks): no difference from placebo on ZAN-BPD change (-6.56 vs -6.25, P=0.661); response 64.7% vs 53.5% (P=0.062) [[Schulz2008Olanzapine]].
- The first olanzapine pilot (28 women, 6 months, randomised 2:1) reported faster improvement on several self-rated symptoms, but not depression [[Zanarini2001Olanzapine]].
- Aripiprazole 15 mg/day (52 people, 8 weeks) improved several self-rated scales in one trial; self-injury occurred in both groups [[Nickel2006Aripiprazole]].
- Ziprasidone (60 people, 12 weeks, mean dose 84.1 mg/day) showed no difference from placebo on the primary outcome or on depressive, anxiety, psychotic or impulsive symptoms [[Pascual2008Ziprasidone]].
- Historic trials: haloperidol beat amitriptyline and placebo in a 1986 inpatient trial (61 completers), but a 1993 trial (108 inpatients) could not replicate haloperidol's benefit and found phenelzine better on anger and hostility [[Soloff1986Haloperidol,Soloff1993Phenelzine]].
- Clozapine, CALMED (UK, aimed for 222 inpatients with severe BPD): closed early with only 29 participants because of poor recruitment and COVID-19; adjusted ZAN-BPD difference at 6 months -3.86 (95% CI -10.04 to 2.32), so the primary question could not be answered [[Crawford2022CALMED]].
- CALMED: 14 serious adverse events (6 clozapine, 8 placebo); 73% on clozapine and 43% on placebo were still taking trial medicine at 6 months [[Crawford2022CALMED]].
- Danish mirror-image study (1107 people with BPD who started clozapine): psychiatric bed-days fell from 190.08 to 65.95 over two years, and people with intentional self-harm or overdose fell from 189 to 114 after starting; observational, no control group (Early evidence) [[Rohde2018Clozapine]].
- Finnish-Swedish register study (2025): clozapine was linked to fewer psychiatric hospitalisations than other oral antipsychotics in every disorder studied except BPD, where only 0.3% (n=36) used clozapine [[Luykx2025Clozapine]].

### meds · Key trials: antidepressants and others
- Fluoxetine (22 volunteers with mild-moderate BPD, 13 weeks) reduced anger; the authors noted the small sample and high placebo response [[Salzman1995]].
- Fluvoxamine (38 women, 6 weeks plus crossover) reduced rapid mood shifts but not impulsivity or aggression [[Rinne2002]].
- In the Cochrane 2022 review, antidepressants had too few trials for firm conclusions; for self-harm there was a single 20-person trial [[StoffersWinterling2022]].
- Omega-3 (ethyl-EPA 1 g/day vs placebo, 30 women, 8 weeks): superior to placebo for aggression and depressive symptoms in this pilot (Early evidence) [[Zanarini2003Omega3]].
- Naltrexone for dissociation (two small crossover trials, total n=29): dissociation was numerically lower on naltrexone, but the effect did not reach statistical significance [[Schmahl2012]].

### meds · Newer agents and the research pipeline
- Brexpiprazole single-site trial (80 adults, 12 weeks, 1 mg then 2 mg/day): significant treatment-by-time interaction on ZAN-BPD (P=0.0031) only because of week 12; secondary measures did not differ; authors urged caution given small sample, high drop-out and strong placebo response [[Grant2022Brexpiprazole]].
- Brexpiprazole multicentre phase 2 (324 randomised, 2-3 mg/day, 11 weeks; NCT04100096): primary endpoint not met (difference -1.02, 95% CL -2.75 to 0.70; p=0.24); akathisia 14.0% vs 1.2% on placebo [[Rothman2024Brexpiprazole,NCT04100096]].
- BI 1358894, a TRPC4/5 channel inhibitor (phase 2, 390 randomised, 12 weeks; NCT04566601): no difference from placebo on any primary or secondary endpoint; serious adverse events 10.3% vs 8.6%, and serious suicidal ideation events 4.2% vs 6.3% [[Dwyer2025BI1358894,NCT04566601]].
- Registered BPD drug studies listed as recruiting or not yet recruiting in October 2026 include an NMDA receptor antagonist (NCT02097706), intravenous ketamine (NCT07099534), MDMA for social cognition (NCT06683014, 10 participants), intranasal oxytocin plus self-compassion training (NCT07551882) and methylphenidate (NCT06759298); all are small or early-phase (Hypothesis) [[NCT02097706,NCT07099534,NCT06683014,NCT07551882,NCT06759298]].

### meds · How often medicines are prescribed
- UK audit (2012, 2600 people with a personality disorder): 68% had emotionally unstable (borderline) personality disorder, and 92% of these were prescribed psychotropic medicine, mostly antidepressants or antipsychotics [[Paton2015]].
- In that audit, people with BPD and no comorbid diagnosis were less likely to have had a medication review in the past year, and treatment "may be continued long term by default" [[Paton2015]].
- European inpatient data (2195 inpatients with BPD, 2001-2011): 90% received at least one psychotropic drug, 80% two or more and 54% three or more (mean 2.8) [[Bridler2015]].
- In the same data, 70% took antipsychotics and/or antidepressants, 33% anticonvulsants, 30% benzodiazepines and 4% lithium; quetiapine was the single most used drug (22%) and rising [[Bridler2015]].
- An Austrian inpatient sample (110 people) found the same pattern of frequent polypharmacy, mostly quetiapine, lamotrigine and sertraline, with no difference between people with and without comorbid diagnoses [[Riffer2019]].
- McLean Study of Adult Development (16 years): a significantly higher share of people with BPD than comparison subjects took antidepressants, anxiolytics, antipsychotics and mood stabilisers, and use of antipsychotics and mood stabilisers stayed stable over time [[Zanarini2015Meds]].
- In the same cohort, "as needed" (PRN) psychotropic use was about 3 times higher in BPD than in other personality disorders at baseline, declined by about one third over 14 years, and was about twice as common in people who never recovered [[Martinho2014PRN]].

### meds · Harms and safety
- Weight gain, olanzapine 12-week trial: +3.17 kg (5-10 mg), +2.09 kg (2.5 mg) and +0.02 kg (placebo) (P<.001); somnolence, fatigue and increased appetite were more common on olanzapine [[Zanarini2011Olanzapine]].
- Weight gain, olanzapine variable-dose trial: +2.86 kg vs -0.35 kg on placebo (P<0.001), with more raised prolactin [[Schulz2008Olanzapine]].
- Quetiapine adverse events were sedation, appetite change and dry mouth, more frequent at 300 mg than 150 mg [[Black2014Quetiapine]].
- APA 2024 lists harms to discuss before prescribing: metabolic syndrome, weight gain, movement side effects or tardive dyskinesia with antipsychotics; neural tube defects with divalproex early in pregnancy; polycystic ovary disease with divalproex; Stevens-Johnson syndrome with lamotrigine; cognitive effects with topiramate [[APA2024Guideline]].
- APA 2024: medication decisions "should also consider potential risks of toxicity in overdose or potential for misuse, particularly in individuals with a co-occurring SUD" [[APA2024Guideline]].
- NICE NG225: when prescribing for someone who has self-harmed or may self-harm, consider the toxicity of medicines in overdose (examples given: opiate-containing painkillers and tricyclic antidepressants), alcohol and drug use, and access to other medicines [[NICE2022NG225]].
- NICE NG225: use shared decision making to discuss limiting the quantity supplied (for example weekly prescriptions) and ask people to return unwanted medicines [[NICE2022NG225]].
- Valproate boxed warning (US label): valproate "can cause major congenital malformations, particularly neural tube defects", and "decreased IQ scores and neurodevelopmental disorders following in utero exposure" [[FDA-Depakote]].
- Valproate label: it "should not be administered to a woman of childbearing potential unless other medications have failed to provide adequate symptom control or are otherwise unacceptable", with effective contraception if used [[FDA-Depakote]].
- EURAP registry (9840 pregnancies on monotherapy, 1999-2022): major congenital malformations in 9.9% with valproate vs 3.1% with lamotrigine and 2.5% with levetiracetam; valproate risk rose with dose [[Battino2024EURAP]].
- NEAD study: mean IQ at age 6 was 97 after fetal valproate exposure vs 105 (carbamazepine), 108 (lamotrigine) and 108 (phenytoin) [[Meador2013NEAD]].
- Lamotrigine boxed warning: serious rash including Stevens-Johnson syndrome in about 0.08% to 0.3% of adults; risk rises when combined with valproate [[FDA-Lamictal]].
- Lithium boxed warning: "Lithium toxicity is closely related to serum lithium levels, and can occur at doses close to therapeutic levels"; APA 2024 notes its narrow therapeutic index and toxicity in overdose for people with BPD and bipolar disorder [[FDA-Lithobid,APA2024Guideline]].
- Antidepressant boxed warning: antidepressants increased suicidal thoughts and behaviour in children, adolescents and young adults in short-term studies; monitor all ages when starting [[FDA-Prozac]].
- Clozapine's boxed warning covers severe neutropenia, orthostatic hypotension, bradycardia and syncope, seizure, and myocarditis; "because of the risks of severe neutropenia and of seizure", the label limits its schizophrenia use to people who did not respond to standard antipsychotics [[FDA-Clozaril]].

### meds · Medicines and suicidal behaviour in real-world data
- Swedish national cohort (22 601 people with BPD, mean follow-up 6.9 years; 8513 hospitalisations for attempted suicide and 316 suicides), each person compared with themselves on and off medicine (Early evidence: observational) [[Lieslehto2023Suicide]].
- In that cohort, hazard ratios for attempted or completed suicide on vs off treatment were: benzodiazepines 1.61 (1.45-1.78), antidepressants 1.38 (1.25-1.53), antipsychotics 1.18 (1.07-1.30), mood stabilisers 0.97 (0.87-1.08) and ADHD medicines 0.83 (0.73-0.95) [[Lieslehto2023Suicide]].
- The authors concluded that benzodiazepines "should be used with care" in BPD; the design cannot fully exclude that people start medicines when they are already worse (they ran sensitivity analyses for this) [[Lieslehto2023Suicide]].
- A sister analysis (17 532 people with BPD) linked benzodiazepines (HR 1.38), antipsychotics (1.19) and antidepressants (1.18) to more psychiatric rehospitalisation, and ADHD medicines to less (HR 0.88); clozapine alone was linked to less rehospitalisation (HR 0.54, 0.32-0.91) [[Lieslehto2023Outcomes]].
- Cochrane review of medicines after self-harm (7 trials, 574 adults, not limited to BPD): no clear benefit of newer antidepressants (OR 0.59, 0.29-1.19) or mood stabilisers (OR 0.99) on repeat self-harm; one 30-person antipsychotic trial suggested benefit, all low or very low certainty [[Witt2021]].

### crisis · How common suicide and suicide attempts are
- A 2025 meta-analysis (35 studies, 34 832 people with BPD) estimated lifetime suicidal ideation at 80% (95% CI 61-94%), suicide attempts at 52% (47-58%) and suicide at 6% (4-8%) [[Lak2025]].
- McLean 24-year follow-up (290 inpatients with BPD, 72 comparison subjects): 5.9% of people with BPD and 1.4% of comparison subjects died by suicide; 14.0% vs 5.5% died of other causes [[Temes2019]].
- In that cohort, more prior psychiatric hospitalisations predicted suicide (HR 1.62), and 87.5% of those who died by suicide had not recovered before death [[Temes2019]].
- Montreal 27-year follow-up (64 people): the cumulative proportion who died by suicide reached 10.3%, and only 5 still met BPD criteria [[Paris2001]].
- Reviews by Paris give "one in ten" (2002) and "up to 10%" (2019) as the share of people with BPD who die by suicide [[Paris2002Chronic,Paris2019Suicidality]].
- Pittsburgh cohort (118 people followed a mean 14.4 years): 46.6% attempted suicide in the 10-year interval, but suicidal ideation, number of attempts and NSSI "diminished markedly" from baseline [[Soloff2019Suicidal]].
- In adult (290) and adolescent (104) inpatients with BPD, lifetime self-injury was reported by about 90% and suicide attempts by about 75% at admission [[Goodman2017]].
- Over 10 years, the share of people with BPD reporting repeated self-destructive acts declined significantly; the authors called the course "initially more serious and ultimately more benign than previously recognized" [[Zanarini2008SelfHarm]].
- Self-harm and proneness to crisis "can decrease over time", and most people with BPD gradually enter remission (Established) [[Gunderson2018]].

### crisis · Non-suicidal self-injury: how common and why
- NSSI (self-injury without suicidal intent) is estimated to occur in about 65-80% of people with BPD, as cited in a college-sample study [[Brickman2014]].
- Across 18 studies, the best-supported function of self-injury is regulating emotion: negative feelings come first, relief follows, and relief is the most common stated reason (Established) [[Klonsky2007]].
- Self-punishment has "strong support" as a function; anti-dissociation, interpersonal influence, anti-suicide and sensation-seeking functions have modest evidence [[Klonsky2007]].
- In 101 women with BPD, most reported several motives at once, relief from negative emotion was "almost always involved", and positive reinforcement played an extra role in about half [[Kleindienst2008]].
- NSSI is an early, easily seen marker of risk for developing BPD, and affective instability helps maintain NSSI and raises the risk of suicidal behaviour [[Reichl2021]].
- NSSI and suicide attempts are different behaviours, but both need a caring, non-judgemental response; NICE says people should "not be excluded from any health or social care service because of their diagnosis or because they have self-harmed" [[NICE2009]].

### crisis · Chronic versus acute risk
- Paris (2002) distinguishes chronic suicidality, which in outpatient therapy "can be best understood as a way of communicating distress", from acute risk [[Paris2002Chronic]].
- Paris (2002): "Hospitalization is of unproven value in preventing suicide by these patients and can sometimes have negative effects" (Hypothesis-level, review opinion) [[Paris2002Chronic]].
- Paris (2019): "no research data support the effectiveness of suicide prevention in this disorder, and hospitalization has not been shown to be useful"; specifically designed psychotherapies have the best evidence [[Paris2019Suicidality]].
- APA 2024 implementation text: because of the heightened risk of suicide attempts and death, people with BPD should be monitored for suicide risk, assessments documented, and "individualized safety plans" developed [[APA2024Guideline]].
- Specialist psychotherapies do reduce self-harm: in Cochrane 2020, DBT vs usual care reduced self-harm (SMD -0.28, -0.48 to -0.07; 7 trials) and MBT reduced self-harm (RR 0.62, 0.49-0.80; 3 trials) [[Storebo2020]].

### crisis · Why risk scales should not be used to predict suicide
- NICE NG225 1.6.1: "Do not use risk assessment tools and scales to predict future suicide or repetition of self-harm" [[NICE2022NG225]].
- NICE NG225 1.6.2-1.6.4: do not use tools, scales or "global risk stratification into low, medium or high risk" to decide who gets treatment or who is discharged [[NICE2022NG225]].
- NICE NG225 1.6.5-1.6.6: focus assessment on the person's needs and safety, and undertake a risk formulation in every psychosocial assessment [[NICE2022NG225]].
- Meta-analysis of risk scales: pooled positive predictive value was 5.5% for suicide, 26.3% for self-harm and 35.9% for either; "No 'high-risk' classification was clinically useful" [[Carter2017PPV]].
- Meta-analysis of 53 risk-assessed samples: over about 63 months, 5.5% of "high-risk" vs 0.9% of "lower-risk" patients died by suicide; sensitivity 56% and specificity 79%, with no improvement in newer studies [[Large2016]].
- NICE NG225: the psychosocial assessment should not be delayed until medical treatment is completed, nor because of alcohol levels (1.5.2, 1.5.4) [[NICE2022NG225]].

### crisis · Safety planning and emergency department care
- The Safety Planning Intervention is "a brief clinical intervention that combined evidence-based strategies to reduce suicidal behavior through a prioritized list of coping skills and strategies"; in the VA ED study it was paired with at least 2 follow-up phone calls to monitor risk, revise the plan and support engagement in treatment [[Stanley2018ED]].
- US Veterans ED study (1640 patients; 5 intervention vs 4 control EDs): safety planning plus at least two follow-up calls was followed by suicidal behaviour in 3.03% vs 5.29% over 6 months (OR 0.56, 95% CI 0.33-0.95) [[Stanley2018ED]].
- In the same study, the intervention group had more than double the odds of attending at least one outpatient mental health visit (OR 2.06, 1.57-2.71) [[Stanley2018ED]].
- Meta-analysis of safety planning-type interventions (6 studies, 3536 participants): relative risk of suicidal behaviour 0.570 (95% CI 0.408-0.795; number needed to treat 16), with no effect on suicidal ideation (Established for behaviour) [[Nuij2021]].
- ED-SAFE (8 US EDs, 1376 adults with recent suicide attempt or ideation): screening alone did not change outcomes (23% vs 22%), but screening plus a brief ED intervention (secondary screening by the physician, discharge resources and post-ED telephone calls) lowered suicide attempts to 18% (relative reduction 20%) and total attempts by 30% [[Miller2017EDSAFE]].
- These ED studies did not select people with BPD; they show what helps people in suicidal crisis generally (applicability to BPD assumed, not tested) [[Stanley2018ED,Miller2017EDSAFE]].
- NICE NG225: after self-harm, agree a care plan and aftercare, give the person a copy with contact details, and develop a safety plan written with and agreed by the person [[NICE2022NG225]].
- NICE CG78 crisis principles: maintain a calm and non-threatening attitude, try to understand the crisis from the person's point of view, use validating statements, avoid minimising their reasons, and "explore other options before considering admission" [[NICE2009]].
- NICE CG78: each person's care plan should include a crisis plan that names triggers, self-management strategies that work, and how to reach services, including out-of-hours and crisis team numbers [[NICE2009]].

### crisis · Crisis plans, brief admission and hospital care
- Joint crisis plans RCT (88 community adults with BPD): no difference in self-harm at 6 months (OR 1.9, 95% CI 0.53-6.5), though the plan "appears to have high face validity" [[Borschmann2013JCP]].
- Brief Admission Skåne: people with self-harm or recurrent suicidality and at least 3 BPD criteria agree a contract in advance and can self-admit at times of rising risk, for up to 3 days and at most 3 times a month [[Liljedahl2017BA]].
- Brief Admission Skåne RCT (125 participants, 85% women; NCT02985047): no advantage in hospital days at 12 months versus usual care [[Westling2019BA,NCT02985047]].
- In the same trial, only the brief-admission group showed within-group reductions in compulsory admission days and NSSI, and it improved more in mobility functioning [[Westling2019BA]].
- Cochrane 2022 on crisis interventions in BPD found just two RCTs (213 participants: joint crisis plans and brief admission) and "no clear evidence of a benefit over TAU" for any main outcome, all low or very low certainty [[MonkCunliffe2022]].
- NICE CG78: before acute admission, first refer to a crisis resolution and home treatment team; consider admission only for crises with significant risk that cannot be managed elsewhere, agreeing length and purpose in advance [[NICE2009]].

### crisis · Medicines during a crisis
- NICE CG78: short-term drug treatment "may be helpful" during a crisis; use a single drug, avoid polypharmacy, and choose one with a low side-effect profile, low addictive properties, low misuse potential and "relative safety in overdose" (1.3.7.2-1.3.7.3) [[NICE2009]].
- NICE CG78 gives a sedative antihistamine as an example (not licensed for this use; informed consent needed), and advises prescribing "fewer tablets more frequently if there is a significant risk of overdose" [[NICE2009]].
- NICE CG78: plan to stop crisis medicines "usually within 1 week"; if they cannot be stopped, review regularly for effectiveness, side effects, misuse and dependency [[NICE2009]].
- APA 2024: medication review "is especially useful following stabilization of an acute crisis", which is often what prompts starting a medicine [[APA2024Guideline]].

### crisis · Safe messaging and crisis lines
- A meta-analysis of 31 studies found suicide risk rose 13% after media reports of a celebrity's death by suicide (rate ratio 1.13, 1.08-1.18), and more when the method was reported (1.30, 1.18-1.44); responsible-reporting guidelines are the best population-level response [[Niederkrotenthaler2020]].
- Stories of people coping with suicidal thoughts without acting on them ("mastery of crisis") were linked to fewer suicides, the "Papageno effect" (Early evidence) [[Niederkrotenthaler2010]].
- Samaritans' tips include: avoid reporting methods; "Include references to suicide being preventable and signpost sources of support"; avoid sensational language, dramatic images and speculation about a single cause [[SamaritansMedia]].
- WHO publishes "Preventing suicide: a resource for media professionals, update 2023" (ISBN 978-92-4-007684-6) [[WHO2023Media]].
- In the US, the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline offers 24/7, free, confidential support; people can call, text or chat 988 [[SAMHSA988]].
- In the UK and Ireland, Samaritans can be called free on 116 123, any time, from any phone [[Samaritans116123]].

### Exam hooks
- No medicine is approved by the FDA for BPD; all drug use for BPD is off-label [[FDA-LabelSearch2026,StoffersWinterling2022]].
- Cochrane 2022 (46 RCTs): no medicine beat placebo on BPD severity, self-harm, suicide-related outcomes or functioning; evidence mostly very low certainty [[StoffersWinterling2022]].
- NICE CG78: do not use drugs specifically for BPD or its individual symptoms; do not use antipsychotics medium- or long-term [[NICE2009]].
- NICE CG78: crisis sedatives only short-term, usually stopped within 1 week [[NICE2009]].
- APA 2024 (2C): any medicine should be time-limited, target a measurable symptom and be adjunctive to psychotherapy [[APA2024Guideline]].
- APA 2024 (1C): review and reconcile medicines at least every 6 months [[APA2024Guideline]].
- LABILE: lamotrigine was no better than placebo at 52 weeks (ZAN-BPD 11.3 vs 11.5) [[Crawford2018LABILE]].
- Olanzapine 5-10 mg/day gave a modest ZAN-BPD advantage but about 3 kg weight gain in 12 weeks [[Zanarini2011Olanzapine]].
- About 90% of inpatients with BPD get psychotropics and over half get three or more [[Bridler2015]].
- Valproate: about 10% major malformation risk in exposed pregnancies and lower child IQ; avoid in people who could become pregnant unless alternatives fail [[Battino2024EURAP,FDA-Depakote]].
- Benzodiazepines are not generally recommended in BPD (disinhibition, misuse, dependence) and were linked to the highest suicidal-behaviour hazard in Swedish registry data (HR 1.61) [[APA2024Guideline,Lieslehto2023Suicide]].
- About 1 in 10 (5.9-10.3% in long cohorts) of people with BPD die by suicide; the meta-analytic estimate is 6% [[Temes2019,Paris2001,Lak2025]].
- Suicidal behaviour and self-harm in BPD decline markedly over time [[Soloff2019Suicidal,Zanarini2008SelfHarm]].
- NICE NG225: do not use risk scales or low/medium/high stratification to predict suicide or decide treatment [[NICE2022NG225]].
- Risk scales: positive predictive value for suicide only 5.5% [[Carter2017PPV]].
- Safety planning cuts suicidal behaviour by about 43% (RR 0.57; NNT 16) [[Nuij2021]].
- The main function of NSSI is short-term relief from intense negative emotion [[Klonsky2007,Kleindienst2008]].
- Brief admission by self-referral did not reduce hospital days compared with usual care [[Westling2019BA]].
- NICE NG225: do not offer drug treatment as a specific intervention to reduce self-harm [[NICE2022NG225]].

### In plain words
- No medicine is approved to treat BPD itself. Talking therapies are the main treatment.
- Medicines can still help with other conditions that often come with BPD, such as depression, or for a short time in a crisis.
- If you take medicines, it is reasonable to ask your doctor what each one is for and when it will be reviewed. Guidelines say at least every 6 months.
- Some medicines can cause weight gain, sleepiness or restlessness. Tell your care team about side effects.
- Valproate can seriously harm an unborn baby. Anyone who could become pregnant should talk to their doctor about this before starting or stopping it.
- Thoughts of suicide and self-harm are common in BPD, and they usually become less frequent over the years, especially with treatment.
- Self-harm is often a way to get relief from overwhelming feelings. Therapy can teach other ways to cope.
- A safety plan is a short written plan you make with a clinician: your warning signs, things that help, people to call, and crisis numbers. Studies show it lowers suicidal behaviour.
- Checklists and scores cannot predict who will die by suicide. Good care means listening to the person and planning together.
- If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).
- Families can help by staying calm, listening without judging, and helping the person use their safety plan.
- Recovery from BPD is common. Many people stop meeting the diagnosis over time.

## Open questions and controversies
- Is there any role for medicines on BPD core symptoms? Older small trials and meta-analyses reported benefits for mood stabilisers and some antipsychotics [[Lieb2010Cochrane,Ingenhoven2010]]; the larger Cochrane 2022 and Gartlehner 2021 reviews and the LABILE trial found no consistent effect [[StoffersWinterling2022,Gartlehner2021,Crawford2018LABILE]]; status: current guidelines favour no routine drug treatment, with time-limited adjunctive use at most [[NICE2009,APA2024Guideline]].
- NICE vs APA tone: NICE says drugs should not be used for BPD or its individual symptoms [[NICE2009]]; APA allows time-limited, symptom-targeted medicines adjunctive to psychotherapy (a 2C suggestion) [[APA2024Guideline]]; status: both agree medicines are not first-line; NICE CG78 is under review after ICD-11.
- Clozapine for the most severely ill inpatients: observational data suggest fewer bed-days and self-harm [[Rohde2018Clozapine,Lieslehto2023Outcomes]]; the only RCT could not recruit enough people [[Crawford2022CALMED]] and a 2025 register study found no hospitalisation benefit in BPD [[Luykx2025Clozapine]]; status: unresolved.
- Medicines and suicidal behaviour: Swedish registry data link benzodiazepines, antidepressants and antipsychotics to higher risk and ADHD medicines to lower risk [[Lieslehto2023Suicide]]; observational designs may still reflect that medicines are started when people are worse; status: hypothesis-generating, no RCT.
- Why do drug trials in BPD keep failing? Large placebo responses and high drop-out are common [[Grant2022Brexpiprazole,Rothman2024Brexpiprazole,Dwyer2025BI1358894]]; the BI 1358894 authors suggest more targeted populations and new outcome measures [[Dwyer2025BI1358894]]; status: open.
- Brief admission: the Swedish RCT found no reduction in hospital days but less compulsory care and NSSI within the brief-admission group [[Westling2019BA]]; Cochrane rates the crisis evidence low to very low certainty [[MonkCunliffe2022]]; status: more trials registered.
- Hospital admission for chronic suicidality: Paris argues admission is of unproven value and can harm [[Paris2002Chronic]]; NICE allows admission for crises with significant risk that cannot be managed elsewhere [[NICE2009]]; status: no RCT comparing admission strategies in BPD.
- Safety planning in BPD specifically: safety planning reduces suicidal behaviour in mixed ED populations [[Stanley2018ED,Nuij2021]], and APA recommends individualized safety plans in BPD [[APA2024Guideline]], but no BPD-specific trial was found; joint crisis plans showed no effect in one BPD pilot [[Borschmann2013JCP]].

## Provenance
- All 65 journal sources were checked in one PubMed E-utilities esummary batch (authors, title, journal, year, volume, pages, PMID, PMCID, DOI taken from the record). Abstracts were read for every paper a number is taken from: via PubMed efetch for the first eleven (Cochrane 2022, Pereira Ribeiro 2025, LABILE, quetiapine, three olanzapine trials, CALMED, omega-3, Gartlehner 2021, Paton 2015) and via the Europe PMC core API for the rest, because PubMed searches were heavily rate-limited during this pass.
- APA 2024 guideline: the AJP article has no abstract and psychiatryonline returned 403. Statement wording and ratings were checked against the APA news release (10 Dec 2024) and the full guideline PDF on psychiatry.org ("under copyediting" version, footer dated 2 Oct 2023, "not for citation"); the eight statements are identical in both. Implementation quotes come from that PDF and may differ in minor wording from the final published text.
- NICE CG78 and NG225 were read on nice.org.uk on 2026-10-25. FDA labels were read via the openFDA label endpoint (set ids given). Trials were checked on the ClinicalTrials.gov API v2. Crisis lines were checked on SAMHSA (988lifeline.org served a bot check) and samaritans.org.
- Dropped: Stanley and Brown 2012 (Cogn Behav Pract 19(2):256-264, doi:10.1016/j.cbpra.2011.01.001) was confirmed on Crossref but has no abstract there and the publisher page returned 403, so its content was not read and it is not cited; the safety-plan description comes from Stanley 2018 instead. The WHO 2023 media resource PDF was blocked, so only its catalogue record is cited and none of its wording is used.
- Not cited although found: Hawton 2015 Cochrane (superseded by Witt 2021), Willems 2014 movement-disorder meta-analysis (no abstract), Crawford 2018 HTA report (same trial as the AJP paper).
- The figure "65-80% of people with BPD engage in NSSI" is quoted as Brickman 2014 states it in its background (a secondary estimate); the primary studies behind it were not read.
- Safe messaging: method details given in some sources (for example Goodman 2017, Zanarini 2008 and Niederkrotenthaler 2020) were deliberately left out.
- No fact in this file is marked as not confirmed.
