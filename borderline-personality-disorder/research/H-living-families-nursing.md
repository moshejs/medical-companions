# H · Living, families, work and school & nursing care

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: families, living, nursing.

This pass covers daily life with borderline personality disorder (BPD): what families and carers go through and which family programmes have been tested; parenting, pregnancy and medicines in pregnancy; work, study and disability benefits; romantic relationships and intimate partner violence; physical health, sleep, pain and substance use; stigma, self-stigma, social media and peer support; and nursing care (the therapeutic relationship, emergency and inpatient care, brief admission, crisis plans, observation, de-escalation, staff attitudes and training, supervision, and the measures nurses use). Numbers come from PubMed abstracts unless a line says otherwise. Guideline wording comes from the NICE web pages, and the valproate pregnancy statements come from the current US label in openFDA.

## Findings

### families · What caring for someone with BPD is like
- A PRISMA systematic review (6 studies, 465 carers, 1996–2011) found that carers of people with personality disorders had raised objective and subjective burden, grief, low empowerment, and depression and anxiety. Five of the 6 studies were of carers of people with BPD. [[Bailey2013]]
- In that review, carers' objective and subjective burden scores were about half a standard deviation above those of carers of inpatients with other serious mental illnesses. [[Bailey2013]]
- In a survey of 287 carers using validated scales, burden and grief were significantly higher than published figures for carers of people with other serious mental illnesses. Carers also reported symptoms consistent with mood, anxiety and posttraumatic stress disorders. [[Bailey2014]]
- Carers in that study described grieving for the change in their lives. [[Bailey2014]]
- Among 280 carers of a relative with BPD, 82.9% reported high criticism and 69.6% high emotional overinvolvement in the family. Higher emotional overinvolvement went with higher burden and more mental health problems. [[Bailey2015]]
- The 82 carers of 54 young outpatients (mean age 18.8) with 3–9 BPD features reported more distress, more negative caregiving experiences, more expressed emotion and more maladaptive coping than general-population adults. The adversity was similar to or greater than that of carers of young people with eating disorders, cancer or psychosis. [[Seigerman2020]]
- Contrary to expectation, in 32 family members, knowing more about BPD went with more burden, distress, depression and hostility. The authors raised concern about the source and accuracy of the information families receive. (Early evidence) [[Hoffman2003]]
- A meta-synthesis of 38 studies found that families and carers valued professionalism, respect, compassion and good information. Their expectations broke down when knowledge, communication, information sharing or support were lacking. [[Lamont2021]]
- In a co-produced study of 33 people with BPD and 30 carers, all participants reported stigmatising language. Carers felt frustrated when they felt blamed or their needs went unacknowledged. Both groups called helpful language "connecting, validating and accepting". [[vanSchie2024]]

### families · Family Connections and other family programmes
- Family Connections (FC) is a 12-week manualised programme for relatives of people with BPD. Trained family members lead it. It is based on standard dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT) and DBT for families, and covers information about BPD, coping skills, family skills and building a support network. [[Hoffman2005]]
- In the first uncontrolled FC study (44 participants, 34 families), grief and burden fell and mastery rose from before to after the programme. The gains held at 6 months. (Early evidence) [[Hoffman2005]]
- The developers published a second uncontrolled FC study in 2007. Before 2017 there were only these two uncontrolled developer studies. [[Hoffman2007JMH,Flynn2017]]
- In a non-randomised controlled study in Ireland (n = 80; FC 51, optimised treatment-as-usual 29), FC improved burden, grief, depression and mastery (all p < 0.001). The comparison programme showed only non-significant change. [[Flynn2017]]
- In that study, the between-group effect favoured FC for subjective burden (p = .02), objective burden (p = .048) and grief (p = 0.013). Gains held at 3 and 12–19 months. [[Flynn2017]]
- A three-session psychoeducation programme (the comparison arm) was "of limited benefit" in that study. [[Flynn2017]]
- In a Spanish two-arm RCT (121 relatives from 82 families, plus 82 patients), FC was not statistically superior to an active treatment-as-usual of similar length on burden. [[Guillen2024]]
- In that RCT, FC's adjusted means and effect sizes were consistently better than TAU for burden, stress, depression, family functioning and quality of life. Patients whose relatives took FC improved significantly in stress, depression and anxiety. [[Guillen2024]]
- In 202 Italian caregivers, burden, grief, depression, global distress and suppressed and expressed anger fell after FC. The gains held at 4 months. [[Lanfredi2025]]
- In 75 FC participants (57% parents), mean hopelessness before FC was 4.61, in the "mild" range. It did not change significantly after the programme. 82% scored minimal or mild at baseline. [[Joyce2024]]
- In an RCT of group psychoeducation for carers (33 intervention vs 35 waitlist; groups of 6–8 carers), carers in the intervention reported better dyadic adjustment with their relative, more family empowerment and less expressed emotion after 10 weeks. The gains held at 12 months. [[Grenyer2019]]
- In a clustered RCT at Orygen (79 carers of 15–25-year-olds), adding three in-person multi-family sessions (Making Sense of BPD) to two online modules gave no extra benefit on negative caregiving experiences (d = −0.32). Carers' knowledge improved in both arms. [[Betts2023]]
- DBT-Family Skills Training aims to help all family members understand borderline behaviour patterns without judgement, build a mutually validating environment, and address everyone's emotion-regulation and interpersonal skills. [[Hoffman1999]]
- The transactional model describes how invalidating, critical family responses and a child's emotional vulnerability shape each other over time. Its authors state that the model blames neither the person with BPD nor the parents. (Hypothesis) [[Fruzzetti2005]]
- Gunderson and colleagues described a psychoeducational approach for families of people with BPD, prompted by changing views of the disorder and of health care. The 1997 report gives only preliminary suggestions of effectiveness. (Early evidence) [[Gunderson1997]]
- A 2007 review noted that programmes for families of people with personality disorders lag well behind those for other mental disorders, despite evidence that families matter to the course of BPD. [[Hoffman2007CPR]]
- NICE CG78: ask directly whether the person wants family or carers involved. Subject to consent and confidentiality, encourage involvement, make sure it does not reduce the person's access to services, and tell families about local carer support groups. [[NICE2009]]
- NICE NG225 (self-harm): even when a person has not consented to family involvement, family members or carers can still give information about the person. [[NICE2022SelfHarm]]
- Peer workers with their own lived experience can support carers as well as people with BPD. In interviews, carers described the value of support from carer peer workers. [[Barr2020]]

### families · Parenting, children and the perinatal period
- A systematic review (33 studies) found that mothers with BPD or BPD symptoms were more likely than other mothers to parent in insensitive, overprotective and hostile ways. [[Eyden2016]]
- Offspring outcomes in that review included BPD symptoms, internalising and externalising problems, insecure attachment and emotional dysregulation. The authors propose that risk is partly passed on through parenting and the mother's emotional difficulties. Prospective studies from pregnancy onward are needed. (Early evidence) [[Eyden2016]]
- A systematic review of 17 studies found that mothers with BPD often parent with added risk factors (depression, substance use, low support). Their interactions with infants tend to be low in sensitivity and high in intrusiveness, and parenting stress is high. [[Petfield2015]]
- An overview of 8 systematic reviews (140 primary studies, 121,895 participants) concluded that BPD pathology is linked with maladaptive parenting and poorer offspring outcomes. It called for parenting interventions for people with personality disorders. [[Steele2019]]
- One review suggests that switching between hostile control and passive aloofness may be specific to mothers with BPD. It recommends psychoeducation about child development and skills for consistent warmth and monitoring, including mindfulness-based parenting. (Hypothesis) [[Stepp2012]]
- In the McLean 16-year follow-up, recovered patients were more likely than non-recovered patients to have married or cohabited and to have become parents, and less likely to have lost or given up custody of a child. [[Zanarini2015Marriage]]
- A meta-analysis of perinatal studies found a pooled BPD prevalence of 14.0% (95% CI 7.0–22.0) in clinical perinatal samples. In non-clinical samples, BPD was 0.7–1.7% across the perinatal period. [[Prasad2022]]
- In a US inpatient sample of births (2003–2012; 989 births to women with BPD, 11.65 per 100,000 births), BPD was linked after adjustment with preterm birth (OR 1.54, 95% CI 1.29–1.83), caesarean delivery (OR 1.44, 1.26–1.64), gestational diabetes (OR 1.45, 1.13–1.85), premature rupture of membranes (OR 1.40, 1.07–1.83), chorioamnionitis (OR 1.65, 1.14–2.39) and venous thromboembolism (OR 2.11, 1.12–3.96). [[PareMiron2016]]
- In an Australian case review (42 women with BPD vs 14,313 controls), mothers with BPD had more prematurity, lower Apgar scores and more special-care nursery admissions. They also had high rates of substance-use comorbidity and of referral to child protection. [[Blankley2015]]
- A systematic review found only 7 studies of interventions for perinatal BPD or complex PTSD, just 2 of them RCTs. DBT group skills, mother–baby unit care and child–parent psychotherapy were linked with better outcomes, but the evidence was low in quantity and quality. (Early evidence) [[May2023]]
- Mother–Infant DBT is a 25-week group: mothers learn skills while infants are cared for, then they reunite for a shared activity. 69 of 98 women completed it (71%). Depression, anxiety and BPD symptoms improved, but observed mother–infant interaction often stayed of concern. (Early evidence) [[SvedWilliams2021]]
- In interviews with 13 women who completed Mother-Infant DBT, the women described better emotional literacy and regulation and more confidence reading their child's cues. [[Francis2023]]

### families · Medicines in pregnancy (relevant because these drugs are used off-label in BPD)
- The US Depakote label's boxed warning states that valproate can cause major congenital malformations, particularly neural tube defects, and decreased IQ scores and neurodevelopmental disorders after exposure in the womb. [[FDA-Depakote]]
- The label states that valproate "should not be administered to a woman of childbearing potential unless other medications have failed to provide adequate symptom control or are otherwise unacceptable", with effective contraception in such cases. It also states that the malformation risk is dose-dependent with no threshold dose that is free of risk. [[FDA-Depakote]]
- In the EURAP registry (7,355 monotherapy pregnancies), the rate of major congenital malformations was 10.3% with valproate, 5.5% with carbamazepine, 3.9% with topiramate, 3.0% with oxcarbazepine, 2.9% with lamotrigine and 2.8% with levetiracetam. [[Tomson2018]]
- In the Nordic SCAN-AED cohort (4,494,926 children), at age 8, 2.7% of children exposed to valproate and 4.3% exposed to topiramate had autism, compared with 1.5% of unexposed children of mothers with epilepsy. Adjusted HRs for autism were 2.4 (1.7–3.3) for valproate and 2.8 (1.4–5.7) for topiramate. [[Bjork2022]]
- In the same cohort, intellectual disability had aHR 2.5 (1.7–3.7) after valproate and 3.5 (1.4–8.6) after topiramate. Lamotrigine monotherapy showed no consistently increased risk. [[Bjork2022]]
- At age 6, children exposed in the womb to high-dose valproate (>800 mg daily) had an adjusted mean IQ 9.7 points lower (95% CI 4.9–14.6) than controls. They had an 8-fold higher need for educational intervention. [[Baker2015]]
- In UK and Swedish data (3,182,773 children), valproate exposure was linked with autism, intellectual disability and ADHD diagnoses. There was little evidence of raised risk with lamotrigine. [[MadleyDowd2024]]
- In 1,325,563 Medicaid pregnancies, cardiac malformations occurred in 2.41% of infants exposed to lithium in the first trimester, against 1.15% of unexposed infants (adjusted RR 1.65, 95% CI 1.02–2.68). The risk was dose-related: RR 3.22 above 900 mg/day. [[Patorno2017]]

### living · Work, study and benefits
- Recovery in the McLean study required both symptomatic remission and good social and vocational functioning. Over 10 years, 50% of patients reached recovery, while 93% had a symptomatic remission lasting at least 2 years. [[Zanarini2010Recovery]]
- By 16 years, remission rates were 78–99% but recovery rates only 40–60%, depending on how long it had to last. In comparison patients with other personality disorders, recovery rates were 75–85%. [[Zanarini2012]]
- In the CLPS study, 85% of people with BPD remitted over 10 years and 12% relapsed. Social functioning stayed severely impaired with only modest improvement. [[Gunderson2011]]
- In the McLean study, people with BPD were 3 times more likely than personality-disordered comparison subjects to receive US Social Security Disability Income (SSDI). Of those on SSDI at baseline, 40% came off it, but 43% of them went back on. [[Zanarini2009]]
- By the 10-year follow-up, 55% of people with BPD receiving SSDI had worked or studied at least half of the previous 2 years. About 70% had a supportive friendship, and over 50% had a good relationship with a romantic partner. [[Zanarini2009]]
- Over 24 years of follow-up, on average 47.2% of people with BPD were on SSDI at each wave. Predictors were age 26 or older, lower IQ, severity of non-sexual childhood abuse and PTSD. [[Kramer2023SSDI]]
- In a US national survey, BPD was associated with substantial mental and physical disability, especially among women. [[Grant2008]]
- An integrative review of 11 studies linked BPD with lower education, higher dropout from tertiary education and low employment. Barriers included symptoms, self-stigma and non-disclosure. Enablers included boldness and honesty, and employment itself appeared to improve symptoms. [[Larcombe2022]]
- A scoping review found only 7 papers on employment interventions for BPD, all small or non-randomised. Every programme combined a psychotherapy component with work goals. [[Kernot2023]]
- In a Dutch registry of Individual Placement and Support (IPS), people with personality disorders found competitive work as often as people with other mental disorders (37.6% vs 38.0%; adjusted OR 0.97, 0.74–1.27). [[Juurlink2022]]
- In that IPS registry, median time to a job was 195.5 days for people with personality disorders and 178.5 days for others (aHR 0.95). [[Juurlink2022]]
- INVEST is an RCT protocol testing IPS added to early-intervention care for 108 young people (15–25) with 3 or more BPD features. Its primary outcome is days in education or employment at 52 weeks. (Hypothesis) [[Chanen2020INVEST]]
- In a Dutch cohort of workers, those with BPD symptoms alone had higher odds of impaired work performance (OR 6.02, 2.76–13.09). In dimensional analysis the effect was mediated by depressive symptoms, which suggests that treating mood may help work functioning. [[Juurlink2020NESDA]]
- In a chronic pain clinic sample (n = 147), BPD features were linked with being on disability for pain and with unemployment, even after controlling for pain severity, depression and anxiety. The negative-relationships facet explained this link. [[Reynolds2019]]
- In a meta-analysis of 43 college studies, lifetime prevalence of clinically significant BPD features in university samples was 9.7% (95% CI 7.7–12.0), with wide variation by method (0.5–32.1%). [[Meaney2016]]
- NICE CG78: care plans should set long-term goals, including employment and occupation, that the person would like to achieve, linked to short-term treatment aims. [[NICE2009]]

### living · Romantic relationships and intimate partner violence
- In a meta-analysis of 27 effect sizes, attachment anxiety correlated most strongly with BPD traits (r = 0.48). Attachment avoidance also correlated (r = 0.30), and the two interacted, which suggests disorganised attachment. [[Smith2020Attachment]]
- Among 35 couples in which the woman had BPD, 68.7% had frequent breakups and reconciliations, and nearly 30% separated over 18 months. Nearly half of the male partners met criteria for a personality disorder. [[Bouchard2009]]
- Compared with 35 non-clinical couples, these couples had lower satisfaction, more attachment insecurity, more demand/withdraw communication and more violence. [[Bouchard2009]]
- Relationship difficulties ease with recovery. In the McLean study, sexual relationship difficulties became less common over 16 years, and over 95% of both recovered and non-recovered patients reached remission from them. [[Karan2016]]
- In a meta-analysis of 163 studies on intimate partner violence (IPV), antisocial and borderline personality disorders had the most robust effect sizes for both perpetration and victimisation. [[Collison2021]]
- In a British population sample (n = 14,753), a categorical BPD diagnosis was linked only with IPV. Serious and repeated violence was better explained by co-occurring substance misuse, anxiety and antisocial personality disorder. Anger and impulsivity items, not affective instability or suicidal behaviour, carried the link. [[Gonzalez2016]]
- In a dyadic study of 109 community couples, men's borderline traits were linked with both perpetrating and experiencing IPV, while women's borderline traits were linked with experiencing it only. The study modelled both partners because violence in couples is interdependent. [[Maneta2013]]
- Safeguarding: NICE NG225 asks all staff who see someone after self-harm to ask about domestic abuse, violence or exploitation at the earliest opportunity, when the person is alone if appropriate. [[NICE2022SelfHarm]]

### living · Physical health, sleep, pain and substance use
- In US national survey data (NESARC Wave 2, n = 34,653), BPD was linked with hypertension or arteriosclerosis, liver disease, cardiovascular disease, gastrointestinal disease, arthritis, sexually transmitted disease and "any medical condition" after full adjustment (adjusted ORs 1.46–2.80). [[ElGabalawy2010]]
- 6 years after admission, people whose BPD had not remitted were more likely than those who had remitted to report obesity, diabetes, hypertension, osteoarthritis, back pain and chronic fatigue, fibromyalgia or TMJ syndrome. They also used more emergency and hospital care. [[Frankenburg2004]]
- 74 of 264 McLean patients (28%) were obese at 6 years. Risk factors were chronic PTSD, lack of exercise, family history of obesity and recent psychotropic polypharmacy. [[Frankenburg2006Obesity]]
- Over a decade, never-recovered patients were more likely to have obesity, diabetes, osteoarthritis and multiple medical conditions, to smoke a pack a day, to use sleep or pain medication heavily, and to have medical ER visits and hospitalisations. [[Keuroghlian2013]]
- In a Hong Kong health-record cohort (3,092 people with BPD vs 902,927 primary-care controls), BPD was linked with all-cause mortality (HR 5.65, 4.83–6.61), natural-cause mortality (HR 2.02, 1.56–2.62) and external-cause mortality (HR 30.35, 23.52–39.17). These are population statistics. [[Chan2026]]
- In that cohort, 37.3% of deaths were from natural causes, with cardiovascular disease a leading contributor. Excess life-years lost were 13.01. The authors called for physical-health monitoring alongside suicide prevention. [[Chan2026]]
- In a meta-analysis of 32 studies, people with BPD had objective sleep problems (longer sleep onset, shorter total sleep, lower efficiency) and more nightmares and poorer sleep quality than healthy controls, independent of depression. [[Winsper2017Sleep]]
- At 16 years, non-recovered McLean patients had worse sleep quality (PSQI 12.01 vs 10.73) and longer sleep onset (39.2 vs 28.1 minutes) than recovered patients. [[Plante2013]]
- In a small primary-care chronic pain sample (n = 17), 47.1% screened positive for BPD on a semi-structured interview (DIB). (Early evidence) [[Sansone2001]]
- In a review of 70 studies (2000–2017), 45.46% of people with BPD had a current substance use disorder (SUD) and 75.28% a lifetime SUD. Current and lifetime alcohol use disorder were 46.39% and 59.46%. [[Trull2018SUD]]
- In that review, about 22.1% of people with an SUD met criteria for BPD, rising to 33.80% among people with opioid dependence. [[Trull2018SUD]]
- NICE CG78: monitor the effect of psychological treatment on a broad range of outcomes, including drug and alcohol use, self-harm, depression and BPD symptoms. [[NICE2009]]

### living · Stigma, self-stigma, media and lived experience
- BPD may carry stigma beyond that attached to other mental illnesses. Clinicians' emotional distancing can be felt as rejection by people who are especially sensitive to abandonment, which can set up a self-fulfilling cycle. [[Aviram2006]]
- A review of 30 studies (12 patient, 18 clinician) found six stigma themes, including perceived untreatability, stigma as a response to feeling powerless, and low BPD health literacy in both patients and clinicians. [[Ring2019]]
- In a narrative review, the public may read BPD symptoms as "purposeful misbehaviour". Combining recovery messages with biological explanations was the most promising way to reduce stigma. (Early evidence) [[Ociskova2023]]
- Women with BPD (n = 60) showed more self-stigma than women with social phobia (n = 30). Self-stigma predicted low self-esteem after controlling for depression and shame. [[Rusch2006]]
- Lived-experience research describes recovery as non-linear. It moves through being stuck, diagnosis and an improving experience, and is driven by hope, active engagement, treatment and meaningful activities and relationships. [[Ng2019LivedRecovery]]
- Of 207 TikTok videos about BPD, 59.9% of creators gave no professional credentials. Personal-experience videos drew far more engagement than treatment videos (mean weighted likes 2,389 vs 222). Misinformation appeared in 24.7% of "understanding personality disorders" videos and 29.2% of diagnosis videos. [[Evoy2026]]
- An analysis of 25,197 comments on 141 BPD TikTok videos found worries about confusing BPD with autism and ADHD, frequent self-diagnosis and distrust of professional diagnosis. Social media acted as both support and a source of misinformation. [[Theriault2026]]
- Consumers, carers and clinicians felt that peer workers with lived experience can offer hope, connection and validation. Peer workers need supervision, clear role boundaries and a duty-of-care code. [[Barr2020]]
- A critical review found peer support for BPD feasible and in line with personal recovery goals: self-acceptance, hope, meaningful roles and self-determination. Formal programme evaluations are lacking, and burnout and boundary challenges are real. (Early evidence) [[Turner2024]]
- A scoping review of 11 studies (2019–2025) reported better emotion regulation, less isolation and more hope with peer support. It also noted emotional exhaustion and role ambiguity among peer workers. Few studies measured symptom change. (Early evidence) [[Dufour2025]]
- NICE CG78: work in an atmosphere of hope and optimism, "explaining that recovery is possible and attainable". Bear in mind that many people have experienced rejection, abuse, trauma and stigma. [[NICE2009]]
- NICE CG78: people with BPD "should not be excluded from any health or social care service because of their diagnosis or because they have self-harmed". [[NICE2009]]

### nursing · Nurse attitudes and what changes them
- A systematic integrative review of 40 studies found that mental health nurses' attitudes toward people with BPD were poorer than those of other professionals and poorer than toward other diagnoses. Nurses asked for a coherent therapeutic framework, and care seemed better where one existed. [[Dickens2016]]
- A review of UK nurse attitude research (42 studies) found that BPD, substance misuse and acute presentations were the most negatively appraised targets. Education raised positive attitudes in the short term, but there was little evidence the gains lasted. [[Dickens2022]]
- A systematic review of training for mental health nurses found 8 studies, only 1 with a control group. Effects were largest for knowledge and cognitive attitudes and smaller for feelings. Gains were greatest in staff with the poorest baseline attitudes and no previous training. [[Dickens2016Interventions]]
- Among 710 Israeli hospital clinicians, nurses and psychiatrists held more negative attitudes and less empathy toward BPD than psychologists and social workers. Negative attitudes rose with the number of people with BPD cared for. Nurses showed the most interest in learning short-term treatment methods. [[Bodner2015]]
- In a smaller study (25 nurses, 13 psychologists, 19 psychiatrists), patients' suicidal tendencies mainly explained clinicians' negative emotions and sense of difficulty. Nurses scored lowest on empathy. [[Bodner2011]]
- After a 1-day STEPPS workshop, 271 clinicians reported more empathy, a better attitude, more desire to work with people with BPD, less dislike and more competence. [[Shanks2011]]
- A 3-hour BPD/DBT workshop with education and social contact reduced healthcare providers' stigma scores, more for BPD than for mental illness in general. Baseline stigma was higher toward BPD. [[Knaak2015]]
- A 1-day training co-produced with an expert by experience and built on the biosocial model produced some attitude gains that lasted 4 months among 28 nursing staff. Participants valued the lived-experience input but were sometimes hostile to the biosocial model. [[Dickens2019]]
- Watching a first-person film about living with BPD improved nursing and counselling students' views on treatment deservingness (n = 66), but did not raise knowledge. [[Dickens2018]]
- A 6-week DBT skills-coaching training for 22 nursing staff in a youth residential unit increased DBT knowledge and reduced personal and work burnout and BPD stigma. Stigma before training predicted later client-related burnout. (Early evidence) [[Haynos2016]]
- A 7-hour "Clinician Connections" workshop for emergency department and community clinicians was reported to improve understanding, use of validation and self-efficacy. [[Burke2019]]
- A meta-analysis of 5 studies found limited empathy and negative attitudes among emergency department nurses toward people who self-harm. It recommended education and supervision. [[Rayner2019]]

### nursing · The therapeutic relationship, validation and consistency
- People with BPD cared for by community mental health nurses named three useful parts of the relationship: learning about the disorder, building trust, and being empowered through validation. [[RomeuLabayen2020]]
- People who had stayed on acute wards valued being listened to, time to talk with staff and other patients, time out from daily life, and feeling safe. They found negative staff attitudes, lack of staff contact, lack of BPD knowledge, coercive admission and poor discharge planning harmful. [[Stapleton2019]]
- A thematic synthesis of 7 studies found that inpatient nurses caring for people with BPD and/or non-suicidal self-injury start with strong motivation. Unexpected demands then lead to emotional exhaustion and compassion fatigue. [[Nordkamp2024]]
- NICE CG78, during a crisis: maintain a calm, non-threatening attitude, try to understand the crisis from the person's point of view, use empathic open questions with validating statements, avoid minimising the stated reasons, and do not offer solutions before the problem is clear. [[NICE2009]]
- NICE CG78: expect that endings and transitions between services may evoke strong emotions. Discuss changes beforehand, structure and phase them, and keep access to crisis services during transitions. [[NICE2009]]
- NICE CG78: offer post-assessment support, particularly after sensitive topics such as childhood trauma have been discussed. [[NICE2009]]

### nursing · Emergency department care after self-harm
- An integrative review of 9 papers on BPD presentations to the emergency department found mostly negative experiences reported by people with BPD, carers and clinicians. The main themes were barriers to timely care and stigmatising attitudes, which were felt to drive further visits. [[Miller2024ED]]
- Danish emergency nurses (n = 10) saw their competence as biomedical and felt they lacked the skills for in-depth mental health care after self-harm. They suggested close collaboration with psychiatry. [[Ostervang2022]]
- NICE NG225: carry out physical care and the psychosocial assessment at the same time, as soon as possible, and do not delay the assessment until medical treatment is complete. [[NICE2022SelfHarm]]
- NICE NG225: do not use breath or blood alcohol levels to delay the psychosocial assessment. Hold it in a private area where the person cannot be overheard. [[NICE2022SelfHarm]]
- NICE NG225: do not use risk assessment tools or scales, or a global low/medium/high risk rating, to predict suicide or repeat self-harm or to decide who gets treatment or discharge. Use a risk formulation within every psychosocial assessment instead. [[NICE2022SelfHarm]]
- NICE NG225: develop a safety plan with the person, and provide initial aftercare within 48 hours of the psychosocial assessment if there are ongoing safety concerns. [[NICE2022SelfHarm]]
- NICE NG225: the emergency department waiting area for people who have self-harmed should be close to staff who can provide care, support and observation. [[NICE2022SelfHarm]]

### nursing · Admission, brief admission and crisis plans
- NICE CG78: before considering acute admission, first refer the person to a crisis resolution and home treatment team or another local alternative to admission. [[NICE2009]]
- NICE CG78: consider admission only for crises with significant risk to self or others that cannot be managed elsewhere, or for detention under the Mental Health Act. Agree the length and purpose of any admission in advance, and resume voluntary care as soon as possible if compulsion was needed. [[NICE2009]]
- NICE CG78: arrange a formal care programme review for anyone admitted twice or more in the previous 6 months. [[NICE2009]]
- Brief Admission by self-referral (BA) is a contract-based, time-limited admission that the person can request. In a Swedish RCT (125 adults with self-harm or recurrent suicidality, at least 3 BPD criteria and a recent hospitalisation; 84.8% women), BA plus usual care did not reduce total hospital days compared with usual care. [[Westling2019]]
- In that trial, only the BA group showed within-group reductions in days of compulsory admission and in non-suicidal self-injury. The BA group also improved more in the mobility domain of daily functioning. [[Westling2019]]
- In 4-year follow-up of 62 BA participants, mean BA use fell from about 8 days per 6 months to 3–4 days. A small cluster (n = 8) kept using it heavily, reported more impairment and was strongly satisfied with BA. [[Daukantaite2025]]
- A mixed-methods review of 6 brief-admission studies, all European and all alongside community treatment, found conflicting evidence on readmission. It judged BA acceptable and possibly effective when preplanned. [[Tan2025]]
- In a joint crisis plan feasibility RCT (88 adults with BPD), the plans did not significantly reduce the proportion who self-harmed (OR 1.9, 0.53–6.5) or self-harm frequency (rate ratio 0.74, 0.34–1.63) at 6 months. [[Borschmann2013]]
- In 41 joint crisis plans, people with BPD asked to be treated with dignity and respect and to receive emotional and practical support. Some wanted contact during crises and others wanted to be left alone. Some stated refusals of particular medicines or of involuntary treatment. [[Borschmann2014]]
- A 2022 Cochrane review (2 RCTs, 213 participants: joint crisis plans and brief admission) found no clear benefit over usual care on any main outcome, with low- to very-low-certainty evidence. [[MonkCunliffe2022]]
- NICE CG78: care plans should include a crisis plan that names likely triggers, effective self-management strategies, and how to reach services, including out-of-hours and crisis team numbers. Share it with the GP and the person. [[NICE2009]]
- NICE CG78: after a crisis, update the crisis plan and care plan with the person, and with family or carers if possible, including a review of what triggered the crisis. [[NICE2009]]
- NICE CG78: before discharge from secondary care, agree a plan with the person (and family where possible) for managing distress, coping with future crises and re-engaging with services, and inform the GP. [[NICE2009]]

### nursing · Observation, de-escalation and the ward environment
- NICE NG225: mental health and acute ward staff should decide the need for close observation case by case, taking the person's views into account. Observation should be by skilled, trained staff, with informed consent or within a legal framework, and reviewed regularly. [[NICE2022SelfHarm]]
- De-escalation is defined as "a collective term for a range of interwoven staff-delivered components comprising communication, self-regulation, assessment, actions, and safety maintenance". Its aim is to reduce aggression while minimising coercion. [[Hallett2017]]
- In that concept analysis (79 studies), mental health settings and nursing dominated, but none of the five proposed de-escalation theories had empirical support. [[Hallett2017]]
- In the Safewards cluster RCT (31 wards, 15 hospitals), simple interventions to improve staff–patient relationships reduced conflict events by 15% (95% CI 5.7–23.7%) and containment events by 23.2% (9.9–35.5%). [[Bowers2015]]

### nursing · Medicines on the ward and in the community
- NICE CG78: do not use drug treatment specifically for BPD or for its individual symptoms or behaviours. [[NICE2009,Kendall2009]]
- NICE CG78: in a crisis, short-term sedative medication may be considered cautiously, for no longer than 1 week. Choose a drug with a low side-effect profile, low addictive potential, low misuse potential and relative safety in overdose. [[NICE2009]]
- NICE CG78: "prescribe fewer tablets more frequently if there is a significant risk of overdose". Make sure prescribers agree on the drug and name one primary prescriber. [[NICE2009]]
- NICE CG78: review medicines in people without a diagnosed comorbid illness with the aim of reducing and stopping unnecessary drugs. Polypharmacy was a risk factor for obesity in the McLean cohort. [[NICE2009,Frankenburg2006Obesity]]

### nursing · Structured groups nurses can deliver
- STEPPS is a 20-week manualised group combining cognitive-behavioural skills training with a systems component that involves the person's support network. In an RCT (n = 124), STEPPS plus usual care improved ZAN-BPD total scores, impulsivity, negative affect, mood and global functioning more than usual care alone. It did not reduce suicide attempts, self-harm or hospitalisation. [[Blum2008]]
- In that RCT, fewer STEPPS participants had emergency department visits during treatment and follow-up. Drop-out was high in both arms. [[Blum2008]]
- In a Dutch RCT (n = 79), STEPPS plus limited individual therapy reduced general and BPD-specific symptoms and improved quality of life more than usual care. There was no difference in impulsive or self-harming behaviour. [[Bos2010]]
- Nurses value a coherent therapeutic framework, and DBT skills training for nursing staff has reduced burnout and stigma in an uncontrolled study. (Early evidence) [[Dickens2016,Haynos2016]]

### nursing · Measures nurses use
- The BSL-23 is a 23-item self-rating short form of the 95-item Borderline Symptom List. It was developed from 379 patients and tested in 659 patients with BPD. Correlation with the long form was 0.958–0.963, and Cronbach's alpha 0.935–0.969. [[Bohus2009]]
- The BSL-23 detected change after 3 months of DBT (d = 0.47), slightly more than the long form (d = 0.38). [[Bohus2009]]
- BSL-23 severity grades (mean item score 0–4): none or low 0–0.3; mild 0.3–0.7; moderate 0.7–1.7; high 1.7–2.7; very high 2.7–3.5; extremely high 3.5–4. [[Kleindienst2020]]
- High to extremely high BSL-23 scores were found in 70.0% of treatment-seeking people with BPD, 17.6% of clinical controls and 0.0% of healthy controls. A cut-off of 1.50 best separated BPD from other mental disorders. [[Kleindienst2020]]
- The ZAN-BPD is a clinician-rated scale of the 9 DSM criteria over the past week. Each criterion is scored 0–4, for a total of 0–36. Cronbach's alpha was 0.85, and the scale detected change over 7–10 days. [[Zanarini2003]]

### nursing · Staff support and supervision
- NICE CG78: professionals working with people with BPD should have routine access to supervision and staff support. Community teams should manage risk as a whole multidisciplinary team, with good supervision, especially for less experienced staff. [[NICE2009]]
- NICE NG225: staff working with people who self-harm should have regular, high-quality supervision that addresses the emotional impact on staff, promotes compassionate care and includes reflective practice. [[NICE2022SelfHarm]]
- NICE CG78: mental health professionals in secondary care, including inpatient services, should be trained to diagnose BPD, assess risk and need, and provide treatment according to the guideline. [[NICE2009]]

### Exam hooks
- Carers of people with BPD report burden about half an SD higher than carers of people with other serious mental illnesses. [[Bailey2013]]
- Family Connections: 12 weeks, DBT-based, led by trained family members. It reduced burden and grief in uncontrolled and controlled studies, but was not superior to active TAU in the one RCT. [[Hoffman2005,Flynn2017,Guillen2024]]
- More knowledge about BPD alone does not lower family burden. In one study it went with more burden and hostility, so the source and accuracy of information matter. [[Hoffman2003]]
- NICE CG78: offer a crisis resolution or home treatment team before acute admission, and agree the length and purpose of any admission in advance. [[NICE2009]]
- NICE CG78: no drug treatment specifically for BPD. Crisis sedation for no longer than 1 week, and "fewer tablets more frequently" when overdose risk is significant. [[NICE2009]]
- NICE NG225: do not use risk tools or low/medium/high risk stratification to predict suicide or to decide who is offered treatment. [[NICE2022SelfHarm]]
- Brief admission by self-referral did not reduce total hospital days compared with usual care in its RCT (n = 125). [[Westling2019]]
- Joint crisis plans and brief admission: Cochrane 2022 found no clear benefit over usual care (low-certainty evidence). [[MonkCunliffe2022]]
- Nurses hold the poorest attitudes of the professions toward people with BPD, and training effects are largest for knowledge, not feelings. [[Dickens2016Interventions]]
- STEPPS is a 20-week group that includes the person's support system. It improved BPD symptoms but not self-harm or hospitalisation. [[Blum2008]]
- BSL-23 severity bands run from 0 to 4. A mean score of 1.50 best separated BPD from other disorders. [[Kleindienst2020]]
- ZAN-BPD: clinician-rated, 9 criteria × 0–4, total 0–36, past-week timeframe. [[Zanarini2003]]
- Remission is far more common than recovery: at 16 years, remission was 78–99% but recovery (which includes work or study) was 40–60%. [[Zanarini2012]]
- People with BPD were 3 times more likely than other personality-disorder patients to receive US disability income, but SSDI status was fluid: 40% came off it. [[Zanarini2009]]
- Valproate: about 10% major malformations in EURAP, and an IQ 9.7 points lower at age 6 after high-dose exposure in the womb. The label says avoid in women of childbearing potential unless alternatives have failed. [[Tomson2018,Baker2015,FDA-Depakote]]
- About 45% of people with BPD have a current substance use disorder. About 1 in 5 people with an SUD meet BPD criteria. [[Trull2018SUD]]
- IPV: antisocial and borderline personality disorders show the most robust links with both perpetration and victimisation. Assess safety in both directions. [[Collison2021]]
- Safewards reduced conflict by 15% and containment by 23.2% on acute wards. [[Bowers2015]]

### In plain words
- Caring for someone with BPD can be exhausting and lonely. Many family members feel grief, worry and burnout, and that is a common reaction, not a failure. [[Bailey2014]]
- Family skills courses such as Family Connections teach you about BPD and how to stay calm, listen and validate. Families who take part often feel less burdened and less grief. [[Hoffman2005,Flynn2017]]
- Good information matters more than lots of information. Look for sources that are accurate and hopeful. [[Hoffman2003,vanSchie2024]]
- Recovery is common. Most people with BPD stop meeting the criteria over time, though getting back to steady work and relationships can take longer. [[Zanarini2012,Gunderson2011]]
- Work and study are possible, and supported-employment programmes help people with personality disorders find jobs about as often as people with other mental illnesses. [[Juurlink2022]]
- If you are pregnant or planning a baby, ask your doctor about every medicine you take. Some, like valproate, carry serious risks for the baby. [[FDA-Depakote]]
- Being a parent with BPD is possible. Mother-and-baby skills groups exist and have helped mothers feel calmer and more confident. [[SvedWilliams2021,Francis2023]]
- Physical health matters too: sleep, exercise, smoking, alcohol and regular check-ups are part of getting well. [[Keuroghlian2013,Winsper2017Sleep]]
- If a relationship involves hurting or being hurt, you deserve to be safe. Health workers can help you find support without judging you. [[NICE2022SelfHarm]]
- You should never be turned away from care because of your diagnosis or because you have self-harmed. [[NICE2009]]
- A crisis plan written with your care team lists your warning signs, what helps, and who to call. [[NICE2009]]
- Talking with peers who have lived through BPD can bring hope and connection. [[Barr2020]]
- If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

## Open questions and controversies
- Does Family Connections beat active alternatives? Uncontrolled and non-randomised studies show reductions in burden and grief [[Hoffman2005,Flynn2017,Lanfredi2025]]. The one RCT against active TAU of similar length found no significant difference on burden, though effect sizes favoured FC [[Guillen2024]]. Status: promising, not yet proven superior.
- Is a brief in-person carer group worth adding to online psychoeducation for families of young people? An Orygen RCT found no added benefit [[Betts2023]], while a 10-week group versus waitlist improved empowerment and expressed emotion over 12 months [[Grenyer2019]]. Status: the comparator matters, and the "dose" of carer support is unsettled.
- Does brief admission by self-referral reduce hospital use? The RCT found no difference in total days [[Westling2019]]. A mixed-methods review judges it acceptable and possibly effective when preplanned [[Tan2025]], and Cochrane rates the evidence low certainty [[MonkCunliffe2022]]. Status: open. Ongoing RCTs were noted in 2022.
- Do joint crisis plans help? The feasibility RCT found no effect on self-harm but high face validity [[Borschmann2013]]. People with BPD clearly want individually tailored plans [[Borschmann2014]]. Status: underpowered evidence.
- Do training programmes change nurses' behaviour, not just reported attitudes? Workshops improve self-reported attitudes [[Shanks2011,Knaak2015]], but gains are mostly cognitive, few studies are controlled, and the link with practice is barely studied [[Dickens2016Interventions,Dickens2022]]. Status: open.
- Is BPD itself, or co-occurring conditions, behind violence? Population data suggest comorbidity explains serious violence, and only IPV is linked with a categorical BPD diagnosis [[Gonzalez2016]]. Meta-analysis finds robust links with both perpetration and victimisation [[Collison2021]]. Status: framing should cover both directions and the role of anger and impulsivity traits.
- Does treating BPD improve physical health? Remission and recovery go with fewer medical illnesses and better lifestyle [[Frankenburg2004,Keuroghlian2013]], but these are associations, and the direction of causation is unknown. Status: no trial evidence.
- Does social media help or harm? TikTok offers validation and community but carries misinformation and self-diagnosis [[Evoy2026,Theriault2026]]. Status: descriptive evidence only.
- Can parenting interventions break intergenerational risk? Mother-Infant DBT improves maternal symptoms, but observed mother–infant interaction often stays of concern [[SvedWilliams2021]], and controlled trials are scarce [[May2023]]. Status: early.
- Does supported employment (IPS) work specifically for BPD? Registry data show similar job rates for personality disorders and other diagnoses [[Juurlink2022]], but no completed BPD-specific RCT was found; INVEST is under way [[Chanen2020INVEST]]. Status: open.
