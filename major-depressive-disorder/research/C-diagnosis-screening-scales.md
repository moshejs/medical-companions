## Findings

### diagnosis · DSM-5-TR criteria for a major depressive episode
- A major depressive episode requires five or more of nine symptoms present within the same 2-week period, and at least one of them must be depressed mood or loss of interest or pleasure [[APA2022DSM5TR,Tolentino2018]].
- The nine symptoms are: (1) depressed mood, (2) markedly diminished interest or pleasure, (3) appetite or weight change, (4) insomnia or hypersomnia, (5) psychomotor agitation or retardation, (6) fatigue or loss of energy, (7) feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt, (8) diminished ability to think or concentrate, or indecisiveness, and (9) recurrent thoughts of death or suicidal thoughts [[Tolentino2018,Fried2015Profiles,Kennedy2008Core]].
- The criterion wording asks that each symptom be present "nearly every day", with depressed mood and loss of interest present "most of the day, nearly every day" [[Kennedy2008Core]].
- The weight criterion gives the example of a change of more than 5% of body weight in a month, when not dieting [[Kennedy2008Core]].
- Psychomotor change must be observable by others, not only a subjective feeling of restlessness or being slowed down [[Kennedy2008Core]].
- Symptoms that are clearly due to a general medical condition are not counted towards the five [[Kennedy2008Core]].
- In children and adolescents the mood criterion can be met by irritable rather than depressed mood [[Kennedy2008Core,Thapar2012]].
- DSM-5 widened the wording of the core mood criterion to include hopelessness, which may broaden the diagnosis [[Uher2014]].
- The remaining criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment, exclude episodes attributable to a substance or another medical condition or better explained by a psychotic disorder, and require that there has never been a manic or hypomanic episode [[APA2022DSM5TR]] (unverified).
- DSM-5 placed major depressive disorder in a "Depressive disorders" chapter separate from "Bipolar disorders", dividing what had been "Mood disorders" [[Uher2014]].
- The symptom threshold is polythetic: there are 227 possible ways to meet the symptom criteria (any five or more of nine, including a core symptom) [[Zimmerman2015Combinations]].
- Among more than 1,500 psychiatric outpatients with DSM-IV major depressive disorder, 170 of the 227 combinations occurred; the commonest was all nine criteria (10.1%, n=157), and nine combinations accounted for more than 40% of diagnoses [[Zimmerman2015Combinations]].
- In 3,703 STAR*D outpatients, 1,030 unique symptom profiles were found; 864 (83.9%) were shared by five or fewer people, 501 (48.6%) by one person, and the commonest profile had a frequency of 1.8% (Established) [[Fried2015Profiles]].
- STAR*D participants reported a mean of 6.03 symptoms (SD 2.75); the three most endorsed were sad mood, loss of energy and concentration problems, and the least severe were hypersomnia, suicidal ideation and psychomotor retardation [[Fried2015Profiles]].
- In 1,538 psychiatric outpatients every DSM-IV symptom criterion was associated with the diagnosis, and insomnia, fatigue and impaired concentration (shared with other disorders) performed about as well as criteria unique to depression [[Zimmerman2006]].
- In the same study five items (increased weight, decreased weight, psychomotor retardation, indecisiveness and suicidal thoughts) were not independently associated with the diagnosis once symptom covariation was controlled [[Zimmerman2006]].
- With semi-structured interviews, agreement between mental health professionals on a diagnosis of major depression was substantial to almost perfect (kappa 0.64–0.93); non-standardised interviews gave kappa 0.55–0.74 [[Williams2002JAMA]].

### diagnosis · Grief, the bereavement exclusion and prolonged grief disorder
- From DSM-III (1980) to DSM-IV, a major depressive episode was not diagnosed if symptoms were "better accounted for by bereavement", unless they were severe and very impairing [[Zisook2012]].
- DSM-5 replaced that operationalised exclusion with a call for clinical judgement in distinguishing a normal reaction to a significant loss from a disorder needing clinical attention [[Uher2014]].
- The review behind the change concluded that bereavement-related depression resembles other major depressive episodes in genetic influence, personal and family history, comorbidity, chronicity or recurrence, and response to antidepressant medicines [[Zisook2012]].
- Critics note that removing the exclusion makes the diagnosis less objective and complicates research on adversity and depression [[Uher2014]].
- In NESARC-III, 12.9% of adults who had ever had DSM-5 major depressive disorder had all of their episodes characterised as bereavement under the DSM-IV rule [[Hasin2018]].
- DSM-5-TR (2022) added prolonged grief disorder as a new diagnosis: continued presence, at least 12 months after the death of a loved one, of intense yearning for or persistent preoccupation with the person who died, with other grief symptoms severe enough to impair functioning [[First2022]].
- The DSM-5-TR prolonged grief criteria require the death at least 12 months ago (6 months for children and adolescents), at least one of two core symptoms, at least 3 of 8 additional symptoms nearly every day for at least the last month, and a reaction that clearly exceeds cultural norms and is not better explained by major depressive disorder [[Prigerson2021]].
- ICD-11 prolonged grief disorder requires a grief response persisting for more than 6 months [[Reed2019]].
- Prolonged grief disorder was distinct from major depressive disorder in the Yale validation sample (phi = 0.25) and far more stable over time (r = 0.86) [[Prigerson2021]].
- DSM-5-TR also added free-standing symptom codes so that clinicians can record suicidal behaviour and non-suicidal self-injury whatever the diagnosis [[First2022]].

### diagnosis · ICD-11 compared with ICD-10 and DSM-5
- ICD-11 requires a minimum of five of ten symptoms for a depressive episode, where ICD-10 required four of nine; the change increases consistency with DSM-5 [[Reed2019]].
- ICD-11 groups depressive symptoms into three clusters (affective, cognitive and neurovegetative) and requires almost daily depressed mood or diminished interest lasting at least 2 weeks [[Reed2019]].
- ICD-11 added hopelessness as a cognitive symptom, and fatigue alone no longer counts as an entry-level symptom [[Reed2019]].
- ICD-11 depressive episodes are characterised by severity (mild, moderate or severe), with qualifiers including melancholic features [[Reed2019]].
- ICD-11 moved mixed depressive and anxiety disorder from the anxiety disorders into the depressive disorders [[Reed2019]].
- ICD-11 lists premenstrual dysphoric disorder primarily under diseases of the genitourinary system and cross-lists it with the depressive disorders [[Reed2019]].
- ICD-11 has no atypical-features specifier, and its melancholic qualifier omits the "distinct quality of depressed mood" and excessive guilt used in DSM-5 [[Maj2020]].

### diagnosis · Severity
- DSM-5 calls an episode mild when "few, if any, symptoms in excess of those required to make the diagnosis are present" and severe when the number of symptoms is "substantially in excess"; it does not explicitly define moderate [[Maj2020]].
- ICD-11 severe episodes include "complete or near-complete inability to function in some domain" [[Maj2020]].
- CANMAT 2023 rates severity on symptom severity and degree of functional impairment, "whichever is higher", and recommends a validated rating scale to quantify them [[Lam2024CANMAT]].
- In NESARC-III, with severity defined by symptom count (mild 5, moderate 6–7, severe 8–9 symptoms), lifetime major depressive disorder was mild in 10.8%, moderate in 39.7% and severe in 49.5% [[Hasin2018]].
- In the NCS-R, 12-month cases classified with the QIDS-SR were mild in 10.4%, moderate in 38.6%, severe in 38.0% and very severe in 12.9% [[Kessler2003]].
- Severity bands for the 17-item HAM-D derived from 627 outpatients were: no depression 0–7, mild 8–16, moderate 17–23, severe 24 or more [[Zimmerman2013HAMD]].
- Self-report tools classify severity differently from one another, and one study found that anhedonia with non-somatic symptoms marked severe episodes while depressed mood with somatic symptoms marked moderate ones (Early evidence) [[Maj2020,Tolentino2018]].

### symptoms · Specifiers and their frequency
- DSM-5-TR episode specifiers are melancholic, atypical, psychotic and catatonic features (carried over from DSM-IV-TR) plus anxious distress and mixed features; course specifiers are seasonal pattern and peripartum onset [[Lam2024CANMAT]].
- Anxious distress requires at least two of five symptoms: feeling keyed up or tense, unusual restlessness, difficulty concentrating because of worry, fear that something awful may happen, and feeling that one might lose control [[Maj2020]].
- The anxious/distressed specifier applied to 74.6% of US adults with lifetime DSM-5 major depressive disorder (NESARC-III, n=7,432) [[Hasin2018]].
- In STAR*D, 53.2% of 2,876 outpatients had anxious depression (HAM-D anxiety/somatisation factor score of 7 or more); remission was less likely and slower in this group [[Fava2008]].
- Mixed features requires at least three manic or hypomanic symptoms from a list of seven during the depressive episode [[Maj2020]].
- The mixed-features specifier applied to 15.5% of adults with lifetime major depressive disorder in NESARC-III [[Hasin2018]].
- Melancholic features require loss of pleasure or non-reactive mood plus at least three of: a distinct quality of mood, morning worsening, early-morning waking, psychomotor change, appetite or weight loss, and excessive guilt [[Maj2020]].
- Melancholic features were present in 23.5% of 2,875 STAR*D participants and were associated with higher severity and more psychiatric comorbidity [[McGrath2008]].
- Atypical features centre on mood reactivity with increased appetite and sleep, marked loss of energy and sensitivity to rejection [[Maj2020]].
- Over 18% of the first 1,500 STAR*D outpatients met atypical criteria; they were more often female, with earlier onset and more anxiety [[Novick2005]].
- Defined only by hypersomnia or hyperphagia, lifetime major depressive episode with atypical features had a US prevalence of 10.23% against 6.31% without (NESARC 2001–2002); onset was earlier (27.45 vs 31.71 years) and bipolar I disorder more frequent (13.99% vs 8.51%) [[Blanco2012]].
- Psychotic features are delusions or hallucinations occurring during the depressive episode [[Maj2020]].
- In a five-country European general-population sample (n=18,980), 18.5% of people meeting criteria for a major depressive episode had psychotic features; point prevalence was 0.4% with and 2.0% without psychotic features [[Ohayon2002]].
- Catatonia occurred in a mean of 9.0% of clinical samples across 74 studies (95% CI 6.9–11.7), with high heterogeneity [[Solmi2018]].
- "With peripartum onset" replaced DSM-IV "postpartum onset" and now includes onset during pregnancy; perinatal screening is covered in pass I [[Uher2014]].
- Seasonal affective disorder was first described in 1984 in 29 patients with recurrent depressions at the same time each year, typically with hypersomnia, overeating and carbohydrate craving; most had a bipolar disorder [[Rosenthal1984]].
- The commonest seasonal pattern is autumn or winter onset with spring or summer resolution [[Maj2020]].
- A questionnaire survey in four US areas from New Hampshire to Florida found winter seasonal affective disorder significantly more common at more northern latitudes, mainly in people over 35; summer seasonal affective disorder showed no latitude gradient [[Rosen1990]].
- Across 20 retrospective population studies, prevalence estimates of seasonal affective disorder ranged from 0% to 9.7%, higher at northern latitudes but varying across ethnic groups [[Magnusson2000]].
- Persistent depressive disorder (DSM-5) combines dysthymia, with or without superimposed major depressive episodes, chronic major depression, and recurrent major depression without recovery between episodes [[Schramm2020,Uher2014]].
- Persistent depressive disorder is often more disabling than episodic major depression and is easily missed until a major depressive episode is superimposed [[Schramm2020]].
- The minimum duration for dysthymia in children and adolescents is 1 year [[Thapar2012]].

### symptoms · How depression presents in different groups
- Men: in the NCS-R, men reported more anger attacks or aggression, substance misuse and risk-taking than women; on a scale combining these with traditional symptoms, 30.6% of men and 33.3% of women met criteria (P = .57) (Early evidence) [[Martin2013]].
- Older adults: a meta-analysis of 11 studies found more agitation, hypochondriasis and general and gastrointestinal somatic symptoms, and less guilt and loss of sexual interest, than in younger adults with major depression [[Hegeman2012]].
- Adolescents: vegetative symptoms (appetite and weight change, loss of energy, insomnia) were more common in adolescents, and anhedonia and concentration problems more common in adults with major depressive disorder [[Rice2019Profiles]].
- Adolescents: one-year prevalence is about 4–5% in mid to late adolescence against under 1% in children; after puberty there is a female preponderance of about 2:1, and depression is missed more often than in adults [[Thapar2012]].
- Adolescents: two-thirds have at least one comorbid psychiatric disorder [[Thapar2012]].
- Across cultures: in the WHO primary care study (15 centres, 14 countries), 1,146 patients met criteria for major depression; 69% (range 45–95% by centre) presented with somatic symptoms only, half reported multiple unexplained somatic symptoms and 11% denied psychological symptoms on direct questioning [[Simon1999]].
- Somatic presentation was more common where patients had no ongoing relationship with a primary care physician (OR 1.8, 95% CI 1.2–2.7), and the frequencies of psychological and physical symptoms were similar across centres [[Simon1999]].
- Somatisation is ubiquitous, not peculiar to non-Western groups; somatic symptoms act as cultural idioms of distress [[Kirmayer2001]].
- Fatigue: 73% of almost 2,000 patients in the six-country DEPRES II study "felt tired" [[Kennedy2008Core]].
- Cognitive deficits (executive function, attention, memory, processing speed) are a core dimension of the syndrome [[Maj2020]].

### diagnosis · Recognition in primary care
- In a meta-analysis of 41 studies (50,371 patients), general practitioners correctly identified depression in 47.3% of cases; weighted sensitivity was 50.1% and specificity 81.3% [[Mitchell2009Lancet]].
- At a prevalence of 21.9% that gave a positive predictive value of 42.0% and a negative predictive value of 85.8%: per 100 unselected patients, about 15 false positives, 10 missed cases and 10 identified cases [[Mitchell2009Lancet]].
- Accuracy improved when patients were re-assessed over 3–12 months and not judged at a single visit [[Mitchell2009Lancet]].
- The USPSTF estimates that only about 50% of people with major depression are identified [[USPSTF2023Depression]].

### diagnosis · Differential diagnosis
- Bipolar depression: in a 2000 survey of 600 support-group members with bipolar disorder, 69% reported having been misdiagnosed, most often with unipolar depression; over one third waited 10 years or more for an accurate diagnosis (self-report) [[Hirschfeld2003]].
- Bipolar depression: of 5,635 adults with a current major depressive episode in a multinational study, 16.0% met DSM-IV-TR criteria for bipolar disorder and 47.0% met broader "bipolarity specifier" criteria [[Angst2011BRIDGE]].
- Conversion: 22.5% of adults and adolescents with major depressive disorder followed for a mean of 12–18 years developed bipolar disorder, with the greatest risk in the first 5 years [[Ratheesh2017]].
- Predictors of conversion were family history of bipolar disorder (OR 2.89), earlier age at onset, and psychotic symptoms (OR 4.76) [[Ratheesh2017]].
- Features pointing to a bipolar course in 1,036 people were psychosis, diurnal mood variation and hypersomnia during depressive episodes, and a greater number of shorter episodes [[Forty2008]].
- The Mood Disorder Questionnaire, at 7 or more items, had sensitivity 0.73 and specificity 0.90 in 198 mood-clinic outpatients [[Hirschfeld2000MDQ]].
- Overdiagnosis also occurs: fewer than half of 700 outpatients who reported a previous bipolar diagnosis had it confirmed on structured interview [[Zimmerman2008]].
- Adjustment disorder: DSM-5 allows onset within 3 months of a stressor (ICD-11 within 1 month), and symptoms should not persist more than 6 months after the stressor and its consequences have ended [[ODonnell2019]].
- Hypothyroidism: across 25 studies (348,014 participants), hypothyroidism was associated with clinical depression (OR 1.30, 95% CI 1.08–1.57), more strongly for overt (OR 1.77) than subclinical (OR 1.13) disease; the effect is smaller than previously assumed [[Bode2021]].
- Anaemia: among 44,173 healthy adults, those with depression were more likely to have anaemia (adjusted OR 1.36, 95% CI 1.18–1.57), with a dose-response by depression severity [[Vulser2016]].
- Vitamin B12 and folate: in older adults, low B12 (OR 1.20, 95% CI 1.02–1.42) and low folate (OR 1.23, 95% CI 1.07–1.43) were associated with depression [[Petridou2016]].
- Obstructive sleep apnoea: prevalence may be increased in major depressive disorder, on heterogeneous studies at high risk of bias; treating both conditions is advised (Early evidence) [[Gupta2015]].
- Medicines: 37.2% of US adults used at least one prescription medicine with depression as a potential adverse effect; more than 200 such medicines were identified, including some antihypertensives, hormonal agents, proton pump inhibitors, analgesics, anticonvulsants and corticosteroids [[Qato2018]].
- Among adults not taking antidepressants, PHQ-9 depression (score 10 or more) was present in 4.7% of those using none of these medicines and about 15% of those using three or more (cross-sectional; not proof of cause) [[Qato2018]].
- Dementia versus depression with reversible cognitive impairment ("pseudodementia"): among 57 older inpatients, irreversible dementia developed in 43% of those whose depression came with reversible dementia against 12% with depression alone over a mean 33.8 months [[Alexopoulos1993]].
- In a 5–7 year follow-up, 71.4% of older patients with depressive pseudodementia had developed dementia against 18.2% of those cognitively intact (relative risk 3.93) [[SaezFonseca2007]].
- Premenstrual dysphoric disorder affects 2–5% of premenopausal women; symptoms are confined to the final week before menses and are minimal or absent the week after, core symptoms are affective lability and irritability, and prospective daily ratings help exclude premenstrual worsening of depression [[Epperson2012]].
- Burnout: a review of 92 studies judged the burnout–depression distinction conceptually fragile [[Bianchi2015]]; a meta-analysis found a moderate correlation (r = 0.52) and concluded they are different constructs [[Koutsimani2019]].
- Baseline tests: CANMAT 2023 describes a complete blood count and thyroid-stimulating hormone as low-cost tests that can help rule out anaemia and thyroid dysfunction; other tests only when history and examination suggest another condition, with no support for routine ECG, EEG or neuroimaging [[Lam2024CANMAT]].
- CANMAT 2023 states that the differential diagnosis should include psychiatric and non-psychiatric medical conditions and that a comprehensive diagnostic assessment is the first step of care [[Lam2024CANMAT]].

### screening · PHQ-9
- The PHQ-9 scores each of nine DSM criteria over the last 2 weeks from 0 ("not at all") through 1 ("several days") and 2 ("more than half the days") to 3 ("nearly every day"), for a total of 0–27 [[Kroenke2001PHQ9]].
- The items, in order, cover little interest or pleasure, feeling down or hopeless, sleep, tiredness, appetite, feeling bad about oneself, concentration, moving slowly or being restless, and thoughts of being better off dead or of self-harm [[Kroenke2001PHQ9]].
- A tenth, unscored question asks how difficult the problems have made work, home life or getting along with others (not difficult at all, somewhat, very, extremely difficult) [[Kroenke2001PHQ9]].
- Severity bands: 0–4 minimal, 5–9 mild, 10–14 moderate, 15–19 moderately severe, 20–27 severe [[Kroenke2001PHQ9]].
- In the validation study (6,000 patients; 580 re-interviewed), a score of 10 or more had sensitivity 88% and specificity 88% for major depression; area under the ROC curve was 0.95 [[Kroenke2001PHQ9]].
- Cronbach's alpha was 0.89 in primary care and 0.86 in obstetrics-gynaecology clinics; the correlation between clinic and telephone administration within 48 hours was 0.84 [[Kroenke2001PHQ9]].
- Mean scores were 17.1 with major depression, 10.4 with other depressive disorder and 3.3 with no depressive disorder [[Kroenke2001PHQ9]].
- Positive likelihood ratios by band were 0.04 (0–4), 0.5 (5–9), 2.6 (10–14), 8.4 (15–19) and 36.8 (20–27) [[Kroenke2001PHQ9]].
- The PHQ-9 diagnostic algorithm counts a symptom when rated "more than half the days" or more, except item 9, which counts if present at all; five or more symptoms including a core item suggest major depression [[Kroenke2001PHQ9]].
- Individual participant data meta-analysis (58 studies, 17,357 participants, 2,312 cases): at a cut-off of 10 or above against semi-structured interviews (29 studies, 6,725 participants), sensitivity was 0.88 (95% CI 0.83–0.92) and specificity 0.85 (0.82–0.88) [[Levis2019PHQ9]].
- Against fully structured interviews sensitivity at 10 or above was 0.70, and against the MINI 0.77; specificity was similar (0.84 and 0.87) [[Levis2019PHQ9]].
- The 2021 update (100 studies, 44,503 participants) gave sensitivity 0.85 (0.79–0.89) and specificity 0.85 (0.82–0.87) at 10 or above against semi-structured interviews [[Negeri2021]].
- Specificity was higher in men (median 3–4%) and in people aged 60 or older (median 5–6%); the authors judged the differences too small for subgroup cut-offs [[Negeri2021,Levis2019PHQ9]].
- An earlier meta-analysis of 18 studies found no substantial difference in pooled accuracy for cut-offs between 8 and 11 [[Manea2012]].
- The PHQ-9 is not a prevalence measure: across 44 studies, 24.6% scored 10 or more while 12.1% had major depression on structured clinical interview (pooled difference 11.9%) [[Levis2020Prevalence]].
- Monitoring: in 434 older adults the minimal clinically important difference for individual change was 5 points [[Lowe2004PHQ9]].
- Adolescents (13–17 years, n=442): a cut-off of 11 or more gave sensitivity 89.5% and specificity 77.5% (AUC 0.88); 10 or more gave 89.5% and 72.1% [[Richardson2010]].

### screening · PHQ-2 and the Whooley questions
- The PHQ-2 is the first two PHQ-9 items ("little interest or pleasure in doing things"; "feeling down, depressed, or hopeless"), each scored 0–3, total 0–6 [[Levis2020PHQ2]].
- In the original study a PHQ-2 score of 3 or more had sensitivity 83% and specificity 92% [[Kroenke2003]].
- Pooled across 48 semi-structured-interview studies, PHQ-2 of 2 or more had sensitivity 0.91 and specificity 0.67; 3 or more had 0.72 and 0.85 [[Levis2020PHQ2]].
- PHQ-2 of 2 or more followed by PHQ-9 of 10 or more had sensitivity 0.82 and specificity 0.87, against 0.86 and 0.85 for the PHQ-9 alone, and cut the number of full PHQ-9s needed by 57% [[Levis2020PHQ2]].
- CANMAT 2023 recommends the PHQ-2 (positive at 2 or more) followed, if positive, by the PHQ-9 (positive at 10 or more), for people with risk factors [[Lam2024CANMAT]].
- The two Whooley questions ask whether, during the past month, the person has often been bothered by feeling down, depressed or hopeless, and by little interest or pleasure in doing things (yes/no; a yes to either is positive) [[Bosanquet2015]].
- In 536 patients a yes to either question had sensitivity 96% and specificity 57% [[Whooley1997]]; pooled across 10 studies, sensitivity was 0.95 and specificity 0.65 [[Bosanquet2015]].
- Across primary care case-finding questionnaires the median positive likelihood ratio was 3.3 and negative 0.19, with no significant difference between instruments [[Williams2002JAMA]].

### screening · Item 9 and follow-up of suicidal thoughts
- PHQ-9 item 9 asks about "thoughts that you would be better off dead, or of hurting yourself in some way"; in 207,265 questionnaires from 84,418 outpatients, 77% answered "not at all", 14% "several days", 5% "more than half the days" and 4% "nearly every day" [[Simon2013]].
- Cumulative one-year risk of a suicide attempt rose from about 0.4% ("not at all") to about 4% ("nearly every day"); risk of death by suicide rose from about 0.03% to about 0.3% (population figures, not individual predictions) [[Simon2013]].
- Excess risk emerged over days and kept growing for months, so the authors describe suicidal ideation as an enduring vulnerability, not only a short-term crisis [[Simon2013]].
- About one fifth of later attempts and deaths occurred in people who had answered "not at all" every time, so a zero on item 9 does not rule out risk [[Simon2013]].
- In 297,290 mental health outpatients the association held across age groups and persisted over two years [[Rossom2017]].
- A review found no study showing a significant increase in suicidal ideation after people were asked about suicide; asking may reduce ideation [[Dazzi2014]].
- CANMAT 2023: suicide risk assessment should be a routine part of the interview; validated risk scales have low sensitivity and predictive power and are used only alongside clinical judgement [[Lam2024CANMAT]].
- The USPSTF finds the evidence insufficient to assess screening for suicide risk in adults, and in children and adolescents (I statements) [[USPSTF2023Depression,USPSTF2022Youth]].

### screening · Recommendations and predictive value
- USPSTF 2023: screen for depression in adults, including pregnant and postpartum persons and adults 65 or older (B recommendation) [[USPSTF2023Depression]].
- USPSTF 2022: screen for major depressive disorder in adolescents aged 12–18 years (B recommendation); evidence is insufficient for children 11 or younger (I statement) [[USPSTF2022Youth]].
- The USPSTF found no evidence on the optimal screening interval; all positive results should lead to further assessment to confirm the diagnosis, and screening needs systems for accurate diagnosis, treatment and follow-up [[USPSTF2023Depression]].
- Instruments named by the USPSTF are the PHQ in adults, the CES-D, the Geriatric Depression Scale in older adults and the EPDS in pregnant and postpartum persons [[USPSTF2023Depression]].
- GLAD-PC recommends annual universal screening of young people aged 12 and over at health maintenance visits [[Zuckerbrot2018]].
- In adolescents the PHQ-A had sensitivity 0.73 and specificity 0.94 in one study [[USPSTF2022Youth]].
- Predictive value depends on prevalence: at a PHQ-9 cut-off of 10, positive predictive value ranged from 24% to 66% and negative predictive value from 96% to 99% as prevalence rose from 5% to 25% [[Levis2019PHQ9]].
- At 14% prevalence the positive predictive value was 49%, so about half of positive screens are false positives [[Levis2019PHQ9]].
- Worked example (calculated from sensitivity 0.88, specificity 0.85 at 10% prevalence): of 1,000 people screened, 88 true positives, 12 missed, 135 false positives and 765 true negatives, giving a positive predictive value of 39% and a negative predictive value of 98% [[Levis2019PHQ9]].
- Major depression was present in 10.1% of patients in the WHO primary care study and in 10% of participants pooled across PHQ accuracy studies [[Simon1999,Levis2020PHQ2]].

### screening · Clinician-rated scales: HAM-D and MADRS
- The Hamilton scale was published in 1960; its 17-item version has a possible range of 0–52 [[Hamilton1960,Hengartner2020MADRS]].
- On the HAM-D17, response is conventionally a reduction of 50% or more from baseline and remission a total of 7 or less [[Leucht2013HAMD]].
- Linking 43 trials (n=7,131), "much improved" on the Clinical Global Impression corresponded to a 50–60% HAM-D reduction and "very much improved" to 75–85%; "normal, not at all ill" corresponded to a HAM-D below 5 [[Leucht2013HAMD]].
- A review of 70 studies judged the HAM-D adequate in internal reliability but with poorly performing items, poor content validity and an unstable factor structure [[Bagby2004]].
- The MADRS was built in 1979 from the 10 items of a 65-item scale that changed most with treatment; it has a possible range of 0–60 [[Montgomery1979,Hengartner2020MADRS]].
- MADRS remission cut-offs differ: 10 or less corresponds to HAM-D 7 or less by regression [[Zimmerman2004Derivation]]; item-response linking puts HAM-D 7 at MADRS 8–9 [[Carmody2006]]; 9 or less matched a broad and 4 or less a narrow definition of remission [[Zimmerman2004MADRS]]; CANMAT uses 10 or less [[Lam2024CANMAT]].
- The MADRS showed about twice the precision of the HAM-D17 at average severity, and a HAM-D17 of 20 approximated a MADRS of 27 [[Carmody2006]].
- Seven common depression scales contain 52 distinct symptoms; mean content overlap is low (Jaccard 0.36), 40% of symptoms appear in only one scale and 12% in all seven [[Fried2017Scales]].

### screening · Other self-report scales
- BDI-II: 21 items rated 0–3 about the past 2 weeks; bands from the original authors are 0–13 minimal, 14–19 mild, 20–28 moderate and 29–63 severe [[Wang2013BDI]].
- BDI-II internal consistency averaged about 0.9 and retest reliability ranged from 0.73 to 0.96 across 118 studies; screening cut-offs varied by sample [[Wang2013BDI]].
- QIDS-SR16: a 16-item self-report derived from the 30-item IDS; alpha 0.86 and correlation 0.86 with the HAM-D24 in 596 outpatients; about 1.3 times the QIDS-SR16 total predicts the HAM-D17 total [[Rush2003QIDS]].
- STAR*D defined remission as HAM-D 7 or less (primary) or QIDS-SR 5 or less, and response as a 50% or greater fall in QIDS-SR [[Trivedi2006STARD]].
- Geriatric Depression Scale: developed in 1982 as a 30-item self-rating scale for older adults [[Yesavage1982]].
- GDS-15 in medical settings: sensitivity 84.3% and specificity 73.8% (GDS-30: 81.9% and 77.7%), not significantly affected by dementia [[Mitchell2010GDS]]; a later meta-analysis of 53 studies gave 86% and 79% [[Krishnamoorthy2020]].
- At a cut-off of 5 the USPSTF review cites GDS-15 sensitivity 0.94 (95% CI 0.85–0.98) and specificity 0.81 (0.70–0.89) [[USPSTF2023Depression]].
- EPDS (pointer; pass I covers perinatal care): a 10-item scale developed in 1987 [[Cox1987]]; a cut-off of 11 or higher maximised combined sensitivity (0.81) and specificity (0.88) [[Levis2020EPDS]].

### screening · Response, remission and measurement-based care
- Consensus definitions of remission, recovery, relapse and recurrence were proposed in 1991 to end inconsistent usage [[Frank1991]].
- The ACNP task force recommended ascribing remission after 3 consecutive weeks with neither sadness nor reduced interest and fewer than three of the other seven criterion symptoms, and recovery after at least 4 months of remission [[Rush2006ACNP]].
- The task force preferred remission to response as the end point, because response depends heavily on the baseline score [[Rush2006ACNP]].
- CANMAT 2023 defines early improvement as a reduction of 20% or more within 2–4 weeks, response as 50% or more improvement, and symptom remission as HAM-D 7 or less, MADRS 10 or less or PHQ-9 4 or less [[Lam2024CANMAT]].
- Measurement-based care means routinely giving validated scales, reviewing the scores with the patient and using them with clinical assessment to guide decisions; CANMAT suggests scales every 2–4 weeks in the acute phase [[Lam2024CANMAT]].
- In a randomised trial of 120 outpatients, measurement-based care gave higher response (86.9% vs 62.7%) and remission (73.8% vs 28.8%) at 24 weeks than standard care, and shorter time to remission (10.2 vs 19.2 weeks) [[Guo2015MBC]].
- A meta-analysis of 7 trials (2,019 participants) found higher remission with measurement-based care (OR 1.83, 95% CI 1.12–2.97) and better adherence (OR 1.68), but no significant difference in response (OR 1.66, 0.66–4.17) (Early evidence) [[Zhu2021MBC]].
- A review of 51 articles found that frequent, timely feedback of patient-reported symptoms during the visit improved outcomes, whereas one-time screening and infrequent assessment did not [[Fortney2017]].
- In STAR*D step 1 with measurement-based care, remission was 28% on the HAM-D and 33% on the QIDS-SR, and response 47% (group averages) [[Trivedi2006STARD]].
- Minimal important difference: estimates for the HAM-D17 range from 3 to 8 points (most likely 3–5) and for the MADRS and BDI-II from 3 to 9 points (most likely 3–6) [[Hengartner2022]].
- The average drug–placebo difference in trials is about 2 points on the HAM-D17 and 3 points on the MADRS [[Hengartner2020MADRS]]; a tentative threshold for clinical relevance of a standardised mean difference of 0.24 has also been proposed [[Cuijpers2014MID]].

### Exam hooks
- Five of nine symptoms in the same 2 weeks, one of which must be depressed mood or loss of interest or pleasure [[APA2022DSM5TR,Tolentino2018]].
- There are 227 symptom combinations that satisfy the criteria; 170 were seen in practice [[Zimmerman2015Combinations]].
- STAR*D: 1,030 unique symptom profiles among 3,703 outpatients [[Fried2015Profiles]].
- In children and adolescents irritable mood can stand in for depressed mood [[Kennedy2008Core,Thapar2012]].
- DSM-5 removed the bereavement exclusion; DSM-5-TR added prolonged grief disorder (12 months in adults, 6 months in children and adolescents) [[Uher2014,Prigerson2021]].
- ICD-11: five of ten symptoms; ICD-10: four of nine [[Reed2019]].
- PHQ-9: nine items, 0–3 each, total 0–27; bands start at 5, 10, 15 and 20 [[Kroenke2001PHQ9]].
- PHQ-9 at 10 or above: sensitivity 0.88, specificity 0.85 against semi-structured interview [[Levis2019PHQ9]].
- PHQ-2 at 2 or above is sensitive (0.91) but not specific (0.67); at 3 or above the figures are 0.72 and 0.85 [[Levis2020PHQ2]].
- HAM-D17 remission is 7 or less; response is a 50% reduction [[Leucht2013HAMD]].
- HAM-D17 severity: 8–16 mild, 17–23 moderate, 24 or more severe [[Zimmerman2013HAMD]].
- Anxious distress is the commonest specifier (74.6% lifetime in NESARC-III) [[Hasin2018]].
- About 18.5% of major depressive episodes in the community have psychotic features [[Ohayon2002]].
- 22.5% of people with major depressive disorder followed for 12–18 years converted to bipolar disorder; family history, early onset and psychosis predict it [[Ratheesh2017]].
- 69% of people with depression in primary care worldwide presented with somatic symptoms only [[Simon1999]].
- USPSTF: B for adults (2023) and for ages 12–18 (2022); I for suicide-risk screening [[USPSTF2023Depression,USPSTF2022Youth]].
- A positive screen is not a diagnosis: at 10% prevalence about 6 in 10 positive PHQ-9 screens are false positives [[Levis2019PHQ9]].
- Asking about suicide does not increase suicidal thoughts [[Dazzi2014]].

### In plain words
- Depression is diagnosed by a clinician who listens to the whole story. There is no blood test or scan for it [[Lam2024CANMAT]].
- Doctors look for at least five of nine symptoms lasting two weeks or more, including low mood or loss of interest [[Tolentino2018]].
- Two people with depression can have very different symptoms. One may sleep too much and another hardly at all [[Fried2015Profiles]].
- Many people first notice body symptoms such as tiredness, aches or poor sleep, and not sadness [[Simon1999]].
- Grief after a loss is normal and is not an illness. Depression can still happen during grief, and a clinician can help tell them apart [[Uher2014]].
- A questionnaire such as the PHQ-9 is a starting point for a conversation. A high score does not prove depression and a low score does not rule it out [[USPSTF2023Depression,Levis2020Prevalence]].
- About half of the people who score above the usual PHQ-9 line turn out not to have major depression when properly assessed [[Levis2019PHQ9]].
- Doctors often check a blood count and thyroid test, because anaemia and thyroid problems can look like depression [[Lam2024CANMAT]].
- Some common medicines can affect mood. Changes to any medicine are made with the prescriber [[Qato2018]].
- A doctor will ask about past periods of unusually high energy or little need for sleep, because bipolar disorder needs different care [[Forty2008,Angst2011BRIDGE]].
- Thoughts of suicide are a symptom that can be treated. Being asked about them does not put the idea in someone's head [[Dazzi2014]].
- Repeating a short questionnaire during treatment helps the care team see whether the plan is working [[Guo2015MBC,Fortney2017]].
- Teenagers with depression may seem irritable more than sad [[Thapar2012]].
- If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123) [[USPSTF2023Depression]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Is major depressive disorder one syndrome? Position A: the polythetic criteria hide very different conditions (227 combinations; 1,030 profiles), so symptom-level study is needed [[Fried2015Profiles,Zimmerman2015Combinations]]; position B: a small set of prototypical combinations covers more than 40% of diagnoses and all nine criteria track the diagnosis [[Zimmerman2015Combinations,Zimmerman2006]]. Status: unresolved (Established heterogeneity; implications debated).
- Bereavement: position A: bereavement-related depression is not different from other depression, so the exclusion should go [[Zisook2012]]; position B: removal makes the diagnosis less objective and risks labelling normal grief [[Uher2014]]. Status: exclusion removed in DSM-5, prolonged grief disorder added in DSM-5-TR [[First2022]].
- Which PHQ-9 cut-off? 10 maximises combined sensitivity and specificity [[Levis2019PHQ9,Negeri2021]]; cut-offs of 8–11 perform similarly [[Manea2012]]; adolescents may need 11 [[Richardson2010]]. Status: 10 remains standard.
- Does screening improve outcomes? The USPSTF finds moderate net benefit when systems for diagnosis and follow-up exist [[USPSTF2023Depression]]; one-time screening without feedback into care does not help [[Fortney2017]], and scores of 10 or more overestimate prevalence about twofold [[Levis2020Prevalence]]. Status: debated between task forces.
- Is the HAM-D still fit for purpose? Position A: psychometrically flawed and should be replaced [[Bagby2004]]; position B: its response and remission thresholds map sensibly onto clinical global impressions [[Leucht2013HAMD]], and effect sizes on HAM-D and MADRS are nearly identical [[Hengartner2020MADRS]]. Status: both scales remain in use.
- What change on a scale matters to patients? Estimates of the minimal important difference (3–5 HAM-D points) exceed the average drug–placebo difference (about 2 points) [[Hengartner2022]]; others propose a lower threshold (standardised mean difference 0.24) [[Cuijpers2014MID]]. Status: unresolved; group averages do not predict individual benefit.
- MADRS remission threshold: 4, 9, 10 or 8–9 depending on method [[Zimmerman2004MADRS,Zimmerman2004Derivation,Carmody2006]]. Status: 10 or less is most used in guidelines [[Lam2024CANMAT]].
- Burnout: a form of depression [[Bianchi2015]] or a distinct construct [[Koutsimani2019]]? Status: unresolved (Hypothesis).
- Under- or over-diagnosis of bipolar disorder among people presenting with depression: under-recognition [[Hirschfeld2003,Angst2011BRIDGE]] versus overdiagnosis [[Zimmerman2008]]. Status: both occur.
- "Male-type" symptoms: adding anger, risk-taking and substance use removes the sex difference in prevalence in one survey [[Martin2013]]. Status: (Early evidence); not part of the criteria.
