# B · Causes & neurobiology

Pass date: 2026-11-13. Modules served: causes, brain, stress.

This pass covers why depression develops: inherited risk (family, twin, adoption and genome-wide studies), the candidate-gene story, life experience (childhood maltreatment, stressful events, loneliness, poverty, physical illness, medicines and alcohol), the stress-diathesis and kindling models, and the main biological accounts (monoamines, brain circuits and structure, neuroplasticity, glutamate and GABA, the HPA axis, inflammation, sleep and circadian rhythm, the gut). Depression is treated throughout as a common, treatable illness with biological, psychological and social causes. All figures are group or population statistics, not predictions for one person. No finding here is a diagnostic test.

Crisis box for any page built from this file: "If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123)".

## Findings

### causes · A multifactorial illness
- The aetiology of major depressive disorder (MDD) is multifactorial, with heritability estimated at about 35%, and no established mechanism explains all aspects of the illness (Established). [[Otte2016MDD]]
- A meta-analysis of family and twin studies concluded that major depression "does not result from either genetic or environmental influences alone but rather from both". [[Sullivan2000]]
- In 1,942 adult female twins interviewed up to four times over 9 years, a developmental model with 18 risk factors in five tiers (childhood, early adolescence, late adolescence, adulthood, the last year) explained 52% of the variance in liability to depressive episodes in the previous year. [[Kendler2002]]
- That model grouped risk into three broad pathways: internalising symptoms, externalising symptoms and psychosocial adversity. The authors caution that causal inference is limited by retrospective recall and a purely additive model. [[Kendler2002]]
- In a case-control study of 1,809 adults (861 with MDD), group differences in environmental variables were substantially larger than differences in any brain-imaging measure or in a polygenic risk score. [[Winter2022]]

### causes · Family, twin and adoption studies
- First-degree relatives of people with major depression have higher odds of the illness than relatives of comparison subjects: Mantel-Haenszel odds ratio 2.84 (95% CI 2.31-3.49) across five family studies. [[Sullivan2000]]
- Across five twin studies, heritability of liability to major depression was 37% (95% CI 31-42%), shared family environment contributed 0% (95% CI 0-5%), and individual-specific environment plus measurement error contributed 63% (95% CI 58-67%). [[Sullivan2000]]
- Recurrence is the clinical feature that best predicts familial aggregation of major depression. [[Sullivan2000]]
- In 42,161 Swedish twins (15,493 complete pairs) interviewed for lifetime major depression, heritability of liability was higher in women (42%) than in men (29%), and the genetic risk factors were only moderately correlated between the sexes. [[Kendler2006Twin]]
- The same study found no difference in the roles of genetic and environmental factors across birth cohorts from 1900 to 1958. [[Kendler2006Twin]]
- In a 30-year follow-up of 147 offspring, children of parents with moderate to severe depression had about three times the risk of major depression of children of parents without depression. [[Weissman2016]]
- In both groups the period of highest risk for a first episode was age 15 to 25; onset before puberty was uncommon, but was more than 10 times as likely in the high-risk offspring. [[Weissman2016]]
- In a Swedish register study of 2,269,552 offspring, the parent-offspring correlation for treated major depression was 0.17 (95% CI 0.16-0.17) for genes plus rearing, 0.08 (0.06-0.09) for genes only and 0.08 (0.07-0.09) for rearing only. [[Kendler2018Adoption]]
- The authors concluded that parent-to-child resemblance arises from genetic factors and rearing experiences "to an approximately equal extent", and that the two act additively. [[Kendler2018Adoption]]
- Twin heritability may be greater for recurrent, early-onset and postpartum MDD. [[Wray2018]]

### causes · Genome-wide association studies
- 2018 (Psychiatric Genomics Consortium): 135,458 cases and 344,901 controls; 44 independent genome-wide significant loci. [[Wray2018]]
- In that study, heritability from common variants (SNP heritability) was 8.7% on the liability scale (assuming lifetime risk 0.15), about a quarter of the heritability estimated from twin and family studies. [[Wray2018]]
- The 2018 genetic risk score explained 1.9% of the variance in liability; it ranked a random case above a random control with probability 0.57 (AUC 0.57), and the odds ratio for the top versus bottom risk-score decile was 2.4. [[Wray2018]]
- "All humans carry lesser or greater numbers of genetic risk factors for major depression"; the findings imply a continuous measure of risk underlying the clinical diagnosis. [[Wray2018]]
- Lower educational attainment and higher body mass were putatively causal for major depression in the 2018 analysis, and depression and schizophrenia shared part of their biological aetiology. [[Wray2018]]
- 2019: a meta-analysis of 807,553 people (246,363 cases, 561,190 controls) found 102 independent variants, 269 genes and 15 gene sets, including genes for synaptic structure and neurotransmission. [[Howard2019GWAS]]
- In an independent replication sample of 1,306,354 people (414,055 cases, 892,299 controls), 87 of the 102 variants remained significant after correction for multiple testing. [[Howard2019GWAS]]
- SNP heritability in the 2019 study was 0.089 (SE 0.003) on the liability scale, and heritability was enriched in prefrontal brain regions including the anterior cingulate and frontal cortex. [[Howard2019GWAS]]
- 2023: more than 1.3 million people (371,184 with depression); 243 risk loci, 64 of them new, including genes encoding glutamate and GABA receptors. [[Als2023]]
- Depression is highly polygenic: about 11,700 variants explain 90% of the SNP heritability. [[Als2023]]
- Among people with depression, those with the highest polygenic burden had absolute risks of recurrence and psychiatric comorbidity up to 5-fold higher than cases with the lowest burden and up to 32-fold higher than the background population (group statistics). [[Als2023]]
- 2024: a multi-ancestry study added 88,316 cases and 902,757 controls from 21 cohorts (effective sample 36% African, 26% East Asian, 6% South Asian ancestry, 32% Hispanic/Latin American) and found 53 novel loci. [[Meng2024NatGenet]]
- Fewer loci from European-ancestry studies than expected transferred to other ancestry groups, so genetic findings cannot be assumed to apply equally across populations. [[Meng2024NatGenet]]
- 2025: 688,808 cases and 4,364,225 controls from 29 countries; 697 associations at 635 loci, 293 of them novel, with 308 high-confidence gene associations. [[PGCMDD2025]]
- The 2025 associations were enriched in postsynaptic density and receptor clustering, implicated excitatory, inhibitory and medium spiny neurons and amygdala neurons, and were enriched for antidepressant targets (Established as association; mechanisms are Early evidence). [[PGCMDD2025]]
- SNP heritability in the 2025 study was 8.4% on the liability scale (assuming lifetime risk 15%), and polygenic scores explained up to 5.8% of liability variance in people of European ancestry. [[PGCMDD2025]]
- In 3,024 cases and 2,741 controls, a polygenic risk score was associated with MDD (odds ratio 1.24, R2 1.18%) and so was childhood trauma (odds ratio 2.63), but there was no interaction between the two (odds ratio 1.00, p = .89). [[Peyrot2018]]
- Differences between people with and without MDD on a polygenic risk score were comparable in size to those on brain-imaging measures, where the best single measures gave classification accuracies of 54-56%. [[Winter2022]]

### causes · The candidate-gene story (5-HTTLPR)
- 2003: in a birth cohort, people with one or two short alleles of the serotonin-transporter promoter polymorphism (5-HTTLPR) showed more depressive symptoms and diagnosable depression after stressful life events than people with two long alleles. [[Caspi2003]]
- 2009: a meta-analysis of 14 studies (14,250 participants, 1,769 with depression) found that stressful life events were associated with depression (odds ratio 1.41, 95% CI 1.25-1.57), but 5-HTTLPR genotype was not (1.05, 0.98-1.13), and there was no gene-by-stress interaction (1.01, 0.94-1.10). [[Risch2009]]
- 2018: a pre-registered collaborative analysis of 31 data sets (38,802 people of European ancestry) found no subgroup or definition in which the stress-by-5-HTTLPR interaction was significant; stress was a strong risk factor and genotype had no impact on risk. [[Culverhouse2018]]
- The authors concluded that if the interaction exists, "it is not broadly generalisable, but must be of modest effect size and only observable in limited situations". [[Culverhouse2018]]
- 2019: in samples of 62,138 to 443,264 people, none of 18 much-studied candidate genes showed clear main effects or gene-by-environment effects, and as a set they were no more associated with depression than non-candidate genes. [[Border2019]]
- The authors' conclusion: early candidate-gene hypotheses were incorrect, and the many reported associations "are likely to be false positives". [[Border2019]]

### causes · Childhood maltreatment
- In a meta-analysis of 184 studies, nearly half of patients with depression reported a history of childhood maltreatment. [[Nelson2017Maltreatment]]
- People who were maltreated were 2.66 (95% CI 2.38-2.98) to 3.73 (95% CI 2.88-4.83) times more likely to develop depression in adulthood, had an earlier onset, and were twice as likely to develop chronic or treatment-resistant depression. [[Nelson2017Maltreatment]]
- Depression severity was linked most strongly to childhood emotional maltreatment (emotional abuse and neglect). [[Nelson2017Maltreatment]]
- In eight high-quality cohort studies that did not rely on recall, the pooled odds ratio for depression after any maltreatment was 2.03 (95% CI 1.37-3.01). [[Li2016Maltreatment]]
- For depression or anxiety disorders by type: physical abuse odds ratio 2.00 (1.25-3.19), sexual abuse 2.66 (1.88-3.75), neglect 1.74 (1.35-2.23). [[Li2016Maltreatment]]
- Population attributable fractions suggested that over one-half of global depression and anxiety cases are potentially attributable to self-reported childhood maltreatment, and that a 10-25% reduction in maltreatment could prevent 31.4-80.3 million cases worldwide. [[Li2016Maltreatment]]
- Childhood maltreatment also predicts course: odds ratio 2.27 (95% CI 1.80-2.87) for recurrent or persistent episodes (16 studies, 23,544 participants) and 1.43 (1.11-1.83) for lack of response or remission during treatment (10 trials, 3,098 participants). [[Nanni2012]]
- In a birth cohort followed to age 32, children who were maltreated had a higher risk of clinically relevant C-reactive protein levels 20 years later (risk ratio 1.80, 95% CI 1.26-2.58) (Established as association). [[Danese2007]]

### causes · Stressful life events, loss and humiliation
- In female twins (24,648 person-months, 316 onsets), the odds ratio for onset of major depression in the month of a stressful life event was 5.64 in all subjects, 4.52 within dizygotic pairs and 3.58 within monozygotic pairs. [[Kendler1999]]
- Stressful life events have a substantial causal relationship with onset, but about one-third of the association is non-causal, because people predisposed to depression select themselves into high-risk environments. [[Kendler1999]]
- In 7,322 adult twins (98,592 person-months), onsets of major depression were predicted by higher ratings of loss and humiliation in the preceding events. [[Kendler2003]]
- Events combining humiliation (especially separation initiated by the other person) with loss were more depressogenic than pure loss events, including death; there were no sex differences in which event types predicted episodes. [[Kendler2003]]
- Stressful experiences lead to depression in some people but not in others, which is why researchers look for factors that modify the response to stress. [[Caspi2003]]

### causes · Loneliness, poverty and unemployment
- In 4,211 English adults aged 50 and over followed for 12 years, a 1-point higher loneliness score was associated with a 0.16-point (95% CI 0.13-0.19) higher depressive-symptom score after adjustment for social isolation, social support, polygenic risk and other confounders. [[Lee2021Loneliness]]
- The population attributable fraction for depression associated with loneliness was 18% (95% CI 12-24) at 1 year and 11% (3-19) at the final follow-up. [[Lee2021Loneliness]]
- Across eight cohorts, adults who were often lonely had a pooled adjusted odds ratio of 2.33 (95% CI 1.62-3.34) for new-onset depression; the authors advise caution because of heterogeneity. [[Mann2022Loneliness]]
- People in the lowest socioeconomic group had higher odds of depression than the highest group (odds ratio 1.81); the odds were 1.24 for a new episode and 2.06 for persisting depression, with a dose-response relation for education and income. [[Lorant2003]]
- The link between poverty and depression runs both ways: negative economic shocks cause mental illness and cash transfers improve mental health, while mental illness reduces employment and income. [[Ridley2020]]
- Unemployment was associated with major depressive disorder (odds ratio 1.88, 95% CI 1.57-2.25) and depressive symptoms (2.06, 1.85-2.30); the association was 2.27 in men and 1.62 in women. [[Amiri2022]]

### causes · Sex and personality
- In nationally representative samples from over 90 nations, the odds ratio for a major depression diagnosis in women versus men was 1.95 (95% CI 1.88-2.03). [[Salk2017]]
- The gender difference was already present at age 12 (odds ratio 2.37), peaked at ages 13-15 (3.02), then declined and stayed stable in adulthood. [[Salk2017]]
- The authors stress that the size of the difference means depression in men "should not be overlooked". [[Salk2017]]
- In 20,692 Swedish twins assessed for personality in 1972-1973 and interviewed more than 25 years later, neuroticism strongly predicted lifetime and new-onset major depression, largely through shared genetic risk (genetic correlation +0.46 to +0.47). [[Kendler2006Pers]]
- Extraversion was only weakly and inversely related to risk, and the effect disappeared after controlling for neuroticism. [[Kendler2006Pers]]
- Across 175 studies, groups with depressive, anxiety and substance use disorders were high on neuroticism (mean Cohen's d 1.65) and low on conscientiousness (mean d -1.01). [[Kotov2010]]

### causes · Physical illness and the perinatal period
- In the WHO World Health Survey (245,404 participants, 60 countries), between 9.3% and 23.0% of people with one or more chronic physical diseases (angina, arthritis, asthma, diabetes) had comorbid depression, against a 1-year prevalence of depressive episode alone of 3.2%. [[Moussavi2007]]
- People with multimorbidity had about twice the risk of a depressive disorder of people without it (relative risk 2.13, 95% CI 1.62-2.80), and about three times the risk of people with no chronic physical condition (2.97, 2.06-4.27). [[Read2017Multimorbidity]]
- Each additional chronic condition was associated with 45% greater odds of a depressive disorder (odds ratio 1.45, 95% CI 1.28-1.64). [[Read2017Multimorbidity]]
- Major depression is the most common psychiatric disorder accompanying Cushing's syndrome (chronic glucocorticoid excess), and it generally improves when cortisol production is lowered. [[Sonino2001]]
- In Danish registers, first-time mothers had a raised risk of hospital admission for any mental disorder through the first 3 months after childbirth, highest at 10-19 days postpartum (relative risk 7.31, 95% CI 5.44-9.81, compared with women 11-12 months after birth); fatherhood carried no increased risk. This figure covers all mental disorders, not depression alone. [[MunkOlsen2006]]

### causes · Medicines and substances linked to depressive symptoms
- Interferon alfa: among people treated for chronic hepatitis C who had no depressive episode at baseline, the cumulative incidence of a major depressive episode was 0.25 (95% CI 0.16-0.35) at 24 weeks and 0.28 (0.17-0.42) at 48 weeks (26 observational studies). [[Udina2012]]
- Predictors of interferon-induced depression were a history of major depressive episode (odds ratio 3.96, 95% CI 2.52-6.21), a history of psychiatric disorder (3.18, 1.60-6.32), female gender (1.40, 1.02-1.91), higher baseline interleukin-6 and subthreshold depressive symptoms. [[Udina2012]]
- In 40 people with malignant melanoma starting high-dose interferon alfa, major depression developed in 11% (2 of 18) of those pretreated with paroxetine and 45% (9 of 20) of those given placebo (relative risk 0.24, 95% CI 0.08-0.93). This was a research trial, not a general recommendation. [[Musselman2001]]
- During interferon alfa treatment, fatigue, loss of appetite and pain appeared within 2 weeks in a large proportion of patients, while depressed mood, anxiety and cognitive symptoms appeared later and mainly in those who met criteria for major depression. [[Capuron2002]]
- The US peginterferon alfa-2a label carries a boxed warning that alpha interferons "may cause or aggravate fatal or life-threatening neuropsychiatric, autoimmune, ischemic, and infectious disorders", and lists depression among the neuropsychiatric reactions. [[FDA-Pegasys]]
- Oral glucocorticoids: in UK primary care (372,696 patients, 786,868 courses), the hazard ratio for depression was 1.83 (95% CI 1.72-1.94) compared with people with the same underlying disease who were not treated; risk was greater with higher dosages and with a previous neuropsychiatric history. [[Fardet2012]]
- Corticosteroids can cause substance-induced mood disorders with depressive, manic or mixed features, and this effect is described as "remarkably unpredictable". [[Patten2000]]
- Isotretinoin: the US label warns that it "may cause depression, psychosis and, rarely, suicidal ideation" and tells prescribers to ask about psychiatric history before treatment and assess mood at each visit. [[FDA-Absorica]]
- Isotretinoin: a meta-analysis of 31 studies (none randomised) found no increased risk; depression scores fell from baseline (standardised mean difference -0.335, 95% CI -0.498 to -0.172) and the prevalence of depression declined after treatment (relative risk 0.588, 0.382-0.904). [[Huang2017Isotretinoin]]
- Isotretinoin: in a propensity-matched cohort (30,866 people prescribed isotretinoin), the odds ratio for any new neuropsychiatric outcome within 1 year was 0.80 (95% CI 0.74-0.87) versus oral antibiotics and 1.06 (0.97-1.16) versus people with acne and no prescription. [[Paljarvi2022]]
- Hormonal contraception: in 1,061,997 Danish women aged 15-34, users of combined oral contraceptives had a rate ratio of 1.23 (95% CI 1.22-1.25) for first use of an antidepressant compared with non-users, and adolescents aged 15-19 had a rate ratio of 1.8 (1.75-1.84). This is an observational association. [[Skovlund2016]]
- Beta-blockers: in 285 double-blind randomised trials (53,533 patients), depression was no more common with beta-blockers than with placebo (odds ratio 1.02, 95% CI 0.83-1.25). [[Riemer2021]]
- In US adults surveyed 2005-2014, 37.2% used at least one prescription medicine listing depression as a potential adverse effect; estimated prevalence of depression (PHQ-9 score 10 or more) was 15% in those using three or more such medicines and 4.7% in those using none. The survey was cross-sectional and cannot show cause. [[Qato2018]]
- Alcohol: having either an alcohol use disorder or major depression doubles the risk of the other (pooled adjusted odds ratios 2.00 to 2.09), and the most plausible causal direction is that alcohol use disorder increases the risk of depression. [[Boden2011]]

### stress · Stress-diathesis and kindling
- The stress-diathesis view holds that life stress acts on pre-existing vulnerability; in twin data about one-third of the stress-depression association reflects predisposed people entering high-risk environments, and the rest is consistent with a causal effect of stress. [[Kendler1999]]
- Kindling hypothesis (Post, 1992): psychosocial stressors play a greater role in the first episode of a mood disorder than in later ones, and both stressors and episodes may leave lasting vulnerability (Hypothesis). [[Post1992]]
- In 2,395 female twins followed over 9 years (97,515 person-months, 1,380 onsets), the depressogenic effect of stressful life events declined substantially as the number of previous episodes rose from zero to nine, then stayed largely unchanged with further episodes. [[Kendler2000]]
- The authors read this pattern as consistent with kindling, with a threshold at about nine episodes beyond which there is no further sensitisation. [[Kendler2000]]
- A later review noted inconsistencies and confusion in the kindling literature about how the role of life stress changes across recurrences, and set out competing models to test. [[Monroe2005]]
- Polygenic risk and childhood trauma each raised the odds of MDD but did not multiply each other in a meta-analysis of nine cohorts, which argues against a simple genome-wide gene-by-trauma interaction. [[Peyrot2018]]

### stress · The acute stress response
- The Trier Social Stress Test consists of a 10-minute anticipation period and a 10-minute test period (free speech and mental arithmetic in front of an audience); it reliably raised salivary cortisol 2- to 4-fold above baseline and also raised ACTH, growth hormone, prolactin and heart rate. [[Kirschbaum1993]]
- Across 208 laboratory studies, psychological stressors raised cortisol mainly when the task was uncontrollable or carried social-evaluative threat; tasks with both features produced the largest cortisol and ACTH changes and the longest recovery times. [[Dickerson2004]]
- In seven studies (98 people with MDD, 98 without), cortisol levels were similar at baseline and during the stressor, but people with MDD had much higher cortisol in the recovery period (more than 25 minutes after the stressor ended) (Established in small samples). [[Burke2005Cortisol]]
- With individual data from 14 MDD studies (n = 1,129), women with current MDD showed a blunted cortisol response to psychosocial stress while men with current MDD showed an increased response; in remitted MDD the changes were smaller in women and absent in men. [[Zorn2017]]
- Women with a history of childhood abuse and current major depression had a more than 6-fold greater ACTH response to a laboratory stressor than controls (net peak 9.0 versus 1.4 pmol/L). [[Heim2000]]

### stress · The HPA axis in depression
- Hyperactivity of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis is described as one of the most consistent biological findings in major depression, although the mechanisms remain unclear (Established). [[Pariante2008]]
- Across 361 studies (18,454 people), people with depression had higher cortisol (d = 0.60, 95% CI 0.54-0.66) and ACTH (d = 0.28, 0.16-0.41) but no elevation of corticotropin-releasing hormone (d = 0.02, -0.47 to 0.51). [[Stetler2011]]
- The cortisol effect fell by almost half (d = 0.33, 95% CI 0.21-0.45) when only studies meeting minimal methodological standards were counted. [[Stetler2011]]
- HPA hyperactivity varies by group: it was greater in older inpatients and in melancholic or psychotic depression. [[Stetler2011]]
- Dexamethasone suppression test (1 mg overnight, cortisol threshold 5 micrograms/dL): in 438 subjects it identified melancholia with a sensitivity of 67% and a specificity of 96%. [[Carroll1981]]
- Later meta-analysis found non-suppression in 64% of patients with psychotic depression, 41% of non-psychotic patients, 36% in studies of melancholia and 12% of non-melancholic outpatients, so the test is not a general diagnostic test for depression. [[Nelson1997DST]]
- Reduced sensitivity to dexamethasone points to impaired glucocorticoid feedback; the evidence supports reduced glucocorticoid receptor function in major depression without clear evidence of reduced receptor expression (glucocorticoid resistance) (Early evidence). [[Pariante2001]]
- Some antidepressants have direct effects on the glucocorticoid receptor, enhancing its function and increasing its expression (Early evidence). [[Pariante2001]]
- Among biomarkers tested prospectively (75 studies), only cortisol predicted onset, relapse or recurrence of MDD (odds ratio 1.294, p = 0.024), and the effect disappeared when studies including participants with a baseline diagnosis were removed. [[Kennis2020]]

### brain · Monoamines and the serotonin debate
- The catecholamine hypothesis of affective disorders was set out in a 1965 review. [[Schildkraut1965]]
- Monoamine oxidase A density, measured by PET in 17 medication-free people with MDD and 17 controls, was elevated by 34% on average (2 standard deviations) throughout the brain during a major depressive episode (Early evidence). [[Meyer2006]]
- Monoamine depletion: lowering serotonin (acute tryptophan depletion) or noradrenaline and dopamine did not lower mood in healthy controls; it slightly lowered mood in healthy people with a family history of MDD and moderately lowered mood in drug-free people with MDD in remission (Established). [[Ruhe2007]]
- Tryptophan depletion induced relapse in people in remission who were taking serotonergic antidepressants. The authors conclude that depletion studies "fail to demonstrate a causal relation" and probably reveal a vulnerability trait. [[Ruhe2007]]
- Serotonin transporter imaging: across 18 studies (364 unmedicated people with MDD, 372 controls), transporter availability was lower in the midbrain (Hedges' g -0.49, 95% CI -0.84 to -0.14) and amygdala (g -0.50, -0.78 to -0.22), a reduction of about 10%, with no difference in frontal or cingulate cortex (Established as a small group difference). [[Gryglewski2014]]
- 5-HT1A receptor imaging: across 10 PET studies (218 people with depression, 261 controls), binding was lower in the mesiotemporal cortex (summary effect -0.8, 95% CI -1.36 to -0.24), with smaller reductions in hippocampus, raphe nuclei, insula and anterior cingulate. [[Wang2016HT1A]]
- A 2022 umbrella review of 17 studies concluded that the main areas of serotonin research "provide no consistent evidence of there being an association between serotonin and depression, and no support for the hypothesis that depression is caused by lowered serotonin activity or concentrations". [[Moncrieff2022Serotonin]]
- That review rated the 5-HT1A and transporter imaging evidence as weak and inconsistent, noted that prior antidepressant use was not reliably excluded, and found no association for the serotonin metabolite 5-HIAA or for the serotonin transporter gene. [[Moncrieff2022Serotonin]]
- A published reply argued that this conclusion is overstated, citing methodological weaknesses, selective reporting and errors in interpreting the tryptophan-depletion and molecular-imaging evidence. [[Jauhar2023]]
- A 2023 PET study (17 antidepressant-free people in a major depressive episode, 20 controls) measured serotonin release after a d-amphetamine challenge: frontal binding fell by 15% (SD 14%) in controls and 6.5% (SD 20%) in the depression group (p = .041), read as reduced serotonin release capacity (Early evidence; small single study). [[Erritzoe2023]]
- Antidepressant efficacy does not depend on settling this debate: in 522 double-blind trials (116,477 participants), all 21 antidepressants were more effective than placebo for acute treatment in adults, with odds ratios from 1.37 (95% CrI 1.16-1.63) to 2.13 (1.89-2.41) (Established). [[Cipriani2018]]
- A purely neurotransmitter-based account of antidepressant action is challenged by the delayed clinical onset of most agents; newer accounts stress early changes in emotional processing and neural plasticity (Early evidence). [[Harmer2017]]

### brain · Circuits and networks
- Induced sadness in healthy volunteers raised blood flow in limbic-paralimbic regions (subgenual cingulate, anterior insula) and lowered it in neocortical regions (right dorsolateral prefrontal, inferior parietal); recovery from depression showed the reverse pattern in the same regions. [[Mayberg1999]]
- Activity in the subgenual cingulate and right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex was inversely correlated in both states (limbic-cortical reciprocity). [[Mayberg1999]]
- The subgenual prefrontal cortex showed lower activity and lower grey-matter volume in people with familial mood disorders (mean volume reductions of 39% in the bipolar and 48% in the unipolar sample) in an early PET and MRI study (Early evidence; small samples). [[Drevets1997]]
- In six people with treatment-resistant depression, chronic deep brain stimulation of white matter next to the subgenual cingulate (Brodmann area 25) was associated with sustained remission in four, with reduced local blood flow on PET (Early evidence; open-label proof of concept). [[Mayberg2005]]
- In response to negative stimuli, people with MDD showed greater activation of the amygdala, insula and dorsal anterior cingulate and lower activation of the dorsal striatum and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex than healthy people (whole-brain meta-analysis). [[Hamilton2012Neuroimaging]]
- In 11 patients and 11 controls, left amygdala activation to masked emotional faces was exaggerated in depression and normalised after antidepressant treatment (Early evidence). [[Sheline2001]]
- Resting-state connectivity (27 data sets; 556 people with MDD, 518 controls): hypoconnectivity within the frontoparietal control network, hyperconnectivity within the default network, and hyperconnectivity between frontoparietal control systems and the default network (Established at group level). [[Kaiser2015]]
- The authors link this pattern to a bias "toward internal thoughts at the cost of engaging with the external world". [[Kaiser2015]]
- Reward processing (38 fMRI and 12 EEG studies): striatal activation during reward feedback was reduced in depression, the feedback-related negativity was blunted, and both preceded the onset of depression in longitudinal studies of adolescents. [[Keren2018]]
- One integrative model proposes that anhedonia arises from stress acting on mesocorticolimbic dopamine pathways that support motivation and reinforcement learning (Hypothesis). [[Pizzagalli2014]]

### brain · Brain structure
- ENIGMA subcortical analysis (1,728 people with MDD, 7,199 controls, 15 samples): hippocampal volume was lower in MDD (Cohen's d -0.14, -1.24%). [[Schmaal2016]]
- The difference was driven by recurrent MDD (d -0.17, -1.44%); first-episode patients did not differ from controls. Onset at age 21 or younger was associated with a smaller hippocampus (d -0.20, -1.85%). [[Schmaal2016]]
- Symptom severity at inclusion was not associated with any regional volume. [[Schmaal2016]]
- ENIGMA cortical analysis (2,148 people with MDD, 7,957 controls, 20 sites): adults with MDD had thinner cortex in the orbitofrontal cortex, anterior and posterior cingulate, insula and temporal lobes (Cohen's d -0.10 to -0.14). [[Schmaal2017]]
- Adolescents with MDD had lower total cortical surface area and regional reductions (d -0.26 to -0.57) but no difference in cortical thickness. [[Schmaal2017]]
- In 38 women treated as outpatients for major depression, longer time spent in untreated depressive episodes was associated with smaller hippocampal volume, while time depressed on antidepressant medication was not (Early evidence; observational). [[Sheline2003]]
- Across structural, diffusion and functional MRI in 1,809 adults, the largest single-measure differences had partial eta-squared of 0.004 to 0.017, the distributions of people with and without MDD overlapped by 87-95%, and single-participant prediction "was not possible". [[Winter2022]]

### brain · Neuroplasticity and BDNF
- Neurotrophic hypothesis: stress lowers brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) expression in limbic structures, and antidepressant treatment reverses or blocks this effect (largely preclinical evidence) (Hypothesis). [[Duman2006]]
- In mice, blocking hippocampal neurogenesis by X-irradiation prevented the behavioural effects of two classes of antidepressant (Early evidence; animal model). [[Santarelli2003]]
- Serum BDNF was lower in 2,384 antidepressant-free people with depression than in 2,982 healthy controls (Cohen's d -0.71), falling to d -0.47 after correction for publication bias; serum BDNF was not consistently associated with symptom severity. [[Molendijk2014]]
- Synaptic hypothesis: depression is associated with smaller prefrontal and hippocampal regions and fewer synapses there, and ketamine rapidly induces synaptogenesis and reverses stress-induced synaptic deficits in basic studies (Hypothesis). [[Duman2012]]
- Post-mortem dorsolateral prefrontal cortex from people with MDD showed lower expression of synaptic-function genes and a lower number of synapses (Early evidence). [[Kang2012]]
- In 26 unmedicated people with MDD, PTSD or both, PET with the SV2A ligand [11C]UCB-J showed that more severe depressive symptoms went with lower synaptic density (Early evidence). [[Holmes2019SV2A]]
- In rats, ketamine rapidly activated the mTOR pathway and increased the number and function of spine synapses in prefrontal cortex; blocking mTOR blocked both the synaptogenesis and the behavioural response (Early evidence; animal model). [[Li2010mTOR]]

### brain · Glutamate and GABA
- Proton MRS (49 studies; 1,180 patients, 1,066 controls): glutamate plus glutamine (Glx) was lower in the medial frontal cortex in depression (standardised mean difference -0.38, 95% CI -0.69 to -0.07); the difference was significant in medicated patients (-0.50) but not in unmedicated patients (-0.27, -0.76 to 0.21). [[Moriguchi2019]]
- First placebo-controlled test of an NMDA antagonist: in seven people with major depression, 25-item Hamilton scores fell by a mean of 14 points (SD 10) within 72 hours of intravenous ketamine 0.5 mg/kg versus 0 points (SD 12) after saline. This was a supervised research infusion. [[Berman2000]]
- Brain GABA measured by MRS was lower in people currently depressed with MDD (standardised mean difference -0.52, p = 0.005) but not in remitted MDD (-0.24, p = 0.310), so it behaves as a state marker (Early evidence). [[Schur2016]]
- The GABAergic deficit hypothesis proposes that reduced GABA transmission has a causal role and is reversed by antidepressant treatment (Hypothesis). [[Luscher2011]]
- Genetic support: the 64 new loci found in 2023 included genes encoding glutamate and GABA receptors. [[Als2023]]

### brain · Inflammation
- Cytokines (24 studies): TNF-alpha was higher in people with major depression by a weighted mean difference of 3.97 pg/mL (95% CI 2.24-5.71) and interleukin-6 by 1.78 pg/mL (1.23-2.33); IL-1beta, IL-4, IL-2, IL-8, IL-10 and interferon-gamma did not differ. [[Dowlati2010]]
- Larger update (107 studies; 5,166 patients, 5,083 controls): CRP g = 0.71 (95% CI 0.50-0.92), IL-6 g = 0.61 (0.39-0.82), TNF-alpha g = 0.54 (0.32-0.76) (Established as a group difference). [[Osimo2020]]
- Variability analysis suggested that some marker elevations reflect a right shift of the whole distribution, not only an inflamed subgroup. [[Osimo2020]]
- Low-grade inflammation (CRP above 3 mg/L) was present in 27% (95% CI 21-34%) of people with depression, and CRP above 1 mg/L in 58% (47-69%); the odds ratio for low-grade inflammation versus matched controls was 1.46 (1.22-1.75). [[Osimo2019]]
- Children in the top third of IL-6 at age 9 were more likely to have depression at age 18 than those in the bottom third (adjusted odds ratio 1.55, 95% CI 1.13-2.14) in a UK birth cohort of about 4,500 (Early evidence for direction of effect). [[Khandaker2014]]
- Pooled prospective studies of immune markers, however, did not significantly predict onset or recurrence of MDD. [[Kennis2020]]
- Sickness behaviour: pro-inflammatory cytokines produced during infection act on the brain to cause sickness behaviour, and sustained immune activation can lead to depressive symptoms in vulnerable people (Established in experimental models; partly Hypothesis for clinical depression). [[Dantzer2008]]
- Central markers: cerebrospinal fluid IL-6 (standardised mean difference 0.37, 95% CI 0.17-0.57) and TNF-alpha (0.58, 0.26-0.90) were higher in MDD, and the PET marker TSPO was elevated in anterior cingulate (0.78, 0.41-1.16); central and peripheral markers were not correlated. [[Enache2019]]
- In 20 medication-free people in a major depressive episode and 20 controls, TSPO distribution volume was 26% higher in prefrontal cortex, 32% higher in anterior cingulate and 33% higher in insula (Early evidence). [[Setiawan2015]]
- Infliximab trial (60 outpatients with treatment-resistant depression, three infusions of 5 mg/kg or placebo): no overall difference in Hamilton score change. [[Raison2013]]
- In an exploratory subgroup with baseline hs-CRP above 5 mg/L, response was 62% (8 of 13) with infliximab and 33% (3 of 9) with placebo (p = .19); below that level placebo did better (Early evidence). [[Raison2013]]
- Anti-inflammatory add-on or monotherapy trials pooled: standardised mean difference -0.34 (95% CI -0.57 to -0.11; 14 trials, I2 = 90%) in 2014 and -0.55 (-0.75 to -0.35; 26 trials) in 2020, with high heterogeneity and high risk of bias noted (Early evidence). [[Kohler2014,Bai2020AntiInflammatory]]
- Inflammation is proposed to contribute to depression and to non-response to current antidepressants in a subgroup of people (Hypothesis). [[Miller2016]]

### brain · Sleep and circadian rhythm
- Shortened REM latency (the interval from sleep onset to the first REM period) was reported in 1976 as a persistent marker of primary depressive illness. [[Kupfer1976]]
- Polysomnography in depression shows disturbed sleep continuity, reduced slow-wave sleep, shortened REM latency, longer REM sleep and higher REM density; REM changes are also seen in healthy relatives at high risk (Established). [[Palagini2013]]
- These changes are not specific: in a meta-analysis across mental disorders no sleep parameter was altered in only one condition, although no two conditions shared the same profile. [[Baglioni2016]]
- People with insomnia and no depression had about twice the risk of later depression (21 longitudinal studies; odds ratio 2.60, 95% CI 1.98-3.42 random effects; 2.10, 1.86-2.38 after adjusting for outliers). [[Baglioni2011]]
- In 91,105 UK Biobank participants wearing wrist accelerometers, each one-quintile reduction in circadian relative amplitude was associated with lifetime MDD (odds ratio 1.06, 95% CI 1.04-1.08); the design was cross-sectional. [[Lyall2018]]
- Experimental sleep deprivation produced an antidepressant response in 45% of participants in studies with a randomised control group and 50% in those without (66 studies), which links sleep-wake regulation to mood (Established). [[Boland2017]]

### brain · Gut-brain axis
- In a Flemish population cohort (n = 1,054, validated in 1,070 more), Coprococcus and Dialister bacteria were depleted in depression after correcting for antidepressant use, and butyrate-producing genera were associated with higher quality of life (Early evidence). [[VallesColomer2019]]
- Across 59 case-control studies of psychiatric disorders, depleted Faecalibacterium and Coprococcus and enriched Eggerthella were shared by MDD, bipolar disorder, psychosis and anxiety, so the pattern is transdiagnostic and not specific to depression (Early evidence). [[Nikolova2021]]
- Faecal microbiota from 34 people with major depression, transferred to microbiota-depleted rats, induced anhedonia-like and anxiety-like behaviour and changes in tryptophan metabolism (Hypothesis for causation in humans; animal model). [[Kelly2016Microbiota]]
- Only one prospective study of gastrointestinal factors as predictors of MDD was found in a 2020 systematic review. [[Kennis2020]]

### Exam hooks
- Heritability of major depression from twin studies is about 37% (95% CI 31-42%); shared family environment contributes about 0%. [[Sullivan2000]]
- First-degree relatives: odds ratio 2.84. [[Sullivan2000]]
- Heritability is higher in women (42%) than men (29%) in the Swedish twin registry. [[Kendler2006Twin]]
- SNP heritability is 8-9%, about a quarter of twin heritability. [[Wray2018,Howard2019GWAS,PGCMDD2025]]
- GWAS milestones: 44 loci (2018), 102 variants (2019), 243 loci (2023), 697 associations at 635 loci (2025). [[Wray2018,Howard2019GWAS,Als2023,PGCMDD2025]]
- Polygenic scores explain at most about 2-6% of liability variance and cannot diagnose depression. [[Wray2018,PGCMDD2025]]
- The 5-HTTLPR-by-stress interaction (2003) did not replicate in large meta-analyses (2009, 2018) or in a 2019 test of 18 candidate genes. [[Caspi2003,Risch2009,Culverhouse2018,Border2019]]
- Childhood maltreatment roughly doubles to quadruples the odds of adult depression and doubles the odds of a chronic or treatment-resistant course. [[Nelson2017Maltreatment,Li2016Maltreatment]]
- Odds ratio for onset of major depression in the month of a stressful life event: 5.64; about one-third of the association is non-causal. [[Kendler1999]]
- Loss plus humiliation is more depressogenic than loss alone. [[Kendler2003]]
- Kindling: the link between life events and onset weakens over the first nine or so episodes. [[Kendler2000]]
- Women to men odds ratio for a depression diagnosis: 1.95, peaking at ages 13-15 (3.02). [[Salk2017]]
- Interferon alfa for hepatitis C: about 1 in 4 people developed a major depressive episode. [[Udina2012]]
- Beta-blockers were not associated with depression in randomised trials (odds ratio 1.02). [[Riemer2021]]
- Tryptophan depletion lowers mood in people in remission from MDD and in relatives, but not in healthy volunteers. [[Ruhe2007]]
- Cortisol in depression: d = 0.60 overall, 0.33 in better-quality studies; CRH not elevated. [[Stetler2011]]
- Dexamethasone suppression test for melancholia: sensitivity 67%, specificity 96%; non-suppression is most frequent in psychotic depression (64%). [[Carroll1981,Nelson1997DST]]
- Hippocampal volume: d = -0.14 (-1.24%), driven by recurrent illness; no difference at first episode. [[Schmaal2016]]
- Default-network hyperconnectivity and frontoparietal hypoconnectivity characterise MDD at group level. [[Kaiser2015]]
- CRP above 3 mg/L is found in about a quarter (27%) of people with depression. [[Osimo2019]]
- Shortened REM latency is the classic sleep finding, but it is not specific to depression. [[Kupfer1976,Baglioni2016]]

### In plain words
- Depression is an illness, not a weakness or a character flaw. It comes from a mix of inherited risk, life experience and biology. [[Otte2016MDD,Sullivan2000]]
- It runs in families to a degree. Genes explain roughly a third of the difference in risk between people, and the rest comes from what happens in each person's life. [[Sullivan2000]]
- There is no single "depression gene". Hundreds of tiny genetic differences each add a little risk, and everyone carries some of them. [[Wray2018,PGCMDD2025]]
- A gene test cannot tell you whether you will get depression. [[Wray2018,Winter2022]]
- Being abused or neglected in childhood raises the risk of depression in adult life. It was never the child's fault, and this risk is not destiny. [[Nelson2017Maltreatment]]
- Hard events, above all losses and being humiliated, can set off an episode. Not everyone who goes through them becomes ill. [[Kendler2003,Caspi2003]]
- Loneliness, money trouble and losing a job all make depression more likely. [[Mann2022Loneliness,Lorant2003,Amiri2022]]
- Long-term physical illness and depression often come together, and each can make the other harder. [[Read2017Multimorbidity,Moussavi2007]]
- A few medicines can bring on low mood in some people, such as interferon and steroid tablets. Do not stop a medicine on your own; talk to the prescriber if your mood changes. [[Udina2012,Fardet2012]]
- Heavy drinking and depression feed each other. [[Boden2011]]
- "Chemical imbalance" is too simple. Scientists have not shown that depression is caused by low serotonin. Antidepressants have still been shown to work better than placebo in many trials. [[Moncrieff2022Serotonin,Cipriani2018]]
- In depression the body's stress system often stays switched on for longer, with more of the stress hormone cortisol. [[Stetler2011,Burke2005Cortisol]]
- Brain scans show small average differences between groups of people with and without depression. A scan cannot diagnose depression in one person. [[Schmaal2016,Winter2022]]
- About one in four people with depression has signs of mild inflammation in the blood. Researchers are testing whether this matters for treatment. [[Osimo2019,Raison2013]]
- Poor sleep can come before depression as well as with it. [[Baglioni2011]]

## Open questions and controversies
- Is depression caused by low serotonin? Position A: an umbrella review found no consistent evidence of an association and no support for a lowered-serotonin cause [[Moncrieff2022Serotonin]]. Position B: critics say the review over-simplified and misread the depletion and imaging evidence [[Jauhar2023]], and a PET study reports reduced serotonin release capacity [[Erritzoe2023]]; imaging meta-analyses show small regional reductions in transporter and 5-HT1A binding [[Gryglewski2014,Wang2016HT1A]]. Status: a simple serotonin deficiency is not supported; a role for the serotonin system in some people remains open; antidepressant efficacy in trials is established either way [[Cipriani2018]].
- Missing heritability: twin studies give about 37% [[Sullivan2000]], common variants 8-9% [[Wray2018,Howard2019GWAS,PGCMDD2025]]. Status: unresolved.
- Do genes and stress interact? Position A: yes, 5-HTTLPR moderates the effect of life stress [[Caspi2003]]. Position B: no reliable interaction in large collaborative analyses, for single genes [[Risch2009,Culverhouse2018,Border2019]] or for polygenic scores with childhood trauma [[Peyrot2018]]. Status: stress and genetic risk both matter, mostly additively on current evidence.
- Kindling: the event-onset link weakens with episode number [[Kendler2000]], as first proposed by Post [[Post1992]]; whether this reflects sensitisation of the brain or a change in which stresses count is disputed [[Monroe2005]]. Status: open.
- Is the hippocampus smaller before depression or because of it? The difference is seen in recurrent but not first-episode illness [[Schmaal2016]] and tracks time spent untreated in one small study [[Sheline2003]], yet prospective brain-volume predictors were not significant in pooled analyses [[Kennis2020]]. Status: open; effect is small.
- Is there an "inflammatory subtype"? Position A: about a quarter have CRP above 3 mg/L [[Osimo2019]] and a high-CRP subgroup seemed to respond to infliximab [[Raison2013]]. Position B: marker elevations look like a shift of the whole distribution [[Osimo2020]], the infliximab trial was negative overall [[Raison2013]], and anti-inflammatory trials are heterogeneous with high risk of bias [[Kohler2014,Bai2020AntiInflammatory]]. Status: Early evidence; no anti-inflammatory medicine is an established depression treatment on this evidence.
- HPA axis as a biomarker: hypercortisolaemia is consistent at group level [[Stetler2011,Pariante2008]] but the dexamethasone test is insensitive outside psychotic and melancholic depression [[Nelson1997DST]], and cortisol's prospective prediction disappears in people without a baseline diagnosis [[Kennis2020]]. Status: not a clinical test.
- Isotretinoin and depression: the label warns of depression [[FDA-Absorica]]; pooled studies and a large matched cohort found no excess risk at population level [[Huang2017Isotretinoin,Paljarvi2022]]. Status: unresolved for individuals; mood monitoring remains the labelled practice.
- Hormonal contraception: a large register study found higher rates of first antidepressant use, especially in adolescents [[Skovlund2016]]. Status: observational; causality not established.
- Can biology diagnose depression? Imaging, polygenic and blood markers show small average differences with 87-95% overlap between groups [[Winter2022]]. Status: no validated biological test; diagnosis remains clinical.
- Gut microbiome: associations are reproducible but transdiagnostic, and region and medication are confounders [[Nikolova2021,VallesColomer2019]]; causal evidence comes from animal transfer studies [[Kelly2016Microbiota]]. Status: Hypothesis in humans.
