## Findings

### perinatal · How common is depression in pregnancy and after birth
- Perinatal depression (a depressive disorder during pregnancy or after childbirth) "affects as many as 1 in 7 women" and is described by the USPSTF as one of the most common complications of pregnancy and the postpartum period. [[USPSTF2019Perinatal]]
- In a systematic review of 28 interview-based studies from developed countries, the point prevalence of major plus minor depression was 6.5% to 12.9% across trimesters and months of the first postpartum year (major depression alone 1.0% to 5.6%). [[Gavin2005]]
- In the same review, as many as 19.2% of women had a depressive episode (7.1% a major depressive episode) in the first 3 months postpartum, and most of those episodes began after delivery; all estimates had wide confidence intervals. [[Gavin2005]]
- A meta-analysis of 96 studies (140 estimates) gave a pooled perinatal depression prevalence of 11.9% (95% CI 11.4–12.5). [[Woody2017]]
- Prevalence was higher in low- and middle-income countries than in high-income countries (OR 1.8, 95% CI 1.4–2.2), and higher when symptom scales were used rather than diagnostic interviews (OR 1.6, 95% CI 1.3–2.0). [[Woody2017]]
- Among healthy mothers with no history of depression (58 studies, 37,294 women), postpartum depression prevalence was 17% (95% CI 15–20) and incidence 12% (95% CI 4–20); regional prevalence ranged from 8% (Europe) to 26% (Middle East). [[Shorey2018]]
- A narrative review puts postpartum depression prevalence at 13% to 19% and identifies the first six months after delivery as a high-risk time. [[OHara2013]]
- Depression often starts before birth: among 1,396 women who screened positive (EPDS 10 or higher) at 4 to 6 weeks postpartum (14.0% of 10,000 screened), the episode began postpartum in 40.1%, during pregnancy in 33.4% and before pregnancy in 26.5%. [[Wisner2013]]
- In that screen-positive group the most common primary diagnosis was unipolar depressive disorder (68.5%), almost two-thirds had a comorbid anxiety disorder, 22.6% had a bipolar disorder and 19.3% reported thoughts of self-harm. [[Wisner2013]]
- Fathers are affected too: a meta-analysis of 43 studies (28,004 participants) estimated prenatal and postpartum depression in 10.4% of fathers (95% CI 8.5–12.7), highest at 3 to 6 months postpartum (25.6%, 95% CI 17.3–36.1), with a moderate correlation with maternal depression (r = 0.308). [[Paulson2010]]
- An updated meta-analysis estimated paternal perinatal depression at 8.4% (95% CI 7.2–9.6), varying with measurement method, location and maternal depression. [[Cameron2016]]

### perinatal · Baby blues, postpartum depression and postpartum psychosis
- Maternity ("baby") blues is common: pooled prevalence 39.0% (95% CI 32.3–45.6) across 26 studies and 5,667 women, with a range of 13.7% to 76.0% between studies. [[RezaieKeikhaie2020]]
- The DSM-5-TR "with peripartum onset" specifier applies to a major depressive episode that begins during pregnancy or in the 4 weeks after delivery; the zuranolone trials used onset in the third trimester or within 4 weeks of delivery. [[APA2022DSM5TR,FDA-Zurzuvae]] (DSM wording unverified: book text not opened)
- Psychosis or mania after childbirth is a psychiatric emergency with serious risk to mother and baby; inpatient care is usually required to ensure safety, complete the diagnostic evaluation and start treatment. [[Bergink2016]]
- First-lifetime-onset postpartum psychosis or mania occurs in 0.25 to 0.6 per 1,000 births in population register studies of psychiatric admissions. [[Bergink2016]]
- A systematic review of population-based studies found incidence of puerperal psychosis between 0.89 and 2.6 per 1,000 women in five studies, too heterogeneous to pool. [[VanderKruik2017]]
- After a first episode, 20% to 50% of women have isolated postpartum psychosis; the rest go on to have episodes outside the perinatal period, usually within the bipolar spectrum. [[Bergink2016]]
- Relapse risk after a later pregnancy is 31% (95% CI 22–42) for women with isolated postpartum psychosis; in women with bipolar disorder postpartum relapse was 66% (95% CI 57–75) without medication in pregnancy versus 23% (95% CI 14–37) with prophylactic medication. [[Bergink2016,Wesseloo2016]]
- Because 22.6% of screen-positive postpartum women had a bipolar disorder in one large cohort, a positive depression screen is followed by a check for bipolar disorder before treatment is chosen. [[Wisner2013]]

### perinatal · Risk factors and consequences
- The USPSTF lists a history of depression, current depressive symptoms and socioeconomic factors such as low income or young or single parenthood as markers of increased risk of perinatal depression. [[USPSTF2019Perinatal]]
- Risk factors for postpartum depression mirror those of major depression at other times, with the addition of postpartum-specific factors such as sensitivity to hormonal change. [[OHara2013,Howard2014Lancet]]
- Perinatal mental disorders are associated with an increased risk of psychological and developmental disturbance in children, but these outcomes "are not inevitable"; parenting quality, social and material support and the duration of the parent's illness are modifiable. [[Stein2014]]
- In the Avon Longitudinal Study of Parents and Children (9,848 mothers), postnatal depression roughly doubled the odds of child behavioural disturbance (moderate OR 2.22, 95% CI 1.74–2.83; severe OR 2.39, 95% CI 1.78–3.22). [[Netsi2018]]
- When postnatal depression was both severe and persistent, odds were higher still: behavioural problems at 3.5 years OR 4.84 (95% CI 2.94–7.98), lower mathematics grades at 16 years OR 2.65 (95% CI 1.26–5.57) and depression at 18 years OR 7.44 (95% CI 2.89–19.11). [[Netsi2018]]
- Women with persistent postnatal depression still had raised depressive symptoms up to 11 years after childbirth, which is why early treatment of the parent is prioritised. [[Netsi2018]]

### perinatal · Screening and the EPDS
- The Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS) is a 10-item self-report scale developed for community screening; it was validated in 84 mothers and takes about 5 minutes to complete. [[Cox1987]]
- Each EPDS item is scored 0 to 3, giving a total of 0 to 30. [[Cox1987]] (unverified: scoring range not stated in the abstract)
- In an individual-participant-data meta-analysis (58 studies, 15,557 women, 2,069 with major depression) a cut-off of 11 or higher maximised combined sensitivity and specificity. [[Levis2020EPDS]]
- Against semi-structured interviews, EPDS sensitivity and specificity were 0.85 and 0.84 at a cut-off of 10 or higher, 0.81 and 0.88 at 11 or higher, and 0.66 and 0.95 at 13 or higher; accuracy was similar in pregnant and postpartum women. [[Levis2020EPDS]]
- A positive screen is not a diagnosis: at a cut-off of 11 or higher and a prevalence of major depression of 5% to 25%, positive predictive values ranged from 26% to 69% and negative predictive values from 93% to 99%. [[Levis2020EPDS]]
- Cut-offs of 10 or higher and 13 or higher are the ones most often used in practice; the EPDS is the most commonly used depression screening tool in perinatal care. [[Levis2020EPDS]]
- EPDS item 10 asks about thoughts of self-harm; in a 10,000-woman screening programme all mothers with the highest intensity of self-harm ideation were identified by an EPDS score of 10 or higher. [[Wisner2013]]
- The USPSTF recommends screening for depression in adults "including pregnant and postpartum persons" (B recommendation). [[USPSTF2023Depression]]
- The USPSTF recommends that clinicians provide or refer pregnant and postpartum persons at increased risk of perinatal depression to counselling interventions (B recommendation); it found convincing evidence that cognitive behavioural therapy and interpersonal therapy prevent perinatal depression. [[USPSTF2019Perinatal]]
- ACOG Clinical Practice Guideline No. 4 (2023) covers screening and diagnosis of depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder, acute postpartum psychosis and suicidality in pregnancy and the first postpartum year; No. 5 covers treatment, with a focus on psychopharmacotherapy in pregnancy and lactation. [[ACOG2023Screening,ACOG2023Treatment]]

### perinatal · Psychological and preventive treatment
- Psychosocial or psychological interventions reduced the risk of developing postpartum depression compared with standard care (RR 0.78, 95% CI 0.66–0.93; 20 trials, 14,727 women). [[Dennis2013]]
- Promising preventive interventions included intensive home visits by nurses or midwives (RR 0.56, 95% CI 0.43–0.73), peer telephone support (RR 0.54, 95% CI 0.38–0.77) and interpersonal psychotherapy. [[Dennis2013]]
- Most randomised trials in the perinatal period have tested psychosocial and psychological interventions for postnatal depression, with evidence that they treat and prevent it; high-quality trials of medicines are few. [[Howard2014Lancet]]
- Controlled trials have validated individual and group psychological interventions for postpartum depression, whereas trials of antidepressants often showed no significant difference from placebo or active control. [[OHara2013]]

### perinatal · Antidepressants in pregnancy: what the data show
- In a prospective study of 201 pregnant women with a history of major depression who were well on antidepressants, 43% relapsed during pregnancy: 26% (21 of 82) of those who kept taking their medicine and 68% (44 of 65) of those who stopped (HR 5.0, 95% CI 2.8–9.1). [[Cohen2006]]
- The authors concluded that pregnancy is not "protective" against relapse of major depression; the US sertraline label cites this study and tells prescribers to "consider the risks of untreated depression when discontinuing or changing treatment". [[Cohen2006,FDA-Zoloft]]
- In a Medicaid cohort of 949,504 pregnancies, 6.8% of women used an antidepressant in the first trimester; cardiac defects occurred in 72.3 per 10,000 unexposed infants and 90.1 per 10,000 exposed infants. [[Huybrechts2014NEJM]]
- The relative risk of any cardiac defect with SSRIs fell from 1.25 (95% CI 1.13–1.38) unadjusted to 1.06 (95% CI 0.93–1.22) after restriction to women with depression and full adjustment, "no substantial increase" in the authors' words. [[Huybrechts2014NEJM]]
- The earlier specific signals were not confirmed: paroxetine and right ventricular outflow tract obstruction RR 1.07 (95% CI 0.59–1.93); sertraline and ventricular septal defects RR 1.04 (95% CI 0.76–1.41). [[Huybrechts2014NEJM]]
- The sertraline label states that published studies of first-trimester exposure "suggest no difference in major birth defect risk" from the background rate; US background risks are 2–4% for major birth defects and 15–20% for miscarriage. [[FDA-Zoloft]]
- Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN) occurred in 20.8 per 10,000 births without antidepressant exposure, 31.5 per 10,000 with SSRI exposure and 29.1 per 10,000 with non-SSRI exposure in the last 90 days of pregnancy (3,789,330 pregnancies). [[Huybrechts2015]]
- After restriction to women with depression and propensity adjustment the odds ratio for SSRIs and PPHN was 1.10 (95% CI 0.94–1.29), and 1.28 (95% CI 1.01–1.64) for primary PPHN; the authors call the absolute risk small. [[Huybrechts2015]]
- An earlier meta-analysis of seven studies found late-pregnancy SSRI exposure associated with PPHN (OR 2.50, 95% CI 1.32–4.73) but not early exposure (OR 1.23, 95% CI 0.58–2.60); the absolute risk difference was 2.9 to 3.5 per 1,000 infants (286 to 351 women treated per additional case). [[Grigoriadis2014]]
- The sertraline label gives a general-population PPHN rate of 1–2 per 1,000 live births. [[FDA-Zoloft]]
- Neonates exposed to SSRIs or SNRIs late in the third trimester have developed complications needing prolonged hospital stay, respiratory support and tube feeding; reported signs include respiratory distress, feeding difficulty, jitteriness, irritability, tremor and constant crying. [[FDA-Zoloft]]
- Antidepressant use in pregnancy was associated with preterm birth (pooled adjusted OR 1.53, 95% CI 1.40–1.66 for use at any time; 1.96, 95% CI 1.62–2.38 for third-trimester use; 1.16, 95% CI 0.92–1.45 for first-trimester-only exposure); residual confounding by the illness could not be ruled out. [[Huybrechts2014Preterm]]
- SSRI exposure in the month before delivery has been associated with a less than 2-fold increase in the risk of postpartum haemorrhage. [[FDA-Zoloft]]
- Treatment decisions in pregnancy are individual risk-benefit analyses made with the care team: the risks of untreated illness for mother and child sit beside the medicine risks above. [[Howard2014Lancet,Stein2014,FDA-Zoloft]]

### perinatal · Breastfeeding
- Of 167 infant sertraline blood levels in breastfed infants, 146 (87.4%) were below the limit of detection; the review calls sertraline "a first-line drug for breastfeeding women" and routine paediatric care appropriate monitoring. [[Pinheiro2015]]
- The US label reports a pooled analysis of 53 mother-infant pairs in which exclusively human-milk-fed infants had on average 2% (range 0% to 15%) of maternal sertraline serum levels, with no adverse reactions observed. [[FDA-Zoloft]]
- LactMed states that sertraline is usually not detected in infant serum, that no adverse developmental effects were found in infants followed up to 5 years, and that most authoritative reviewers consider it a preferred antidepressant during breastfeeding; preterm infants may rarely accumulate the drug. [[LactMed-Sertraline]]
- Zuranolone passes into milk at low levels: in 14 lactating women the mean relative infant dose was 0.357% of the maternal dose (calculated maximum under 1%); there are no data on effects in breastfed infants. [[FDA-Zurzuvae]]

### perinatal · Neuroactive steroids: brexanolone and zuranolone
- Brexanolone, an intravenous GABA-A receptor positive allosteric modulator given as a single 60-hour infusion, reduced HAM-D scores at 60 hours by 17.7 points (90 μg/kg/h) and 19.5 points (60 μg/kg/h) versus 14.0 with placebo in study 1 (differences −3.7 and −5.5), and by 14.6 versus 12.1 in study 2 (difference −2.5, 95% CI −4.5 to −0.5). (Established) [[MeltzerBrody2018]]
- In the brexanolone trials the commonest adverse events were headache, dizziness and somnolence, and one participant had an altered state of consciousness and syncope judged treatment-related. [[MeltzerBrody2018]]
- ROBIN trial: zuranolone 30 mg each evening for 2 weeks (n = 153; baseline HAMD-17 26 or higher) improved HAMD-17 at day 15 by −17.8 versus −13.6 with placebo (difference −4.2, 95% CI −6.9 to −1.5), with differences from day 3 (−2.7) through day 45 (−4.1). (Established) [[Deligiannidis2021ROBIN]]
- In ROBIN, day-15 response (odds ratio 2.63, 95% CI 1.34–5.16) and remission (odds ratio 2.53, 95% CI 1.24–5.17) favoured zuranolone. [[Deligiannidis2021ROBIN]]
- SKYLARK trial: zuranolone 50 mg/day for 14 days (196 randomised; 170 completed the 45-day study) improved HAM-D at day 15 by −15.6 versus −11.6 (difference −4.0, 95% CI −6.3 to −1.7), with significant differences at days 3, 28 and 45. (Established) [[Deligiannidis2023SKYLARK]]
- The US label indicates zuranolone for postpartum depression in adults; the recommended dosage as published is 50 mg orally once daily in the evening for 14 days with fat-containing food, reducible to 40 mg if CNS depressant effects occur, used alone or with an oral antidepressant. [[FDA-Zurzuvae]]
- Zuranolone carries a boxed warning for impaired ability to drive: patients are advised not to drive or do other potentially hazardous activities until at least 12 hours after each dose for the whole 14-day course, and may not be able to judge their own impairment. [[FDA-Zurzuvae]]
- Somnolence occurred in 36% of people taking zuranolone 50 mg versus 6% on placebo in Study 1; zuranolone is a Schedule IV controlled substance. [[FDA-Zurzuvae]]
- The zuranolone trials enrolled women with severe symptoms (baseline HAMD-17 26 or higher) and followed them for 45 days, so evidence for milder illness and for longer-term outcomes is limited. (Early evidence) [[Deligiannidis2023SKYLARK,FDA-Zurzuvae]]

### young · How common and how it presents
- Worldwide, the pooled prevalence of any depressive disorder in children and adolescents is 2.6% (95% CI 1.7–3.9), from 41 studies in 27 countries. [[Polanczyk2015]]
- In adolescents aged 10 to 19 the pooled point prevalence of major depressive disorder is 8% (95% CI 2–13), one-year prevalence 8% (95% CI 5–12) and lifetime prevalence 19% (95% CI 12–26). [[Shorey2022]]
- Elevated self-reported depressive symptoms were found in 34% of adolescents (95% CI 30–38), rising from 24% in 2001–2010 to 37% in 2011–2020, and were more common in girls. [[Shorey2022]]
- In the US National Surveys on Drug Use and Health the 12-month prevalence of major depressive episodes in adolescents rose from 8.7% in 2005 to 11.3% in 2014, with little change in mental health treatment. [[Mojtabai2016]]
- Between 2009 and 2019 past-year major depressive episodes in US adolescents aged 12–17 (National Survey on Drug Use and Health, N = 167,783) rose from 8.1% to 15.8%, and from 11.4% to 23.4% among girls. [[Daly2022]]
- For mood disorders, 2.5% of cases begin before age 14, 11.5% before 18 and 34.5% before 25, with a peak age at onset of 20.5 years. [[Solmi2022]]
- DSM-5-TR allows irritable mood to stand in for depressed mood in children and adolescents. [[APA2022DSM5TR]] (unverified: book text not opened)

### young · Screening and primary care guidance
- The USPSTF recommends screening for major depressive disorder in adolescents aged 12 to 18 years (B recommendation) and finds the evidence insufficient for children aged 11 or younger (I statement). [[USPSTF2022Youth]]
- The USPSTF finds the evidence insufficient to assess screening for suicide risk in children and adolescents (I statement). [[USPSTF2022Youth]]
- In adolescents a PHQ-9 score of 11 or more had a sensitivity of 89.5% and a specificity of 77.5% for major depression (area under the curve 0.88, 95% CI 0.82–0.94); the optimal cut-off is higher than in adults. [[Richardson2010]]
- GLAD-PC (ages 10 to 21) recommends annual universal screening of young people aged 12 and over at health maintenance visits, systematic assessment with depression scales plus patient and caregiver interviews, patient and family psychoeducation, community links and a safety plan. [[Zuckerbrot2018]]
- For treatment GLAD-PC recommends active monitoring for mild depression, evidence-based psychotherapy and/or medication for moderate or severe depression, close monitoring of side effects, consultation and co-management with mental health specialists, and defined steps when there is partial or no improvement. [[Cheung2018]]

### young · Treatment trials
- TADS (439 adolescents, 12 weeks): response rates were 71.0% (95% CI 62–80) with fluoxetine plus CBT, 60.6% (95% CI 51–70) with fluoxetine alone, 43.2% (95% CI 34–52) with CBT alone and 34.8% (95% CI 26–44) with placebo. [[March2004TADS]]
- In TADS clinically significant suicidal thinking, present in 29% at baseline, improved in all four groups, most with the combination; there were no deaths by suicide. [[March2004TADS]]
- TADS long-term: response was 73%, 62% and 48% at week 12, 85%, 69% and 65% at week 18, and 86%, 81% and 81% at week 36 for combination, fluoxetine and CBT respectively, so the treatments converged while medication sped up response. [[March2007TADS]]
- Over 36 weeks suicidal events were more common with fluoxetine alone (14.7%) than with combination therapy (8.4%) or CBT alone (6.3%); the authors concluded that adding CBT enhances the safety of medication. [[March2007TADS]]
- TORDIA (334 adolescents not responding to a first SSRI): CBT plus a medication switch gave a 54.8% response versus 40.5% for a medication switch alone; switching to venlafaxine was no better than a second SSRI (48.2% vs 47.0%) and had more adverse effects. [[Brent2008TORDIA]]
- ADAPT (208 adolescents aged 11–17 in UK outpatient clinics): adding CBT to an SSRI plus routine care did not improve outcome by 28 weeks; 57% were much or very much improved and 20% remained unimproved. [[Goodyer2007ADAPT]]
- IMPACT (470 adolescents aged 11–17 in English CAMHS clinics): CBT and short-term psychoanalytical psychotherapy were not superior to a brief psychosocial intervention at 36, 52 or 86 weeks, which widens the choice of psychological therapies. [[Goodyer2017IMPACT]]
- Network meta-analysis of 34 trials (5,260 participants, 14 antidepressants): only fluoxetine was significantly more effective than placebo (SMD −0.51, 95% CrI −0.99 to −0.03); most evidence was rated very low quality. [[Cipriani2016]]
- In that analysis imipramine, venlafaxine and duloxetine caused more discontinuations for adverse events than placebo. [[Cipriani2016]]
- A larger network meta-analysis (71 trials, 9,510 participants) found only fluoxetine plus CBT and fluoxetine alone more effective than pill placebo or psychological controls, and interpersonal therapy more effective than all psychological controls; no medicine alone beat psychotherapy alone. [[Zhou2020]]
- Across 27 paediatric trials antidepressants improved response in major depression by a risk difference of 11.0% (95% CI 7.1–14.9; number needed to treat 10); in children under 12 with major depression only fluoxetine showed benefit over placebo. [[Bridge2007]]
- US labels: fluoxetine's efficacy in major depressive disorder was shown in paediatric outpatients aged 8 to 18, and escitalopram is indicated for major depressive disorder in patients aged 12 years and older. [[FDA-Prozac,FDA-Lexapro]]

### young · The boxed warning and what followed
- The FDA analysis of 24 placebo-controlled paediatric trials (4,582 patients) found a risk ratio for suicidal thoughts or behaviour of 1.66 (95% CI 1.02–2.68) for SSRIs in depression trials and 1.95 (95% CI 1.28–2.98) for all drugs and indications, a risk difference of 0.02 (95% CI 0.01–0.03); no one died by suicide in any trial. [[Hammad2006]]
- Current labels express this as the drug-minus-placebo difference per 1,000 patients treated: 14 additional cases under age 18, 5 additional cases at 18–24, 1 fewer case at 25–64 and 6 fewer cases at 65 and over. [[FDA-Prozac]]
- The boxed warning asks for close monitoring of people of all ages starting antidepressants for worsening or emerging suicidal thoughts, and for families and caregivers to be told to watch closely and communicate with the prescriber. [[FDA-Prozac]]
- A later meta-analysis put the pooled risk difference for suicidal ideation or attempt at 0.7% (95% CI 0.1–1.3; number needed to harm 143) across indications and judged the benefits "much greater" than these risks. [[Bridge2007]]
- After the warnings SSRI prescriptions for young people fell by about 22% in both the United States and the Netherlands; youth suicide rates rose by 14% in the US (2003–2004) and by 49% in the Netherlands (2003–2005). [[Gibbons2007]]
- In 11 US health plans, antidepressant use in the second year after the warnings fell by 31.0% in adolescents, 24.3% in young adults and 14.5% in adults, while a validated proxy measure of suicide attempts rose by 21.7% in adolescents and 33.7% in young adults; deaths by suicide did not change in any age group. [[Lu2014]]
- These are ecological and quasi-experimental associations, not proof that the warning caused harm; they are the basis for the argument that under-treatment of depression also carries risk. [[Gibbons2007,Lu2014]]

### young · Course into adulthood
- In the 5-year TADS follow-up, 96.4% of adolescents recovered from the index episode, and 46.6% of those who recovered had a recurrence (57.0% of females, 32.9% of males). [[Curry2011]]
- Recovery by 2 years was more likely in short-term responders (96.2%) than in partial or non-responders (79.1%). [[Curry2011]]
- Adolescent-onset major depression shows continuity into adulthood: 37% of those affected had no adult episode compared with 69% of controls (relative risk of adult MDD 2.2, 95% CI 1.0–4.7), with impaired work, social and family functioning. [[Weissman1999]]

### living · Later life: prevalence and presentation
- Major depression occurs in about 2% of adults aged 55 or older and rises with age; a further 10% to 15% of older adults have clinically significant depressive symptoms without major depression. [[Kok2017]]
- In people aged 75 and over, pooled prevalence was 7.2% (95% CI 4.4–10.6) for major depression and 17.1% (95% CI 9.7–26.1) for depressive disorders. [[Luppa2012]]
- Depression presents with the same symptoms in older as in younger adults, but older adults more often have several medical disorders and cognitive impairment, and their depression is often undetected or under-treated. [[Kok2017]]
- In later life depression mainly affects people with chronic medical illness and cognitive impairment, worsens the outcomes of many medical illnesses and increases mortality; psychosocial adversity such as isolation, bereavement, disability and caregiving adds to risk. [[Alexopoulos2005]]
- The vascular depression hypothesis proposes that cerebrovascular disease may "predispose, precipitate, or perpetuate" some late-life depressive syndromes by disrupting prefrontal systems. (Hypothesis) [[Alexopoulos1997]]
- Late-life depression is associated with later dementia: pooled risk 1.85 (95% CI 1.67–2.04) for all-cause dementia, 1.65 (95% CI 1.42–1.92) for Alzheimer's disease and 2.52 (95% CI 1.77–3.59) for vascular dementia in community cohorts. [[Diniz2013]]
- The Geriatric Depression Scale was built as a 30-item self-rating scale for older people; the 15-item version has pooled sensitivity 0.89 (95% CI 0.80–0.94) and specificity 0.77 (95% CI 0.65–0.86) at the recommended cut-off of 5. [[Yesavage1982,Pocklington2016]]
- The USPSTF recommendation to screen adults for depression explicitly includes older adults. [[USPSTF2023Depression]]

### living · Later life: treatment response and safety
- In placebo-controlled trials in older adults, the number needed to treat with an antidepressant was 6.7 (95% CI 4.8–10) for response and 14.4 (95% CI 8.3–50) for remission, with no difference between drug classes. [[Kok2012]]
- Psychotherapy is recommended for mild to moderate depression in older adults; high-quality evidence does not support antidepressants for depression in people with dementia. [[Kok2017]]
- IMPACT collaborative care (1,801 adults aged 60 and over; a depression care manager supervised by a psychiatrist and a primary care expert): at 12 months 45% had a 50% or greater fall in symptoms versus 19% with usual care (OR 3.45, 95% CI 2.71–4.38). [[Unutzer2002IMPACT]]
- PROSPECT (598 older primary care patients with depression; care management plus tailored guidelines): suicidal ideation fell by 12.9 percentage points (29.4% to 16.5%) at 4 months versus 3.0 points (20.1% to 17.1%) with usual care. [[Bruce2004]]
- In a UK cohort of 60,746 people aged 65 and over with depression, SSRIs were associated with falls (adjusted HR 1.66, 95% CI 1.58–1.73) and hyponatraemia (HR 1.52, 95% CI 1.33–1.75) compared with periods of no antidepressant use; the study is observational and open to confounding by indication. [[Coupland2011]]
- Hyponatraemia with SSRIs is usually due to SIADH; older people and people taking diuretics or who are volume-depleted are at greater risk, and signs include headache, difficulty concentrating, confusion, weakness and unsteadiness that may lead to falls. [[FDA-Prozac]]
- Reported hyponatraemia rates differ by class: 7.44% with SNRIs, 5.59% with SSRIs, 2.66% with tricyclics, 1.02% with mirtazapine and 0.89% with trazodone in a meta-analysis of 39 studies. [[Gheysens2024]]
- Older age (OR 6.3) and thiazide diuretics (OR 11.2–13.5) are the main patient risk factors for antidepressant-associated hyponatraemia. [[DePicker2014]]
- ECT in older adults: in PRIDE phase 1 (240 patients with geriatric depression, right unilateral ultrabrief pulse ECT plus venlafaxine) 61.7% remitted and 70% responded, after a mean of 7.3 treatments. [[Kellner2016PRIDE1]]
- In PRIDE phase 2, continuation ECT plus medication gave HAM-D scores 4.2 points lower (95% CI 1.6–6.9) at 24 weeks than medication alone, with no difference in MMSE score. [[Kellner2016PRIDE2]]
- In the CORE study of bilateral ECT (253 patients), remission was 90% at age 65 and over, 89.8% at 46–64 and 70% at 45 and under; older age predicted a better response. [[OConnor2001]]
- OPTIMUM (619 adults aged 60 and over with treatment-resistant depression): remission occurred in 28.9% with aripiprazole augmentation, 28.2% with bupropion augmentation and 19.3% after a switch to bupropion; falls were most frequent with bupropion augmentation. [[Lenze2023]]
- In OPTIMUM step 2, remission was 18.9% with lithium augmentation and 21.5% after a switch to nortriptyline, with similar rates of falls. [[Lenze2023]]

### living · Work, routine and relapse prevention
- Compared with people without depression, people with depression have more unemployment, more absence and more at-work performance problems; the work impact tracks symptom severity, and symptom relief only partly reverses it. [[Lerner2008]]
- US workers with depression reported 5.6 hours per week of lost productive time versus an expected 1.5 hours; 81% of the cost came from reduced performance while at work rather than absence. [[Stewart2003]]
- Adding a work-directed intervention to clinical treatment probably reduces sickness absence (SMD −0.25, 95% CI −0.38 to −0.12; about 25 fewer sick days over a year; moderate-certainty evidence). [[Nieuwenhuijsen2020]]
- Psychological interventions may reduce sickness absence days (SMD −0.15, 95% CI −0.28 to −0.03) as well as depressive symptoms, compared with care as usual (low-certainty evidence). [[Nieuwenhuijsen2020]]
- Many people stop antidepressants early: in the US Medical Expenditure Panel Survey (829 adults starting treatment) 42.4% stopped in the first 30 days and only 27.6% continued for more than 90 days; continuing was more likely with psychotherapy (68.0% vs 43.7% beyond 30 days). [[Olfson2006]]
- A review of the previous decade's data put non-adherence at about 50% of patients six months after starting an antidepressant, with side-effect worries, fear of addiction and poor follow-up among the reasons. [[Sansone2012]]
- In ANTLER (478 primary care patients who felt well enough to stop long-term antidepressants) relapse by 52 weeks occurred in 56% of those assigned to discontinue and 39% of those who continued (HR 2.06, 95% CI 1.56–2.70). [[Lewis2021]]
- Stopping an SSRI, particularly abruptly, can cause discontinuation symptoms such as dizziness, irritability, sensory disturbances, anxiety, insomnia and headache; labels recommend gradual dose reduction "whenever possible", so changes are made with the prescriber. [[FDA-Prozac,FDA-Zoloft]]
- Early warning signs before a depressive episode are commonly anxiety, tension, irritability and somatic complaints; the pattern tends to be consistent within a person from one episode to the next, which is the basis for personal relapse-signature plans. [[Benasi2021]]
- The CANMAT 2023 guideline frames care as a journey from assessment through treatment selection to prevention of recurrence, built on shared decision-making that reflects the person's values, preferences and treatment history. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Insomnia is a risk marker: non-depressed people with insomnia had about twice the odds of developing depression (OR 2.10, 95% CI 1.86–2.38). [[Baglioni2011]]
- Alcohol use disorder and major depression each roughly double the risk of the other (pooled adjusted OR 2.00–2.09), and the more plausible causal direction is from alcohol to depression. [[Boden2011]]
- Driving and medicines: zuranolone carries a boxed driving warning (no driving for at least 12 hours after each dose), esketamine patients must not drive until the next day after a restful sleep, and the fluoxetine label advises caution with machinery because of possible cognitive and motor impairment. [[FDA-Zurzuvae,FDA-Spravato,FDA-Prozac]]

### living · Stigma, disclosure, men and culture
- In a 35-country survey of 1,082 people with major depressive disorder, 79% reported discrimination in at least one life domain. [[Lasalvia2013]]
- Anticipated discrimination limited lives: 37% had stopped themselves from starting a close relationship, 25% from applying for work and 20% from applying for education or training; nearly half of those who anticipated discrimination had not actually experienced it. [[Lasalvia2013]]
- Experienced discrimination was associated with lower willingness to disclose a diagnosis of depression, and non-disclosure is itself a barrier to seeking help. [[Lasalvia2013]]
- For comparison, in the parallel survey of people with schizophrenia 72% felt the need to conceal their diagnosis. [[Thornicroft2009]]
- Stigma has a small to moderate negative effect on help-seeking (median d = −0.27 across 144 studies and 90,189 participants); internalised stigma and concerns about disclosure matter most, and young people, men, ethnic minorities and health and military professionals are disproportionately deterred. [[Clement2015]]
- Both education and contact with people with lived experience reduce public stigma; for adults contact works better than education, and face-to-face contact better than video. [[Corrigan2012]]
- Conformity to traditional masculine norms shapes how men express depressive symptoms, their attitudes to and use of help, and how they manage symptoms. [[Seidler2016]]
- When anger attacks or aggression, substance misuse and risk-taking were counted alongside traditional symptoms, men and women met criteria for depression in equal proportions (30.6% vs 33.3%). (Early evidence) [[Martin2013]]
- Physical presentation is common everywhere: in a WHO primary care study at 15 centres in 14 countries, 69% of patients with major depression (range 45% to 95% across centres) reported only somatic symptoms, and 11% denied psychological symptoms on direct questioning. [[Simon1999]]

### living · Families, peers, shared decisions and apps
- About 25% to 50% of caregivers of people with depression worried about the person's health, treatment, safety and future; caregivers often felt distressed themselves, and children in the household were affected. [[vanWijngaarden2004]]
- Family atmosphere affects course: over nine months 59% of patients with depression living with a highly critical ("high expressed emotion") spouse relapsed, and none of those with a low expressed emotion spouse (39 patients). [[Hooley1986]]
- In meta-analysis, expressed emotion predicted relapse more strongly in mood disorders than in schizophrenia. [[Butzlaff1998,Hooley2007]]
- Guidance for adolescents builds family involvement in from the start, through caregiver interviews, family psychoeducation and a shared safety plan. [[Zuckerbrot2018]]
- Peer support reduced depressive symptoms more than usual care (pooled SMD −0.59, 95% CI −0.98 to −0.21; 7 trials, 869 participants) and did not differ significantly from group CBT (SMD 0.10, 95% CI −0.20 to 0.39). [[Pfeiffer2011]]
- A shared decision-making intervention in primary care increased patient participation and satisfaction without lengthening consultations, but did not change depression severity. [[Loh2007]]
- Smartphone apps reduced depressive symptoms modestly: g = 0.38 (95% CI 0.24–0.52) across 18 trials, falling to g = 0.22 against active controls. (Early evidence) [[Firth2017Apps]]
- An updated meta-analysis of 176 trials found a small effect of apps on depressive symptoms (g = 0.28; number needed to treat 11.5), larger when apps used CBT features or chatbot technology. (Established) [[Linardon2024]]

### nursing · Assessment, screening and asking about suicide
- The PHQ-9 scores each of the nine DSM-IV criteria from 0 (not at all) to 3 (nearly every day); scores of 5, 10, 15 and 20 mark mild, moderate, moderately severe and severe depression, and a score of 10 or more had 88% sensitivity and 88% specificity for major depression. [[Kroenke2001PHQ9]]
- Rising PHQ-9 scores track falling functional status and more sick days and health care use, which is why the score is recorded alongside function. [[Kroenke2001PHQ9]]
- Asking about suicide does not plant the idea: a review of studies in adults and adolescents found none showing a significant increase in suicidal ideation after being asked, and suggested that talking about it may reduce ideation. [[Dazzi2014]]
- The Joint Commission's National Patient Safety Goal 15.01.01 requires accredited hospitals to screen all patients aged 12 and over whose primary reason for care is a behavioural health condition for suicidal ideation, using a validated tool. [[Chitavi2025]]
- In a survey of accredited general hospitals (284 responders), 79.2% screened all patients hospital-wide and 65.1% had protocols for positive screens and risk assessment; insufficient staffing was among the challenges reported. [[Chitavi2025]]
- Antidepressant labels list symptoms to report when treatment starts or the dose changes: anxiety, agitation, panic attacks, insomnia, irritability, hostility, impulsivity, akathisia, and hypomania or mania. [[FDA-Prozac]]

### nursing · Observation levels and safety planning
- Constant observation is widely used for inpatients at risk of harming themselves, but there is little evidence on its efficacy and no consensus on policy; it can be restrictive and distressing if overused. [[Reen2020,Holland2026]]
- Teamwork interventions, more therapeutic engagement and involving patients in problem-solving reduced constant observation without increasing high-risk behaviour in some studies. [[Reen2020,Holland2026]]
- One practice paper argues that the absence of trial evidence should not lead to under-use of observation in high-risk situations, and that units should agree a clear observation protocol. [[Russ2016]]
- The Safety Planning Intervention is a brief clinical intervention built around a prioritised list of coping skills and strategies. [[Stanley2012SPI,Stanley2018SafetyPlan]]
- In emergency departments, safety planning plus at least two follow-up telephone contacts was associated with fewer suicidal behaviours over 6 months (3.03% vs 5.29%; OR 0.56, 95% CI 0.33–0.95) and double the odds of attending outpatient mental health care (OR 2.06, 95% CI 1.57–2.71). [[Stanley2018SafetyPlan]]
- GLAD-PC includes establishing a safety plan as part of initial management of adolescent depression in primary care. [[Zuckerbrot2018]]

### nursing · Medication teaching points from labels
- Onset: "the full effect may be delayed until 4 weeks of treatment or longer", so early lack of benefit is expected and is not a reason to stop without advice. [[FDA-Prozac]]
- Early effects: in US trials 12% to 16% of people taking fluoxetine for depression reported anxiety, nervousness or insomnia, versus 7% to 9% on placebo. [[FDA-Prozac]]
- Stopping: gradual dose reduction rather than abrupt cessation is recommended whenever possible, and patients are monitored for discontinuation symptoms. [[FDA-Prozac,FDA-Zoloft]]
- Serotonin syndrome signs listed in labels: mental status changes (agitation, hallucinations, delirium), autonomic instability (tachycardia, labile blood pressure, sweating, hyperthermia), neuromuscular signs (tremor, rigidity, myoclonus, hyperreflexia) and gastrointestinal symptoms. [[FDA-Prozac]]
- Risk of serotonin syndrome rises with other serotonergic drugs (triptans, tricyclics, fentanyl, lithium, tramadol, tryptophan, buspirone, amphetamines, St John's wort); SSRIs are contraindicated with MAOIs. [[FDA-Prozac]]
- MAOI diet: tranylcypromine carries a boxed warning for hypertensive crisis with significant tyramine intake; patients are told to avoid high-tyramine foods and drinks during treatment and for 2 weeks after stopping. [[FDA-Parnate]]
- Hypertensive crisis warning signs: occipital headache, palpitations, neck stiffness, nausea or vomiting, sweating, dilated pupils and photophobia; blood pressure above 180/120 mm Hg with organ dysfunction is an emergency. [[FDA-Parnate]]
- Older adults on SSRIs or SNRIs: watch for hyponatraemia (headache, confusion, weakness, unsteadiness) and for falls, especially with diuretics. [[FDA-Prozac,Coupland2011]]
- Young people and families: the boxed warning applies up to age 24, and families are asked to observe closely and stay in contact with the prescriber. [[FDA-Prozac]]

### nursing · Serotonin syndrome versus neuroleptic malignant syndrome
- The Hunter Serotonin Toxicity Criteria use seven features (clonus that is spontaneous, inducible or ocular; agitation; diaphoresis; tremor; hyperreflexia; hypertonicity; temperature above 38 °C) and were more sensitive (84% vs 75%) and more specific (97% vs 96%) than Sternbach's criteria. [[Dunkley2003]]
- Under the Hunter rules, in someone taking a serotonergic drug, spontaneous clonus alone is enough; inducible or ocular clonus needs agitation or diaphoresis; tremor needs hyperreflexia; hypertonicity needs fever above 38 °C plus ocular or inducible clonus. [[Francescangeli2019]]
- Serotonin syndrome follows soon after exposure to a serotonergic medicine and is marked by clonus, hyperreflexia and hyperactive bowel sounds; neuroleptic malignant syndrome follows dopamine antagonists, typically develops over several days, and shows rigidity and bradykinesia without clonus or hyperreflexia. [[Francescangeli2019]]
- Mild serotonin syndrome can look like diarrhoea and flu-like symptoms and be missed; untreated it can progress to rigidity and severe hyperthermia. [[Francescangeli2019]]
- Management is to stop the serotonergic medicines and give supportive care; about 70% of patients recover completely within 24 hours and 40% need intensive care. [[Francescangeli2019]]

### nursing · ECT care before, during and after
- ECT is a controlled procedure in which a seizure is induced under anaesthesia; nurses provide education and support, help with informed consent, and care for clinical and psychological needs before and after treatment. [[Kavanagh2009]]
- Consent: written informed consent is taken before starting ECT, based on shared decision-making, with written information and time for questions. [[Thirthalli2023ECT]]
- Fasting as published in the Indian Psychiatric Society clinical practice guideline: clear liquids until 2 hours before, milk and light meals until 6 hours before, and fried or fatty food or meat until 8 hours before; the scalp should be clean and dry. [[Thirthalli2023ECT]]
- The same guideline defines an ECT nurse (pre-ECT assessment and consent, assisting anaesthesia, monitoring vital signs) and a recovery nurse (vital signs, pulse oximetry, ECG, oxygen, suction, and supportive care for disorientation or agitation). [[Thirthalli2023ECT]]
- Equipment in the treatment room includes a bite block, EEG and ECG monitors, anaesthetic and muscle relaxant drugs, emergency medicines, suction and a defibrillator. [[Thirthalli2023ECT]]
- Recovery: pulse, blood pressure and oxygen saturation are monitored; the patient is watched for agitation or delirium, aspiration and arrhythmia; nothing is given by mouth until full consciousness returns; orientation and gait are checked before leaving recovery. [[Thirthalli2023ECT]]
- Post-ictal confusion: orientation generally returns within 5 to 45 minutes; longer or severe confusion calls for a safe, low-stimulation environment and attention to airway, breathing and circulation. [[Thirthalli2023ECT]]
- Common after-effects are headache (generally mild) and muscle soreness (worst after the first session). [[Thirthalli2023ECT]]
- Acute courses are given 2 to 3 times a week; the number of treatments is not fixed in advance, and progress and cognitive effects are reviewed at least weekly. [[Thirthalli2023ECT]]
- Cognitive effects are mainly limited to the first 3 days after a treatment; by 15 days after ECT no measure was below baseline and several improved beyond it (84 studies, 2,981 patients). [[Semkovska2010]]
- ECT-related mortality is estimated at 2.1 per 100,000 treatments (95% CI 1.2–3.4), with one death reported in 414,747 treatments in studies published after 2001. [[Torring2017]]

### nursing · Esketamine clinic monitoring and behavioural activation
- Esketamine nasal spray is given only in certified healthcare settings under a REMS, self-administered under direct observation by a healthcare provider. [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Before dosing: blood pressure is assessed (above 140/90 mm Hg prompts a risk-benefit review); patients are advised to avoid food for at least 2 hours and liquids for at least 30 minutes beforehand. [[FDA-Spravato]]
- After dosing: blood pressure is reassessed at about 40 minutes (when the rise peaks; it lasts about 4 hours), respiratory status is monitored with pulse oximetry, and the patient is observed for at least 2 hours until safe to leave. [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Sedation occurred in 48% to 61% of esketamine-treated patients in trials and loss of consciousness in 0.3% to 0.4%; systolic rises of 40 mm Hg or more (or diastolic 25 mm Hg or more) occurred in about 3% to 19%. [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Patients must arrange transport home and not drive or operate machinery until the next day after a restful sleep. [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Behavioural activation delivered by junior mental health workers was non-inferior to CBT delivered by therapists (PHQ-9 at 12 months 8.4 vs 8.4; difference 0.1, 95% CI −1.3 to 1.5), supporting activity scheduling by non-specialist staff. [[Richards2016COBRA]]
- Collaborative care built around a depression care manager who offers education, care management and support for antidepressant treatment or brief psychotherapy more than doubled the 12-month response rate in older adults (45% vs 19%). [[Unutzer2002IMPACT]]

### Exam hooks
- EPDS: 10 items; cut-off 11 or higher gives sensitivity 0.81 and specificity 0.88; 13 or higher is more specific (0.95) but less sensitive (0.66). [[Cox1987,Levis2020EPDS]]
- Postpartum psychosis is a psychiatric emergency, with incidence estimates from 0.25 to 2.6 per 1,000 births, and is usually linked to the bipolar spectrum. [[Bergink2016,VanderKruik2017]]
- A positive postpartum depression screen needs a bipolar check: 22.6% of screen-positive women had a bipolar disorder. [[Wisner2013]]
- Stopping antidepressants around conception: 68% relapsed versus 26% who continued. [[Cohen2006]]
- First-trimester SSRIs and cardiac defects: adjusted RR 1.06 (95% CI 0.93–1.22). [[Huybrechts2014NEJM]]
- PPHN: about 21 per 10,000 births unexposed versus about 32 per 10,000 with late SSRI exposure. [[Huybrechts2015]]
- Zuranolone: 50 mg nightly for 14 days as labelled; boxed warning about driving for 12 hours after each dose. [[FDA-Zurzuvae]]
- Sertraline is a preferred antidepressant during breastfeeding; infant levels are usually undetectable. [[LactMed-Sertraline,Pinheiro2015]]
- TADS week-12 response: combination 71.0%, fluoxetine 60.6%, CBT 43.2%, placebo 34.8%. [[March2004TADS]]
- Only fluoxetine beat placebo in the paediatric network meta-analysis. [[Cipriani2016]]
- FDA approvals for depression in young people: fluoxetine from age 8, escitalopram from age 12. [[FDA-Prozac,FDA-Lexapro]]
- Boxed warning: 14 additional cases of suicidal thoughts or behaviour per 1,000 treated under 18; 6 fewer per 1,000 at 65 and over; no deaths by suicide in the paediatric trials. [[FDA-Prozac,Hammad2006]]
- USPSTF: screen adolescents 12 to 18 for major depressive disorder (B); insufficient evidence at 11 and under (I). [[USPSTF2022Youth]]
- Adolescent PHQ-9 cut-off of 11: sensitivity 89.5%, specificity 77.5%. [[Richardson2010]]
- Older adults on SSRIs: think hyponatraemia (SIADH) and falls. [[Coupland2011,FDA-Prozac]]
- GDS-15 cut-off 5: sensitivity 0.89, specificity 0.77. [[Pocklington2016]]
- Hunter criteria: clonus is the key sign; sensitivity 84%, specificity 97%. [[Dunkley2003]]
- Serotonin syndrome: fast onset, clonus and hyperreflexia; NMS: slower onset, rigidity and bradykinesia, no clonus. [[Francescangeli2019]]
- MAOIs: avoid high-tyramine foods during treatment and for 2 weeks after; occipital headache signals hypertensive crisis. [[FDA-Parnate]]
- Esketamine: observe at least 2 hours, recheck blood pressure at about 40 minutes, no driving until the next day. [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Asking about suicide does not increase suicidal ideation. [[Dazzi2014]]

### In plain words
- Depression in pregnancy or after a birth is common, affecting up to about 1 in 7 women, and it is treatable. [[USPSTF2019Perinatal]]
- The "baby blues" after a birth are very common, affecting about 4 in 10 women; postpartum depression is a separate, treatable illness that affects up to about 1 in 5 in the first three months. [[RezaieKeikhaie2020,Gavin2005]]
- Hearing or seeing things, or very confused or racing thoughts, soon after a birth is an emergency and needs urgent medical help the same day. [[Bergink2016]]
- A screening questionnaire such as the EPDS shows who should talk to a clinician; it cannot diagnose depression by itself. [[Levis2020EPDS]]
- Talking therapies help prevent and treat depression around a birth. [[USPSTF2019Perinatal,Dennis2013]]
- Decisions about antidepressants in pregnancy and breastfeeding are made with your care team, weighing the risks of untreated depression against the small risks of the medicine. [[Cohen2006,Huybrechts2014NEJM,FDA-Zoloft]]
- Fathers and partners can develop depression around a birth too, about 1 in 10. [[Paulson2010]]
- Teenagers with depression may seem irritable more than sad; most recover with treatment, though it can come back, so follow-up matters. [[Curry2011]]
- For teenagers, talking therapy and fluoxetine both have evidence, and the two together had the highest response at 12 weeks in the TADS trial. [[March2004TADS,March2007TADS]]
- Young people starting an antidepressant are checked closely in the first weeks, and families are asked to tell the prescriber about any worsening or new thoughts of self-harm. [[FDA-Prozac]]
- Depression in later life is common and often missed, but it can be treated effectively; team-based care more than doubled response rates in one large trial. [[Kok2017,Unutzer2002IMPACT]]
- Antidepressants take time, often 4 weeks or longer for the full effect; stopping suddenly can cause unpleasant symptoms, so changes are planned with the prescriber. [[FDA-Prozac]]
- Many people with depression hide it for fear of being judged; most have met discrimination, and support from family, peers and clinicians makes seeking help easier. [[Lasalvia2013,Clement2015,Pfeiffer2011]]
- Asking someone directly whether they are thinking about suicide does not put the idea in their head, and it can open the way to help. [[Dazzi2014]]
- A calm, supportive home, rather than criticism, is linked with staying well. [[Hooley1986]]

## Open questions and controversies
- Do SSRIs in late pregnancy cause PPHN? An earlier meta-analysis found OR 2.50 for late exposure [[Grigoriadis2014]]; the largest confounding-adjusted cohort found OR 1.10 (95% CI 0.94–1.29) overall and 1.28 for primary PPHN [[Huybrechts2015]]; status: a small absolute increase cannot be excluded.
- Are antidepressants or the underlying illness responsible for preterm birth? Pooled adjusted OR 1.53, persisting after control for a depression diagnosis [[Huybrechts2014Preterm]]; residual confounding by severity remains possible; status: unresolved.
- Which EPDS cut-off? 11 or higher maximises combined accuracy, 13 or higher is more specific, 10 or higher more sensitive [[Levis2020EPDS]]; status: choice depends on whether missed cases or false positives matter more.
- Do antidepressants work for postpartum depression specifically? Trials often show no difference from placebo or active control [[OHara2013]] while psychological interventions have the larger trial base [[Howard2014Lancet]]; neuroactive steroids have short-term placebo-controlled evidence only [[Deligiannidis2023SKYLARK]]; status: head-to-head and long-term data are lacking.
- Do antidepressants help adolescents? Only fluoxetine beat placebo in network meta-analysis with mostly very-low-quality evidence [[Cipriani2016]]; the pooled benefit across trials is a number needed to treat of 10 [[Bridge2007]]; status: fluoxetine alone or with CBT is the best-supported option [[Zhou2020]].
- Does CBT add to medication in adolescents? Yes in TADS at 12 weeks and in TORDIA [[March2004TADS,Brent2008TORDIA]]; no in ADAPT [[Goodyer2007ADAPT]]; status: differences in severity and background care may explain the conflict.
- Did the 2004 boxed warning do net harm? Prescribing fell and attempt proxies or youth suicide rates rose in ecological and quasi-experimental studies [[Gibbons2007,Lu2014]]; the trial signal that prompted it is real but small and involved no deaths [[Hammad2006]]; status: debated, causality unproven.
- Is "vascular depression" a distinct entity? Proposed on the basis of comorbidity and lesion patterns [[Alexopoulos1997]]; late-life depression is more strongly linked to vascular dementia than to Alzheimer's disease [[Diniz2013]]; status: hypothesis, and whether depression is a risk factor or a prodrome of dementia is open.
- Does constant observation prevent harm? Little evidence of efficacy and no consensus [[Reen2020,Holland2026]]; under-use in high-risk situations is also a concern [[Russ2016]]; status: unresolved, practice rests on policy rather than trials.
- Do mental health apps help? Small average effects (g = 0.28) [[Linardon2024]], smaller against active controls [[Firth2017Apps]]; status: a modest adjunct, not a substitute for care.
- Are men under-counted? Equal prevalence when externalising symptoms are counted in one survey analysis [[Martin2013]]; status: early evidence, not part of diagnostic criteria.
