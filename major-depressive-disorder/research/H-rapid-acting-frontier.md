## Findings

### ketamine · History and first trials
- The first placebo-controlled, double-blind trial of an NMDA receptor antagonist in depression gave 7 people with major depression intravenous ketamine 0.5 mg/kg or saline on separate days. Mean 25-item Hamilton scores fell by 14 (SD 10) points within 72 hours after ketamine and by 0 (SD 12) after saline [[Berman2000]]
- The NIMH crossover trial gave 18 adults with treatment-resistant major depression one infusion of ketamine 0.5 mg/kg or placebo a week apart, after a 2-week drug-free period [[Zarate2006]]
- In that trial ketamine separated from placebo within 110 minutes, with effect sizes of d = 1.46 (95% CI 0.91–2.01) at 24 hours and d = 0.68 (0.13–1.23) at 1 week [[Zarate2006]]
- Of 17 participants treated with ketamine, 71% met response and 29% met remission criteria the day after the infusion; 35% kept their response for at least 1 week [[Zarate2006]]
- The first trial used saline as its control. A later two-site trial judged the control conditions of earlier studies inadequate and used the anaesthetic midazolam as an active placebo [[Berman2000,Murrough2013AJP]]
- In the two-site trial with midazolam as the control (n = 73, randomised 2:1), the MADRS score 24 hours after one infusion was 7.95 points lower with ketamine (95% CI 3.20–12.71) [[Murrough2013AJP]]
- Response at 24 hours in that trial was 64% with ketamine and 28% with midazolam (odds ratio 2.18, 95% CI 1.21–4.14) [[Murrough2013AJP]]
- A dose-ranging trial (n = 99) compared single 40-minute infusions of 0.1, 0.2, 0.5 and 1.0 mg/kg with midazolam. Only 0.5 and 1.0 mg/kg were superior to the active placebo after adjustment, and most of the effect was at day 1, with no significant adjusted differences at day 3 [[Fava2020Ketamine]]
- A meta-analysis of 49 randomised trials (3,299 participants) found standardised mean differences right after the first dose of −0.73 (95% CI −0.91 to −0.56) for higher-dose racemic ketamine and −0.48 (−0.75 to −0.20) for higher-dose esketamine [[Nikolin2023]]
- In the same meta-analysis all-cause dropout did not differ between active and control arms (odds ratio 1.18, 95% CI 0.85–1.64) [[Nikolin2023]]

### ketamine · Mechanism
- Ketamine is a non-competitive antagonist of the NMDA receptor, an ionotropic glutamate receptor; the esketamine label states that the mechanism of the antidepressant effect is unknown (Established for the receptor action; the downstream mechanism remains under study) [[FDA-Spravato]]
- In rats, low (subanaesthetic) doses of ketamine raised glutamate outflow in the prefrontal cortex, while an anaesthetic dose lowered it; this is the basis of the "glutamate surge" model, in which glutamate then acts on non-NMDA (AMPA) receptors (Early evidence, animal data) [[Moghaddam1997]]
- In rats, ketamine rapidly activated the mTOR pathway, raised synaptic signalling proteins and increased the number and function of new spine synapses in the prefrontal cortex; blocking mTOR blocked both the synapse formation and the behavioural response (Early evidence, animal data) [[Li2010mTOR]]
- In mice, the fast behavioural effect of NMDA antagonists depended on rapid synthesis of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), triggered by deactivation of eEF2 kinase when NMDA receptors are blocked at rest (Early evidence, animal data) [[Autry2011]]
- The synaptogenic hypothesis holds that depression involves fewer synapses in the prefrontal cortex and hippocampus, and that ketamine rapidly induces synapse formation and reverses stress-related synaptic loss (Hypothesis, built on animal and post-mortem data) [[Duman2012]]
- Longitudinal imaging in mice showed that ketamine restored dendritic spines lost under chronic stress. New spines were needed to maintain the behavioural effect over days but not to start it (Early evidence, animal data) [[ModaSava2019]]
- A separate model places the fast effect in the lateral habenula: ketamine blocked NMDA receptor-dependent burst firing there in rat and mouse models, which releases downstream reward centres from inhibition (Early evidence, animal data) [[Yang2018Habenula]]
- In mice, the metabolite (2R,6R)-hydroxynorketamine produced antidepressant-like effects that did not depend on NMDA receptor inhibition but involved AMPA receptor activation, without ketamine-like side effects (Hypothesis for humans; animal data) [[Zanos2016]]
- The human test of the mTOR model did not confirm it. In 20 people with a major depressive episode, pretreatment with the mTORC1 inhibitor rapamycin did not block ketamine's effect at 24 hours; at 2 weeks, response was 41% with rapamycin plus ketamine and 13% with placebo plus ketamine (Early evidence) [[Abdallah2020]]
- In a crossover study stopped at a planned interim analysis, naltrexone 50 mg given before ketamine reduced the antidepressant effect on days 1 and 3 in 12 completers but did not change dissociation. The authors concluded that the acute effect needs opioid-system activation (Early evidence; small, not replicated at scale) [[Williams2018]]
- Because naltrexone blocked the antidepressant effect but not the dissociation, the same study suggests that dissociation alone does not produce the antidepressant effect [[Williams2018]]

### ketamine · Time course and durability
- Onset is measured in hours: in an open-label series (n = 24), MADRS fell by a mean of 18.9 (SD 6.6) points 2 hours after the first infusion [[Murrough2013BP]]
- After a single infusion the group effect is largest at about 24 hours and has shrunk by 1 week (d = 1.46 and 0.68) [[Zarate2006]]
- Six infusions over 12 days (three per week) gave an overall response rate of 70.8% at the end of the course. Among responders, the median time to relapse after the last infusion was 18 days [[Murrough2013BP]]
- Response 4 hours after the first infusion predicted response at the end of the six-infusion course (94% sensitivity, 71% specificity) [[Murrough2013BP]]
- In a 4-week placebo-controlled trial (n = 67 treated), twice-weekly and thrice-weekly ketamine 0.5 mg/kg over 40 minutes gave similar results: mean MADRS change at day 15 of −18.4 versus −5.7 with placebo (twice weekly) and −17.7 versus −3.1 (thrice weekly) [[Singh2016]]
- In a 41-person trial, repeated infusions (six over 2 weeks) had cumulative effects: 59% met response criteria, with a median of three infusions needed before response. Responders held their gains through four further once-weekly infusions [[Phillips2019]]
- A systematic review of maintenance ketamine found only three randomised trials, eight open-label trials and 30 case series or reports. It judged maintenance treatment promising but the evidence methodologically limited [[SmithApeldoorn2022]]
- A proof-of-concept trial randomised 28 ketamine responders to cognitive behavioural therapy or treatment as usual. The MADRS effect size at study end was d = 0.65 (95% CI −0.55 to 1.82) and the primary interaction was not significant (Early evidence) [[Wilkinson2021CBT]]

### ketamine · Racemic ketamine in practice: off-label use, routes and comparisons
- Ketamine's US label lists anaesthetic indications only. Its use for depression is off-label [[FDA-Ketalar,Sanacora2017]]
- The research protocol used in most trials is 0.5 mg/kg given intravenously over 40 minutes (quoted as published, not as an instruction) [[Singh2016,Fava2020Ketamine]]
- The APA task-force consensus statement notes that off-label use grew despite relatively small trials, a lack of longer-term efficacy data and limited safety data, and it sets out points to consider before, during and after treatment [[Sanacora2017]]
- The CANMAT task force rated a single intravenous infusion as Level 1 evidence in adults with treatment-resistant depression and repeated or maintenance infusions as Level 3. It made single-dose intravenous ketamine a third-line recommendation [[Swainson2021]]
- The same task force advised that oral and other non-intravenous formulations be limited to specialists in tertiary or specialised centres, because of limited evidence and the risk of misuse and diversion [[Swainson2021]]
- An international expert group described ketamine and esketamine as the first non-monoaminergic agents with proven rapid-onset efficacy in major depression, while noting unresolved questions about safety, place in treatment algorithms and the setting and staff needed [[McIntyre2021]]
- KARMA-Dep 2 randomised 65 inpatients with a major depressive episode to up to eight twice-weekly infusions of ketamine 0.5 mg/kg or midazolam 0.045 mg/kg on top of usual inpatient care. End-of-treatment MADRS did not differ (adjusted mean difference −3.16, 95% CI −8.54 to 2.22; P = .25) [[Jelovac2025H]]
- In KARMA-Dep 2 most patients and raters guessed the allocation correctly; raters identified ketamine-arm patients correctly 90.6% of the time after the first infusion [[Jelovac2025H]]
- ELEKT-D, an open-label non-inferiority trial in 403 people referred for ECT with non-psychotic treatment-resistant depression, found response in 55.4% with ketamine (twice weekly for 3 weeks) and 41.2% with ECT (difference 14.2 percentage points, 95% CI 3.9–24.2) [[Anand2023,NCT03113968]]
- In ELEKT-D, delayed recall fell after 3 weeks of ECT (T-score change −9.7 versus −0.9 with ketamine) and recovered gradually; ECT was linked to musculoskeletal adverse effects and ketamine to dissociation [[Anand2023]]
- KetECT, an open-label non-inferiority trial in 186 inpatients, found the opposite ranking: remission in 63% with ECT and 46% with ketamine (P = .026). Among remitters, 70% (ketamine) and 63% (ECT) relapsed within 12 months [[Ekstrand2022]]
- The KADS phase 3 trial tested twice-weekly subcutaneous racemic ketamine against midazolam for 4 weeks. Flexible dosing (0.5–0.9 mg/kg) gave remission in 19.6% versus 2.0% (odds ratio 12.1, 95% CI 2.1–69.2); the fixed 0.5 mg/kg cohort showed no difference (6.3% versus 8.8%) [[Loo2023KADS]]

### ketamine · Suicidal thoughts as a treatment target
- Suicidal thoughts are a symptom that can be treated. In an individual-participant meta-analysis, a single ketamine infusion reduced suicidal ideation within 1 day and for up to 1 week (Cohen's d 0.48–0.85), partly independently of the change in mood [[Wilkinson2018]]
- In a midazolam-controlled trial in people with major depression and clinically significant suicidal ideation, the ideation score at day 1 was 4.96 points lower with ketamine (95% CI 2.33–7.59), and 55% versus 30% were responders [[Grunebaum2018]]
- In a placebo-controlled trial of 156 people admitted with severe suicidal ideation, full remission of suicidal ideas at day 3 was reached by 63.0% with ketamine and 31.6% with placebo (odds ratio 3.7, 95% CI 1.9–7.3). The effect was strong in bipolar disorder and not significant in the depressive-disorder subgroup [[Abbar2022]]
- The esketamine label states that its effectiveness in preventing suicide or in reducing suicidal ideation or behaviour has not been demonstrated, and that its use does not remove the need for hospital care when that is clinically warranted [[FDA-Spravato]]

### ketamine · Adverse effects and risks
- A systematic review of 60 studies found psychiatric, psychotomimetic, cardiovascular and neurological side effects more often after ketamine than placebo, and it flagged selective reporting and little assessment of long-term or repeated dosing [[Short2018]]
- The most common adverse events (20% or more) with repeated ketamine in a 4-week trial were headache, anxiety, dissociation, nausea and dizziness; dissociative symptoms were transient and lessened with repeated dosing [[Singh2016]]
- The ketamine label describes transient rises in blood pressure, heart rate and cardiac index as frequent, and it contraindicates ketamine where a significant rise in blood pressure would be a serious hazard [[FDA-Ketalar]]
- Ketamine is a Schedule III controlled substance in the US, and the label warns that people with a history of drug misuse or dependence may be at greater risk; physical dependence has been reported with prolonged use [[FDA-Ketalar]]
- The label links long-term ketamine use or misuse to urological complications (cystitis, reduced bladder capacity, ureteral narrowing, hydronephrosis) and to hepatobiliary problems, and recurrent high-dose misuse to memory and attention impairment [[FDA-Ketalar]]
- In a survey of 3,806 people, 1,285 reported non-medical ketamine use in the past year; 26.6% of them reported urinary symptoms, which rose with dose and frequency, and 17% met criteria for dependence [[Winstock2012]]
- Of 251 users who described their symptoms over time, 51% reported improvement in urinary symptoms after stopping [[Winstock2012]]
- A systematic review of ketamine-induced uropathy describes severe urinary storage symptoms and pelvic pain in regular users, with hydronephrosis in long-term heavy use; stopping ketamine is the first step in management [[Castellani2020]]
- In supervised subcutaneous dosing for depression, acute psychotomimetic effects and blood-pressure rises resolved within 2 hours [[Loo2023KADS]]

### ketamine · Esketamine nasal spray: label
- Esketamine is the S-enantiomer of racemic ketamine. Spravato was first approved in the US on 5 March 2019, with efficacy supplements approved on 31 July 2020 and 17 January 2025 [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Current label indications: treatment-resistant depression in adults, as monotherapy or with an oral antidepressant; and depressive symptoms in adults with major depressive disorder with acute suicidal ideation or behaviour, with an oral antidepressant [[FDA-Spravato]]
- The boxed warning covers sedation, dissociation, respiratory depression, misuse, and suicidal thoughts and behaviours in paediatric and young adult patients taking antidepressants. Esketamine is not approved for paediatric use [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Spravato is available only through a restricted programme (the Spravato REMS). It is dispensed and given only in certified healthcare settings, self-administered under direct observation, and followed by at least 2 hours of monitoring [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Label dosing for treatment-resistant depression, quoted as published: 56 mg or 84 mg twice a week in weeks 1–4, once weekly in weeks 5–8, then every 2 weeks or once weekly from week 9, using the least frequent dosing that maintains remission or response [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Label dosing for depressive symptoms with acute suicidal ideation or behaviour, quoted as published: 84 mg twice a week for 4 weeks, with reduction to 56 mg allowed for tolerability; use beyond 4 weeks has not been systematically evaluated [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Each device delivers 28 mg, so a session uses two devices (56 mg) or three (84 mg) with a 5-minute rest between devices; mean absolute bioavailability is about 48% and peak plasma concentration comes 20–40 minutes after the last spray [[FDA-Spravato]]
- In clinical trials 61–84% of people given esketamine had dissociative or perceptual changes and 48–61% had sedation; 0.3–0.4% lost consciousness [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Blood pressure rises at all recommended doses, peaking about 40 minutes after dosing and lasting about 4 hours. About 3–19% of esketamine-treated and 1–4% of placebo-treated patients had a rise of at least 40 mmHg systolic or 25 mmHg diastolic in the first 1.5 hours [[FDA-Spravato]]
- The label asks for blood pressure to be assessed before dosing and again at about 40 minutes, and for respiratory status including pulse oximetry to be monitored for at least 2 hours [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Contraindications are aneurysmal vascular disease or arteriovenous malformation, a history of intracerebral haemorrhage, and hypersensitivity to esketamine or ketamine [[FDA-Spravato]]
- People are told not to drive or operate machinery until the next day after a restful sleep, and they need transport home [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Cognitive performance fell 40 minutes after a dose in healthy volunteers and matched placebo at 2 hours. In 1-year and 3-year open-label trials, cognitive function stayed stable [[FDA-Spravato]]
- No case of esketamine-related interstitial cystitis was seen in trials lasting up to a year, though lower urinary tract symptoms were more common than with placebo [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Esketamine is a Schedule III controlled substance; the label states it is not recommended during pregnancy and may cause fetal harm, so pregnancy planning is discussed with the care team [[FDA-Spravato]]
- A post-marketing analysis estimated respiratory depression at about 1 case per 20,000 treatment sessions, manageable with minor supportive measures [[Chepke2024]]

### ketamine · Esketamine nasal spray: trials
- The phase 2 dose-finding trial (n = 67) showed a dose-response: MADRS differences from placebo of −4.2 (28 mg), −6.3 (56 mg) and −9.0 (84 mg) [[Daly2018]]
- TRANSFORM-2 randomised 227 adults with treatment-resistant depression to flexibly dosed esketamine or placebo spray, each with a newly started oral antidepressant. At day 28 the MADRS difference was −4.0 (95% CI −7.31 to −0.64) [[Popova2019TRANSFORM2]]
- In the label's analysis of the same trial, MADRS fell by 19.8 points with esketamine plus an oral antidepressant and by 15.8 with placebo spray plus an oral antidepressant, from a baseline of about 37 [[FDA-Spravato]]
- The label notes that most of the difference from placebo was present at 24 hours, and that it did not grow between 24 hours and day 28 [[FDA-Spravato]]
- In TRANSFORM-2, 7% of the esketamine arm and 0.9% of the placebo arm stopped study drug because of an adverse event; adverse events generally appeared shortly after dosing and resolved by 1.5 hours [[Popova2019TRANSFORM2]]
- TRANSFORM-1 (fixed doses) did not reach statistical significance: the MADRS difference for 84 mg was −3.2 (95% CI −6.88 to 0.45; P = .088), and 56 mg (−4.1, −7.67 to −0.49) could not be formally tested [[Fedgchin2019]]
- TRANSFORM-3, in adults aged 65 and over, also missed its primary endpoint (median-unbiased difference −3.6, 95% CI −7.20 to 0.07; P = .059). The difference was −4.9 in those aged 65–74 and −0.4 in those 75 and over [[OchsRoss2020]]
- SUSTAIN-1 randomised 297 people who had reached stable remission or response on esketamine to continue it or switch to placebo spray, with their oral antidepressant. Among stable remitters, 26.7% relapsed on esketamine and 45.3% on placebo (hazard ratio 0.49, 95% CI 0.29–0.84; number needed to treat 6) [[Daly2019]]
- Among stable responders in SUSTAIN-1, 25.8% relapsed on esketamine and 57.6% on placebo (hazard ratio 0.30, 95% CI 0.16–0.55; number needed to treat 4) [[Daly2019]]
- SUSTAIN-1 used a randomised-withdrawal design in people who had already responded, so it shows the effect of stopping in responders, not the effect in everyone who starts [[Daly2019]]
- SUSTAIN-2 followed 802 people for up to a year: the most common adverse events were dizziness (32.9%), dissociation (27.6%), nausea (25.1%) and headache (24.9%); 9.5% stopped because of adverse events, and there was no case of interstitial cystitis or respiratory depression [[Wajs2020]]
- SUSTAIN-3, the long-term extension (1,148 participants, 2,769 patient-years at interim cut-off), reported no new safety signal over up to 4.5 years; 35.6% were in remission at the end of induction and 46.1% at the maintenance endpoint [[Zaki2023]]
- ASPIRE I and II enrolled adults with major depressive disorder and active suicidal ideation with intent, all receiving comprehensive standard care including initial hospital admission. MADRS at 24 hours fell more with esketamine: difference −3.8 (95% CI −6.56 to −1.09) and −3.9 (−6.60 to −1.11) [[Fu2020,Ionescu2021]]
- In ASPIRE II the difference was already present 4 hours after the first dose (−4.2, 95% CI −6.38 to −1.94) [[Ionescu2021]]
- Neither ASPIRE trial showed a significant difference in clinician-rated severity of suicidality at 24 hours; both groups improved rapidly [[Fu2020,Ionescu2021]]
- ESCAPE-TRD compared esketamine with extended-release quetiapine, each added to an SSRI or SNRI, in 676 people. Remission at week 8 was 27.1% versus 17.6% (P = .003), and remission at week 8 with no relapse through week 32 was 21.7% versus 14.1% [[Reif2023]]
- The monotherapy trial (n = 378) found MADRS differences from placebo at day 28 of −5.1 (95% CI −7.91 to −2.33) for 56 mg and −6.8 (−9.48 to −4.07) for 84 mg, with differences already present at day 2 (−3.8 and −3.4) [[Janik2025]]
- In the monotherapy trial the most common adverse events were nausea (24.8%), dissociation (24.3%), dizziness (21.7%) and headache (19.0%) [[Janik2025]]
- In pooled meta-analysis, effects during repeated dosing were significant for racemic ketamine but not for esketamine (for example −0.61, 95% CI −1.02 to −0.20, versus −0.22, −0.54 to 0.10, at higher doses); the trials differ in design, so this is an indirect comparison [[Nikolin2023]]

### frontier · Neurosteroids (GABA-A positive modulators)
- Zuranolone is a neuroactive steroid that positively modulates GABA-A receptors. It was first approved in the US on 4 August 2023, and its only label indication is postpartum depression in adults; it is a Schedule IV controlled substance (Established for postpartum depression) [[FDA-Zurzuvae]]
- The label course is 50 mg once daily in the evening for 14 days, quoted as published, with a boxed warning about impaired driving for at least 12 hours after each dose [[FDA-Zurzuvae]]
- Pointer to pass I: in SKYLARK (n = 196, postpartum depression) the Hamilton score at day 15 fell by 15.6 points with zuranolone and 11.6 with placebo (difference −4.0, 95% CI −6.3 to −1.7) [[Deligiannidis2023SKYLARK]]
- Pointer to pass I: intravenous brexanolone, given as a 60-hour infusion, lowered Hamilton scores more than placebo at 60 hours in two phase 3 trials (differences −2.5 to −5.5 points) [[MeltzerBrody2018]]
- In major depressive disorder outside the postpartum period, the phase 2 trial (n = 89) found a day-15 Hamilton difference of −7.0 points (95% CI −10.2 to −3.9) (Early evidence) [[GunduzBruce2019]]
- The phase 3 MOUNTAIN trial (n = 581) missed its primary endpoint: day-15 change −12.5 with zuranolone 30 mg versus −11.1 with placebo (P = .116), although days 3, 8 and 12 favoured zuranolone [[Clayton2023MOUNTAIN]]
- The phase 3 WATERFALL trial (n = 543) of 50 mg met its endpoint with a small difference: day-15 change −14.1 versus −12.3; the day-3 change was −9.8 versus −6.8, and the difference stayed nominally significant only through day 12 [[Clayton2023WATERFALL]]
- In CORAL (n = 440 randomised), zuranolone started together with a standard antidepressant improved the day-3 Hamilton score more than placebo plus antidepressant (−8.9 versus −7.0; P = .0004) [[Parikh2024CORAL]]
- Taken together, zuranolone in major depressive disorder shows a fast but small and short-lived advantage over placebo (Early evidence for MDD) [[Clayton2023WATERFALL,Clayton2023MOUNTAIN]]

### frontier · Oral glutamate modulators
- Dextromethorphan-bupropion (Auvelity) combines dextromethorphan, an NMDA receptor antagonist and sigma-1 agonist, with bupropion, which raises dextromethorphan plasma levels by inhibiting CYP2D6. It was first approved in the US on 18 August 2022 for major depressive disorder in adults (Established) [[FDA-Auvelity]]
- In GEMINI (n = 318 in the label analysis), MADRS at week 6 fell by 15.9 points with dextromethorphan-bupropion and 12.1 with placebo (difference −3.9, 95% CI −6.4 to −1.4); the difference was significant from week 1 [[FDA-Auvelity,Iosifescu2022]]
- In a trial against bupropion alone (n = 80 in the efficacy population), week-6 remission was 46.5% versus 16.2% [[Tabuteau2022]]
- The label gives the dose as one 45 mg/105 mg tablet once daily for 3 days, then twice daily, quoted as published; it carries the antidepressant-class boxed warning on suicidal thoughts and behaviours in young people [[FDA-Auvelity]]
- Esmethadone (REL-1017), an NMDA channel blocker, looked promising in a phase 2a trial with effect sizes of 0.7 to 1.0 (Early evidence) [[Fava2022REL1017]]
- The first phase 3 trial of esmethadone (n = 227) missed its primary endpoint: MADRS change at day 28 of 15.1 versus 12.9 points (difference 2.3; P = .154). A post hoc analysis in people with baseline MADRS of 35 or more favoured the drug [[Fava2024REL1017,NCT04688164]]
- The second phase 3 trial of esmethadone was stopped early after a prespecified interim analysis; the registry reports day-28 changes of −14.81 and −13.9 points [[NCT04855747]]

### frontier · Psilocybin
- In the first randomised trial in major depressive disorder (n = 24 completers, waiting-list control), psilocybin with supportive therapy gave Hamilton scores of 8.0 at week 1 and 8.5 at week 4 versus 23.8 and 23.5 in the delayed-treatment group; 71% responded and 54% were in remission at week 4 (Early evidence) [[Davis2021Psilocybin]]
- A 6-week trial compared two doses of psilocybin 25 mg with daily escitalopram in 59 people. The primary outcome (self-rated depression score) did not differ: −8.0 versus −6.0 points (difference 2.0, 95% CI −5.0 to 0.9; P = .17) [[CarhartHarris2021]]
- Secondary outcomes in that trial favoured psilocybin (for example remission 57% versus 28%) but were not corrected for multiple comparisons [[CarhartHarris2021]]
- At 6 months the two arms still did not differ on depression severity (difference 1.51, 95% CI −1.35 to 4.38) [[Erritzoe2024]]
- The phase 2b COMP360 trial randomised 233 people with treatment-resistant depression to a single dose of 25 mg, 10 mg or 1 mg (control). MADRS change at week 3 was −12.0, −7.9 and −5.4; 25 mg versus 1 mg differed by −6.6 (95% CI −10.2 to −2.9), and 10 mg did not differ from 1 mg (Early evidence) [[Goodwin2022]]
- In that trial adverse events occurred in 77% of participants (headache, nausea, dizziness); suicidal ideation or behaviour or self-injury occurred in all dose groups; and sustained response at week 12 did not clearly support the primary result [[Goodwin2022]]
- In long-term follow-up of the same cohort, median time to a depressive event was 92 days after 25 mg, 83 days after 10 mg and 62 days after 1 mg [[Goodwin2025]]
- A US phase 2 trial (n = 104) compared a single 25 mg dose with niacin as an active placebo, both with psychological support: the MADRS difference was −12.0 at day 8 and −12.3 at day 43 (95% CI −17.5 to −7.2). There were no serious adverse events, but more overall and severe adverse events with psilocybin [[Raison2023]]
- A placebo-controlled trial (n = 52) of a single moderate dose (0.215 mg/kg) with psychological support found a 13.0-point MADRS fall at 14 days, significantly more than placebo; 54% of the psilocybin group (14 of 26) met remission criteria [[vonRotz2023]]
- A meta-analysis of seven trials (436 participants) found a pooled effect of Hedges' g = 0.66 (95% CI 0.46–0.86). Effects were larger with self-report scales, in secondary depression and in people who had used psychedelics before, which points to expectancy effects [[Metaxa2024]]
- In a network meta-analysis, the effect size of high-dose psilocybin shrank from large (0.88) to small (0.31) when it was compared with the placebo response of antidepressant trials, because placebo response in psychedelic trials was lower [[Hsu2024]]
- In pooled individual data (n = 102), clinically significant worsening of symptoms occurred in about 10% of participants in the psilocybin and escitalopram arms and in 63.6% of those on a waiting list [[Simonsson2023]]
- Phase 3 status on 2026-11-13: COMP005 (COMP360 25 mg versus placebo, 258 enrolled) is completed, and COMP006 (two administrations, 25 mg versus 1 mg, 572 enrolled) is active and no longer recruiting. Neither has results posted on the registry [[NCT05624268,NCT05711940]]
- A separate phase 3 trial of psilocybin in major depressive disorder (uAspire, 238 enrolled) is active and no longer recruiting, with no results posted [[NCT06308653]]
- Psilocybin is not an approved medicine for depression. In these trials it was given once or twice in a supervised clinical setting with psychological support (Early evidence overall) [[Goodwin2022,Raison2023]]

### frontier · Other psychedelics
- Ayahuasca: in a placebo-controlled trial in treatment-resistant depression, response at day 7 was 64% versus 27% (Early evidence) [[PalhanoFontes2019]]
- DMT: an open-label phase 1 study gave intravenous DMT to 7 people with treatment-resistant depression. Hamilton scores fell by 4.5 points the day after the higher dose, and effects on blood pressure, heart rate and anxiety resolved within 20–30 minutes (Early evidence) [[DSouza2022]]
- 5-MeO-DMT (mebufotenin), inhaled: in a phase 2b trial (n = 81), MADRS at day 8 fell 15.5 points more with GH001 than placebo, and remission was 57.5% versus 0% (Early evidence) [[Cubala2026]]
- 5-MeO-DMT, intranasal: in a phase 2b trial (n = 193), a single 12 mg dose lowered MADRS at day 29 by 5.3 points more than a 0.3 mg control dose (95% CI −8.9 to −1.8). Most participants were ready for discharge within 2 hours (Early evidence) [[Feifel2026]]
- LSD, pointer only (anxiety, not depression): a single dose of MM120 lowered Hamilton anxiety scores at week 4 by 5.0 points (100 µg) and 6.0 points (200 µg) more than placebo in 198 people with generalised anxiety disorder. Visual perceptual changes occurred in 92.5% and 100% of those dose groups and in 10.3% on placebo [[Robison2025]]
- In an earlier trial of LSD-assisted therapy for anxiety, co-existing depression scores also fell (Hamilton difference −7.0, 95% CI −10.8 to −3.2) (Early evidence) [[Holze2023]]
- Phase 3 trials of the same LSD formulation are registered in generalised anxiety disorder and in major depressive disorder; none has posted results [[NCT06741228,NCT06941844]]

### frontier · Blinding and expectancy
- Participants in trials of psychedelics and ketamine can usually tell whether they received the active drug. A methods review found that psychedelic trials had generally not reported pre-trial expectancy or whether blinding held, and it argued that effect sizes are likely overestimated [[Muthukumaraswamy2021]]
- The same problem was measured directly for ketamine: in KARMA-Dep 2 most patients and raters guessed allocation correctly, and the trial found no advantage over midazolam [[Jelovac2025H]]
- One analysis points the other way. In the psilocybin-versus-escitalopram trial, participants expected more from psilocybin, yet expectancy predicted response to escitalopram and not to psilocybin [[Szigeti2024]]
- Trials have tried low-dose active comparators (1 mg psilocybin, 0.3 mg mebufotenin) and active placebos (niacin, midazolam) to reduce unblinding [[Goodwin2022,Feifel2026,Raison2023,Murrough2013AJP]]

### frontier · Kappa-opioid antagonists (cautionary examples)
- The NIMH "fast-fail" trial showed that the kappa-opioid antagonist JNJ-67953964 (the compound later developed as aticaprant) increased ventral striatum activation during reward anticipation in 89 people with anhedonia, which supported further testing (Early evidence) [[Krystal2020]]
- A phase 2 trial of aticaprant 10 mg added to an SSRI or SNRI found a small MADRS advantage at week 6 (−3.1 points in the full analysis; effect size 0.36) [[Schmidt2024]]
- Phase 3 did not confirm it. VENTURA-1 found a day-43 MADRS difference of −0.9 (95% CI −3.24 to 1.49; P = .467) and VENTURA-2 a difference of −0.5 (−2.95 to 1.90; P = .670) [[NCT05455684,NCT05550532]]
- Two further aticaprant trials were stopped early for insufficient efficacy, and the long-term study was stopped by the sponsor in March 2025 [[NCT06514742,NCT06635135,NCT05518149]]
- Navacaprant, another kappa-opioid antagonist, missed its primary endpoint in phase 2 (Hamilton difference at week 8 −1.7; P = .121), with a signal only in a moderate-to-severe subgroup [[Mathew2025]]
- The navacaprant long-term study was terminated with the registry statement that the drug "did not achieve the primary endpoint across three randomized controlled trials" and that the programme was discontinued for lack of efficacy in major depressive disorder [[NCT06029439]]
- Teaching point: a plausible mechanism, a positive biomarker study and a positive phase 2 trial did not predict phase 3 success for this class (Established as a record of these programmes) [[Krystal2020,Schmidt2024,NCT05455684]]

### frontier · Orexin antagonists
- Seltorexant is a selective orexin-2 receptor antagonist studied in major depressive disorder with insomnia symptoms (Early evidence) [[Savitz2021]]
- In the phase 2b trial, adjunctive seltorexant 20 mg improved MADRS at week 3 (difference −4.5, 90% CI −6.96 to −2.07), and the week-6 difference was larger in people with insomnia scores of 15 or more (−4.9) than in those below 15 (−0.7) [[Savitz2021]]
- In the first phase 3 trial (588 enrolled; adults with major depressive disorder and insomnia symptoms who had not responded adequately to an antidepressant), the registry reports a day-43 MADRS difference of −2.6 (95% CI −4.53 to −0.74; P = .007) for seltorexant 20 mg versus placebo [[NCT04533529]]
- In a monotherapy study, 20 mg (but not 40 mg) improved Hamilton scores at week 5, and the benefit remained after sleep items were removed [[Mesens2025]]
- In a 6-month comparison with extended-release quetiapine as add-on treatment, adverse events were reported by 65.4% on seltorexant and 80.8% on quetiapine; discontinuation did not differ significantly [[Pinter2026]]
- Further phase 3 trials are recruiting; seltorexant is not an approved medicine [[NCT06559306]]

### frontier · Anti-inflammatory strategies
- About 27% of people with depression have low-grade inflammation (C-reactive protein above 3 mg/L; 95% CI 21–34%), with an odds ratio of 1.46 against matched controls (Established as an association) [[Osimo2019]]
- The infliximab trial in treatment-resistant depression found no overall benefit. There was an interaction with baseline inflammation: above a C-reactive protein of 5 mg/L the change favoured infliximab, and at or below 5 mg/L it favoured placebo (Early evidence) [[Raison2013]]
- A meta-analysis of 14 trials (6,262 participants) found that anti-inflammatory treatment reduced depressive symptoms (standardised mean difference −0.34, 95% CI −0.57 to −0.11), with high heterogeneity and a high risk of bias in every trial [[Kohler2014]]
- A later meta-analysis of 30 trials (1,610 participants) in major depressive disorder gave a pooled effect of −0.55 (95% CI −0.75 to −0.35) [[Bai2020AntiInflammatory]]
- In trials of anti-cytokine drugs for chronic inflammatory conditions, depressive symptoms improved more than with placebo (standardised mean difference 0.40, 95% CI 0.22–0.59) [[Kappelmann2018]]
- Minocycline: a 39-person trial selected for raised C-reactive protein found no overall difference, but participants with C-reactive protein of 3 mg/L or more improved most on minocycline (Early evidence) [[Nettis2021]]
- A larger trial (n = 168 analysed) of minocycline 200 mg/day for 6 weeks in treatment-resistant depression found no benefit over placebo [[HellmannRegen2022]]
- Status: anti-inflammatory treatment for an "inflamed" subgroup remains a hypothesis that needs trials which select participants by inflammatory markers in advance (Hypothesis) [[Raison2013,HellmannRegen2022]]

### frontier · Nitrous oxide and botulinum toxin
- Nitrous oxide is an inhaled anaesthetic and an NMDA receptor antagonist. In a proof-of-concept crossover trial (n = 20), a 1-hour inhalation of 50% nitrous oxide lowered Hamilton scores more than placebo at 2 hours (−4.8 points) and 24 hours (−5.5 points) (Early evidence) [[Nagele2015]]
- A phase 2 trial (n = 24) found that 25% nitrous oxide had similar efficacy to 50% with far fewer adverse effects; differences from placebo at week 2 were −5.19 points (25%) and −7.00 points (50%) [[Nagele2021]]
- A phase 2b trial of repeated weekly sessions found a mean difference of −1.9 Hamilton points over 4 weeks (95% CI −3.9 to 0.0; P = .051) [[Myles2025]]
- A meta-analysis of three single-session trials found benefit at 2 hours and 24 hours but not at 1 week (−1.52, 95% CI −4.07 to 1.03) [[Gill2025]]
- Botulinum toxin injected into the glabellar (frown) muscles has been tested as a treatment for depression. A meta-analysis of five randomised trials reported a large effect (d = 0.98), and its authors acknowledge methodological limitations (Hypothesis) [[Schulze2021]]
- In the largest trial (255 women), the 30-unit dose narrowly missed significance at week 6 (MADRS difference −3.7; P = .053) and the 50-unit dose did not separate from placebo [[Brin2020]]

### frontier · Precision psychiatry
- A 2017 study of 1,188 people reported four connectivity-based "biotypes" of depression on resting-state fMRI that predicted response to transcranial magnetic stimulation (Early evidence) [[Drysdale2017]]
- An independent replication attempt reproduced high correlations between connectivity and symptoms, but neither the correlations nor the clusters were statistically significant; the authors advised caution about the biotypes [[Dinga2019]]
- A 2024 study derived six biotypes from task-free and task-based fMRI in 801 treatment-free people with depression and anxiety, and linked them to symptoms, cognitive test performance and treatment response in 250 randomised participants (Early evidence; no prospective biotype-guided trial yet) [[Tozzi2024]]
- Pharmacogenomic testing: in the PRIME Care trial (n = 1,944), testing reduced prescriptions with predicted drug-gene interactions, and remission over 24 weeks was slightly higher (risk difference 2.8%, 95% CI 0.6–5.1%), but not at week 24 itself (1.5%, −2.4 to 5.3) [[Oslin2022]]
- In the GUIDED trial (n = 1,167), the primary outcome of symptom improvement at week 8 did not differ (27.2% versus 24.4%; P = .107); response and remission were modestly higher with guided care (remission 15.3% versus 10.1%) [[Greden2019]]
- A meta-analysis of 13 trials (4,767 participants) found remission 1.41 times more likely (95% CI 1.15–1.74) with pharmacogenomic-guided prescribing, which the authors call a modest effect (Early evidence) [[Brown2022PGx]]
- Digital phenotyping uses passive smartphone data to predict mood. A systematic review found moderate predictive performance, frequent missing data and a lack of external validation (Hypothesis for clinical use) [[Leaning2024]]
- Prescription digital therapeutics: CT-152 (Rejoyn), a smartphone-delivered cognitive-emotional and behavioural intervention used alongside an antidepressant, was tested against a sham app in 386 adults. The primary analysis gave a MADRS difference of −1.78 at week 6 (P = .0568), and the intention-to-treat analysis −2.12 (P = .0211); it became the first FDA-authorised prescription digital therapeutic for adjunctive treatment of major depressive disorder [[Rothman2025]]
- Closed-loop deep brain stimulation: in a single person with treatment-resistant depression, a device that sensed a personalised brain-activity biomarker and stimulated only when it appeared gave rapid and sustained improvement (Early evidence; n = 1) [[Scangos2021]]
- Accelerated, connectivity-targeted theta-burst stimulation (Stanford neuromodulation therapy): in the first sham-controlled trial (29 treated), MADRS fell by 52.5% with active and 11.1% with sham treatment at 4 weeks (Early evidence) [[Cole2022SNT]]
- A second sham-controlled trial by the same group (48 randomised) found remission at 1 month in 50.0% versus 20.8% [[Kratter2026]]
- An independent trial (n = 100) of a simpler protocol, with three sessions a day and no neuronavigation, found Hamilton score reductions of 54.7% versus 31.87% with sham (Cohen's d 0.65) [[Ramos2025]]
- A further trial (n = 123) found that connectivity-guided targeting improved response over the standard 5-cm rule at weeks 2 and 6, but not by week 12 [[Li2026TBS]]

### frontier · Microbiome and probiotics
- A meta-analysis of 34 studies found that major depressive disorder shares a gut-microbiota pattern with other psychiatric disorders: fewer butyrate-producing genera (Faecalibacterium, Coprococcus) and more Eggerthella. Diet, region and medicines confound these studies (Hypothesis for causation) [[Nikolova2021]]
- A pilot trial of an add-on probiotic in 49 adults with major depressive disorder and an incomplete response to antidepressants found good tolerability and a standardised effect size of 0.70 on the Hamilton scale at week 4 (Early evidence; pilot) [[Nikolova2023]]

### frontier · Prevention trials
- Psychological preventive interventions lowered the incidence of depressive disorders by 21% compared with control groups (incidence rate ratio 0.79, 95% CI 0.69–0.91; number needed to treat 20) (Established) [[vanZoonen2014]]
- An updated meta-analysis of 50 trials (14,665 participants) found a relative risk of 0.81 (95% CI 0.72–0.91) at 1 year; with a control event rate of 30%, 21 people needed to take part to prevent one case [[Cuijpers2021Prevention]]
- Vitamin D3 did not prevent depression: among 18,353 adults aged 50 or more followed for a median of 5.3 years, the hazard ratio was 0.97 (95% CI 0.87–1.09) [[Okereke2020]]
- Multinutrient supplements and food-related behavioural activation did not prevent major depressive disorder in 1,025 adults with overweight or obesity and subsyndromal symptoms; 105 (10%) developed major depressive disorder within 12 months across the four arms [[Bot2019]]

### frontier · What the new drugs say about older theories
- Rapid-acting drugs act on glutamate, GABA-A and other systems, not on serotonin reuptake. An umbrella review found no consistent evidence that depression is caused by lowered serotonin activity or concentration [[Moncrieff2022Serotonin]]
- That review addresses the cause of depression, not whether treatments work; the trials above show that medicines with several different mechanisms reduce symptoms more than placebo [[Moncrieff2022Serotonin,Popova2019TRANSFORM2,Iosifescu2022]]

### Exam hooks
- The first controlled trial of ketamine in depression used 0.5 mg/kg intravenously in 7 people [[Berman2000]]
- After a single ketamine infusion, the effect begins within about 2 hours, is largest at about 24 hours and is much smaller by 1 week [[Zarate2006,Murrough2013BP]]
- Ketamine and esketamine are non-competitive NMDA receptor antagonists; esketamine is the S-enantiomer of ketamine [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Midazolam, an anaesthetic, serves as the active placebo in the better-controlled ketamine trials [[Murrough2013AJP,Jelovac2025H]]
- Racemic ketamine for depression is off-label; esketamine nasal spray is the labelled product [[FDA-Ketalar,FDA-Spravato]]
- Esketamine nasal spray requires a REMS, administration in a certified healthcare setting and at least 2 hours of observation after each dose [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Esketamine boxed warning: sedation, dissociation, respiratory depression, misuse, and suicidal thoughts and behaviours in young people on antidepressants [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Blood pressure after esketamine peaks at about 40 minutes and settles over about 4 hours [[FDA-Spravato]]
- Esketamine is contraindicated in aneurysmal vascular disease, arteriovenous malformation and previous intracerebral haemorrhage [[FDA-Spravato]]
- TRANSFORM-2: MADRS difference −4.0 at day 28 for esketamine plus a new oral antidepressant versus placebo spray plus a new oral antidepressant [[Popova2019TRANSFORM2]]
- SUSTAIN-1: continuing esketamine lowered relapse risk by 51% in stable remitters and 70% in stable responders [[Daly2019]]
- ESCAPE-TRD: remission at week 8 was 27.1% with esketamine and 17.6% with extended-release quetiapine [[Reif2023]]
- Esketamine improved depressive symptoms at 24 hours in people with acute suicidal ideation, but the label states that an effect on suicidal ideation or behaviour itself has not been demonstrated [[FDA-Spravato,Fu2020]]
- Frequent, heavy non-medical ketamine use is associated with ulcerative cystitis; no esketamine-related case was seen in trials of up to a year [[Castellani2020,FDA-Spravato]]
- Zuranolone is a GABA-A positive allosteric modulator labelled for postpartum depression as a 14-day oral course [[FDA-Zurzuvae]]
- Dextromethorphan-bupropion: bupropion inhibits CYP2D6 and raises dextromethorphan exposure [[FDA-Auvelity]]
- In the COMP360 phase 2b trial only the 25 mg dose separated from the 1 mg control (−6.6 MADRS points at week 3) [[Goodwin2022]]
- Functional unblinding is the main methodological weakness of psychedelic and ketamine trials [[Muthukumaraswamy2021,Jelovac2025H]]
- Aticaprant's two phase 3 trials showed MADRS differences under 1 point from placebo, after a positive phase 2 trial [[NCT05455684,NCT05550532,Schmidt2024]]
- The four fMRI "biotypes" of depression reported in 2017 did not replicate in an independent sample [[Drysdale2017,Dinga2019]]

### In plain words
- Most antidepressants take weeks to work. A few newer treatments can lift symptoms within hours or days [[Zarate2006]]
- Ketamine is an anaesthetic. At a much lower dose, given by a clinician, it can ease depression quickly in some people whose depression has not improved with other treatments [[Murrough2013AJP]]
- The effect of one dose often wears off within about a week, so trials have tested repeated doses [[Zarate2006,Singh2016]]
- Esketamine is a nasal spray related to ketamine. It is taken only in a clinic, and staff watch the person for at least two hours afterwards [[FDA-Spravato]]
- During the first hour or two many people feel "spaced out", dizzy or sleepy, and blood pressure goes up for a while. These effects usually pass the same day [[FDA-Spravato,Popova2019TRANSFORM2]]
- People cannot drive until the next day after an esketamine session [[FDA-Spravato]]
- These treatments are added to other care. They do not replace talking therapy, other medicines or support, and decisions about them are made with a specialist [[McIntyre2021]]
- Using ketamine without medical supervision carries real risks, including lasting bladder damage and dependence [[Winstock2012,FDA-Ketalar]]
- Thoughts of suicide are a symptom of illness and they can be treated. Trials show these thoughts often ease quickly with good care [[Wilkinson2018,Ionescu2021]]
- Psilocybin and similar drugs are being studied in clinics with trained staff. They are not approved treatments for depression, and results so far come from small or medium-sized trials [[Goodwin2022,Raison2023]]
- In trials of these drugs people can usually tell whether they got the real drug, which makes the results harder to judge [[Muthukumaraswamy2021]]
- Many promising new medicines fail in large trials. That is a normal part of research and a reason to wait for strong evidence [[NCT05455684,Fava2024REL1017]]
- Brain scans, gene tests and phone apps cannot yet tell a person which depression treatment will work for them [[Dinga2019,Oslin2022,Leaning2024]]
- Depression is common and treatable, and researchers are testing many new ways to help people recover [[Daly2019,Cuijpers2021Prevention]]

## Open questions and controversies
- Is ketamine's effect larger than its unblinding? Single-infusion trials against midazolam found clear benefit at 24 hours [[Murrough2013AJP,Fava2020Ketamine]]; a trial of serial infusions against midazolam in inpatients found none, with most guesses correct [[Jelovac2025H]]; status: unresolved, and the answer may differ between a single dose and a course added to full inpatient care.
- How does ketamine work? NMDA blockade with a glutamate surge and synapse formation [[Li2010mTOR,Duman2012,ModaSava2019]]; a metabolite acting without NMDA inhibition [[Zanos2016]]; lateral habenula burst firing [[Yang2018Habenula]]; opioid-system involvement [[Williams2018]]; and a human study in which blocking mTOR did not block the effect [[Abdallah2020]]; status: no single mechanism is established in humans.
- Ketamine or ECT for treatment-resistant depression? Ketamine was non-inferior in people without psychosis who had been referred to ECT clinics [[Anand2023]]; ECT gave more remission in a more severely ill inpatient sample that included older people [[Ekstrand2022]]; status: the populations differ, and both trials were open-label.
- Racemic ketamine or esketamine? Pooled effect sizes are numerically larger for racemic ketamine [[Nikolin2023]]; esketamine has the larger regulated trial programme, relapse-prevention data and long-term safety follow-up [[Daly2019,Zaki2023]]; status: no adequately powered head-to-head trial was found.
- How strong is the esketamine evidence? One of three short-term phase 3 trials with an oral antidepressant met its primary endpoint [[Popova2019TRANSFORM2,Fedgchin2019,OchsRoss2020]]; later trials against quetiapine and as monotherapy were positive [[Reif2023,Janik2025]]; status: efficacy is accepted by regulators, with debate about effect size and trial design.
- How should the benefit be kept? Repeated dosing [[Phillips2019,Singh2016]], maintenance dosing with limited controlled evidence [[SmithApeldoorn2022]], or added psychotherapy [[Wilkinson2021CBT]]; status: open, and long-term safety of repeated racemic ketamine is poorly documented [[Short2018]].
- Do rapid-acting drugs reduce suicidal behaviour? Suicidal ideation falls quickly after ketamine [[Wilkinson2018,Grunebaum2018,Abbar2022]]; esketamine trials did not show a difference from placebo on suicidality ratings, and no trial has shown fewer deaths [[Fu2020,Ionescu2021,FDA-Spravato]]; status: unproven for behaviour.
- Are psychedelic effect sizes real? Large differences against inactive or low-dose comparators [[Raison2023,Goodwin2022,Cubala2026]]; no difference from escitalopram on the primary outcome [[CarhartHarris2021]]; shrinkage when compared against antidepressant-trial placebo response [[Hsu2024]]; expectancy concerns [[Muthukumaraswamy2021]] against one analysis finding expectancy did not predict psilocybin response [[Szigeti2024]]; status: awaiting phase 3 results [[NCT05624268,NCT05711940]].
- Is the psychedelic experience needed for benefit? Short-acting agents with discharge within 2 hours still showed effects at 4 weeks [[Feifel2026]], and ketamine's dissociation was separable from its antidepressant effect in one small study [[Williams2018]]; status: open.
- Does an "inflamed depression" subgroup respond to anti-inflammatory drugs? Subgroup signals [[Raison2013,Nettis2021]] and positive but biased meta-analyses [[Kohler2014,Bai2020AntiInflammatory]]; a large unselected minocycline trial was negative [[HellmannRegen2022]]; status: hypothesis.
- Are there reproducible biological subtypes of depression? Four biotypes [[Drysdale2017]] that failed replication [[Dinga2019]]; six newer biotypes not yet tested prospectively [[Tozzi2024]]; status: not ready for clinical use.
- Does pharmacogenomic testing improve outcomes? Small, non-persistent gains in remission [[Oslin2022]]; a missed primary outcome with positive secondary outcomes [[Greden2019]]; a modest pooled benefit [[Brown2022PGx]]; status: modest effect, value debated.
- Why do positive phase 2 results fail in phase 3? Kappa-opioid antagonists [[Schmidt2024,NCT05455684,NCT06029439]], esmethadone [[Fava2022REL1017,Fava2024REL1017]] and zuranolone 30 mg [[GunduzBruce2019,Clayton2023MOUNTAIN]] all shrank or failed; status: high placebo response and small samples are the usual explanations offered, without a tested remedy.

- Full text was used once: the open-access PMC version of KARMA-Dep 2 (PMC12547681) for the rater-guess figure. All other numbers come from abstracts or labels, so figures that appear only in full-text tables (for example MADRS means at each visit in the ketamine trials) are not included.
