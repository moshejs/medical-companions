## Findings

### psychotherapy · How well psychological therapies work overall
- In a meta-analysis of 228 randomised trials (75 at low risk of bias), the overall response rate (at least 50% symptom reduction) about 2 months after baseline was 41% (95% CI 38–43) with psychotherapy, 17% (15–20) with usual care and 16% (14–18) on a waiting list. [[Cuijpers2021Response]]
- The number needed to treat (NNT) for one extra response was 5.3 for psychotherapy versus usual care and 3.9 versus waiting list. [[Cuijpers2021Response]]
- About one third of people remitted after psychotherapy, compared with 7–13% in control conditions; more than half of people receiving therapy did not respond (Established: psychotherapy is efficacious, with modest absolute rates). [[Cuijpers2021Response]]
- Response rates by therapy type ranged from 0.37 (life-review therapy, interpersonal psychotherapy) to 0.46 (behavioural activation), with CBT at 0.42; the differences between types were not significant (p = 0.39). [[Cuijpers2021Response]]
- Across 441 trials (33,881 patients) in eight mental disorders, the absolute response rate to psychotherapy was 0.42 (95% CI 0.39–0.45) for major depressive disorder, the highest of the eight (PTSD 0.38, OCD 0.38, panic disorder 0.38, generalised anxiety disorder 0.36, social anxiety disorder 0.32, specific phobia 0.32, borderline personality disorder 0.24). [[Cuijpers2024Absolute]]
- A network meta-analysis of 331 randomised trials (34,285 patients) compared eight therapy types: cognitive behavioural, interpersonal, psychodynamic, problem-solving, behavioural activation, life-review, "third wave" therapies and non-directive supportive counselling. [[Cuijpers2021Psychotherapies]]
- All eight were more efficacious than care as usual and waiting list. All except non-directive supportive counselling and psychodynamic therapy were more efficacious than pill placebo. [[Cuijpers2021Psychotherapies]]
- Standardised mean differences versus care as usual ranged from −0.81 (life-review therapy) to −0.32 (non-directive supportive counselling). [[Cuijpers2021Psychotherapies]]
- The individual therapies did not differ significantly from each other, except that non-directive supportive counselling was less efficacious than all the others; results were similar in low-risk-of-bias trials. [[Cuijpers2021Psychotherapies]]
- Most therapies still had significant effects at 12-month follow-up versus care as usual, and no consistent differences in acceptability (all-cause dropout) were found. [[Cuijpers2021Psychotherapies]]
- An earlier network meta-analysis (198 studies, 15,118 adults) found each of seven therapies superior to waiting list (d −0.62 to −0.92), with differences between therapies absent to small (d 0.01 to −0.30). [[Barth2013]]
- In that analysis, effects were smaller in larger trials; robust evidence from large studies existed for CBT, interpersonal therapy and problem-solving therapy (all d > 0.46 versus waiting list). [[Barth2013]]
- CBT is the most-studied therapy: 409 trials with 52,702 patients. Versus control conditions (care as usual, waiting list) the effect was g = 0.79 (95% CI 0.70–0.89), and still significant at 6–12 months. [[Cuijpers2023CBT]]
- CBT was only marginally better than other psychotherapies (g = 0.06, 95% CI 0–0.12), and the difference became non-significant in most sensitivity analyses. [[Cuijpers2023CBT]]
- CBT also worked as unguided self-help (g = 0.45), in institutional settings (g = 0.65) and in children and adolescents (g = 0.41). [[Cuijpers2023CBT]]
- Across 366 trials (36,702 patients), psychotherapy effect sizes versus control by age group were: children 0.35, adolescents 0.55, young adults 0.98, middle-aged adults 0.77, older adults 0.66 and adults aged 75 and over 0.97 (overall g = 0.75, I² = 80%). [[Cuijpers2020Age]]
- The 1989 NIMH Treatment of Depression Collaborative Research Program randomised 250 outpatients to 16 weeks of interpersonal psychotherapy, CBT, imipramine plus clinical management or placebo plus clinical management. In the primary analyses neither psychotherapy was significantly less effective than imipramine, and differences among treatments appeared only in the more severely affected subgroup. [[Elkin1989]]

### psychotherapy · The CBT model (Beck) and the thought–feeling–behaviour cycle
- Beck's cognitive model was first formulated more than 40 years before his 2008 review and was expanded in stages to include automatic thoughts, cognitive distortions, dysfunctional beliefs and information-processing biases. [[Beck2008]]
- In the developmental version of the model, early adverse experiences lead to dysfunctional beliefs (the predisposition), and later stressors that match those beliefs act as precipitating factors. [[Beck2008]]
- Beck linked the model to biology: a hyperactive amygdala together with hypoactive prefrontal regions is associated with diminished cognitive appraisal and the occurrence of depression (Early evidence). [[Beck2008]]
- NICE describes individual CBT for a new episode as usually 8 regular sessions in less severe depression and usually 16 regular sessions in more severe depression, with more sessions possible for comorbid problems or residual symptoms. [[Kendrick2022NICE]]
- ACP 2023 recommends monotherapy with either CBT or a second-generation antidepressant as initial treatment for adults in the acute phase of moderate to severe major depressive disorder (strong recommendation; moderate-certainty evidence). [[Qaseem2023ACP]]
- ACP 2023 suggests CBT monotherapy as initial treatment in the acute phase of mild major depressive disorder (conditional recommendation; low-certainty evidence). [[Qaseem2023ACP]]

### psychotherapy · Behavioural activation
- In a placebo-controlled trial of 241 adults with major depressive disorder, behavioural activation was comparable to antidepressant medication among people with more severe depression, and both outperformed cognitive therapy in that subgroup. [[Dimidjian2006]]
- In the 2-year follow-up of responders from that trial, prior behavioural activation and prior cognitive therapy each did at least as well as continued medication; people withdrawn from medication onto pill placebo relapsed more during the first year. [[Dobson2008]]
- COBRA (UK, non-inferiority) randomised 440 adults with major depressive disorder: 221 to behavioural activation delivered by junior mental health workers and 219 to CBT delivered by therapists. [[Richards2016COBRA]]
- At 12 months the mean PHQ-9 score was 8.4 in both groups (mean difference 0.1 points, 95% CI −1.3 to 1.5; p = 0.89), within the prespecified non-inferiority margin of 1.9 PHQ-9 points. [[Richards2016COBRA]]
- Both COBRA treatments were delivered as a maximum of 20 sessions over 16 weeks, with four optional booster sessions. [[Richards2016COBRA]]
- Behavioural activation cost less than CBT in COBRA (after imputation £1841.67 versus £2282.40 per participant; difference −£440.73, 95% CI −1007.71 to 126.26), with QALYs not significantly different. [[Richards2016COBRA]]
- A meta-analysis of 26 randomised trials (1524 participants) found behavioural activation superior to controls (SMD −0.74, 95% CI −0.91 to −0.56) and to medication in four trials (SMD −0.42, 95% CI −0.83 to −0.00); study quality was mostly low and follow-up short. [[Ekers2014]]
- In a component network meta-analysis of internet CBT (76 trials), adding a behavioural activation component was associated with lower PHQ-9 scores (incremental mean difference −1.83, 95% CrI −2.90 to −0.80), while relaxation might be harmful (+1.20, 0.17 to 2.27) (Early evidence). [[Furukawa2021iCBT]]
- NICE lists individual behavioural activation as usually 8 regular sessions in less severe depression and 12 to 16 in more severe depression. [[Kendrick2022NICE]]

### psychotherapy · Interpersonal, problem-solving and psychodynamic therapies
- Interpersonal psychotherapy (IPT) for acute depression had a moderate-to-large effect versus control groups (g = 0.60, 95% CI 0.45–0.75) in a meta-analysis of 90 studies (11,434 participants) across all conditions. [[Cuijpers2016IPT]]
- IPT did not differ significantly from other therapies (differential g = 0.06) or from pharmacotherapy (g = −0.13); combined treatment was more effective than IPT alone (g = 0.24). [[Cuijpers2016IPT]]
- IPT in subthreshold depression significantly prevented the onset of major depression, and maintenance IPT significantly reduced relapse. [[Cuijpers2016IPT]]
- Problem-solving therapy: 30 randomised trials (3530 patients) gave g = 0.79 (95% CI 0.57–1.01) versus control with very high heterogeneity (I² = 84%); the nine low-risk-of-bias trials gave g = 0.34 (0.22–0.46). [[Cuijpers2018PST]]
- Short-term psychodynamic psychotherapy (STPP): 54 studies (33 randomised trials, 3946 subjects); STPP was more effective than control conditions after treatment (d = 0.49 to 0.69) and did not differ significantly from other psychotherapies after treatment (d = −0.14) or at follow-up (d = −0.06). [[Driessen2015STPP]]
- In a randomised trial of 341 adults assigned to 16 sessions of CBT or short-term psychodynamic supportive therapy, no significant differences were found; the average post-treatment remission rate was only 22.7%, and non-inferiority was shown for symptom scores but not for remission rates. [[Driessen2013]]
- NICE gives usual course lengths of 8 to 16 sessions (less severe) or 16 sessions (more severe) for IPT and for STPP, 6 to 12 sessions for individual problem-solving, and 8 (less severe) or 12 to 16 (more severe) for counselling. [[Kendrick2022NICE]]

### psychotherapy · Therapy, medicines or both
- A network meta-analysis of 101 studies (11,910 patients, mostly moderate to severe depression) found combined treatment more effective for response than psychotherapy alone (RR 1.27, 95% CI 1.14–1.39) and than pharmacotherapy alone (RR 1.25, 1.14–1.37). [[Cuijpers2020Combination]]
- In the same analysis psychotherapy alone and pharmacotherapy alone did not differ (RR 0.99, 95% CI 0.92–1.08). [[Cuijpers2020Combination]]
- Combined treatment (RR 1.23, 95% CI 1.05–1.45) and psychotherapy alone (RR 1.17, 1.02–1.32) were more acceptable (less dropout) than pharmacotherapy alone. [[Cuijpers2020Combination]]
- Adding psychotherapy to antidepressant medication gave g = 0.43 (95% CI 0.31–0.56) over medication alone across 52 studies in depressive and anxiety disorders, corresponding to an NNT of 4.20. [[Cuijpers2014Adding]]
- At six months or longer after randomisation, acute-phase combined treatment outperformed antidepressants alone (OR 2.93, 95% CI 2.15–3.99) but was no better than psychotherapy alone (23 trials, 2184 participants). [[Karyotaki2016Combined]]
- In a network meta-analysis of sustained response (81 trials, 13,722 participants; response followed by no relapse to about 12 months), combination beat pharmacotherapy (OR 2.52, 95% CI 1.66–3.85) and psychotherapy beat pharmacotherapy (OR 1.53, 1.00–2.35) when treatments were continued. [[Furukawa2021Sustained]]
- With an average sustained response of 29% on standard treatment, the advantage of psychotherapy or combination over pharmacotherapy or standard treatment was 12 to 16 percentage points. [[Furukawa2021Sustained]]
- In a 240-person placebo-controlled trial in moderate to severe depression, 8-week response was 50% with medication (paroxetine, with augmentation if needed), 43% with cognitive therapy and 25% with placebo; at 16 weeks response was 58% in each active arm and remission 46% (medication) versus 40% (cognitive therapy). [[DeRubeis2005]]
- The authors concluded that cognitive therapy can be as effective as medication for moderate to severe depression, but that this may depend on a high level of therapist experience (a site difference favoured medication at one centre). [[DeRubeis2005]]
- Individual patient data from 16 trials (1700 outpatients) showed that baseline severity did not moderate the difference between CBT and antidepressant medication for symptoms, response (OR 0.99) or remission (OR 1.00); medication had a small main-effect advantage on the HAM-D (β = −0.88). [[Weitz2015]]
- Against pill placebo (5 trials, n = 509), CBT's advantage was SMD −0.22 (95% CI −0.42 to −0.02) and was not influenced by baseline severity (interaction p = 0.43). [[Furukawa2017Severity]]
- ACP 2023 suggests combination of CBT and a second-generation antidepressant as an initial option in moderate to severe depression (conditional recommendation; low-certainty evidence), and switching to or augmenting with CBT as one option after non-response to an adequate antidepressant dose. [[Qaseem2023ACP]]
- CANMAT 2023: for a mild episode with low safety risk, psychotherapy and pharmacotherapy have similar efficacy, and the balance of benefits and risks favours a first-line psychotherapy if readily accessible; for moderate severity the choice is between structured psychotherapy and antidepressant medication. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

### psychotherapy · Lasting effects and relapse prevention
- People who responded to cognitive therapy and then stopped it relapsed less over 12 months than people withdrawn from medication onto placebo (30.8% versus 76.2%; p = 0.004), and no more than people kept on continuation medication (30.8% versus 47.2%; p = 0.20). [[Hollon2005]]
- Of those initially assigned in that trial, 104 patients (57.8%) had responded and entered the continuation phase; people withdrawn from cognitive therapy were allowed at most three booster sessions. [[Hollon2005]]
- A meta-analysis of 9 studies (506 patients) found that people given acute-phase CBT were less likely to relapse than people withdrawn from pharmacotherapy (OR 2.61, 95% CI 1.58–4.31), with no significant difference versus continued pharmacotherapy. [[Cuijpers2013Enduring]]
- At 6–12 months' follow-up, CBT's effects were larger than those of pharmacotherapies (g = 0.34, 95% CI 0.09–0.58), although the number of trials was small. [[Cuijpers2023CBT]]
- Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT), individual patient data from 9 trials (1258 patients with recurrent depression in full or partial remission): lower risk of relapse over 60 weeks versus no MBCT (HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.58–0.82) and versus active treatments (HR 0.79, 0.64–0.97). [[Kuyken2016]]
- The MBCT effect appeared larger in people with greater residual symptoms before treatment; age, sex, education and number of previous episodes did not modify it. [[Kuyken2016]]
- PREVENT (UK, 424 adults with three or more previous episodes, on maintenance antidepressants): MBCT with support to taper medication was not superior to maintenance antidepressants for time to relapse over 24 months (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.67–1.18; p = 0.43). [[Kuyken2015PREVENT]]
- In 84 people in remission after 8 months of antidepressant treatment, MBCT and maintenance medication each lowered relapse hazard by 73% versus placebo among "unstable remitters"; stable remitters showed no group differences. [[Segal2010]]
- A network meta-analysis (14 trials, 2077 participants) found MBCT better than treatment as usual for relapse (RR 0.73, 95% CI 0.54–0.98) and time to relapse (HR 0.57, 0.37–0.88), with no significant difference from active treatments. [[McCartney2021]]
- MBCT for current depressive symptoms (13 trials, n = 1046) was superior to non-specific controls after treatment (d = 0.71, 95% CI 0.47–0.96) and did not differ from other active therapies (d = 0.002). [[Goldberg2019MBCT]]
- Preventive cognitive therapy (289 people in remission with at least two previous episodes): antidepressants alone were not superior to preventive cognitive therapy while tapering (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.56–1.32), and adding the therapy to antidepressants cut relapse risk by 41% versus antidepressants alone (HR 0.59, 0.38–0.94) over 24 months. [[Bockting2018]]
- Individual participant data from 4 trials (714 participants): a psychological intervention during or after antidepressant tapering did not differ from continued antidepressants for time to relapse over 15 months (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.60–1.23). [[Breedvelt2021]]
- Sequential treatment (psychotherapy after response to acute pharmacotherapy; 17 trials, 2283 patients) lowered relapse or recurrence (RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.74–0.94). [[Guidi2021]]
- NICE: group CBT or MBCT for relapse prevention usually consists of 8 sessions over 2 to 3 months with the option of additional sessions in the next 12 months. [[Kendrick2022NICE]]
- CANMAT 2023 states that maintenance psychotherapies (CBT, MBCT, IPT, CBASP, problem-solving therapy) are superior to control conditions for preventing relapse with comparable effects between types, and notes booster sessions (for example 4 sessions over 12 months) with MBCT. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

### psychotherapy · Chronic depression and CBASP
- In 681 adults with chronic major depression treated for 12 weeks, the overall response rate was 48% with nefazodone, 48% with the cognitive behavioural-analysis system of psychotherapy (CBASP, 16 to 20 sessions) and 73% with the combination (662 analysed). [[Keller2000]]
- REVAMP: among 491 people with chronic depression who had not remitted on medication, adding CBASP or brief supportive psychotherapy for 12 weeks did not improve outcomes over continued optimised medication alone (phase 2 remission 15.0%, partial response 22.5%, non-response 62.5% overall). [[Kocsis2009REVAMP]]
- In 268 unmedicated outpatients with early-onset chronic depression, CBASP beat supportive psychotherapy at 20 weeks (adjusted HRSD-24 difference −2.51, 95% CI −4.16 to −0.86; d = 0.31), with response 38.7% versus 24.3% and remission 21.8% versus 12.6%. [[Schramm2017]]
- CoBalT follow-up: in people whose depression had not responded to antidepressants, adding CBT to usual care still showed lower BDI-II scores at a median of 45.5 months (19.2 versus 23.4; difference over 46 months −4.7, 95% CI −6.4 to −3.0) at £5374 per QALY. [[Wiles2016]]

### psychotherapy · Internet, digital and other delivery formats
- Individual patient data network meta-analysis of internet CBT (39 trials, 9751 participants): guided and unguided programmes both beat control; guided was better than unguided after treatment (PHQ-9 mean difference −0.8, 95% CI −1.4 to −0.2) but not at 6 or 12 months. [[Karyotaki2021iCBT]]
- Baseline severity was the main modifier: for subthreshold symptoms (PHQ-9 5–9) guided and unguided programmes differed little, while guidance gave better outcomes when baseline PHQ-9 was above 9. [[Karyotaki2021iCBT]]
- Self-guided internet CBT (13 trials, 3876 participants): Hedges g = 0.27 for symptom severity and OR 1.95 (95% CI 1.52–2.50) for response versus controls, NNT 8. [[Karyotaki2017iCBT]]
- Across 155 trials (15,191 participants), individual, group, telephone and guided self-help CBT did not differ significantly in effectiveness; all beat waiting list (SMD 0.87–1.02), care as usual (0.47–0.72) and unguided self-help (0.34–0.59). [[Cuijpers2019Formats]]
- Guided self-help was less acceptable (more dropout) than individual CBT (RR 1.44) and group CBT (RR 1.38). [[Cuijpers2019Formats]]
- Combining human and automated encouragement reduced dropout from internet CBT (incremental OR 0.32, 95% CrI 0.13–0.93). [[Furukawa2021iCBT]]
- CANMAT 2023 recommends guided internet CBT as a first-line monotherapy for mild depression. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

### psychotherapy · Collaborative care, stepped care and course length
- IMPACT randomised 1801 adults aged 60 or older with major depression (17%), dysthymia (30%) or both (53%) in 18 primary care clinics to collaborative care (a depression care manager supervised by a psychiatrist and a primary care expert, for up to 12 months) or usual care. [[Unutzer2002IMPACT]]
- At 12 months, 45% of collaborative care patients had at least a 50% reduction in depressive symptoms versus 19% with usual care (OR 3.45, 95% CI 2.71–4.38). [[Unutzer2002IMPACT]]
- Cochrane review of collaborative care (79 trials, 24,308 participants): better depression outcomes for adults in the short term (SMD −0.34, 95% CI −0.41 to −0.27; RR 1.32), medium term (SMD −0.28; RR 1.31) and long term (SMD −0.35; RR 1.29), but not clearly in the very long term (RR 1.12, 0.98–1.27). [[Archer2012]]
- An earlier meta-analysis of 37 trials (12,355 patients) found improved outcomes at 6 months (SMD 0.25, 95% CI 0.18–0.32) and up to 5 years (SMD 0.15, 0.001–0.31); effect size was related to medication adherence and to the professional background and supervision of case managers. [[Gilbody2006]]
- NICE NG222 splits depression into "less severe" (PHQ-9 below 16; subthreshold and mild) and "more severe" (PHQ-9 16 or more; moderate and severe). [[Kendrick2022NICE]]
- For less severe depression NICE lists eleven first-line options in order of the committee's view of clinical and cost effectiveness: guided self-help, group CBT, group behavioural activation, individual CBT, individual behavioural activation, group exercise, group mindfulness and meditation, IPT, SSRIs, counselling and STPP. [[Kendrick2022NICE]]
- NICE says not to routinely offer antidepressant medication as first-line treatment for less severe depression unless that is the person's informed preference. [[Kendrick2022NICE]]
- For more severe depression the NICE order is: individual CBT plus an antidepressant, individual CBT, individual behavioural activation, antidepressant medication, individual problem-solving, counselling, STPP, IPT, guided self-help and group exercise. [[Kendrick2022NICE]]
- NICE defines stepped care as delivering the most effective, least intrusive and least resource-intensive treatments first, with people who do not benefit "stepped up" to more intensive treatment; matched care adds the person's needs and preferences. [[Kendrick2022NICE]]
- NICE session counts for low-intensity options: guided self-help usually 6 to 8 structured sessions; group CBT and group behavioural activation usually 8 sessions with usually 8 participants in the group. [[Kendrick2022NICE]]
- CANMAT 2023: most improvement occurs in early sessions, and the strongest evidence suggests an optimal dose of 12 to 16 sessions for a first-line psychotherapy for most patients. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- A meta-regression of 70 studies (5403 patients) found only a small association between number of sessions and effect, none after adjustment, but a strong association with frequency: two sessions a week instead of one raised the effect size by g = 0.45 with the total number of sessions held constant. [[Cuijpers2013Sessions]]

### psychotherapy · Therapist, alliance, harms and bias
- The alliance between person and therapist correlates with outcome at r = 0.278 (95% CI 0.256–0.299; equivalent to d = 0.579) across 295 studies and more than 30,000 patients; the correlation for internet-based therapy was similar (r = 0.275). [[Fluckiger2018]]
- Therapist effects (the share of outcome variance explained by which therapist a person sees) ranged from 0.2% to 29% across 20 studies, with a weighted average of 5% (8.2% in randomised trials). [[Johns2019]]
- Deterioration: across 18 studies, psychotherapy lowered the risk of clinically significant deterioration versus control (RR 0.39, 95% CI 0.27–0.57; NNT 20 to avoid one case), but the median deterioration rate in therapy groups was still 4%. [[Cuijpers2018Deterioration]]
- Only 6% of trials comparing psychotherapy with a control condition reported deterioration rates at all. [[Cuijpers2018Deterioration]]
- In the larger response meta-analysis, deterioration was 5% with psychotherapy versus 12–13% in control conditions. [[Cuijpers2021Response]]
- Publication bias: in 117 trials the mean effect of psychotherapy versus control fell from 0.67 to 0.42 after adjustment (51 imputed studies). [[Cuijpers2010PubBias]]
- Of 55 US NIH-funded trials of psychological treatment for major depressive disorder (grants 1972–2008), 13 (23.6%) were never published; adding the unpublished data lowered the effect versus control from g = 0.52 to g = 0.39, a 25% reduction. [[Driessen2015NIH]]
- Adjusting for risk of bias, publication bias and waiting-list controls together lowered the overall effect of psychotherapy for adult depression from g = 0.70 (NNT 4.18) to g = 0.31; only 23% of studies were at low risk of bias. [[Cuijpers2019Eysenck]]
- Component (dismantling) studies cannot yet say which ingredients matter: 16 studies gave a pooled difference of g = 0.21 (95% CI 0.03–0.39) between full and reduced treatments, and only one study was adequately powered. [[Cuijpers2019Components]]

### psychotherapy · Guidelines in brief
- CANMAT 2023 keeps CBT, IPT and behavioural activation as first-line psychological treatments for acute major depressive disorder; CBASP, mindfulness-based interventions including MBCT, problem-solving therapy and STPP are second-line; acceptance and commitment therapy is third-line. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- The VA/DoD 2022 guideline contains 36 GRADE-based recommendations and two one-page algorithms covering pharmacological management, psychotherapy, complementary and alternative therapies and telemedicine. [[McQuaid2022VADoD]]
- The American Psychological Association guideline covers adolescents, adults and older adults (60 and over), and is based on ten systematic reviews and meta-analyses of psychological and complementary treatments, alone and with pharmacotherapy. [[APAPanel2022]]
- ACP 2023 asks that the choice between CBT, a second-generation antidepressant or both be personalised through discussion of benefits, harms, cost, feasibility, symptoms, comorbidities and the person's preferences. [[Qaseem2023ACP]]

### lifestyle · Exercise as a treatment
- Cochrane 2013 (39 trials, 2326 participants): exercise versus no treatment or control gave SMD −0.62 (95% CI −0.81 to −0.42; 35 trials), but only −0.18 (−0.47 to 0.11, not significant) in the six trials with adequate concealment, intention-to-treat analysis and blinded outcome assessment. [[Cooney2013]]
- Cochrane 2026 update (73 trials, at least 4985 participants): SMD −0.67 (95% CI −0.82 to −0.52; 57 trials; low-certainty evidence), and −0.46 (−0.88 to −0.04) in the seven methodologically strongest trials. [[Clegg2026]]
- In the 2026 update, exercise and psychological therapy probably differ little (SMD 0.03, 95% CI −0.16 to 0.23; 10 trials; moderate certainty), and exercise and pharmacological treatment may differ little (SMD −0.11, −0.33 to 0.10; 5 trials; low certainty). [[Clegg2026]]
- Long-term follow-up evidence for exercise is very uncertain (SMD −0.53, 95% CI −1.11 to 0.06; 9 trials), and adverse events were uncommon but included musculoskeletal injuries. [[Clegg2026]]
- A meta-analysis adjusting for publication bias (25 trials) reported a large effect (SMD 1.11, 95% CI 0.79–1.43), larger for major depressive disorder, aerobic exercise, moderate and vigorous intensity and supervised formats. [[Schuch2016]]
- A trial-sequential meta-analysis (35 trials, 2498 participants) found SMD −0.66 (95% CI −0.86 to −0.46) overall but −0.11 (−0.41 to 0.18) in the four trials less affected by bias. [[Krogh2017]]
- A 2023 meta-analysis (41 studies, 2264 participants) found SMD −0.946 (95% CI −1.18 to −0.71; NNT 2), and −0.666 (−0.99 to −0.34; NNT 2.8) in low-risk-of-bias studies. [[Heissel2023]]
- An umbrella review of 97 systematic reviews (1039 trials, 128,119 participants) found a median effect of physical activity on depression of −0.43 (IQR −0.66 to −0.27) versus usual care, with higher intensity associated with greater improvement. [[Singh2023]]
- Network meta-analysis of 218 studies (14,170 participants with major depression), Hedges g versus active controls: walking or jogging −0.62 (95% CrI −0.80 to −0.45), yoga −0.55 (−0.73 to −0.36), strength training −0.49 (−0.69 to −0.29), mixed aerobic exercise −0.43 (−0.61 to −0.24), tai chi or qigong −0.42 (−0.65 to −0.21). [[Noetel2024]]
- In the same network, dance had the largest estimate (g −0.96, −1.36 to −0.56) but from only 5 arms and 107 participants; CBT alone was −0.55 (−0.75 to −0.37) and SSRIs −0.26 (−0.50 to −0.01). [[Noetel2024]]
- Effects rose with prescribed intensity: light activity (walking, hatha yoga) g −0.58 (−0.82 to −0.33) versus vigorous exercise (running, interval training) g −0.74 (−1.10 to −0.38). [[Noetel2024]]
- Only one of the 218 studies met Cochrane criteria for low risk of bias, so confidence was low for walking or jogging and very low for the other modalities. [[Noetel2024]]
- Resistance training alone (33 trials, 1877 participants) reduced depressive symptoms (effect 0.66, 95% CI 0.48–0.83; NNT 4), with smaller effects in trials that blinded allocation or assessment. [[Gordon2018]]
- Dose-response trial (80 adults aged 20–45 with mild to moderate major depressive disorder, 12 weeks): HRSD-17 fell 47% with the "public health dose" (17.5 kcal/kg/week), 30% with the low dose (7.0 kcal/kg/week) and 29% with stretching control; frequency (3 or 5 days a week) made no difference. [[Dunn2005]]
- TREAD (126 sedentary adults not remitted on an SSRI): 12-week remission was 28.3% with 16 kcal/kg/week of added exercise versus 15.5% with 4 kcal/kg/week (NNT 7.8; trend, p < 0.06). [[Trivedi2011TREAD]]
- SMILE (202 adults, 16 weeks): remission was 45% with supervised exercise, 40% with home exercise, 47% with sertraline and 31% with placebo (p = 0.057 for active treatments versus placebo). [[Blumenthal2007]]
- A pragmatic UK primary care trial (361 adults) of facilitated physical activity added to usual care found no improvement in depression at 4 months (BDI difference −0.54, 95% CI −3.06 to 1.99), although participants did become more active. [[Chalder2012]]
- In non-severe depression (21 trials, n = 2551), exercise, antidepressants and their combination did not differ (exercise versus antidepressants SMD −0.12, 95% CI −0.33 to 0.10); dropout was higher with exercise than with antidepressants (RR 1.31). [[Recchia2022]]
- In a partially randomised preference trial (141 people with depression or anxiety disorders), 16-week remission was 44.8% with antidepressants and 43.3% with group running therapy, while physical health measures (weight, waist, blood pressure, heart rate) favoured running. [[Verhoeven2023]]
- Dropout from exercise arms in trials was 18.1% (95% CI 15.0–21.8), lower than in control arms (OR 0.642), and lower when sessions were supervised by physiotherapists or exercise physiologists. [[Stubbs2016Dropout]]
- CANMAT 2023 supports supervised exercise (low to moderate intensity, 30 to 40 minutes at a time, 3 to 4 times a week, for a minimum of 9 weeks) as first-line monotherapy for mild depression and second-line adjunctive treatment for moderate severity. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- NICE describes group exercise for depression as a programme from a trained practitioner, usually more than one session a week for 10 weeks, usually 8 participants per group, including moderate-intensity aerobic exercise. [[Kendrick2022NICE]]
- The WFSBP/ASLM lifestyle guideline gives its highest rating (Grade 2) to physical activity and exercise, relaxation techniques, work-directed interventions, sleep and mindfulness-based therapies; diet and green space are Grade 3; smoking cessation and loneliness or social support recommendations rest on expert opinion. [[Marx2023WFSBP]]

### lifestyle · Physical activity and prevention (dose-response)
- Dose-response meta-analysis of 15 prospective cohorts (191,130 adults; 2,110,588 person-years): compared with no activity, 4.4 mMET-h/week (half the recommended volume) was associated with 18% lower risk of depression (95% CI 13–23%). [[Pearce2022]]
- At the recommended 8.8 mMET-h/week (equivalent to 2.5 hours a week of brisk walking) risk was 25% lower (95% CI 18–32%), with diminishing extra benefit and more uncertainty above that level (Established as an association; causality not proven). [[Pearce2022]]
- If less active adults had reached the recommended level, 11.5% (95% CI 7.7–15.4%) of depression cases could have been prevented (population estimate, assuming causality). [[Pearce2022]]
- In 49 prospective studies (266,939 people), high versus low physical activity was associated with lower odds of incident depression (adjusted OR 0.83, 95% CI 0.79–0.88): youths 0.90, adults 0.78, older people 0.79. [[Schuch2018]]
- HUNT cohort (33,908 healthy adults, 11 years): most of the protection came at low levels of exercise regardless of intensity; 12% of future cases could have been prevented if everyone had done at least 1 hour of activity a week (assuming causality). [[Harvey2018HUNT]]
- Mendelian randomisation supports a protective effect of accelerometer-measured activity on major depressive disorder (OR 0.74 per 1-SD increase, 95% CI 0.59–0.92), with no evidence that depression lowers measured activity (Early evidence). [[Choi2019MR]]

### lifestyle · Diet
- SMILES (Australia, 12 weeks, 67 adults with moderate to severe depression, most on other treatment): seven dietitian sessions versus social support; remission (MADRS below 10) 32.3% (10 people) versus 8.0% (2 people), NNT 4.1 (95% CI 2.3–27.8); Cohen's d −1.16 (Early evidence: one small single-blind trial). [[Jacka2017SMILES]]
- Meta-analysis of 16 randomised trials (45,826 participants, almost all non-clinical samples): dietary interventions reduced depressive symptoms slightly (g = 0.162, 95% CI 0.055–0.269) with no effect on anxiety. [[Firth2019Diet]]
- Observational studies: highest versus lowest adherence to a Mediterranean diet was associated with lower incident depression (RR 0.67, 95% CI 0.55–0.82; four longitudinal studies), as was a lower Dietary Inflammatory Index (RR 0.76, 0.63–0.92). [[Lassale2019]]
- MooDFOOD prevention trial (1025 overweight adults with subsyndromal symptoms, 1 year): neither multinutrient supplements (OR 1.06, 95% CI 0.87–1.29) nor food-related behavioural activation therapy (OR 0.93, 0.76–1.13) reduced new episodes of major depressive disorder; 105 (10%) developed one. [[Bot2019]]
- CANMAT 2023 gives diet a third-line recommendation, noting only one randomised trial of a Mediterranean-type diet in adults with major depressive disorder. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

### lifestyle · Omega-3, vitamin D and other supplements
- Omega-3 meta-analysis (26 trials, 2160 participants): overall SMD −0.28; benefit was confined to EPA-pure or EPA-major (at least 60% EPA) formulations at 1 g/day or less (SMD −0.50 and −1.03); DHA-pure and DHA-major formulations showed none (Early evidence). [[Liao2019]]
- Cochrane 2021 (35 studies): omega-3 versus placebo SMD −0.40 (95% CI −0.64 to −0.16; very low certainty), about 2.5 HDRS-17 points, below the 3.0-point minimal important difference; no difference in remission (OR 1.13) or response (OR 1.20). [[Appleton2021]]
- VITAL-DEP (18,353 US adults aged 50 or older, median 5.3 years): vitamin D3 2000 IU/day did not change the risk of depression or clinically relevant symptoms (HR 0.97, 95% CI 0.87–1.09) or mood scores. [[Okereke2020]]
- In the same trial, omega-3 (1 g/day fish oil) did not prevent depression; risk was slightly higher than with placebo (HR 1.13, 95% CI 1.01–1.26) with no difference in mood scores. [[Okereke2021Omega3]]
- A review of adjunctive nutraceuticals found mainly positive replicated results for SAMe, methylfolate, omega-3 and vitamin D added to antidepressants, with marked heterogeneity and funnel-plot asymmetry (Early evidence). [[Sarris2016]]
- CANMAT 2023 downgraded omega-3 fatty acids and SAMe to third-line and lists L-methylfolate as second-line because of small effect sizes. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

### lifestyle · Sleep and CBT for insomnia
- People without depression who have insomnia have about twice the risk of later depression (OR 2.60, 95% CI 1.98–3.42 random effects; 2.10, 1.86–2.38 fixed effects; 21 longitudinal studies). [[Baglioni2011]]
- Across 65 trials (8608 participants), interventions that improved sleep also improved depression (g = −0.63), with a dose-response relationship between sleep improvement and mental health improvement. [[Scott2021Sleep]]
- OASIS (3755 UK university students with insomnia): digital CBT for insomnia reduced insomnia at 10 weeks (Cohen's d = 1.11) and also paranoia and hallucinations, with insomnia mediating the change. [[Freeman2017OASIS]]
- In people with insomnia disorder (358 digital CBT for insomnia, 300 sleep education), the number reporting no depression at 1 year was 51% higher with digital CBT for insomnia, and incident moderate-to-severe depression was halved among those with minimal or no depression at baseline. [[Cheng2019]]
- GoodNight Study (1149 adults with insomnia and subclinical depressive symptoms): an online insomnia programme lowered PHQ-9 scores at 6 weeks and 6 months, but new major depressive disorder at 6 months was rare (22 participants, 4%) and did not differ between groups. [[Christensen2016]]
- Pilot trial (30 people with major depressive disorder and insomnia): escitalopram plus CBT for insomnia gave depression remission in 61.5% versus 33.3% with escitalopram plus control therapy (Early evidence). [[Manber2008]]
- CANMAT 2023: sleep hygiene instruction is likely beneficial despite limited trial evidence, and CBT for insomnia shows promising effects in small trials. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

### lifestyle · Bright light therapy and seasonal depression
- The typical published protocol is 10,000 lux fluorescent white light for 30 minutes daily in the morning, shortly after awakening. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Can-SAD (96 adults with winter seasonal depression, 8 weeks): 10,000-lux light for 30 minutes each morning versus fluoxetine 20 mg/day gave response in 67% of each group and remission in 50% versus 54%; light worked faster at week 1 and fluoxetine caused more agitation, sleep disturbance and palpitations. [[Lam2006CANSAD]]
- Non-seasonal major depressive disorder (122 adults, 8 weeks): mean MADRS change was 13.4 with light, 8.8 with fluoxetine, 16.9 with the combination and 6.5 with placebo. [[Lam2016Light]]
- In that trial, response was 50.0% (light), 29.0% (fluoxetine), 75.9% (combination) and 33.3% (placebo); remission 43.8%, 19.4%, 58.6% and 30.0%; combination versus placebo NNT 2.4 for response and 3.5 for remission. [[Lam2016Light]]
- A 2005 meta-analysis gave effect sizes of 0.84 (95% CI 0.60–1.08) for bright light in seasonal depression, 0.73 (0.37–1.08) for dawn simulation in seasonal depression and 0.53 (0.18–0.89) for bright light in non-seasonal depression; only 13% of studies met inclusion criteria. [[Golden2005]]
- A 2020 meta-analysis of 19 trials in seasonal depression gave a smaller effect (SMD −0.37, 95% CI −0.63 to −0.12; response RR 1.42, 1.08–1.85). [[Pjrek2020]]
- In non-seasonal depression (11 trials, 858 patients), adjunctive bright light gave remission 40.7% versus 23.5% (OR 2.42, 95% CI 1.50–3.91). [[MenegazdeAlmeida2025]]
- Dawn simulation trial (99 adults with winter depression, 3 weeks): improvement was 57.1% with bright light (30 minutes, 10,000 lux), 49.5% with naturalistic dawn simulation, 42.7% with a dawn light pulse, 47.9% with high-density negative ions and 22.7% with low-density ions. [[Terman2006]]
- CBT tailored for seasonal depression versus light therapy (177 adults, 6 weeks): remission 47.6% versus 47.2%; light was 10,000 lux started at 30 minutes each morning, and the CBT was 12 group sessions (two 90-minute sessions a week). [[Rohan2015]]
- Two winters later, recurrence was 27.3% after the CBT versus 45.6% after light therapy; the first winter showed no difference. [[Rohan2016]]
- Prevention with light is unproven: the only eligible trial (46 people) was too small and biased (bright light RR 0.64, 95% CI 0.30–1.38; very low-quality evidence). [[NussbaumerStreit2019]]
- Bupropion XL is labelled in the US for prevention of seasonal major depressive episodes in people with seasonal affective disorder; in the three placebo-controlled trials combined, 84.3% versus 72.0% were depression-free at the end of treatment. [[FDA-WellbutrinXL]]
- The label describes starting 150 mg once daily in the autumn before symptoms begin, a target of 300 mg once daily after 7 days, continuation through winter and tapering in early spring; it carries the antidepressant class boxed warning on suicidal thoughts and behaviours in children, adolescents and young adults. [[FDA-WellbutrinXL]]
- The three prevention trials (1042 people) reported recurrence of 19% versus 30%, 13% versus 21% and 16% versus 31% (bupropion XL versus placebo), a 44% relative risk reduction overall. [[Modell2005]]
- CANMAT 2023: light therapy is first-line monotherapy for seasonal (winter) depression (Established), second-line for mild non-seasonal depression and adjunctive for moderate non-seasonal depression. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- NICE advises that people with winter seasonal depression who prefer light therapy be told that the evidence for its efficacy is uncertain (a 2009 recommendation). [[Kendrick2022NICE]]

### lifestyle · Wake therapy and triple chronotherapy
- A 2005 expert committee statement described wake therapy (supervised sleep deprivation) as producing marked improvement within hours in approximately 60% of patients, with relapse prevented by light therapy, medication or a sleep phase advance. [[WirzJustice2005]]
- A meta-analysis of 66 studies put the response rate to sleep deprivation at 45% in studies with a randomised control group and 50% in those without. [[Boland2017]]
- A stricter review of controlled trials (8 studies, 368 patients) found no significant benefit one week after starting (SMD −0.29, 95% CI −0.84 to 0.25), and reported switch to mania in 2.7% to 10.7%. [[Ioannou2021]]
- Triple chronotherapy (sleep deprivation, sleep phase advance and bright light): six studies reported response rates of 50% to 84%, but only one had a control group (Early evidence). [[DAgostino2020]]
- CANMAT 2023 keeps sleep deprivation as a third-line recommendation. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

### lifestyle · St John's wort, acupuncture and yoga
- Cochrane review of St John's wort (29 trials, 5489 patients with major depression): response rate ratio versus placebo 1.28 (95% CI 1.10–1.49) in nine larger trials and 1.87 in nine smaller ones; versus SSRIs 1.00 (0.90–1.11; 12 trials). [[Linde2008]]
- Fewer people stopped St John's wort because of adverse effects than stopped older antidepressants (OR 0.24) or SSRIs (OR 0.53); trials from German-speaking countries reported more favourable results. [[Linde2008]]
- A later review (35 studies, 6993 patients) found more responders than placebo (RR 1.53, 95% CI 1.19–1.97) and no difference from antidepressants in mild and moderate depression (RR 1.01), with a lack of research in severe depression. [[Apaydin2016]]
- A US trial in moderately severe major depression (340 adults, 8 weeks) found neither St John's wort nor sertraline better than placebo on the primary outcomes (full response 23.9%, 24.8% and 31.9%). [[HDTSG2002]]
- Interactions: of 19 pharmacokinetic trials with plasma data, 17 found that St John's wort lowered the blood levels of the conventional drug taken with it (Established). [[Mills2004]]
- CANMAT 2023 calls St John's wort a potent CYP3A4 inducer, warns of an uncertain risk of serotonin syndrome when combined with serotonergic medicines, and still rates it first-line for mild and second-line for moderate episodes. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- NICE takes the opposite practical line: do not prescribe or advise St John's wort because of uncertain doses, variable preparations and potentially serious interactions (including hormonal contraceptives, anticoagulants and anticonvulsants). [[Kendrick2022NICE]]
- Acupuncture, Cochrane 2018 (64 studies, 7104 participants): versus no treatment or usual care SMD −0.66 (95% CI −1.06 to −0.25; low quality); versus sham acupuncture only 1.69 HAMD points (−3.33 to −0.05; low quality) (Early evidence). [[Smith2018Acupuncture]]
- Yoga: 12 trials (619 participants) gave SMD −0.69 (95% CI −0.99 to −0.39) versus usual care; no trial reported safety data (Early evidence). [[Cramer2013]]
- In people with diagnosed mental disorders (13 trials in meta-analysis), physically active yoga reduced depressive symptoms (SMD −0.41, 95% CI −0.65 to −0.17), more so with more sessions per week. [[Brinsley2021]]
- CANMAT 2016 recommended exercise, light therapy, St John's wort, omega-3, SAMe and yoga as first- or second-line options for mild to moderate depression; the 2023 update rates acupuncture second-line for mild depression. [[Ravindran2016CANMAT,Lam2024CANMAT]]

### lifestyle · Alcohol, smoking, social connection and peer support
- Alcohol use disorder and major depression each roughly double the risk of the other (pooled adjusted ORs 2.00 to 2.09); the most plausible causal direction is that alcohol use disorder raises the risk of depression. [[Boden2011]]
- Among people with depression, the median prevalence of current alcohol problems was 16% (range 5–67%) and lifetime 30% (range 10–60%), versus 7% current in the general population; alcohol problems were associated with a worse course of depression. [[Sullivan2005Alcohol]]
- Stopping smoking is associated with less depression (SMD −0.25, 95% CI −0.37 to −0.12) and anxiety (−0.37) than continuing to smoke, in people with and without psychiatric disorders (observational, 26 studies). [[Taylor2014Smoking]]
- Loneliness: adults who were often lonely had higher odds of new-onset depression (pooled adjusted OR 2.33, 95% CI 1.62–3.34; eight cohorts, heterogeneous). [[Mann2022Loneliness]]
- In adults aged 50 and over in England followed for 12 years, loneliness accounted for an estimated 18% (95% CI 12–24) of depression at 1 year and 11% (3–19) at the final follow-up (population attributable fraction). [[Lee2021Loneliness]]
- In more than 100,000 UK Biobank participants screened for 106 modifiable factors, confiding in others was protective and supported by Mendelian randomisation (OR 0.76, 95% CI 0.67–0.86); television watching (OR 1.09) and daytime napping (OR 1.34) were associated with higher risk (Early evidence). [[Choi2020]]
- Peer support: seven trials (869 participants) found peer support better than usual care for depressive symptoms (SMD −0.59, 95% CI −0.98 to −0.21) and not significantly different from group CBT (SMD 0.10; seven trials, 301 participants). [[Pfeiffer2011]]
- A meta-review of lifestyle factors found convergent evidence for physical activity in prevention and treatment, emerging evidence that smoking is a causal factor, poor sleep as a risk factor, and a need to establish causality for diet. [[Firth2020Lifestyle]]

### Exam hooks
- Psychotherapy response in depression is about 41%, versus about 17% with usual care and 16% on a waiting list; NNT about 5 versus usual care. [[Cuijpers2021Response]]
- The main bona fide psychotherapies have similar efficacy; only non-directive supportive counselling was less efficacious in the 331-trial network meta-analysis. [[Cuijpers2021Psychotherapies]]
- Combined psychotherapy plus antidepressant beats either alone for response (RR about 1.25–1.27); psychotherapy alone equals pharmacotherapy alone (RR 0.99). [[Cuijpers2020Combination]]
- Baseline severity does not moderate CBT versus antidepressant outcomes in outpatients. [[Weitz2015]]
- Cognitive therapy has an enduring effect: relapse 30.8% after stopping cognitive therapy versus 76.2% after stopping medication. [[Hollon2005]]
- MBCT lowers relapse in recurrent depression (HR 0.69) but was not superior to maintenance antidepressants in PREVENT (HR 0.89). [[Kuyken2016,Kuyken2015PREVENT]]
- Chronic depression: nefazodone 48%, CBASP 48%, combination 73%. [[Keller2000]]
- Behavioural activation delivered by junior workers is non-inferior to CBT (COBRA) and costs less. [[Richards2016COBRA]]
- Collaborative care (IMPACT): 45% versus 19% response at 12 months in older adults. [[Unutzer2002IMPACT]]
- Guided internet CBT beats unguided mainly when baseline PHQ-9 is above 9. [[Karyotaki2021iCBT]]
- NICE NG222 uses a PHQ-9 score of 16 to divide less severe from more severe depression and lists guided self-help first for less severe depression. [[Kendrick2022NICE]]
- ACP 2023: CBT or a second-generation antidepressant for moderate to severe depression; CBT for mild depression. [[Qaseem2023ACP]]
- CANMAT first-line psychotherapies are CBT, IPT and behavioural activation; 12 to 16 sessions is the cited optimal dose. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Published psychotherapy effects are inflated by publication bias (0.67 falls to 0.42), as are antidepressant effects. [[Cuijpers2010PubBias,Driessen2015NIH]]
- Exercise effect shrinks in the most rigorous trials: Cochrane SMD −0.62 overall versus −0.18 (2013) and −0.67 versus −0.46 (2026). [[Cooney2013,Clegg2026]]
- Physical activity at 8.8 mMET-h/week (2.5 hours of brisk walking) is associated with 25% lower risk of depression; most of the benefit is at low volumes. [[Pearce2022]]
- Standard bright light protocol: 10,000 lux for 30 minutes in the early morning; light equalled fluoxetine in Can-SAD (67% response each). [[Lam2006CANSAD,Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Bupropion XL is the US-labelled medicine for preventing seasonal depressive episodes. [[FDA-WellbutrinXL]]
- St John's wort induces drug metabolism and lowers blood levels of many medicines. [[Mills2004,Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Vitamin D3 and omega-3 supplements did not prevent depression in VITAL-DEP. [[Okereke2020,Okereke2021Omega3]]

### In plain words
- Talking therapies help many people with depression. In studies, about 4 in 10 people improved a lot with therapy, compared with fewer than 2 in 10 who had usual care only. These are averages for groups, not a forecast for one person. [[Cuijpers2021Response]]
- No single type of talking therapy is clearly the best. What is available, and what suits the person, matters more. [[Cuijpers2021Psychotherapies]]
- CBT looks at how thoughts, feelings and actions affect each other, and teaches skills to change unhelpful patterns. [[Beck2008]]
- Behavioural activation helps a person slowly get back to activities that matter to them. It worked as well as CBT in a large UK study. [[Richards2016COBRA]]
- For many people with moderate or severe depression, therapy and medicine together work better than either one alone. [[Cuijpers2020Combination]]
- The skills learned in therapy can keep helping after therapy ends. [[Hollon2005,Cuijpers2013Enduring]]
- For people who have had depression several times, mindfulness-based cognitive therapy can lower the chance of it coming back. [[Kuyken2016]]
- Online CBT programmes can help, and they tend to work better with support from a trained person. [[Karyotaki2021iCBT]]
- Therapy does not help everyone, and a small number of people feel worse during it. Telling the therapist or doctor means the plan can be changed. [[Cuijpers2018Deterioration]]
- Regular physical activity can ease depression. Walking, jogging, yoga and strength training all showed benefit in studies, and even small amounts are linked to lower risk. [[Noetel2024,Pearce2022]]
- Good sleep matters. Treating insomnia can also improve mood. [[Scott2021Sleep]]
- Bright light in the morning is an established treatment for winter depression. [[Lam2006CANSAD,Lam2024CANMAT]]
- St John's wort is a herbal product that can interfere with many medicines, including the contraceptive pill. Anyone thinking about it should talk to a pharmacist or doctor first. [[Mills2004,Kendrick2022NICE]]
- Vitamin D and fish oil capsules did not prevent depression in a very large study. [[Okereke2020,Okereke2021Omega3]]
- Feeling connected to other people is linked with a lower chance of depression, and peer support groups helped in studies. [[Choi2020,Pfeiffer2011]]
- Lifestyle steps work alongside therapy and medicine; they do not replace care from a clinician. Any change to a medicine is made with the prescriber. [[Marx2023WFSBP]]

## Open questions and controversies
- Is any psychotherapy better than another? Network meta-analyses find few differences among bona fide therapies [[Cuijpers2021Psychotherapies,Barth2013]]; CBT has a very small, fragile advantage [[Cuijpers2023CBT]]; CANMAT still ranks CBT, IPT and behavioural activation first because their evidence is most robust [[Lam2024CANMAT]]. Status: open; allegiance and lack of blinding limit comparisons.
- How large is the true effect of psychotherapy? Unadjusted g about 0.7 [[Cuijpers2019Eysenck]]; about 0.3–0.4 after adjusting for publication bias, risk of bias and waiting-list controls [[Cuijpers2010PubBias,Driessen2015NIH,Cuijpers2019Eysenck]]; against pill placebo CBT gives SMD −0.22 [[Furukawa2017Severity]]. Status: effect is real but smaller than early literature suggested, as for antidepressants.
- Should severe depression be treated with medicine rather than therapy? Individual patient data find no moderation by severity in outpatients [[Weitz2015,Furukawa2017Severity]]; ACP offers CBT or an antidepressant for moderate to severe depression [[Qaseem2023ACP]]; NICE ranks CBT plus an antidepressant first for more severe depression [[Kendrick2022NICE]]. Status: combination favoured for more severe illness; the data do not cover inpatients.
- Can therapy replace long-term antidepressants? MBCT or preventive cognitive therapy with tapering was not inferior in pooled data (HR 0.86) [[Breedvelt2021]] and PREVENT found no superiority either way [[Kuyken2015PREVENT]]; CANMAT cites newer large trials in which medication maintenance had lower recurrence [[Lam2024CANMAT]]. Status: individual decision with the prescriber; evidence still mixed.
- Is exercise a "core" treatment? Noetel and Heissel report moderate to large effects and call exercise comparable to therapy and medicines [[Noetel2024,Heissel2023]]; Cochrane and Krogh show the effect shrinks or vanishes in the most rigorous trials and that long-term evidence is very uncertain [[Clegg2026,Cooney2013,Krogh2017]]; a pragmatic UK trial of facilitated activity was null [[Chalder2012]]. Status: recommended by NICE, CANMAT and WFSBP, with low-certainty evidence.
- Does more exercise help more? Intensity showed a dose-response in trials, but weekly energy dose did not [[Noetel2024]]; Dunn found a dose effect [[Dunn2005]]; cohort data show the steepest gains at low volumes [[Pearce2022,Harvey2018HUNT]]. Status: unresolved.
- Does diet treat depression? SMILES was positive but small and single-blind [[Jacka2017SMILES]]; pooled trials show a small effect in mostly non-clinical samples [[Firth2019Diet]]; MooDFOOD prevention was null [[Bot2019]]. Status: early evidence; third-line in CANMAT [[Lam2024CANMAT]].
- Omega-3: EPA-predominant products looked helpful in one meta-analysis [[Liao2019]]; Cochrane judged the effect unlikely to be clinically meaningful and probably biased [[Appleton2021]]; prevention failed [[Okereke2021Omega3]]. Status: downgraded by CANMAT [[Lam2024CANMAT]].
- Light therapy outside winter depression: positive trial and meta-analyses [[Lam2016Light,MenegazdeAlmeida2025]] versus NICE's view that even the seasonal evidence is uncertain [[Kendrick2022NICE]] and a smaller pooled effect in seasonal depression in 2020 [[Pjrek2020]]. Status: guidelines disagree.
- St John's wort: CANMAT rates it first-line for mild episodes [[Lam2024CANMAT]]; NICE says do not prescribe or advise it [[Kendrick2022NICE]]; the largest US trial was null but so was its sertraline arm [[HDTSG2002]]. Status: efficacy signal in mild to moderate depression, with interaction risk the deciding concern [[Mills2004]].
- Wake therapy: expert consensus and uncontrolled studies report about 45–60% rapid response [[WirzJustice2005,Boland2017]]; controlled trials do not confirm a benefit at one week [[Ioannou2021]]. Status: third-line, specialist setting only [[Lam2024CANMAT]].
- Does adding psychotherapy help when chronic depression has not remitted on medicine? Keller found a large benefit of combination from the start [[Keller2000]]; REVAMP found none as augmentation [[Kocsis2009REVAMP]]; CoBalT found a lasting benefit of adding CBT in primary care [[Wiles2016]]. Status: depends on population and timing.
