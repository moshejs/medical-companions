## Findings

### trd · Definitions and staging
- There is no consensus definition of treatment-resistant depression (TRD); the definition used most often, and the one adopted by the US FDA and the European Medicines Agency, is an inadequate response to at least two antidepressants despite adequate treatment trials and adherence. [[McIntyre2023TRD]]
- A systematic review for the US Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality also found no consensus definition; the most common one required at least two prior treatment failures with confirmed adequate dose and duration, and there was no agreement on what counts as an adequate dose or duration. [[Gaynes2020]]
- Only 17% of intervention studies reviewed enrolled samples that met the most frequently specified TRD criteria. [[Gaynes2020]]
- The US esketamine label describes its TRD trial population as adults with DSM-5 major depressive disorder who, in the current episode, "had not responded adequately to at least two different antidepressants of adequate dose and duration". [[FDA-Spravato]]
- The US olanzapine-fluoxetine label defines TRD as major depressive disorder in a person who does not respond to "2 separate trials of different antidepressants of adequate dose and duration in the current episode". [[FDA-OlanzapineFluoxetine]]
- An older, broader usage counted an inadequate response to one adequate antidepressant trial as treatment resistance; on that usage up to 50–60% of patients do not achieve an adequate response. [[Fava2003TRD]]
- The Thase–Rush model (1997) arranges resistance in five stages from lesser to greater, moving from an alternative monotherapy to combination or augmentation regimens, with non-selective monoamine oxidase inhibitors typically reserved for stage III and electroconvulsive therapy for stage IV. [[Thase1997]]
- The Maudsley Staging Method is a points-based model with three factors: treatment (5-point subscale by number of medications), severity (score 1 subsyndromal to 5 severe with psychosis) and duration of the presenting episode (3-point subscale); totals run from 3 (minimal resistance) to 15 (severe resistance). [[Fekadu2009]]
- CANMAT 2023 criticises the two-antidepressant definition because it neglects psychological and neurostimulation treatments, assumes switching is the preferred first strategy, leaves "failure" undefined and ignores partial response. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- "Difficult-to-treat depression" (DTD) was proposed by an international consensus group and defined as "depression that continues to cause significant burden despite usual treatment efforts". [[McAllisterWilliams2020]]
- In the DTD model the first aim of treatment shifts from remission to optimal symptom control, daily psychosocial function and quality of life, using shared decision-making. [[McAllisterWilliams2020]]
- CANMAT 2023 adopted DTD as its framework and reports that patients find it a more supportive, hopeful and collaborative term than "treatment-resistant". [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- The DTD consensus authors note the definition rests largely on expert consensus and may be too imprecise to define populations for regulatory trials. [[McAllisterWilliams2020]]

### trd · How common it is
- At least 30% of people with depression are estimated to meet the two-antidepressant definition of TRD. [[McIntyre2023TRD]]
- A US claims-based model estimated 8.9 million adults with medication-treated major depressive disorder in a 12-month period, of whom 2.8 million (30.9%) had TRD. [[Zhdanava2021]]
- In that model TRD accounted for US$43.8 billion (47.2%) of the US$92.7 billion annual burden of medication-treated major depressive disorder. [[Zhdanava2021]]
- A German claims study (2.47 million insured people, 2019) used a stricter proxy (at least three consecutive antidepressant prescriptions with two or more changes of strategy) and found TRD in 6.46% of all moderate-to-severe depression cases and 17.49% of those on continuous pharmacological treatment. [[Bayas2026]]
- In the German data only 27.20% of patients received combination or augmentation therapy after first-line treatment had not worked. [[Bayas2026]]
- In STAR*D, more than 40% of participants had not reached remission after two optimally delivered antidepressant trials. [[Nierenberg2006]]

### trd · Pseudo-resistance checks
- A significant percentage of people labelled as having TRD are "pseudo-resistant", for example because treatment trials were inadequate or the medicine was not taken as prescribed. [[McIntyre2023TRD]]
- CANMAT's first steps for a poor response are to re-evaluate the diagnosis (for example a missed bipolar disorder), comorbidities and adherence, and to consider laboratory tests for medical contributors. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- CANMAT's list of factors behind poor response includes incorrect diagnosis, psychiatric comorbidity (anxiety disorders, personality disorders, ADHD, substance use disorders), medical comorbidity (anaemia, obesity, sleep apnoea, thyroid disease), acute or chronic stressors, inadequate dose, inadequate duration, side effects masking as symptoms, poor adherence and pharmacogenetic variability. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- If there is no early improvement (a 20% or greater fall in a symptom score) by 4 weeks on an antidepressant, the likelihood of response or remission at 8 to 12 weeks is low. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Higher than minimal therapeutic doses are more effective up to a plateau around the equivalent of 50 mg of fluoxetine, but are less well tolerated. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- A diagnostic re-evaluation, including medical and psychiatric comorbidity, is described as essential in the assessment of TRD. [[Fava2003TRD]]

### trd · STAR*D: what happened at each step
- STAR*D enrolled outpatients with non-psychotic major depressive disorder who received one (N=3,671) to four (N=123) successive treatment steps. [[Rush2006STARD]]
- Step 1 (citalopram, 2,876 analysed, mean exit dose 41.8 mg/day): remission was 28% on the HAM-D and 33% on the QIDS-SR; response was 47%. [[Trivedi2006STARD]]
- Many who responded or remitted at step 1 did so at or after 8 weeks of treatment. [[Trivedi2006STARD]]
- QIDS-SR remission rates were 36.8%, 30.6%, 13.7% and 13.0% for steps 1 to 4, and the reported overall cumulative remission rate was 67%. [[Rush2006STARD]]
- People who needed more steps had higher relapse rates during the 12-month naturalistic follow-up. [[Rush2006STARD]]
- Step 2 switch (727 randomised after citalopram): HRSD-17 remission was 21.3% with bupropion-SR, 17.6% with sertraline and 24.8% with venlafaxine-XR, with no significant differences in outcomes or tolerability. [[Rush2006NEJM]]
- In the step 2 switch about one in four participants reached remission (QIDS-SR 25.5%, 26.6% and 25.0%). [[Rush2006NEJM]]
- Step 2 augmentation (565 randomised, citalopram continued): HRSD-17 remission was 29.7% with added bupropion-SR and 30.1% with added buspirone. [[Trivedi2006NEJM]]
- Added bupropion-SR gave a larger fall in QIDS-SR score than buspirone (25.3% vs 17.1%) and fewer dropouts for intolerance (12.5% vs 20.6%). [[Trivedi2006NEJM]]
- Step 3 switch (N=235): HAM-D remission was 12.3% with mirtazapine and 19.8% with nortriptyline, not significantly different. [[Fava2006STARD]]
- Step 3 augmentation (N=142): remission was 15.9% with lithium and 24.7% with triiodothyronine (T3), not significantly different; 23.2% left lithium because of side effects versus 9.6% on T3. [[Nierenberg2006]]
- Step 4 (N=109): remission was 6.9% with tranylcypromine and 13.7% with venlafaxine-XR plus mirtazapine, not significantly different; tranylcypromine had more attrition from intolerance. [[McGrath2006]]
- A review of STAR*D concluded that there was no difference in effectiveness between any treatments at any level. [[Sinyor2010]]
- A 2023 reanalysis of STAR*D patient-level data, using the protocol-specified blinded HRSD and the protocol inclusion rules, put cumulative remission after up to four steps at 35.0% rather than 67%. [[Pigott2023]]

### trd · Switch versus augment: later trials
- VAST-D (1,522 US veterans, 85.2% men, unresponsive to at least one adequate antidepressant course): 12-week remission was 22.3% after switching to bupropion, 26.9% with added bupropion and 28.9% with added aripiprazole. [[Mohamed2017]]
- In VAST-D aripiprazole augmentation beat switching for remission (relative risk 1.30, 95% CI 1.05–1.60); the other remission comparisons were not significant. [[Mohamed2017]]
- VAST-D response was 74.3% with added aripiprazole, 65.6% with added bupropion and 62.4% with a switch; somnolence, akathisia and weight gain were more frequent with aripiprazole and anxiety with bupropion. [[Mohamed2017]]
- OPTIMUM step 1 (619 adults aged 60 or older with TRD, about 10 weeks): remission occurred in 28.9% with aripiprazole augmentation, 28.2% with bupropion augmentation and 19.3% after a switch to bupropion. [[Lenze2023]]
- In OPTIMUM, well-being improved by 4.83, 4.33 and 2.04 points respectively; aripiprazole augmentation was better than a switch to bupropion (difference 2.79 points, 95% CI 0.56–5.02), and falls were most frequent with bupropion augmentation. [[Lenze2023]]
- OPTIMUM step 2 (N=248): remission occurred in 18.9% with lithium augmentation and 21.5% after a switch to nortriptyline, with similar well-being change and fall rates. [[Lenze2023]]
- CANMAT 2023 states that switching to another antidepressant has not been shown to be superior to continuing the same one, and that adjunctive strategies should be considered earlier, after a limited response to the first or second antidepressant. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- CANMAT suggests switching first when there is no response or troublesome side effects, and adding an adjunct first when there is a partial response and the first medicine is tolerated. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- CANMAT places tricyclics second line and irreversible monoamine oxidase inhibitors third line, noting that both keep a role in TRD and DTD. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

### trd · Atypical antipsychotic augmentation
- A meta-analysis of 16 placebo-controlled trials (3,480 patients) found adjunctive atypical antipsychotics more effective than placebo for response (odds ratio 1.69, 95% CI 1.46–1.95) and remission (odds ratio 2.00, 95% CI 1.69–2.37). [[Nelson2009]]
- In the same meta-analysis discontinuation for adverse events was higher with the antipsychotic than placebo (odds ratio 3.91, 95% CI 2.68–5.72). [[Nelson2009]]
- A network meta-analysis of 18 trials (n=4,422) found all standard-dose atypical antipsychotics better than placebo (standardised mean differences −0.27 to −0.43) with no significant differences between drugs; low doses were not better than placebo. [[Zhou2015]]
- A 2022 network meta-analysis (33 trials, 10,602 participants) found response odds ratios from 1.34 (olanzapine, cariprazine) to 2.17 (risperidone) versus placebo, with ziprasidone not effective, and described the class as "high effectiveness and low acceptability". [[Yan2022]]
- A 2026 bias-adjusted meta-analysis (56 trials, n=13,616) found second-generation antipsychotic augmentation raised response versus placebo (odds ratio 1.53, 95% CI 1.42–1.65), an effect confirmed by trial sequential analysis for aripiprazole, quetiapine, brexpiprazole and cariprazine. [[Hsu2026]]
- Aripiprazole pivotal trial (362 randomised, 6 weeks): MADRS fell by 8.8 points with adjunctive aripiprazole versus 5.8 with placebo; akathisia occurred in 23.1% versus 4.5%. [[Berman2007]]
- US aripiprazole label, adjunctive MDD in adults: initial 2 to 5 mg/day, recommended 5 to 10 mg/day, maximum 15 mg/day. [[FDA-Abilify]]
- Aripiprazole label, pooled adjunctive MDD trials: akathisia 25% versus 4% with placebo, restlessness 12% versus 2%, and weight gain of 7% or more of body weight in 5.2% versus 0.6%. [[FDA-Abilify]]
- Brexpiprazole 2 mg trial (6 weeks): MADRS fell 8.36 versus 5.15 points with placebo; weight gain was reported in 8.0% versus 3.1% and akathisia in 7.4% versus 1.0%. [[Thase2015Brex2mg]]
- Brexpiprazole 1 and 3 mg trial: 3 mg beat placebo on the MADRS (−8.29 vs −6.33) and 1 mg did not (−7.64 vs −6.33); akathisia was 4.4%, 13.5% and 2.3% with 1 mg, 3 mg and placebo. [[Thase2015Brex1and3mg]]
- US brexpiprazole label, adjunctive MDD: start 0.5 or 1 mg/day, target 2 mg/day, maximum 3 mg/day; akathisia was 4%, 7% and 14% at 1, 2 and 3 mg versus 2% with placebo, and mean weight change at last visit was +1.3 to +1.6 kg versus +0.3 kg. [[FDA-Rexulti]]
- Quetiapine XR trial (493 randomised, 6 weeks): MADRS fell 15.26 (150 mg) and 14.94 (300 mg) versus 12.21 with placebo; remission was 36.1%, 31.1% and 23.8%. [[Bauer2009Quetiapine]]
- US quetiapine XR label, adjunctive MDD: start 50 mg/day, recommended 150–300 mg/day; somnolence 37% (150 mg) and 43% (300 mg); weight gain of 7% or more in 3%, 7% and 2% (150 mg, 300 mg, placebo). [[FDA-SeroquelXR]]
- Cariprazine phase 3 trial (751 in the modified intention-to-treat set, 6 weeks): MADRS fell 14.1 with 1.5 mg/day versus 11.5 with placebo; 3.0 mg/day (−13.1) was not significantly better; response was 44.0% versus 34.9%, and remission did not differ. [[Sachs2023]]
- US cariprazine label, adjunctive MDD: start 1.5 mg daily, recommended 1.5 or 3 mg daily, maximum 3 mg; akathisia in fixed-dose trials was 7% (1.5 mg) and 10% (3 mg) versus 2% with placebo. [[FDA-Vraylar]]
- Olanzapine-fluoxetine combination (605 randomised after a prospective fluoxetine failure, 8 weeks): pooled MADRS change was −12.7 versus −9.0 with fluoxetine and −8.8 with olanzapine; pooled remission was 27%, 17% and 15%. [[Thase2007OFC]]
- In those trials mean weight change was +4.9 kg with the combination versus +0.4 kg with fluoxetine, with cholesterol +15.1 mg/dL and non-fasting glucose +11.4 mg/dL. [[Thase2007OFC]]
- The olanzapine-fluoxetine label advises starting TRD treatment with the 6 mg/25 mg capsule once daily in the evening and monitoring glucose, lipids and weight. [[FDA-OlanzapineFluoxetine]]
- Among second-generation antipsychotics, only the olanzapine-fluoxetine combination has been studied in FDA-defined TRD; the others were tested in partial responders to one or more antidepressants. [[McIntyre2023TRD]]
- These antipsychotic labels carry a boxed warning about increased mortality in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis, alongside the antidepressant-class boxed warning about suicidal thoughts and behaviours in young people. [[FDA-Abilify,FDA-OlanzapineFluoxetine]]
- CANMAT 2023 lists aripiprazole and brexpiprazole as first-line adjuncts, cariprazine as second line (fewer studies), and olanzapine, quetiapine XR and risperidone as second line because of side effects. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- CANMAT notes little evidence on maintenance treatment with adjuncts, so it is unclear how long they should be continued. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

### trd · Lithium, thyroid hormone, combinations and psychotherapy
- Lithium augmentation meta-analysis (9 placebo-controlled trials, 237 patients): odds ratio for response 2.89 (95% CI 1.65–5.05), with effects seen with tricyclics and second-generation antidepressants. [[Nelson2014]]
- An earlier meta-analysis of 10 trials gave an odds ratio of 3.11, a number needed to treat of 5 and mean response of 41.2% with lithium versus 14.4% with placebo; most trials were small and added lithium to tricyclics. [[Bauer2014Lithium]]
- Sparse continuation evidence suggests that, where lithium augmentation works, the combination should be maintained for at least 1 year to prevent early relapse. [[Bauer2014Lithium]]
- A 2026 reappraisal found the lithium effect (17 trials, odds ratio 2.06, 95% CI 1.30–3.27) was not supported by trial sequential analysis and lost significance after publication-bias adjustment. [[Hsu2026]]
- T3 augmentation of tricyclics (8 studies, 292 patients): relative response 2.09 (95% CI 1.31–3.32), a 23.2% absolute improvement, with the authors asking for more placebo-controlled data. [[Aronson1996]]
- In the 2026 reappraisal T3 augmentation was not significantly better than control (6 trials, odds ratio 1.19, 95% CI 0.53–2.70). [[Hsu2026]]
- A network meta-analysis of 65 augmentation trials (N=12,415) found response rates better than placebo for liothyronine, nortriptyline, aripiprazole, brexpiprazole, quetiapine, lithium, modafinil, olanzapine-fluoxetine, cariprazine and lisdexamfetamine. [[Nunez2022]]
- A meta-analysis restricted to people with insufficient response to at least two antidepressants found only 28 augmentation trials and called the evidence "sparse". [[Strawbridge2019]]
- Combining two antidepressants (39 trials, 6,751 patients) was better than monotherapy (standardised mean difference 0.31, 95% CI 0.19–0.44), most clearly for a reuptake inhibitor plus a presynaptic α2-autoreceptor antagonist such as mirtazapine (0.37); bupropion combinations were not superior (0.10). [[Henssler2022]]
- The MIR trial (480 UK primary care patients still depressed after at least 6 weeks of an SSRI or SNRI) found no clinically important benefit of added mirtazapine at 12 weeks: Beck Depression Inventory II 18.0 versus 19.7 with placebo, adjusted difference −1.83 (95% CI −3.92 to 0.27). [[Kessler2018]]
- CoBalT (469 UK primary care patients not responding to at least 6 weeks of antidepressants): at 6 months 46% responded with CBT added to usual care versus 22% with usual care (odds ratio 3.26, 95% CI 2.10–5.06). [[Wiles2013]]
- CoBalT long-term follow-up (median 45.5 months): mean Beck score 19.2 with added CBT versus 23.4 with usual care (difference −4.7, 95% CI −6.4 to −3.0). [[Wiles2016]]
- Across 22 comparisons, psychotherapy added to treatment as usual in TRD had a moderate effect (effect size 0.42, 95% CI 0.29–0.54). [[vanBronswijk2019]]
- Manual-based psychotherapies are not established as effective on their own in TRD but give significant relief when added to antidepressants. [[McIntyre2023TRD]]
- Pointer to pass H: esketamine nasal spray with a newly started antidepressant improved the MADRS at day 28 by 4.0 points more than antidepressant plus placebo spray (95% CI −7.31 to −0.64; 227 randomised). [[Popova2019TRANSFORM2]]
- The esketamine label requires administration under direct supervision of a healthcare provider, monitoring for at least 2 hours after each dose, and supply only through a restricted programme (REMS). [[FDA-Spravato]]

### neuromodulation · ECT: who it is for and how well it works
- ECT delivers an electrical stimulus through scalp electrodes to induce a brief generalised seizure, under general anaesthesia and after a muscle relaxant. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- CANMAT 2023: ECT can be a first-line treatment in severe illness (a major depressive episode with psychotic or catatonic features, severe suicidal ideation or deteriorating physical condition) or after a prior good response to ECT. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- CANMAT 2016 ranked ECT second line for treatment-resistant depression, first line in some situations, and rTMS first line after at least one antidepressant has not worked. [[Milev2016]]
- Real ECT was more effective than simulated ECT (6 trials, 256 patients, standardised effect size −0.91, 95% CI −1.27 to −0.54). [[UKECT2003]]
- ECT was more effective than pharmacotherapy (18 trials, 1,144 participants, effect size −0.80, 95% CI −1.29 to −0.29). [[UKECT2003]]
- Bilateral ECT was moderately more effective than unilateral ECT (22 trials, 1,408 participants, effect size −0.32, 95% CI −0.46 to −0.19), and high dose more effective than low dose. [[UKECT2003]]
- CORE cohort (253 patients, bilateral ECT three times weekly): remission occurred in 75% and sustained response in 79%. [[Husain2004]]
- In CORE, 54% had a first response by the third ECT (end of week 1), 34% had remitted by the sixth (week 2) and 65% by the tenth (weeks 3–4). [[Husain2004]]
- Among CORE completers remission was 87% overall: 95% with psychotic depression and 83% with non-psychotic depression. [[Petrides2001]]
- PRIDE phase 1 (240 adults aged 60 or older, right unilateral ultrabrief ECT plus venlafaxine): 61.7% remitted, 70% responded, and the mean number of treatments to remission was 7.3. [[Kellner2016PRIDE1]]
- CANMAT cites ECT response rates of 65% to 75% and an acute course of usually 6 to 12 sessions, with no difference in response between twice- and thrice-weekly schedules. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Medication failure lowers but does not remove the chance of benefit: ECT response was 58% with and 70% without medication failure in the current episode (11 studies, 1,175 patients). [[Haq2015]]
- Remission after ECT was 48.0% with and 64.9% without previous pharmacotherapy failure (7 observational studies). [[Heijnen2010]]
- Shorter episode duration predicted better ECT response; roughly one-third of people with depression do not respond to ECT. [[Haq2015]]
- Among 131 CORE patients with high baseline scores on the suicide item, the score fell to 0 in 38.2% after 1 week, 61.1% after 2 weeks and 80.9% by the end of the course. [[Kellner2005]]
- In Ontario, ECT during a psychiatric admission was associated with a lower risk of death by suicide in the year after discharge (cause-specific hazard ratio 0.53, 95% CI 0.31–0.92; observational, propensity-weighted). [[Kaster2022]]
- A 2025 meta-analysis (17 studies) linked ECT with lower all-cause mortality (hazard ratio 0.70, 95% CI 0.61–0.81); the association with suicide deaths was seen at 3 months but not at 1, 6 or 12 months. [[Rhee2025]]

### neuromodulation · ECT technique: placement, dose and pulse width
- Right unilateral ECT at 500% above seizure threshold matched bilateral ECT for response (65% each) and was about twice as effective as low-dose (35%) or moderate-dose (30%) unilateral ECT. [[Sackeim2000]]
- Bilateral ECT caused more anterograde and retrograde memory impairment in the week after treatment, and greater retrograde amnesia at 2 months. [[Sackeim2000]]
- With an ultrabrief pulse (0.3 ms), remission was 73% for right unilateral ECT at 6 times threshold but 35% for bilateral ECT; with a 1.5 ms brief pulse it was 59% and 65%. [[Sackeim2008]]
- Ultrabrief right unilateral ECT produced less severe cognitive side effects than the other three conditions. [[Sackeim2008]]
- In a three-arm trial (N=230) remission was 55% with right unilateral, 61% with bifrontal and 64% with bitemporal placement; bitemporal ECT worked fastest. [[Kellner2010]]
- Seven trials (n=792): high-dose right unilateral ECT did not differ from moderate-dose bitemporal ECT in remission (relative risk 1.06, 95% CI 0.93–1.20) and gave faster reorientation (8.28 minutes sooner) and less retrograde autobiographical memory loss. [[Kolshus2017]]
- EFFECT-Dep (N=138, twice weekly): high-dose unilateral ECT was non-inferior to bitemporal ECT; median recovery of orientation was 19.1 versus 26.4 minutes. [[Semkovska2016]]
- Brief-pulse right unilateral ECT was slightly more efficacious than ultrabrief (standardised mean difference 0.25) and needed fewer sessions (8.7 vs 9.6) but caused more cognitive side effects in every domain examined. [[Tor2015]]
- CANMAT summarises: ultrabrief (about 0.3 ms) right unilateral ECT is slightly less effective than brief pulse (about 1.0 ms) but has fewer cognitive adverse effects, while bitemporal brief-pulse ECT may be more effective at a higher cognitive cost. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- CANMAT notes that benzodiazepines and anticonvulsants may interfere with ECT efficacy and that lithium may worsen cognitive side effects during a course. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

### neuromodulation · ECT safety and cognitive effects
- Pooled ECT-related mortality was 2.1 per 100,000 treatments (95% CI 1.2–3.4; 16 deaths in 766,180 treatments from 32 countries). [[Torring2017]]
- In the nine studies published after 2001 (414,747 treatments) there was one ECT-related death. [[Torring2017]]
- The same review cites general-anaesthesia mortality for surgical procedures as 3.4 per 100,000. [[Torring2017]]
- Major adverse cardiac events and death after ECT "occur in about 1 of 50 patients and after about 1 of 200 to 500" treatments; all-cause mortality was 0.06 per 1,000 treatments (82 studies, 786,995 treatments). [[Duma2019]]
- In a matched Ontario cohort (5,008 admissions per group), serious medical events within 30 days were 0.25 per person-year with ECT and 0.33 without (hazard ratio 0.78, 95% CI 0.61–1.00). [[Kaster2021]]
- Objective cognitive tests (84 studies, 2,981 patients): performance fell on 72% of variables 0 to 3 days after ECT. [[Semkovska2010]]
- From 4 to 15 days after ECT nearly all deficits had resolved, and after 15 days no test showed a decline; 57% of variables were better than before treatment. [[Semkovska2010]]
- That meta-analysis found no standardised tests of retrograde amnesia, so it could not address autobiographical memory. [[Semkovska2010]]
- In a community cohort (347 patients, seven facilities), bilateral placement produced more severe and persisting retrograde amnesia than right unilateral ECT, and sine-wave stimulation slowed reaction time at 6 months. [[Sackeim2007]]
- A later meta-analysis (91 studies, 3,762 individuals) found decreases in autobiographical memory, verbal fluency and verbal memory at 1–28 days and improvement in almost all domains after 1 month. [[Landry2021]]
- CANMAT notes that some patients report distressing gaps in autobiographical memory long after ECT and that the gap between test performance and subjective memory problems "remains controversial". [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

### neuromodulation · After ECT: relapse and continuation
- Without active continuation treatment, 84% relapsed within 24 weeks on placebo, 60% on nortriptyline and 39% on nortriptyline plus lithium (84 remitters randomised). [[Sackeim2001]]
- With modern continuation pharmacotherapy, 37.7% relapsed within 6 months and 51.1% within 12 months of successful ECT. [[Jelovac2013]]
- Antidepressant continuation halved the 6-month relapse risk versus placebo (risk ratio 0.49, 95% CI 0.39–0.62; number needed to treat 3.3). [[Jelovac2013]]
- CORE continuation trial (201 remitters, 6 months): relapse was 37.1% with continuation ECT and 31.6% with lithium plus nortriptyline; 46.1% and 46.3% remained in remission. [[Kellner2006]]
- PRIDE phase 2 (120 older remitters): four continuation ECT sessions plus further ECT as needed, added to venlafaxine and lithium, gave HAM-D scores 4.2 points lower at 24 weeks than medication alone (95% CI 1.6–6.9). [[Kellner2016PRIDE2]]
- Four trials (n=254): continuation ECT plus pharmacotherapy lowered 6-month relapse versus pharmacotherapy alone (relative risk 0.57, 95% CI 0.37–0.88; number needed to treat 7). [[Jelovac2025]]
- Lithium after ECT was associated with less relapse (odds ratio 0.53, 95% CI 0.34–0.82; number needed to treat 7; evidence quality rated very low). [[Lambrichts2021]]

### neuromodulation · ECT compared with ketamine
- ELEKT-D (403 randomised outpatients with non-psychotic TRD, open label, 3 weeks): response was 55.4% with intravenous ketamine and 41.2% with ECT (difference 14.2 points, 95% CI 3.9–24.2), meeting non-inferiority for ketamine. [[Anand2023]]
- In ELEKT-D, ECT was associated with a fall in memory recall after 3 weeks that recovered gradually, and with musculoskeletal adverse effects; ketamine was associated with dissociation. [[Anand2023]]
- KetECT (186 Swedish inpatients, open label): remission was 63% with ECT and 46% with ketamine (P=0.026); 63% and 70% of remitters relapsed within 12 months. [[Ekstrand2022]]
- A meta-analysis of six trials (340 patients) favoured ECT over ketamine for depression severity (standardised mean difference −0.69, 95% CI −0.89 to −0.48). [[Rhee2022]]

### neuromodulation · rTMS and theta-burst stimulation
- rTMS stimulates cortical neurons with focused magnetic pulses from a coil on the scalp; it needs no anaesthesia, and a conventional course is 20 to 40 minutes a session, 5 days a week for 4 to 6 weeks. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- The first TMS device for depression (NeuroStar, De Novo DEN070003) has an FDA decision date of 7 October 2008. [[FDA-NeuroStarDEN070003]]
- Pivotal trial (301 medication-free patients, 10 Hz left prefrontal TMS, 3,000 pulses a session): active TMS beat sham on the HAM-D at weeks 4 and 6, remission was about twofold higher at week 6, and 4.5% dropped out for adverse events. [[OReardon2007]]
- OPT-TMS (190 analysed, antidepressant-free): remission was 14.1% with active rTMS and 5.1% with sham (odds ratio 4.2, 95% CI 1.32–13.24; number needed to treat 12). [[George2010]]
- THREE-D (414 randomised): a 3-minute intermittent theta-burst session was non-inferior to a 37.5-minute 10 Hz session; HRSD-17 fell from 23.5 to 13.4 and from 23.6 to 13.4. [[Blumberger2018]]
- In THREE-D headache was the most common adverse event (64% and 65%) and dropout was 6% and 8%. [[Blumberger2018]]
- A theta-burst TMS system received FDA clearance on 14 August 2018. [[FDA-MagVitaK173620]]
- Network meta-analysis of 81 rTMS trials (4,233 patients), response versus sham: bilateral odds ratio 3.96, high-frequency left 3.07, low-frequency right 2.37, theta-burst 2.54. [[Brunoni2017NMA]]
- Deep TMS with an H-coil (212 patients): week-5 response was 38.4% versus 21.4% with sham and remission 32.6% versus 14.6%. [[Levkovitz2015]]
- A deep TMS system received FDA clearance on 7 January 2013. [[FDA-BrainswayK122288]]
- In routine US practice (307 patients, 42 sites) clinician-rated response was 58.0% and remission 37.1%; patient-rated remission was 26.5–28.7%. [[Carpenter2012]]
- In a registry of 5,010 patients, response ranged from 58% to 83% and remission from 28% to 62% depending on scale and sample (open label, voluntary site participation). [[Sackeim2020TMS]]
- CANMAT 2023 cites response rates of 40% to 50% for rTMS in difficult-to-treat depression and recommends high-frequency left, low-frequency right and theta-burst protocols as first line. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- CANMAT advises that, outside emergencies, rTMS should generally be considered before ECT because it is less invasive and people can keep working or studying. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

### neuromodulation · Accelerated protocols (SAINT/SNT)
- Open-label SAINT (22 participants): 50 theta-burst sessions of 1,800 pulses, 10 a day for 5 days with 50-minute gaps, targeted by functional MRI; 19 of 21 completers (90.5%) met remission criteria. (Early evidence) [[Cole2020]]
- Sham-controlled SNT trial (29 treated: 14 active, 15 sham): mean MADRS reduction 4 weeks after treatment was 52.5% with active versus 11.1% with sham stimulation. (Early evidence) [[Cole2022SNT]]
- Replication trial (48 randomised): 1-month remission 50.0% with active versus 20.8% with sham SNT, and response 54.2% versus 25.0%. (Early evidence) [[Kratter2026]]
- A system using SAINT technology received FDA clearance on 1 September 2022. [[FDA-MagnusK220177]]

### neuromodulation · rTMS safety and durability
- Survey of 586,656 sessions in 25,526 patients: 18 seizures, a rate of 0.31 per 10,000 sessions (95% CI 0.18–0.48), about 1 in 32,600 sessions, and 0.71 per 1,000 patients. [[Taylor2021]]
- In that survey the H-coil seizure rate was 5.56 per 1,000 patients versus 0.14 per 1,000 for the three most used figure-of-8 coil devices combined. [[Taylor2021]]
- TMS given within published guidelines to people without risk factors caused fewer than 1 seizure per 60,000 sessions; brain lesions and epilepsy raised the risk substantially. [[Lerner2019]]
- International safety guidelines call seizure "theoretically the most serious risk of TMS" and judge the risk low with traditional parameters and focal coils. [[Rossi2021]]
- CANMAT states that rTMS has no cognitive side effects. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Among people who responded to a course of rTMS, 66.5% were still responders at 3 months, 52.9% at 6 months and 46.3% at 12 months; maintenance treatment predicted better durability. [[Senova2019]]
- In a 12-month follow-up of 257 patients, 62.5% of initial responders or remitters kept response throughout, and 36.2% received further TMS. [[Dunner2014]]

### neuromodulation · Vagus nerve stimulation
- VNS uses an electrode around the left vagus nerve in the neck connected to a pulse generator under the skin of the chest; it was approved for TRD in the United States in 2005. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Acute sham-controlled trial (222 evaluable, 10 weeks): HRSD-24 response was 15.2% with active and 10.0% with sham VNS (P=0.251), not definitive evidence of short-term efficacy. [[Rush2005VNS]]
- 5-year registry (795 patients, at least four failed treatments, non-randomised): cumulative response was 67.6% with adjunctive VNS versus 40.9% with treatment as usual, and first-time remission 43.3% versus 25.7%. [[Aaronson2017]]
- RECOVER (493 adults with marked TRD, 12 months, sham-controlled): the primary outcome, percent time in MADRS response, did not separate active from sham VNS; several secondary scales favoured active VNS. (Early evidence) [[Conway2025RECOVER]]
- RECOVER is registered as NCT03887715 (sponsor LivaNova; status recruiting, estimated enrolment 6,800 across cohorts). [[NCT03887715]]
- Common VNS side effects are implant-site pain, hoarseness or voice change, cough, headache, sore throat and neck pain; CANMAT ranks VNS third line. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

### neuromodulation · Deep brain stimulation, tDCS and magnetic seizure therapy
- First report of subcallosal cingulate deep brain stimulation: sustained remission in four of six patients. (Early evidence) [[Mayberg2005]]
- BROADEN (90 randomised, 6 months double-blind): response was 20% with active and 17% with sham subcallosal cingulate stimulation, with no significant difference; the trial stopped after a futility analysis. (Early evidence) [[Holtzheimer2017]]
- Ventral capsule/ventral striatum stimulation (30 patients, 16 weeks): response 20% active versus 14.3% sham. (Early evidence) [[Dougherty2015]]
- Ventral anterior limb of the internal capsule (25 patients): 40% responded after open-label optimisation, and in the blinded crossover HAM-D-17 was 13.6 during active versus 23.1 during sham stimulation. (Early evidence) [[Bergfeld2016]]
- A meta-analysis of nine sham-controlled studies (190 participants) found higher response with active stimulation but concluded that deep brain stimulation "remains an experimental treatment". (Early evidence) [[Kisely2018]]
- CANMAT 2023 classes deep brain stimulation and magnetic seizure therapy as investigational. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- ELECT-TDCS (245 randomised, 10 weeks): HDRS-17 fell 11.3 points with escitalopram, 9.0 with tDCS and 5.8 with placebo; tDCS beat placebo but did not show non-inferiority to escitalopram. [[Brunoni2017ELECT]]
- DepressionDC (150 analysed): tDCS added to an SSRI was no better than sham at week 6 (MADRS −8.2 vs −8.0). [[Burkhardt2023]]
- Home-based tDCS with remote supervision (174 randomised, 10 weeks): HAM-D improved 9.41 points with active versus 7.14 with sham stimulation (P=0.012). (Early evidence) [[Woodham2025]]
- CANMAT 2023 downgraded tDCS to third line after negative large trials and does not support it for severe or difficult-to-treat depression. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Magnetic seizure therapy versus ultrabrief right unilateral ECT (73 randomised): response 51.4% versus 42.1% and remission 37.1% versus 26.3%, with no significant difference; MST needed 9.0 versus 6.7 treatments to remission and had faster reorientation. (Early evidence) [[Deng2024]]
- Network meta-analysis of 113 trials (6,750 patients), response versus sham: bitemporal ECT odds ratio 8.91, high-dose right unilateral ECT 7.27, magnetic seizure therapy 5.55, bilateral rTMS 4.92, intermittent theta-burst 3.20, high-frequency left rTMS 3.17, tDCS 2.65. [[Mutz2019]]

### Exam hooks
- The most used definition of TRD is an inadequate response to at least two antidepressants of adequate dose and duration, with adherence confirmed. [[McIntyre2023TRD]]
- Before calling depression treatment-resistant, check diagnosis (bipolar disorder), dose, duration, adherence and comorbidity: "pseudo-resistance". [[Lam2024CANMAT,McIntyre2023TRD]]
- STAR*D QIDS-SR remission by step: 36.8%, 30.6%, 13.7%, 13.0%. [[Rush2006STARD]]
- STAR*D step 2: no switch drug (bupropion-SR, sertraline, venlafaxine-XR) beat another. [[Rush2006NEJM]]
- VAST-D: aripiprazole augmentation 28.9% remission versus 22.3% for a switch to bupropion. [[Mohamed2017]]
- Akathisia is the signature adverse effect of adjunctive aripiprazole (25% vs 4% with placebo in the label). [[FDA-Abilify]]
- The olanzapine-fluoxetine combination is the only second-generation antipsychotic regimen studied in FDA-defined TRD; weight gain averaged +4.9 kg in 8 weeks. [[McIntyre2023TRD,Thase2007OFC]]
- Lithium augmentation: pooled odds ratio for response 2.89 from nine small trials. [[Nelson2014]]
- The Maudsley Staging Method scores treatment, severity and duration, total 3 to 15. [[Fekadu2009]]
- Real versus sham ECT: effect size −0.91; ECT versus drugs: −0.80. [[UKECT2003]]
- ECT remission is higher in psychotic depression (95% vs 83% among completers). [[Petrides2001]]
- High-dose right unilateral ECT matches bilateral ECT for efficacy with fewer cognitive effects. [[Sackeim2000,Kolshus2017]]
- Ultrabrief pulse width is about 0.3 ms; it must be paired with right unilateral placement at high dose to keep efficacy. [[Sackeim2008]]
- ECT-related mortality: 2.1 per 100,000 treatments. [[Torring2017]]
- After ECT without continuation treatment 84% relapse within 6 months; nortriptyline plus lithium cut this to 39%. [[Sackeim2001]]
- Objective cognitive deficits after ECT are mainly limited to the first 3 days; no deficits on testing after 15 days. [[Semkovska2010]]
- Theta-burst (3 minutes) is non-inferior to 10 Hz rTMS (37.5 minutes). [[Blumberger2018]]
- rTMS seizure risk is about 0.31 per 10,000 sessions. [[Taylor2021]]
- Subcallosal cingulate deep brain stimulation: positive open-label series, negative sham-controlled BROADEN trial. [[Mayberg2005,Holtzheimer2017]]
- ELEKT-D: ketamine non-inferior to ECT in non-psychotic TRD outpatients (55.4% vs 41.2% response). [[Anand2023]]

### In plain words
- "Treatment-resistant" does not mean nothing will work. It usually means two antidepressants, taken properly for long enough, have not helped enough. [[McIntyre2023TRD]]
- Before changing course, clinicians check the basics: is the diagnosis right, was the dose high enough and taken for long enough, and is something else such as a thyroid problem or poor sleep getting in the way. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Many clinicians now prefer the term "difficult-to-treat depression", which people living with depression find more hopeful. [[Lam2024CANMAT,McAllisterWilliams2020]]
- When the first medicine does not work, the next step is usually a different medicine or adding a second one. In a large US study about one in four people recovered on a switch and about three in ten with an add-on. [[Rush2006NEJM,Trivedi2006NEJM]]
- Each further medicine step helps fewer people, which is why other kinds of treatment are brought in. [[Rush2006STARD]]
- Adding a low dose of certain antipsychotic medicines can help, but restlessness and weight gain are common and need watching. [[Nelson2009,FDA-Abilify]]
- Adding talking therapy helps too: in a UK study, almost half of people improved with CBT added, compared with about a fifth without. [[Wiles2013]]
- ECT is done under a short general anaesthetic with a muscle relaxant. A course is usually 6 to 12 sessions. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- ECT is among the most effective treatments for severe depression; in one large study three in four people recovered. [[Husain2004]]
- Memory and thinking can be affected around the time of ECT. On tests this mostly clears within about two weeks, though some people report longer-lasting gaps in memory of past events. [[Semkovska2010,Lam2024CANMAT]]
- ECT is very safe for a procedure under anaesthetic: about 2 deaths per 100,000 treatments across many countries. [[Torring2017]]
- Depression often comes back after ECT, so follow-on treatment with medicines, more ECT sessions or both is planned from the start. [[Jelovac2013,Sackeim2001]]
- rTMS uses magnetic pulses on the scalp while you are awake. There is no anaesthetic, and seizures are very rare. [[Lam2024CANMAT,Taylor2021]]
- Implanted devices such as vagus nerve stimulators are for a small number of people whose depression has not improved with many treatments, and brain implants remain research treatments. [[Lam2024CANMAT,Holtzheimer2017]]
- Study results are averages for groups. They cannot say what will happen for one person, and every change in treatment is planned with the care team. [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

## Open questions and controversies
- How should TRD be defined? Two failed antidepressants is the regulatory convention [[McIntyre2023TRD]]; a broader, function-focused "difficult-to-treat" framing is preferred by a consensus group and CANMAT [[McAllisterWilliams2020,Lam2024CANMAT]]; status: no consensus, and only 17% of trials enrol by the common criteria [[Gaynes2020]].
- How many people recover across sequential medicine steps? STAR*D reported 67% cumulative remission [[Rush2006STARD]]; a protocol-faithful reanalysis reports 35.0% [[Pigott2023]]; status: disputed, the two figures use different outcome measures and inclusion rules.
- Switch or augment first? Augmentation with aripiprazole beat switching in VAST-D and OPTIMUM by modest margins [[Mohamed2017,Lenze2023]], while STAR*D found no differences between options [[Sinyor2010]]; status: CANMAT favours earlier adjuncts, weighed against side effects [[Lam2024CANMAT]].
- Is lithium or T3 augmentation robustly supported? Older meta-analyses are positive [[Nelson2014,Aronson1996]]; a 2026 bias-adjusted reappraisal finds the evidence insufficient [[Hsu2026]]; status: uncertain, trials are small and mostly old.
- Does adding mirtazapine help? Pooled trials favour α2-antagonist combinations [[Henssler2022]]; the large pragmatic MIR trial was null [[Kessler2018]]; status: unresolved, with publication bias detected in the pooled data.
- Is ketamine as good as ECT? Non-inferior for response in non-psychotic outpatients in ELEKT-D [[Anand2023]]; inferior for remission in inpatients in KetECT and in a meta-analysis [[Ekstrand2022,Rhee2022]]; status: depends on population, setting and ECT technique.
- How long do memory effects of ECT last? Objective tests recover within about 15 days [[Semkovska2010]]; some patients report lasting autobiographical gaps and bilateral ECT shows persisting retrograde amnesia in a community cohort [[Sackeim2007,Lam2024CANMAT]]; status: controversial, standardised retrograde memory tests are lacking.
- How large is the benefit of accelerated theta-burst (SNT)? Open-label remission was 90.5% [[Cole2020]]; sham-controlled replication found 50.0% versus 20.8% [[Kratter2026]]; status: efficacy replicated in small trials, durability and comparison with standard rTMS unknown.
- Are newer TMS variants better than standard rTMS? A 2017 network meta-analysis found accelerated, synchronised and deep rTMS not better than sham [[Brunoni2017NMA]]; individual trials of deep TMS and SNT are positive [[Levkovitz2015,Cole2022SNT]]; status: evolving.
- Does VNS work? The acute sham-controlled trial and the RECOVER primary endpoint were negative [[Rush2005VNS,Conway2025RECOVER]]; a 5-year non-randomised registry favours VNS [[Aaronson2017]]; status: approved but efficacy evidence is mixed.
- Does deep brain stimulation work for depression? Open-label series and one crossover trial are positive [[Mayberg2005,Bergfeld2016]]; two sham-controlled trials are negative [[Holtzheimer2017,Dougherty2015]]; status: investigational (Early evidence).
- Is tDCS useful? Positive against sham in single-centre and home-based trials [[Brunoni2017ELECT,Woodham2025]]; negative as an add-on to SSRIs in a multicentre trial [[Burkhardt2023]]; status: downgraded to third line by CANMAT [[Lam2024CANMAT]].

## Provenance
- Method: each PMID was found with PubMed E-utilities esearch or a Europe PMC title search, and its authors, title, journal, year, volume, pages and identifiers were checked against the PubMed efetch record or the Europe PMC core record (the service named on each line is the one whose record the citation text was checked against; most were seen in both). Numbers were read from the abstract in that record. McIntyre2023TRD was additionally confirmed on Crossref by DOI.
- Full text read: Lam2024CANMAT only (Europe PMC full-text XML, questions 7 and 8). Its Tables 7.2 and 8.1–8.3 are images and were not readable, so line-of-treatment rankings are quoted only where the running text states them.
- Labels: openFDA drug label endpoint, 2026-11-13. FDA-Abilify and FDA-Vraylar reuse the key and text already in other companions' sources.json. The Rexulti label is in the Alzheimer's companion under the key FDARexulti2026 with dementia-specific wording; it is cited here as FDA-Rexulti (same set id) with the MDD sections read. Symbyax is not in openFDA under its brand name, so the generic olanzapine and fluoxetine label is cited as FDA-OlanzapineFluoxetine.
- Device dates: openFDA device 510(k) endpoint. The NeuroStar De Novo record lists its decision description as "Unknown"; only the decision date (2008-10-07) is used.
- Registry: NCT03887715 (RECOVER) and NCT03113968 (ELEKT-D) were confirmed on ClinicalTrials.gov API v2.
- Differences from the brief, after checking the records:
  - The 75% ECT remission figure comes from Husain2004 (CORE acute phase, N=253), not from Kellner 2006, which reports the continuation phase (relapse 37.1% vs 31.6%).
  - Cole 2022 (SNT): the abstract reports mean MADRS reduction (52.5% vs 11.1%) in 29 participants; the remission figures 78.6% vs 13.3% are not in the abstract and the full text was not accessible, so they are not cited. The 2026 replication trial (Kratter2026) gives remission 50.0% vs 20.8%.
  - RECOVER (brief: Conway 2024) is indexed as Brain Stimul 2025;18(3), online December 2024, so the key is Conway2025RECOVER. The home-based tDCS trial (brief: Woodham 2024) is indexed as Nat Med 2025;31(1), online October 2024, so the key is Woodham2025.
  - "About 1 in 30,000 sessions" for rTMS seizures is given as the source states it: 0.31 per 10,000 sessions (18 in 586,656; about 1 in 32,600).
