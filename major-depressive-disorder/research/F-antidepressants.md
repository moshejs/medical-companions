## Findings

### antidepressants · classes, targets and label mechanisms
- US labels describe antidepressant mechanisms cautiously: for fluoxetine "the exact mechanism ... is unknown" but is "presumed to be linked to its inhibition of CNS neuronal uptake of serotonin" [[FDA-Prozac]]
- SSRIs (fluoxetine, sertraline, citalopram, escitalopram, paroxetine, fluvoxamine) block the serotonin transporter (SERT); sertraline "potentiates serotonergic activity in the central nervous system through inhibition of neuronal reuptake of serotonin (5-HT)" [[FDA-Zoloft]]
- Fluvoxamine is an SSRI whose US label indication is obsessive compulsive disorder, not major depressive disorder; it is a potent inhibitor of CYP1A2 [[FDA-Fluvoxamine]]
- SNRIs block both serotonin and noradrenaline (norepinephrine) reuptake: venlafaxine and its active metabolite O-desmethylvenlafaxine (ODV) are "potent and selective inhibitors of neuronal serotonin and norepinephrine" reuptake in vitro [[FDA-EffexorXR]]
- Duloxetine's actions "are believed to be related to its potentiation of serotonergic and noradrenergic activity in the CNS" [[FDA-Duloxetine]]
- Levomilnacipran binds the human serotonin and noradrenaline transporters with Ki 11 nM and 91 nM, and inhibits 5-HT and NE reuptake with IC50 16–19 nM and 11 nM; it lacks significant affinity for serotonergic, adrenergic, muscarinic or histaminergic receptors [[FDA-Fetzima]]
- Desvenlafaxine (the ODV metabolite of venlafaxine, marketed as Pristiq) has a labelled dose of 50 mg once daily that "is both a starting dose and the therapeutic dose" [[FDA-Pristiq]]
- Bupropion (an NDRI) "is a relatively weak inhibitor of the neuronal uptake of norepinephrine and dopamine and does not inhibit monoamine oxidase or the reuptake of serotonin" [[FDA-WellbutrinXL]]
- A review of bupropion pharmacology concludes that it acts through dual noradrenaline and dopamine reuptake inhibition with no clinically significant serotonergic effect, which is the proposed reason it is not associated with sexual dysfunction, weight gain or sedation [[Stahl2004]]
- Mirtazapine is an antagonist at central presynaptic α2-adrenergic inhibitory autoreceptors and heteroreceptors, at 5-HT2 and 5-HT3 receptors, and at histamine H1 receptors; the label attributes its prominent somnolence to H1 blockade and orthostatic hypotension to peripheral α1 blockade [[FDA-Remeron]]
- Vortioxetine binds SERT (Ki 1.6 nM) and is a 5-HT3, 5-HT1D and 5-HT7 antagonist, a 5-HT1B partial agonist and a 5-HT1A agonist; the contribution of the receptor actions to its antidepressant effect "has not been established" [[FDA-Trintellix]]
- Vilazodone combines selective serotonin reuptake inhibition with partial agonism at 5-HT1A receptors; the label recommends 20–40 mg once daily taken with food [[FDA-Viibryd]]
- Trazodone inhibits serotonin reuptake (Ki 367 nM) and is a more potent antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors (Ki 35.6 nM); it also blocks 5-HT2B, 5-HT2C, α1A and α2C receptors [[FDA-Trazodone]]
- Agomelatine acts on melatonergic MT1 and MT2 receptors and on 5-HT2B/5-HT2C receptors [[Guaiana2013]]; no agomelatine label exists in openFDA (checked 2026-11-13), consistent with it not being marketed in the US (unverified beyond the openFDA search)
- Agomelatine versus placebo: SMD 0.24 (95% CI 0.12–0.35) in 20 trials (7,460 participants), with published trials more favourable than unpublished ones; versus other antidepressants SMD 0.00 (−0.09 to 0.10) [[Taylor2014Agomelatine]]
- Tricyclic antidepressants: amitriptyline "inhibits the membrane pump mechanism responsible for uptake of norepinephrine and serotonin" [[FDA-Amitriptyline]]; nortriptyline is described as not an MAO inhibitor and as inhibiting the activity of histamine, 5-hydroxytryptamine and acetylcholine [[FDA-Pamelor]]
- Among tricyclics, nortriptyline and desipramine are described as the most selective noradrenaline reuptake inhibitors, and clomipramine as the only tricyclic with good evidence of clinically relevant combined serotonin and noradrenaline reuptake inhibition [[Gillman2007]]
- Tricyclic antidepressants as a class have a narrow safety margin in overdose (label overdosage section) [[FDA-Amitriptyline]]
- MAOIs: selegiline irreversibly inhibits monoamine oxidase; it has greater affinity for MAO-B than MAO-A, but "at antidepressant doses, selegiline inhibits both isoenzymes" [[FDA-Emsam]]
- The selegiline transdermal system (Emsam) is labelled for adults with MDD at a starting and target dose of 6 mg per 24 hours, with increases of 3 mg per 24 hours at intervals of no less than 2 weeks to a maximum of 12 mg per 24 hours [[FDA-Emsam]]
- Tyramine reaction: MAOIs can cause hypertensive crisis after foods with a high concentration of tyramine; the phenelzine label lists aged cheeses, pickled herring, beer, wine, liver, yeast extract, dry sausage, broad bean pods and yogurt, and says sympathomimetic drugs must not be taken [[FDA-Nardil]]
- The tranylcypromine label carries a second boxed warning, "hypertensive crisis with significant tyramine use", beside the class warning on suicidal thoughts and behaviours [[FDA-Parnate]]
- For the selegiline patch, the label requires tyramine-rich foods and beverages to be avoided from the first day of the 9 mg and 12 mg per 24 hours doses and for 2 weeks after a reduction to 6 mg or after stopping those doses [[FDA-Emsam]]
- A review describes MAOIs as effective for depression with atypical features or depression that has not responded to other antidepressants, but underused because of concern about dietary and drug interactions [[Flockhart2012]]; CANMAT 2023 keeps MAOIs as a third-line option [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Dextromethorphan-bupropion (Auvelity): dextromethorphan is "an uncompetitive antagonist of the NMDA receptor ... and a sigma-1 receptor agonist"; bupropion raises dextromethorphan plasma levels by competitively inhibiting CYP2D6 [[FDA-Auvelity]]
- Labelled Auvelity dosing for MDD: one 45 mg/105 mg tablet once daily in the morning, increased on day 4 to the maximum of one tablet twice daily at least 8 hours apart [[FDA-Auvelity]]
- GEMINI (phase 3, n = 327, baseline MADRS about 33): MADRS change at week 6 was −15.9 with dextromethorphan-bupropion versus −12.0 with placebo (difference −3.87, 95% CI −6.36 to −1.39; P = .002); remission 39.5% vs 17.3% and response 54.0% vs 34.0% at week 6; separation from placebo was reported from week 1 [[Iosifescu2022]]
- In a randomised double-blind trial against bupropion alone, dextromethorphan-bupropion gave a larger MADRS fall at week 6 (−17.3 vs −12.1) and remission of 46.5% vs 16.2% [[Tabuteau2022]]
- Gepirone extended-release (Exxua) is thought to act "through selective agonist activity at 5HT1A receptors"; its mean terminal half-life is about 5 hours [[FDA-Exxua]]
- Exxua label: start 18.2 mg once daily with food, with titration to 36.3 mg on day 4, 54.5 mg after day 7 and 72.6 mg after a further week; an ECG is required before starting and during titration, and it is contraindicated with baseline QTc above 450 msec, congenital long QT syndrome, strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or severe hepatic impairment [[FDA-Exxua]]
- Exxua pivotal trials (8 weeks, HAMD-17): placebo-subtracted difference −2.47 (95% CI −4.41 to −0.53; n = 101 vs 103) in Study 1 and −2.45 (−4.47 to −0.43; n = 116 vs 122) in Study 2 [[FDA-Exxua]]
- Published gepirone-ER trials: HAM-D-17 remission at week 8 was 28.7% vs 14.9% with placebo [[Feiger2003]]; the commonest adverse events were dizziness (45% vs 10%), nausea (36% vs 13%) and headache (24% vs 16%) [[Bielski2008]]
- Reboxetine (a noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor, not in openFDA) was the least effective of 21 drugs against placebo in Cipriani 2018 [[Cipriani2018]]; a review including unpublished trials found it inferior to SSRIs for response (OR 0.80, 95% CI 0.67–0.95) and concluded that published data had overestimated its benefit versus placebo by up to 115% [[Eyding2010]]

### antidepressants · synapse pharmacology, occupancy and delayed onset
- PET study ([11C]DASB, 77 subjects, 4 weeks of citalopram, fluoxetine, sertraline, paroxetine or venlafaxine XR): mean striatal SERT occupancy at the minimum therapeutic dose was 76–85%, and occupancy rose non-linearly with dose, reaching a plateau at higher doses [[Meyer2004]]
- The authors' reading is that about 80% SERT blockade "is important for therapeutic effect" (Early evidence: an association across five drugs, not a tested threshold) [[Meyer2004]]
- Vortioxetine SERT occupancy in two PET studies was about 50% at 5 mg/day, 65% at 10 mg/day and about 80% at 20 mg/day [[FDA-Trintellix]]
- Most antidepressants target monoamine neurotransmitter function, but "a purely neurotransmitter-based explanation for antidepressant drug action is challenged by the delayed clinical onset of most agents" [[Harmer2017]]
- Delayed-onset theory 1 (Hypothesis): the therapeutic effect follows a slow, adaptive enhancement of 5-HT neurotransmission rather than the immediate reuptake block; this model motivated strategies to speed response [[Blier1994]]. The specific step usually taught, gradual desensitisation of inhibitory 5-HT1A autoreceptors on raphe neurons, was not confirmed against the full text (unverified)
- Delayed-onset theory 2 (Hypothesis): antidepressants "do not act as direct mood enhancers" but shift emotional processing towards positive information early, before mood changes, giving a platform for later psychological relearning [[Harmer2009]]
- Delayed-onset theory 3 (Hypothesis): downstream neural plasticity; emotional-processing and plasticity accounts are presented as complementary [[Harmer2017]]
- The "serotonin deficiency" idea is not the same as "SSRIs work": an umbrella review found no consistent evidence that depression is caused by lowered serotonin activity or concentrations [[Moncrieff2022Serotonin]], while a published reply by 35 researchers argues that the evidence clearly implicates the serotonin system [[Jauhar2023]]; drug efficacy against placebo is shown separately in trials [[Cipriani2018]]

### antidepressants · half-lives and kinetics (labels)
- Fluoxetine: elimination half-life 1–3 days after acute and 4–6 days after chronic dosing; its active metabolite norfluoxetine 4–16 days; dose changes are "not fully reflected in plasma for several weeks" [[FDA-Prozac]]
- Sertraline: the label cites a 24-hour elimination half-life and a one-week interval between dose changes [[FDA-Zoloft]]
- Escitalopram: mean terminal half-life about 27–32 hours, about 50% longer in people aged 65 and over [[FDA-Lexapro]]
- Citalopram: mean terminal half-life about 35 hours; steady state in about one week [[FDA-Celexa]]
- Paroxetine: mean elimination half-life about 21 hours [[FDA-Paxil]]
- Venlafaxine: apparent elimination half-life 5 ± 2 hours; ODV 11 ± 2 hours [[FDA-EffexorXR]]
- Duloxetine: about 12 hours (range 8–17) [[FDA-Duloxetine]]
- Levomilnacipran: about 12 hours [[FDA-Fetzima]]
- Bupropion: mean 21 (±9) hours [[FDA-WellbutrinXL]]
- Mirtazapine: about 20–40 hours, longer in women than in men [[FDA-Remeron]]
- Vilazodone: about 25 hours [[FDA-Viibryd]]
- Vortioxetine: about 66 hours, steady state within about two weeks [[FDA-Trintellix]]
- Half-life and stopping: when maintenance SSRI treatment was interrupted with blinded placebo for 5–8 days (n = 242 enrolled, 220 completed), new somatic and psychological symptoms appeared with paroxetine, to a lesser degree with sertraline, and rarely with fluoxetine (P < .001) [[Rosenbaum1998]]

### antidepressants · CYP interactions and pharmacogenomic testing
- Fluoxetine is primarily metabolised by CYP2D6 and inhibits it; pimozide and thioridazine are contraindicated with it [[FDA-Prozac]]
- Paroxetine "is a strong CYP2D6 inhibitor" [[FDA-Paxil]]
- Citalopram: the maximum labelled dose is 20 mg once daily for CYP2C19 poor metabolisers, people older than 60 and people with hepatic impairment [[FDA-Celexa]]
- Vortioxetine: maximum recommended dose 10 mg/day in known CYP2D6 poor metabolisers [[FDA-Trintellix]]
- Tricyclics: the amitriptyline label states that about 7–10% of Caucasians are CYP2D6 "poor metabolizers" with higher than expected plasma concentrations [[FDA-Amitriptyline]]
- CYP2C19 genotype and escitalopram (n = 2,087): serum concentration was 3.3-fold higher in people with two inactive alleles and 10–20% lower with the *17 gain-of-function allele; switching to another antidepressant within 1 year was 3.3 times more frequent in the Null/Null group and 3.0 times in the *17/*17 group than in *1/*1 [[Jukic2018]]
- Meta-analysis of exposure: escitalopram levels were 2.63-fold (95% CI 2.40–2.89) higher in CYP2C19 poor versus normal metabolisers; sertraline 1.38-fold (1.27–1.51) higher in intermediate metabolisers [[Milosavljevic2021]]
- CPIC 2023 gives prescribing recommendations based on CYP2D6, CYP2C19 and CYP2B6 genotype for SSRIs, SNRIs, vilazodone and vortioxetine, and states that data for SLC6A4 and HTR2A "do not support their clinical use in antidepressant prescribing" [[Bousman2023]]; a separate CPIC guideline covers CYP2D6/CYP2C19 and tricyclic dosing [[Hicks2017]]
- GUIDED trial (n = 1,167 with inadequate response to at least one antidepressant): pharmacogenomic-guided prescribing did not improve the primary outcome, mean HAM-D17 symptom improvement at week 8 (27.2% vs 24.4%, P = .107); response (26.0% vs 19.9%) and remission (15.3% vs 10.1%) were higher [[Greden2019]]
- PRIME Care (n = 1,944 US veterans): testing cut prescriptions with a predicted drug-gene interaction (no interaction 59.3% vs 25.7%); remission (PHQ-9 ≤ 5) over 24 weeks was higher (OR 1.28, 95% CI 1.05–1.57; risk difference 2.8%) but not at week 24 (risk difference 1.5%, −2.4% to 5.3%) [[Oslin2022]]
- Meta-analysis of 13 trials (4,767 patients): pharmacogenomic-guided prescribing was associated with remission RR 1.41 (95% CI 1.15–1.74), with heterogeneous tests and trial quality [[Brown2022PGx]]
- CANMAT 2023 summarises the meta-analyses as showing modest absolute gains (response differences of 4.6 to 7.2 percentage points in favour of testing) [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

### antidepressants · dose-response
- Dose-response meta-analysis (77 trials, 19,364 participants): SSRI efficacy rose gradually up to 20–40 mg fluoxetine equivalents, then was flat or declined up to 80 mg, while dropouts due to adverse effects rose steeply across the range [[Furukawa2019Dose]]
- In the same analysis venlafaxine efficacy rose up to about 75–150 mg with a more modest increase beyond; mirtazapine efficacy rose up to about 30 mg and then fell [[Furukawa2019Dose]]
- A second meta-analysis (40 trials, 10,039 participants) found a small positive dose-efficacy relation for SSRIs that plateaus at about 250 mg imipramine equivalents (50 mg fluoxetine), offset by more dropouts due to side effects [[Jakubovski2016]]
- Raising the dose after initial non-response was no better than staying on the standard dose (Hedges g −0.04, 95% CI −0.20 to 0.12; 7 trials, n = 999) and caused more dropouts due to adverse effects [[Dold2017]]
- SNRIs: medium versus low and high versus medium doses differed by SMD −0.06 each (not significant; 26 studies, 10,242 patients) [[Rink2022]]
- Labels agree for several drugs: fluoxetine 20 mg/day "is sufficient to obtain a satisfactory response" in most cases [[FDA-Prozac]]; a fixed-dose escitalopram trial "failed to demonstrate a greater benefit of 20 mg over 10 mg" [[FDA-Lexapro]]; for duloxetine "there is no evidence that doses greater than 60 mg/day confer any additional benefits" [[FDA-Duloxetine]]; for desvenlafaxine there was no evidence that doses above 50 mg/day add benefit [[FDA-Pristiq]]
- Labelled adult MDD dose ranges (quoted, not instructions): fluoxetine 20 mg/day, maximum 80 mg/day [[FDA-Prozac]]; sertraline 50–200 mg/day [[FDA-Zoloft]]; escitalopram 10 mg, maximum 20 mg [[FDA-Lexapro]]; citalopram 20 mg, maximum 40 mg [[FDA-Celexa]]; paroxetine 20 mg, maximum 50 mg [[FDA-Paxil]]; venlafaxine XR 75 mg, maximum 225 mg [[FDA-EffexorXR]]; bupropion XL 150 mg then 300 mg once daily [[FDA-WellbutrinXL]]; vortioxetine 10 mg then 20 mg as tolerated [[FDA-Trintellix]]

### trials · efficacy against placebo
- Cipriani 2018: 522 double-blind trials, 116,477 participants (87,052 on an active drug, 29,425 on placebo); median acute treatment 8 weeks (IQR 6–8); mean baseline HAMD-17 25.7, so most participants had moderate-to-severe depression [[Cipriani2018]]
- All 21 antidepressants were more effective than placebo for response: odds ratios ranged from 2.13 (95% CrI 1.89–2.41) for amitriptyline to 1.37 (1.16–1.63) for reboxetine [[Cipriani2018]]
- The summary standardised mean difference for all antidepressants versus placebo was 0.30 (95% CrI 0.26–0.34) [[Cipriani2018]]
- Acceptability (all-cause dropout) versus placebo: only agomelatine (OR 0.84, 0.72–0.97) and fluoxetine (0.88, 0.80–0.96) had fewer dropouts; clomipramine had more (1.30, 1.01–1.68). Dropouts due to adverse events were higher than placebo for active drugs, with ORs from 1.64 to 4.44 [[Cipriani2018]]
- Head-to-head trials: agomelatine, amitriptyline, escitalopram, mirtazapine, paroxetine, venlafaxine and vortioxetine were more effective than other antidepressants (ORs 1.19–1.96); fluoxetine, fluvoxamine, reboxetine and trazodone were the least efficacious (0.51–0.84) [[Cipriani2018]]
- Head-to-head acceptability: agomelatine, citalopram, escitalopram, fluoxetine, sertraline and vortioxetine were better tolerated (ORs 0.43–0.77); amitriptyline, clomipramine, duloxetine, fluvoxamine, reboxetine, trazodone and venlafaxine had the highest dropout rates (1.30–2.32) [[Cipriani2018]]
- Evidence quality in Cipriani 2018: 9% of trials at high risk of bias, 73% moderate, 18% low; 78% industry funded; certainty moderate to very low; smaller and older trials showed larger effects [[Cipriani2018]]
- The earlier 12-drug network meta-analysis favoured escitalopram and sertraline for the balance of efficacy and acceptability [[Cipriani2009]]
- FDA individual participant data (232 trials, 73,388 participants, 1979–2016): mean drug-placebo difference 1.75 HAMD-17 points (95% CI 1.63–1.86), larger with greater baseline severity [[Stone2022]]
- The same analysis fitted three response groups with mean improvements of 16.0, 8.9 and 1.7 points; a "Large" response occurred in 24.5% on drug versus 9.6% on placebo, so about 15% of participants had a substantial drug effect beyond placebo [[Stone2022]]
- Placebo response rate in antidepressant trials has been stable at 35–40% since 1991 (252 trials, 26,324 people on placebo; RR 1.00 per 5 years, 95% CI 0.97–1.03) [[Furukawa2016Placebo]]
- NNT in primary care trials (Cochrane, 14 studies, 6–8 weeks): response RR 1.28 (1.15–1.43) for SSRIs and 1.24 (1.11–1.38) for tricyclics; median NNT 7 for SSRIs and 9 for tricyclics; NNH for withdrawal due to side effects 20–90 for SSRIs and 4–30 for tricyclics [[Arroll2009]]
- Severity moderation, FDA data on four newer antidepressants: the drug-placebo difference grew with baseline severity and reached conventional clinical significance only at the upper end of very severe depression, mainly because placebo response fell [[Kirsch2008]]
- Severity moderation, patient-level data (6 trials, 718 patients): effect size was below d = 0.20 for baseline HDRS under 23 and crossed the NICE threshold for clinical significance at a baseline HDRS of 25 [[Fournier2010]]
- A contrasting patient-level analysis (18 trials, 6,669 adults): judged by the single depressed-mood item, SSRIs separated from placebo in 29 of 32 comparisons (91%), versus 14 of 32 (44%) using the HDRS-17 total [[Hieronymus2016]]
- PANDA primary care trial (people with depressive symptoms of any severity where benefit was uncertain): sertraline did not reduce PHQ-9 depressive symptoms at 6 weeks (adjusted proportional difference 0.95, 95% CI 0.85–1.07) but improved anxiety, mental health-related quality of life and self-rated mental health; weak evidence of a depressive-symptom effect by 12 weeks [[Lewis2019PANDA]]
- Quality of life: antidepressants versus placebo SMD 0.22 (95% CI 0.18–0.26) in 46 RCTs (16,171 patients) [[Wiesinger2023]]
- Psychiatric drugs, antidepressants included, were "not generally less efficacious" than common general-medicine drugs when effect sizes from 94 meta-analyses were set side by side [[Leucht2012]]
- Publication bias: of 74 FDA-registered trials of 12 antidepressants, 31% were unpublished; the literature made 94% of trials look positive whereas FDA review found 51% positive; the apparent effect size was inflated by 32% overall (11–69% by drug) [[Turner2008]]
- For four newer antidepressants (30 trials, 13,747 patients) reporting improved: transparent reporting of negative trials rose from 11% to 47% and effect-size inflation fell from 0.10 to 0.05 (FDA-based 0.24 vs journal-based 0.29) [[Turner2022]]

### trials · onset of response
- Meta-analysis of 28 placebo-controlled SSRI trials (n = 5,872): an early-response model fitted best; improvement over placebo was present by the end of week 1 and continued at a decreasing rate for at least 6 weeks; the chance of a 50% HDRS reduction by week 1 was higher with SSRIs (RR 1.64, 95% CI 1.2–2.25) [[Taylor2006]]
- In 47 trials, 60.2% of the total improvement on active drug and 61.6% of that on placebo occurred in the first 2 weeks, and drug-placebo separation was most pronounced in those weeks [[Posternak2005]]
- Early improvement (at least 20% HAM-D-17 reduction by week 2) in 41 trials (n = 6,562): sensitivity at least 81% for stable response and at least 87% for stable remission; negative predictive values 82–100%, positive predictive values 19–60% [[Szegedi2009]]
- Meaning of those figures: lack of early improvement makes later stable response unlikely (high NPV), but early improvement does not guarantee it (low PPV) [[Szegedi2009]]
- CANMAT 2023 defines early improvement as a 20% or greater fall in score within 2 to 4 weeks, response as 50% or greater, and remission as HAM-D ≤ 7, MADRS ≤ 10 or PHQ-9 ≤ 4; it states that no early improvement by 4 weeks should trigger consideration of a dose increase or a switch [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- In STAR*D step 1 a substantial portion of people who reached response or remission did so at or after 8 weeks of treatment [[Trivedi2006STARD]]

### trials · STAR*D, next steps and continuation
- STAR*D step 1 (citalopram, up to 14 weeks, measurement-based care, 2,876 analysed outpatients in 23 psychiatric and 18 primary care settings): remission 28% (HAM-D ≤ 7) and 33% (QIDS-SR ≤ 5); response 47% (QIDS-SR); mean exit dose 41.8 mg/day [[Trivedi2006STARD]]
- STAR*D across steps: QIDS-SR remission was 36.8%, 30.6%, 13.7% and 13.0% for steps 1 to 4, with a reported cumulative remission of 67%; people needing more steps relapsed more during follow-up [[Rush2006STARD]]
- A 2023 reanalysis using the protocol-specified blinded HRSD and the protocol inclusion criteria put the cumulative remission rate at 35.0% rather than 67% [[Pigott2023]]
- Pointer (pass G owns resistant depression): after citalopram, switching to bupropion SR, sertraline or venlafaxine XR gave HRSD remission of 21.3%, 17.6% and 24.8%, with no significant difference [[Rush2006NEJM]]; augmenting citalopram with bupropion SR or buspirone gave 29.7% and 30.1% [[Trivedi2006NEJM]]
- A meta-analysis found no high-level evidence that switching beats continuing the same antidepressant after non-response (SMD −0.17, 95% CI −0.59 to 0.26; 4 RCTs) [[Bschor2018]]
- ACP 2023: monotherapy with either CBT or a second-generation antidepressant as initial treatment for moderate to severe MDD (strong recommendation, moderate certainty); for non-response, switching to or augmenting with CBT, or switching to another second-generation antidepressant or augmenting with a second medicine (conditional, low certainty) [[Qaseem2023ACP]]
- CANMAT 2023 lists 17 of 31 antidepressants as first-line and recommends 6 to 12 months of maintenance treatment after remission for all patients, and 2 years or more for those with risk factors for recurrence [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Relapse prevention meta-analysis (31 randomised trials, 4,410 participants): continuing treatment reduced the odds of relapse by 70% (95% CI 62–78); relapse 41% on placebo versus 18% on continued antidepressant, an effect that held for up to 36 months though most trials lasted 12 months [[Geddes2003]]
- A later meta-analysis found relapse of 20.2% versus 37.2% with continuation versus placebo substitution, similar from 6 months (19.6% vs 37.6%) to beyond 1 year (19.9% vs 39.8%) [[Kato2021]]
- ANTLER (478 primary care patients who felt well enough to stop): relapse by 52 weeks 39% with maintenance versus 56% with tapered discontinuation (HR 2.06, 95% CI 1.56–2.70); so 44% of those who stopped did not relapse within a year [[Lewis2021]]
- Caveat: relapse after stopping in these trials can be confounded with withdrawal symptoms, and most tapers in trials lasted four weeks or less [[VanLeeuwen2021]]

### sideeffects · boxed warning on suicidal thoughts and behaviours
- US antidepressant labels carry a class boxed warning: antidepressants "increased the risk of suicidal thoughts and behavior in children, adolescents, and young adults in short-term studies"; no increase was seen over age 24 and there was a reduction in people aged 65 and older [[FDA-Prozac]]
- Label numbers (pooled 295 short-term adult trials, median 2 months, 11 antidepressants, over 77,000 patients, plus paediatric trials): drug-placebo difference in cases of suicidality per 1,000 treated was 14 more under 18, 5 more at 18–24, 1 fewer at 25–64 and 6 fewer at 65 and over [[FDA-Prozac]]
- The label states that no suicides occurred in the paediatric trials, and that the adult trials had too few to draw any conclusion about a drug effect on suicide [[FDA-Prozac]]
- The label instruction is close monitoring of people of all ages starting treatment for worsening and for emergence of suicidal thoughts, and telling families and caregivers to watch and communicate with the prescriber [[FDA-Prozac]]
- FDA paediatric analysis (24 placebo-controlled trials, 4,582 patients): risk ratio for suicidality (ideation or behaviour) 1.66 (95% CI 1.02–2.68) for SSRIs in depression trials and 1.95 (1.28–2.98) for all drugs and indications; risk difference 0.02 (0.01–0.03); there were no deaths by suicide in these trials [[Hammad2006]]
- FDA adult analysis (372 trials, 99,231 adults): odds ratio for suicidal behaviour or ideation 1.62 (0.97–2.71) under age 25, 0.79 (0.64–0.98) at 25–64 and 0.37 (0.18–0.76) at 65 and over; the odds ratio fell by 2.6% for each year of age [[Stone2009]]
- Clinical study report review (70 trials, 18,526 patients; duloxetine, fluoxetine, paroxetine, sertraline, venlafaxine): in adults the odds ratio for suicidality was 0.81 (0.51–1.28); in children and adolescents 2.39 (1.31–4.33) [[Sharma2016]]
- Teaching frame: the signal is an age-dependent rise in suicidal thoughts and behaviours early in treatment, set beside the risks of untreated depression; suicidal thoughts are a symptom that can be treated, and the label response is closer monitoring, not avoidance of treatment [[FDA-Prozac,Stone2009]]

### sideeffects · common adverse reactions (label trial rates, drug vs placebo)
- Fluoxetine, MDD trials (n = 1,728 vs 975): nausea 21% vs 9%, insomnia 16% vs 9%, nervousness 14% vs 9%, somnolence 13% vs 6%, anxiety 12% vs 7%, anorexia 11% vs 2%, tremor 10% vs 3%, sweating 8% vs 3% [[FDA-Prozac]]
- Sertraline, pooled adult trials across indications (n = 3,066 vs 2,293): nausea 26% vs 12%, diarrhoea 20% vs 10%, insomnia 20% vs 13%, dry mouth 14% vs 9%, somnolence 11% vs 6%, tremor 9% vs 2%, hyperhidrosis 7% vs 3% [[FDA-Zoloft]]
- Escitalopram, MDD trials (n = 715 vs 592): nausea 15% vs 7%, insomnia 9% vs 4%, somnolence 6% vs 2%, increased sweating 5% vs 2%, fatigue 5% vs 2% [[FDA-Lexapro]]
- Paroxetine, 6-week MDD trials (n = 421 vs 421): nausea 26% vs 9%, somnolence 23% vs 9%, dry mouth 18% vs 12%, asthenia 15% vs 6%, constipation 14% vs 9%, insomnia 13% vs 6%, sweating 11% vs 2% [[FDA-Paxil]]
- Vortioxetine: nausea in 21%, 26%, 32% and 32% at 5, 10, 15 and 20 mg/day versus 9% with placebo [[FDA-Trintellix]]
- Vilazodone 20 and 40 mg/day: diarrhoea 26% and 29% vs 10% with placebo; nausea 22% and 24% vs 7% [[FDA-Viibryd]]
- Mirtazapine: somnolence 54% vs 18% (leading to discontinuation in 10.4% vs 2.2%), increased appetite 17% vs 2%, weight gain 12% vs 2% [[FDA-Remeron]]
- Dextromethorphan-bupropion: dizziness 16%, headache 8%, diarrhoea 7%, somnolence 7%, dry mouth 6%, sexual dysfunction 6%, hyperhidrosis 5%; 4% stopped for adverse reactions vs 0% on placebo [[FDA-Auvelity]]
- Class contrast: SSRIs and SNRIs most often cause gastrointestinal and sexual adverse effects, tricyclics anticholinergic effects such as dry mouth and constipation [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- In a part-randomised study (n = 811), dry mouth (74%), constipation (33%) and weight gain (15%) were associated with nortriptyline, and insomnia (36%), yawning (16%) and diarrhoea (9%) with escitalopram; many complaints listed as adverse reactions were more common before treatment than during it [[Uher2009]]
- CANMAT 2023 recommends telling patients about possible side effects before prescribing and asking about them within 2 weeks of starting [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

### sideeffects · sexual dysfunction
- Meta-analysis of studies that asked directly: treatment-emergent sexual dysfunction ranged from 25.8% to 80.3% across sertraline, venlafaxine, citalopram, paroxetine, fluoxetine, imipramine, phenelzine, duloxetine, escitalopram and fluvoxamine (all higher than placebo); agomelatine, bupropion, mirtazapine, moclobemide and nefazodone did not differ from placebo [[Serretti2009]]
- Prospective Spanish study (n = 1,022): overall incidence 59.1%; citalopram 72.7%, paroxetine 70.7%, venlafaxine 67.3%, sertraline 62.9%, fluvoxamine 62.3%, fluoxetine 57.7%, mirtazapine 24.4%, nefazodone 8%, moclobemide 3.9% [[Montejo2001]]
- US cross-sectional study: bupropion IR 22% and SR 25% versus 36–43% for SSRIs, mirtazapine and venlafaxine XR; physicians consistently underestimated prevalence [[Clayton2002]]
- Spontaneous reports in label trials are far lower (sertraline: ejaculation failure 8% vs 1%, decreased libido 6% vs 2%), which is why labels tell prescribers to ask specifically [[FDA-Zoloft,FDA-Prozac]]
- Persistent symptoms: the fluoxetine label now states that "symptoms of sexual dysfunction occasionally persist after discontinuation of fluoxetine treatment" [[FDA-Prozac]]; no incidence figure for post-SSRI sexual dysfunction was found in a validated source (Early evidence)

### sideeffects · weight
- Target-trial emulation (183,118 new users, 8 US health systems): versus sertraline, 6-month weight gain was higher with escitalopram (+0.41 kg), paroxetine (+0.37 kg), duloxetine (+0.34 kg), venlafaxine (+0.17 kg) and citalopram (+0.12 kg), similar with fluoxetine (−0.07 kg) and lower with bupropion (−0.22 kg) [[Petimar2024]]
- In the same study escitalopram, paroxetine and duloxetine carried a 10–15% higher risk of gaining at least 5% of baseline weight and bupropion a 15% lower risk; 6-month adherence was only 28–41% [[Petimar2024]]
- UK cohort over 10 years: at least 5% weight gain occurred at 11.2 per 100 person-years in people prescribed antidepressants versus 8.1 in those not prescribed them (adjusted rate ratio 1.21, 95% CI 1.19–1.22), with risk raised for at least 6 years [[Gafoor2018]]
- Mirtazapine: weight gain of at least 7% of body weight in 7.5% versus 0% on placebo in US controlled studies [[FDA-Remeron]]

### sideeffects · hyponatraemia, bleeding, QT, blood pressure, seizures
- Labels: hyponatraemia with SSRIs and SNRIs often reflects the syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH); older people and those taking diuretics or who are volume depleted are at greater risk [[FDA-Prozac]]
- Meta-analysis (39 studies, n = 8,175,111): antidepressant exposure OR 3.16 (95% CI 1.91–5.23) for hyponatraemia; event rates 7.44% with SNRIs, 5.59% with SSRIs, 2.66% with tricyclics, 1.02% with mirtazapine and 0.89% with trazodone [[Gheysens2024]]
- Risk factors: older age (OR 6.3) and thiazide diuretics (OR 11.2–13.5) [[DePicker2014]]; in a Danish register study the association at the first sodium measurement after starting was strongest for citalopram (incidence rate ratio 7.8) [[LethMoller2016]]
- Older adults (UK cohort, 60,746 people aged 65–100 with depression): SSRIs had adjusted hazard ratios of 1.66 (1.58–1.73) for falls and 1.52 (1.33–1.75) for hyponatraemia (comparison: periods without antidepressant use); the authors caution that confounding by indication is likely [[Coupland2011]]
- CANMAT 2023 says serum electrolyte monitoring should be considered for patients aged 60 and older [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Bleeding: SSRIs were associated with upper gastrointestinal bleeding (OR 1.66, 95% CI 1.44–1.92 in case-control studies); the number needed to harm was 3,177 in a low-risk and 881 in a high-risk population; with an NSAID the OR rose to 4.25 (2.82–6.42) [[Anglin2014]]
- Any-site bleeding across 42 observational studies: OR 1.41 (95% CI 1.27–1.57) [[Laporte2017]]
- Citalopram and QT: in a thorough QT study the maximum mean QTc increase versus placebo was 8.5 msec at 20 mg and 18.5 msec at 60 mg; the label says doses above 40 mg once daily are not recommended, caps the dose at 20 mg for people over 60, and says to discontinue with persistent QTc above 500 ms [[FDA-Celexa]]
- Health-record study (38,397 adults): dose-related QTc prolongation was found for citalopram, escitalopram and amitriptyline; within-person QTc rose 7.8 ms from 10 to 20 mg and 10.3 ms from 20 to 40 mg of citalopram; bupropion was associated with QTc shortening [[Castro2013]]
- A Veterans Health cohort found no higher risk of ventricular arrhythmia or death with citalopram above 40 mg/day (adjusted HR for ventricular arrhythmia 0.68, 95% CI 0.61–0.76, versus 1–20 mg) and questioned the warning [[Zivin2013]]
- Venlafaxine and blood pressure: sustained hypertension (supine diastolic ≥ 90 mm Hg and ≥ 10 mm Hg above baseline on three consecutive visits) occurred in 19 of 705 (3%) in MDD premarketing studies at 75–375 mg/day; the label requires blood pressure checks before and during treatment [[FDA-EffexorXR]]
- The venlafaxine effect on diastolic pressure is dose dependent and was clinically significant only above 300 mg/day in a meta-analysis of 3,744 patients [[Thase1998]]
- Bupropion seizure risk is dose related: about 0.1% (1/1,000) with sustained-release doses up to 300 mg/day, about 0.4% (13/3,200) with immediate-release 300–450 mg/day, and an almost tenfold rise between 450 and 600 mg/day; it is contraindicated with a seizure disorder, anorexia nervosa or bulimia, and abrupt withdrawal from alcohol, benzodiazepines, barbiturates or antiepileptics [[FDA-WellbutrinXL]]
- Bupropion can also raise blood pressure; the label asks for a blood pressure check before starting and periodically after [[FDA-WellbutrinXL]]
- Mirtazapine: agranulocytosis occurred in 2 of 2,796 patients in premarketing trials [[FDA-Remeron]]
- Trazodone: the label warns of priapism needing immediate medical attention, and notes drowsiness that may require most of the dose at bedtime [[FDA-Trazodone]]
- Umbrella review of 45 meta-analyses of observational studies: most proposed long-term harms were not supported by convincing evidence once confounding by indication was considered, and no absolute contraindication emerged [[Dragioti2019]]

### sideeffects · serotonin syndrome and MAOI washouts
- Labels describe serotonin syndrome as potentially life-threatening, with autonomic instability (labile blood pressure, dizziness, diaphoresis, flushing, hyperthermia), neuromuscular symptoms (tremor, rigidity, myoclonus, hyperreflexia, incoordination), seizures and gastrointestinal symptoms [[FDA-Fetzima]]
- Risk rises when serotonergic drugs are combined (the sertraline label lists triptans, tricyclics, fentanyl, lithium, tramadol, meperidine, methadone, tryptophan, buspirone, amphetamines and St John's Wort) and with MAOIs, which impair serotonin metabolism [[FDA-Zoloft]]
- Hunter Serotonin Toxicity Criteria: decision rules built on clonus (inducible, spontaneous or ocular), agitation, diaphoresis, tremor and hyperreflexia, plus hypertonicity and temperature above 38 °C; sensitivity 84% and specificity 97%, versus 75% and 96% for the Sternbach criteria [[Dunkley2003]]
- The standard clinical review of the syndrome is Boyer and Shannon, NEJM 2005 [[Boyer2005]]
- Washouts as labelled: at least 14 days between stopping an MAOI and starting fluoxetine, and at least 5 weeks after stopping fluoxetine before an MAOI, because of its long half-life [[FDA-Prozac]]; 14 days in both directions for paroxetine [[FDA-Paxil]]
- The phenelzine label gives at least 14 days between an MAOI and a serotonin reuptake inhibitor in either direction, "with the exception of fluoxetine" (five weeks) [[FDA-Nardil]]; the tranylcypromine label notes MAO inhibition may persist up to 10 days after stopping [[FDA-Parnate]]

### sideeffects · activation, mania or hypomania
- Labels ask prescribers to screen for a personal or family history of bipolar disorder, mania or hypomania before starting any antidepressant [[FDA-Celexa]]
- In venlafaxine XR premarketing MDD studies mania or hypomania was reported in 0.3% versus 0.0% on placebo [[FDA-EffexorXR]]
- Across 109 trials (114,521 adults with bipolar disorder or MDD), mania or hypomania occurred in 12.5% with and 7.5% without antidepressants; tricyclics carried more risk than serotonin reuptake inhibitors [[Tondo2010]]
- In a UK cohort of people with unipolar depression, SSRIs (HR 1.34, 95% CI 1.18–1.52) and venlafaxine (1.35, 1.07–1.70) were associated with later mania or bipolar disorder; overall incidence was 10.9 per 1,000 person-years [[Patel2015]]

### sideeffects · discontinuation symptoms and tapering
- Labels list discontinuation reactions, particularly after abrupt stopping: nausea, sweating, dysphoric mood, irritability, agitation, dizziness, sensory disturbances such as electric shock sensations, tremor, anxiety, confusion, headache, lethargy, emotional lability, insomnia, hypomania, tinnitus and seizures; a gradual dose reduction is recommended whenever possible [[FDA-Paxil]]
- Estimates of how common these are differ by method. A 2019 review of 14 studies (including surveys) gave 27–86%, weighted average 56%, with 46% of those affected choosing the most severe rating [[Davies2019]]
- A 2024 meta-analysis (79 studies, 21,002 patients) found at least one symptom in 31% (95% CI 27–35%) after stopping an antidepressant and 17% (14–21%) after stopping placebo, giving about 15% attributable to the drug (one in six to seven); severe symptoms in 2.8% versus 0.6% [[Henssler2024]]
- In that analysis desvenlafaxine, venlafaxine, imipramine and escitalopram had higher frequencies, and imipramine, paroxetine and (des)venlafaxine higher severity [[Henssler2024]]
- A 2025 meta-analysis of 35 studies pooled incidence at 42.9% (44.4% in 11 RCTs), rising with treatment duration (35.1% at 6–12 weeks, 42.7% at 12–24 weeks, 51.4% beyond 24 weeks); tapering versus abrupt stopping gave 34.5% versus 42.5% (not significant) [[Zhang2025]]
- A 2025 JAMA Psychiatry meta-analysis (50 studies, 17,828 participants) found about one extra symptom on the DESS scale at week 1 after stopping (SMD 0.31, 95% CI 0.23–0.39); dizziness was commonest (OR 5.52; risk difference 6.24%), then nausea (OR 3.16); mood worsening was not associated with discontinuation in the first weeks [[Kalfas2025]]
- CANMAT 2023: up to 50% may have symptoms after stopping long-term treatment, especially abruptly; they usually start within a few days, are mild to moderate and resolve within a few weeks, but later, more severe and longer-lasting courses are reported; the FINISH mnemonic covers flu-like symptoms, insomnia, nausea, imbalance, sensory disturbances and hyperarousal; shorter half-life drugs such as paroxetine and venlafaxine carry more risk [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Hyperbolic tapering (Early evidence): because SERT occupancy rises steeply at low doses and flattens at higher ones, Horowitz and Taylor proposed reducing doses hyperbolically, over months and to doses well below the therapeutic minimum, so that occupancy falls in even steps [[Horowitz2019,Meyer2004]]
- That proposal rests on PET occupancy data and observational reports; a Cochrane review found too few adequate trials to draw firm conclusions about any discontinuation approach [[VanLeeuwen2021]]
- Teaching line: stopping suddenly can cause discontinuation symptoms, these are not addiction or relapse by themselves, and any change is planned with the prescriber [[FDA-Paxil,Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding are covered in pass I; decisions there are planned with the care team, weighing the risks of untreated illness beside medicine risks.

### Exam hooks
- SERT occupancy at minimum therapeutic SSRI doses is about 80% (76–85% across five drugs) [[Meyer2004]]
- Fluoxetine has the longest half-life of the SSRIs (norfluoxetine 4–16 days), hence the 5-week gap before an MAOI [[FDA-Prozac]]
- Standard MAOI washout is 14 days; tyramine-rich food plus an MAOI can cause hypertensive crisis [[FDA-Nardil]]
- Boxed warning applies to people under 25: 14 extra cases of suicidality per 1,000 under 18, 5 extra at 18–24, 6 fewer at 65 and over [[FDA-Prozac]]
- Cipriani 2018: 522 trials, 116,477 participants, all 21 drugs beat placebo, summary SMD 0.30 [[Cipriani2018]]
- Placebo response in antidepressant trials is about 35–40% [[Furukawa2016Placebo]]
- STAR*D step 1 citalopram remission: 28% (HAM-D), 33% (QIDS-SR) [[Trivedi2006STARD]]
- Lack of at least 20% improvement by week 2 predicts non-response with a negative predictive value of 82–100% [[Szegedi2009]]
- SSRI dose-response is flat above 20–40 mg fluoxetine equivalents while adverse-effect dropouts keep rising [[Furukawa2019Dose]]
- Citalopram maximum is 40 mg/day, 20 mg/day over age 60 or in CYP2C19 poor metabolisers, because of dose-dependent QT prolongation [[FDA-Celexa]]
- Bupropion: seizure risk is dose related; contraindicated in seizure disorder and in anorexia nervosa or bulimia [[FDA-WellbutrinXL]]
- Mirtazapine blocks α2, 5-HT2, 5-HT3 and H1 receptors: sedation and weight gain, little sexual dysfunction [[FDA-Remeron,Serretti2009]]
- SSRI plus NSAID raises upper GI bleeding odds about fourfold (OR 4.25) [[Anglin2014]]
- Hyponatraemia (SIADH) with SSRIs/SNRIs: think older adults and thiazides [[DePicker2014]]
- Hunter criteria centre on clonus, agitation, diaphoresis, tremor and hyperreflexia [[Dunkley2003]]
- Venlafaxine raises blood pressure in a dose-dependent way [[FDA-EffexorXR,Thase1998]]
- Paroxetine is a strong CYP2D6 inhibitor; fluvoxamine a potent CYP1A2 inhibitor [[FDA-Paxil,FDA-Fluvoxamine]]
- Turner 2008: 94% of published trials positive versus 51% by FDA review [[Turner2008]]
- Continuation: relapse about 18% on drug versus 41% on placebo [[Geddes2003]]
- Paroxetine and venlafaxine (short half-lives) give the most discontinuation symptoms; fluoxetine the fewest [[Rosenbaum1998,Lam2024CANMAT]]

### In plain words
- Antidepressants are medicines for a common, treatable illness. Taking one is not a sign of weakness [[Lam2024CANMAT]]
- In large studies, every antidepressant tested helped more people than a dummy pill did. The extra benefit is real but modest on average, and it is bigger when depression is more severe [[Cipriani2018,Fournier2010]]
- These are group averages. They cannot say how one person will do: some people improve a lot, some a little [[Stone2022]]
- Many people in studies also get better on a dummy pill (about 35 to 40 in 100), partly because depression can lift with time, support and care [[Furukawa2016Placebo]]
- The medicine reaches its target in the brain within days, but mood usually lifts over weeks. Some improvement in the first two weeks is a good sign [[Taylor2006,Szegedi2009]]
- Depression is not simply "low serotonin". Scientists still debate how these medicines work, but trials show that they do help many people [[Moncrieff2022Serotonin,Cipriani2018]]
- A higher dose is not always better. For common antidepressants the lower licensed doses give most of the benefit with fewer side effects [[Furukawa2019Dose]]
- Feeling sick, trouble sleeping and sweating are common early on. Sexual side effects are common too and are worth raising, because doctors may not ask [[FDA-Zoloft,Clayton2002]]
- In children, teenagers and young adults under 25, these medicines can increase suicidal thoughts in the first weeks, so closer check-ins are planned. Suicidal thoughts are a symptom that can be treated. If you are in crisis, contact emergency services or a crisis line (US: call or text 988; UK and Ireland: Samaritans 116 123) [[FDA-Prozac]]
- Staying on the medicine for some months after feeling well lowers the chance of depression coming back [[Geddes2003,Lam2024CANMAT]]
- Stopping suddenly can cause dizziness, nausea, sleep problems or "electric shock" feelings. Any change in dose is planned with the prescriber [[FDA-Paxil,Henssler2024]]
- Some antidepressants do not mix safely with certain other medicines or, for the older MAOI group, with some foods. A pharmacist or prescriber checks this [[FDA-Nardil,FDA-Zoloft]]
- Gene tests can show how fast a body clears some antidepressants, but so far they have only slightly improved results in trials [[Oslin2022]]

## Open questions and controversies
- How large is the benefit over placebo? Position A: all 21 drugs beat placebo (SMD 0.30) and the effect is in the range of many general-medicine drugs [[Cipriani2018,Leucht2012]]; position B: the mean difference of about 2 HAMD-17 points is below proposed minimal important differences (3–5 points) and trials carry high risk of bias [[Jakobsen2017,Munkholm2019,Hengartner2022]]; status: unresolved, with attention shifting from the average to who benefits (about 15% with a large drug-specific response) [[Stone2022]]
- Does benefit depend on severity? Position A: little average benefit over placebo in mild to moderate depression [[Kirsch2008,Fournier2010]]; position B: the HDRS total hides a consistent effect on core mood [[Hieronymus2016]], and benefits on anxiety and quality of life appear in milder primary care populations [[Lewis2019PANDA]]; status: guidelines offer psychotherapy or medicine for moderate to severe illness and favour psychotherapy first when mild [[Qaseem2023ACP]]
- The serotonin debate: no consistent evidence that low serotonin causes depression [[Moncrieff2022Serotonin]] versus the view that the serotonin system is clearly implicated [[Jauhar2023]]; status: the simple "chemical imbalance" explanation is not supported, antidepressant efficacy in trials is, and mechanism research has moved to emotional processing and plasticity [[Harmer2017]]
- Is the onset of action really delayed? Classical teaching of a 2 to 4 week lag versus meta-analyses showing drug-placebo separation in week 1 to 2 [[Taylor2006,Posternak2005]]; status: improvement starts early and accumulates; full effect takes weeks.
- How common and how severe are discontinuation symptoms? 56% with nearly half severe [[Davies2019]] versus about 15% attributable and 3% severe [[Henssler2024]] versus 43% pooled [[Zhang2025]] versus about one extra symptom at week 1 [[Kalfas2025]]; differences come from surveys versus RCTs, short versus long treatment, and whether placebo-arm symptoms are subtracted; status: active dispute, long-term users under-studied [[VanLeeuwen2021]]
- Should tapers be hyperbolic and prolonged? Pharmacological rationale from occupancy curves [[Horowitz2019]]; no adequate randomised comparison yet [[VanLeeuwen2021]]; status: Early evidence.
- The citalopram 40 mg limit: thorough QT data and label restriction [[FDA-Celexa,Castro2013]] versus no excess arrhythmia or mortality at higher doses in a large cohort [[Zivin2013]]; status: label unchanged.
- Does pharmacogenomic testing improve outcomes? Higher remission in some trials and pooled analyses [[Greden2019,Brown2022PGx]] versus small, non-persistent benefit in the largest pragmatic trial [[Oslin2022]]; status: CPIC supports using existing CYP2D6/CYP2C19 results to adjust dosing, not routine testing for drug choice [[Bousman2023]]
- The boxed warning: an age-graded increase in suicidal thoughts and behaviours in short-term trials [[Hammad2006,Stone2009]], with incomplete harm reporting in study reports noted by critics [[Sharma2016]]; observational associations are sensitive to confounding by indication [[Dragioti2019]]; status: warning retained, with emphasis on monitoring rather than withholding treatment.
- STAR*D's headline 67% cumulative remission versus 35% on protocol-specified analysis [[Rush2006STARD,Pigott2023]]; status: the lower figure is increasingly quoted alongside the original.
- How long to continue? Relapse roughly halves with continuation [[Geddes2003,Kato2021,Lewis2021]], yet withdrawal symptoms may be misread as relapse in discontinuation trials [[VanLeeuwen2021]]; status: 6–12 months after remission as published by CANMAT, longer with risk factors [[Lam2024CANMAT]]

## Provenance
- Every journal source was confirmed with PubMed E-utilities esummary on 2026-11-13 (authors, title, journal, year, volume, pages, PMID, PMCID and DOI taken from the record); the Sources lines were generated from those records. Abstracts were read through Europe PMC (core result type). PubMed was rate-limited during the pass, so searching was done on Europe PMC and the final check on PubMed.
- Full text read (open access, Europe PMC): Cipriani 2018 (PMC5889788) for the SMD 0.30, sample split, baseline severity, funding and risk-of-bias figures; CANMAT 2023 (PMC11351064) for maintenance duration, early-improvement definitions, discontinuation text, pharmacogenetic summary and monitoring. All other papers: abstract only.
- Labels were read from the openFDA drug label endpoint on 2026-11-13; set ids and effective dates are in each Sources line. Where openFDA returned several labels for a brand, the brand owner's label was used. Cymbalta, Luvox, Desyrel and Elavil brand labels are not in the endpoint; generic labels were used and are named as such. FDA-Prozac and FDA-Remeron keep the text already used in other companions' sources.json; Gafoor2018 keeps its existing key and text.
- Not transcribed because it could not be read as text: the 21 per-drug odds ratios in Cipriani 2018 Figure 3 (only the highest, lowest and outliers printed in the text are given).
- No abstract in PubMed: Boyer2005 (cited only as the standard review; clinical features come from labels and Dunkley2003), Hicks2017 (cited only for the existence and scope of the CPIC tricyclic guideline, from its title), Jauhar2023 (position taken from its title).
- Marked (unverified): the 5-HT1A autoreceptor desensitisation step of the delayed-onset theory (Blier1994 abstract supports only the general adaptive model); agomelatine's non-US status (inferred from absence in openFDA).
- Dropped for lack of a validated source: the FDA's 2011–2012 citalopram Drug Safety Communication as a separate document (the current Celexa label carries the same limits and QT data); US approval years for Auvelity and Exxua; any incidence figure for post-SSRI sexual dysfunction and the EMA label change; desvenlafaxine half-life (not located in the label text); the NICE 2022 guideline summary (no abstract); suicide-specific outcome figures from long-term trials (omitted under safe-messaging rules).
