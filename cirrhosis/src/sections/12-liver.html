What goes wrong when it fails

Switch on the functions a scarred liver loses. Each one has its own signs and its own blood test, and several are built into the Child–Pugh and MELD scores. Bodies and tests are drawn schematically; real people rarely lose functions one at a time.

The liver that rebuilds itself

Established

The "hepatostat"

The liver is the only solid organ that restores itself to 100% of the liver-to-body-weight ratio the body needs. Acute regeneration after a single injury is beneficial. [[Michalopoulos2021]]

Established

Slow turnover, huge reserve

In a healthy liver, hepatocytes are replaced very slowly. Yet in recolonisation experiments hepatocytes show unlimited regenerative capacity. [[Michalopoulos2021]]

The catch

When injury never stops

Chronic, repeated hepatocyte loss is different: instead of clean regrowth it often leads to fibrosis, cirrhosis and cancer. That story is the next module, From injury to scar. [[Michalopoulos2021]]

Established

Zone 2 steps in

Zonation is a continuum, not three fixed boxes: after damage, zone 2 hepatocytes can take on zone 1 functions. In liver disease, zonation is often lost. [[Trefts2017,Kietzmann2017]]

Established

A built-in blood bank

The sinusoids hold up to 60% of the liver's blood volume, and the liver can make up for up to 25% of a haemorrhage by squeezing blood out of its capacitance vessels. [[Eipel2010]]

Early evidence

Cells as medicine

Hepatocyte transplantation was first reported for Crigler–Najjar syndrome type I in 1998. It is still limited by tissue shortage, early cell loss and poor engraftment. See Research toward better treatment. [[Fox1998,Nguyen2020]]
