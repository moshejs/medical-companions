Viral hepatitis B & C

Two blood-borne viruses that scar the liver slowly over decades. Hepatitis B can be prevented by a vaccine and kept quiet with tablets, and the scar can then recede. Hepatitis C can now be cured with a short course of tablets. Follow both side by side.

AASLD 2018 lists entecavir, tenofovir DF, tenofovir alafenamide and peginterferon as preferred hepatitis B therapies, with no preference between entecavir and tenofovir. For decompensated hepatitis B cirrhosis it states that people should be referred for transplant consideration while antiviral therapy is started, that therapy is indefinite, and that peginterferon is contraindicated [[Terrault2018AASLD]]. AASLD-IDSA 2023 hepatitis C guidance stresses universal screening and widens access to simplified, minimal-monitoring treatment [[Bhattacharya2023HCV]].

Prevention, hepatitis D and safety

Vaccine

A vaccine that prevents a cancer

Taiwan began universal hepatitis B vaccination in July 1984. Liver cancer in children aged 6–14 fell from 0.70 to 0.36 per 100,000 a year [[Chang1997NEJM]]. After 20 years, people vaccinated at birth had a relative risk of liver cancer of 0.31 against unvaccinated birth cohorts. Risk stayed higher with fewer than three doses [[Chang2009JNCI]].

Mother to baby

Protecting newborns

In a trial of 200 HBeAg-positive mothers with high viral load, tenofovir DF from 30–32 weeks of pregnancy lowered transmission to the baby from 18% to 5%. All babies also had immunoprophylaxis [[Pan2016NEJM]]. Worldwide, 85% of infants get three vaccine doses, but only 46% get a timely birth dose, and only 3% of mothers with a high viral load receive antivirals to prevent transmission [[Polaris2023HBV]].

Hepatitis D

Delta: the most aggressive

Hepatitis D virus needs hepatitis B to complete its life cycle. EASL calls it the most aggressive hepatitis virus, with rapid progression to cirrhosis, decompensation and HCC [[EASL2023HDV]]. A meta-analysis estimated antibodies in 4.5% of HBsAg-positive people [[Stockdale2020HDV]]. Later adjusted estimates were substantially lower, so how common it is remains unresolved [[Polaris2024HDV]].

Bulevirtide (Hepcludex) won FDA accelerated approval on 22 May 2026. The label states that clinical outcome benefit has not been established, and it carries a boxed warning about flares after stopping [[FDA-Hepcludex]]. In MYR301, 45–48% had a combined response at week 48, against 2% of controls [[Wedemeyer2023MYR301]]. An AASLD expert opinion published in November 2026 says everyone who is HBsAg-positive should be tested at least once for HDV antibody, with an HDV RNA test to confirm active infection. It says all adults with chronic HDV and compensated liver disease should be considered for bulevirtide-based treatment, and suggests HCC surveillance imaging every 6 months [[Lim2026AASLDHDV]].

Boxed warning

Hepatitis B can wake up during hepatitis C cure

Epclusa, Mavyret and Harvoni carry a boxed warning about hepatitis B reactivation in people with both viruses. The labels say to test everyone for current or prior hepatitis B before starting [[FDA-Epclusa,FDA-Mavyret,FDA-HarvoniD]]. An FDA review found 29 reports of reactivation during DAA therapy between 2013 and 2016. Two people died and one needed a transplant [[BersoffMatcha2017]].

After cure

Lower risk, not zero

In Sicily, one-year HCC incidence after DAAs was 2.1% with cure versus 6.6% without in Child–Pugh A, and 7.8% versus 12.4% in Child–Pugh B [[Calvaruso2018]]. Kanwal and colleagues found no evidence that DAAs promote HCC [[Kanwal2017]]. AASLD 2023 says HCC risk stays raised for up to 10 years after SVR in hepatitis C cirrhosis, so surveillance continues indefinitely in that group [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]]. See Liver cancer surveillance.

Alcohol and HBV

Other things that add up

AASLD 2018 links more than 7 drinks a week for women, or more than 14 for men, to a higher risk of cirrhosis and HCC in hepatitis B. It calls abstinence or minimal intake "the conservative approach" [[Terrault2018AASLD]]. In hepatitis C, drinking 50 g a day or more speeds scarring [[Poynard1997]]. Scar can recede once the cause is controlled; see From injury to scar.

Get urgent care for: vomiting blood, black or tarry stools, new confusion or drowsiness, or fever or belly pain with a swollen abdomen. Call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department.
