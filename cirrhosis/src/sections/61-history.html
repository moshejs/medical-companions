History & people

Two hundred years in one timeline: from a footnote that named a tawny liver, through the first liver transplants, the scores that rank illness, a shunt placed through the neck and the cure of hepatitis C, to the new names of 2023. Press Play, or tap a year.

The 1819–1950 stretch is compressed. The first human TIPS is dated 1989, the earliest validated report. [[Richter1989Radiologe]]

From incurable to curable: hepatitis C

Hepatitis C was identified in 1989 and causes about 700,000 deaths a year; modern antivirals clear the virus in up to 95% of those treated. [[Choo1989,Lin2021Nobel]] Clearing it matters for the liver. People behind the ideas

1819

René Laennec

He named cirrhosis in a brief footnote to his treatise on mediate auscultation, from kirrhos, tawny. In France the eponym is rarely used; North America took it up through William Osler's textbook. [[Duffin1987]]

1964 · 1973

C. G. Child, J. G. Turcotte and R. N. Pugh

Child and Turcotte's classification came from a monograph on surgery and portal hypertension; Pugh and colleagues modified it in a 1973 paper on oesophageal transection for bleeding varices. [[ChildTurcotte1964,Pugh1973]] Try the scores in Child–Pugh & MELD.

1963 · 1967 · 1989

T. E. Starzl

Performed the first human liver transplant in 1963 (none of the first five recipients lived beyond 23 days), began a successful series in 1967, and by 1989 reported 1,179 consecutive transplants with 73% 1-year and 64% 5-year survival. [[Starzl1963,Meirelles2015]]

1967 · 1979

R. Y. Calne

His antilymphocyte serum encouraged Starzl's 1967 series, and in 1979 he reported cyclosporin A as the first immunosuppressant in 34 recipients of cadaveric organs, 2 of them livers. [[Meirelles2015,Calne1979]]

1969 · 1989

J. Rösch, G. M. Richter and J. C. Palmaz

Rösch's team described an experimental transjugular shunt in 1969; Richter, Palmaz and colleagues in Heidelberg placed the first stent-supported TIPS in a person in 1989. [[Rosch1969,Richter1989Radiologe]]

1965 · 1989 · 2020

B. S. Blumberg, Harvey Alter, Michael Houghton, Charles Rice

Blumberg and Alter described the Australia antigen of hepatitis B; Houghton's group cloned hepatitis C; Alter, Houghton and Rice shared the 2020 Nobel Prize for its discovery. [[Blumberg1965,Choo1989,Lin2021Nobel]]

2000 · 2008 · 2021

M. Malinchoc, P. S. Kamath and W. R. Kim

A model built to predict survival after TIPS became MELD; Kim's group added sodium (MELD-Na) and then female sex and albumin (MELD 3.0), adopted for US allocation in July 2023. [[Malinchoc2000,Kamath2001MELD,Kim2008MELDNa,Kim2021MELD3,Kwong2025OPTN]]

2023

M. E. Rinella and 236 panellists

A multisociety Delphi process with panellists from 56 countries renamed NAFLD as MASLD, partly because many felt "nonalcoholic" and "fatty" were stigmatising. [[Rinella2023MASLD]] See MASLD & MASH.
