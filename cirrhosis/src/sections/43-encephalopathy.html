Part 4 · Complications

Hepatic encephalopathy

When a scarred liver and its bypass veins let ammonia slip past, the brain is affected: from changes only a test can find, to confusion, sleepiness and coma. Follow ammonia from the gut to the brain, see the West Haven grades as published, and how lactulose and rifaximin are used.

Urgent careVomiting blood, black or tarry stools, new confusion or drowsiness, or fever or belly pain with a swollen abdomen need urgent care: call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department.

The West Haven grades, as published

Overt hepatic encephalopathy (HE) will occur "in 30%-40% of those with cirrhosis at some time during their clinical course", and minimal or covert HE "occurs in 20%-80%"; a 2024 meta-analysis of 101 studies pooled covert HE at 40.9%. In a US cohort of 294 people without prior HE, the 1-year chance of a first overt episode was 10% in Child A and 25% in Child B. [[Vilstrup2014HE,Lv2024CHE,Tapper2020OHE]]

GradeGroupDescription (AASLD/EASL 2014, Table 2)
MinimalCovertAbnormal psychometric or neurophysiological tests "without clinical evidence of mental change".
Grade ICovert"Trivial lack of awareness", euphoria or anxiety, shortened attention span, impaired addition or subtraction, altered sleep rhythm. Still oriented in time and space.
Grade IIOvertLethargy or apathy, disorientation for time, obvious personality change, inappropriate behaviour, dyspraxia, asterixis (the "liver flap", a flapping tremor). [[Lui2026Asterixis]] Operative criterion: at least three of day of the month, day of the week, month, season or year are wrong.
Grade IIIOvertSomnolence to semistupor, responsive to stimuli, confused, gross disorientation, bizarre behaviour. Also disoriented for space: at least three of country, state or region, city or place are wrong.
Grade IVOvertComa: "does not respond even to painful stimuli".

Quoted from the AASLD/EASL 2014 guideline, which calls the West Haven criteria the gold standard while noting "limited interobserver reliability, especially for grade I HE". HE is also classified by cause (type A acute liver failure, type B mainly bypass, type C cirrhosis), course (episodic, recurrent, persistent) and whether it was precipitated, as in the guideline's example: "Type C, Grade 3, Recurrent, Precipitated (by urinary tract infection)". HE is diagnosed by excluding other causes of brain dysfunction. [[Vilstrup2014HE]]

How lactulose and rifaximin are used

Toggle a treatment · schematic gut

The trials · per 100 people

Guideline statements, quoted as published (AASLD/EASL 2014), not instructions:

  • "An episode of OHE (whether spontaneous or precipitated) should be actively treated."
  • "Lactulose is the first choice for treatment of episodic OHE": "25 mL of lactulose syrup every 12 hours until at least two soft or loose bowel movements per day are produced", then "titrated to maintain two to three bowel movements per day".
  • "Lactulose is recommended for prevention of recurrent episodes of HE after the initial episode."
  • "Rifaximin as an add-on to lactulose is recommended for prevention of recurrent episodes of HE after the second episode."
  • "Primary prophylaxis for prevention of episodes of OHE is not required, except in patients with cirrhosis with a known high risk to develop HE."
  • "Recurrent intractable OHE, together with liver failure, is an indication for LT [liver transplantation]."

[[Vilstrup2014HE]]

The guideline warns that too much lactulose can cause "aspiration, dehydration, hypernatremia, and severe perianal skin irritation, and overuse can even precipitate HE". The US label (Enulose) adjusts the dose "every day or two to produce 2 or 3 soft stools daily" and reports lower blood ammonia "by 25 to 50%". The US rifaximin label (Xifaxan) is for "reduction in risk of overt hepatic encephalopathy (HE) recurrence in adults", "550 mg taken orally two times a day", with caution in "severe (Child-Pugh Class C) hepatic impairment". [[Vilstrup2014HE,FDA-Enulose,FDA-Xifaxan]]

Across 38 trials (1,828 people), lactulose and lactitol improved HE (RR 0.63) and lowered mortality in overt HE (RR 0.36). Rifaximin added to lactulose for an acute episode gave complete reversal in 76% vs 50.8% in one trial of 120. [[Gluud2016NAD,Sharma2013RifaxLact]] Early evidence In a 50-person single-centre trial, a polyethylene glycol bowel prep improved HE grade at 24 hours more often than lactulose (91% vs 52%). [[Rahimi2014HELP]]

Everyday life with HE

Covert HE

Driving

Drivers with minimal HE on an inhibitory control test had more crashes in the year before (17% vs 3% by transport records; relative risk 5.77) and more crashes or violations over the next year (22% vs 7%). The guideline says a covert HE diagnosis "does not automatically mean that the affected subject is a dangerous driver", that doctors should counsel on the risks, and that "often, the safest advice is to stop driving until the responsible driving authorities have formally cleared the patient" (quoted, not advice). [[Bajaj2009Crashes,Vilstrup2014HE]] Ask your care team whether driving is safe for you.

For carers

Spotting early signs

Early changes can be small: the published grade I signs include altered sleep rhythm, shortened attention, euphoria or anxiety, and trouble with simple sums. [[Vilstrup2014HE]] The LiverHope programme teaches carers to look for early signs of HE and infection and to call the nurse or go to the emergency department; HE education about giving and adjusting medicines can ease the load on carers, whose burden is higher after HE. [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope,Rogal2022AASLD,Bajaj2011Burden]] New confusion or drowsiness needs urgent care.

Food

Protein is not cut

In a trial of 30 people admitted with HE, normal protein did as well as a low-protein diet, which caused more protein breakdown. EASL 2019: "Avoid protein restriction in patients with HE"; AASLD 2021: 1.2–1.5 g/kg/day "is safe, does not worsen HE" (quoted, not instructions). Muscle is a second place the body can clear ammonia, which is why muscle loss raises HE risk. [[Cordoba2004,EASL2019Nutrition,Lai2021AASLD,Rose2020]] More in Nutrition, muscle & medicine safety.

Tests

Is a blood ammonia level needed?

The guideline: "High blood-ammonia levels alone do not add any diagnostic, staging, or prognostic value in HE patients with CLD". A normal level in someone with overt HE puts the diagnosis "in question". [[Vilstrup2014HE]]

Falls

Falls and medicines

40% of outpatients with minimal HE reported a fall in the prior year, against 12.9% without (adjusted OR 2.91). Opioids (HE in 3.3% with none, 6.3% with chronic prescriptions), long-acting or high-dose benzodiazepines and proton pump inhibitors (adjusted HR 1.36) have all been linked to HE. [[Roman2011Falls,Moon2020Opioids,Lee2014BZD,Dam2016PPI]]

Early evidence

Fully reversible?

HE has been seen as fully reversible after transplant, but evidence of neuronal cell death and lasting deficits after overt episodes "fundamentally changes our understanding of HE as a reversible syndrome". [[Rose2020]]

Outlook

What the numbers mean

After a first acute episode, 42% of one Barcelona cohort were alive at 1 year and 23% at 3 years, and the authors said such people "should be considered as potential candidates" for transplant. In a Danish cohort with alcohol-related cirrhosis, 1-year mortality was 64% after HE vs 17% without complications. These are population statistics from older cohorts, not predictions for one person. [[Bustamante1999,Jepsen2010]] See Liver transplant.

Support

Palliative care alongside treatment

AASLD describes palliative care as multidisciplinary specialised care for the physical, spiritual and psychosocial needs of people with serious illness and their carers, which can be considered at any stage of cirrhosis alongside treatment of the liver disease. Near the end of life, HE treatment can be shaped around the person's goals. [[Rogal2022AASLD]] See Living with cirrhosis.

Doses and guideline statements on this page are quoted as published (AASLD/EASL, US labels), never as instructions. HE can happen in cirrhosis of any cause: MASLD, alcohol-related liver disease, viral hepatitis, autoimmune and genetic diseases.
