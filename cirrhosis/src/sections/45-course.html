Compensated to decompensated: the course

Cirrhosis is not one state but a ladder of stages, from compensated without varices to further decompensation, with acute-on-chronic liver failure able to strike from any rung. Climb it, then switch on Cause removed to see the evidence for climbing back down. [[DAmico2014APT,DAmico2018JHep,deFranchis2022BavenoVII]]

Urgent careVomiting blood, black or tarry stools, new confusion or drowsiness, or fever or belly pain with a swollen abdomen need urgent care: call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department.

Removing or suppressing the cause: SVR for hepatitis C, HBV suppression, long-term abstinence from alcohol. [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]]

Population statistics from cohorts, not a forecast for any one person. People do not always move up the stairs in order. [[DAmico2014APT,DAmico2018JHep]]

What counts as decompensation, as Baveno VII defines it

StageBaveno VII definition (as published, summarised)
CompensatedNo present or past complications. Split into two stages by the absence or presence of clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH); people with CSPH are at higher risk of decompensation.
Decompensation
  • overt ascites (or pleural effusion with SAAG >1.1 g/dL)
  • overt hepatic encephalopathy (West Haven grade II or higher)
  • variceal bleeding
Jaundice alone can be a first sign; whether it counts as true decompensation needs more research.
Further decompensationA stage with even higher mortality: a second portal hypertension-driven event or jaundice, recurrent bleeding, recurrent ascites (3 or more large-volume paracenteses within 1 year), recurrent encephalopathy, or SBP or HRS-AKI.
RecompensationAll three of: the cause removed, suppressed or cured; ascites resolved off diuretics, encephalopathy off lactulose/rifaximin and no recurrent variceal bleeding for at least 12 months; stable improvement in albumin, INR and bilirubin. Resolution of ascites on diuretics or after TIPS, without these, is not recompensation.

Source: Baveno VII consensus. [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]] Triggers of decompensation include bacterial infections, alcohol-related hepatitis, acute hepatitis A or E, HBV flares, drug-induced liver injury, liver cancer and major surgery. [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]] In decompensated hepatitis C cirrhosis, ascites was the most common first event (48%), then bleeding (32.5%), severe infection (14.5%) and encephalopathy (5%). [[Planas2004]]

Acute-on-chronic liver failure, in 100 people

EASL describes ACLF as a severe form of acutely decompensated cirrhosis with organ failure and high short-term mortality, driven by an excessive inflammatory response to triggers such as infection with sepsis or severe alcohol-related hepatitis. [[EASL2023ACLF]] The CANONIC study first defined it in 1,343 people admitted with acute decompensation. Living through the stages

What it feels like

When decompensation comes

The step is usually visible: a belly swelling with fluid, confusion or sleepiness, vomiting blood or passing black stools, or yellowing of the skin and eyes. [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII,Tapper2023JAMA]] For about 40% of people this is how cirrhosis is first found. [[Tapper2023JAMA]] Hospital admissions, a poorer quality of life and higher mortality follow, which is why the earlier, silent phase is the time to act. [[Gines2021Lancet]]

Established

Why stopping the cause matters

Removing or suppressing the cause lowers portal pressure in most people and substantially reduces the risk of decompensation. Weight, diabetes and alcohol still push the disease on and need attention too. [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]] In hepatitis C with advanced fibrosis, 10-year all-cause mortality was 8.9% after SVR against 26.0% without. [[vanderMeer2012]] Across 7 cohorts of alcohol-related cirrhosis, abstinence went with better survival from 1.5 years on (5-year HR 0.63). [[Xie2014HepatolRes]] See hepatitis B and C and alcohol-related liver disease.

Prognosis talk

Numbers for groups, not for you

In England, people diagnosed as outpatients had average survival of 84% at 1 year and 66% at 5 years; after an emergency liver admission, 55% and 31%. [[Ratib2014]] These are averages over 5,118 people diagnosed in 1998–2009, and an emergency admission worsened the outlook independently of stage (HR 2.78). [[Ratib2014]] A care team can say more about one person's outlook, and talking about palliative care, for symptoms and planning, can sit alongside active treatment, including transplant assessment. See Living with cirrhosis and Liver transplant.

Controversy

Is bleeding or ascites the worse first event?

The classic 4-stage model put variceal bleeding (about 57% 1-year mortality) above ascites (about 20%). A German cohort found ascites separated risk better, and D'Amico's later 5-stage model moved bleeding alone below first non-bleeding decompensation. The newer view now predominates. [[Zipprich2012,DAmico2006,DAmico2014APT]]

The staircase is a simplification. D'Amico's later review describes compensated, decompensated and late decompensated clinical states that people do not pass through in a fixed order, with outcomes such as bleeding, ascites, cancer and death competing to happen first. [[DAmico2018JHep]] About 1% to 4% of people with cirrhosis develop liver cancer each year: see Liver cancer surveillance. [[Tapper2023JAMA]]
