Cards tagged memory aid are short phrases written for this guide, or used in teaching, to hold a published fact in mind. They are not official groupings from AASLD, EASL, Baveno or a drug label, and none replaces the source; the one tagged Baveno VII uses that consensus's own name. Doses, albumin amounts and thresholds are quoted as published, not as instructions.

Mnemonic in motion: the ABCDE of Child–Pugh

Child–Pugh adds five measures, each worth 1 to 3 points, into a total from 5 to 15 and a class of A, B or C [[Costa2023ChildB,InfanteRivard1987]]. As a memory aid, the letters A to E hold the five: albumin, bilirubin, clotting, distension (ascites) and encephalopathy. Play the animation to watch an example patient's points fill in, the 1973 swap that put clotting in, the two items that rest on bedside judgement, and the total landing in a class. Example points are illustrative, not from a real patient. ABCDE is a memory aid, not the published layout. A teaching tool, never for diagnosis, prognosis or transplant decisions. [[Costa2023ChildB,Pugh1973,Johnson2015ALBI]]

These cards are for learning, not for working out your own stage, score or treatment. Vomiting blood, black or tarry stools, new confusion or drowsiness, or fever or belly pain with a swollen abdomen need urgent care: call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].

Sources

Every reference behind the Cirrhosis Companion: journal articles, systematic reviews and meta-analyses, AASLD, EASL, ACG and Baveno guidance, drug labels and trial registrations. The small numbers throughout the app point here; filter by research area or search.

Eight research passes ran in parallel, one per area: epidemiology, burden, natural history and history; liver biology, fibrosis and regression; alcohol-related liver disease and MASLD; viral hepatitis and the other causes; diagnosis, staging scores and liver cancer surveillance; portal hypertension, varices, ascites, SBP and kidney injury; hepatic encephalopathy, nutrition, medicine safety and transplant; and living with cirrhosis, nursing and palliative care. Before inclusion each source was checked, where possible, against PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, ClinicalTrials.gov or openFDA, and the green tag on an entry names the service that confirmed it. Works found by more than one pass appear once, with any extra keys listed beside them.

Entries marked check are pages with no PMID or DOI, or works that could not be confirmed. They are not cited for any fact in this guide.
