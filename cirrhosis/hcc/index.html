Part 4 · Complications

Liver cancer surveillance

People with cirrhosis have a higher chance of liver cancer (hepatocellular carcinoma, HCC). An ultrasound, often with an AFP blood test, every 6 months aims to find it while it is small and easier to treat. Follow one illustrative tumour with and without that check.

Years 0–2 · the background risk

Risk builds year by year

About 1% to 4% of people with cirrhosis develop hepatocellular carcinoma each year 453. In a VA cirrhosis cohort the incidence was 1.3 per 100 patient-years, the rate the icons follow; hepatitis C, hepatitis B, obesity and low platelets were independent predictors 208. AASLD 2023 recommends surveillance once the risk reaches 1.0% a year 429.

In plain wordsPeople with cirrhosis have a higher chance of liver cancer. Each year a small number of them develop it 453.
Exam hookSurveillance threshold (AASLD 2023): HCC risk ≥1.0% a year, which covers Child–Pugh A–B cirrhosis of any cause 429.

Who is offered surveillance

AASLD 2023 recommends surveillance when HCC risk is at least 1.0% a year, a lower threshold than the traditional 1.5%. That covers Child–Pugh A–B cirrhosis of any cause, Child–Pugh C transplant candidates, and selected people with hepatitis B without cirrhosis 429. Risk differs by cause. In a classic review, the 5-year cumulative incidence of HCC in cirrhosis was:

Why every 6 months?

Surveillance interval

Every 6 months

AASLD 2023: "HCC surveillance should be performed using ultrasound and AFP at semiannual (approximately every 6 months) intervals" (Level 2, Strong Recommendation) 429. The Shanghai trial that cut HCC mortality by 37% used a 6-monthly schedule 515.

In plain wordsTwice a year is the schedule the guidelines recommend.

BCLC stages and the treatments offered

The Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) system combines the tumour, liver function and how well the person can carry out daily activities (performance status, PS) 374. Pick a stage. Treatments are listed as published; survival figures describe large groups, not any one person.

BCLC 0 · very early

A single HCC ≤2 cm without vascular invasion or spread, with preserved liver function and no cancer symptoms 374.

  • Ablation or resection: for HCC ≤2 cm they give the same survival benefit; if transplant is not feasible, ablation gives survival similar to resection 374.

Transplant and the Milan criteria

Teaching toolThis shows how the published Milan criteria are built. It is never for diagnosis or transplant decisions, which need full imaging and a transplant team.

Within the Milan criteria (as published).

Milan: a single tumour ≤5 cm, or up to 3 tumours each ≤3 cm, with no macrovascular invasion. In the original 48 recipients, 4-year survival was 75% and recurrence 8% 295. In 1556 transplants, 5-year survival was 73.3% within Milan versus 53.6% beyond 296. Wider criteria: UCSF (single tumour ≤6.5 cm, or ≤3 nodules with the largest ≤4.5 cm and total ≤8 cm) gave 75.2% 5-year survival 504; successful downstaging to Milan is accepted 429.

Making surveillance work

Underused

Only about 1 in 4

Across 29 studies of 118,799 people with cirrhosis, pooled surveillance use was 24.0%: 73.7% in specialist clinics but 8.8% in population-based cohorts, and lower with alcohol-related or NASH-related cirrhosis. All eight intervention studies (education, reminders or recall, outreach) raised it, by 9.4–63.6% 500. AASLD recommends best-practice alerts or outreach programmes 429. It is fine to ask your care team when your next scan is due.

After hepatitis C cure

Lower risk, not zero

In 22,500 US veterans treated with direct-acting antivirals, cure (SVR) lowered HCC incidence from 3.45 to 0.90 per 100 person-years, but with cirrhosis it stayed at 1.82 225. In Sicily, 1-year HCC incidence in Child–Pugh A was 2.1% with SVR versus 6.6% without 59. AASLD 2023 says risk stays raised for up to 10 years after SVR in cirrhosis, so surveillance should continue indefinitely 429. See Viral hepatitis B & C.

Hard scans

When ultrasound cannot see well

In 941 people with cirrhosis, 20.3% of ultrasounds were inadequate to exclude HCC, more often with a higher BMI, Child–Pugh B/C, alcohol-related or NASH cirrhosis 425. When visualisation is severely limited, AASLD says MRI may be considered 429. Early evidence Abbreviated MRI reached a pooled 86% sensitivity and 96% specificity 235; the GALAD blood panel 60–80% sensitivity for early HCC 217,429. AASLD does not yet recommend them routinely 429.

The blood test

AFP

At the usual surveillance cut-off of 20 ng/mL, AFP has about 60% sensitivity and 90% specificity, and over 40% of HCCs have a normal AFP 429. Added to ultrasound it raised sensitivity for early HCC from 45% to 63%, with a small loss of specificity 472. A new solid lesion of 1 cm or more, or a rising AFP, leads to multiphase CT or contrast MRI, read with LI-RADS 75,429.

Established

Does surveillance save lives?

In a Shanghai trial of 18,816 people with hepatitis B or chronic hepatitis, AFP plus ultrasound every 6 months cut HCC mortality by 37% despite 58.2% adherence; resection was possible in 46.5% of screened versus 7.5% of control cancers 515. In cirrhosis the evidence is mainly cohort studies: in 47 studies surveillance was linked with early detection (OR 2.08), curative treatment (OR 2.24) and longer survival (OR 1.90) 427. There is no randomised trial in cirrhosis itself, and cohort studies are prone to lead-time bias 427.

Beyond the stage

Care alongside treatment

AASLD describes palliative care as specialised care for physical, spiritual and psychosocial needs that can be considered at any stage of cirrhosis, alongside treatment aimed at the disease 387. It is about support and comfort, and it does not mean stopping treatment. See Living with cirrhosis.

Primary liver cancer is the sixth most common cancer worldwide and the third leading cause of cancer death; HCC makes up 75–86% of cases, and men are affected 2–3 times more than women 429. Every survival number here describes large groups of people and cannot predict what will happen to any one person. Earlier: portal hypertension, which also decides whether some cancer treatments are safe.
