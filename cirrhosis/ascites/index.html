Ascites, infection & kidney injury

Why fluid gathers in the belly when the liver is scarred, how the kidneys are pulled into it, and what guidelines and trials say about salt, diuretics, drainage with albumin, TIPS, infection of the fluid and hepatorenal syndrome.

Step 1 · the pressure

Portal pressure rises

Scar and nodules make it hard for blood from the gut to pass through the liver, so pressure builds in the portal vein. Baveno VII: a hepatic venous pressure gradient (HVPG) above 5 mmHg indicates sinusoidal portal hypertension, and 10 mmHg or more is clinically significant portal hypertension. Ascites is the most common decompensating event: 5–10% of people with compensated cirrhosis develop it each year. 97,106 The pressure itself is the subject of Portal hypertension & varices.

In plain wordsScarring makes it hard for blood from the gut to get through the liver, so pressure builds up in the portal vein. 101,180
Exam hookHVPG >5 mmHg = portal hypertension; ≥10 mmHg = clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH). 97

The ascites steps, as published

The International Ascites Club set out a stepwise approach: modest salt restriction and spironolactone first, drainage plus a colloid for large (gross) ascites, and repeated drainage or TIPS for refractory ascites, with early transplant evaluation. Current EASL and Baveno VII wording is below. 97,106,311

Alone or together? In moderate ascites, spironolactone alone and spironolactone plus furosemide worked about as well (94% vs 98% response in 100 patients), but the combination needed dose reductions more often (68% vs 34%). Another 100-patient trial found fewer side effects when both were started together than when they were added in sequence (20% vs 38%), including less hyperkalaemia (4% vs 18%). 17,400 For what nurses watch during and after a drain, see Nursing care.

Infection in the fluid: spontaneous bacterial peritonitis

Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP) is infection of the ascitic fluid. It is found in 1.5–3.5% of outpatients and about 10% of people in hospital, and half of episodes are already present on admission. It can cause few symptoms, and the culture is negative in up to 60% of people with clinical SBP, so the diagnosis rests on counting neutrophils in a fluid sample. 106,120 Baveno VII says everyone admitted with decompensation should be checked for infection, including a diagnostic paracentesis. 97

What it means

Why SBP matters

Bacterial infections raise mortality in cirrhosis about four-fold (pooled OR 3.75); after SBP, median mortality across 101 studies was 31.5% at 1 month and 66.2% at 12 months. EASL notes about 20% hospital mortality even when the infection clears, and about 70% recurrence within a year. These are population statistics from older and newer cohorts. 19,106

Prevention

Antibiotics to prevent a repeat

After a first SBP, norfloxacin 400 mg/day cut 1-year recurrence from 68% to 20%. In high-risk people who had never had SBP (ascitic protein <15 g/L with advanced liver or kidney dysfunction), 1-year SBP fell from 61% to 7% and 3-month survival rose from 62% to 94%. Baveno VII limits primary prophylaxis to selected high-risk people and says rifaximin is not indicated for SBP prophylaxis. 97,155,177

Trade-off

Resistant bacteria

Multidrug-resistant bacteria caused 4% of community-acquired, 14% of healthcare-associated and 35% of hospital-acquired infections, and doubled mortality (25% vs 12%). Long-term norfloxacin was itself a risk factor (HR 2.69). 156 Infections come from immune dysfunction and gut bacteria crossing the bowel wall, and can tip a person into bleeding, low sodium, encephalopathy or kidney failure. 213

Kidney injury and hepatorenal syndrome

Acute kidney injury (AKI) affects about 20% of people in hospital with cirrhosis. Most is pre-renal (about 68%, the kind that responds to fluid), then acute tubular necrosis and hepatorenal syndrome (HRS). HRS-AKI is kidney failure from tight narrowing of the kidney's blood vessels without major structural damage, driven by the same splanchnic vasodilation shown above and by systemic inflammation. SBP is its main trigger. 106,170,179 In people with ascites and normal kidney function, the chance of HRS was 18% at 1 year and 39% at 5 years. 178

Boxed warning on the US label (Terlivaz)

Terlipressin can cause "serious or fatal respiratory failure". The label says the risk is higher with volume overload or ACLF grade 3, that it should not be started if oxygen saturation is below 90%, and that patients are monitored continuously. Its indication is "to improve kidney function in adults with hepatorenal syndrome with rapid reduction in kidney function", and people with creatinine >5 mg/dL are unlikely to benefit. Established

Albumin with a vasoconstrictor

Transplantation is the ideal treatment for HRS-AKI; medical treatment pairs a vasoconstrictor with albumin. EASL: for AKI above stage 1A without an obvious cause, or with infection, 20% albumin 1 g/kg (maximum 100 g) on two consecutive days, quoted as published. 106,179

Mixed results

How well does terlipressin work?

CONFIRM met its main goal (HRS reversal 32% vs 17%). REVERSE (196 patients) did not (19.6% vs 13.1%), and a 2008 trial of 112 patients found 34% vs 13%. In a CONFIRM analysis, respiratory failure occurred in 9.4% with ACLF grades 1–2 and 30% with ACLF grade 3; low oxygen predicted it. 48,401,501,502

Low sodium in the blood (hyponatraemia)

Low sodium (often called dilutional hyponatraemia) is common: by 5 years after a first significant ascites, 37.1% of one cohort had developed it. 356 It went with refractory ascites, encephalopathy, SBP and HRS in a study of 997 people, and on the transplant waiting list each 1 mmol/L fall between 125 and 140 raised the risk of death by 5% on top of MELD, which led to MELD-Na (see Child–Pugh & MELD). 16,233

EASL: "fluid restriction to 1,000 ml/day is recommended in the management of hypervolemic hyponatremia"; hypertonic saline is kept for severely symptomatic cases. The US tolvaptan (Samsca) label says to avoid it in underlying liver disease, including cirrhosis; in its trials, GI bleeding occurred in 10% of people with cirrhosis on tolvaptan vs 2% on placebo. 106,144 Do not change how much you drink unless your care team advises it.

Living with ascites

Every day

Daily weights

Weight is how teams see whether fluid is coming or going: EASL judges diuretic response by weight loss (furosemide is added below 2 kg a week), and refractory ascites is defined partly by losing less than 0.8 kg over 4 days. 106 The LiverHope nursing programme teaches people to weigh themselves and to call when weight rises by more than 500 g a day for at least 3 days in a row (quoted as published). 122 Your own team will tell you what to watch for.

Medicine safety

Why NSAIDs are avoided

Ibuprofen-type painkillers (NSAIDs) can harm the kidneys and cause bleeding in people with cirrhosis. A prescribing review: NSAIDs "should be used cautiously (or not at all) in advanced cirrhosis", and drugs that can precipitate renal failure, GI bleeding, SBP and encephalopathy "should be identified and avoided". AASLD palliative guidance: "Systemic NSAIDs should be avoided". The US ibuprofen label tells people with liver cirrhosis to ask a doctor before use. 126,264,387 More in Nutrition, muscle & medicine safety.

Sodium

Low sodium: what to know

About 1 in 5 people with cirrhosis have sodium of 130 mmol/L or less, and it predicts outcome beyond MELD. EASL stops diuretics below 125 mmol/L. Low sodium can cause encephalopathy on its own and can also set off an episode of hepatic encephalopathy. 16,106,233,485

Early evidence

Long-term albumin infusions?

ANSWER (431 outpatients with ascites): 18-month survival 77% with weekly albumin vs 66% with standard care (HR 0.62). ATTIRE (777 inpatients) and a midodrine-plus-albumin waitlist trial found no benefit, and volume overload is a recognised harm. Baveno VII: "a formal recommendation cannot be given until further data become available". 61,77,97,432,466

Outlook

What the numbers mean

EASL gives about 40% mortality at 1 year and 50% at 2 years after ascites appears; a hospital cohort after a first significant ascites found 85% alive at 1 year and 56.5% at 5 years. These describe large groups, cannot predict one person's course, and care improves: at one French centre, in-hospital mortality after variceal bleeding fell from 42.6% in 1980 to 14.5% in 2000. 62,106,356 Transplant is discussed in Liver transplant.

Ask early

A drain in hospital

In 17,711 US admissions with ascites, only 61% had a paracentesis, and those who did had lower in-hospital mortality (6.5% vs 8.5%; observational). 340 LiverHope teaches early signs of infection: fever, abdominal pain, early signs of encephalopathy, painful urination and breathing symptoms, with a call to the nurse or, if that is not possible, a visit to the emergency department. 122
