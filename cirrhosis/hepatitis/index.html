Part 2 · Causes

Viral hepatitis B & C

Two blood-borne viruses that scar the liver slowly over decades. Hepatitis B can be prevented by a vaccine and kept quiet with tablets, and the scar can then recede. Hepatitis C can now be cured with a short course of tablets. Follow both side by side.

Two viruses

Who has hepatitis B and C

Hepatitis B and C were still the main causes of the global cirrhosis burden in GBD 2017, especially in low-income countries, although NASH (now MASH) was expected to overtake them "in the near future" 173. Modelling estimates 257.5 million people living with hepatitis B in 2022 (3.2%), of whom 36.0 million were diagnosed and only 6.8 million of the 83.3 million eligible were treated 365. For hepatitis C it estimates 56.8 million viraemic infections in 2020, down from 63.6 million in 2015 364. Estimates differ by method: a 2023 seminar gives 296 million with chronic hepatitis B in 2019 216.

In plain wordsHepatitis B often passes from mother to baby at birth, and a vaccine given at birth protects babies 216,464. Hepatitis C spreads through blood: unsafe injection drug use and unsterile medical procedures are the major risk factors 293.
Exam hookMost people with hepatitis B are undiagnosed: 36.0 million of 257.5 million in 2022 365.

Prevention, hepatitis D and safety

Vaccine

A vaccine that prevents a cancer

Taiwan began universal hepatitis B vaccination in July 1984. Liver cancer in children aged 6–14 fell from 0.70 to 0.36 per 100,000 a year 66. After 20 years, people vaccinated at birth had a relative risk of liver cancer of 0.31 against unvaccinated birth cohorts. Risk stayed higher with fewer than three doses 67.

Mother to baby

Protecting newborns

In a trial of 200 HBeAg-positive mothers with high viral load, tenofovir DF from 30–32 weeks of pregnancy lowered transmission to the baby from 18% to 5%. All babies also had immunoprophylaxis 348. Worldwide, 85% of infants get three vaccine doses, but only 46% get a timely birth dose, and only 3% of mothers with a high viral load receive antivirals to prevent transmission 365.

Hepatitis D

Delta: the most aggressive

Hepatitis D virus needs hepatitis B to complete its life cycle. EASL calls it the most aggressive hepatitis virus, with rapid progression to cirrhosis, decompensation and HCC 110. A meta-analysis estimated antibodies in 4.5% of HBsAg-positive people 444. Later adjusted estimates were substantially lower, so how common it is remains unresolved 366.

Bulevirtide (Hepcludex) won FDA accelerated approval on 22 May 2026. The label states that clinical outcome benefit has not been established, and it carries a boxed warning about flares after stopping 135. In MYR301, 45–48% had a combined response at week 48, against 2% of controls 496. An AASLD expert opinion published in November 2026 says everyone who is HBsAg-positive should be tested at least once for HDV antibody, with an HDV RNA test to confirm active infection. It says all adults with chronic HDV and compensated liver disease should be considered for bulevirtide-based treatment, and suggests HCC surveillance imaging every 6 months 271.

Boxed warning

Hepatitis B can wake up during hepatitis C cure

Epclusa, Mavyret and Harvoni carry a boxed warning about hepatitis B reactivation in people with both viruses. The labels say to test everyone for current or prior hepatitis B before starting 132,134,140. An FDA review found 29 reports of reactivation during DAA therapy between 2013 and 2016. Two people died and one needed a transplant 37.

After cure

Lower risk, not zero

In Sicily, one-year HCC incidence after DAAs was 2.1% with cure versus 6.6% without in Child–Pugh A, and 7.8% versus 12.4% in Child–Pugh B 59. Kanwal and colleagues found no evidence that DAAs promote HCC 226. AASLD 2023 says HCC risk stays raised for up to 10 years after SVR in hepatitis C cirrhosis, so surveillance continues indefinitely in that group 434. See Liver cancer surveillance.

Alcohol and HBV

Other things that add up

AASLD 2018 links more than 7 drinks a week for women, or more than 14 for men, to a higher risk of cirrhosis and HCC in hepatitis B. It calls abstinence or minimal intake "the conservative approach" 464. In hepatitis C, drinking 50 g a day or more speeds scarring 371. Scar can recede once the cause is controlled; see From injury to scar.
