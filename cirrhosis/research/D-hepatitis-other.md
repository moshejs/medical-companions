# D · Causes II: viral hepatitis and other causes

Pass date: 2026-11-04. Modules served: hepatitis, othercauses.

This pass covers the viral causes of cirrhosis (hepatitis B, C and D) and the less common causes: haemochromatosis, Wilson disease, autoimmune hepatitis, primary biliary cholangitis, primary sclerosing cholangitis, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, drug-induced liver injury, congestive and Fontan-associated liver disease, and cryptogenic cirrhosis. Alcohol-related liver disease and MASLD belong to pass C. Burden, staging, HCC surveillance and transplant belong to passes A, E and G. They appear here only where a cause-specific number needs them.

(2) Drugs@FDA lists Ocaliva (obeticholic acid, NDA207999) as "Discontinued", and no current Ocaliva label is left in the openFDA label endpoint. Language: people with cirrhosis; alcohol-related liver disease; people who inject drugs. Viral hepatitis, genetic, autoimmune, cholestatic, drug and heart-related causes are all covered here, so this pass is a reminder that cirrhosis is not only an alcohol disease. Doses and guideline statements are quoted as published. They are not instructions. Survival and risk figures are population statistics and do not predict what will happen to any one person.

## Findings

### hepatitis · Why viral hepatitis matters for cirrhosis
- Cirrhosis can result from many causes: obesity and MASLD, high alcohol intake, hepatitis B or C, autoimmune and cholestatic diseases, and iron or copper overload [[Gines2021Lancet]].
- In GBD 2017, cirrhosis caused more than 1.32 million deaths worldwide (95% UI 1.27–1.45 million), up from fewer than 899,000 in 1990. That was 2.4% of all deaths [[GBD2020Cirrhosis]].
- GBD 2017 estimated 112 million prevalent cases of compensated cirrhosis and 10.6 million of decompensated cirrhosis worldwide in 2017 [[GBD2020Cirrhosis]].
- GBD 2017 concluded that, although effective prevention and treatment exist, hepatitis B and C were still the main causes of cirrhosis burden worldwide, especially in low-income countries. It expected NASH (now MASH) to overtake them "in the near future" [[GBD2020Cirrhosis]].
- In GBD 2017, Egypt had the highest age-standardised cirrhosis death rate of any country in every year since 1990: 103.3 per 100,000 in 2017, against 3.7 in Singapore, the lowest [[GBD2020Cirrhosis]].

### hepatitis · Hepatitis B: how many people, and the elimination goal
- The Polaris modelling study estimated a global HBsAg prevalence of 3.2% (95% UI 2.7–4.0) in 2022, which is 257.5 million people (216.6–316.4 million) [[Polaris2023HBV]].
- Of those 257.5 million, an estimated 36.0 million were diagnosed. Only 6.8 million of the estimated 83.3 million eligible for treatment were receiving it [[Polaris2023HBV]].
- Prevalence among children aged 5 years or younger was 0.7% (0.6–1.0), or 5.6 million children, in 2022 [[Polaris2023HBV]].
- In the most recent data, 85% of infants had three doses of hepatitis B vaccine before age 1. Only 46% had a timely birth dose, and only 3% of mothers with a high viral load had antiviral treatment to prevent mother-to-child transmission [[Polaris2023HBV]].
- Estimates of how many people live with hepatitis B differ by method. Polaris gives 257.5 million in 2022 [[Polaris2023HBV]]. A 2023 Lancet seminar gives 296 million chronically infected and 820,000 deaths in 2019 [[Jeng2023Lancet]]. EASL 2025 says "over 250 million" [[EASL2025HBV]].
- The 2016 World Health Assembly endorsed eliminating hepatitis B as a public health threat by 2030. Polaris concluded that the targets "remain out of reach for many countries" under current frameworks [[Polaris2023HBV]].
- The 2024 Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology Commission update found that the 20 most-burdened countries carry more than 75% of the global viral hepatitis burden. Elimination policy had improved in 14 of those 20 since 2018 [[Cooke2024Commission]].
- Universal infant immunisation, including the birth dose, is described as the most effective way to prevent chronic HBV infection [[Jeng2023Lancet]].

### hepatitis · Hepatitis B: becoming chronic, phases and natural history
- The risk of chronic infection after acute HBV exposure depends on age at infection. It is 90% in newborns of HBeAg-positive mothers, 25–30% in infants and children under 5, and less than 5% in adults (AASLD 2018) [[Terrault2018AASLD]].
- Chronic hepatitis B typically passes through phases, each of which may last decades [[Yuen2018NRDP]].
- There is an early replicative phase (HBeAg-positive chronic hepatitis), then a low- or non-replicative phase after HBeAg seroconversion (the inactive carrier state). HBeAg-negative chronic hepatitis then develops at 1–3 per 100 person-years after seroconversion [[Fattovich2008]].
- Cirrhosis incidence appears to be about 2-fold higher in HBeAg-negative than in HBeAg-positive chronic hepatitis [[Fattovich2008]].
- In people with HBV cirrhosis, the 5-year cumulative HCC risk is 17% in East Asia and 10% in Western Europe and the US. The 5-year liver-related death rate is 15% in Europe and 14% in East Asia [[Fattovich2008]].
- AASLD 2018 notes that people with inactive chronic hepatitis B generally have HBV DNA below 2,000 IU/mL, and people with immune-active disease above 20,000 IU/mL. It calls the 20,000 cut-off arbitrary and stresses serial monitoring [[Terrault2018AASLD]].
- For managing chronic hepatitis B, AASLD 2018 recommends ALT upper limits of normal of 35 U/L for men and 25 U/L for women, rather than local laboratory ranges [[Terrault2018AASLD]].
- AASLD 2018 links more than 7 drinks a week for women, or more than 14 for men, to a higher risk of cirrhosis and HCC in hepatitis B. It calls abstinence or minimal intake "the conservative approach" [[Terrault2018AASLD]].
- An elevated serum HBV DNA level is the main risk factor for disease progression in chronic HBV infection [[Seto2018Lancet]].

### hepatitis · REVEAL-HBV: the viral-load dose–response
- REVEAL-HBV (Taiwan) followed 3,582 untreated people with hepatitis B for a mean of 11 years (40,038 person-years). 365 of them developed cirrhosis, diagnosed by ultrasound [[Iloeje2006]].
- The cumulative incidence of cirrhosis rose with baseline HBV DNA, from 4.5% at <300 copies/mL to 36.2% at ≥10^6 copies/mL [[Iloeje2006]].
- After adjustment for HBeAg and ALT, the relative risks of cirrhosis were 2.5 (95% CI 1.6–3.8) for HBV DNA 10^4 to <10^5, 5.6 (3.7–8.5) for 10^5 to <10^6 and 6.5 (4.1–10.2) for ≥10^6 copies/mL [[Iloeje2006]].
- In the companion HCC analysis (3,653 people aged 30–65, 41,779 person-years), HCC incidence rose from 108 per 100,000 person-years at HBV DNA <300 copies/mL to 1,152 per 100,000 at ≥1 million copies/mL [[Chen2006JAMA]].
- The matching cumulative HCC incidence was 1.3% at <300 copies/mL and 14.9% at ≥1 million copies/mL, over a mean of 11.4 years [[Chen2006JAMA]].
- The authors judged HBV DNA ≥10,000 copies/mL a strong HCC predictor independent of HBeAg, ALT and cirrhosis. The gradient was clearest in people who were HBeAg-negative, had normal ALT and had no cirrhosis [[Chen2006JAMA]].

### hepatitis · Hepatitis B treatment: suppression, regression, fewer complications
- Nucleos(t)ide analogues and pegylated interferon can prevent cirrhosis and HCC, but they do not eradicate HBV and rarely clear HBsAg [[Jeng2023Lancet]].
- AASLD 2018 lists entecavir, tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF), tenofovir alafenamide (TAF) and peginterferon as preferred HBV therapies. It suggests no preference between entecavir and tenofovir [[Terrault2018AASLD]].
- In the AASLD systematic review (73 studies, 59 reporting clinical outcomes), moderate-quality evidence showed that antiviral therapy in immune-active chronic HBV lowers the risk of cirrhosis, decompensation and HCC [[Lok2016]].
- In the lamivudine RCT of 651 people with HBV and advanced fibrosis or cirrhosis (98% Asian), disease progression occurred in 7.8% on lamivudine versus 17.7% on placebo (HR 0.45; P=0.001). The trial stopped early at a median of 32.4 months [[Liaw2004NEJM]].
- In that trial, HCC occurred in 3.9% versus 7.4% (HR 0.49; P=0.047). YMDD resistance mutations developed in 49% of people on lamivudine [[Liaw2004NEJM]].
- In the 5-year tenofovir DF study, 348 people had paired biopsies. 87% showed histological improvement and 51% showed fibrosis regression at week 240 [[Marcellin2013]].
- Of the 96 people (28%) with cirrhosis (Ishak 5–6) at baseline, 71 (74%) no longer had cirrhosis at year 5. Only 3 of 252 people without cirrhosis progressed to it (Established) [[Marcellin2013]].
- In the long-term entecavir cohort (57 evaluable people, median biopsy at 6 years), all had HBV DNA <300 copies/mL. 96% had histological improvement and 88% improved by ≥1 Ishak fibrosis point, including all 10 who started with advanced fibrosis or cirrhosis [[Chang2010ETV]].
- For decompensated HBV cirrhosis, AASLD 2018 states that people should be referred for liver transplant consideration while antiviral therapy is started at the same time. It recommends indefinite therapy and notes that peginterferon is contraindicated [[Terrault2018AASLD]].
- AASLD 2018 states that HCC risk persists despite antiviral suppression, so surveillance should continue in people at risk, including those with treated decompensated cirrhosis [[Terrault2018AASLD]].
- PAGE-B (age, sex, platelets) predicted 5-year HCC risk in 1,815 Caucasian adults on entecavir or tenofovir (c-index 0.82). Scores ≤9, 10–17 and ≥18 had 5-year HCC rates of 0%, 3% and 17% in the derivation set [[Papatheodoridis2016PAGEB]].

### hepatitis · Hepatitis B drug labels (US, quoted as published)
- Baraclude (entecavir) label: 0.5 mg once daily for nucleoside-naïve adults (≥16 years) with compensated liver disease, and 1 mg once daily for decompensated liver disease, on an empty stomach [[FDA-Baraclude]].
- Viread (tenofovir disoproxil fumarate) label: one 300 mg tablet once daily for adults and children weighing at least 35 kg. Kidney function tests are listed before and during use [[FDA-Viread]].
- Vemlidy (tenofovir alafenamide) label: 25 mg once daily with food. The label says it is not recommended in decompensated (Child–Pugh B or C) hepatic impairment [[FDA-Vemlidy]].
- All three HBV labels carry a boxed warning that stopping anti-hepatitis B therapy can cause severe acute exacerbations of hepatitis B, with close monitoring for at least several months after stopping [[FDA-Baraclude,FDA-Viread,FDA-Vemlidy]].

### hepatitis · Preventing hepatitis B: vaccine and mother-to-child transmission
- Taiwan began universal hepatitis B vaccination in July 1984. Average annual HCC incidence in children aged 6–14 fell from 0.70 per 100,000 (1981–86) to 0.57 (1986–90) and 0.36 (1990–94) (P<0.01) [[Chang1997NEJM]].
- In Taiwanese children aged 6–9, HCC incidence fell from 0.52 per 100,000 among those born 1974–84 to 0.13 among those born 1984–86 (P<0.001) [[Chang1997NEJM]].
- In the 20-year follow-up, people aged 6–19 who were vaccinated at birth had an age- and sex-adjusted relative risk of HCC of 0.31 compared with unvaccinated birth cohorts (64 cancers in 37.7 million person-years vs 444 in 78.5 million) [[Chang2009JNCI]].
- Among vaccinated cohorts, HCC risk was higher with fewer than three vaccine doses (OR 4.32) and with a prenatal maternal HBsAg-positive status (OR 29.50) [[Chang2009JNCI]].
- In an RCT of 200 HBeAg-positive mothers with HBV DNA >200,000 IU/mL, TDF from 30–32 weeks of gestation lowered mother-to-child transmission from 18% to 5% (intention-to-treat; P=0.007). All infants had immunoprophylaxis [[Pan2016NEJM]].

### hepatitis · Hepatitis C: how many people, and natural history
- Polaris estimated 56.8 million viraemic HCV infections worldwide on 1 January 2020 (0.7% prevalence), down from 63.6 million at the start of 2015 [[Polaris2022HCV]].
- An estimated 12.9 million people were living with a diagnosed viraemic HCV infection by the end of 2020, and 641,000 started treatment that year. Polaris judged the world not on track for 2030 elimination [[Polaris2022HCV]].
- An older Primer estimated that 64–103 million people were chronically infected. It named unsafe injection drug use and unsterile medical procedures as the major risk factors [[Manns2017NRDP]].
- In a systematic review of 31 longitudinal studies (675 people), a weighted mean of 26% (95% CI 22–29%) cleared HCV spontaneously after acute infection. Clearance was more likely in women [[Micallef2006]].
- Chronic HCV is generally slowly progressive. A 2014 review puts progression to cirrhosis at about 10–20% over 20–30 years [[Westbrook2014]].
- Estimates of progression to cirrhosis differ by setting. A meta-analysis of 111 studies (33,121 people) estimated 16% (14–19%) with cirrhosis 20 years after infection. The figure was 18% in clinical settings and 7% in non-clinical settings [[Thein2008]]. Seeff reported at least 20% within 20 years in older adult cohorts, and 5% or fewer in young cohorts, especially young women [[Seeff2002]].
- The annual stage-to-stage fibrosis transition probabilities in that meta-analysis were F0→F1 0.117, F1→F2 0.085, F2→F3 0.120 and F3→F4 0.116 [[Thein2008]].
- In 2,235 untreated French patients, the median fibrosis progression rate was 0.133 METAVIR units per year. The median estimated time to cirrhosis was 30 years [[Poynard1997]].
- That median time ranged from 13 years in men infected after age 40 to 42 years in women infected before age 40 who did not drink alcohol. Age over 40 at infection, drinking 50 g/day or more, and male sex sped progression. Genotype did not [[Poynard1997]].
- Once cirrhosis has developed in HCV, there is a 1–5% annual risk of HCC and a 3–6% annual risk of decompensation. After a first decompensation, the risk of death within a year is 15–20% [[Westbrook2014]].

### hepatitis · Hepatitis C cure: direct-acting antivirals
- Combinations of DAAs targeting NS3/4A, NS5A and NS5B cure HCV (sustained virological response 12 weeks after treatment, SVR12) in more than 90% of patients [[Manns2017NRDP]].
- ASTRAL-1 tested sofosbuvir–velpatasvir for 12 weeks in genotypes 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 (624 treated; 19% with cirrhosis). The SVR12 rate was 99% (95% CI 98 to >99), versus 0 of 116 on placebo [[Feld2015ASTRAL1]].
- EXPEDITION-1 tested glecaprevir–pibrentasvir for 12 weeks in 146 people with compensated cirrhosis (genotypes 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6). 145 (99%, 95% CI 98–100) achieved SVR12 [[Forns2017EXPEDITION1]].
- ASTRAL-4 enrolled people with decompensated (Child–Pugh B) cirrhosis. SVR12 was 83% with 12 weeks of sofosbuvir–velpatasvir, 94% with 12 weeks plus ribavirin, and 86% with 24 weeks [[Curry2015ASTRAL4]].
- AASLD-IDSA 2023 guidance stresses universal HCV screening and widens eligibility for simplified, minimal-monitoring treatment in adults [[Bhattacharya2023HCV]].
- Epclusa label (sofosbuvir 400 mg/velpatasvir 100 mg once daily): 12 weeks for people without cirrhosis or with compensated cirrhosis (Child–Pugh A). People with decompensated cirrhosis (Child–Pugh B and C) take it with ribavirin for 12 weeks [[FDA-Epclusa]].
- Mavyret label (glecaprevir 300 mg/pibrentasvir 120 mg daily, as three tablets with food): 8 weeks for treatment-naïve people without cirrhosis or with compensated cirrhosis. It is indicated only for people without cirrhosis or with compensated (Child–Pugh A) cirrhosis [[FDA-Mavyret]].
- Epclusa, Mavyret and Harvoni carry a boxed warning about HBV reactivation in people coinfected with HCV and HBV. The labels say to test everyone for current or prior HBV (HBsAg and anti-HBc) before starting, because some cases caused fulminant hepatitis, liver failure and death [[FDA-Epclusa,FDA-Mavyret,FDA-HarvoniD]].
- An FDA review of its adverse event reporting system found 29 reports of HBV reactivation during DAA therapy (November 2013 to October 2016). Two people died and one needed a liver transplant [[BersoffMatcha2017]].

### hepatitis · After HCV cure: lower mortality and lower HCC, but not zero
- In 530 people with HCV and advanced fibrosis or cirrhosis treated with interferon (median follow-up 8.4 years), 10-year all-cause mortality was 8.9% with SVR versus 26.0% without (HR 0.26; 95% CI 0.14–0.49) [[vanderMeer2012]].
- In the same cohort, the 10-year incidence of liver-related death or transplant was 1.9% with SVR versus 27.4% without (HR 0.06) [[vanderMeer2012]].
- 10-year HCC incidence was 5.1% versus 21.8%, and liver failure 2.1% versus 29.9% (Established) [[vanderMeer2012]].
- In 22,500 US veterans treated with DAAs, SVR lowered HCC incidence from 3.45 to 0.90 per 100 person-years (adjusted HR 0.28; 95% CI 0.22–0.36) [[Kanwal2017]].
- After SVR, people with cirrhosis still had an HCC incidence of 1.82 per 100 person-years, against 0.34 in people without cirrhosis (adjusted HR 4.73). The authors advised ongoing HCC surveillance for people with cirrhosis [[Kanwal2017]].
- In 2,249 Sicilian patients with HCV cirrhosis treated with DAAs, 1-year HCC incidence was 2.1% for Child–Pugh A with SVR versus 6.6% without SVR. For Child–Pugh B it was 7.8% with SVR versus 12.4% without [[Calvaruso2018]].
- In that cohort, albumin <3.5 g/dL (HR 1.77), platelets <120 × 10^9/L (HR 3.89) and no SVR (HR 3.40) independently predicted HCC [[Calvaruso2018]].
- Kanwal and colleagues found no evidence that DAAs promote HCC [[Kanwal2017]].
- A 2014 review notes that HCV eradication lowers HCC risk at every fibrosis stage but "the risk is not eliminated" [[Westbrook2014]].

### hepatitis · Hepatitis D (delta)
- HDV is a defective virus that needs HBV to complete its life cycle. EASL calls it the most aggressive hepatitis virus, with rapid and frequent progression to cirrhosis, decompensation and HCC [[EASL2023HDV]].
- A meta-analysis of 282 studies (120,293 HBsAg-positive people from 95 countries) estimated anti-HDV prevalence at 4.5% (3.6–5.7) of all HBsAg-positive people and 16.4% of those attending hepatology clinics [[Stockdale2020HDV]].
- That meta-analysis estimated 12.0 million people (8.7–18.7 million; 0.16% of the world population) as anti-HDV positive. Preliminary population attributable fractions among HBsAg-positive people were 18% for cirrhosis and 20% for HCC [[Stockdale2020HDV]].
- The Polaris HDV study (25 countries) found substantially lower HDV prevalence than earlier meta-analyses had reported. It attributed the gap to earlier sampling of tertiary centres and high-risk groups, and found the highest anti-HDV prevalence (60.1%) in Mongolia [[Polaris2024HDV]].
- Chronic hepatitis D was associated with higher HCC risk than HBV alone (pooled OR 1.28; 95% CI 1.05–1.57). In prospective cohorts with no heterogeneity the OR was 2.77 (1.79–4.28) [[Alfaiate2020HDV]].
- In the MYR301 phase 3 trial, the week-48 combined response (HDV RNA undetectable or ≥2 log10 IU/mL drop, plus normal ALT) was 45% with bulevirtide 2 mg/day, 48% with 10 mg/day and 2% in the delayed-treatment control [[Wedemeyer2023MYR301]].
- In MYR301, HBsAg loss did not occur with bulevirtide by week 48, and bile acid levels rose with dose [[Wedemeyer2023MYR301]].
- The FDA approved bulevirtide (Hepcludex) on 22 May 2026 as a new molecular entity with orphan designation, under accelerated approval [[FDA-Hepcludex]].
- The Hepcludex label indication is chronic HDV in adults without cirrhosis or with compensated cirrhosis. Approval rests on HDV RNA decline and ALT normalisation, and the label states that clinical outcome benefit has not been established [[FDA-Hepcludex]].
- The Hepcludex label dose is 8.5 mg once daily by subcutaneous injection. A boxed warning covers severe hepatitis D and B flares after stopping, especially in cirrhosis, with monitoring for at least six months [[FDA-Hepcludex]].

### othercauses · Hereditary haemochromatosis (HFE)
- In HEIRS (99,711 primary-care participants in North America), C282Y homozygosity was found in 0.44% of non-Hispanic white people, which is about 1 in 227 [[Adams2005HEIRS]].
- The same study found C282Y homozygosity in 0.11% of Native Americans, 0.027% of Hispanic people, 0.014% of Black people, 0.012% of Pacific Islanders and 0.000039% of Asian people [[Adams2005HEIRS]].
- In UK Biobank (451,243 people of European descent), 2,890 (0.6%, or 1 in 156) were p.C282Y homozygous [[Pilling2019BMJ]].
- By the end of follow-up, haemochromatosis had been diagnosed in 21.7% of homozygous men and 9.8% of homozygous women. Homozygous men aged 40–70 had higher odds of liver disease (OR 4.30) [[Pilling2019BMJ]].
- In the Melbourne cohort (all 203 C282Y homozygotes followed for about 12 years), documented iron-overload-related disease occurred in 28.4% of men (95% CI 18.8–40.2) and 1.2% of women [[Allen2008NEJM]].
- Estimates of penetrance differ. A US screening study of 41,038 people estimated that "less than 1%" of homozygotes develop frank clinical haemochromatosis, although homozygotes reported more liver trouble [[Beutler2002Lancet]]. The Melbourne cohort found iron-overload disease in 28.4% of homozygous men [[Allen2008NEJM]].
- In 182 people with phenotypic haemochromatosis, cirrhosis was present in 1 of 93 with ferritin <1,000 µg/L, against 39 of 89 with ferritin >1,000 µg/L [[Morrison2003]].
- After adjustment in that study, the probability of cirrhosis was 7.4% with ferritin <1,000 µg/L and 72% with ferritin >1,000 µg/L [[Morrison2003]].
- ACG 2019 states that a serum ferritin below 1,000 ng/mL at diagnosis identifies people at low risk of advanced fibrosis. It names phlebotomy as the mainstay of therapy [[Kowdley2019ACG]].
- EASL 2022 states that in p.Cys282Tyr homozygotes, raised iron indices are enough to diagnose haemochromatosis: TSAT >45% and ferritin >200 µg/L in females, or TSAT >50% and ferritin >300 µg/L in males and postmenopausal women [[EASL2022HH]].
- EASL 2022 phlebotomy targets are ferritin <50 µg/L during induction and <100 µg/L during maintenance. People with advanced fibrosis should enter HCC screening [[EASL2022HH]].

### othercauses · Wilson disease
- Wilson disease is an autosomal recessive disorder of hepatic copper metabolism caused by mutations in ATP7B (chromosome 13q14.3). More than 200 disease-causing mutations had been described by 2003 [[Ferenci2003Leipzig]].
- Symptomatic Wilson disease is estimated at about 1 in 30,000, and symptoms most often begin between ages 5 and 35 [[Czlonkowska2018]].
- Genetic sequencing of 1,000 UK controls predicted that about 1 in 7,026 people carry two pathogenic ATP7B variants. That is far more than the 1 in 30,000 clinical prevalence, which suggests reduced penetrance and missed diagnoses [[Coffey2013]].
- Kayser–Fleischer rings (copper in Descemet's membrane) occur in almost 100% of people with neurological Wilson disease, 40–50% with hepatic disease and 20–30% of presymptomatic people. Correct identification needs slit-lamp examination by an experienced observer [[Czlonkowska2018]].
- The Leipzig score, from the 8th International Meeting on Wilson disease (2001), combines clinical signs, copper tests and mutation analysis. A total of 4 or more establishes the diagnosis, 3 makes it possible, and 2 or less makes it very unlikely [[Czlonkowska2018,Ferenci2003Leipzig]].
- The EASL-ERN 2025 guidelines recommend the Leipzig score, plus relative exchangeable copper, for diagnosis [[EASL2025Wilson]].
- EASL-ERN 2025 lists chelators (penicillamine, trientine) and zinc salts as drug therapy, with only chelators recommended for significant liver disease. Liver transplant has a defined role in Wilsonian acute liver failure [[EASL2025Wilson]].
- In CHELATE (53 stable adults), trientine tetrahydrochloride was non-inferior to penicillamine for maintenance therapy. The mean difference in non-caeruloplasmin-bound copper at 24 weeks was −9.1 µg/L (95% CI −24.2 to 6.1) [[Schilsky2022CHELATE]].
- With early diagnosis and treatment, the prognosis of Wilson disease is described as good [[Czlonkowska2018]].

### othercauses · Autoimmune hepatitis
- A meta-analysis of 37 population-based studies (18 countries) pooled a global AIH incidence of 1.28 per 100,000 person-years and a prevalence of 15.65 per 100,000 [[Hahn2023AIH]].
- Pooled AIH prevalence rose from 9.95 per 100,000 (1970–1999) to 27.91 per 100,000 (2015–2022). Incidence was higher in women, adults, North America and Oceania, and at high latitudes [[Hahn2023AIH]].
- AIH is diagnosed from raised transaminases and IgG, autoantibodies, and interface hepatitis on biopsy [[MieliVergani2018]].
- Standard treatment starts with fairly high doses of prednisone or prednisolone, tapered as azathioprine is introduced. Second-line drugs belong in specialised centres, and liver transplantation is an option for end-stage disease [[MieliVergani2018]].
- AIH can present anywhere from no symptoms to acute liver failure. EASL 2025 includes guidance on AIH-associated cirrhosis, covering portal hypertension and HCC surveillance and transplant [[EASL2025AIH]].

### othercauses · Primary biliary cholangitis
- PBC is a chronic autoimmune cholestatic disease that, untreated, leads to end-stage biliary cirrhosis. It is usually diagnosed from cholestatic liver tests plus antimitochondrial antibodies [[EASL2017PBC]].
- A 2025 meta-analysis (59 studies, 381 million people, 25 countries) pooled a global PBC prevalence of 18.1 per 100,000 and an incidence of 1.8 per 100,000 person-years. Prevalence was higher in women and at higher latitude [[Tan2026PBC]].
- In the 1991 two-year RCT (146 people), ursodiol 13–15 mg/kg/day led to treatment failure in 6 people versus 13 on placebo (P<0.01), with improvements in bilirubin, ALP and histology [[Poupon1991]].
- The US ursodiol label (URSO 250/URSO Forte) gives 13–15 mg/kg/day for adults with PBC, in two to four divided doses with food [[FDA-Urso]].
- In the GLOBAL PBC meta-analysis (4,845 patients), 84% of those with ALP ≤2.0 × ULN at 1 year survived 10 years, against 62% above it [[Lammers2014]].
- In the same analysis, 86% of those with bilirubin ≤1.0 × ULN at 1 year survived 10 years, against 41% above it [[Lammers2014]].
- In POISE (217 people with an inadequate response to or intolerance of ursodiol), obeticholic acid met the biochemical endpoint in 46% (5–10 mg) and 47% (10 mg) of people, versus 10% on placebo. Pruritus occurred in 56% and 68% versus 38% [[Nevens2016POISE]].
- In the COBALT confirmatory RCT, the composite clinical endpoint occurred in 28.6% on obeticholic acid versus 28.9% on placebo (ITT HR 1.01; 95% CI 0.68–1.51). Unblinding and crossover confounded the result [[Kowdley2025COBALT]].
- COBALT's propensity-weighted external-control comparison favoured obeticholic acid (HR 0.39; 95% CI 0.22–0.69). The randomised analysis did not confirm benefit [[Kowdley2025COBALT,Fiorucci2026OCA]].
- Drugs@FDA lists Ocaliva (obeticholic acid, first approved 27 May 2016) as Discontinued. A 2026 review describes the withdrawal of its PBC authorisations in the European Union and the United States [[FDA-Ocaliva,Fiorucci2026OCA]].
- In ELATIVE (161 people), elafibranor 80 mg (a PPAR α/δ agonist) achieved the biochemical response in 51% versus 4% on placebo at week 52. ALP normalised in 15% versus 0%. Pruritus did not differ significantly [[Kowdley2024ELATIVE]].
- In RESPONSE (193 people), seladelpar 10 mg (a PPAR-δ agonist) achieved the biochemical response in 61.7% versus 20.0% at month 12. ALP normalised in 25.0% versus 0%, and the pruritus score fell by −3.2 versus −1.7 [[Hirschfield2024RESPONSE]].
- The FDA approved Iqirvo (elafibranor) on 10 June 2024 and Livdelzi (seladelpar) on 14 August 2024 [[FDA-Iqirvo,FDA-Livdelzi]].
- Both are indicated with UDCA for an inadequate UDCA response, or alone for UDCA intolerance. Both have accelerated approval based on ALP reduction, and both labels state that improvements in survival or prevention of decompensation "have not been demonstrated" [[FDA-Iqirvo,FDA-Livdelzi]].

### othercauses · Primary sclerosing cholangitis
- PSC causes multifocal bile-duct strictures and progressive liver disease. IBD is usually present, the risk of cholangiocarcinoma and colorectal cancer is high, and most patients eventually need a liver transplant [[Karlsen2017]].
- A meta-analysis of 25 studies found IBD in 71.1% of people with PSC (95% CI 68.2–75.1%), most often ulcerative colitis (55.9%). IBD was more common in men and in large-duct PSC [[Zhang2022PSCIBD]].
- A Dutch population-based study (590 patients) found a PSC incidence of 0.5 and a point prevalence of 6.0 per 100,000 [[Boonstra2013]].
- In that study, the median survival from diagnosis to transplant or PSC-related death was 21.3 years in the population cohort, against 13.2 years in transplant-centre patients [[Boonstra2013]].
- Colorectal cancer risk was 10-fold higher than in controls with ulcerative colitis, at a median age of 39. The authors advised colonoscopic surveillance from PSC diagnosis [[Boonstra2013]].

### othercauses · Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency
- Swedish screening of 200,000 newborns found 120 with Pi Z. 14 of the 120 had prolonged obstructive jaundice, and liver tests were abnormal in many of the rest in early infancy [[Sveger1976]].
- In the Pi*ZZ genotype, mutant Z-AAT accumulates in hepatocytes and can cause progressive liver disease and fibrosis [[Strnad2022Fazirsiran]].
- Up to 4% of Europeans carry Pi*MZ. In the European Alpha-1 Liver Cohort, 10% of Pi*MZ adults versus 4% of non-carriers had liver stiffness ≥7.1 kPa (adjusted OR 4.8). AAT inclusions appeared in 63% of Pi*MZ and 97% of Pi*ZZ biopsies [[Schneider2020PiZ]].
- In an open-label phase 2 trial (16 Pi*ZZ adults with fibrosis), the RNAi drug fazirsiran reduced liver Z-AAT by a median of 83%. Fibrosis regressed in 7 of 15 people and progressed in 2 of 15 (Early evidence) [[Strnad2022Fazirsiran]].
- In the placebo-controlled phase 2 SEQUOIA trial (40 people), serum Z-AAT fell by 61%, 83% and 94% with fazirsiran 25, 100 and 200 mg at week 16 [[Clark2024SEQUOIA]].
- In SEQUOIA, liver Z-AAT fell by a median of 93%, against a 26% rise on placebo. No phase 3 outcome data were found (Early evidence) [[Clark2024SEQUOIA]].

### othercauses · Drug-induced liver injury
- In Iceland (2010–2011), the crude annual incidence of DILI, excluding paracetamol toxicity, was 19.1 per 100,000 (95% CI 15.4–23.3) [[Bjornsson2013]].
- Amoxicillin–clavulanate was the most common cause (22% of cases), at 1 in 2,350 users. Azathioprine (1 in 133) and infliximab (1 in 148) had the highest per-user risk [[Bjornsson2013]].
- ACG 2021 names antimicrobials, herbal and dietary supplements, and anticancer drugs as the most common DILI causes in the Western world. It states that DILI is a diagnosis of exclusion [[Chalasani2021ACG]].
- ACG 2021 gives DILI mortality of up to 10% when hepatocellular jaundice is present, and advises referral to a tertiary centre for progressive jaundice [[Chalasani2021ACG]].
- No specific biomarker exists for DILI. Diagnosis relies on excluding other causes, with causality scales such as CIOMS/RUCAM to support it [[Andrade2019]].

### othercauses · Congestive hepatopathy and Fontan-associated liver disease
- The Fontan circulation keeps systemic venous pressure chronically high. Up to 70,000 people may be alive with a Fontan circulation worldwide, and liver fibrosis is common and may progress [[Rychik2019Fontan]].
- A meta-analysis of 23 studies estimated cirrhosis incidence after Fontan surgery at 0.97 per 100 patient-years. At 20 years or more after surgery the incidence was 1.61 per 100 patient-years and the cumulative incidence 32.2% [[Hitawala2024Fontan]].
- HCC incidence was 0.12 per 100 patient-years, with a cumulative incidence of 3.9% at 20 years or more. Only about 70% of the HCC cases had underlying cirrhosis [[Hitawala2024Fontan]].
- Fibrosis severity correlated only weakly with time since Fontan (R=0.36). Platelet count, APRI and FIB-4 were associated with severe fibrosis, and MR and shear-wave elastography correlated strongly with biopsy [[Seol2024FALD]].

### othercauses · Cryptogenic cirrhosis
- In 70 people with cryptogenic cirrhosis, 73% had type 2 diabetes, obesity or both. The authors concluded that NASH (now MASH) plays an under-recognised role (Early evidence) [[Caldwell1999]].
- Under the 2023 nomenclature, steatotic liver disease with no metabolic risk factors and no known cause is called cryptogenic steatotic liver disease [[Rinella2023MASLDD]].

### Exam hooks
- The younger the age at HBV infection, the more likely it becomes chronic: 90% in newborns of HBeAg-positive mothers, 25–30% in under-5s, under 5% in adults [[Terrault2018AASLD]].
- REVEAL-HBV: HBV DNA level predicts cirrhosis independently of HBeAg and ALT (RR 6.5 at ≥10^6 copies/mL) [[Iloeje2006]].
- Long-term tenofovir reversed cirrhosis in 74% of people with cirrhosis at baseline on biopsy at 5 years [[Marcellin2013]].
- Taiwan's 1984 universal HBV vaccination is the classic example of a vaccine preventing cancer (HCC in children) [[Chang1997NEJM]].
- HBV antivirals: stopping them can cause a severe hepatitis flare (boxed warning) [[FDA-Viread,FDA-Vemlidy,FDA-Baraclude]].
- HCV DAAs: test for HBV first, because DAAs can reactivate HBV (boxed warning) [[FDA-Epclusa,FDA-Mavyret]].
- About 1 in 4 people with acute HCV clear it without treatment [[Micallef2006]].
- About 16% of people with chronic HCV have cirrhosis 20 years after infection [[Thein2008]].
- Pangenotypic DAAs reach about 99% SVR12, including in compensated cirrhosis [[Feld2015ASTRAL1,Forns2017EXPEDITION1]].
- After SVR, people with cirrhosis still need HCC surveillance (1.82 HCC per 100 person-years) [[Kanwal2017]].
- HDV only infects people who have HBV, and is the most aggressive viral hepatitis [[EASL2023HDV]].
- In HFE haemochromatosis, ferritin <1,000 µg/L makes cirrhosis unlikely [[Morrison2003,Kowdley2019ACG]].
- C282Y homozygosity is common in people of northern European descent (about 1 in 156–227), but disease penetrance is much higher in men than in women [[Pilling2019BMJ,Adams2005HEIRS,Allen2008NEJM]].
- Wilson disease: Kayser–Fleischer rings are seen in almost all neurological cases but only 40–50% of hepatic cases. A Leipzig score of 4 or more establishes the diagnosis [[Czlonkowska2018]].
- PBC: antimitochondrial antibodies plus cholestatic liver tests; first-line UDCA 13–15 mg/kg/day (as labelled) [[EASL2017PBC,FDA-Urso]].
- Elafibranor and seladelpar are PPAR agonists with accelerated approval for PBC (2024). Obeticholic acid is now discontinued in the US [[FDA-Iqirvo,FDA-Livdelzi,FDA-Ocaliva]].
- PSC: about 70% of people also have IBD, mostly ulcerative colitis. Colorectal cancer risk is high [[Zhang2022PSCIBD,Boonstra2013]].
- Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency (Pi*ZZ) damages the liver through retained misfolded protein, not through low serum levels [[Strnad2022Fazirsiran]].
- Amoxicillin–clavulanate is the most common single cause of DILI in population data [[Bjornsson2013]].

### In plain words
- Cirrhosis has many causes. Viruses, too much iron or copper, the immune system attacking the liver, bile-duct diseases, some medicines and long-term heart problems can all lead to it, not only alcohol [[Gines2021Lancet]].
- Hepatitis B is a virus that can stay in the body for life, especially when babies catch it at birth. A vaccine given at birth protects them [[Terrault2018AASLD,Jeng2023Lancet]].
- The more hepatitis B virus there is in the blood, the higher the chance of scarring and liver cancer [[Iloeje2006,Chen2006JAMA]].
- Hepatitis B pills do not remove the virus, but they keep it quiet. In many people the liver scarring gets better over years. Do not stop them without talking to your liver team [[Marcellin2013,FDA-Viread]].
- Hepatitis C can now be cured in almost everyone with 8 to 12 weeks of tablets [[Feld2015ASTRAL1,FDA-Mavyret]].
- If you already have cirrhosis, keep having your liver cancer checks after hepatitis C is cured. The risk goes down but does not go away [[Kanwal2017]].
- Hepatitis D only happens in people who also have hepatitis B, and it can damage the liver faster [[EASL2023HDV]].
- Haemochromatosis means the body stores too much iron. Regular blood removal (like giving blood) lowers the iron and can prevent liver damage [[EASL2022HH]].
- Wilson disease means copper builds up. Medicines that remove copper work well if they are started early [[Czlonkowska2018]].
- In autoimmune hepatitis, the body's defence system attacks the liver. Steroid and immune-calming medicines usually control it [[MieliVergani2018]].
- PBC and PSC are diseases of the small and large bile ducts. Many people with PSC also have bowel inflammation (colitis) [[EASL2017PBC,Zhang2022PSCIBD]].
- Some medicines and herbal or dietary supplements can injure the liver, so tell your care team about everything you take [[Chalasani2021ACG]].
- Vomiting blood, black or tarry stools, new confusion or drowsiness, or fever or belly pain with a swollen abdomen need urgent care: call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department.

## Open questions and controversies
- How many people live with hepatitis B? Polaris models 257.5 million in 2022 [[Polaris2023HBV]]. A 2023 Lancet seminar cites 296 million in 2019 [[Jeng2023Lancet]]. The WHO 2024 report figure (254 million) was not validated here. Status: the estimates differ by method and year. Builders should show a range and name the source.
- How much HDV is there? A meta-analysis estimated 12 million people and 4.5% of HBsAg-positive people [[Stockdale2020HDV]]. A Polaris adjusted analysis found substantially lower prevalence in most of 25 countries and blamed sampling bias [[Polaris2024HDV]]. Status: unresolved. Reflex testing of HBsAg-positive people is proposed as the way to settle it.
- What share of people with HCV develop cirrhosis? Estimates run from 7% in non-clinical settings to 18% in clinical settings at 20 years [[Thein2008]], and from 10–20% over 20–30 years [[Westbrook2014]] to at least 20% within 20 years in older cohorts [[Seeff2002]]. Status: progression is non-linear and depends on age at infection, sex and alcohol [[Poynard1997]].
- What is the penetrance of HFE C282Y homozygosity? It was "less than 1%" clinical haemochromatosis in Beutler 2002 [[Beutler2002Lancet]], 28.4% iron-overload disease in men in Melbourne [[Allen2008NEJM]], and 21.7% of men diagnosed in UK Biobank [[Pilling2019BMJ]]. Status: the answer depends on the definition. Clinically significant disease is more common in men.
- Does obeticholic acid help people with PBC clinically? COBALT showed no difference in the randomised analysis (HR 1.01) but benefit against external controls (HR 0.39) [[Kowdley2025COBALT]]. The authorisations have since been withdrawn [[Fiorucci2026OCA,FDA-Ocaliva]]. Status: the drug has left the US market. Elafibranor and seladelpar also have accelerated approval on ALP, with outcome benefit not yet shown [[FDA-Iqirvo,FDA-Livdelzi]].
- Do DAAs raise HCC risk early after cure? Kanwal found no evidence that DAAs promote HCC (SVR adjusted HR 0.28) [[Kanwal2017]]. Calvaruso found that SVR lowered HCC over 14 months [[Calvaruso2018]]. Status: SVR lowers risk, but the residual risk in cirrhosis justifies surveillance.
- Bulevirtide dosing: the MYR301 arms were labelled 2 mg and 10 mg [[Wedemeyer2023MYR301]], while the US label states 8.5 mg once daily [[FDA-Hepcludex]]. Status: I did not check how the label's dose expression relates to the trial arms. The difference is flagged, not explained (unverified).
- Can fazirsiran reverse AATD liver fibrosis? Phase 2 data show large Z-AAT reductions and some fibrosis regression [[Strnad2022Fazirsiran,Clark2024SEQUOIA]]. Status: Early evidence. No phase 3 outcome data were found.
