# C · Causes I: alcohol-related liver disease and MASLD

Pass date: 2026-11-04. Modules served: alcohol, masld.

This pass covers the two commonest causes of cirrhosis in high-income countries. For alcohol, it covers the disease spectrum, dose–response and drinking pattern, genetic modifiers, alcohol-associated hepatitis (scores, steroids, early transplant), abstinence and treatment of alcohol use disorder (AUD) in people with liver disease, and US trends. For metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), it covers the 2023 nomenclature, prevalence, fibrosis as the main prognostic marker, weight loss and bariatric surgery, and the two approved MASH medicines (resmetirom and semaglutide). Cirrhosis has many causes. Viral hepatitis and genetic and autoimmune causes are pass D. Fibrosis biology is pass B, and scoring tools and liver cancer surveillance are pass E.

## Findings

### alcohol · Spectrum of alcohol-related liver disease
- ARLD progresses from alcohol-related fatty liver (steatosis) to alcohol-related steatohepatitis (inflammation). Chronic steatohepatitis can lead to fibrosis, cirrhosis and in some people hepatocellular carcinoma. Severe steatohepatitis, with or without cirrhosis, can present as alcohol-associated hepatitis, an acute syndrome with liver failure and high mortality (Established) [[Seitz2018NRDP]].
- Most people who drink more than 40 g of alcohol a day develop fatty liver, but only a subset develop more advanced disease [[Seitz2018NRDP]].
- The ACG 2018 guideline states that fatty liver (simple steatosis, usually macrovesicular) "develops in ~90% of heavy drinkers". It may appear within 2 weeks of heavy, regular drinking and "resolves rapidly following complete abstinence" [[Singal2018ACG]].
- The ACG 2018 guideline states that "only about 10–20% of chronic heavy drinkers develop severe forms such as AH or cirrhosis". It names behavioural, environmental and genetic cofactors as likely modifiers [[Singal2018ACG]].
- The ACG 2024 guideline states that people with ARLD progress faster than people with other causes of liver disease and more often present at an advanced stage [[Jophlin2024ACG]].
- "Pure" alcohol-related fatty liver is not benign. In 88 people with biopsy-proven fatty liver and no fibrosis or hepatitis, followed for a median of 10.5 years, 9 developed cirrhosis and 7 more developed fibrosis [[Teli1995Lancet]].
- In that cohort, 8 of the 9 people who developed cirrhosis were still drinking more than 40 units a week at follow-up. With continued heavy drinking, mixed macro/microvesicular fat or giant mitochondria on the first biopsy marked a 47–61% risk of progression [[Teli1995Lancet]].
- Alcohol-related cirrhosis carries a lifetime risk of hepatocellular carcinoma of about 3–10%, an annual risk of about 1% (ACG 2018). Obesity and smoking add to the risk [[Singal2018ACG]].
- The AASLD 2019 guidance uses "alcohol-associated liver diseases" in place of the older "alcoholic liver disease" [[Crabb2020AASLD]].
- The ACG 2018 guideline defines a US standard drink as about 14 g of alcohol: 12 oz of 5% beer, 5 oz of wine or 1.5 oz of 40–45% liquor. It describes heavy drinking as more than 3 drinks a day for men and more than 2 for women. Binge drinking (NIAAA) is more than 5 drinks for men and more than 4 for women within 2 hours [[Singal2018ACG]].

### alcohol · Dose–response, sex and drinking pattern
- A 2019 meta-analysis (7 cohort and 2 case-control studies; 2,629,272 participants; 5,505 cirrhosis cases) found no increased cirrhosis risk for occasional drinkers compared with long-term abstainers [[Roerecke2019Cirrhosis]].
- In that meta-analysis, one drink a day (about 12 g, the paper's standard drink) raised cirrhosis risk in women but not in men, compared with long-term abstainers. Women's risk was "consistently higher" than men's at every level [[Roerecke2019Cirrhosis]].
- Relative risk of cirrhosis for women vs long-term abstainers: RR 12.44 (95% CI 6.65–23.27) at 5–6 drinks a day, and RR 24.58 (95% CI 14.77–40.90) at 7 or more drinks a day [[Roerecke2019Cirrhosis]].
- For men, the same comparisons gave RR 3.80 (95% CI 0.85–17.02) at 5–6 drinks a day and RR 6.93 (95% CI 1.07–44.99) at 7 or more [[Roerecke2019Cirrhosis]].
- The authors describe cirrhosis risk as "increasing exponentially" with intake. Heterogeneity between studies points to other risk factors: genetics, body weight, metabolic factors and drinking patterns [[Roerecke2019Cirrhosis]].
- Copenhagen City Heart Study (13,285 adults, 12-year follow-up): alcohol-related cirrhosis occurred at 0.2% a year in men and 0.03% a year in women [[Becker1996Hepatology]].
- In the same study, the lowest risk of liver disease was at 1–6 drinks a week. Risk became significantly raised from 7–13 drinks a week in women and from 14–27 in men. Women had a higher relative risk than men at any given intake [[Becker1996Hepatology]].
- An earlier meta-analysis (17 studies) found that the same average intake carried a higher cirrhosis risk in women than in men. Alcohol had a larger effect on cirrhosis mortality than on cirrhosis morbidity [[Rehm2010Cirrhosis]].
- Danish Diet, Cancer and Health cohort (55,917 adults aged 50–64): no cases of alcohol-related cirrhosis occurred in lifetime abstainers. In men, daily drinking carried HR 3.65 (95% CI 2.39–5.55) compared with drinking 2–4 days a week [[Askgaard2015JHep]].
- In that cohort, recent intake (at ages 40–59) was linked to cirrhosis risk, but intake at ages 20–39 was not [[Askgaard2015JHep]].
- Among men drinking 14–28 drinks a week in that cohort, cirrhosis risk vs drinking under 14 a week depended on how much of the intake was wine:
  - little wine (<1% of weekly amount): HR 7.47
  - some wine (1–15%): HR 3.12
  - mostly wine (50–100%): HR 1.69 (95% CI 0.79–3.65)
  - The authors say wine "might be" linked to lower risk. This is observational (Early evidence) [[Askgaard2015JHep]].
- Meta-analysis of 5 studies at the same total intake: daily drinking vs non-daily drinking raised cirrhosis risk, RR 1.71 (95% CI 1.23–2.23) in men and 1.56 (95% CI 1.39–1.74) in women (Early evidence) [[LlamosasFalcon2023]].
- US national survey (21,925 current drinkers, 3-year follow-up): very-high-risk drinkers who dropped 1, 2 or 3 WHO drinking-risk levels had lower odds of liver disease (aOR 0.34, 0.23 and 0.17). Cutting down without stopping still helps [[Knox2018Liver]].

### alcohol · Genetic modifiers
- A genome-wide study of alcohol-related cirrhosis in people of European descent (712 cases, 1,426 controls, plus two validation cohorts) confirmed PNPLA3 rs738409 as a major risk locus (P = 1.54 × 10⁻⁴⁸). It also found TM6SF2 and MBOAT7 as new loci, all involved in lipid processing (Established) [[Buch2015NatGenet]].
- PNPLA3 rs738409[G] encodes the I148M variant. It was first linked to higher liver fat and inflammation in a multi-ethnic US study (Hispanic, African American and European American participants), and was commonest in Hispanic participants. Liver fat was more than twofold higher in GG homozygotes than in non-carriers [[Romeo2008PNPLA3]].
- The ACG 2018 guideline notes that the risk allele (rs738409) is more frequent in Hispanic populations, who are "particularly sensitive to fatty liver diseases" [[Singal2018ACG]].
- AASLD 2023 states that PNPLA3 I148M impairs the breakdown of triglyceride in lipid droplets. It names TM6SF2, MBOAT7 and loss-of-function HSD17B13 variants as other modifiers. Genetic testing "is currently not recommended, as it does not alter management" [[Rinella2023AASLD]].

### alcohol · Alcohol-associated hepatitis: definition and severity scores
- NIAAA consortium criteria for AH in trials:
  - jaundice starting within the prior 8 weeks
  - ongoing intake of more than 40 g a day (women) or 60 g a day (men) for 6 months or more, with less than 60 days of abstinence before jaundice
  - AST over 50 IU/L, AST/ALT over 1.5, and both under 400 IU/L
  - total bilirubin over 3.0 mg/dL [[Crabb2016NIAAA]]
- The same consortium notes that AST/ALT ratios below 1.5 are seen in under 2% of people with biopsy-proven AH. They recommend biopsy when the picture is atypical [[Crabb2016NIAAA]].
- In STOPAH, short-term mortality in severe AH "exceeds 30%" [[Thursz2015STOPAH]].
- Maddrey's 1978 trial (55 patients) found that prolonged prothrombin time and high bilirubin independently predicted death. This was the basis of the discriminant function [[Maddrey1978]].
- ACG 2018: severe AH is defined by Maddrey discriminant function ≥32, which predicts about 20–50% mortality over 30 days. A MELD score over 20 has been proposed as defining severe AH, with about 20% mortality [[Singal2018ACG]].
- ACG 2024 names MELD as "the most accurate score" to stratify AH severity, with a score over 20 defining severe disease. It puts 1-month mortality in severe AH at 20%–50% [[Jophlin2024ACG]].
- Global AH cohort (2,581 patients, 85 centres, 11 countries; median MELD 23.5): mortality was 20% at 28 days and 30.9% at 90 days. For 28-day mortality, AUROC ranged from 0.776 (MELD-sodium) to 0.701 (Maddrey). The authors conclude Maddrey "should no longer be used" for prognosis [[MoralesArraez2022]].
- In 73 people with AH at Mayo Clinic, MELD 21 predicted 90-day mortality with 75% sensitivity and 75% specificity [[Dunn2005MELD]].
- The Lille model combines six variables: age, renal insufficiency, albumin, prothrombin time, bilirubin, and the change in bilirubin by day 7 of steroids. Its AUROC for 6-month death was 0.89, compared with 0.62 for Child–Pugh and 0.66 for Maddrey [[Louvet2007Lille]].
- Above the Lille cutoff of 0.45 ("non-responders", about 40% of cases), 6-month survival was 25% vs 85% below it [[Louvet2007Lille]].
- ACG 2018 places the Lille score at 4–7 days of corticosteroids and takes Lille <0.45 as response [[Singal2018ACG]].

### alcohol · Alcohol-associated hepatitis: corticosteroids
- STOPAH (2×2 factorial RCT; 1,103 randomised, 1,053 analysed; severe AH) gave these 28-day mortalities:
  - placebo–placebo: 17%
  - prednisolone: 14%
  - pentoxifylline: 19%
  - both drugs: 13% [[Thursz2015STOPAH]]
- In STOPAH, prednisolone's odds ratio for 28-day mortality was 0.72 (95% CI 0.52–1.01; P = 0.06). Pentoxifylline's was 1.07 (95% CI 0.77–1.49). Neither drug changed outcomes at 90 days or 1 year [[Thursz2015STOPAH]].
- Serious infections occurred in 13% of people on prednisolone vs 7% of those not on it (P = 0.002) [[Thursz2015STOPAH]].
- Individual-patient meta-analysis of 11 RCTs (2,111 patients): corticosteroids lowered the risk of death within 28 days vs control (HR 0.64; 95% CI 0.48–0.86) and vs pentoxifylline (HR 0.64; 95% CI 0.43–0.95). There was no difference in 6-month mortality (Established) [[Louvet2018Gastro]].
- Individual-patient meta-analysis of 5 RCTs: 28-day survival was 79.97% with corticosteroids vs 65.7% without (p = 0.0005). By Lille response, 28-day survival was:
  - complete responders (Lille ≤0.16): 91.1%
  - partial responders: 79.4%
  - null responders (Lille ≥0.56): 53.3% [[Mathurin2011Gut]]
- In that analysis, steroids helped complete responders (HR 0.18) and partial responders (HR 0.38) but not null responders [[Mathurin2011Gut]].
- ACG 2018 dosing, as published: prednisolone "40 mg per day for a total duration of 4 weeks", or IV methylprednisolone 32 mg a day if oral intake is not possible. "No studies" have examined other doses or durations [[Singal2018ACG]].
- ACG 2024: corticosteroids are "the only available therapeutic with proven efficacy" in severe AH, "providing survival benefit at 1 month in 50%-60% of patients" [[Jophlin2024ACG]].

### alcohol · Early liver transplant for severe alcohol-associated hepatitis
- Before 2011, a 6-month abstinence period was usually required before transplant. People with AH not responding to medical therapy had about 30% 6-month survival, and most deaths occurred within 2 months [[Mathurin2011NEJM]].
- Franco-Belgian pilot (26 highly selected people with a first episode of severe AH not responding to treatment; median Lille 0.88): 6-month survival was 77% with early transplant vs 23% in matched controls (P < 0.001). The benefit held through 2 years (HR 6.08) [[Mathurin2011NEJM]].
- In that pilot, fewer than 2% of people admitted with severe AH were selected, and the centres used 2.9% of their grafts for this indication. Three recipients resumed drinking, at 720, 740 and 1,140 days [[Mathurin2011NEJM]].
- ACCELERATE-AH (12 US centres, 147 early transplants before 6 months of abstinence; median abstinence 55 days, median MELD-Na 39) reported survival of 94% at 1 year and 84% at 3 years. This is similar to transplant for other indications [[Lee2018Gastro]].
- Alcohol use after early transplant in ACCELERATE-AH:
  - any use: 25% at 1 year, 34% at 3 years
  - sustained use: 10% at 1 year, 17% at 3 years
  - Sustained use raised the risk of death (HR 4.59). Younger age was the only predictor of return to drinking [[Lee2018Gastro]].
- QuickTrans (19 French and Belgian centres; prospective, non-randomised) compared early transplant (n = 68) with standard transplant after 6 months of abstinence (n = 93). Relapse occurred in 34% vs 25%, so non-inferiority was not shown. High alcohol intake was more frequent after early transplant (absolute difference 16.7%) [[Louvet2022Lancet]].
- In QuickTrans, 2-year post-transplant survival was similar between early and standard transplant (HR 0.87). Two-year overall survival was much higher with early transplant than in non-eligible patients (HR 0.27) and historical controls (HR 0.21) [[Louvet2022Lancet]].
- ACG 2024 says transplant "should be considered in selected patients with AH, who are unresponsive to medical therapy and have a low risk of relapse" [[Jophlin2024ACG]].

### alcohol · Abstinence and recompensation
- ACG 2024 calls abstinence "a crucial determinant of long-term outcomes". The ARLD primer calls abstinence "the best treatment for all stages of ALD" [[Jophlin2024ACG,Seitz2018NRDP]].
- Biopsy cohort (192 people with ARLD): 5-year liver-related mortality was 13% in early/compensated disease and 43% in decompensated disease [[Lackner2017JHep]].
- In the early/compensated group, 52% already had severe fibrosis (F3/4). Their 10-year mortality was 45%, vs 0% with F0–2 (p < 0.001). Abstinence predicted survival in both early and decompensated disease [[Lackner2017JHep]].
- Meta-analysis of 7 cohorts (1,235 people with alcohol-related cirrhosis): the survival benefit of abstinence vs continued drinking reached significance from 1.5 years (HR 0.51; 95% CI 0.33–0.81). It persisted to 5 years (HR 0.63; 95% CI 0.52–0.76). At 0.5 and 1 year, the difference was not significant [[Xie2014HepatolRes]].
- Multicentre cohort (633 people with decompensated alcohol-related cirrhosis who stopped drinking; 17 centres): 31.1% achieved recompensation by Baveno VII criteria. Cumulative incidence was 12.3% at 1 year, 23.4% at 2 years and 33.8% at 5 years [[Hofer2026JHep]].
- In that cohort, stopping drinking within 1 month of decompensation raised the chance of recompensation (aSHR 2.042). Further decompensation lowered it (aSHR 0.650) [[Hofer2026JHep]].
- Recompensation was linked to lower all-cause mortality (aHR 0.255). No recompensated person who stayed abstinent died of liver causes or developed hepatocellular carcinoma [[Hofer2026JHep]].

### alcohol · Treating alcohol use disorder in people with liver disease
- ACG 2024 notes that people with ARLD are "rarely treated for AUD". It calls for integrated care with hepatology, addiction medicine and social work [[Jophlin2024ACG]].
- US veterans with cirrhosis and AUD (n = 35,682): only 14% received AUD treatment within 180 days. Pharmacotherapy was rare: 0.4% received medicine alone and 1% received medicine plus behavioural therapy [[Rogal2020Hepatology]].
- In the same veterans, AUD treatment was linked to:
  - less hepatic decompensation (6.5% vs 11.6%; AOR 0.63, 95% CI 0.52–0.76)
  - lower long-term mortality (51% vs 58%; AOR 0.87, 95% CI 0.80–0.96)
  - This is observational (Early evidence) [[Rogal2020Hepatology]].
- Mass General Brigham Biobank (9,635 people with AUD; mean follow-up 9.2 years): medicine for AUD was linked to less incident ARLD (aOR 0.37; 95% CI 0.31–0.43). In people with cirrhosis, it was linked to less hepatic decompensation (aOR 0.35; 95% CI 0.23–0.53). This is observational [[Vannier2022]].
- Baclofen RCT (84 people with AUD and cirrhosis, 12 weeks): 71% on baclofen achieved and kept abstinence vs 29% on placebo (OR 6.3; 95% CI 2.4–16.1). Cumulative abstinence averaged 62.8 vs 30.8 days, and no hepatic side effects were recorded [[Addolorato2007Lancet]].
- ACG 2018 states that of the AUD medicines (disulfiram, acamprosate, gabapentin, naltrexone, topiramate, sertraline, baclofen), "only baclofen ... has been found to be safe in patients with ALD and cirrhosis". It quotes a starting dose of 5 mg three times a day, titrated every 3–5 days. This is quoted as published, not as an instruction [[Singal2018ACG]].
- Acamprosate label: acamprosate "is not metabolized by the liver". Its pharmacokinetics "are not altered" in Child–Pugh A or B hepatic impairment. It is contraindicated in severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance ≤30 mL/min), and the dose is reduced at 30–50 mL/min [[FDA-Campral]].
- Naltrexone tablet label (no boxed warning) reports cases of hepatitis and "clinically significant liver dysfunction". It notes that pre-existing alcohol-related liver disease, viral hepatitis or other hepatotoxic drugs were often contributors. It says there "may be a higher risk of hepatocellular injury with single doses above 50 mg" [[FDA-Naltrexone]].
- VA cohort (3,285 people with cirrhosis who started naltrexone): only 2% had enzyme rises, and no case was classed as possible or probable drug-induced liver injury (RUCAM). No deaths or decompensations were attributed to naltrexone. The authors judge it safe in compensated and decompensated cirrhosis (Early evidence) [[Thompson2024JHEP]].
- Safety-net cohort (160 people prescribed naltrexone; 47 with cirrhosis, 47% of them decompensated): AST and ALT fell after naltrexone. The authors call it safe in liver disease, including compensated cirrhosis, but say "more safety data are needed" for decompensated cirrhosis [[Ayyala2022]].

### alcohol · US trends
- From 1999 to 2016, annual US cirrhosis deaths rose 65%, to 34,174. From 2009 to 2016, people aged 25–34 had the fastest rise in cirrhosis mortality (10.5% a year), "driven entirely by alcohol related liver disease" [[Tapper2018BMJ]].
- NHANES 2001–2016: overall ARLD prevalence was stable (8.8% to 8.1%), but the share with stage ≥3 fibrosis (FIB-4 >2.67) rose from 2.2% to 6.6% [[Dang2020AJG]].
- From 2007 to 2017, US adults with ARLD listed for transplant rose 63.4% (UNOS). Hospitalisations with ≥3 cirrhosis complications rose from 11.6% to 25.8% of alcohol-related cirrhosis admissions (2007–2014) [[Dang2020AJG]].
- Age-adjusted US death rates from ARLD (ICD-10 K70, ages ≥25), per 100,000:
  - men: 13.1 in 2017 to 16.9 in 2020
  - women: 5.6 in 2017 to 7.7 in 2020 [[DeutschLink2022]]
- Joinpoint analysis found one change point in February 2020. Monthly growth in ARLD deaths rose from 0.36% to 3.18% a month in men and from 0.46% to 3.8% a month in women [[DeutschLink2022]].

### masld · Nomenclature (2023)
- A modified Delphi process led by three pan-national liver associations, with the consensus conference cohosted by AASLD and EASL (236 panellists from 56 countries; consensus = 67% supermajority) renamed NAFLD as metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) [[Rinella2023MASLD]].
- In that survey, 74% felt the old names were flawed enough to change. "Nonalcoholic" and "fatty" were felt to be stigmatising by 61% and 66% of respondents [[Rinella2023MASLD]].
- "Steatotic liver disease" (SLD) is the umbrella term. MASLD requires steatosis plus at least 1 of 5 cardiometabolic risk factors. Factors named in the paper include BMI, waist circumference, blood pressure and HDL cholesterol [[Rinella2023MASLD]].
- People with steatosis, no metabolic risk factors and no known cause have "cryptogenic" SLD [[Rinella2023MASLD]].
- MASLD with steatohepatitis is called MASH (formerly NASH) [[Rinella2023MASLD]].
- MetALD is MASLD plus greater alcohol intake: 140–350 g a week for women and 210–420 g a week for men. Within MetALD there is a continuum of how much each cause contributes [[Rinella2023MASLD]].
- The panel notes that most people with ARLD have cardiometabolic risk factors [[Rinella2023MASLD]].
- The EASL–EASD–EASO 2024 guideline defines MASLD as SLD with one or more cardiometabolic risk factors and no harmful alcohol intake. Its spectrum runs from steatosis through MASH and fibrosis to cirrhosis and MASH-related HCC [[EASL2024MASLD]].
- A 2024 analysis reported that 99% of people with NAFLD meet MASLD criteria, so the natural-history data carry over [[Hagstrom2024]].
- The older AASLD NAFLD definition was ≥5% macrovesicular steatosis without another cause, in people drinking little or no alcohol (<20 g a day for women, <30 g a day for men) [[Rinella2023AASLD]].

### masld · Prevalence and who is at risk
- Meta-analysis of 92 population studies (9.4 million people; 1990–2019): global NAFLD prevalence was 30.05% (95% CI 27.88–32.32%). It rose from 25.26% (1990–2006) to 38.00% (2016–2019), a 50.4% increase [[Younossi2023Hepatology]].
- Regional prevalence in that meta-analysis:
  - Latin America: 44.37% (highest)
  - Middle East and North Africa: 36.53%
  - North America: 31.20%
  - Western Europe: 25.10% [[Younossi2023Hepatology]]
- In people with NAFLD diagnosed without biopsy, death rates per 1,000 person-years were 12.60 all-cause, 4.20 cardiac and 0.92 liver-specific. Heart disease kills more people with NAFLD than liver disease does [[Younossi2023Hepatology]].
- A second meta-analysis (72 studies, about 1.03 million people, 17 countries) estimated global prevalence at 32.4%. It rose from 25.5% (≤2005) to 37.8% (2016 or later) and was higher in men than women (39.7% vs 25.6%) [[Riazi2022]].
- Type 2 diabetes (80 studies; 49,419 people): NAFLD prevalence was 55.5% (95% CI 47.3–63.7%), NASH 37.3%, and advanced fibrosis 17.0% among those with NAFLD [[Younossi2019T2D]].
- AASLD 2023 gives the prevalence of advanced fibrosis in high-risk groups:
  - type 2 diabetes: 6–19%
  - medically complicated obesity: 4–33%
  - NAFLD with moderate alcohol use: 17%
  - first-degree relatives of a person with NAFLD cirrhosis: 18%
  - background population: 0.9–2% [[Rinella2023AASLD]]
- AASLD 2023: lean people with NAFLD are more often of Hispanic or Asian origin, partly because PNPLA3 I148M is more common in those groups [[Rinella2023AASLD]].
- A 2015 JAMA review estimated that NAFLD affects about 30% and NASH about 5% of the US population. In NASH, half of deaths are from cardiovascular disease and cancer, and cirrhosis is the third leading cause of death in NAFLD [[Rinella2015JAMA]].

### masld · Fibrosis is the main prognostic marker
- International biopsy cohort (619 people with NAFLD; median follow-up 12.6 years): fibrosis stage, "but no other histologic features", predicted outcomes. Hazard ratios for death or transplant vs stage 0:
  - F1: 1.88
  - F2: 2.89
  - F3: 3.76
  - F4: 10.9 [[Angulo2015]]
- In that cohort, liver-related events rose steeply with stage: HR 14.2 for F3 and 51.5 for F4 vs F0. Statin use was linked to lower death or transplant (HR 0.32) [[Angulo2015]].
- Meta-analysis of 5 cohorts (1,495 people; 17,452 patient-years) gave mortality rate ratios vs F0 [[Dulai2017]]:

  | Fibrosis stage | All-cause MRR | Liver-related MRR |
  |---|---|---|
  | F1 | 1.58 | 1.41 |
  | F2 | 2.52 | 9.57 |
  | F3 | 3.48 | 16.69 |
  | F4 | 6.40 | 42.30 |

- Meta-analysis of 13 studies (4,428 people), F4 vs F0 [[Taylor2020Gastro]]:
  - all-cause mortality: RR 3.42
  - liver-related mortality: RR 11.13
  - liver-related events: RR 12.78
- NASH CRN prospective cohort (1,773 adults; median 4 years): all-cause deaths per 100 person-years were 0.32 at F0–F2, 0.89 at F3 and 1.76 at F4 [[Sanyal2021NEJM]].
- In that cohort, liver complications per 100 person-years, F0–F2 vs F3 vs F4:
  - encephalopathy: 0.02 vs 0.75 vs 2.39
  - ascites: 0.04 vs 0.52 vs 1.20
  - Any decompensation raised all-cause mortality (adjusted HR 6.8) [[Sanyal2021NEJM]].
- Swedish nationwide cohort (10,568 people with biopsy-confirmed NAFLD vs 49,925 matched controls; median 14.2 years): adjusted mortality HRs vs controls were:
  - simple steatosis: 1.71
  - non-fibrotic NASH: 2.14
  - non-cirrhotic fibrosis: 2.44
  - cirrhosis: 3.79 [[Simon2021Gut]]
- In the Swedish cohort, most of the excess deaths were from extrahepatic cancer (4.5 per 1,000 person-years), then cirrhosis (2.7), cardiovascular disease (1.4) and HCC (1.2) [[Simon2021Gut]].
- Paired-biopsy meta-analysis (11 cohorts; 411 people; 2,145.5 person-years): over follow-up, 33.6% progressed, 43.1% were stable and 22.3% improved [[Singh2015CGH]].
- In that meta-analysis, starting from F0, fibrosis advanced about one stage every 14.3 years in simple steatosis (NAFL) and every 7.1 years in NASH [[Singh2015CGH]].

### masld · Lifestyle, weight loss and bariatric surgery
- 52-week lifestyle programme with paired biopsies (293 people with NASH): overall, 25% had NASH resolution and 19% had fibrosis regression. Weight loss of ≥5% gave NASH resolution in 58% [[VilarGomez2015]].
- In that programme, everyone who lost ≥10% of body weight had a lower NAS score: 90% had NASH resolution and 45% had fibrosis regression. Only 30% of participants reached ≥5% weight loss [[VilarGomez2015]].
- AASLD 2023: "Patients with clinically significant hepatic fibrosis (≥ F2) should abstain from alcohol use completely" [[Rinella2023AASLD]].
- AASLD 2023 notes that diets of different composition (low-carbohydrate, low-fat, Mediterranean, intermittent fasting) "appear comparable" for NAFLD. Exercise of at least 150 minutes a week, or more than 60 extra minutes a week, can prevent or improve NAFLD [[Rinella2023AASLD]].
- AASLD 2023: statins are "safe and recommended" for cardiovascular risk reduction across NAFLD, including compensated cirrhosis [[Rinella2023AASLD]].
- EASL–EASD–EASO 2024 advises lifestyle change (weight loss, diet, exercise, "discouraging alcohol consumption"). It also advises incretin-based therapy (for example semaglutide or tirzepatide) for type 2 diabetes or obesity where indicated, and names bariatric surgery as an option with obesity [[EASL2024MASLD]].
- Bariatric surgery cohort with repeat biopsies: NASH resolved without fibrosis worsening in 84% at 5 years. Fibrosis fell in 70.2%, and disappeared in 56% overall and in 45.5% of those with baseline bridging fibrosis [[Lassailly2020]].
- In that cohort, NASH resolution was already 84% at 1 year. Fibrosis kept falling through 5 years [[Lassailly2020]].
- SPLENDOR (1,158 people with biopsy-proven NASH and obesity; median 7 years): 10-year major adverse liver outcomes occurred in 2.3% after bariatric surgery vs 9.6% without (adjusted HR 0.12). Major adverse cardiovascular events occurred in 8.5% vs 15.7% (adjusted HR 0.30) [[Aminian2021JAMA]].
- In SPLENDOR, 0.6% of surgical patients died of surgical complications within the first year [[Aminian2021JAMA]].
- PIVENS (adults with NASH without diabetes): vitamin E improved NASH more often than placebo (43% vs 19%, P = 0.001). Pioglitazone did not meet the primary endpoint (34% vs 19%; P = 0.04, which the trial did not count as significant), and neither drug improved fibrosis [[Sanyal2010PIVENS]].

### masld · Resmetirom (Rezdiffra)
- MAESTRO-NASH (phase 3; 966 people in the primary analysis with biopsy-proven NASH and liver fibrosis; week-52 biopsy) [[Harrison2024MAESTRO]]:

  | Group | NASH resolution, no fibrosis worsening | Fibrosis improved ≥1 stage |
  |---|---|---|
  | Resmetirom 80 mg | 25.9% | 24.2% |
  | Resmetirom 100 mg | 29.9% | 25.9% |
  | Placebo | 9.7% | 14.2% |

  - P < 0.001 for both doses on both endpoints.
- In MAESTRO-NASH, LDL cholesterol changed by −13.6% (80 mg) and −16.3% (100 mg) vs +0.1% on placebo. Diarrhoea and nausea were more common on resmetirom. Serious adverse events were similar (10.9%, 12.7%, 11.5%) [[Harrison2024MAESTRO]].
- Drugs@FDA records the original approval of Rezdiffra (NDA 217785, a new molecular entity) on 14 March 2024 [[FDA-Rezdiffra]].
- The label indication (thyroid hormone receptor-beta agonist), as published: "in conjunction with diet and exercise for the treatment of adults with noncirrhotic metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) ... with moderate to advanced liver fibrosis (consistent with stages F2 to F3 fibrosis)." It is an accelerated approval, and continued approval may depend on confirmatory trials [[FDA-Rezdiffra]].
- The label says to "avoid use" in decompensated cirrhosis and in moderate to severe hepatic impairment (Child–Pugh B or C). Labelled dosing is by weight: 80 mg once daily under 100 kg and 100 mg once daily at 100 kg or more. Doses are quoted as published [[FDA-Rezdiffra]].
- The MAESTRO-NASH registry entry (NCT03900429; 1,759 enrolled) lists the trial as active, with outcomes to "reduce progression to cirrhosis and/or hepatic decompensation". Estimated primary completion is January 2028 [[NCT03900429]].
- MAESTRO-NASH-OUTCOMES (NCT05500222) tests resmetirom in well-compensated (Child–Pugh A) MASH cirrhosis, with an estimated 845 participants. It was active and not recruiting on 2026-11-04, with estimated primary completion December 2026 [[NCT05500222]].
- EASL–EASD–EASO 2024: adults with non-cirrhotic MASH and fibrosis stage ≥2 "should be considered" for resmetirom where locally approved. "No MASH-targeted pharmacotherapy can currently be recommended for the cirrhotic stage" [[EASL2024MASLD]].

### masld · GLP-1-based medicines
- Phase 2 semaglutide trial (320 people with NASH, 72 weeks): NASH resolution without fibrosis worsening occurred in 59% on 0.4 mg daily vs 17% on placebo. Fibrosis improvement did not differ significantly (43% vs 33%, P = 0.48). Mean weight loss was 13% vs 1% [[Newsome2021NEJM]].
- ESSENCE part 1 (phase 3; 800 people with MASH and moderate or advanced fibrosis; semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly) [[Sanyal2025ESSENCE]]:
  - steatohepatitis resolution without fibrosis worsening: 62.9% vs 34.3% on placebo (difference 28.7 points)
  - fibrosis reduction without worsening of steatohepatitis: 36.8% vs 22.4% (difference 14.4 points)
- In ESSENCE, mean body weight changed by −10.5% vs −2.0%, and gastrointestinal adverse events were more common on semaglutide [[Sanyal2025ESSENCE]].
- ESSENCE (NCT04822181; 1,205 enrolled) continues to a clinical-outcomes part, with estimated primary completion in April 2029 [[NCT04822181]].
- The current Wegovy label (semaglutide injection) includes treatment of "noncirrhotic metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) ... with moderate to advanced liver fibrosis (consistent with stages F2 to F3 fibrosis) in adults". This is an accelerated approval. Drugs@FDA lists a Wegovy efficacy supplement (NDA 215256, supplement 24) approved on 15 August 2025. Drugs@FDA does not name the indication of each supplement [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- In compensated NASH cirrhosis (phase 2; 71 people; 48 weeks), semaglutide 2.4 mg did not improve fibrosis (11% vs 29% on placebo; p = 0.087) or NASH resolution. No decompensations occurred [[Loomba2023Cirrhosis]].
- SYNERGY-NASH (phase 2; 190 people with MASH and moderate or severe fibrosis; 52 weeks), MASH resolution without fibrosis worsening (Early evidence) [[Loomba2024SYNERGY]]:
  - placebo: 10%
  - tirzepatide 5 mg: 44%
  - tirzepatide 10 mg: 56%
  - tirzepatide 15 mg: 62%

### Exam hooks
- Fatty liver develops in about 90% of heavy drinkers, but only about 10–20% develop severe AH or cirrhosis [[Singal2018ACG]].
- At the same intake, women have a higher cirrhosis risk than men. One drink a day raised risk in women but not men in the 2019 meta-analysis [[Roerecke2019Cirrhosis,Becker1996Hepatology]].
- Daily drinking carries more cirrhosis risk than the same amount spread over fewer days [[Askgaard2015JHep,LlamosasFalcon2023]].
- PNPLA3 rs738409 (I148M) is the strongest common genetic risk variant for both alcohol-related cirrhosis and MASLD [[Buch2015NatGenet,Romeo2008PNPLA3]].
- AH lab pattern: AST over 50 IU/L, AST/ALT over 1.5, both under 400 IU/L, and bilirubin over 3 mg/dL [[Crabb2016NIAAA]].
- Severe AH: Maddrey DF ≥32, or MELD >20 (ACG 2024 prefers MELD) [[Singal2018ACG,Jophlin2024ACG]].
- A Lille score ≥0.45 at day 7 of steroids means non-response (6-month survival 25% vs 85%) [[Louvet2007Lille]].
- STOPAH: prednisolone gave a non-significant fall in 28-day mortality (OR 0.72, P = 0.06), with no 90-day or 1-year benefit. Pentoxifylline did not help [[Thursz2015STOPAH]].
- Steroids raised serious infections (13% vs 7%) in STOPAH [[Thursz2015STOPAH]].
- Early transplant for selected people with severe AH not responding to treatment: 6-month survival 77% vs 23% [[Mathurin2011NEJM]].
- Abstinence is the key treatment for ARLD at every stage. Its survival benefit in cirrhosis is significant from about 1.5 years [[Xie2014HepatolRes,Seitz2018NRDP]].
- Baclofen is the AUD medicine with an RCT in people with cirrhosis (abstinence 71% vs 29%) [[Addolorato2007Lancet]].
- Acamprosate is not metabolised by the liver but is renally cleared, and is contraindicated at CrCl ≤30 mL/min [[FDA-Campral]].
- MetALD means MASLD plus 140–350 g a week (women) or 210–420 g a week (men) of alcohol [[Rinella2023MASLD]].
- In MASLD, fibrosis stage, not steatohepatitis activity, is the strongest predictor of death [[Angulo2015,Dulai2017]].
- Weight loss of ≥10% gave NASH resolution in 90% and fibrosis regression in 45% [[VilarGomez2015]].
- Resmetirom and semaglutide are approved for non-cirrhotic MASH with F2–F3 fibrosis. Neither label covers cirrhosis [[FDA-Rezdiffra,FDA-Wegovy]].
- Cardiovascular disease and extrahepatic cancer cause more deaths in MASLD than liver disease does [[Younossi2023Hepatology,Simon2021Gut]].

### In plain words
- Cirrhosis has many causes. Alcohol and fatty liver linked to weight, diabetes or blood pressure (MASLD) are the two most common in many countries, but viral hepatitis, genetic and autoimmune diseases also cause it [[Seitz2018NRDP,Younossi2023Hepatology]].
- Almost everyone who drinks heavily gets a fatty liver, but most never get cirrhosis. Genes, body weight and drinking pattern help decide who does [[Singal2018ACG,Roerecke2019Cirrhosis]].
- Women's livers are harmed by smaller amounts of alcohol than men's [[Roerecke2019Cirrhosis,Becker1996Hepatology]].
- Having days without alcohol each week may be easier on the liver than drinking every day [[LlamosasFalcon2023]].
- Cutting down helps even if you do not stop completely, but stopping helps the most [[Knox2018Liver,Xie2014HepatolRes]].
- After people with alcohol-related cirrhosis stop drinking, about one in three can recover enough that the complications go away. These are averages across many people, not a promise for any one person [[Hofer2026JHep]].
- Alcohol-associated hepatitis is a sudden, serious illness that causes yellow skin and eyes. It needs hospital care [[Jophlin2024ACG]].
- There are medicines that help people drink less or stop, and some can be used safely with liver disease. Ask your liver team [[Addolorato2007Lancet,Thompson2024JHEP]].
- "MASLD" is the new, less blaming name for fatty liver linked to metabolism. It used to be called NAFLD [[Rinella2023MASLD]].
- About 3 in 10 adults worldwide have MASLD, and it is becoming more common [[Younossi2023Hepatology]].
- What matters most for your future is how much scarring (fibrosis) the liver has, not how much fat it holds [[Angulo2015,Taylor2020Gastro]].
- Losing about 1 in every 10 kg of body weight can calm liver inflammation and sometimes reverse scarring [[VilarGomez2015]].
- Two medicines are approved in the US for MASH with moderate or advanced scarring, before cirrhosis. A doctor decides if either one is right for you [[FDA-Rezdiffra,FDA-Wegovy]].
- Urgent-care box for every complications page: "Vomiting blood, black or tarry stools, new confusion or drowsiness, or fever or belly pain with a swollen abdomen need urgent care: call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department."

## Open questions and controversies
- Is there a "safe" level of drinking for the liver? Roerecke 2019 found no raised risk for occasional drinking and a raised risk at 1 drink a day in women only [[Roerecke2019Cirrhosis]]. Becker 1996 found the lowest risk at 1–6 drinks a week [[Becker1996Hepatology]]. Both are observational and depend on self-report. Status: unresolved, with sex-specific thresholds favoured.
- Does wine carry less risk? Askgaard 2015 found a lower hazard among wine drinkers at the same intake [[Askgaard2015JHep]]. This may reflect confounding by lifestyle (Hypothesis). Status: not established.
- Corticosteroids in severe AH: STOPAH's effect on 28-day mortality was not significant [[Thursz2015STOPAH]]. The individual-patient meta-analysis found a 28-day benefit (HR 0.64) but none at 6 months [[Louvet2018Gastro]]. Status: guidelines keep steroids for selected severe AH and stop them early in Lille non-responders [[Jophlin2024ACG,Singal2018ACG]].
- Maddrey vs MELD: the global cohort favours MELD [[MoralesArraez2022]], and ACG 2024 adopted MELD >20 [[Jophlin2024ACG]]. ACG 2018 still used Maddrey ≥32 [[Singal2018ACG]]. Status: shifting toward MELD.
- Early transplant for AH: survival is high [[Mathurin2011NEJM,Lee2018Gastro]], but QuickTrans did not show non-inferiority for relapse, and heavy drinking after early transplant was more common [[Louvet2022Lancet]]. Status: accepted for carefully selected people, with relapse risk debated.
- Naltrexone in cirrhosis: the label still warns about hepatotoxicity [[FDA-Naltrexone]]. Large VA data found no drug-induced liver injury, including in decompensated cirrhosis [[Thompson2024JHEP]]. A smaller cohort asks for more data in decompensated disease [[Ayyala2022]]. ACG 2018 named only baclofen as safe [[Singal2018ACG]]. Status: practice is moving, and the evidence is observational.
- MASLD vs NAFLD: the new definition captures about 99% of the old NAFLD population [[Hagstrom2024]], so older outcome data are thought to carry over. Status: largely accepted. The MetALD alcohol band is still being validated.
- MASH medicines in cirrhosis: no MASH-targeted medicine is recommended at the cirrhotic stage [[EASL2024MASLD]]. Semaglutide did not improve fibrosis in compensated NASH cirrhosis [[Loomba2023Cirrhosis]]. Resmetirom outcomes in Child–Pugh A cirrhosis are pending [[NCT05500222]]. Status: open.
- Surrogate endpoints: both approvals are accelerated approvals based on biopsy endpoints. Clinical-outcome confirmation is ongoing [[FDA-Rezdiffra,FDA-Wegovy,NCT03900429,NCT04822181]]. Status: open.
