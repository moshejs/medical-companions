# H · Living with cirrhosis, nursing care, palliative care & self-management

Pass date: 2026-11-04. Modules served: living, nursing.

## Findings

### living · What living with cirrhosis means (framing)
- Cirrhosis has many causes, including obesity and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, high alcohol consumption, hepatitis B or C, autoimmune and cholestatic diseases, and iron or copper overload; it moves from an asymptomatic compensated phase to a symptomatic decompensated phase whose complications often bring hospital stays, impaired quality of life and high mortality [[Gines2021Lancet]].
- In the US, the main causes overlap: alcohol use disorder in about 45% of cases, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease in 26% and hepatitis C in 41% (a person can have more than one) [[Tapper2023JAMA]].
- About 40% of people with cirrhosis are diagnosed only when they present with a complication such as hepatic encephalopathy (HE) or ascites [[Tapper2023JAMA]].
- Patients with cirrhosis need "constant and rigorous monitoring both in and outside the hospital"; LiverHope says nurses with liver expertise should be part of the inpatient and outpatient team and bridge clinicians, families and primary care [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- Health-related quality of life in cirrhosis is linked to ascites, HE, leg oedema, muscle cramps, insomnia and low serum sodium, as well as treatment side effects and worry about the future [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].

### living · Quality of life and symptom burden
- A systematic review of 80 studies in end-stage liver disease found pain in 30–79%, breathlessness in 20–88%, muscle cramps in 56–68%, insomnia in 26–77%, daytime sleepiness in 29.5–71%, depression in 4.5–64%, anxiety in 14–45% and erectile dysfunction in 53–93% of men [[Peng2019]].
- In the same review, moving from compensated to decompensated cirrhosis lowered the SF-36 Physical Component Summary by an average 6.4 points (95% CI 4.0–8.8) and the Mental Component Summary by 4.5 points (95% CI 2.4–6.6) [[Peng2019]].
- The JAMA review gives approximate symptom prevalences of muscle cramps 64%, pruritus 39%, poor-quality sleep 63% and sexual dysfunction 53% [[Tapper2023JAMA]].
- The AASLD palliative guidance reports fatigue in 50–86%, dyspnoea in 47–88% and sleep disturbance in 50–80% of people with cirrhosis [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- At the end of life, the symptom and distress burden of decompensated cirrhosis is described as comparable to that of advanced colorectal and lung cancer [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- In the 19-site PAL-LIVER cohort (935 patients), symptom trajectories were stable over 12 months, and HE was the strongest independent predictor of high symptom burden (ESAS OR 1.83, 95% CI 1.38–2.41), depression (PHQ-9 OR 1.93, 95% CI 1.44–2.60) and higher caregiver burden (ZBI-12 OR 1.53, 95% CI 1.02–2.30) [[Tapper2026PALLIVER]].
- The Chronic Liver Disease Questionnaire (CLDQ) has 29 items in six domains (fatigue, activity, emotional function, abdominal symptoms, systemic symptoms, worry), takes about 10 minutes, and scores worse from no cirrhosis to Child A to Child B/C [[Younossi1999CLDQ]].
- In 193 ambulatory patients (154 decompensated), 81% had disturbed sleep (Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index >5); disturbed sleep was independently linked with hypoalbuminaemia, opiate therapy and muscle cramps and with worse CLDQ scores [[Ghabril2017]].
- In a multicentre study of 1,631 people with chronic liver disease, 40.3% had pruritus; the back was the commonest site (63.1%), 38.3% had itch for more than 6 months, and 57.8% of those treated reported insufficient relief [[Oeda2018]].
- In a cross-sectional study of 104 people with cirrhosis and their caregivers, only 56% of patients were employed; those with previous HE had higher unemployment (87.5% vs 19%) and worse financial status (85% vs 61%) [[Bajaj2011Burden]].
- Patients removed from or declined for liver transplant (n=102) had a median of 52 days from denial to death; on the Edmonton Symptom Assessment System 65% had pain, 58% nausea, 48% dyspnoea, 49% anorexia and 36% anxiety [[Poonja2014]].

### living · Muscle cramps, sleep, pruritus, fatigue and pain (symptom control as published)
- AASLD guidance: "Checking serum electrolyte levels and repleting potassium, magnesium, and zinc is a first step in the management of muscle cramps" in decompensated cirrhosis [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- AASLD guidance: taurine (2–3 g daily), vitamin E (200 mg three times a day) and baclofen (5–10 mg three times a day) "have preliminary supportive data and can be considered" for significant cramps (quoted, not a prescription) (Early evidence) [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- In the PICCLES RCT (82 enrolled, 74 completed), sips of pickle brine at cramp onset reduced cramp severity by 2.25 ± 3.61 points on a 0–10 scale versus 0.36 ± 2.87 for tap water at 28 days (P=0.03), without adverse events (Early evidence) [[Tapper2022PICCLES]].
- In the RELAX RCT (98 people with frequent cramps, 48% with cirrhosis), stretching and meditation both reduced cramp severity (median 1.44 and 1.97 points) with no difference between arms; 79.2% would recommend stretching vs 55.3% meditation (Early evidence) [[Tapper2024RELAX]].
- AASLD guidance on sleep: first evaluate and treat HE, pruritus, obstructive sleep apnoea and restless legs; review the timing of meals, activity and medicines, since late diuretics and lactulose can cause night waking [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- AASLD guidance: short-term melatonin 3 mg or hydroxyzine 25 mg nightly can improve sleep in Child-Pugh A and B cirrhosis; chronic benzodiazepines should generally be avoided in decompensated cirrhosis, with end-of-life comfort as a possible exception [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- AASLD guidance on pruritus: start with moisturising creams, avoid hot baths and harsh soaps, use loose clothing and cool humidified air; cholestyramine (4 g/d titrated to 16 g/d) is first-line, with low-dose naltrexone and sertraline among the alternatives that need careful titration in decompensated cirrhosis [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- AASLD guidance on fatigue: evaluate contributors such as HE, hypothyroidism, adrenal insufficiency, depression and medicines; give behavioural education and recommend physical activity; there are insufficient data for stimulants [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- AASLD guidance on pain: "Acetaminophen, 500 mg every 6 h, up to a maximum dose of 2 g/d, is the preferred first-line pharmacotherapy" in cirrhosis, and "Systemic NSAIDs should be avoided" (quoted as published) [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- AASLD guidance: avoid opioids for chronic pain when possible; codeine, morphine and tramadol should generally be avoided; if needed, low-dose oxycodone or hydromorphone can be used, with laxatives such as lactulose started proactively to prevent constipation and HE [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- AASLD guidance on nausea: ondansetron (maximum 8 mg/d) is first-line, with caution for constipation and QTc monitoring; medical marijuana is not first-line for any symptom in decompensated cirrhosis [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- AASLD guidance on breathlessness: start with non-drug measures such as a bedside fan or supplemental oxygen, given the general aim of minimising sedating medicines [[Rogal2022AASLD]].

### living · Mental health, stigma and discrimination
- A 2026 meta-analysis (23 studies) pooled depression prevalence at 37% (95% CI 29–46%) and anxiety at 53% (95% CI 33–73%) in cirrhosis, with high heterogeneity [[Abureesh2026]].
- In a US telephone survey of 1,021 people with cirrhosis, 15.6% had moderately severe to severe depression (PHQ-9 ≥15) and 42.6% had high anxiety (STAI ≥40); fear of liver cancer was associated with both [[Hernaez2022Depression]].
- Using a structured psychiatric interview as the reference, 18% of 305 outpatients with cirrhosis (not on antidepressants) had depression, which was associated with lower quality of life and higher frailty [[BuganzaTorio2019]].
- AASLD guidance gives a depression and anxiety prevalence of 30–60% and advises a low threshold for referral to mental health professionals, particularly when medicines are considered [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- In a South Korean nationwide cohort, people with new-onset cirrhosis had 2.37 times the suicide risk of matched controls (HR 2.37, 95% CI 1.44–3.88), highest in the first 2 years and in ages 18–49 (HR 3.72) [[Jang2021Suicide]].
- In a mailed survey (149 respondents), 89% of people with cirrhosis agreed with at least one of 18 stigma items; more stigma was linked to younger age and to hepatitis C or alcohol as the cause, and to less social support, more depression, worse quality of life and less care-seeking [[VaughnSandler2014]].
- Population studies show that people with alcohol dependence are held more responsible for their condition, provoke more social rejection and are at particular risk for structural discrimination compared with people with other mental disorders [[Schomerus2011]].
- A systematic review of 28 studies found negative attitudes of health professionals towards people with substance use disorders were common and reduced patients' empowerment, engagement and empathy in care [[vanBoekel2013]].
- In a 4-system US survey (1,037 respondents), 27.1% of people with cirrhosis reported experiencing discrimination and 9.8% high medical mistrust; frequent discrimination was reported by 40.5% of Black, 22.2% of Hispanic and 18.7% of White patients [[Rich2026Discrimination]].
- Language guidance for this companion: say "people with cirrhosis", "alcohol-related liver disease" and "person with alcohol use disorder"; stigma is a barrier to care, not a moral fact (framing based on [[VaughnSandler2014,Schomerus2011]]).

### living · Sexual health
- AASLD guidance: about two thirds of men and 80% of women with cirrhosis have sexual dysfunction, which impairs quality of life [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- A meta-analysis of 14 studies (770 men) put erectile dysfunction prevalence at 79% overall (88.4% decompensated, 53.6% compensated); decompensation, beta-blocker and diuretic use, diabetes and higher MELD were linked [[Yoo2023ED]].
- In an RCT of 140 men with cirrhosis (Child-Turcotte-Pugh <10) and erectile dysfunction, tadalafil 10 mg daily for 12 weeks produced a >5-point gain in erectile function score in 62.9% vs 30% with placebo, with no difference in side effects or portal pressure (Early evidence) [[Jagdish2023Tadalafil]].
- AASLD guidance: first remove medicines and substances that can cause sexual dysfunction (for example beta-blockers, alcohol, tobacco) where appropriate, and treat contributors such as depression and diabetes [[Rogal2022AASLD]].

### living · Work, money and caregivers
- Among 453 US transplant candidates, 23.3% reported high financial burden (out-of-pocket costs ≥10% of income) and only 27.6% were employed; high burden was linked to inability to pay for basic needs (aOR 4.76) and delayed or forgone care (aOR 4.02) [[Aby2026Financial]].
- In a related cohort of 459 transplant candidates, about 44% were unable to work because of illness or disability and over 44% had household income below $50,000 [[Zeng2026COST]].
- Caregivers of people with previous HE carried higher burden (Perceived Caregiver Burden 75 vs 65; Zarit 16 vs 11) than caregivers of people without HE; depression score, MELD and cognitive tests predicted burden [[Bajaj2011Burden]].
- In PAL-LIVER, 559 of 935 patients co-enrolled with a caregiver and 32% of caregivers had high burden (ZBI-12 ≥12); high burden was associated with an 11-point lower FACT-Hep and 3-point higher PHQ-9 in the patient [[Orman2026Caregiver]].
- Among 127 caregivers of people with decompensated cirrhosis, 52% had clinically significant anxiety and 35% depression symptoms, 27% high caregiving burden, and over 85% had never discussed end-of-life preferences with the hepatologist; more frequent prognostic communication was linked to lower burden [[Li2026Caregivers]].
- AASLD guidance: "Caring for caregivers is a central component of providing palliative and hospice care"; HE education about medicine administration, dose titration and the waxing-and-waning course can relieve patient and caregiver burden [[Rogal2022AASLD]].

### living · Self-management, education and care models
- In 150 outpatients, only 53% of 15 self-management questions were answered correctly at baseline (most missed: sodium content of sea salt, safety of paracetamol and statins); after a short booklet, median scores rose from 53% to 67% at 3 months (Early evidence) [[Volk2013Knowledge]].
- The 7-item Cirrhosis Knowledge Questionnaire (ascites, variceal bleeding, HE) had Cronbach's alpha 0.82; scores were higher with case management (5.6 ± 1.1) than without (4.3 ± 2.1) [[Ramachandran2020CKQ]].
- In an RCT (57 vs 59), a pharmacist-led patient-oriented education and medication-management intervention improved knowledge of cirrhosis and self-care tasks and quality of life in decompensated cirrhosis (Early evidence) [[Hayward2020]].
- In a 100-patient study of a coordinated outpatient care model for cirrhosis with ascites, 12-month mortality was 23.1% vs 45.7% and 30-day readmission 15.4% vs 42.4% compared with standard care (Early evidence) [[Morando2013]].
- The ALFIE RCT (147 patients, median MELD 19) of a liver-nurse-coordinated chronic disease management model found no reduction in overall liver-related emergency admissions (IRR 0.89, 95% CI 0.53–1.50) or survival, but fewer encephalopathy admissions and better quality-of-care measures (Established, for "no overall effect") [[Wigg2025ALFIE]].
- In a pilot of the Patient Buddy app (40 patients, 40 caregivers) tracking medicines, sodium intake, daily weights and cognition, 17 were readmitted within 30 days but none for HE; 8 potential HE readmissions were judged prevented by app alerts (Early evidence) [[Ganapathy2017]].
- LiverHope's three main education aims for decompensated cirrhosis: recognising and preventing complications, falls awareness and prevention, and the effects and side effects of medicines; the main caregiver should attend each visit [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- AASLD: all people with cirrhosis should receive education, motivation and behavioural skills support to lower the risk of malnutrition, frailty and sarcopenia (primary prevention) [[Lai2021AASLD]].

### living · Daily weight and fluid self-monitoring
- LiverHope nursing table (ascites): confirm a low-sodium diet (<100 mmol/day); monitor body weight and urine volume daily in hospital; discuss diuretic changes with the physician if weight rises, does not fall, or falls >500 g/day for 2 consecutive days without leg oedema or >1 kg with oedema (quoted as published) [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- LiverHope outpatient education: teach patients how to monitor body weight and to call when weight increases by >500 g/day for at least 3 consecutive days; explain that NSAIDs (for example aspirin, ibuprofen) must be avoided [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- EASL nutrition guideline: in ascites under sodium restriction the recommended sodium intake is about 80 mmol/day, equal to 2 g sodium or 5 g salt per day, while taking care to keep food palatable [[EASL2019Nutrition]].
- In a study of 120 outpatients with ascites, only 31% adhered to a 2-g sodium diet and adherent patients ate 20% fewer calories per day; AASLD suggests balancing salt restriction with palatability advice [[Lai2021AASLD]].
- AASLD: in people with frailty or sarcopenia who cannot meet nutrition targets on a sodium-restricted diet, liberalising sodium should be considered [[Lai2021AASLD]].

### living · Nutrition and activity (self-management angle; detail in pass G)
- AASLD: recommended protein intake is 1.2–1.5 g/kg ideal body weight per day, protein should not be restricted in HE, and fasting gaps should be no more than 3–4 hours while awake, with an early breakfast and/or late-evening snack (quoted as published) [[Lai2021AASLD]].
- EASL: "The late-evening snack is the most important, as it covers the long interval between dinner and breakfast" (patient section); include a late-evening supplement and breakfast in malnourished decompensated patients [[EASL2019Nutrition]].
- In an RCT of 103 patients, a nighttime supplement (710 kcal/day) raised total body protein by 0.38 kg at 3 months, 0.48 kg at 6 months and 0.53 kg at 12 months, about 2 kg of lean tissue; the daytime supplement did not [[Plank2008]].
- A review of late-evening snack studies found less fat oxidation and better nitrogen balance, with inconsistent gains in muscle mass and no reported survival benefit (Early evidence) [[Tsien2012LES]].
- AASLD: it is reasonable to follow CDC guidance of 150–300 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous exercise per week plus muscle strengthening at least 2 days per week, combining aerobic and resistance training tailored to frailty [[Lai2021AASLD]].
- A meta-analysis of 4 small RCTs (81 patients) found no severe adverse events with adapted physical activity and no worsening of Child or MELD scores (Early evidence) [[Brustia2018]].
- In a 17-patient pilot, a home-based activity programme with a tracker raised daily steps by 2,627 and improved 6-minute walk distance (423 to 482 m) versus decline in controls (Early evidence) [[Chen2020HBPAP]].
- AASLD: frailty should be reassessed at least annually in compensated disease and as often as every 8–12 weeks in decompensated cirrhosis [[Lai2021AASLD]].

### living · Red flags and when to seek urgent care
- LiverHope: teach patients and caregivers to recognise GI bleeding, particularly melaena (black stools) [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- LiverHope: early signs of infection to teach are fever, abdominal pain, early signs of HE, painful urination and respiratory symptoms; patients should contact their nurse or, if not possible, go to the emergency room [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- LiverHope: teach that reduced urine volume can signal acute kidney injury and that new HE may be the first sign of kidney injury [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- In a meta-analysis of 178 studies (11,987 patients), infection raised the odds of death about fourfold (OR 3.75, 95% CI 2.12–4.23); 30.3% of infected patients died within 1 month and 63% by 12 months (population figures) [[Arvaniti2010]].
- Among people with ascites, the annual incidence of spontaneous bacterial peritonitis is 11% and of hepatorenal syndrome 8% [[Tapper2023JAMA]].
- Because the time course of complications is fast, the companion's urgent-care box applies to every complications page: vomiting blood, black or tarry stools, new confusion or drowsiness, or fever or belly pain with a swollen abdomen (framing from [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope,Arvaniti2010]]).

### living · Hospital readmissions and care bundles
- In 402 people discharged after a decompensation, 14% were readmitted by 1 week and 37% within 1 month; 69% had at least one non-elective readmission (median time to first readmission 67 days; median follow-up 203 days) [[Volk2012Readmit]].
- In the same cohort, 22% of 165 readmissions within 30 days were judged possibly preventable, and more frequent readmissions predicted higher mortality independent of MELD [[Volk2012Readmit]].
- In a 6-state population cohort of 119,722 index admissions, 30-day and 90-day readmission rates were 12.9% and 21.2%; with more than 3 complications they were 24.2% and 35.9%, and HE was most strongly associated with readmission (OR 1.77) [[Tapper2016Readmit]].
- Almost 1 in 4 readmissions was to a different hospital from the discharging one [[Tapper2016Readmit]].
- At two academic centres the 30-day readmission rate was 20%, and 90-day mortality was 26.8% in those readmitted vs 9.8% in those not (OR 2.6) [[Berman2011]].
- A checklist-based quality-improvement programme (goal-directed lactulose, rifaximin for overt HE, prompts for SBP prophylaxis) built into electronic order entry was associated with 40% lower adjusted odds of 30-day readmission; readmission among people with HE grade >2 fell from 48.9% to 26.0% [[Tapper2016QI]].
- A systematic review of quality-improvement initiatives in liver disease summarises checklist, electronic decision-support and care-coordination approaches (Early evidence) [[Tapper2016QIReview]].
- The 2010 Delphi panel selected 41 quality indicators from 169 candidates, covering ascites (13), variceal bleeding (18), HE (4), liver cancer (1), transplantation (2) and general care (3) [[Kanwal2010QI]].
- The 2019 AASLD Practice Metrics Committee set 46 quality measures (26 process, 7 clinical outcome including rehospitalisation within 7 and 30 days, and 13 patient-reported outcome measures) [[Kanwal2019QM]].

### living · Palliative care, advance care planning and hospice
- AASLD defines palliative care as multidisciplinary specialised care for the physical, spiritual and psychosocial needs of people with serious illness and their caregivers, and states it can be considered at any stage of cirrhosis alongside disease-directed treatment [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- AASLD: "Only 11% of patients with cirrhosis receive specialty palliative care or hospice care referrals", and consultation often comes very late [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- Among 102 people removed from or declined for transplant, only 11% were referred for palliative care and 28% had a documented do-not-resuscitate status, while 48% were later admitted to intensive care [[Poonja2014]].
- In the US Nationwide Inpatient Sample (39,349 hospitalised people with end-stage liver disease), palliative consultation occurred in 4.5%, rising from 0.97% in 2006 to 7.1% in 2012; Hispanic patients and insured patients were less likely to be referred [[Rush2017]].
- In terminal hospitalisations (2009–2013), palliative consultation rose from 18.0% to 36.6% and was associated with lower procedure burden and $10,062 lower cost; African American, Hispanic and Asian patients and transplant candidates were less likely to receive it [[Patel2017CGH]].
- At one transplant centre, palliative care was consulted for only 17% (18/107) of listed patients who died or were removed; half of these consultations came within 72 hours of death [[Kathpalia2016]].
- In an AASLD member survey (396 respondents), 81% thought advance care planning happens too late; top barriers were cultural perceptions of palliative care (95%), unrealistic patient expectations (93%) and clinician time (91%) [[Ufere2019Barriers]].
- AASLD guidance: "ACP is an iterative process that should start with a diagnosis of cirrhosis and preferably occur before hepatic decompensation and loss of decision-making capacity" [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- AASLD guidance: hepatology clinicians should provide primary palliative care, including symptom management, basic advance care planning such as naming surrogate decision makers, counselling and referral; serious illness conversations should use the patient's preferred language and a professional interpreter [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- In qualitative interviews at 3 California transplant centres (42 patients, 46 clinicians), optimistic transplant-team attitudes hindered talk about dying and non-aggressive options were rarely discussed [[Patel2021ACP]].
- In the Veterans Health Administration, 29.8% of 167,727 veterans with cirrhosis had a documented goals-of-care discussion by July 2023; completion rose from 0.4% to 21.2% after quality-improvement work, but the median time from first discussion to death was 37 days [[Patel2026GCD]].
- In the COMPASS pilot RCT (63 hospitalised patients, stopped early for slow accrual), specialist palliative consultation plus nurse phone follow-up lowered the hazard of readmission (HR 0.36, 95% CI 0.16–0.83) and increased days alive outside hospital (Early evidence) [[Shinall2019COMPASS]].
- A 6-month supportive-care liver nurse specialist intervention (47 patients, 27 carers) was acceptable and feasible and increased shared electronic care plans (Early evidence) [[Kimbell2018]].
- PAL-LIVER (19-site cluster RCT) compared consultative palliative care with trained-hepatologist-led palliative care; mean FACT-Hep change at 3 months was +7.02 (95% CI 4.34–9.71; n=499) vs +8.01 (95% CI 5.38–10.65; n=410) (registry results) [[NCT03540771]].
- AASLD guidance: hospice is comfort-focused care for people with life expectancy measured in months; it is underused by people dying with cirrhosis; teams may consider MELD >21 and Child-Pugh >12 when judging hospice eligibility (estimated survival ≤6 months), which is a population threshold, not a personal forecast [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- AASLD guidance: patients on the transplant list can (uncommonly) receive Medicare hospice benefits, and the most common end-of-life scenarios to plan for are infection, haemorrhage and encephalopathy [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- AASLD guidance: at the end of life, HE treatment may depart from standard care to match goals; some patients or caregivers may prefer polyethylene glycol to lactulose or stopping treatment as preferences change [[Rogal2022AASLD]].

### nursing · Assessment priorities
- LiverHope decompensated-cirrhosis nursing measures: regular visits or phone calls; vital signs, weight and abdominal girth; nutrition counselling; adherence; and assessment of quality of life, frailty, sleep, depression and anxiety [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- LiverHope HE nursing care: mental status evaluation, aspiration prevention, looking for triggers (especially infection and GI bleeding), nutrition support, skin-breakdown prevention, accurate intake and output, and bowel cleansing [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- LiverHope HE table: use lactulose or lactitol to produce 3–4 bowel movements/day, consider cleansing enemas if there are none, protect the airway in grade 3 HE, and admit grade 4 (deep coma) to intensive care (quoted as published) [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- The US lactulose label states the dose "may be adjusted every day or two to produce 2 or 3 soft stools daily" (quoted as published); LiverHope's 3–4/day reflects European practice, so the companion should show both and defer to the prescriber [[FDA-Enulose,Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- LiverHope outpatient HE care: evaluate covert HE at every visit, request a nutrition consultation for everyone with previous HE, and assess quality of life and frailty at least yearly [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- Asterixis (flapping tremor) is a brief, irregular loss of muscle tone while holding a posture, first described as the "liver flap" in hepatic encephalopathy [[Lui2026Asterixis]].
- In 167 drivers with cirrhosis, those with minimal HE on an inhibitory control test had more crashes in the preceding year (17% vs 3% by transport-department records; relative risk 5.77) [[Bajaj2009Driving]].
- LiverHope GI-bleeding nursing: monitor supine and standing blood pressure and heart rate, mental status, skin pallor and temperature, urine volume, and the colour of emesis or stool (black vs bright red); be aware of variceal severity before passing a nasogastric tube [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- LiverHope outpatient: check that people on beta-blockers have an adequate effect (heart-rate reduction of 25% to about 55–60 bpm) and teach home blood pressure and heart-rate monitoring [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].

### nursing · Paracentesis care
- LiverHope: after large-volume paracentesis, monitor blood pressure every 15 minutes for 2 hours and then every 30 minutes for 2 hours; give 25% albumin as ordered; check puncture sites for leak or infection (quoted as published) [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- LiverHope: if the site leaks, apply a wafer/urostomy bag to measure the fluid, and if it leaks immediately afterwards have the patient lie on the opposite side for a few hours [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- A meta-analysis of 17 RCTs (1,225 patients) found albumin reduced post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction (OR 0.39, 95% CI 0.27–0.55), hyponatraemia (OR 0.58) and mortality (OR 0.64, 95% CI 0.41–0.98) compared with alternative treatments [[Bernardi2012]].
- In 4,729 paracenteses, severe haemorrhage occurred in 0.19% (death 0.016%); it was not related to INR or platelets but occurred in people with severe liver failure and usually kidney dysfunction [[Pache2005]].

### nursing · Variceal bleeding emergency (nursing view; detail in pass F)
- LiverHope: insert a large-bore peripheral IV (16–18 gauge); transfuse red cells if haemoglobin is <7 g/dL; start oral or rectal lactulose or enemas to prevent HE; check for infections (quoted as published) [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- Baveno VII 6.2: red-cell transfusion should be conservative, with a target haemoglobin of 7–8 g/dl, adjusted for cardiovascular disease, age, haemodynamics and ongoing bleeding [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Baveno VII 6.17: after resuscitation, people with suspected acute variceal bleeding should have upper endoscopy within 12 h of presentation [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Baveno VII 6.33 and 6.36: rapid removal of blood from the gut with lactulose (oral or enemas) should be used to prevent HE, and fresh frozen plasma is not recommended because it will not correct coagulopathy and may worsen portal hypertension [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Baveno VII 7.7: in people with ascites, beta-blockers or carvedilol should be dose-reduced or stopped if systolic pressure stays <90 mmHg or mean arterial pressure <65 mmHg and/or with HRS-AKI, and can be restarted when these resolve [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].

### nursing · Infection surveillance and skin care
- LiverHope: nursing priorities for infection are prevention (especially in people with HE or leg oedema), watching for signs of sepsis, minimising invasive procedures and timely antibiotics [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- LiverHope HE table: avoid bladder catheters unless the patient is incontinent with skin breakdown or has severe acute kidney injury needing exact output [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- LiverHope: use preventive measures for skin breakdown and pressure ulcers, especially in fragile and malnourished patients; teach care of the skin of oedematous legs; leg wraps over a foam or cotton barrier may be used for significant oedema [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- AASLD pruritus advice for skin: moisturise, avoid hot baths and harsh soaps, loose clothing and cool humidified air [[Rogal2022AASLD]].

### nursing · Nutrition support in hospital
- AASLD: every hospitalised person with cirrhosis should see a registered dietitian within 24 hours of admission, or be screened with the RFH-NPT if a dietitian is not available [[Lai2021AASLD]].
- AASLD: minimise fasting and "nil by mouth" periods (for example a pre-bedtime snack, or an early-morning snack before a late-afternoon procedure); oral supplements are first-line when intake is short [[Lai2021AASLD]].
- AASLD: oesophageal varices are not an absolute contraindication to an enteric feeding tube, but watch closely for rebleeding after recent banding; percutaneous gastrostomy tubes should not be placed in people with ascites [[Lai2021AASLD]].
- AASLD: protein target for critically ill adults with cirrhosis is 1.2–2.0 g/kg ideal body weight per day (quoted as published) [[Lai2021AASLD]].

### nursing · Medicine education (labels quoted, not instructions)
- Spironolactone label: in cirrhosis, start in hospital and titrate slowly; recommended initial dose for oedema 100 mg/day (range 25–200 mg); monitor potassium within one week of starting; it can cause hyperkalaemia, hyponatraemia, gynaecomastia and, in cirrhosis with ascites, sudden fluid and electrolyte shifts that may worsen HE and coma [[FDA-Aldactone]].
- Furosemide label: in cirrhosis with ascites, therapy is best started in hospital; sudden fluid and electrolyte shifts may precipitate hepatic coma, so strict observation is needed; potassium supplements or an aldosterone antagonist help prevent hypokalaemia and metabolic alkalosis [[FDA-Lasix]].
- Combination aldosterone antagonist plus loop diuretic resolved ascites more often than sequential initiation (76% vs 56%) with less hyperkalaemia (4% vs 18%) [[Tapper2023JAMA]].
- Lactulose label: may cause gaseous distension, flatulence or belching and abdominal cramping in about 20%; excessive dosing can cause diarrhoea with fluid loss, hypokalaemia and hypernatraemia [[FDA-Enulose]].
- In meta-analyses, lactulose was associated with lower mortality than placebo (8.5% vs 14%; 705 patients) and less recurrent overt HE (25.5% vs 46.8%; 1,415 patients) [[Tapper2023JAMA]].
- Rifaximin label: 550 mg twice daily to reduce the risk of overt HE recurrence in adults; in the pivotal trial 91% also took lactulose; use with caution in Child-Pugh C, since trials included only MELD <25 [[FDA-Xifaxan]].
- In the rifaximin RCT (299 patients in remission), breakthrough HE occurred in 22.1% vs 45.9% with placebo over 6 months (HR 0.42) and HE hospitalisation in 13.6% vs 22.6% (HR 0.50) [[Bass2010Rifaximin]].
- Baveno VII: rifaximin is indicated for secondary prophylaxis of HE and not for prevention of spontaneous bacterial peritonitis [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Carvedilol label: do not stop abruptly; may cause bradycardia, hypotension and fluid retention; contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment (label wording, set against Baveno VII's preference for carvedilol in compensated cirrhosis with clinically significant portal hypertension) [[FDA-Coreg,deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- In the 3-year PREDESCI trial as summarised in JAMA (201 patients with portal hypertension), beta-blockers (carvedilol or propranolol) reduced decompensation or death from 27% to 16% [[Tapper2023JAMA]].

### nursing · Falls and safety in HE
- In 122 outpatients followed prospectively, the 1-year probability of falling was 52.3% with cognitive dysfunction on PHES vs 6.5% without; cognitive dysfunction was the only independent predictor (OR 10.2, 95% CI 3.4–30.4) [[Soriano2012]].
- Previous falls independently worsened the mental component of quality of life in 118 outpatients with cirrhosis [[Roman2013Falls]].
- In US emergency visits after a fall (102,977 involving cirrhosis), cirrhosis carried higher adjusted risk of intracranial haemorrhage (2.33), skull fracture (1.75) and pelvic fracture (1.71), and higher in-hospital death; HE, alcohol use and infection were linked to severe injury [[Ezaz2018]].
- In the LiveSMART trial (230 adults without prior overt HE), lactulose followed by tele-tai-chi reduced injurious falls (4% vs 12%) and non-injurious falls (19% vs 32%) versus enhanced usual care (Early evidence) [[Tapper2026LiveSMART]].
- AASLD sleep guidance: benzodiazepines should generally not be used for sleep because of sedation, respiratory depression, cognitive impairment and falls [[Rogal2022AASLD]].

### nursing · Alcohol withdrawal monitoring and brief interventions
- The CIWA-Ar is a shortened 10-item scale for quantifying alcohol withdrawal severity in routine care [[Sullivan1989CIWA]].
- ACG 2024: alcohol withdrawal in people with alcohol-related liver disease "should be assessed and managed as per the CIWA-Ar protocol", and clinicians should distinguish it from HE, recognising the two can coexist [[Jophlin2024ACG]].
- ACG 2024: mild or moderate withdrawal usually begins 6–24 hours after the last drink; withdrawal scores have not been validated in severe liver disease and a symptom-triggered approach is preferred; short- and intermediate-acting benzodiazepines (lorazepam, oxazepam) are safer in poor liver function [[Jophlin2024ACG]].
- ACG 2024: in severe withdrawal with liver disease, benzodiazepines are the treatment of choice used cautiously, with careful monitoring because they can precipitate or worsen HE (strong recommendation, moderate evidence) [[Jophlin2024ACG]].
- ACG 2024 recommends integrated multidisciplinary care models that combine behavioural and/or medicine treatment for alcohol use disorder in people with alcohol-related liver disease [[Jophlin2024ACG]].
- In 35,682 veterans with cirrhosis and alcohol use disorder, only 14% received treatment in 6 months; treatment was associated with less decompensation (6.5% vs 11.6%; aOR 0.63) and lower long-term mortality (51% vs 58%; aOR 0.87) [[Rogal2020AUD]].
- In hospital inpatients (14 studies, 4,041 people), brief interventions reduced drinking at 6 and 9 months and deaths at 6 months (RR 0.42) and 1 year (RR 0.60) [[McQueen2011]].
- In primary care (34 studies, 15,197 people), brief intervention reduced drinking by 20 g/week at 1 year (95% CI 12–28) versus minimal or no intervention [[Kaner2018Cochrane]].

### nursing · Transplant evaluation support
- AASLD: transplant teams should not use frailty or sarcopenia as an absolute contraindication to liver transplantation [[Lai2021AASLD]].
- ACG 2024: selecting people with severe alcohol-associated hepatitis for transplant requires rigorous psychosocial evaluation by a multidisciplinary team, using tools such as SIPAT [[Jophlin2024ACG]].
- AASLD palliative guidance: goals-of-care discussions should be prioritised when transplant is being considered, and an outpatient palliative referral for all patients in transplant evaluation improved physical and psychological symptom scores in a single-arm study (Early evidence) [[Rogal2022AASLD]].

### Exam hooks
- Paracetamol (acetaminophen) up to 2 g/day is the AASLD-preferred first-line analgesic in cirrhosis; systemic NSAIDs should be avoided [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- Lactulose is titrated to 2–3 soft stools daily (US label) [[FDA-Enulose]].
- Rifaximin 550 mg twice daily halves HE breakthrough (22.1% vs 45.9%) when added mostly to lactulose [[Bass2010Rifaximin]].
- Spironolactone: watch potassium (hyperkalaemia) and gynaecomastia; start in hospital in cirrhosis per label [[FDA-Aldactone]].
- Furosemide: hypokalaemia and fluid shifts can precipitate hepatic coma [[FDA-Lasix]].
- After large-volume paracentesis: BP every 15 min for 2 h, then every 30 min for 2 h; watch the puncture site [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- Variceal bleed transfusion target: haemoglobin 7–8 g/dl; endoscopy within 12 h [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Black tarry stool = melaena = upper GI bleeding until proven otherwise; teach caregivers to recognise it [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- Infection raises death risk about fourfold in cirrhosis [[Arvaniti2010]].
- Cognitive dysfunction (covert HE) predicts falls: 52.3% vs 6.5% at 1 year [[Soriano2012]].
- Readmission: 37% within 1 month after a decompensation; HE is the strongest predictor [[Volk2012Readmit,Tapper2016Readmit]].
- Advance care planning should start at diagnosis, before decompensation and loss of capacity [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- Only about 11% of people with cirrhosis get specialist palliative or hospice referral [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- Protein should not be restricted in HE; target 1.2–1.5 g/kg/day; late-evening snack shortens overnight fasting [[Lai2021AASLD]].
- CIWA-Ar has 10 items; in liver disease, ACG prefers symptom-triggered treatment and shorter-acting benzodiazepines (lorazepam, oxazepam) [[Sullivan1989CIWA,Jophlin2024ACG]].
- Asterixis = "liver flap", a negative myoclonus of HE [[Lui2026Asterixis]].
- First step for cramps: check and replete potassium, magnesium and zinc [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- Cholestyramine is first-line for cholestatic pruritus [[Rogal2022AASLD]].

### In plain words
- Cirrhosis has many causes, such as fatty liver linked to weight and diabetes, hepatitis viruses, alcohol, and autoimmune or inherited conditions. No cause makes a person less deserving of care [[Gines2021Lancet]].
- Feeling tired, itchy, crampy or sleeping badly is common with cirrhosis. Tell your team: many of these can be helped [[Peng2019,Rogal2022AASLD]].
- Feeling low or worried is common too. Asking for mental health support is part of good liver care [[Hernaez2022Depression]].
- If your team asks you to weigh yourself, do it at the same time each day and call if your weight goes up quickly, as your team has explained [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- Ask before taking any pain medicine. Many people with cirrhosis are told to avoid ibuprofen and similar drugs, and paracetamol has a lower daily limit [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- A small snack before bed, with some protein, can help protect your muscles overnight [[EASL2019Nutrition,Plank2008]].
- Confusion can creep in slowly. If someone with cirrhosis becomes sleepy, muddled or unsteady, get help early and avoid driving [[Soriano2012,Bajaj2009Driving]].
- Vomiting blood, black or tarry stools, new confusion or drowsiness, or fever or belly pain with a swollen abdomen need urgent care: call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]].
- Caring for someone with cirrhosis is hard work. Caregivers deserve support too [[Bajaj2011Burden,Rogal2022AASLD]].
- Palliative care is extra support for comfort and planning. You can have it at the same time as treatment aimed at your liver, including while waiting for a transplant [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- It helps to talk early about what matters to you and who should speak for you if you cannot [[Rogal2022AASLD]].
- Numbers about survival describe large groups of people, not what will happen to you [[Rogal2022AASLD]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Lactulose stool target: the US label targets 2–3 soft stools/day [[FDA-Enulose]]; LiverHope's nursing table uses 3–4 bowel movements/day [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]]; status: present both, defer to the treating team.
- Chronic disease management and nurse-led models: a non-randomised coordinated model reported lower 30-day readmission and 12-month mortality [[Morando2013]]; the ALFIE RCT found no reduction in overall liver-related emergency admissions [[Wigg2025ALFIE]]; status: benefit on hard outcomes unproven, process and patient-reported gains more consistent.
- Who should deliver palliative care: hepatologist-led and consultative models produced similar 3-month FACT-Hep gains in PAL-LIVER registry results [[NCT03540771]]; early specialist palliative care cut readmissions in a small stopped-early pilot [[Shinall2019COMPASS]]; status: peer-reviewed primary PAL-LIVER results awaited.
- Sodium restriction vs nutrition: EASL recommends about 80 mmol (2 g) sodium/day in ascites [[EASL2019Nutrition]], LiverHope uses <100 mmol/day [[Fabrellas2020LiverHope]], and AASLD warns adherent patients eat 20% fewer calories and suggests liberalising salt in frail patients [[Lai2021AASLD]]; status: individualise.
- Carvedilol labelling vs guideline use: the US label contraindicates carvedilol in severe hepatic impairment [[FDA-Coreg]], while Baveno VII names it the preferred beta-blocker in compensated cirrhosis with clinically significant portal hypertension [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]]; status: guideline use is off-label nuance for prescribers.
- Hospice eligibility in cirrhosis: AASLD suggests MELD >21 and Child-Pugh >12 as supplementary prognostic markers [[Rogal2022AASLD]]; these are population thresholds, and prognosis in cirrhosis is uncertain and fluctuates [[Rogal2022AASLD]]; status: expert consensus.
- Withdrawal scales in severe liver disease: CIWA-Ar is recommended for assessment [[Jophlin2024ACG]], but the same guideline notes scores are not validated in severe liver disease and overlap with HE [[Jophlin2024ACG]]; status: unresolved.
- Cramp treatments: pickle brine, taurine, baclofen, stretching and meditation each have small trials only [[Tapper2022PICCLES,Tapper2024RELAX,Rogal2022AASLD]]; status: Early evidence.
- Stigma: perceived stigma is common and linked to less care-seeking [[VaughnSandler2014]], but whether stigma-reduction interventions change outcomes in cirrhosis has not been tested in trials found in this pass; status: research gap.
