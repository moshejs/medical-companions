## Findings

### tests · What liver blood tests mean
- Cirrhosis has many causes: obesity and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, high alcohol use, hepatitis B or C, autoimmune and cholestatic diseases, and iron or copper overload [[Gines2021Lancet]].
- Cirrhosis begins with an asymptomatic compensated phase and moves to a symptomatic decompensated phase. Portal hypertension, systemic inflammation and liver failure drive outcomes [[Gines2021Lancet]].
- "Liver function tests" is a misnomer. ACG recommends calling ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase and bilirubin "liver chemistries" or "liver tests" [[Kwo2017ACG]].
- Hepatocellular injury is defined as AST and ALT raised disproportionately to alkaline phosphatase. Cholestatic injury is alkaline phosphatase raised disproportionately to AST and ALT [[Kwo2017ACG]].
- Most circulating bilirubin is unconjugated. A raised conjugated (direct) bilirubin implies hepatocellular disease or biliary obstruction in most settings [[Kwo2017ACG]].
- ACG gives a "true healthy normal" ALT of 29–33 IU/L for males and 19–25 IU/L for females, and says levels above this should be assessed. An elevated ALT has been associated with increased liver-related mortality [[Kwo2017ACG]].
- The ACG workup for hepatocellular injury covers hepatitis A, B and C, fatty liver disease, alcohol-related liver disease, hereditary haemochromatosis, autoimmune hepatitis, Wilson disease, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency and a medicine history. A hepatic alkaline phosphatase elevation prompts testing for PBC and PSC [[Kwo2017ACG]].
- ACG says a liver biopsy may be considered when serology and imaging do not give a diagnosis, to stage a condition, or when several diagnoses are possible [[Kwo2017ACG]].
- Thrombocytopenia (platelets <150,000/µL) has been observed in up to 76% of people with chronic liver disease. Moderate thrombocytopenia (50,000–75,000/µL) occurs in about 13% of people with cirrhosis [[Afdhal2008Platelets]].
- Low platelets in liver disease have several causes: splenic sequestration (hypersplenism), marrow suppression by hepatitis C or interferon therapy, and reduced thrombopoietin level or activity [[Afdhal2008Platelets]].
- In 100 people with chronic hepatitis B, the mean AST/ALT ratio was 0.59 without cirrhosis and 1.02 with cirrhosis. The ratio often rose above 1.0 when cirrhosis first appeared [[Williams1988ASTALT]].
- Williams and Hoofnagle also noted that an AST/ALT ratio above 2.0 is usually seen in alcohol-related liver disease and below 1.0 in chronic hepatitis. The ratio is less helpful for telling causes apart once cirrhosis is present [[Williams1988ASTALT]].
- In 252 people with HCV-related chronic liver disease, an AST/ALT ratio ≥1 was more frequent in cirrhosis (P<.001). AST/ALT combined with platelet count gave a 97.0% PPV and 97.9% NPV for cirrhosis [[Giannini2003ASTALT]].
- In a meta-analysis of 86 studies (19,533 patients; 24% biopsy-proven cirrhosis), the findings that most raised the likelihood of cirrhosis were ascites (LR 7.2), platelets <160 ×10³/µL (LR 6.3), spider naevi (LR 4.3) and a Bonacini discriminant score >7 (LR 9.4) [[Udell2012JAMA]].
- In the same meta-analysis, the findings that most lowered the likelihood of cirrhosis were a Lok index <0.2 (LR 0.09), platelets ≥160 ×10³/µL (LR 0.29) and no hepatomegaly (LR 0.37). The clinician's overall impression was less informative than individual findings [[Udell2012JAMA]].
- Low platelet count is also an HCC risk marker. In 2126 veterans with cirrhosis, platelets ≤110 ×10³/µL carried an adjusted HR of 4.7 (95% CI 2.0–11.4) for HCC compared with >266 ×10³/µL [[Ioannou2007HCC]].

### tests · Imaging features of cirrhosis
- In 300 asymptomatic people with raised aminotransferases who had both ultrasound and biopsy, liver surface nodularity was the most accurate sign of severe fibrosis or cirrhosis: specificity 95%, LR+ 11.6, LR− 0.51 [[Colli2003US]].
- Surface nodularity alone raised the post-test probability from 35% to 86%. Adding caudate hypertrophy and hepatic-vein flow pattern raised it only to 88% [[Colli2003US]].
- In cirrhosis the right lobe shrinks and the caudate lobe enlarges. A caudate-to-right-lobe width ratio separated cirrhotic from non-cirrhotic livers with 84% sensitivity, 100% specificity and 94% accuracy (25 cirrhotic, 25 normal, 15 other diffuse disease) [[Harbin1980Caudate]].
- Pathology of 11 cirrhotic cadaver livers showed more fibrosis in the right lobe than in the caudate lobe, which explains the shape change [[Harbin1980Caudate]].
- Ascites on examination is one of the strongest bedside clues to cirrhosis (LR 7.2) [[Udell2012JAMA]].

### tests · Liver biopsy: role, sampling and safety
- Biopsy samples taken at the same time from the right and left lobes in 124 people with chronic HCV differed by at least one fibrosis stage in 33.1%. In 14.5%, one lobe read as cirrhosis and the other as stage 3 [[Regev2002Sampling]].
- Intra-observer variation was low (staging differed in 6% and 10% on re-reading). The authors attribute the lobe differences to sampling error [[Regev2002Sampling]].
- An optimal specimen is 20–25 mm long and/or has more than 11 complete portal tracts. Published percutaneous biopsy series averaged 17.7 ± 5.8 mm and 7.5 ± 3.4 portal tracts [[Cholongitas2006Biopsy]].
- In 68,276 percutaneous biopsies (Italy, 1973–1983), death occurred in 9 per 100,000. It was always due to haemoperitoneum and only in people with cancer or cirrhosis. 61% of complications appeared within 2 hours and 96% within 1 day [[Piccinino1986]].
- In 61,187 elective percutaneous biopsies in England (1998–2005), 7-day all-cause mortality was 2 per 1000 (95% CI 1.8–2.5), and up to 12 per 1000 when cancer was being investigated. Deaths directly due to biopsy were at most about 1 in 10,000, and major bleeding was about 6 per 1000 [[West2010Biopsy]].
- In 2740 biopsies in people with HCV and bridging fibrosis or cirrhosis (HALT-C), serious adverse events occurred in 1.1% and bleeding in 0.6%, with no biopsy-related deaths. Bleeding was more frequent when platelets were ≤60,000/mm³ [[Seeff2010HALTC]].
- In HALT-C, excluding people with platelets ≤60,000/mm³ would have cut bleeding by 25% while excluding only 2.8% of biopsies. Operator experience, needle type and ultrasound guidance did not change event rates [[Seeff2010HALTC]].
- Transjugular liver biopsy, used mainly when clotting is abnormal or ascites is present, succeeded in 96.8% of 7649 procedures. Minor complications occurred in 6.5%, major in 0.56%, and adult mortality was 0.09% [[Kalambokis2007TJLB]].
- For a suspected liver tumour, AASLD 2023 reports a biopsy complication risk (tumour seeding, bleeding) of about 3%, which has fallen substantially with coaxial needle technique [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].

### noninvasive · FIB-4 and APRI (blood-based scores)
- FIB-4 formula as published: age [years] × AST [U/L] / (platelets [10⁹/L] × √ALT [U/L]). It was developed in 832 people with HIV/HCV coinfection (Ishak staging) [[Sterling2006FIB4]].
- In the derivation study, the AUROC of FIB-4 for Ishak 0–3 versus 4–6 was 0.765. A value <1.45 had a 90% NPV (sensitivity 70%) and >3.25 had a 65% PPV (specificity 97%). Biopsy could have been avoided in 71% of the validation group [[Sterling2006FIB4]].
- In 541 adults with NAFLD, FIB-4 had a jackknife-validated AUROC of 0.802 (95% CI 0.758–0.847) for advanced fibrosis. This was better than the NAFLD fibrosis score (0.768), AST/ALT ratio (0.742), APRI (0.730) and BARD (0.70) [[Shah2009FIB4]].
- Shah 2009 is the source of today's NAFLD cut-offs: FIB-4 ≥2.67 gave an 80% PPV and ≤1.30 a 90% NPV for advanced fibrosis. At a fixed 90% specificity (FIB-4 1.93), sensitivity was only 50% [[Shah2009FIB4]].
- Age changes FIB-4 performance. FIB-4 and NFS did poorly in people ≤35 years (FIB-4 AUROC 0.60). At ≥65 years, FIB-4 specificity for advanced fibrosis fell to 35% [[McPherson2017Age]].
- For people ≥65 years, McPherson proposed a FIB-4 cut-off of 2.0. This raised specificity to 70% with 77% sensitivity [[McPherson2017Age]].
- AASLD 2023 (NAFLD) describes FIB-4 bands of low risk (<1.3), intermediate (1.3–2.67) and high (>2.67). A move between bands may be used to assess progression [[Rinella2023AASLDNAFLD]].
- AASLD 2023 keeps FIB-4 as the first-line test for general practitioners and endocrinologists because it is simple and adds little cost, while noting it is statistically inferior to ELF and elastography [[Rinella2023AASLDNAFLD]].
- For cirrhosis specifically, AASLD 2023 lists FIB-4 ≥3.48 to rule in (90% specificity) and <1.67 to rule out (90% sensitivity) [[Rinella2023AASLDNAFLD]].
- APRI (AST-to-platelet ratio index) was built from platelets and AST in hepatitis C. Its validation-set AUROCs were 0.88 for significant fibrosis and 0.94 for cirrhosis [[Wai2003APRI]].
- In a meta-analysis of 40 studies (n = 8739, HCV), APRI's summary AUROCs were 0.77 for significant fibrosis, 0.80 for severe fibrosis and 0.83 for cirrhosis. At a threshold of 1.0, APRI was 76% sensitive and 72% specific for cirrhosis [[Lin2011APRI]].
- APRI was less accurate in HIV/HCV coinfection than in HCV alone [[Lin2011APRI]].
- In an individual-patient meta-analysis (37 studies, n = 5735, NAFLD), AUROCs for advanced fibrosis were 0.85 for VCTE liver stiffness, 0.76 for FIB-4 and 0.73 for NFS [[Mozes2022IPD]].

### noninvasive · ELF test
- The Enhanced Liver Fibrosis (ELF) panel combines hyaluronic acid, TIMP-1 and N-terminal procollagen III peptide. It is a proprietary test approved in the US as a prognostic biomarker [[Rinella2023AASLDNAFLD,Rosenberg2004ELF]].
- In the original 1021-patient cohort, the serum algorithm had 90% sensitivity for fibrosis, a 92% NPV for significant fibrosis and an AUROC of 0.804 [[Rosenberg2004ELF]].
- In a NAFLD meta-analysis, ELF had >0.90 sensitivity to exclude fibrosis at a threshold of 7.7. Reaching 0.90 specificity for advanced fibrosis needed a threshold of 10.18 (sensitivity 0.57) [[Vali2020ELF]].
- AASLD 2023 states that ELF ≥9.8 identifies people with NAFLD at increased risk of progression to cirrhosis and liver-related events. ELF ≥11.3 predicts liver-related events in confirmed or suspected advanced fibrosis [[Rinella2023AASLDNAFLD]].

### noninvasive · Vibration-controlled transient elastography (FibroScan)
- A meta-analysis of 50 studies found mean AUROCs for transient elastography of 0.84 for significant fibrosis, 0.89 for severe fibrosis and 0.94 (95% CI 0.93–0.95) for cirrhosis [[FriedrichRust2008TE]].
- A second meta-analysis (40 studies) found summary sensitivity 0.83 and specificity 0.89 for cirrhosis. It cautioned that stage cut-offs varied widely and had not been validated [[Tsochatzis2011TE]].
- In alcohol-related liver disease (14 studies, 834 participants), sensitivity for cirrhosis was 0.95 and specificity 0.71 (LR− 0.07). A cut-off of about 12.5 kPa was suggested, with caution [[Pavlov2016ALD]].
- In 450 UK adults with suspected NAFLD, LSM AUROCs were 0.77 for F≥2, 0.80 for F≥3 and 0.89 for F4. The Youden cut-offs were 8.2, 9.7 and 13.6 kPa. Steatosis and probe type did not affect LSM [[Eddowes2019FibroScan]].
- In NAFLD meta-analysis, VCTE summary AUCs were 0.83 for significant fibrosis, 0.85 for advanced fibrosis and 0.89 for cirrhosis. Only 4% of studies used intention-to-diagnose analysis [[Selvaraj2021NAFLD]].
- In 13,369 examinations, liver stiffness measurement failed in 3.1% and was unreliable in a further 15.8%, so nearly 1 in 5 results could not be interpreted. Main causes were BMI >30 (OR 7.5 for failure) and operator experience under 500 exams [[Castera2010Pitfalls]].
- Baveno VII thresholds (by transient elastography): LSM <10 kPa, without other signs, rules out cACLD. 10–15 kPa is suggestive of cACLD and >15 kPa is highly suggestive [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Baveno VII states that people with chronic liver disease and LSM <10 kPa have a negligible 3-year risk (≤1%) of decompensation and liver-related death [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Baveno VII's "rule of 5" says LSM by TE of 10, 15, 20 and 25 kPa should be used to mark progressively higher relative risks of decompensation and liver-related death, whatever the cause [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Baveno VII says an index LSM ≥10 kPa should be repeated while fasting, because TE can give false-positive results [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Baveno VII rules out clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH) with LSM ≤15 kPa plus platelets ≥150 ×10⁹/L (sensitivity and NPV >90%) [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Baveno VII rules in CSPH with LSM ≥25 kPa (specificity and PPV >90%) in virus- or alcohol-related cACLD and in non-obese NASH-related cACLD [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- CSPH is defined as a hepatic venous pressure gradient (HVPG) ≥10 mmHg, and HVPG is the gold standard [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Baveno VII defines a clinically meaningful LSM decrease (linked to lower risk) as a ≥20% fall to below 20 kPa, or any fall to below 10 kPa [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- AASLD 2023 NAFLD bands for VCTE: <8 kPa rules out advanced fibrosis, 8–12 kPa may indicate fibrotic NASH, and >12 kPa makes advanced fibrosis likely, though the PPV is low (0.34–0.71). For cirrhosis, ≥20 kPa rules in and <8 kPa rules out [[Rinella2023AASLDNAFLD]].

### noninvasive · MR elastography and sequential pathways
- In an individual-participant meta-analysis (12 studies, 697 patients), MRE AUROCs were 0.88 for significant fibrosis, 0.93 for advanced fibrosis and 0.92 for cirrhosis. MRE failed in 4.3% [[Singh2015MRE]].
- In NAFLD meta-analysis, MRE summary AUCs were 0.91 for significant fibrosis, 0.92 for advanced fibrosis and 0.90 for cirrhosis [[Selvaraj2021NAFLD]].
- AASLD 2023 states that MRE LSM ≥5 kPa suggests cirrhosis (AUROC 0.89–0.94). MRE values of 5 and 8 kPa were linked with 9% and 20% risks of decompensation or death. VCTE and MRE both report kPa but on different scales [[Rinella2023AASLDNAFLD]].
- The AASLD 2023 NAFLD pathway: assess risk with FIB-4 first. If FIB-4 is ≥1.3, do a secondary assessment with VCTE (preferred over MRE in primary care for cost reasons) or ELF. Refer to specialty care if risk stays intermediate or high [[Rinella2023AASLDNAFLD]].
- In the same pathway, people with ≥2 metabolic risk factors, especially prediabetes or type 2 diabetes, should repeat FIB-4 every 1–2 years [[Rinella2023AASLDNAFLD]].
- Sequential FIB-4 (<1.3; ≥2.67) then VCTE (<8.0; ≥10.0 kPa) gave 66% sensitivity and 86% specificity for advanced fibrosis, with 33% still needing biopsy [[Mozes2022IPD]].
- Sequential FIB-4 (<1.3; ≥3.48) then VCTE (<8.0; ≥20.0 kPa), used to rule out advanced fibrosis or rule in cirrhosis, left 19% needing biopsy [[Mozes2022IPD]].
- The fatty liver naming changed in 2023. NAFLD became metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), defined by steatosis plus at least 1 of 5 cardiometabolic risk factors. Older studies cited here use "NAFLD/NASH" [[Rinella2023MASLD]].

### scores · Child–Turcotte–Pugh
- Child and Turcotte's original classification (for surgical risk in portosystemic shunt surgery) used ascites, encephalopathy, nutritional status, bilirubin and albumin. Pugh et al. replaced nutritional status with prothrombin time/INR [[Peng2016ChildMELD,Pugh1973]].
- Each of the five measures (ascites, encephalopathy, bilirubin, albumin, PT/INR) scores 1–3 points. The total gives Child–Pugh A (5–6 points), B (7–9) or C (10–15) [[Costa2023ChildB,InfanteRivard1987]].
- Point cut-offs used in teaching tables: bilirubin <2 / 2–3 / >3 mg/dL; albumin >3.5 / 2.8–3.5 / <2.8 g/dL; INR <1.7 / 1.7–2.3 / >2.3; ascites none / mild / moderate–severe; encephalopathy none / grade 1–2 / grade 3–4 (unverified: original Pugh 1973 table not accessible).
- In 177 people with cirrhosis, the 3-class Child–Pugh score captured less survival variance than the continuous 5–15 score. The continuous score in turn captured less than a model using the component values in their original units [[InfanteRivard1987]].
- With the original-units Pugh model, sensitivity and specificity for 1-year death averaged 80% at the point of maximum discrimination [[InfanteRivard1987]].
- Child–Pugh includes two subjective items (ascites and encephalopathy). The ALBI grade uses only bilirubin and albumin and performed at least as well as Child–Pugh in more than 6000 people with HCC. It also split Child–Pugh A into two prognostic groups [[Johnson2015ALBI]].
- Across 119 studies comparing Child–Pugh and MELD, 44 comparisons favoured MELD, 16 favoured Child–Pugh and 99 found no significant difference. The scores had similar prognostic value in most settings [[Peng2016ChildMELD]].
- Child–Pugh class A is the usual requirement for surgical resection of HCC (AASLD 2023) and for atezolizumab–bevacizumab (BCLC 2022) [[Singal2023AASLDHCC,Reig2022BCLC]].
- In advanced HCC, Child–Pugh B is linked with much shorter survival. In one retrospective series, median OS was 3.8 months in Child–Pugh B and 10.0 months in Child–Pugh A [[Costa2023ChildB]].

### scores · MELD, MELD-Na, MELD 3.0
- MELD began as the Mayo TIPS model. In 231 elective TIPS patients, bilirubin, creatinine, INR and cause of liver disease predicted survival. A risk score R >1.8 marked a median survival of 3 months or less, and the model beat Child–Pugh [[Malinchoc2000]].
- Kamath 2001 validated MELD (bilirubin, creatinine, INR, cause) in 4 independent cohorts. C-statistics for 3-month death were 0.87 in hospitalised patients, 0.80 in ambulatory non-cholestatic cirrhosis, 0.87 in PBC and 0.78 in historical patients [[Kamath2001MELD]].
- Adding individual portal hypertension complications barely improved MELD (c-statistic gain <0.01 for SBP and variceal bleeding, 0.01–0.03 for ascites) [[Kamath2001MELD]].
- MELD formula as used for US allocation (quoted in Kim 2008): 11.2 × ln(INR) + 9.57 × ln(creatinine, mg/dL) + 3.78 × ln(bilirubin, mg/dL) + 6.43. Each variable has a lower limit of 1, creatinine is capped at 4 (and set to 4 on renal replacement therapy), and the score is rounded to an integer from 6 to 40 [[Kim2008MELDNa]].
- In 3437 adults on the US waiting list (1999–2001), 12% died within 3 months. Mortality was 1.9% with MELD <9 and 71.3% with MELD ≥40. The MELD c-statistic was 0.83 versus 0.76 for Child–Turcotte–Pugh (P<0.001) [[Wiesner2003MELD]].
- MELD cannot accurately predict survival in about 15–20% of patients [[Kamath2007MELD]].
- MELD also predicts survival with infection, variceal bleeding, fulminant liver failure and alcohol-associated hepatitis, and is used for risk before non-transplant surgery [[Kamath2007MELD]].
- An early sodium-adjusted score (Biggins 2006) was MELD + 1.59 × (135 − Na), with Na bounded 120–135 mEq/L. Scores of 20, 30 and 40 matched 6%, 16% and 37% 6-month mortality [[Biggins2006MELDNa]].
- In 6769 US registrants in 2005, death risk rose with an HR of 1.21 per MELD point and 1.05 per 1-mmol/L fall in sodium (125–140). Sodium mattered more when MELD was low [[Kim2008MELDNa]].
- Kim 2008 MELD-Na formula: MELD − Na − [0.025 × MELD × (140 − Na)] + 140, with Na bounded 125–140 mmol/L. People with hyponatraemia could gain up to 13 points [[Kim2008MELDNa]].
- Applied to 2006 data, MELD-Na would have ranked 32 of the 477 people who died (7%) considerably higher than MELD alone did [[Kim2008MELDNa]].
- The OPTN MELD-Na formula quoted in Kim 2021: MELD + 1.32 × (137 − Na) − [0.033 × MELD × (137 − Na)], with Na bounded 125–137 mmol/L, applied only when MELD >11 [[Kim2021MELD3]].
- MELD 3.0 was built from all US waitlist candidates registered 2016–2018. It adds female sex and serum albumin, interactions for bilirubin × sodium and albumin × creatinine, and an upper creatinine bound of 3.0 mg/dL [[Kim2021MELD3]].
- MELD 3.0 formula as published: 1.33 (if female) + 4.56 × ln(bilirubin) + 0.82 × (137 − Na) − 0.24 × (137 − Na) × ln(bilirubin) + 9.09 × ln(INR) + 11.14 × ln(creatinine) + 1.85 × (3.5 − albumin) − 1.83 × (3.5 − albumin) × ln(creatinine) + 6, rounded to the nearest integer [[Kim2021MELD3]].
- MELD 3.0 bounds: Na 125–137 mmol/L, albumin 1.5–3.5 g/dL, creatinine upper bound 3.0 mg/dL. The MELD-era rule of setting values below 1.0 to 1.0 is described for bilirubin, INR and creatinine [[Kim2021MELD3]].
- MELD 3.0 discriminated slightly better than MELD-Na (C 0.869 vs 0.862, P<.01). It reclassified a net 8.8% of decedents to a higher tier, particularly women, and simulated waitlist deaths fell from 7850 to 7788 (P=.02) [[Kim2021MELD3]].
- At the development-set average MELD 3.0 of 20, predicted mortality was 1.9% at 30 days and 5.4% at 90 days [[Kim2021MELD3]].
- Kim 2021 example (high-risk woman: bilirubin 12, Na 128, INR 2.2, creatinine 2.8, albumin 2.0): MELD 35, MELD-Na 36, MELD 3.0 39, with predicted 30-day mortality 41.8% [[Kim2021MELD3]].
- OPTN adopted MELD 3.0 in July 2023. In early UNOS data (August 2022–March 2024), female sex was no longer a significant risk factor for missing transplant or for waitlist death. These results are preliminary [[Akabane2025MELD3]].

### hcc · Burden and who is at risk
- Primary liver cancer is the sixth most common cancer worldwide and the third leading cause of cancer death both worldwide and in the US (2020). HCC makes up 75–86% of cases, and men are affected 2–3 times more than women [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].
- GLOBOCAN 2020 ranked liver cancer third among cancer deaths, with 8.3% of all cancer deaths [[Sung2021GLOBOCAN]].
- EASL 2025 states that HCC accounts for about 90% of primary liver cancers. It also lists personalised, risk-based surveillance among its key updates [[EASL2025HCC]].
- In a VA cirrhosis cohort, HCC incidence was 1.3 per 100 patient-years. HCV (aHR 3.0), HBsAg (aHR 3.3), obesity (aHR 2.5) and low platelets were independent predictors [[Ioannou2007HCC]].
- Five-year cumulative HCC incidence in cirrhosis: about 30% (Japan) and 17% (West) in HCV, 21% in haemochromatosis, 15% (high-endemic) and 10% (West) in HBV, 8% in alcohol-related cirrhosis and 4% in advanced biliary cirrhosis [[Fattovich2004HCC]].
- HCV/HBV and HBV/HDV coinfection raise HCC risk 2- to 6-fold, and continued heavy alcohol use raises it 2- to 4-fold [[Fattovich2004HCC]].
- In HALT-C (HCV with bridging fibrosis or cirrhosis), 5-year cumulative HCC incidence was 7.0% with cirrhosis and 4.1% with bridging fibrosis. Maintenance peginterferon did not lower it [[Lok2009HALTC]].
- After cure of HCV with direct-acting antivirals (VA, 22,500 patients), HCC incidence was 0.90 vs 3.45 per 100 person-years with vs without SVR (aHR 0.28). With cirrhosis, risk after SVR stayed at 1.82 per 100 person-years [[Kanwal2017DAA]].
- In 296,707 VA patients with NAFLD, HCC incidence was 0.21 per 1000 person-years overall and 10.6 per 1000 person-years with cirrhosis. 20% of NAFLD-related HCCs occurred without evidence of cirrhosis [[Kanwal2018NAFLD]].
- AASLD 2023 recommends surveillance when HCC risk is ≥1.0% per year, a lower threshold than the traditional 1.5%. This covers Child–Pugh A–B cirrhosis of any cause, Child–Pugh C transplant candidates, and selected people with non-cirrhotic HBV (≥0.2% per year) [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].
- AASLD 2023 says HCC risk stays raised for up to 10 years after SVR in HCV cirrhosis, so surveillance should continue indefinitely in that group [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].

### hcc · Surveillance: how, how often, how well
- AASLD 2023: "HCC surveillance should be performed using ultrasound and AFP at semiannual (approximately every 6 months) intervals" (Level 2, Strong Recommendation) [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].
- AASLD 2023 does not recommend routine CT- or MRI-based surveillance or tumour biomarkers other than AFP in at-risk people with cirrhosis or chronic HBV (Level 5, Weak) [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].
- Semiannual surveillance was linked with earlier stage and longer survival than annual surveillance after lead-time adjustment (40.3 vs 30 months). Quarterly surveillance did not improve on semiannual in an RCT [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].
- In a meta-analysis of 32 studies (13,367 patients), ultrasound detected any-stage HCC with 84% sensitivity (95% CI 76–92) but early-stage HCC with only 47% (33–61) [[Tzartzeva2018]].
- In studies comparing both, ultrasound plus AFP detected early-stage HCC with 63% sensitivity (95% CI 48–75) versus 45% (30–62) for ultrasound alone (P=.002). Ultrasound alone was slightly more specific (RR 1.08) [[Tzartzeva2018]].
- An older systematic review found pooled HCC detection sensitivity of 60% for ultrasound, 68% for CT and 81% for MRI, with specificities of 97%, 93% and 85% [[Colli2006HCC]].
- An AFP cut-off of 20 ng/mL gives about 60% sensitivity and 90% specificity and is the usual surveillance threshold. Over 40% of HCCs have normal AFP [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].
- In 941 people with cirrhosis, 20.3% of ultrasounds were inadequate to exclude HCC. Inadequate scans were linked with BMI, Child–Pugh B/C (OR 1.93), alcohol-related cirrhosis (OR 2.11) and NASH cirrhosis (OR 2.87). More than a third were inadequate with Child–Pugh C, BMI >35 or NASH cirrhosis [[Simmons2017US]].
- Ultrasound visualisation limits (LI-RADS visualisation score B or C) occur in about 20% of patients. Severe limits (score C) reduce early-HCC sensitivity, so MRI may be considered [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].
- Abbreviated MRI for surveillance had pooled sensitivity 86% and specificity 96% for any-stage HCC (10 studies, 1547 patients) [[Kim2021AMRI]].
- GALAD (gender, age, AFP-L3, AFP, DCP) reached sensitivities of 60–80% for early HCC in a multinational case-control study, and 65% sensitivity with 82% specificity in the phase III HEDS study [[Singal2023AASLDHCC,Johnson2014GALAD]].
- In a Shanghai RCT of 18,816 people with HBV or chronic hepatitis, AFP plus ultrasound every 6 months cut HCC mortality by 37% (rate ratio 0.63, 95% CI 0.41–0.98), despite only 58.2% adherence [[Zhang2004Screening]].
- In the same RCT, resection was possible in 46.5% of screened versus 7.5% of control HCC cases. 5-year survival was 46.4% vs 0 [[Zhang2004Screening]].
- In a meta-analysis of 47 studies (15,158 people with cirrhosis), surveillance was linked with early-stage detection (OR 2.08), curative treatment (OR 2.24) and longer survival (OR 1.90, 95% CI 1.67–2.17), including in lead-time-adjusted studies [[Singal2014PLoSMed]].
- Surveillance is underused. Pooled use was 24.0% (95% CI 18.4–30.1) across 29 studies of 118,799 people with cirrhosis: 73.7% in specialist clinics versus 8.8% in population-based cohorts [[Wolf2021Surveillance]].
- Surveillance use was lower in people with alcohol-associated or NASH-related cirrhosis. All eight intervention studies (education, reminder or recall, outreach) increased surveillance, by 9.4–63.6% [[Wolf2021Surveillance]].
- AASLD recommends best-practice alerts or outreach programmes to raise surveillance adherence (Level 2, Strong) [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].
- AASLD recall pathway: a subcentimetre ultrasound lesion (US-2) can be rechecked in 3–6 months. A new solid lesion ≥1 cm (US-3) or a raised AFP calls for multiphase CT or contrast MRI [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].

### hcc · Diagnosis: LI-RADS
- LI-RADS has four algorithms: ultrasound surveillance, diagnosis by CT/MRI, diagnosis by contrast-enhanced ultrasound, and treatment response by CT/MRI. Each is designed for people with or at risk of HCC [[Chernyak2018LIRADS]].
- LI-RADS grades nodules from LR-1 (benign) to LR-5 (HCC). Arterial phase hyperenhancement (APHE) and delayed washout are the features most strongly associated with HCC [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].
- For nodules ≥2 cm, APHE plus one more major feature (washout, enhancing capsule or threshold growth) is enough for an HCC diagnosis. For 10–19 mm nodules, APHE plus washout or threshold growth is required, or APHE plus two major features [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].
- LI-RADS criteria are validated only in surveillance-eligible people (cirrhosis, higher-risk non-cirrhotic HBV, prior HCC). A biopsy is advised when imaging is inconclusive [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].

### hcc · Staging: BCLC 2022
- BCLC 0 (very early): a single HCC ≤2 cm without vascular invasion or spread, preserved liver function and no cancer symptoms. If transplant is not feasible, ablation gives survival similar to resection [[Reig2022BCLC]].
- BCLC A (early): a single HCC of any size, or up to 3 nodules none >3 cm, without macrovascular invasion or spread, performance status 0, and preserved liver function [[Reig2022BCLC]].
- BCLC B (intermediate): multifocal HCC beyond BCLC A with preserved liver function, PS 0 and no vascular invasion or spread. The 2022 update splits it into 3 groups by tumour burden and liver function [[Reig2022BCLC]].
- BCLC C (advanced): vascular invasion or extrahepatic spread with PS ≤2 and preserved liver function. Systemic therapy is advised, with atezolizumab–bevacizumab as first-choice first line [[Reig2022BCLC]].
- BCLC D (end stage): major cancer-related symptoms (PS >2) and/or impaired liver function without a transplant option [[Reig2022BCLC]].
- BCLC expected median survival (population statistics): more than 5 years for BCLC 0/A, 2.5 years for B, 2 years for C and 3 months for D [[Reig2022BCLC]].
- Atezolizumab–bevacizumab requires preserved liver function (compensated Child–Pugh A if cirrhosis is present) and no high-risk bleeding signs on upper endoscopy, such as properly treated varices and no history of variceal bleeding [[Reig2022BCLC]].

### hcc · Treatments by stage
- Resection and radiofrequency ablation give the same survival benefit for HCC ≤2 cm. Beyond that size ablation is less effective and resection is favoured. Microwave ablation may be best for tumours ≤4 cm [[Reig2022BCLC]].
- In 218 people with a single HCC ≤2 cm treated by RFA, sustained complete response was 97.2%. Perioperative mortality was 0%, major complications 1.8%, and 5-year survival 68.5% in potentially operable patients [[Livraghi2008RFA]].
- In an RCT of 180 people with a solitary HCC ≤5 cm, 4-year overall survival was 67.9% after percutaneous ablation and 64.0% after resection (no significant difference). Ablation had fewer complications [[Chen2006Ablation]].
- AASLD 2023 reports 5-year survival above 70% after resection, with postoperative mortality below 3%, in well-selected people without CSPH. Resection is generally limited to Child–Turcotte–Pugh A [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].
- Milan criteria: a single tumour ≤5 cm, or up to 3 tumours each ≤3 cm, with no macrovascular invasion. In 48 transplant recipients, 4-year survival was 75% and recurrence 8%. In those within criteria on explant pathology, overall and recurrence-free survival were 85% and 92% [[Mazzaferro1996Milan]].
- In the Metroticket cohort (1556 transplants), 5-year survival was 73.3% within Milan versus 53.6% beyond. Patients within "up-to-seven" without microvascular invasion reached 71.2% [[Mazzaferro2009UpToSeven]].
- UCSF expanded criteria (single tumour ≤6.5 cm, or ≤3 nodules with the largest ≤4.5 cm and total diameter ≤8 cm) gave 90% 1-year and 75.2% 5-year survival after transplant [[Yao2001UCSF]].
- AASLD 2023: transplant is linked with a median survival of 10 years. Five-year recurrence is about 10% after transplant versus 50–60% after resection or ablation [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].
- AASLD 2023 describes UNOS downstaging criteria (one lesion 5.1–8 cm; two to three lesions <5 cm; four to five lesions <3 cm with total diameter <8 cm). Successful downstaging to Milan allows an exception score after 6 months [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].
- TACE for intermediate HCC: in an RCT of 112 people (Child–Pugh A/B), chemoembolisation gave 1- and 2-year survival of 82% and 63%, versus 63% and 27% with conservative treatment (HR 0.47) [[Llovet2002TACE]].
- AASLD 2023 cites a systematic review of conventional TACE: objective response 52.5%, median survival 19.4 months and mortality 0.6% [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].
- SHARP (602 people, advanced HCC): median OS was 10.7 months with sorafenib versus 7.9 months with placebo (HR 0.69) [[Llovet2008SHARP]].
- REFLECT (954 people): lenvatinib was non-inferior to sorafenib, with median OS 13.6 vs 12.3 months (HR 0.92) [[Kudo2018REFLECT]].
- IMbrave150 (501 people, unresectable HCC): atezolizumab plus bevacizumab versus sorafenib gave an HR for death of 0.58 (95% CI 0.42–0.79), 12-month OS 67.2% vs 54.6%, and median PFS 6.8 vs 4.3 months [[Finn2020IMbrave150]].
- IMbrave150 update (median follow-up 15.6 months): median OS 19.2 months with atezolizumab–bevacizumab versus 13.4 months with sorafenib (HR 0.66) [[Cheng2022IMbrave150]].
- IMbrave150 doses as published: atezolizumab 1200 mg plus bevacizumab 15 mg/kg IV every 3 weeks, versus sorafenib 400 mg twice daily [[Cheng2022IMbrave150]].
- HIMALAYA (1171 people): STRIDE (tremelimumab 300 mg once plus durvalumab 1500 mg every 4 weeks) gave a median OS of 16.43 months versus 13.77 months with sorafenib (HR 0.78). 36-month OS was 30.7% vs 20.2% [[AbouAlfa2022HIMALAYA]].
- HIMALAYA 4-year update: 48-month OS was 25.2% with STRIDE versus 15.1% with sorafenib (HR 0.78, 95% CI 0.67–0.92) [[Sangro2024HIMALAYA]].
- CheckMate 9DW (668 people, Child–Pugh 5–6): nivolumab plus ipilimumab versus lenvatinib or sorafenib gave a median OS of 23.7 vs 20.6 months (HR 0.79). 36-month OS was 38% vs 24%, but there were more deaths in the first 6 months (HR 1.65) [[Yau2025CheckMate9DW]].
- About 25% of HCCs carry potentially actionable mutations, not yet used in practice (Early evidence) [[Llovet2021NRDP]].

### Exam hooks
- FIB-4 = (age × AST) / (platelets × √ALT). In NAFLD, <1.3 makes advanced fibrosis unlikely and ≥2.67 makes it likely [[Sterling2006FIB4,Shah2009FIB4]].
- FIB-4 loses specificity at age ≥65. The proposed cut-off there is 2.0 [[McPherson2017Age]].
- FIB-4 is unreliable at age ≤35 (AUROC 0.60) [[McPherson2017Age]].
- Baveno VII "rule of 5": 10, 15, 20 and 25 kPa. <10 kPa rules out cACLD and >15 kPa is highly suggestive [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- LSM ≤15 kPa plus platelets ≥150 ×10⁹/L rules out CSPH. LSM ≥25 kPa rules it in [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- CSPH = HVPG ≥10 mmHg [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- An AST/ALT ratio >1 in non-alcohol-related chronic liver disease suggests cirrhosis [[Williams1988ASTALT]].
- A platelet count <160 ×10³/µL raises the likelihood of cirrhosis (LR 6.3) [[Udell2012JAMA]].
- The best single ultrasound sign of cirrhosis is liver surface nodularity (specificity 95%) [[Colli2003US]].
- A liver biopsy can miss cirrhosis through sampling error (14.5% of paired-lobe biopsies disagreed on cirrhosis) [[Regev2002Sampling]].
- Child–Pugh = bilirubin, albumin, INR, ascites and encephalopathy. A 5–6, B 7–9, C 10–15 [[Costa2023ChildB,Peng2016ChildMELD]].
- MELD = bilirubin, creatinine and INR. It was first built to predict survival after TIPS [[Malinchoc2000,Kamath2001MELD]].
- MELD 3.0 adds sex and albumin to MELD-Na. US allocation adopted it in July 2023 [[Kim2021MELD3,Akabane2025MELD3]].
- MELD c-statistic for 3-month waitlist death was 0.83, versus 0.76 for Child–Pugh [[Wiesner2003MELD]].
- HCC surveillance = ultrasound ± AFP every 6 months for people with cirrhosis [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].
- For early HCC, ultrasound alone is 45–47% sensitive and ultrasound plus AFP 63% [[Tzartzeva2018]].
- Only about 1 in 4 people with cirrhosis get HCC surveillance (24.0%) [[Wolf2021Surveillance]].
- Milan criteria = 1 tumour ≤5 cm, or ≤3 tumours each ≤3 cm, with no vascular invasion [[Mazzaferro1996Milan,Mazzaferro2009UpToSeven]].
- First-line therapy for BCLC C with Child–Pugh A is atezolizumab–bevacizumab [[Reig2022BCLC,Finn2020IMbrave150]].
- HCV cure lowers but does not remove HCC risk in cirrhosis (1.82 per 100 person-years after SVR) [[Kanwal2017DAA]].

### In plain words
- Cirrhosis has many causes, including fatty liver linked to weight and diabetes, hepatitis B or C, alcohol, autoimmune and inherited conditions [[Gines2021Lancet]].
- "Liver tests" mostly show whether liver cells are hurt (ALT, AST) or bile flow is blocked (alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin). They do not measure how well the liver works on their own [[Kwo2017ACG]].
- When the liver scars, the spleen can grow and hold on to platelets, so a low platelet count can be a clue to cirrhosis [[Afdhal2008Platelets,Udell2012JAMA]].
- FIB-4 is a free score made from your age and three routine blood tests. It helps decide who needs a liver scan, but it is not a diagnosis [[Sterling2006FIB4,Rinella2023AASLDNAFLD]].
- A FibroScan is like an ultrasound. A gentle pulse measures how stiff the liver is, and stiffer usually means more scarring [[FriedrichRust2008TE,deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Scan results can be wrong if you have just eaten or if the scan is hard to do, so a high result is often repeated while fasting [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII,Castera2010Pitfalls]].
- A liver biopsy takes a tiny piece of liver. Serious bleeding happens in about 6 in 1000 biopsies, which is why blood tests and scans are tried first [[West2010Biopsy]].
- Child–Pugh and MELD scores describe how sick the liver is for groups of people. They cannot tell any one person what will happen [[Kim2021MELD3,Peng2016ChildMELD]].
- People with cirrhosis have a higher chance of liver cancer. An ultrasound, often with an AFP blood test, every 6 months can find it early, when it is easier to treat [[Singal2023AASLDHCC,Singal2014PLoSMed]].
- Many people miss these 6-monthly checks, and reminders help. It is fine to ask your care team when your next one is due [[Wolf2021Surveillance]].
- Liver cancer found early can often be removed, burned away (ablation) or treated with a transplant. Later stages have medicines that help many people live longer [[Reig2022BCLC,Finn2020IMbrave150]].
- Survival numbers come from large groups of people and cannot predict any one person's future [[Reig2022BCLC]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Which FIB-4 cut-offs? The original HIV/HCV cut-offs were 1.45/3.25 [[Sterling2006FIB4]]; NAFLD practice uses 1.3/2.67 [[Shah2009FIB4,Rinella2023AASLDNAFLD]]; ≥65 years may need 2.0 [[McPherson2017Age]]. Status: age- and cause-specific cut-offs are still debated.
- Should AFP be added to ultrasound? AASLD 2023 recommends ultrasound plus AFP [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]], based on higher early-HCC sensitivity with a small loss of specificity [[Tzartzeva2018]]. Some guidelines have treated AFP as optional. Status: the evidence favours adding AFP, and EASL 2025 moves toward personalised, risk-based surveillance [[EASL2025HCC]].
- How strong is the evidence that surveillance saves lives in cirrhosis? There is one RCT, in an HBV population [[Zhang2004Screening]]; in cirrhosis the evidence is mainly cohort meta-analyses, which are prone to lead-time bias [[Singal2014PLoSMed]]. Status: no RCT in cirrhosis, and one is unlikely to be ethical.
- Abbreviated MRI and biomarker panels (GALAD) for people whose ultrasound is inadequate. Accuracy looks promising [[Kim2021AMRI,Johnson2014GALAD]], but AASLD does not yet recommend them routinely [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]]. Status: Early evidence.
- Elastography cut-offs depend on cause. Alcohol-related disease may need about 12.5 kPa [[Pavlov2016ALD]]; NAFLD studies give 13.6 kPa for F4 [[Eddowes2019FibroScan]]; Baveno VII uses a cause-agnostic rule of 5 [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]]. Status: the rule of 5 is accepted for prognosis, and diagnostic cut-offs vary.
- Child–Pugh versus MELD versus ALBI. MELD is objective and used for allocation [[Wiesner2003MELD]]; Child–Pugh includes subjective items but still guides HCC treatment eligibility [[Reig2022BCLC]]; ALBI may refine Child–Pugh A [[Johnson2015ALBI]]. Status: they complement each other [[Peng2016ChildMELD]].
- Does MELD 3.0 fix the sex disparity in transplant access? Early post-implementation data are encouraging but preliminary [[Akabane2025MELD3]].
- How far can transplant criteria expand beyond Milan? Up-to-seven and UCSF show similar survival in selected groups [[Mazzaferro2009UpToSeven,Yao2001UCSF]], and downstaging is accepted [[Singal2023AASLDHCC]].
- Class-specific survival for Child–Pugh A/B/C: widely quoted 1- and 2-year survival figures could not be traced to a validated abstract in this pass, so none are given. Builders should take survival-by-decompensation figures from pass A/F.
