# F · Portal hypertension, varices & bleeding, ascites, SBP & kidney injury

Pass date: 2026-11-04. Modules served: portal, ascites.

Trial and cohort numbers come from PubMed/Europe PMC abstracts. Guideline wording is quoted from the full text of Baveno VII (PMC11090185) and of the EASL 2018 decompensated-cirrhosis guideline (official PDF on easl.eu). All survival and outcome figures are population statistics from the cited studies, not predictions for any one person.

## Findings

### portal · What portal hypertension is and how it is measured
- Cirrhosis replaces healthy liver tissue with scar (fibrosis) and regenerative nodules, which leads to portal hypertension. Progressive portal hypertension, systemic inflammation and liver failure drive outcomes [[Gines2021Lancet]].
- Portal hypertension has two parts. One is structural: fibrosis and nodules account for about 70% of the increased resistance inside the liver. The other is functional: endothelial dysfunction and less nitric oxide raise vascular tone [[DiazSoto2022TAG]].
- As collateral veins form, the splanchnic (gut) arteries widen. This increases blood flow into the portal system and causes systemic "effective hypovolaemia" with a hyperdynamic circulation [[DiazSoto2022TAG]].
- The hepatic venous pressure gradient (HVPG) is measured with a balloon catheter passed through the jugular vein into a hepatic vein. The wedged pressure minus the free pressure estimates the portal-to-caval gradient [[DiazSoto2022TAG]].
- Baveno VII prefers an end-hole, compliant balloon-occlusion catheter over a conventional straight catheter. It also requires at least 1 minute of stabilisation when recording the wedged hepatic venous pressure [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Baveno VII: "HVPG values >5 mmHg indicate sinusoidal portal hypertension" [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]]. The common teaching that normal is about 1–5 mmHg follows from this threshold but is not stated verbatim in the sources read (unverified).
- Clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH) is defined as HVPG ≥10 mmHg. HVPG measurement is the gold standard in viral- and alcohol-related cirrhosis [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- An HVPG >10 mmHg is the strongest predictor of developing varices and of any decompensating event [[DiazSoto2022TAG]].
- In primary biliary cholangitis, an extra pre-sinusoidal component means HVPG may underestimate portal hypertension [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- In 93 people with alcohol-related cirrhosis, none of the 49 who had bled from varices had an HVPG below 12 mmHg. All 72 people with varices had an HVPG above 12 mmHg [[GarciaTsao1985Hepatology]].
- Mean HVPG was 20.4 ± 5.1 mmHg in those who had bled versus 16.0 ± 5.2 mmHg in those who had not (p<0.001) [[GarciaTsao1985Hepatology]].
- In 213 people with compensated cirrhosis and no varices (median follow-up 51 months), 29% decompensated: 21.6% developed ascites, 3% variceal bleeding and 8% encephalopathy [[Ripoll2007Gastro]].
- In that cohort, each 1 mmHg of HVPG raised the hazard of decompensation (HR 1.11, 95% CI 1.05–1.17). People with HVPG <10 mmHg had a 90% probability of not decompensating over a median of about 4 years [[Ripoll2007Gastro]].
- Over 48 months, Baveno VII's "rule of 5" for liver stiffness by transient elastography (10–15–20–25 kPa) marks progressively higher relative risk of decompensation and liver-related death, whatever the cause [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Liver stiffness ≤15 kPa plus platelets ≥150×10⁹/L rules out CSPH (sensitivity and negative predictive value >90%). Liver stiffness ≥25 kPa rules it in (specificity and positive predictive value >90%) in viral, alcohol-related and non-obese NASH-related compensated advanced chronic liver disease [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- In the ANTICIPATE cohort (518 people with compensated advanced chronic liver disease), stiffness plus platelet count picked out 30% of patients with a <5% risk of varices needing treatment. An LSPS score >2.65 was associated with a >80% risk of CSPH [[Abraldes2016Anticipate]].
- Baveno VII: removing or suppressing the cause (SVR in hepatitis C, HBV suppression, long-term abstinence from alcohol) produces potentially meaningful falls in HVPG in most people and substantially reduces the risk of decompensation (Established) [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Statins may lower portal pressure. Baveno VII encourages them for people with cirrhosis who have an approved indication, at a lower dose in Child–Pugh B/C (simvastatin max 20 mg/d as published) (Early evidence for portal-pressure effect) [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII,DiazSoto2022TAG]].

### portal · Compensated vs decompensated cirrhosis
- Baveno VII defines decompensation by overt ascites (or pleural effusion with SAAG >1.1 g/dl), overt hepatic encephalopathy (West Haven ≥II) or variceal bleeding [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Compensated cirrhosis has two stages: without CSPH and with CSPH. People with CSPH are at higher risk of decompensation [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Median survival can exceed 12 years in compensated cirrhosis but is about 1.8 years once decompensated (population figures) [[DiazSoto2022TAG]].
- EASL 2018: the transition from compensated to decompensated cirrhosis occurs at about 5–7% per year [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- In a historical cohort of 293 people with biopsy-proven compensated cirrhosis (1968–1980), the 10-year probability of decompensation was 58% and 10-year survival 47% [[Gines1987]].
- Baveno VII defines "further decompensation" as an even higher-mortality stage. Examples are a second portal-hypertensive event or jaundice, recurrent bleeding, recurrent ascites (≥3 large-volume paracenteses in 1 year), recurrent encephalopathy, SBP or HRS-AKI [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Baveno VII states that people with decompensated cirrhosis should be considered for liver transplantation [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].

### portal · Varices: how common, how often they bleed
- Oesophageal varices are present in 50–60% of people with compensated cirrhosis and up to 85% with decompensated cirrhosis [[DiazSoto2022TAG]]. The brief's lower estimate (~30–40% / ~60%) was not found in sources read (unverified).
- A first variceal bleed occurs at 10–15% per year depending on risk factors. Rebleeding occurs at up to 60% per year [[DiazSoto2022TAG]].
- Without secondary prophylaxis, rebleeding occurs in about 60–70% of people, usually within 1–2 years [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- In 206 people with no or small varices, new varices appeared in 5% at 1 year and 28% at 3 years. Small varices grew in 12% at 1 year and 31% at 3 years [[Merli2003JHepatol]].
- Two-year bleeding risk was 12% with small varices at entry versus 2% without varices (p<0.01). Red wale marks predicted bleeding [[Merli2003JHepatol]].
- NIEC study, 321 people with varices and no prior bleed: 26.5% bled over a median of 23 months. Bleeding risk rose with Child class, varix size and red wale marks [[NIEC1988NEJM]].
- In the NIEC index, 1-year bleeding probability ranged from 6% to 76% across risk groups [[NIEC1988NEJM]].
- Gastric varices occur in about 20% of people with cirrhosis. GOV1 (lesser-curve extension) are the most common type, at 75% of gastric varices [[EASL2018Decomp,DiazSoto2022TAG]].
- In 568 people with portal hypertension, gastric varices bled less often than oesophageal varices (25% vs 64%) but more severely (4.8 vs 2.9 transfusion units). Isolated fundal varices (IGV1) bled in 78% [[Sarin1992Hepatology]].
- High-risk varices for primary prophylaxis (EASL) are small varices with red signs, medium or large varices whatever the Child–Pugh class, or small varices in Child–Pugh C [[EASL2018Decomp]].

### portal · Preventing the first decompensation and the first bleed
- Timolol did not prevent varices in unselected people with HVPG ≥6 mmHg (39% vs 40%, p=0.89). It caused more serious adverse events (18% vs 6%) [[Groszmann2005NEJM]].
- In that trial, varices developed less often when baseline HVPG was <10 mmHg [[Groszmann2005NEJM]].
- PREDESCI (201 people with compensated cirrhosis and CSPH, no high-risk varices): decompensation or death occurred in 16% with β-blockers (propranolol or carvedilol) vs 27% with placebo (HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.26–0.97) [[Villanueva2019PREDESCI]].
- In PREDESCI the benefit came mainly from less ascites (HR 0.44, 95% CI 0.20–0.97). Adverse events were similar between groups [[Villanueva2019PREDESCI]].
- PREDESCI doses were up to 160 mg propranolol twice daily, or carvedilol ≤25 mg/day, titrated individually [[Villanueva2019PREDESCI]].
- Individual-patient meta-analysis of 4 RCTs (352 compensated patients with CSPH): carvedilol lowered decompensation risk (SHR 0.506, 95% CI 0.289–0.887) and death (SHR 0.417, 95% CI 0.194–0.896) vs control [[Villanueva2022Carvedilol]].
- Baveno VII: NSBBs (propranolol, nadolol or carvedilol) "should be considered" to prevent decompensation in people with CSPH. Carvedilol is the preferred NSBB in compensated cirrhosis [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Carvedilol lowers HVPG more because it adds anti-α1-adrenergic vasodilation to non-selective β-blockade [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII,DiazSoto2022TAG]].
- People on NSBBs to prevent decompensation do not need screening endoscopy, because the result would not change management [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- People who cannot take NSBBs should have screening endoscopy if liver stiffness is ≥20 kPa or platelets ≤150×10⁹/L. Those under both thresholds can avoid endoscopy and repeat elastography and platelets yearly [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Baveno VII states there is no indication for NSBBs in people without CSPH [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Carvedilol 12.5 mg once daily vs band ligation in 152 people with grade II+ varices: first bleed 10% vs 23% (relative hazard 0.41, 95% CI 0.19–0.96). Mortality did not differ (35% vs 37%) [[Tripathi2009Hepatology]].
- In compensated people with high-risk varices who cannot take NSBBs, endoscopic band ligation is recommended to prevent a first bleed [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- In people with ascites, Baveno VII advises reducing or stopping NSBBs if systolic BP <90 mmHg, MAP <65 mmHg or HRS-AKI develops, and re-starting once these resolve [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].

### portal · Acute variceal bleeding: the bundle
- Each variceal bleed carries about 15–25% mortality at 6 weeks (EASL) [[EASL2018Decomp]]. Recent series report 15–20% [[DiazSoto2022TAG]]. In a derivation cohort of 178 patients, 6-week mortality was 16% [[Reverter2014Gastro]].
- Five-year mortality is over 80% when bleeding occurs alongside other decompensation, versus 20% when bleeding is the only decompensating event [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- In-hospital mortality after variceal bleeding at one French centre fell from 42.6% (1980) to 14.5% (2000), as vasoactive drugs, endoscopy and antibiotic prophylaxis came into use [[Carbonell2004Hepatology]].
- In that cohort, rebleeding fell from 47% to 13%, and bacterial infection from 38% to 14% [[Carbonell2004Hepatology]].
- A MELD of 19 or more predicted ≥20% 6-week mortality. A MELD below 11 predicted <5% [[Reverter2014Gastro]].
- Baveno VII: transfuse red cells conservatively, with "a target haemoglobin level between 7–8 g/dl", adjusted for heart disease, age, haemodynamics and ongoing bleeding [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Restrictive (Hb <7 g/dl) vs liberal (<9 g/dl) transfusion in 921 people with severe upper GI bleeding: 6-week survival 95% vs 91% (HR for death 0.55, 95% CI 0.33–0.92). Further bleeding was 10% vs 16% [[Villanueva2013NEJM]].
- Benefit was significant in cirrhosis Child–Pugh A/B (HR 0.30, 95% CI 0.11–0.85) but not Child–Pugh C (HR 1.04). Portal pressure rose with liberal transfusion [[Villanueva2013NEJM]].
- Vasoactive drugs (terlipressin, somatostatin, octreotide) "should be started as soon as possible and continued for 2–5 days" [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Meta-analysis of 30 RCTs (3111 patients): vasoactive drugs lowered 7-day mortality (RR 0.74, 95% CI 0.57–0.95) and improved haemostasis (RR 1.21) [[Wells2012APT]].
- EASL 2018 doses as published: terlipressin 2 mg every 4 h for the first 48 h, then 1 mg every 4 h; somatostatin 250 µg bolus then 250 µg/h (up to 500 µg/h); octreotide 50 µg bolus then 50 µg/h [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- Baveno VII warns of hyponatraemia on terlipressin, especially with preserved liver function, and advises monitoring sodium [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Antibiotic prophylaxis "is an integral part of therapy" and starts from admission. Baveno VII suggests ceftriaxone 1 g/24 h in advanced cirrhosis or settings with quinolone resistance [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Cochrane review (12 trials, 1241 patients): antibiotic prophylaxis during upper GI bleeding reduced mortality (RR 0.79, 95% CI 0.63–0.98), bacterial infections (RR 0.36) and rebleeding (RR 0.53) [[ChavezTapia2010Cochrane]].
- An earlier meta-analysis (5 trials, 534 patients) found short-term antibiotic prophylaxis during GI bleeding raised the mean survival rate by 9.1% (95% CI 2.9–15.3) and the rate of being infection-free by 32% [[Bernard1999Hepatology]].
- Bacterial infections occur in more than 50% of people with variceal bleeding, and are already present at admission in 20% [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- Endoscopy should be done within 12 h of presentation once haemodynamically resuscitated, or as soon as safely possible if the person is unstable [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Baveno VII advises considering erythromycin 250 mg IV given 30–120 min before endoscopy, if there is no QT prolongation [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Band ligation is the recommended endoscopic therapy for oesophageal variceal bleeding. Tissue adhesive (cyanoacrylate) is used for isolated gastric varices and GOV2 [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Ligation vs sclerotherapy meta-analysis (7 RCTs): rebleeding OR 0.52, mortality OR 0.67, strictures OR 0.10. Number needed to treat was 4 to avert one rebleed and 10 to prevent one death [[Laine1995AnnIntern]].
- Baveno VII advises intubation before endoscopy in people with altered consciousness or active haematemesis, and stopping PPIs after endoscopy unless strictly indicated [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Five-day treatment failure means uncontrolled bleeding or rebleeding within 5 days. Six-week mortality is the main outcome measure [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].

### portal · Pre-emptive TIPS, rescue and balloon tamponade
- Early TIPS RCT (63 high-risk patients: Child–Pugh C, or B with active bleeding): 1-year survival 86% with early TIPS within 72 h vs 61% with drugs plus band ligation (p<0.001) [[GarciaPagan2010NEJM]].
- In that RCT, freedom from rebleeding or failure to control bleeding at 1 year was 97% vs 50% [[GarciaPagan2010NEJM]].
- Chinese RCT (129 Child–Pugh B/C): transplant-free survival with early TIPS was 86% vs 73% at 1 year (HR 0.50, 95% CI 0.25–0.98) and 99% vs 84% at 6 weeks [[Lv2019LGH]].
- Observational study (671 high-risk patients, 34 centres): in Child–Pugh C, 1-year mortality was 22% with pre-emptive TIPS vs 47% with drugs plus endoscopy [[HernandezGea2019Hepatology]].
- Individual-patient meta-analysis (7 studies, 1327 patients): pre-emptive TIPS improved 1-year survival (HR 0.443, 95% CI 0.323–0.607), both in CP-B with active bleeding and in CP-C <14 points, without increasing encephalopathy [[NicoaraFarcau2021Gastro]].
- Baveno VII criteria for pre-emptive PTFE-covered TIPS within 72 h (ideally <24 h): Child–Pugh C <14 points, or Child–Pugh B >7 with active bleeding at endoscopy, or HVPG >20 mmHg at the time of bleeding [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- ACLF, encephalopathy at admission and high bilirubin should not be treated as contraindications to pre-emptive TIPS [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- TIPS may be futile with Child–Pugh ≥14, or MELD >30 plus lactate >12 mmol/L, unless transplant is planned soon [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Up to 10–15% of people have persistent or early rebleeding despite drugs, ligation and antibiotics. Salvage PTFE-covered TIPS is the rescue therapy [[EASL2018Decomp,deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Balloon tamponade or self-expanding metal stents are a bridge to definitive treatment. EASL limits balloon tamponade to a maximum of 24 h [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII,EASL2018Decomp]].
- Stent vs balloon tamponade RCT (28 patients with refractory bleeding): treatment success 66% vs 20%, bleeding control 85% vs 47%, serious adverse events 15% vs 47%. Six-week survival did not differ significantly (54% vs 40%) [[Escorsell2016Hepatology]].
- First-line prevention of rebleeding is NSBB (or carvedilol) plus band ligation. TIPS is the treatment of choice for rebleeding despite this [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Combination therapy meta-analysis (23 trials, 1860 patients): rebleeding was lower with endoscopy plus β-blocker than with endoscopy alone (RR 0.68) or β-blocker alone (RR 0.71). The mortality difference was not significant [[Gonzalez2008AnnIntern]].
- A ≥20% fall in HVPG on propranolol after a bleed was linked to 3-year rebleeding of 9%, versus 66% with a smaller fall (RR 0.09). No one whose HVPG fell to ≤12 mmHg rebled [[Feu1995Lancet]].
- After a bleed, Baveno VII recommends abdominal imaging (contrast CT or MRI) to look for splanchnic vein thrombosis and HCC and to map collaterals [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].

### ascites · Why fluid builds up
- Ascites is the most common decompensating event: 5–10% of people with compensated cirrhosis develop it each year [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- The mainstay of ascites formation is renal sodium retention from activation of the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone and sympathetic nervous systems [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- The peripheral arterial vasodilation hypothesis: early arterial (splanchnic) vasodilation "underfills" the arterial tree, which activates RAAS, vasopressin and sympathetic tone. Portal hypertension directs the retained fluid into the abdomen [[Schrier1988Hepatology]].
- The same hypothesis explains the continuum from compensated cirrhosis to decompensation to hepatorenal syndrome [[Schrier1988Hepatology]].
- EASL ascites grades: grade 1 is seen only on ultrasound, grade 2 is moderate symmetrical distension, grade 3 is marked distension [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- A serum–ascites albumin gradient (SAAG) ≥1.1 g/dl indicates portal hypertension with about 97% accuracy [[EASL2018Decomp]]. In 901 samples it classified the cause correctly 96.7% of the time, vs 55.6% for the exudate–transudate concept [[Runyon1992AnnIntern]].
- Diagnostic paracentesis is recommended at first ascites and at hospital admission. Neutrophil count, total protein, albumin and culture (bedside inoculation of ≥10 ml into blood-culture bottles) should be assessed [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- In a US national sample of 17,711 admissions with ascites, only 61% had a paracentesis. Those who did had lower in-hospital mortality (6.5% vs 8.5%; adjusted OR 0.55) (observational) [[Orman2014CGH]].
- Prognosis: one- and two-year mortality after ascites develops is about 40% and 50% (EASL; population figures) [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- Hospital cohort after a first significant ascites (263 people, about 41 months): 1- and 5-year survival 85% and 56.5%. Five-year probability of dilutional hyponatraemia was 37.1%, refractory ascites 11.4% and HRS 11.4% [[Planas2006CGH]].
- After developing dilutional hyponatraemia, refractory ascites or type 2 HRS, 1-year survival was 25.6%, 31.6% and 38.5%. Mean survival after type 1 HRS was 7 ± 2 days [[Planas2006CGH]].

### ascites · Salt, diuretics and their limits
- EASL 2018: "a moderate restriction of sodium intake (80–120 mmol/day, corresponding to 4.6–6.9 g of salt)" for moderate, uncomplicated ascites [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- AASLD 2021 is widely reported to recommend sodium restriction of 2 g (88–90 mmol) per day [[Biggins2021AASLD]] (unverified: primary text paywalled; not confirmed against original).
- EASL 2018, first grade 2 ascites: an anti-mineralocorticoid alone, "starting at 100 mg/day with stepwise increases every 72 h (in 100 mg steps) to a maximum of 400 mg/day" [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- EASL 2018: add furosemide if weight loss is <2 kg/week or hyperkalaemia develops, "from 40 mg/day to a maximum of 160 mg/day (in 40 mg steps)". Long-standing or recurrent ascites is treated with the combination [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- The 100:40 mg spironolactone:furosemide starting ratio is attributed to AASLD guidance [[Biggins2021AASLD]] (unverified: primary text paywalled).
- Aldactone US label (oedema in cirrhosis): "initiate therapy in a hospital setting and titrate slowly". Recommended initial daily dosage 100 mg (range 25–200 mg), given at least 5 days before increasing [[FDA-Aldactone]].
- In moderate ascites, spironolactone alone vs spironolactone plus furosemide (100 patients) gave similar response (94% vs 98%). The combination needed dose reduction more often (68% vs 34%) [[Santos2003JHepatol]].
- Combined vs sequential diuretics (100 patients): fewer adverse effects with combined therapy (20% vs 38%), including hyperkalaemia (4% vs 18%) [[Angeli2010Gut]].
- EASL: stop diuretics for serum sodium <125 mmol/L, AKI, worsening encephalopathy or incapacitating cramps. Stop furosemide for potassium <3 mmol/L [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- EASL refractory-ascites criteria include no response to spironolactone 400 mg/day plus furosemide 160 mg/day for at least a week on <90 mmol/day sodium. Lack of response means mean weight loss <0.8 kg over 4 days and urinary sodium below intake [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- The International Ascites Club consensus set the stepwise approach: modest salt restriction and spironolactone first, paracentesis plus colloid for gross ascites, and LVP or TIPS for refractory ascites, with early transplant evaluation [[Moore2003Hepatology]].
- Refractory ascites carries a median survival of about 6 months, and EASL advises immediate transplant referral [[EASL2018Decomp]].

### ascites · Large-volume paracentesis and albumin
- EASL: large-volume paracentesis (LVP) is first-line for grade 3 ascites, removing all the fluid in one session [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- EASL: for LVP of more than 5 L, give albumin "8 g/L of ascites removed". Below 5 L the risk of post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction is low [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- The AASLD and EASL figure is commonly given as 6–8 g of albumin per litre removed for LVP >5 L. This dose was set empirically by expert opinion [[Trebicka2025Hepatology,Biggins2021AASLD]].
- RCT of paracentesis with vs without albumin (105 patients): renal impairment or severe hyponatraemia developed in 1 vs 11 patients (p<0.01) [[Gines1988Gastro]].
- Meta-analysis of 17 trials (1225 patients): compared with alternatives, albumin cut post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction (OR 0.39), hyponatraemia (OR 0.58) and mortality (OR 0.64) [[Bernardi2012Hepatology]].
- EASL: diuretics should be stopped in refractory ascites if urinary sodium stays ≤30 mmol/day on diuretics [[EASL2018Decomp]].

### ascites · TIPS for recurrent or refractory ascites
- Baveno VII: TIPS "should be considered in patients with recurrent ascites (requirement of ≥3 large-volume paracenteses within 1 year)", whether or not varices are present [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Individual-patient meta-analysis (305 patients, uncovered stents): tense ascites recurred in 42% with TIPS vs 89% with paracentesis. Transplant-free survival was better with TIPS (p=0.035), but encephalopathy episodes were more frequent [[Salerno2007Gastro]].
- Covered-stent TIPS vs repeated LVP plus albumin (62 people with recurrent ascites): 1-year transplant-free survival 93% vs 52% [[Bureau2017Gastro]].
- In that trial, paracenteses totalled 32 vs 320, and hospital days 17 vs 35. One-year freedom from encephalopathy was 65% in each group [[Bureau2017Gastro]].
- EASL notes that after-TIPS encephalopathy can affect up to 50% with bare stents and falls to about 18% with 8 mm PTFE-covered stents [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- EASL summary of the evidence: TIPS improves survival over LVP in recurrent ascites but has not been shown to in fully refractory ascites [[EASL2018Decomp]].

### ascites · Long-term albumin: promise and limits
- ANSWER (431 people with uncomplicated ascites on diuretics): 18-month survival 77% with albumin 40 g twice weekly for 2 weeks then 40 g weekly, vs 66% with standard care (HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.40–0.95) [[Caraceni2018ANSWER]].
- ATTIRE (777 hospitalised with decompensated cirrhosis, albumin <30 g/L): targeted daily 20% albumin did not reduce infection, kidney dysfunction or death at days 3–15 (29.7% vs 30.2%) and caused more serious adverse events [[China2021ATTIRE]].
- Midodrine plus albumin (40 g every 15 days) in 196 people awaiting transplant did not reduce complications or 1-year mortality [[Sola2018JHepatol]].
- Baveno VII: long-term albumin "may reduce complications ... and improve transplant-free survival" in uncomplicated ascites, but "a formal recommendation cannot be given until further data become available" (Early evidence) [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Volume overload is increasingly recognised as an albumin complication. Patient selection, dose and schedule still need refining [[Trebicka2025Hepatology]].

### ascites · Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP)
- SBP is diagnosed by an ascitic neutrophil (PMN) count above 250 cells/µl (≥250 cells/mm³) [[EASL2018Decomp,Evans2003Hepatology]].
- Ascitic culture is negative in up to 60% of people with clinical SBP and a raised neutrophil count [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- SBP prevalence is 1.5–3.5% in outpatients and about 10% in hospitalised patients. Half of episodes are present at admission [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- In 427 asymptomatic outpatients having paracentesis, neutrocytic ascites was found in 3.5% [[Evans2003Hepatology]].
- In 405 admissions for decompensation, 34% had a bacterial infection: urinary 41%, ascites 23%, blood 21%, respiratory 17%. Bacterial peritonitis prevalence was 12% [[Borzio2001DLD]].
- Infections increase mortality about 4-fold (pooled OR 3.75). After SBP, median mortality was 31.5% at 1 month and 66.2% at 12 months across 101 studies [[Arvaniti2010Gastro]].
- SBP has about 20% hospital mortality even when the infection resolves. Once a person survives SBP, recurrence at 1 year is about 70% and 1-year survival 30–50% [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- Sort trial (126 people with SBP): renal impairment was 10% with cefotaxime plus albumin vs 33% with cefotaxime alone. In-hospital mortality was 10% vs 29%, and 3-month mortality 22% vs 41% [[Sort1999NEJM]].
- Albumin dosing in the Sort trial and EASL, as published: 1.5 g/kg at diagnosis and 1 g/kg on day 3 [[Sort1999NEJM,EASL2018Decomp]].
- Multidrug-resistant bacteria caused 4% of community-acquired, 14% of healthcare-associated and 35% of hospital-acquired infections. They doubled mortality (25% vs 12%) [[Fernandez2012MDR]].
- Long-term norfloxacin prophylaxis was itself a risk factor for multiresistant infection (HR 2.69) [[Fernandez2012MDR]].
- Secondary prophylaxis RCT: norfloxacin 400 mg/day cut SBP recurrence from 35% to 12%. One-year recurrence probability was 20% vs 68% [[Gines1990Norfloxacin]].
- Primary prophylaxis RCT in high-risk patients (ascitic protein <15 g/L with advanced liver or kidney dysfunction): 1-year SBP was 7% vs 61% and HRS 28% vs 41%. Three-month survival was 94% vs 62% [[Fernandez2007Gastro]].
- Baveno VII: primary antibiotic prophylaxis for selected high-risk people (GI bleeding, Child–Pugh C with low-protein ascites). Secondary prophylaxis after any SBP. Rifaximin is not indicated for SBP prophylaxis [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Baveno VII: everyone admitted with decompensation should be checked for infection, including diagnostic paracentesis, chest X-ray, cultures of blood, ascites and urine, and skin examination [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Bacterial infections arise from cirrhosis-associated immune dysfunction and translocation of gut bacteria. They can trigger bleeding, hyponatraemia, encephalopathy, kidney failure and ACLF [[Jalan2014JHepatol]].

### ascites · Acute kidney injury and hepatorenal syndrome
- AKI occurs in about 20% of people hospitalised with cirrhosis. The main causes are prerenal (volume-responsive), acute tubular necrosis and HRS [[GarciaTsao2008AKI]].
- ICA-AKI definitions in EASL 2018: AKI is a rise in serum creatinine ≥0.3 mg/dl within 48 h, or ≥50% within 7 days. Stage 1 is ≥0.3 mg/dl or 1.5–2-fold, stage 2 is >2–3-fold, stage 3 is >3-fold or ≥4.0 mg/dl, or renal replacement [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- Pre-renal AKI accounts for about 68% of AKI in hospitalised decompensated cirrhosis [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- EASL: for AKI stage >1A without an obvious cause, or infection-related AKI, give 20% albumin 1 g/kg (maximum 100 g) for two consecutive days, as published [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- Among 547 admissions with acute decompensation, 53% had AKI. Ninety-day survival was 82% for stage 1A (creatinine <1.5 mg/dl) vs 55% for stage 1B (≥1.5 mg/dl) [[Huelin2017CGH]].
- Among 463 classifiable cases of renal failure, causes were infection 46%, hypovolaemia 32%, HRS 13% and parenchymal disease 9% [[MartinLlahi2011Gastro]].
- Three-month survival in that series was 73% for parenchymal nephropathy, 46% for hypovolaemia, 31% for infection and 15% for HRS [[MartinLlahi2011Gastro]].
- In 234 people with ascites and normal kidney function, HRS probability was 18% at 1 year and 39% at 5 years. Low serum sodium, high renin and a small liver predicted it [[Gines1993HRS]].
- AKI-HRS is kidney failure from renal vasoconstriction without major structural damage, driven by splanchnic vasodilation and systemic inflammation. SBP is the main trigger, and median survival is ≤3 months [[Gines2018HRS]].
- Transplantation is the ideal treatment for AKI-HRS. Medical therapy pairs vasoconstrictors with albumin [[Gines2018HRS]].
- CONFIRM (300 people with HRS-1): verified HRS reversal was 32% with terlipressin plus albumin vs 17% with placebo plus albumin (p=0.006) [[Wong2021CONFIRM]].
- In CONFIRM, death from respiratory disorders within 90 days occurred in 11% vs 2%, and 90-day mortality was 51% vs 45% [[Wong2021CONFIRM]].
- CONFIRM post hoc analysis: respiratory failure on terlipressin occurred in 9.4% with ACLF grades 1–2 vs 30% with ACLF grade 3. Hypoxaemia predicted risk [[Wong2022APT]].
- REVERSE (196 patients): confirmed HRS reversal 19.6% vs 13.1% (not significant). Creatinine fell 1.1 vs 0.6 mg/dl [[Boyer2016REVERSE]].
- 2008 terlipressin RCT (112 patients): HRS reversal 34% vs 13% [[Sanyal2008Gastro]].
- Terlivaz US label: indicated "to improve kidney function in adults with hepatorenal syndrome with rapid reduction in kidney function". Patients with creatinine >5 mg/dL are unlikely to benefit [[FDA-Terlivaz]].
- Terlivaz boxed warning: "serious or fatal respiratory failure". Risk is higher with volume overload or ACLF grade 3. Do not start if SpO₂ <90%, and monitor continuously [[FDA-Terlivaz]].
- Terlivaz label dosing as published: 0.85 mg IV every 6 h on days 1–3, then adjusted on day 4 by creatinine change (up to 1.7 mg every 6 h, or stop), for up to 14 days [[FDA-Terlivaz]].
- EASL 2018 terlipressin wording for HRS-AKI: IV boluses starting at 1 mg every 4–6 h, or continuous infusion starting at 2 mg/day to reduce adverse effects [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- Baveno VII: short-term albumin is indicated for SBP, AKI >stage 1A, large-volume paracentesis, and with terlipressin for HRS-AKI [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].

### ascites · Hyponatraemia
- In 997 people with cirrhosis, serum sodium was ≤135 mmol/L in 49.4%, ≤130 in 21.6%, ≤125 in 5.7% and ≤120 in 1.2% [[Angeli2006Hepatology]].
- Low sodium went with refractory ascites, encephalopathy, SBP and HRS [[Angeli2006Hepatology]].
- In transplant-waitlist data, death risk rose 5% per 1 mmol/L fall in sodium between 125 and 140 (HR 1.05), on top of MELD. This led to MELD-Na [[Kim2008MELDNa]].
- EASL: "fluid restriction to 1,000 ml/day is recommended in the management of hypervolemic hyponatremia". Hypertonic saline is limited to severely symptomatic cases [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- Samsca (tolvaptan) US label: avoid use in people with underlying liver disease, including cirrhosis. Limit treatment to 30 days and start it in hospital [[FDA-Samsca]].
- In the Samsca hyponatraemia trials, GI bleeding occurred in 10% of tolvaptan-treated people with cirrhosis vs 2% on placebo [[FDA-Samsca]].

### Exam hooks
- HVPG >5 mmHg = portal hypertension. ≥10 mmHg = clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH) [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- No variceal bleeding was seen below an HVPG of 12 mmHg in the classic 1985 series [[GarciaTsao1985Hepatology]].
- A ≥20% fall in HVPG, or a fall to ≤12 mmHg, protects against rebleeding [[Feu1995Lancet]].
- PREDESCI: β-blockers cut decompensation or death from 27% to 16%, mostly by preventing ascites [[Villanueva2019PREDESCI]].
- Carvedilol is the preferred NSBB in compensated cirrhosis with CSPH [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Baveno VI/VII rule: liver stiffness <20 kPa and platelets >150×10⁹/L means a screening endoscopy can be skipped (when not on NSBBs) [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Bleeding bundle: vasoactive drug early for 2–5 days, antibiotics from admission, endoscopy within 12 h, band ligation, transfuse to Hb 7–8 g/dl [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Restrictive transfusion improved 6-week survival (95% vs 91%) [[Villanueva2013NEJM]].
- Pre-emptive TIPS within 72 h for Child–Pugh C <14 or B >7 with active bleeding: 1-year survival 86% vs 61% in the original RCT [[GarciaPagan2010NEJM,deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Balloon tamponade is only a bridge, for a maximum of 24 h. Oesophageal stents are as effective and safer [[EASL2018Decomp,deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- SAAG ≥1.1 g/dl means portal-hypertensive ascites [[EASL2018Decomp,Runyon1992AnnIntern]].
- SBP = ascitic neutrophils >250 cells/µl [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- SBP: albumin 1.5 g/kg day 1 and 1 g/kg day 3 cut renal impairment (33%→10%) and death (29%→10%) [[Sort1999NEJM]].
- LVP >5 L: albumin 8 g per litre removed (EASL). 6–8 g/L is the commonly cited range [[EASL2018Decomp,Trebicka2025Hepatology]].
- EASL diuretics: spironolactone 100→400 mg/day, furosemide 40→160 mg/day [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- Recurrent ascites (≥3 LVPs per year) is an indication to consider TIPS [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- AKI in cirrhosis: creatinine up ≥0.3 mg/dl in 48 h or ≥50% in 7 days [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- HRS-AKI has the worst renal-failure prognosis: 15% 3-month survival in one series [[MartinLlahi2011Gastro]].
- CONFIRM: terlipressin HRS reversal 32% vs 17%. The label carries a boxed warning for respiratory failure [[Wong2021CONFIRM,FDA-Terlivaz]].
- Hyponatraemia (<130 mmol/L) affects about 1 in 5 people with cirrhosis and predicts death beyond MELD [[Angeli2006Hepatology,Kim2008MELDNa]].

### In plain words
- Scarring in the liver makes it hard for blood from the gut to pass through, so pressure builds up in the portal vein. Doctors call this portal hypertension [[Gines2021Lancet,DiazSoto2022TAG]].
- When the pressure is high, blood finds detours. Some of these detours are swollen veins in the food pipe or stomach, called varices, and they can burst and bleed [[DiazSoto2022TAG]].
- A blood-pressure pill from the beta-blocker family, often carvedilol, can lower that pressure and help prevent problems for some people. Only your care team can decide if it fits you [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII,Villanueva2019PREDESCI]].
- A liver stiffness scan plus a simple blood count can sometimes show that a camera test for varices is not needed yet [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Bleeding from varices is an emergency. Hospitals treat it with medicine through a drip, antibiotics, a camera test to put bands on the veins, and careful blood transfusion [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Fluid in the belly (ascites) is the most common sign that cirrhosis has moved to a more serious stage [[EASL2018Decomp]].
- The kidneys hold on to salt and water in cirrhosis, so care teams often advise less salt and may prescribe water pills (diuretics) [[EASL2018Decomp,Schrier1988Hepatology]].
- If a lot of fluid builds up, it can be drained with a needle. Albumin, a protein given through a drip, helps protect the circulation and kidneys afterwards [[EASL2018Decomp,Bernardi2012Hepatology]].
- The belly fluid can get infected (SBP), sometimes with few symptoms. A small sample of fluid is the way to check [[EASL2018Decomp,Evans2003Hepatology]].
- The kidneys can suddenly stop working well in cirrhosis, often after an infection or bleeding. Early treatment matters [[GarciaTsao2008AKI,Gines2018HRS]].
- Low sodium in the blood is common and is a sign to talk with your care team. Do not change how much you drink unless they advise it [[Angeli2006Hepatology,EASL2018Decomp]].
- Vomiting blood, black or tarry stools, new confusion or drowsiness, or fever or belly pain with a swollen abdomen need urgent care: call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department.
- The survival numbers on this page describe large groups of people and cannot predict what will happen to you. Treatments have improved a lot over time [[Carbonell2004Hepatology]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Sodium target: EASL 2018 recommends moderate restriction of 80–120 mmol/day (4.6–6.9 g salt) and warns that strict restriction can impair nutrition [[EASL2018Decomp]]. AASLD 2021 is reported to recommend 2 g/day sodium [[Biggins2021AASLD]] (unverified). The two differ in units and strictness. Status: both stress moderation, but the exact target is guideline-specific.
- Long-term albumin: ANSWER showed a survival benefit in outpatients with ascites [[Caraceni2018ANSWER]]. ATTIRE (hospitalised, targeted albumin) [[China2021ATTIRE]] and midodrine plus albumin (waitlist) [[Sola2018JHepatol]] showed none, with volume-overload harms. Baveno VII withholds a formal recommendation [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]]. Status: open. PRECIOSA (NCT03451292) is cited as pending in Trebicka 2025 [[Trebicka2025Hepatology]].
- Terlipressin benefit vs harm: CONFIRM met its primary endpoint [[Wong2021CONFIRM]] but REVERSE did not [[Boyer2016REVERSE]]. Respiratory deaths were higher, especially in ACLF grade 3 [[Wong2022APT,FDA-Terlivaz]]. Status: approved in the US with a boxed warning. Patient selection by oxygenation and ACLF grade is key.
- Pre-emptive TIPS in Child–Pugh B with active bleeding: the 2019 observational study found no mortality benefit in this subgroup [[HernandezGea2019Hepatology]]. The 2021 individual-patient meta-analysis did find one (HR 0.524) [[NicoaraFarcau2021Gastro]]. Status: Baveno VII includes CP-B >7 with active bleeding [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- TIPS for refractory vs recurrent ascites: covered TIPS clearly helped in recurrent ascites [[Bureau2017Gastro]]. EASL notes survival benefit is not proven in strictly refractory ascites [[EASL2018Decomp]]. Status: Baveno VII frames the indication as ≥3 LVPs per year [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Restrictive transfusion in Child–Pugh C: the survival benefit was seen in Child–Pugh A/B but not C (HR 1.04) [[Villanueva2013NEJM]]. Status: guidelines keep a 7–8 g/dl target with individual adjustment [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- Quinolone prophylaxis and resistance: norfloxacin prevents SBP [[Gines1990Norfloxacin,Fernandez2007Gastro]] but is a risk factor for multidrug-resistant infections [[Fernandez2012MDR]]. Status: Baveno VII restricts primary prophylaxis to selected high-risk people [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]].
- β-blockers in advanced ascites: Baveno VII endorses them for preventing bleeding in ascites but advises dose reduction or stopping with hypotension or HRS-AKI [[deFranchis2022BavenoVII]]. Status: managed by blood-pressure and kidney thresholds rather than an absolute ban.
