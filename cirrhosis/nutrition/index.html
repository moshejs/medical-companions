Nutrition, muscle & medicine safety

Why a night without food is a long fast for a liver with cirrhosis, how a late evening snack shortens it, why muscle matters so much, and which everyday medicines need extra care.

Cirrhosis has many causes, including MASLD, alcohol-related liver disease, viral hepatitis, and autoimmune, cholestatic and genetic diseases. 180 The nutrition and medicine questions on this page apply whatever the cause. Every target and dose below is quoted as published, not as advice: your own team and dietitian set yours.

How common is muscle loss?

Estimates depend on how sarcopenia is measured (usually CT skeletal muscle index at the third lumbar vertebra), on the cut-offs and on who was studied. AASLD 2021 gives "30%-70% of patients with end-stage liver disease"; a 2023 meta-analysis pooled 33%. 255,294 Switch between a single waiting-list cohort and the pooled estimate.

Waiting-list cohort: Tandon 2012 (41% overall; 54% of men, 21% of women). Pooled: Mazeaud 2023, 36 studies, 8,821 people. 294,445

Why the numbers differ

Same word, different rulers

Guideline ranges (30–70%) run higher than the pooled figure (33%) because studies use different CT measures, different cut-offs and different populations. 255,294 In a five-centre study of 396 transplant candidates, the cut-offs that best predicted waiting-list death were a skeletal muscle index under 50 cm²/m² for men and under 39 cm²/m² for women. 63

In plain wordsMuscle loss is common in cirrhosis, and it becomes more common as the liver disease advances.
Exam hookSarcopenia predicted waiting-list death independently of MELD (HR 2.36), and skeletal muscle index barely correlated with MELD (r = −0.07): muscle adds information the score misses. 308,445

Frailty: the Liver Frailty Index bands

The Liver Frailty Index combines three bedside tests: grip strength, chair stands and balance. It was derived in 536 listed outpatients; for death on the waiting list within 3 months its C-statistic was 0.76, against 0.80 for MELD-Na and 0.82 for both together. 253 Move the slider to see where a value falls in the published bands.

Teaching tool

Strength you can measure at the bedside

AASLD 2021 bands: robust under 3.2, prefrail 3.2–4.3, frail 4.4 or more; some studies use 4.5. 194,255 In 1,405 candidates the best cut-off was 4.4 at 3 months and 4.2 at 6 and 12 months, and adding the index raised the 3-month AUC from 0.73 (MELD-Na) to 0.79. 229 Frailty roughly doubled waiting-list mortality at any age (adjusted sHR 1.92). 194 Change matters too: in 1,093 outpatients, each 0.1-unit worsening per 3 months carried an sHR of 2.04 for death or delisting. 254

Teaching tool onlyThese bands are shown to explain the published research. They are not for diagnosis, prognosis or transplant decisions. AASLD 2021 says frailty or sarcopenia should not be used as an absolute contraindication to transplant. 255

What the published per-kilogram targets mean for one example body

Guidelines express energy and protein per kilogram. With ascites, part of the weight on the scale is fluid, so EASL 2019 and AASLD 2021 describe estimating a dry weight by subtracting 5% (mild), 10% (moderate) or 15% (severe ascites), plus 5% for swelling of both feet. 107,255 This worked example applies those published numbers to a weight you choose.

Not a prescriptionThe numbers are arithmetic on published wording: EASL 2019 says energy "should not be lower than the recommended 35 kcal/kg.BW/d (in non-obese individuals)" and protein "should not be lower than the recommended 1.2–1.5 g/kg.BW/d"; AASLD 2021 gives protein per kilogram of ideal body weight, 1.2–2.0 g/kg for critically ill adults, and lower energy figures at a BMI of 30 or more. 107,255 A dietitian sets real targets.

Medicine safety: sort the medicines

Most medicines "have not been adequately studied in cirrhosis", and changed drug handling often means lower doses or less frequent dosing. 264 For each medicine, choose how you think the sources frame it, then read what they actually say.

Label and guideline wording is quoted as published. It describes what the sources say, not what any one person should take: always ask the team that knows your liver before starting, stopping or changing any medicine.

Good to know

Established

Protein restriction is no longer advised for hepatic encephalopathy

In a randomised trial of 30 people admitted with episodic hepatic encephalopathy, a normal-protein diet gave the same outcome as a low-protein diet, and the low-protein group broke down more protein. 85 EASL 2019: "Avoid protein restriction in patients with HE"; normal to high protein intake "does not precipitate HE". AASLD 2021: 1.2–1.5 g/kg/day "is safe, does not worsen HE, and minimizes protein loss". 107,255 See hepatic encephalopathy.

Salt and appetite

Low-salt food can mean too little food

Only 31% of 120 outpatients with ascites kept to a 2-g sodium diet, and those who did ate 20% fewer calories. AASLD 2021 suggests balancing salt restriction with palatability, and considering a less strict sodium limit in people with frailty or sarcopenia who cannot meet their nutrition targets. 255 EASL 2019 quotes about 80 mmol sodium a day (2 g sodium, 5 g salt) in ascites. 107

Exercise

Muscle is built, not only fed

AASLD 2021 recommends combined aerobic and resistance exercise, tailored to a frailty or sarcopenia assessment, and calls following CDC guidance of 150–300 minutes a week "reasonable". 255 Early evidence Four small trials (81 people) found no severe adverse events with adapted activity. 51

In hospital

Dietitian within 24 hours

AASLD 2021 recommends a dietitian consultation "within 24 hours of admission" and cutting down repeated nil-by-mouth orders, for example with a pre-bedtime snack. 255 See nursing care.

Alcohol

Treatment for alcohol use disorder is part of liver care

Among 35,682 veterans with cirrhosis and alcohol use disorder, only 14% received treatment within 6 months; treatment was associated with less decompensation (6.5% vs 11.6%) and lower long-term mortality (51% vs 58%) in this observational study. 386 ACG 2024 recommends integrated care that combines behavioural and medicine treatment. 219 See alcohol-related liver disease.

Weight loss

When obesity and cirrhosis coexist

EASL 2019 describes a nutrition and lifestyle programme for progressive weight loss (more than 5–10%) in obese people with cirrhosis, using a moderately reduced-calorie diet (500 to 800 kcal a day less) that keeps protein above 1.5 g/kg/day. 107 Sarcopenic obesity affects 20–35% of people with cirrhosis. 255
