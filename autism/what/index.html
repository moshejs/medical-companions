Part 1 · Understanding autism

What autism is

Autism is a lifelong neurodevelopmental difference in social communication and interaction, which also shows in focused interests, repetitive behaviours, routines and sensory differences. It appears in early childhood and looks very different from one person to the next. 24,223,337

About 1 in 127 people worldwide are autistic, according to the Global Burden of Disease study, and about 1 in 31 eight-year-olds had been identified in 16 US communities in 2022. Those two numbers are both right: they count different people in different ways. This module shows how autism is counted, why recorded numbers rose, and who used to be missed. 520,543

About the words: many autistic people prefer identity-first language ("autistic person"); many families and clinicians use person-first language ("person with autism"). This guide uses both, with respect. More in 1.2 Words, identity & neurodiversity.

Surveillance years 2000–2002

Early counts

CDC's Autism and Developmental Disabilities Monitoring (ADDM) Network counts autism in 8-year-olds by reviewing health records, and at most sites education records too, in selected US communities. It is not a national sample. In 2000, six sites found 6.7 per 1,000, about 1 in 150; in 2002, 14 sites found 6.6 per 1,000, "one of every 152". 1,2,36

In plain wordsIn the early 2000s, about 1 in 150 eight-year-olds in these US communities had been identified as autistic.
Exam hookADDM is records-based surveillance of 8-year-olds in selected sites, not a national survey. Sites that could read education records found more (2002: 7.2 vs 5.1 per 1,000). 2

Boys and girls: diagnosed versus estimated

Boys are diagnosed far more often than girls, but the ratio depends on how the counting is done. Studies that only count people who already have a diagnosis give the highest ratios; studies that screen whole populations give lower ones. The authors of the largest meta-analysis conclude that the true ratio is "closer to 3:1" and describe a "diagnostic gender bias": girls who meet criteria are at disproportionate risk of not being diagnosed. 333

How old are children when they are diagnosed?

ADDM medians for 8-year-olds in selected US communities; the worldwide figures are means from a meta-analysis of 56 studies in 40 countries. 1,5,36,349,351,543,604

The median age at diagnosis in ADDM fell from 53 months in 2010 to 47 months in 2022, ranging from 36 months in California to 69.5 months in Laredo. In 2014, 85% of autistic 8-year-olds had developmental concerns recorded by 36 months, but only 42% had a comprehensive evaluation by then. Children born in 2018 were 1.7 times as likely to be identified by 48 months as children born in 2014. 5,36,543 See 3.1 Early signs and 3.4 The diagnostic assessment.

Established: recognition

Is autism becoming more common?

Diagnoses certainly are. Most of the rise is explained by wider criteria, better reporting and more awareness: Danish reporting changes explain 60% of the increase, and Swedish autistic traits stayed stable while diagnoses climbed. 204,343

A small true rise cannot be fully ruled out: CDC wrote that it "cannot be ruled out", and 40% of the Danish rise is unexplained, with wide intervals. Status: open, though most evidence points to recognition. 3,204

Lifespan

Autism is lifelong

England's adult survey found about 1.1% of adults are autistic (11 per 1,000), and prevalence "was not related to the respondent's age". Most autistic adults in the community were "unrecognized". 63,64

In English primary care in 2018, 2.94% of 10–14-year-olds had a recorded diagnosis but only 0.02% of people over 70; an estimated 59–72% of autistic people in England were undiagnosed. 433 More in 5.2 Autistic adults.

Profiles

A spectrum is not a line

Over four decades autism came to be seen as lifelong, dimensional and often co-occurring with other conditions. 207 More than 70% of autistic people have at least one co-occurring condition. 307

IQ is "a weak predictor" of day-to-day adaptive skills, so a single label such as "high functioning" can hide real needs. 12 The wheels on the start page show why one line cannot describe a person.

Established

What does not cause autism

Vaccines do not cause autism: a Danish cohort of 657,461 children found an MMR adjusted hazard ratio of 0.93 (0.85–1.02), and a meta-analysis of 1,256,407 children found no association. Parenting style does not cause autism either; autism is highly heritable. 250,516,588 More in 2.2 Risk factors & myths.

Population statistics describe groups, not any one person. An online questionnaire cannot tell you whether you or your child are autistic; only a full assessment by trained professionals can. 223,251
