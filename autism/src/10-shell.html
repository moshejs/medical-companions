Autism Companion · animated and cited

Autism, explained

Here is autism in moving pictures: early development and signs, genes and the brain, the sensory world, how diagnosis works, which supports have evidence behind them, and life as an autistic adult. No two autistic people are alike, and the wheel on this page shows why one line from "mild" to "severe" cannot describe them.

Every animation can be driven from the keyboard: Tab to its timeline, then arrow keys to scrub and Enter to play or pause.

Schematic: four made-up profiles, not measurements. Each spoke is an area where autistic people can differ.

For students

Every module has an Exam hook box and a citation trail, from DSM-5-TR criteria to the genetics and the trials. Finish with flashcards, mnemonics and a quiz.

For autistic people, families and caregivers

Look for the In plain words boxes. Many autistic people say "autistic person" and many families say "person with autism"; this guide uses both, with respect. The questionnaires here are explained, not offered as tests: only a qualified assessment can diagnose autism.

How sure is each claim?

Research claims carry a label: Established Early evidence Hypothesis. Where autistic people and researchers disagree, as over ABA, both sides are shown with their sources. Small numbers link to the Sources list.
