Autism at a glance

What it is
Autism is a lifelong neurodevelopmental difference in social communication and interaction, which also shows in focused interests, routines, repetitive behaviours and sensory differences. [[Lord2018,Hirota2023]] It appears in early childhood and looks very different from one person to the next. [[Lord2018,Hirota2023,APA2022]] Parenting style does not cause autism; autism is highly heritable. [[Hviid2019,Taylor2014Vaccine,Sandin2017]]
Who is autistic
About 1 in 127 people worldwide are autistic. [[Santomauro2025]] Girls who meet the criteria are more likely than boys to be missed. [[Loomes2017]] Most autistic adults in an English community survey had never been recognised. [[Brugha2016,Brugha2011]]
Main features
In the first two years, three common early signs are no response to name, little or no use of gestures, and lack of imaginative play. [[Hirota2023]] More than 70% of autistic people have at least one co-occurring condition. [[Lai2014]]
How it is diagnosed
The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends autism screening of all children at 18 and 24 months. [[Hyman2020]] A screen cannot diagnose autism, and a negative screen does not rule it out. [[Fuentes2021ESCAP]] Only a full assessment by trained professionals can diagnose it. [[Hyman2020,Hirota2023]]
Support and treatment
The aim of support is not to make anyone "less autistic": it is to help a person communicate, learn the skills they want and live well. [[Hyman2020,Pellicano2014]] In randomised trials, several kinds of early support showed helpful effects. [[Sandbank2023BMJ]] Only two medicines carry a US label that mentions autism, and both are for irritability, not for autism itself. [[FDA-Risperdal,FDA-Abilify]]
Outlook
Employment predicted higher quality of life in 370 UK autistic adults. [[Mason2018QoL]] Much of the excess in early deaths among autistic people comes from causes that can be found and treated: epilepsy, mental health conditions and accidents. [[Hirvikoski2016,Taylor2026Mortality]]

For students

For autistic people, families and caregivers

Look for the In plain words boxes. Many autistic people say "autistic person" and many families say "person with autism"; this guide uses both, with respect. The questionnaires here are explained, not offered as tests: only a qualified assessment can diagnose autism.
