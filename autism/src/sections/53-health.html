Physical health, sleep & safety

Autism is not an illness, but autistic people meet some health problems more often: epilepsy, poor sleep, gut and feeding difficulties, dental decay, weight gain, and injuries, especially drowning. Tap a part of the body map, then follow one night's sleep with and without support.

Getting good healthcare

Barriers

Why the doctor's surgery is hard

80% of autistic adults vs 37% of non-autistic adults reported difficulty visiting a GP: deciding whether symptoms warranted a visit (72%), phone booking (62%), not feeling understood (56%) and the waiting room (51%). [[Doherty2022]] Autistic adults named sensory sensitivities, slow processing, body awareness, wrong assumptions and unwillingness to allow written communication. [[Nicolaidis2015]] Asking for online booking, a quiet place to wait and the first or last appointment is reasonable. [[Doherty2022]]

Prevention

Regular health checks

Autistic adults had lower odds of tetanus vaccination and Pap smears (OR 0.5 each) and more unmet physical health needs (OR 1.9). [[Nicolaidis2013]] Nearly every medical condition, including diabetes, hypertension and seizures, was more common in autistic adults, [[Croen2015Adults]] so autism has been described as needing regular follow-up and routine screening, [[Davignon2018]] including in older age. [[Hand2020Medicare]]

Pain

Pain may look different

Autistic children do feel pain: during venepuncture they showed a strong facial pain reaction, and parents' global reports matched it less well than in other children. [[Nader2004]] Observers judged pain from facial activity. [[Messmer2008]] Behaviour checklists help: on the NCCPC-R, 7 or more suggests pain (sensitivity 84%). [[Breau2002]] More in Nursing & healthcare visits.

Early evidence

Catatonia: a treatable complication

About 1 in 10 autistic people (10.4%) have catatonia, [[VaquerizoSerrano2021]] usually emerging in adolescence or early adulthood; in a UK clinic, 17% of referrals aged 15 or older had severe catatonic features. [[Wing2000]] Reported treatments include benzodiazepines, antipsychotics and ECT, and the review suggested ECT might help. [[VaquerizoSerrano2021]] DSM-5-TR records catatonia as a specifier. [[APA2022]]

Conditions of the mind, such as anxiety, depression and ADHD, are in Co-occurring conditions; melatonin and other medicines are in Medication.
