Supports & the evidence

What early supports can do, and how sure we are. Watch the largest review of early autism interventions apply stricter and stricter tests, then follow one parent-coaching trial for six years.

WordsMany autistic adults prefer identity-first language ("autistic child"), while many families and professionals use person-first language ("child with autism"). A UK survey of 3,470 people found no single term that everyone accepts, so this guide uses both. [[Kenny2016]] The aim of support is not to make anyone "less autistic": it is to help a person communicate, learn the skills they want and live well. [[Hyman2020,Pellicano2014]]

One trial over six years: PACT

PACT coached parents to notice and respond to their child's communication. It randomised 152 children aged 2 years to 4 years 11 months at three UK centres: 77 to PACT plus treatment as usual and 75 to treatment as usual alone. [[Green2010]]

Reading intervention research

Established

Who rates the outcome matters

In the 2023 Project AIM update, removing caregiver- and teacher-reported outcomes left three significant effects; also removing outcomes at high risk of detection bias left one: NDBI on diagnostic characteristics, g 0.30 (95% CI 0.03 to 0.57). Parents and teachers who know which group a child is in may rate change more generously. [[Sandbank2023BMJ]]

Early evidence

More hours, bigger effects?

Intensive programmes of 20–40 hours a week are routinely recommended, but across 144 studies (9,038 children) intervention amount was not linked to effect size within any intervention type. [[Sandbank2024JAMAPed]] An RCT of 87 toddlers found no main effect of 15 versus 25 hours a week, or of NDBI versus discrete trial style. [[Rogers2021]]

Harms

Side effects are rarely counted

Only 11 of 150 early-intervention reports mentioned adverse events. [[BottemaBeutel2021AE]] The 2023 update concluded that adverse events were "poorly monitored, but possibly common". [[Sandbank2023BMJ]]

Conflicts of interest

Who wrote the study?

70% of early-intervention reports had at least one conflict of interest, but only about 6% fully disclosed them. Every type of conflict had a larger, though not significantly larger, point estimate. [[BottemaBeutel2021COI]] In a 5-year update of applied behaviour analysis journals, 93% of "no conflict" statements were false. [[BottemaBeutel2026]]

What helps

Practices with an evidence base

The US NCAEP review screened 31,779 articles, synthesised 972 and identified 28 focused practices that met its criteria for evidence-based practice. [[Hume2021]] The American Academy of Pediatrics describes "an increasing evidence base" for interventions aimed at specific skills, and calls for shared decision-making with families. [[Hyman2020]]

Better trials

Raising the standard

A 2025 INSAR group of autistic people, clinicians and researchers recommended that autism journals require trial registration, published protocols and analysis plans. [[LaPoint2025]] A Project AIM review found that most "problem behaviour" measures mixed harmful behaviour with behaviour that was "nonnormative but not inherently harmful". [[BottemaBeutel2025]]

Goals that matter

Everyday life first

UK consultation found a gap between where research money went and what autistic adults, families and practitioners wanted: research that makes a difference to "day-to-day lives". [[Pellicano2014]] Seven priority-setting studies agreed on real-world change and lifelong skills. [[Roche2021]]

Communication and participation

Autistic adults and guardians ranked life skills, co-occurring conditions, work and education, public services, health-care access and, "above all", public acceptance as top priorities. [[Gotham2015]] A project co-led by autistic adults set five mental-health research priorities. [[Benevides2020]]

Not "looking less autistic"

Stimming helps many autistic people calm down or show strong feelings, and autistic adults object to treatment that aims to eliminate it. [[Kapp2019]] Camouflaging (masking) is linked with exhaustion. [[Hull2017]] A goal worth setting improves a person's life as they experience it. ABA and the debate covers the arguments.

Questions to ask any provider

Goals

  1. Which goals will we work on, and who chose them?
  2. How does each goal link to my child's quality of life, now and later?
  3. Will any goal reduce a harmless autistic behaviour, such as stimming?

Assent and comfort

  1. How will you tell if my child is unhappy or wants to stop?
  2. What happens then?
  3. Can I watch any session?

Hours and cost

  1. How many hours a week, and why that number? Studies have not found that more hours give bigger effects. [[Sandbank2024JAMAPed,Rogers2021]]
  2. Who delivers it, and with what training?

Measuring progress

  1. How will progress be measured, and by whom?
  2. When will we review it, and what if nothing changes?
  3. Which side effects or downsides do you track? [[BottemaBeutel2021AE]]

Autism is not caused by vaccines: a Danish cohort of 657,461 children and a meta-analysis of more than 1.2 million children found no link. [[Hviid2019,Taylor2014Vaccine]] No therapy "cures" autism, and that is not the goal. [[Hyman2020]]
