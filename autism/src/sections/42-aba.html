What a functional assessment asks: the ABC analyser

Functional analysis tests which events keep a behaviour going by briefly and repeatedly changing the situation: play materials, demands and attention. In the original 1994 study, 6 of 9 people showed self-injury mostly in one specific condition, so the behaviour served an identifiable function. [[Iwata1994]] It became "a hallmark" of behavioural assessment. [[Hanley2003]] Positive behaviour support builds on it, with a focus on quality of life, prevention and the person's own participation. [[Carr2002PBS]]

Teaching scenario, invented for illustration. A real functional assessment is done by a trained professional, with direct observation, and starts by checking for pain, illness or distress.

What ABA is, and is not

A family of methods

Early intensive behavioural intervention (EIBI) is ABA-based treatment delivered for several years at 20–40 hours a week. [[Reichow2018Cochrane]] Naturalistic developmental behavioural interventions (NDBIs) such as ESDM and JASPER also use behavioural strategies, but in natural settings, with shared control between child and adult. [[Schreibman2015]]

Not one fixed programme

Behaviour analysts argue that critics' concerns should be answered by changing goals and procedures, not by abandoning the approach. [[Leaf2022JADD]] Others in the field call for listening "with humility" and adopting the neurodiversity paradigm. [[Mathur2024]]

Established

Not proven to "recover" children

Lovaas 1987 reported 47% "normal functioning", but the study was not randomised. [[Mudford2001,Gresham1998]] Cochrane rated the EIBI evidence low or very low certainty. [[Reichow2018Cochrane]] An RCT found no extra benefit from 25 rather than 15 hours a week. [[Rogers2021]]

Hypothesis

Not proven to harm, or to be safe

Autistic adults describe trauma and masking. [[McGill2020,Anderson2023]] The only quantitative study is a self-selected survey now under an expression of concern. [[Kupferstein2018,AiA2025EOC]] Only 11 of 150 intervention reports mentioned adverse events, so harm is under-measured. [[BottemaBeutel2021AE]]

What many autistic adults ask for

Keep stims

In 32 autistic adults, stimming served to soothe or to express intense emotion, and participants objected to treatment that aims to eliminate it. [[Kapp2019]]

No compliance for its own sake

One commentary argued that long-term ABA can amount to abuse because of its emphasis on compliance; behaviour analysts published a direct rebuttal. [[SandovalNorton2019,Gorycki2020]] Adults interviewed about childhood ABA rejected being made to behave like peers. [[Anderson2023]]

Don't train masking

Autistic adults describe camouflaging as masking and compensation, with exhaustion and threats to self-perception as consequences. [[Hull2017]] Critics link compliance-focused goals to masking. Hypothesis [[Hull2017,Anderson2023]]

Measure what matters

Most "problem behaviour" measures in early-intervention trials mixed harmful behaviour with behaviour that is "nonnormative but not inherently harmful", such as repetitive movements. [[BottemaBeutel2025]]

Questions families can ask an ABA provider

  1. Which behaviours will you try to reduce, and is each one harmful to my child or others?
  2. Will you try to stop stimming or other harmless autistic behaviour?
  3. Have you done a functional assessment, and what did it find?
  1. How do you notice when my child does not want to take part, and what do you do then?
  2. How many hours, and why? [[Sandbank2024JAMAPed,Rogers2021]]
  3. How will you measure progress, and which downsides do you track? [[BottemaBeutel2021AE]]
