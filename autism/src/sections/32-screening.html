Predictive value: the same screen, different settings

Sensitivity and specificity describe the test. Positive predictive value (PPV), the share of screen-positive children who are autistic, also depends on how common autism is in the group screened. Who should be screened, and how to read a result

Two positions

AAP: screen everyone. USPSTF: evidence insufficient.

The AAP recommends autism-specific screening of all children at 18 and 24 months, with surveillance at every visit. [[Hyman2020]] In 2016 the USPSTF issued an "I statement": the evidence was insufficient to judge benefits against harms of screening children aged 18–30 months when no one has raised a concern. That is not a recommendation against screening. [[Siu2016]] Part of the gap is the question asked: the USPSTF looked only at children without concerns, and weighed the accuracy of the tools and the benefits of early treatment for children found through screening. [[Siu2016,Fuentes2021ESCAP]]

Why numbers differ

Who gets followed up?

Validation studies that follow only screen-positive children cannot count the autistic children who screened negative. Studies that follow everyone find much lower sensitivity: 38.8% in Philadelphia and 33.1% in Utah. [[Guthrie2019,Carbone2020]] In Philadelphia, PPV was lower in girls, and specificity and PPV were lower in children of colour and in lower-income households. [[Guthrie2019]]

Early evidence

Is the follow-up interview worth it at 18 months?

At 18 months, M-CHAT-R alone had sensitivity 0.73 and specificity 0.66; adding the interview gave 0.36 and 0.89. The authors conclude the interview lowers sensitivity with little gain in PPV, and that screening must be repeated after 18 months. [[Sturner2022]] In Utah, clinicians often skipped the interview, yet screen-positive children were still diagnosed earlier: 38.5 vs 48.5 months. [[Carbone2020]]

For adults

Online quizzes measure traits

Among 476 adults referred for assessment, 73% were diagnosed, but the AQ score did not predict who: 64% of those below the cut-off were autistic. Generalised anxiety raised scores and produced false positives. [[Ashwood2016]] A questionnaire can open a conversation; it cannot say whether you are autistic. See The diagnostic assessment.

After a positive screen

Not autism does not mean "nothing"

In the M-CHAT-R/F validation, almost 95% of children who stayed positive after the follow-up interview had a developmental delay or concern, and they were diagnosed about 2 years younger than the US median at the time. [[Robins2014]]

After a negative screen

Keep listening

A negative screen does not exclude autism: if other information points to developmental differences, a full assessment is still warranted. [[Fuentes2021ESCAP]] Among younger siblings, 41% of children diagnosed at 36 months had not been diagnosed at 24 months. [[Ozonoff2015]]
