Unproven & harmful treatments

Families try treatments out of love and hope, often while waiting for help. Some of those treatments were tested and failed; a few have killed children. Watch how interest rises and collapses, then learn a quick way to judge the next claim.

Case

Schematic. The height of the band is interest and use drawn by hand, not measured; the order of events and every marker are from the cited studies. Evidence of benefit is drawn from what the trials found.

VaccinesVaccines do not cause autism. In 657,461 Danish children, the MMR vaccine was not linked to autism (adjusted HR 0.93, 95% CI 0.85–1.02). A meta-analysis of 1,256,407 children found no association with vaccination (OR 0.99), thimerosal (OR 1.00) or mercury (OR 1.00). Treatments built on the idea that vaccines or mercury caused a child's autism, such as chelation, start from a false premise. See Risk factors & myths. [[Hviid2019,Taylor2014Vaccine]]

Why families try these, and how to talk about them

Common

Most families try something

Across 20 studies (9,540 participants), use of complementary and alternative medicine in autism ranged from 28% to 95%, median 54%. Special diets and supplements ranked first in 75% of studies. [[Hofer2017]] In 3,173 children in the Autism Treatment Network, 28% reported use; it was more likely with gastrointestinal symptoms (OR 1.88), seizures (OR 1.58) and behaviour problems (OR 1.29). [[Perrin2012]]

Love & hope

Why it makes sense to parents

Parents want to help now. In a UK survey of 1,047 parents, the delay from first approaching a professional to diagnosis was about 3.5 years. [[Crane2016Diagnosis]] A treatment that seems to work is powerful: 19% of children given placebo in autism drug trials were rated "much improved", which makes an ineffective product look effective in everyday use. [[Siafis2020]]

For clinicians

Ask, without shaming

The AAP advises shared decision-making with families when choosing interventions. [[Hyman2020]] Ask about every product and diet, because some are potentially harmful, and families may not mention them unless asked. [[Hofer2017,Perrin2012]] Start from the goal the family has, then look at the evidence together.

Publicity

When news runs ahead of trials

After one anecdote, "thousands" of children received secretin injections before any trial. After hearing a negative result, 69% of parents in that trial still wanted secretin. [[Sandler1999]] Stem-cell crowdfunding campaigns used testimonials, early academic trials and press releases to lend clinics credibility. [[Snyder2021]]

Harm

Reports and reviews that warn

CDC received reports of 3 deaths from hypocalcaemia during chelation between 2003 and 2005; two were children given edetate disodium. [[Brown2006]] A Cochrane review judged that "the risks of using chelation for ASD currently outweigh proven benefits". [[James2015]] A camel-milk meta-analysis was retracted by its journal in 2023. [[Kandeel2022]]

Guidelines

What NICE says not to use

For the core features of autism in adults, NICE lists chelation, secretin, hyperbaric oxygen, exclusion diets, vitamins, minerals and dietary supplements, oxytocin, testosterone regulation and others as interventions not to use. [[Pilling2012]] For under-19s it lists exclusion diets such as gluten- or casein-free diets. [[Kendall2013]] What does help is in Supports & the evidence and Medication.
