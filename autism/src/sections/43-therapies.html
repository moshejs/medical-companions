Whose words? Facilitated communication and spelling methods

Established

Facilitated communication

In facilitated communication (FC), a facilitator gives physical support while the person types or points. A systematic review found "unequivocal evidence for facilitator control": the messages are authored by the facilitator, and FC "has no validity". [[Schlosser2014FC]]

No evidence of authorship

RPM and spelling to communicate

In the Rapid Prompting Method the facilitator holds and moves the letter board rather than the person's hand, and its proponents make claims similar to those once made for FC. [[Tostanoski2014]] A 2019 review found no evidence supporting RPM. [[Schlosser2019RPM]] A 2026 review of authorship in RPM, Spelling to Communicate and variants found no study that met its design criteria, and advises stopping these methods until independent authorship studies show they are valid. [[Schlosser2026RPM]]

Why authorship testing matters

A person who cannot speak still has a right to their own words. If the facilitator is unknowingly shaping the message, the person's real wishes, including about health, consent or abuse, can be overwritten. Authorship tests give the person information the facilitator does not have and check whose knowledge appears in the message. In the FC studies reviewed, it was the facilitator's. [[Schlosser2014FC]] Users of spelling methods report that it lets them communicate; controlled authorship testing is what is still missing. [[Schlosser2026RPM]] Independent AAC, where the person selects without being touched or having the board moved, is the alternative this page describes above.
