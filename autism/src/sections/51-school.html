How strong is the evidence for school supports?

SupportWhat it isWhat the evidence says
Visual activity scheduleThe day or a task shown as a row of pictures, symbols or words; finished items are moved to "done".Established Meets criteria as an evidence-based practice from preschool through adulthood, especially with systematic instruction (31 studies, 16 of acceptable quality). [[Knight2015]]
Structured teaching (TEACCH)The room, the schedule and each task are organised so the child can see what to do, how much, and what comes next.Early evidence 13 studies, 172 people: small effects on perceptual, motor, verbal and cognitive skills; moderate-to-large gains in social and maladaptive behaviour. The authors call the results exploratory. [[ViruesOrtega2013]]
Social StoriesA short, personalised story describing a situation and what will happen.Early evidence "Low to questionable" overall effectiveness in single-subject studies; better for reducing behaviours that cause problems than for teaching social skills. [[Kokina2010]]
Noise-attenuating headphonesEar defenders worn in loud places.Early evidence Lowered skin-conductance arousal in six children with hyperacusis (single-subject design); parents and teachers reported more participation at school. [[Pfeiffer2019EDA,Pfeiffer2019Participation]]
Focused practices overallSpecific teaching practices, such as prompting, visual supports and modelling.Established The third-generation US review synthesised 972 articles and found 28 focused practices that met its criteria for evidence-based practice. [[Hume2021]]
Parent trainingSessions teaching parents practical strategies for disruptive behaviour.Established 8 RCTs, 653 participants: standardised mean difference −0.59 (95% CI −0.88 to −0.30), with heterogeneity. [[Postorino2017]]

Inclusion or integration? A review of 233 studies of "inclusive" interventions found that most tried to change the autistic student's social skills. Lived-experience consultants argued that this is integration (the student adapts to the school) rather than inclusion (the school adapts to the student). There is no agreed way to measure inclusion. [[Perrelet2025]] More on these approaches in Supports & the evidence.

Family life

Caregivers

Parenting stress is real, and common

A meta-analysis found a large effect size for higher parenting stress in parents of autistic children than in parents of typically developing children. [[Hayes2013]] Across 133 studies of clinical groups, stress tracked child externalising problems (r = 0.57) more than internalising ones (r = 0.37). [[Barroso2018]] In 31 studies (9,208 parents), 31% had a depressive disorder and 33% an anxiety disorder, with wide variation between studies. [[Schnabel2020]]

In plain words: it is fine, and wise, to ask for help for yourself too.

Caregivers

Support for parents works

In the RUBI trial (180 children aged 3–7), parent training cut parent-rated irritability by 47.7% against 31.8% with parent education, and a blinded clinician rated 68.5% against 39.6% as responders; neither parent measure reached its prespecified minimal clinically important difference. [[Bearss2015]] In the REACH-ASD trial (379 caregivers), an online programme with Acceptance and Commitment Therapy improved caregiver mental health at 52 weeks. Early evidence [[Leadbitter2025]]

Family

Brothers and sisters

Across 69 samples, siblings of autistic people had slightly more negative outcomes overall (Hedges g −0.26), including anxiety (−0.25) and depression (−0.36), but no difference in adjustment, coping or family functioning. The authors recommend including siblings in family support. [[Shivers2019]]

Family

Respite and couples

In 101 couples raising an autistic child, each extra hour a week of respite care went with a half standard deviation higher marital quality, through lower daily stress and more daily uplifts (cross-sectional). [[Harper2013]] Divorce was more common than in a matched comparison group (23.5% vs 13.8%) and stayed high into the child's adulthood. [[Hartley2010]]

Family

Diagnosis and after

In a UK survey of 1,047 parents, families waited about a year before seeking help, then about 3.5 years from first approaching a professional to a diagnosis; post-diagnostic support was rated unsatisfactory. [[Crane2016Diagnosis]] The AAP describes service needs across behavioural, educational, health, leisure and family-support areas, and calls for shared decision-making with families. [[Hyman2020]]

Money

The cost to families

In US survey data (2002–2008), mothers of autistic children earned 56% less than mothers of children with no health limitation and worked 7 hours less a week; family earnings were 28% lower. [[Cidav2012]] Estimated lifetime cost of support: $2.4 million (US) and £1.5 million (UK) with intellectual disability, $1.4 million and £0.92 million without; special education and parents' lost work dominate in childhood. [[Buescher2014]]

School life

Bullying

Almost half are bullied

Pooled across 17 studies: 44% of autistic school-age youth had been bullied, 10% had bullied others and 16% both; by type, 50% verbal, 33% physical and 31% relational. [[Maiano2016]] In US national data, 46.3% had been bullied in the past school year; it was more likely with more classes in general education, ADHD, lower social skills and some conversational ability. [[Sterzing2012]]

In plain words: tell the school, and ask what they will do to keep your child safe.

Attendance

Absence and exclusion

Parents of 486 UK autistic students (mean age 11) reported 5 of 23 school days missed in a month; 43% met the threshold for persistent absence. Of absences, 43% were school refusal, 9% exclusion, 9% school withdrawal and 32% illness or appointments; truancy was almost non-existent. [[Totsika2020]] In Norway, school refusal went with lower social motivation, more difficulty starting tasks and more withdrawn and depressive symptoms. [[Munkhaugen2019]] English pupils could attend and enjoy school when they had the right support. [[Gray2023School]]

Outcomes

Leaving school

In US special-education data for 2019, 71% of autistic students exiting high school graduated with a diploma, against 48% with intellectual disability, and dropout was lower (6–8% vs 15–18% in other disabilities). [[McLean2026]] Services start to fall away before school ends: see Autistic adults. [[Laxman2019]]

Strengths

Play and focused interests

Writers on respectful language suggest "focused" or "intense" interests rather than "special interests". [[BottemaBeutel2021Ableist]] Monotropism, a theory co-authored by an autistic researcher, proposes that attention concentrates on a few interests at a time. Hypothesis [[Murray2005]] Therapies that coach parents to follow their child's lead in play can change how parent and child communicate, sometimes for years. [[Pickles2016,Oono2013]] A child's interest is often the easiest door into learning and friendship.

Overload

Meltdown and shutdown

Autistic people describe a meltdown as being overwhelmed by sensory, information and social-emotional load, not as chosen behaviour; a shutdown is quieter and more inward. Young people said compassion and collaboration from trusted adults help, while demands such as eye contact during distress make it worse. [[Lewis2023Meltdown,Phung2021]] More in The sensory world.

Plans

What school plans contain

In a US population cohort of 1,787 autistic adolescents, school IEP services varied widely between sites, students with intellectual disability were less likely to get school-based mental health services, and 37% did not take part in standardised testing. ADHD (47%) and anxiety (39%) were the commonest co-occurring conditions recorded by age 16. [[Hughes2024IEP]]
