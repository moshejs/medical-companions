Co-occurring conditions

Most autistic people have at least one other condition, and many have several. Watch 100 autistic people across 40 years as ADHD, anxiety, depression, epilepsy and others arrive at their usual ages, and see how they stack up.

Counts per 100 use pooled prevalence from 96 studies (Lai 2019), intellectual disability from ADDM 2022 and epilepsy from a 74-study review. Who has which condition, and the onset ages, are schematic: overlap is drawn clustered because it clusters in real samples (41% of autistic 10–14-year-olds had two or more disorders). 309,341,541,549

Getting the right diagnosis

Hypothesis

Diagnostic overshadowing

A new symptom, such as pain, low mood or catatonia, can be put down to autism itself and not assessed. This is one proposed reason why autistic adults report more unmet mental health needs (OR 2.2) and physical health needs (OR 1.9) than non-autistic adults. 425 Anxiety and low mood are not "just part of autism": a change deserves its own check. 230,425

Measurement

How you ask changes the answer

Depression rates in autistic people were higher with standardised interviews (lifetime 28.5%) and highest when people reported their own symptoms (48.6%). 237 Questionnaire measures and intellectual disability changed anxiety estimates in adults. 230 Treating these conditions can make daily life much easier. 309

Hypothesis

Autistic women and "borderline"

Late-diagnosed women describe "pretending to be normal" and being misdiagnosed first. 38 Among Dutch autistic adults, 31.7% of women vs 16.7% of men reported a misdiagnosis, most often personality disorder. 281 Yet a meta-analysis found BPD in only 4% of autistic people, within population ranges, and could not test misdiagnosis directly. 366 See the BPD Companion.

Early evidence

Eating disorders and ARFID

About 11% of autistic groups met criteria for ARFID (avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder), from only 3 studies (95% CI 2.9–35.8%), and 16% of people with ARFID were autistic. 502 In anorexia nervosa, 29% scored above the ADOS cut-off, but starvation itself can produce social and rigid features, so this is a screening signal, not a diagnosis. 251,626

Self-injury

Self-injurious behaviour

A meta-analysis of 37 papers estimated self-injury in 42% of autistic people; hand-hitting was the most common form (23%) and self-cutting the least (3%), and females had higher prevalence. 562

Mood and risk

Depression and suicide

By age 27, 19.8% of autistic people in Stockholm had a depression diagnosis vs 6.0% of the population, more so without intellectual disability. 469 In Denmark, over 90% of autistic people who attempted or died by suicide had another psychiatric diagnosis. 300 See Physical health, sleep & safety.

Need help now?If you or someone you support is in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

Autism Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on autism, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses, and for autistic people, families and caregivers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose anyone, and the questionnaires and criteria it explains are teaching tools, not tests to use on yourself or your child. Nothing here should change a support plan or a medicine without your own clinician. Criteria, doses and guideline statements are quoted as published. Please check the original sources, which are listed in full in the Sources module.
