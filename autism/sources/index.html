Not medical adviceAn educational synthesis of published research for study and understanding. It cannot diagnose autism: only a qualified assessment can, and the questionnaires shown here are teaching tools, not for self-diagnosis. Doses, criteria and guideline statements are reported as published, not as instructions.

Part 6 · Context & review

Sources

Every reference behind the Autism Companion: journal articles, guidelines, drug labels, device decisions and trial registrations. The small numbers throughout the app point here; filter by research area or search.

Eight research passes ran in parallel, one per area: epidemiology, identity, language and history; genetics, causes and risk factors, including what does not cause autism; neuroscience, brain development and sensory processing; early signs, screening, DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 criteria and assessment; supports and non-drug interventions, including the ABA debate; medication, unproven treatments and research; co-occurring conditions, physical health, course and outcomes; and family, school, work, adulthood and nursing. Before inclusion each source was checked, where possible, against PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, ClinicalTrials.gov or openFDA, and the green tag on an entry names the service that confirmed it. Works found by more than one pass appear once, with any extra keys listed beside them.

Entries marked not matched in a research database are regulator pages, laws and society statements that have no PMID or DOI, or works that could not be confirmed. They are listed for completeness; treat them with extra care.

Autism Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on autism, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses, and for autistic people, families and caregivers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose anyone, and the questionnaires and criteria it explains are teaching tools, not tests to use on yourself or your child. Nothing here should change a support plan or a medicine without your own clinician. Criteria, doses and guideline statements are quoted as published. Please check the original sources, which are listed in full in the Sources module.
