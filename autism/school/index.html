Arrival and the visual schedule

A visual activity schedule shows the day as a row of pictures, symbols or words; each finished card is ticked or moved to "done". A review of 31 studies (16 of acceptable quality) concluded that visual activity schedules meet criteria as an evidence-based practice from preschool through adulthood, especially when combined with systematic instruction. 298 Without one, the child sees only "now" and has to guess the rest: here the strip shows question marks. Predictability is one of six principles autistic adults named for enabling spaces. 347

In plain wordsA picture timetable shows what is coming next, so there are fewer surprises in the day.

Inclusion or integration? A review of 233 studies of "inclusive" interventions found that most tried to change the autistic student's social skills. Lived-experience consultants argued that this is integration (the student adapts to the school) rather than inclusion (the school adapts to the student). There is no agreed way to measure inclusion. 453 More on these approaches in Supports & the evidence.

United States · Accommodations under the Rehabilitation Act

A Section 504 plan

Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act gives accommodations to students who may not qualify for an IEP; over 1.38 million students have 504 plans. Most of 23 families of autistic children interviewed reported negative experiences, often schools unwilling to provide accommodations, so put requests in writing and keep records. 329

What to ask for (learning and coping)
  • a visual schedule for the day and for tasks 298
  • structured tasks with a clear end (TEACCH-style) 613
  • warnings before changes, including staff changes 458
  • a quiet space or ear defenders for loud places 457
  • a named, trusted adult
When
  • as soon as there is a concern: you do not need a diagnosis to ask for support to be discussed
  • before every transition: a new class, a new school, leaving school
  • after any exclusion or a run of absences
  • at each review, and whenever the plan stops working

Family life

Caregivers

Parenting stress is real, and common

A meta-analysis found a large effect size for higher parenting stress in parents of autistic children than in parents of typically developing children. 213 Across 133 studies of clinical groups, stress tracked child externalising problems (r = 0.57) more than internalising ones (r = 0.37). 41 In 31 studies (9,208 parents), 31% had a depressive disorder and 33% an anxiety disorder, with wide variation between studies. 531

In plain words: it is fine, and wise, to ask for help for yourself too.

Caregivers

Support for parents works

In the RUBI trial (180 children aged 3–7), parent training cut parent-rated irritability by 47.7% against 31.8% with parent education, and a blinded clinician rated 68.5% against 39.6% as responders; neither parent measure reached its prespecified minimal clinically important difference. 44 In the REACH-ASD trial (379 caregivers), an online programme with Acceptance and Commitment Therapy improved caregiver mental health at 52 weeks. Early evidence 320

Family

Brothers and sisters

Across 69 samples, siblings of autistic people had slightly more negative outcomes overall (Hedges g −0.26), including anxiety (−0.25) and depression (−0.36), but no difference in adjustment, coping or family functioning. The authors recommend including siblings in family support. 547

Family

Respite and couples

In 101 couples raising an autistic child, each extra hour a week of respite care went with a half standard deviation higher marital quality, through lower daily stress and more daily uplifts (cross-sectional). 209 Divorce was more common than in a matched comparison group (23.5% vs 13.8%) and stayed high into the child's adulthood. 211

Family

Diagnosis and after

In a UK survey of 1,047 parents, families waited about a year before seeking help, then about 3.5 years from first approaching a professional to a diagnosis; post-diagnostic support was rated unsatisfactory. 99 The AAP describes service needs across behavioural, educational, health, leisure and family-support areas, and calls for shared decision-making with families. 251

Money

The cost to families

In US survey data (2002–2008), mothers of autistic children earned 56% less than mothers of children with no health limitation and worked 7 hours less a week; family earnings were 28% lower. 88 Estimated lifetime cost of support: $2.4 million (US) and £1.5 million (UK) with intellectual disability, $1.4 million and £0.92 million without; special education and parents' lost work dominate in childhood. 65

School life

Bullying

Almost half are bullied

Pooled across 17 studies: 44% of autistic school-age youth had been bullied, 10% had bullied others and 16% both; by type, 50% verbal, 33% physical and 31% relational. 353 In US national data, 46.3% had been bullied in the past school year; it was more likely with more classes in general education, ADHD, lower social skills and some conversational ability. 568

In plain words: tell the school, and ask what they will do to keep your child safe.

Attendance

Absence and exclusion

Parents of 486 UK autistic students (mean age 11) reported 5 of 23 school days missed in a month; 43% met the threshold for persistent absence. Of absences, 43% were school refusal, 9% exclusion, 9% school withdrawal and 32% illness or appointments; truancy was almost non-existent. 594 In Norway, school refusal went with lower social motivation, more difficulty starting tasks and more withdrawn and depressive symptoms. 394 English pupils could attend and enjoy school when they had the right support. 181

Outcomes

Leaving school

In US special-education data for 2019, 71% of autistic students exiting high school graduated with a diploma, against 48% with intellectual disability, and dropout was lower (6–8% vs 15–18% in other disabilities). 373 Services start to fall away before school ends: see Autistic adults. 319

Strengths

Play and focused interests

Writers on respectful language suggest "focused" or "intense" interests rather than "special interests". 53 Monotropism, a theory co-authored by an autistic researcher, proposes that attention concentrates on a few interests at a time. Hypothesis 395 Therapies that coach parents to follow their child's lead in play can change how parent and child communicate, sometimes for years. 436,459 A child's interest is often the easiest door into learning and friendship.

Overload

Meltdown and shutdown

Autistic people describe a meltdown as being overwhelmed by sensory, information and social-emotional load, not as chosen behaviour; a shutdown is quieter and more inward. Young people said compassion and collaboration from trusted adults help, while demands such as eye contact during distress make it worse. 328,458 More in The sensory world.

Plans

What school plans contain

In a US population cohort of 1,787 autistic adolescents, school IEP services varied widely between sites, students with intellectual disability were less likely to get school-based mental health services, and 37% did not take part in standardised testing. ADHD (47%) and anxiety (39%) were the commonest co-occurring conditions recorded by age 16. 241
