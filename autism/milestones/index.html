Part 3 · Signs & diagnosis

Early signs & development

Social communication grows in a sequence: faces, smiles, a turn to a name, gestures, pointing to share, then words. Scrub from birth to 3 years to see the 2022 CDC milestones arrive, how early signs of autism can look, and what loss of skills means.

A note on words: many autistic adults prefer identity-first language ("autistic child"), while many families and professionals use person-first language ("child with autism"). No single term suits everyone, so this guide uses both. 281

0–3 months

Faces first

Newborns are drawn to faces and voices. On the 2022 CDC checklists, by 2 months a baby looks at the parent's face and smiles back when talked to. Each milestone sits at the age by which at least 75% of children show it, so a quarter of babies are still on the way at that visit. The revision cut 216 milestones on 10 checklists to 159 on 12. 656

In plain wordsLooking at faces and smiling back are the first steps of a conversation, long before words.

If you are worried

Not a screenerThis is a map of the usual next steps, not a test. It cannot tell you whether a child is autistic. Choose what you have noticed to see which step matters most.

Write down what you see

Short notes or videos of what the child does, and when, help any clinician. Parents often notice differences before professionals do. 657

Talk to the child's clinician

Missing a milestone is a reason for developmental screening, not a diagnosis. 656 In US survey data, parents of autistic children who got "reassuring or passive" responses waited longer for a diagnosis. 657

Check hearing (and vision)

Not answering to a name only counts as an early sign of autism when hearing is normal, so hearing is checked. 26 The ESCAP minimum assessment includes hearing and vision. 157

Referral for a full assessment

Diagnosis is made by a multidisciplinary team that combines a caregiver interview with direct observation. 223 A negative screen does not exclude autism when other information points to differences. 157 See The diagnostic assessment.

Support does not have to wait

Early identification shortens families' "diagnostic odyssey". 659 A screen-positive child who turns out not to be autistic usually still needs help: almost 95% had another developmental delay or concern. 484 See Supports & the evidence.

Development varies

Girls & quieter presentations

Easy to miss

In high-quality studies the male-to-female ratio is closer to 3:1 than 4:1, and girls who meet criteria are at disproportionate risk of not being diagnosed. 333 Girls' social differences are often more subtle: they may mask, seek one or two close friends, and show more imaginative play and fewer repetitive behaviours. 157 Girls with high autistic traits were less often diagnosed unless they also had other difficulties. 124

Two languages

Bilingual homes do not cause delay

In 75 autistic children, those from bilingual homes had the same language levels as those from monolingual homes. 199 At school age, the amount of exposure to each language predicted vocabulary in that language, as in non-autistic peers: many autistic children can learn two languages. 176

The 75% line

A milestone age is not a deadline

The 2022 checklists place each milestone at the age by which at least 75% of children reach it, so about 1 in 4 children are still on the way at that visit. The panel chose 75% so that fewer children would be flagged than at 50%, while still discouraging "wait and see". 656

Established

Vaccines do not cause autism

Regression is often noticed in the second year, when vaccines are also given, so the timing can look linked. Large studies find no link: in 657,461 Danish children the MMR hazard ratio was 0.93 (95% CI 0.85–1.02), and a meta-analysis of more than 1.2 million children found an odds ratio of 0.99. 250,588 See Risk factors & myths.

Early evidence

Signs often unfold over time

In a prospective study, gaze to faces, shared smiles and directed sounds were similar at 6 months in infants later diagnosed, then declined, with most differences clear by 12 months. 441 Among younger siblings, 63% of children diagnosed at 36 months had not yet been diagnosed at 18 months, so a single check is not enough. 443

Diagnosed when?

Earlier than before, still later than possible

Autism can be diagnosed as young as 18 months. 251 Yet in 2022 US surveillance the median age at earliest known diagnosis was 47 months, from 36 months at one site to 69.5 at another. 543 In a prospective general-population study, an autism diagnosis made between 12 and 36 months was stable in 84% of toddlers. 461
