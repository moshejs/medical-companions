# E · Supports & non-pharmacological interventions (incl. the ABA debate)

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: supports, aba, therapies.

This pass covers the evidence for non-drug supports and interventions for autistic people. It includes the Project AIM meta-analyses (2020, 2023, 2024) and what happens to effect sizes as study quality rises, conflicts of interest and harms reporting, and NICE CG170/CG142 (what to offer and what not to use). It also covers behavioural intervention (Lovaas, EIBI, functional assessment, positive behaviour support), the ABA debate (autistic critiques, Kupferstein 2018 and its limits, responses from behaviour analysts, the Judge Rotenberg Center and the FDA electrical stimulation device ban), developmental and NDBI approaches (ESDM, JASPER, PACT, iBASIS-VIPP, TEACCH), communication (PECS, AAC and speech-generating devices, facilitated communication and RPM), OT and sensory approaches, psychological therapies (PEERS, adapted CBT, music therapy) and the outcomes autistic people prioritise.

Language: many autistic adults prefer identity-first language ("autistic person"), while many professionals and some families use person-first language ("person with autism"). A UK survey of 3,470 people found no single term that everyone accepts [[Kenny2016]]. This file uses both. It quotes "high-functioning" and similar terms only where a source uses them.

## Findings

### supports · Big picture: how strong is the evidence overall?
- Project AIM (2020) pooled 1,615 effect sizes from 130 independent samples, covering 6,240 children aged 0–8 years. It spanned 7 intervention types: behavioural, developmental, NDBI, TEACCH, sensory-based, animal-assisted and technology-based [[Sandbank2020]].
- Without quality filters, Project AIM 2020 found significant positive effects for behavioural, developmental and NDBI interventions. Restricted to RCTs, only developmental and NDBI interventions still showed positive effects [[Sandbank2020]].
- Project AIM 2020 found that when analysis was limited to RCTs and to outcomes with no risk of detection bias, no intervention type showed a significant effect on any outcome (Established) [[Sandbank2020]].
- The 2023 Project AIM update (search November 2021) included 289 reports of 252 studies, 13,304 participants and effects for 3,291 outcomes. The evidence base roughly doubled in four years [[Sandbank2023BMJ]].
- Significant summary effects in the 2023 update, restricted to RCTs, with Hedges' g and 95% CI [[Sandbank2023BMJ]]:
  - behavioural interventions on social-emotional or challenging behaviour: g 0.58 (0.11 to 1.06)
  - developmental interventions on social communication: 0.28 (0.12 to 0.44)
  - NDBI on adaptive behaviour: 0.23
  - NDBI on language: 0.16
  - NDBI on play: 0.19
  - NDBI on social communication: 0.35 (0.23 to 0.47)
  - NDBI on measures of diagnostic characteristics: 0.38 (0.17 to 0.59)
  - technology-based interventions on social communication: 0.33
  - technology-based interventions on social-emotional or challenging behaviour: 0.57
- In the 2023 update, excluding caregiver- or teacher-reported outcomes left three significant effects [[Sandbank2023BMJ]]:
  - developmental interventions on social communication: g 0.31
  - NDBI on social communication: 0.36
  - NDBI on diagnostic characteristics: 0.44
- When the 2023 update also excluded effects at high risk of detection bias, only one significant summary effect remained: NDBI on measures of diagnostic characteristics of autism, g 0.30 (95% CI 0.03 to 0.57) (Established) [[Sandbank2023BMJ]].
- The 2023 update concluded that adverse events were "poorly monitored, but possibly common" [[Sandbank2023BMJ]].
- Project AIM 2024 (144 studies, 9,038 children, mean age 49.3 months, 82.6% male) found no significant positive link between intervention amount and effect size within any intervention type. This held for daily intensity, duration and cumulative intensity [[Sandbank2024JAMAPed]].
- The 2024 authors note that intensive programmes of 20–40 hours a week are routinely recommended. They concluded there is "little robust evidence" for providing intensive intervention (Early evidence) [[Sandbank2024JAMAPed]].
- An RCT randomised 87 toddlers (mean age 23.4 months) to 15 or 25 hours a week of 1:1 intervention for 12 months, in either NDBI or discrete trial teaching style. Neither style nor intensity had main effects on autism severity, expressive communication, receptive language or nonverbal ability [[Rogers2021]].
- A 2025 Project AIM secondary review looked at 102 studies that targeted behaviour for reduction [[BottemaBeutel2025]]:
  - 62% described reducing behaviour as a study aim
  - 28% conceptualised the behaviours they targeted
  - only 8% gave a conceptual definition
  - most measures mixed harmful behaviour with behaviour that was "nonnormative but not inherently harmful"
- A 2025 INSAR special interest group of autistic people, clinicians and researchers recommended that autism journals require trial registration, published protocols and analysis plans [[LaPoint2025]].
- The US NCAEP third-generation review screened 31,779 articles and synthesised 972. It identified 28 focused intervention practices that met its criteria for evidence-based practice [[Hume2021]].
- The AAP 2020 clinical report states that there is "an increasing evidence base" for behavioural and other interventions for specific skills and symptoms. It calls for shared decision-making with families when choosing interventions [[Hyman2020]].

### supports · Conflicts of interest and harms reporting
- Bottema-Beutel 2021 analysed group-design early intervention studies from 1970–2018 [[BottemaBeutel2021COI]]:
  - 70% of reports had at least 1 conflict of interest (COI)
  - only about 6% had COI statements that accounted for all of them
- In that analysis, meta-regression found no significant influence of any COI type on effect size, but every COI type had a larger point estimate than reports with no COI (Early evidence) [[BottemaBeutel2021COI]].
- Bottema-Beutel and Crowley reviewed 1 year of ABA autism-intervention articles in 8 behavioural journals [[BottemaBeutel2021ABAJ]]:
  - 84% had at least 1 author with a clinical or training-consultancy COI
  - only 2% disclosed it
  - 87% of statements claiming "no conflicts" were by authors with such COIs
- The 5-year update of that study covered 119 studies with 450 authors [[BottemaBeutel2026]]:
  - 78% of authors held clinical or consultancy COIs
  - 93% of studies had at least 1 such author
  - 8% disclosed any COI and 2% disclosed clinical or consultancy COIs
  - 93% of "no COI" statements were false
- Of 150 early-intervention reports, only 11 mentioned adverse events. One reported that adverse events occurred and 3 reported adverse effects [[BottemaBeutel2021AE]].
- In the same review, 54 studies gave reasons for withdrawal. In 10 the reasons could be classed as adverse events, in 8 as adverse effects, and in 12 they were too vague to classify [[BottemaBeutel2021AE]].

### supports · NICE guidance (England)
- NICE CG170 (children and young people) was published 28 August 2013 and last updated 14 June 2021 [[NICE-CG170,Kendall2013]].
- NICE CG170 1.3.1 says to consider a social-communication intervention for the core features of autism. It should include play-based strategies with parents, carers and teachers to increase joint attention, engagement and reciprocal communication [[NICE-CG170]].
- CG170 1.3.1 says these strategies should:
  - be adjusted to developmental level
  - increase partners' understanding of, and sensitivity and responsiveness to, the child's patterns of communication
  - include therapist modelling and video-interaction feedback
  - be delivered by a trained professional, mediated by a parent, carer or teacher for pre-school children and by peers for school-aged children [[NICE-CG170]]
- CG170 1.3.2: do not use antipsychotics, antidepressants, anticonvulsants or exclusion diets (such as gluten- or casein-free diets) to manage the core features of autism [[NICE-CG170]].
- CG170 1.4.7: if no coexisting mental health, physical or environmental problem is triggering or maintaining behaviour that challenges, offer a psychosocial intervention "informed by a functional assessment of behaviour" as first-line treatment [[NICE-CG170]].
- CG170 1.4.8 says the functional assessment should identify triggers, patterns of behaviour, the needs the person is trying to meet with the behaviour, and its consequences (the reinforcement received) [[NICE-CG170]].
- CG170 1.4.9 says the intervention should include [[NICE-CG170]]:
  - a focus on outcomes linked to quality of life
  - a specified timescale to meet goals
  - a systematic measure of the target behaviour taken before and after
  - consistent application across home and school
- CG170 1.4.10 positions antipsychotics only after psychosocial or other interventions are insufficient or cannot be delivered. It states the drug should be stopped if there is no clinically important response at 6 weeks [[NICE-CG170]].
- CG170 1.6: do not use neurofeedback or auditory integration training for speech and language problems, or omega-3 fatty acids for sleep problems. Do not use secretin, chelation or hyperbaric oxygen to manage autism in any context [[NICE-CG170]].
- CG170 1.7.2–1.7.3 say to consider group or individual CBT for anxiety when the child has the verbal and cognitive ability to engage. Suggested adaptations include [[NICE-CG170]]:
  - emotion-recognition training
  - more written and visual information
  - concrete, structured approaches
  - parent involvement
  - regular breaks
  - using the young person's special interests
- NICE CG142 (adults) was published 27 June 2012 and last updated 14 June 2021. It says to consider group-based or individual social learning programmes for adults with social-interaction difficulties [[NICE-CG142]].
- CG142 1.4.3 says: "Do not provide facilitated communication for autistic adults." [[NICE-CG142]]
- CG142 also says to consider [[NICE-CG142]]:
  - structured leisure programmes
  - anger-management interventions adjusted for autism, including functional analysis of anger
  - anti-victimisation training
  - individual supported employment
- CG142 1.4.15 says do not use exclusion diets or vitamins, minerals and dietary supplements for core features. 1.4.17 says do not use oxytocin [[NICE-CG142]].
- CG142 1.5.3–1.5.5 say psychosocial interventions for behaviour that challenges in adults should use a functional analysis and be based on behavioural principles [[NICE-CG142]].

### aba · Behavioural roots: Lovaas, EIBI and what ABA means today
- Lovaas's 1987 UCLA Young Autism Project reported that 47% of the experimental group reached "normal functioning" [[Lovaas1987,Mudford2001]].
- Critics described the claimed outcome as "recovery" in 47% and large reductions in severity in a further 42%. They argued the study had serious threats to internal and external validity, including no random assignment (Established) [[Gresham1998]].
- Lovaas's treatment was delivered at about 40 hours a week. A UK sample of 75 children in EIBI programmes averaged 32 hours a week, and no child met all the original conditions (early start, 40 hours, accredited supervision) [[Mudford2001]].
- McEachin 1993 followed the 19 experimental and 19 control children from Lovaas 1987 to a mean age of 11.5 years. It reported that the experimental group kept its gains. Of the 9 best-outcome children, 8 were described as "indistinguishable from average children" on tests of intelligence and adaptive behaviour [[McEachin1993]].
- In a later small RCT (28 children with autism or PDD-NOS), the intensive group averaged 24.52 hours a week for 1 year. It outperformed a parent-training group on intelligence, visual-spatial skills, language and academics, but not on adaptive functioning or behaviour problems [[Smith2000AJMR]].
- Early behavioural work used physical aversives. In a 1969 study, Lovaas and Simmons gave three children painful electric shock contingent on self-injury. The behaviour was suppressed but came back when shock was removed [[LovaasSimmons1969]].
- A history of ABA describes how Lovaas's early methods used physical aversives. Neurodiversity-movement critics reject both these aversives and ABA's goal of removing benign autistic behaviour [[Kirkham2017]].
- Cochrane 2018 describes EIBI as ABA-based treatment delivered for several years at 20–40 hours a week [[Reichow2018Cochrane]].
- Cochrane 2018 (EIBI) included only 5 studies: 1 RCT and 4 controlled clinical trials, with 219 children (116 EIBI, 103 treatment as usual), aged 30–43 months at start and treated for 24–36 months [[Reichow2018Cochrane]].
- Cochrane 2018 found low-quality evidence that, after treatment, EIBI improved adaptive behaviour (Vineland composite MD 9.58, 95% CI 5.57 to 13.60; 5 studies, 202 participants) and IQ (MD 15.44, 95% CI 9.29 to 21.59) [[Reichow2018Cochrane]].
- Cochrane 2018 found that EIBI's effect on autism symptom severity was not significant (SMD −0.34, 95% CI −0.79 to 0.11; 2 studies, 81 participants) [[Reichow2018Cochrane]].
- Cochrane 2018 rated the overall certainty of evidence "low" or "very low" (GRADE) and called the evidence "weak" because most studies were small and not randomised. No adverse effects were reported [[Reichow2018Cochrane]].
- A 2026 umbrella review of 6 meta-analyses (36 unique studies, 2,241 children) of comprehensive ABA interventions reported for IQ: 3 meta-analyses with SMD above 0.50 and 3 with MD above 11.98 points. For adaptive behaviour it reported SMD above 0.35 or MD of 5.92 or more. The authors concluded the evidence was "consistent, replicated" [[Reichow2026Umbrella]].
- That umbrella review comes from the same lead author as the Cochrane review, but it did not apply the RCT-only and blinded-outcome filters of Project AIM. This is why the two reach different headline conclusions [[Reichow2026Umbrella,Sandbank2023BMJ]].
- Behaviour analysts point out that ABA-informed methods have been researched and used for over 50 years. They argue that critics' concerns should be answered by changing goals and procedures rather than abandoning the approach [[Leaf2022JADD]].

### aba · Functional behaviour assessment and positive behaviour support
- Functional analysis exposes a person briefly and repeatedly to conditions that vary play materials, task demands and social attention, to test which events maintain a behaviour [[Iwata1994]].
- In the original 1994 study (9 participants), 6 of the 9 showed higher self-injury consistently in one specific stimulus condition. This showed that self-injury often serves an identifiable environmental function [[Iwata1994]].
- Functional analysis became "a hallmark" of contemporary behavioural assessment. A review defined it as direct observation of problem behaviour under at least 2 conditions in which an environmental variable is manipulated [[Hanley2003]].
- Positive behaviour support (PBS) uses educational and systems-change methods to improve quality of life and reduce problem behaviour. It grew out of three sources: ABA, the normalisation and inclusion movement, and person-centred values [[Carr2002PBS]].
- Defining features of PBS include lifestyle change, a lifespan perspective, stakeholder participation, social validity, a focus on prevention and multicomponent intervention [[Carr2002PBS]].
- In its 2024 proposed ban, the FDA described state-of-the-art treatment for self-injurious or aggressive behaviour as a functional behavioural assessment followed by a multi-element supportive plan, sometimes with medication. It said these approaches had been "generally successful" without electric shock [[FDA2024ESDProposed]].

### aba · The ABA debate: autistic critiques
- Neurodiversity-movement critics argue that removing benign autistic behaviour through ABA is impermissible because of individual psychological harm and wider social impact. They also condemn painful aversives, with the Judge Rotenberg Center shock campaign as the main example [[Kirkham2017]].
- In a qualitative study of 10 autistic adults who had ABA in childhood, most reflections described detrimental impact, under the theme "recalling hidden harms". The study discussed effects on autistic identity [[McGill2020]].
- Seven autistic adults interviewed about childhood ABA [[Anderson2023]]:
  - remembered traumatic events
  - rejected being made to behave like peers
  - reported some benefits but "significant negative long-term consequences"
  - called ABA unethical
- One commentary argued that long-term ABA can amount to abuse because of its emphasis on compliance [[SandovalNorton2019]]. Behaviour analysts published a direct rebuttal [[Gorycki2020]].
- Camouflaging (masking) is described by autistic adults as a mix of masking and compensation strategies. Its consequences include exhaustion and threats to self-perception (Established as a described phenomenon) [[Hull2017]].
- Critics link compliance-focused goals to masking (Hypothesis) [[Hull2017,Anderson2023]].
- Many outcomes coded as "problem behaviour" in early-intervention trials are nonnormative but not harmful, such as repetitive movements. This is a measurement concern that overlaps with the autistic critique of normalisation goals [[BottemaBeutel2025]].
- Some behaviour analysts call for the field to listen "with humility", adopt the social model of disability and the neurodiversity paradigm, and change practice to match [[Mathur2024]].
- Others argue that claims that ABA is coercive, suppresses identity, causes trauma or is abuse rest on "unfounded criticism and longstanding myths" [[Travers2025]].
- NDBIs have been proposed as a bridge between early intervention and the neurodiversity movement, with suggested changes to goals and practice (Hypothesis) [[Schuck2022]].

### aba · Kupferstein 2018 and its limitations
- Kupferstein (2018) ran an online survey of 460 respondents: 243 autistic adults and 217 caregivers of autistic children. Adult inclusion allowed "diagnosed or self-diagnosed" autism [[Kupferstein2018]].
- The survey reported that 46% of ABA-exposed respondents met a PTSD threshold, and that exposed respondents were 86% more likely to meet PTSD criteria than unexposed respondents [[Kupferstein2018]].
- A critique listed these methodological flaws [[Leaf2018AiA]]:
  - leading questions in a non-validated survey
  - no confirmation of diagnosis
  - incomplete description of the interventions
  - correlational framing
  - the authors advised viewing the results "with extreme caution"
- Other limits of the design: recruitment through social media and support groups, a self-report cross-sectional design, and no way to separate ABA exposure from other childhood adversity. It cannot show causation (Established limitation) [[Kupferstein2018,Leaf2018AiA]].
- The journal published an expression of concern on Kupferstein 2018 in December 2025. Its stated reasons could not be read for this pass [[AiA2025EOC]].

### aba · Judge Rotenberg Center and the FDA electrical stimulation device ban
- The FDA first proposed banning electrical stimulation devices (ESDs) for self-injurious or aggressive behaviour on 25 April 2016 (81 FR 24386) [[FDA2016ESDProposed]].
- The final ban was published 6 March 2020 (85 FR 13312). It covered new devices and devices already in use, with transition time for people currently subject to ESDs [[FDA2020ESDBan]].
- On 19 August 2020, the FDA stayed the ban for individuals who had, or would need, a physician-directed transition plan, in response to petitions (85 FR 50950) [[FDA2020ESDStay]].
- The Judge Rotenberg Educational Center (JRC) in Massachusetts is the only US facility that uses ESDs and their sole manufacturer (the Graduated Electronic Decelerator). It challenged the ban, and on 6 July 2021 the D.C. Circuit vacated it (Judge Rotenberg Educational Center, Inc. v. FDA, 3 F.4th 390) [[FDA2024ESDProposed]].
- The court held that the FD&C Act, as then written, did not let the FDA ban a device for some intended uses but not others [[FDA2024ESDProposed]].
- Congress then passed the Food and Drug Omnibus Reform Act of 2022 (FDORA, Pub. L. 117-328). It amended section 516 of the FD&C Act to authorise bans for one or more intended uses [[FDA2024ESDProposed]].
- The FDA proposed a new ban on 26 March 2024 (89 FR 20882). It found that ESDs present "an unreasonable and substantial risk of illness or injury" that labelling cannot correct [[FDA2024ESDProposed]].
- A Federal Register search on 2026-10-25 found no final rule after the 2024 proposal [[FDA2024ESDProposed]].
- The 2024 proposal notes that most studies supporting ESDs were authored or co-authored by JRC staff, and that use of ESDs "was mostly abandoned decades ago" [[FDA2024ESDProposed]].

### therapies · Naturalistic developmental behavioural interventions (NDBI)
- NDBIs are delivered in natural settings. They share control between child and adult, use natural contingencies, and use behavioural strategies to teach developmentally appropriate skills. ESDM, JASPER and related models fall under this umbrella [[Schreibman2015]].
- ESDM (Dawson 2010 RCT) randomised 48 toddlers aged 18–30 months to 2 years of ESDM (therapists plus parents) or community referral [[Dawson2010]].
- After 2 years, the ESDM group's IQ rose by an average of 17.6 standard-score points, against 7.0 in the comparison group [[Dawson2010]].
- The ESDM group kept pace with typically developing norms in adaptive behaviour, while the comparison group fell further behind. More ESDM children moved from an "autism" to a "PDD-NOS" diagnosis [[Dawson2010]].
- In the same ESDM trial, EEG at age 4–6 years showed that ESDM children, like typically developing children, had greater cortical activation to faces than to objects. The community group showed the opposite pattern (Early evidence) [[Dawson2012]].
- A multisite ESDM replication randomised 118 children aged 14–24 months. ESDM was given as about 15 hours a week of 1:1 therapy for 24 months plus parent coaching. Only 81 of the 118 completed all assessments [[Rogers2019]].
- That replication found an ESDM advantage for language when data were pooled, but at only 2 of the 3 sites. It found no differences in developmental quotient, autism severity or adaptive behaviour: a "partial replication" [[Rogers2019]].
- A meta-analysis of 12 ESDM studies (640 children) found an overall g of 0.357. This was driven by cognition (g 0.412) and language (g 0.408), with no significant effect on autism symptoms, adaptive behaviour, social communication or repetitive behaviours [[Fuller2020]].
- JASPER's precursor RCT randomised 58 children aged 3–4 years to joint attention or symbolic play sessions (30 minutes a day for 5–6 weeks) or control. Each group improved in its targeted skill [[Kasari2006]].
- Twelve months later, both intervention groups had greater expressive-language gains than controls. In children with the lowest starting language, joint-attention training helped language most [[Kasari2008]].
- A sequential multiple assignment randomised trial (SMART) enrolled 61 minimally verbal children aged 5–8. Starting with JASP+EMT plus a speech-generating device gave more spontaneous communicative utterances, novel words and comments than spoken-word intervention alone [[Kasari2014]].
- Longitudinal analysis of the same SMART to week 36 again favoured the adaptive intervention that began with the speech-generating device [[Almirall2016]].
- A meta-analysis of 19 RCTs of parent-mediated interventions for children aged 1–6 found small effects: g 0.18 to 0.27, average 0.23. Dose of parent training did not change outcomes [[Nevill2018]].
- Cochrane 2013 (17 RCTs, 919 children) found strong evidence that parent-mediated interventions changed parent–child interaction [[Oono2013]]:
  - shared attention SMD 0.41
  - parent synchrony SMD 0.90
  - most child outcomes were inconclusive
- In 26 studies (1,738 children, mean age 3.3 years), early intervention improved spoken language, g 0.26 (95% CI 0.11 to 0.42). Effects were largest when both a parent and a clinician delivered it [[Hampton2016]].

### therapies · PACT and pre-emptive infant intervention
- PACT (Green 2010) randomised 152 children aged 2 years to 4 years 11 months at three UK centres: 77 to PACT plus treatment as usual and 75 to treatment as usual [[Green2010]].
- At 13 months, ADOS-G symptom scores fell by 3.9 points with PACT and 2.9 with treatment as usual. The between-group effect size was −0.24 (95% CI −0.59 to 0.11), which was not significant [[Green2010]].
- PACT clearly improved parent synchronous response (ES 1.22) and child initiations with the parent (ES 0.41) [[Green2010]].
- The PACT long-term follow-up traced 121 of 152 children (80%) at a median of 5.75 years after the trial endpoint (mean age 10.5 years) [[Pickles2016]].
- PACT's effect on ADOS CSS symptom severity was ES 0.64 (95% CI 0.07 to 1.20) at endpoint and 0.70 (−0.05 to 1.47) at follow-up. Over the whole period it was ES 0.55 (0.14 to 0.91, p=0.004): the first RCT to show long-term symptom reduction (Established) [[Pickles2016]].
- At follow-up, PACT showed no group difference in language (ES 0.15, 95% CI −0.23 to 0.53). Child initiations with the parent were ES 0.29 at follow-up and 0.33 across the study [[Pickles2016]].
- Mediation analysis found that increased child communication initiation at treatment midpoint explained 73% of PACT's effect on follow-up ADOS CSS (Early evidence) [[Carruthers2024]].
- iBASIS-VIPP in infant siblings (54 infants, 12 sessions at 9–14 months) showed an overall effect on prodromal autism symptoms to age 3 (ES 0.32, 95% CI 0.04 to 0.60). It had no effect on categorical diagnosis or formal language [[Green2017iBASIS]].
- The Australian iBASIS-VIPP trial randomised 103 infants aged 9–14 months who showed at least 3 early signs on the SACS-R. At the 6-month endpoint there was no difference in early autism signs on the AOSI (difference −0.74, 95% CI −2.47 to 0.98). Parent-reported vocabulary was higher [[Whitehouse2019]].
- In the outcomes-to-diagnosis report, 89 children were reassessed at age 3. Diagnostic classification was 3 of 45 (6.7%) with iBASIS-VIPP against 9 of 44 (20.5%) with usual care (OR 0.18, 95% CI 0 to 0.68; NNT 7.2) [[Whitehouse2021]].
- The analyses for that report were preregistered one-tailed tests, which should be considered when interpreting the result (Early evidence) [[Whitehouse2021]].

### therapies · TEACCH and structured teaching
- A meta-analysis of 13 TEACCH studies (172 people) found [[ViruesOrtega2013]]:
  - small effects on perceptual, motor, verbal and cognitive skills
  - negligible to small effects on adaptive behaviour
  - moderate to large gains in social and maladaptive behaviour
- The authors called these results exploratory because few studies were available. Effects were not moderated by duration, weekly hours or setting [[ViruesOrtega2013]].
- Project AIM included TEACCH as one of its 7 intervention types. Restricted to RCTs, only developmental and NDBI types kept positive effects [[Sandbank2020]].

### therapies · PECS, AAC and speech-generating devices
- PECS is taught in 6 phases [[Tamanaha2023]]:
  - I: physical exchange, how to communicate
  - II: distance and persistence
  - III: picture discrimination
  - IV: sentence structure
  - V: responding to "What do you want?"
  - VI: commenting
- In a 24-session programme with 22 minimally verbal children aged 6–12 years [[Tamanaha2023]]:
  - all children reached phases I–III
  - about 82% reached phase IV
  - 64% reached phase V
  - 19% reached phase VI
- A PECS meta-analysis (8 single-subject and 3 group studies) found small to moderate gains in communication, but gains in speech were "small to negative". It called PECS "promising but not yet established" [[Flippin2010]].
- In a group RCT of PECS teacher training in 84 children (mean age 6.8 years), PECS use and communicative initiations rose after treatment (OR 3.90 for PECS use; OR 2.72 for initiations). Speech, ADOS scores and language tests did not improve, and effects faded once the active intervention stopped [[Howlin2007]].
- Secondary analysis of that PECS RCT [[Gordon2011]]:
  - spontaneous communication with pictures rose (RR 1.90), as did speech (RR 1.77) and both together (RR 3.74)
  - requests for objects rose (RR 2.17), but social requests did not
  - only the effect on spontaneous speech lasted to 9 months
- A meta-analysis of 24 single-case studies found large overall effects of aided AAC. PECS and speech-generating devices had larger effects than other picture-based systems [[Ganz2012]].
- A systematic review of 9 single-subject and 2 group studies found that AAC interventions "do not impede speech production". Most studies reported increased speech, though the gains were modest (Established) [[Schlosser2008]].

### therapies · Facilitated communication and spelling-to-communicate/RPM
- In facilitated communication (FC), a facilitator gives physical support while the person types or points [[Schlosser2014FC]].
- An ISAAC-commissioned systematic review found "unequivocal evidence for facilitator control": FC messages are authored by the facilitator, and FC "has no validity" (Established) [[Schlosser2014FC]].
- ASHA's 2018 position statement calls FC "a discredited technique that should not be used". It cites extensive evidence that messages are authored by the facilitator and of harms from FC [[ASHA2018FC]].
- ASHA's 2018 RPM statement says the Rapid Prompting Method "is not recommended because of prompt dependency and the lack of scientific validity". Information obtained through RPM should not be assumed to be the person's communication [[ASHA2018RPM]].
- RPM differs from FC in that the facilitator holds and moves the letter board rather than the person's hand. Its proponents make claims similar to those once made for FC [[Tostanoski2014]].
- A 2019 systematic review found no evidence supporting RPM [[Schlosser2019RPM]].
- A 2026 systematic review of authorship in RPM, Spelling2Communicate (S2C), Spellers Method and variants found no study that met its design criteria (an "empty review"). The authors advise discontinuing these methods until independent authorship studies show they are valid [[Schlosser2026RPM]].
- NICE CG142 recommends not providing facilitated communication for autistic adults [[NICE-CG142]].

### therapies · Occupational therapy and sensory approaches
- A manualised Ayres Sensory Integration RCT in children aged 4–8 (17 treated vs 15 usual care, 30 sessions) found better Goal Attainment Scale scores (d 1.2, p=0.003), caregiver assistance in self-care (d 0.9) and socialisation (d 0.7) [[Schaaf2014]].
- A systematic review using Council for Exceptional Children (CEC) criteria rated Ayres Sensory Integration an evidence-based practice for ages 4–12. It rested on 2 RCTs plus 1 further study, and its authors are linked to the approach [[Schoen2019]].
- The UK SenITA pragmatic RCT randomised 138 children to 26 weeks of manualised sensory integration therapy or usual care. It found no significant effect on its primary outcome, irritability on the Aberrant Behavior Checklist, at 6 months (adjusted MD 0.40, 95% CI −2.33 to 3.14) or 12 months [[Randell2022]].
- SenITA also found the therapy was not cost-effective, although carers and therapists reported improved daily functioning [[Randell2022]].
- The AAP (2012) said research on sensory integration therapy is "limited and inconclusive". It said sensory-based OT may be acceptable as one component of a plan, ideally with a defined trial period [[Zimmer2012]].
- A review of 7 weighted-vest studies concluded that "on balance" weighted vests are ineffective and "cannot be recommended for clinical application" [[Stephenson2009]].
- In a blinded single-case randomised study of 6 children, weighted vests did not reduce motor stereotypy or heart rate in any participant [[Hodgetts2011]].
- In a placebo-controlled crossover RCT, 67 autistic children aged 5–16 with severe sleep problems used a weighted blanket and an identical usual-weight blanket for 2 weeks each. The weighted blanket did not increase total sleep time or improve sleep onset or waking, but children and parents preferred it [[Gringras2014]].

### therapies · Social skills programmes (PEERS and groups)
- PEERS is a manualised, parent-assisted social skills programme. Its first controlled study (teens aged 13–17) found more social-skills knowledge, more hosted get-togethers and better parent-rated social skills than a delayed-treatment group [[Laugeson2009]].
- A later PEERS trial found improved social responsiveness and more peer interactions. Teacher ratings improved and most gains were kept at 14-week follow-up [[Laugeson2012]].
- PEERS for young adults (22 people aged 18–24 randomised) improved social skills, social engagement and knowledge. Most gains held at 16 weeks [[Laugeson2015]].
- Cochrane 2012 (5 RCTs, 196 participants aged 6–21) found that social skills groups improved social competence (ES 0.47, 95% CI 0.16 to 0.78) and friendship quality (ES 0.41). All 5 trials were from the US, mostly of children aged 7–12 with average or above-average IQ [[Reichow2012Cochrane]].
- A 2017 meta-analysis of 19 RCTs of group social skills interventions found an overall g of 0.51. By rater [[Gates2017]]:
  - self-report: 0.92
  - task-based: 0.58
  - parent: 0.47
  - observer: 0.40
  - teacher: not significant
  - the self-report effect reflected social knowledge (g 1.15) rather than social performance (g 0.28, not significant)

### therapies · CBT for anxiety and music therapy
- In a three-arm RCT of 167 children aged 7–13 with autism and interfering anxiety, CBT adapted for autism (BIACA) beat standard CBT (Coping Cat) and treatment as usual on the Pediatric Anxiety Rating Scale [[Wood2020]].
- Treatment response in that RCT was 92.4% (61/66) with adapted CBT, 81.0% (47/58) with standard CBT and 11.1% (2/18) with treatment as usual [[Wood2020]].
- A meta-analysis of 19 RCTs of CBT for anxiety in autistic youth (833 participants) found effect sizes that depended on who rated the symptoms. Benefits were not maintained at follow-up [[Sharma2021]]:
  - clinician-rated: g 0.88
  - parent-rated: 0.40
  - child-rated: 0.25
- Across 48 studies of CBT in autistic people, effects for affective disorders were g 0.24 (not significant) on self-report, 0.66 on informant report and 0.73 on clinician report [[Weston2016]].
- Cochrane 2022 on music therapy (26 studies, 1,165 participants) found music therapy more likely than placebo therapy or standard care to produce global improvement immediately after treatment: RR 1.22 (95% CI 1.06 to 1.40), NNTB 11 in a low-risk population (moderate certainty) [[Geretsegger2022]].
- The same review found that music therapy probably reduces total autism symptom severity (SMD −0.83) and slightly improves quality of life (SMD 0.28). There was no clear effect on social interaction or on verbal or non-verbal communication [[Geretsegger2022]].
- The largest single music therapy trial (TIME-A, 364 children aged 4–7 in 9 countries) found no difference in the ADOS social affect domain over 5 months (mean difference 0.06, 95% CI −0.70 to 0.81) [[Bieleninik2017]].

### supports · What autistic people and families prioritise
- UK community consultation found a mismatch between where autism research money went and what autistic adults, families and practitioners wanted. They prioritised research that makes a difference to "day-to-day lives" [[Pellicano2014]].
- A systematic review of 7 priority-setting studies found consistent priorities for research leading to real-world change in daily life, and for lifelong skill training [[Roche2021]].
- In a survey of 255 self-reporting autistic adults and 143 guardian-reported adults, the top priorities were life skills, co-occurring conditions, vocational and educational opportunities, public services, health-care access and, "above all", public acceptance [[Gotham2015]].
- A stakeholder project co-led by autistic adults (over 350 stakeholders) set 5 mental-health research priorities and preferred outcomes to guide research and practice [[Benevides2020]].

### Exam hooks
- Project AIM: restricting to RCTs with outcomes free of detection bias leaves no significant effects (2020), or only NDBI on diagnostic characteristics, g 0.30 (2023) [[Sandbank2020,Sandbank2023BMJ]].
- More hours did not mean bigger effects: there was no association between intervention amount and effect size in 144 studies [[Sandbank2024JAMAPed]].
- Cochrane EIBI 2018: 5 studies, only 1 RCT, 219 children; certainty low or very low [[Reichow2018Cochrane]].
- Lovaas 1987 claimed 47% "normal functioning" at about 40 hours a week. The study was not randomised [[Mudford2001,Gresham1998]].
- NICE first-line for behaviour that challenges is a psychosocial intervention informed by a functional assessment, after checking for physical, mental health and environmental causes [[NICE-CG170]].
- NICE says do not use neurofeedback or auditory integration training for speech and language, or secretin, chelation or hyperbaric oxygen in any context [[NICE-CG170]].
- PACT: no significant effect at 13 months (ES −0.24), but ADOS CSS ES 0.55 over about 6 years of follow-up [[Green2010,Pickles2016]].
- iBASIS-VIPP: 6.7% vs 20.5% met autism criteria at age 3 (NNT 7.2) [[Whitehouse2021]].
- AAC does not stop children learning to speak [[Schlosser2008]].
- Facilitated communication: messages are authored by the facilitator. ASHA (2018) says it should not be used [[Schlosser2014FC,ASHA2018FC]].
- Adapted CBT response: 92.4% vs 11.1% with treatment as usual [[Wood2020]].
- Weighted blankets did not lengthen sleep in an RCT, though families preferred them [[Gringras2014]].
- Sensory integration therapy: the SenITA RCT (n=138) was negative on its primary outcome and not cost-effective [[Randell2022]].
- About 70% of early-intervention reports had a conflict of interest, but only about 6% fully disclosed it [[BottemaBeutel2021COI]].
- Only 11 of 150 intervention reports mentioned adverse events [[BottemaBeutel2021AE]].
- The FDA banned ESDs in March 2020. The court vacated the ban in July 2021. FDORA 2022 restored the FDA's authority, and a new ban was proposed in March 2024 [[FDA2020ESDBan,FDA2024ESDProposed]].
- Music therapy: global improvement RR 1.22 (Cochrane 2022), but TIME-A found no difference on ADOS social affect [[Geretsegger2022,Bieleninik2017]].

### In plain words
- No therapy "cures" autism, and that is not the goal. Good supports help a person communicate, learn skills they want and live well [[Hyman2020,Pellicano2014]].
- When researchers use the fairest tests, the benefits of most early therapies look smaller than older studies suggested [[Sandbank2023BMJ]].
- More hours of therapy each week has not been shown to work better than fewer hours [[Sandbank2024JAMAPed,Rogers2021]].
- Therapies that coach parents to follow their child's lead in play can change how parent and child communicate, sometimes for years [[Pickles2016,Oono2013]].
- Using pictures, devices or other AAC does not stop a child learning to talk [[Schlosser2008]].
- If someone else is holding your hand or the letter board, the words may be theirs, not yours. That is why facilitated communication and RPM are not recommended [[ASHA2018FC,ASHA2018RPM]].
- If a behaviour is hurting someone, first look for pain, illness, stress or a problem in the environment, and ask what the behaviour is trying to say [[NICE-CG170]].
- Many autistic adults who had ABA as children say parts of it harmed them. Some behaviour analysts are changing how they work in response [[Anderson2023,Mathur2024]].
- Talking therapy (CBT) can help with anxiety when it is adapted to how an autistic person thinks and communicates [[Wood2020,NICE-CG170]].
- Weighted blankets and vests are popular, but studies have not shown that they improve sleep or behaviour [[Gringras2014,Stephenson2009]].
- Ask any therapy provider what the goal is, how progress will be measured, and what side effects or downsides they track [[BottemaBeutel2021AE,NICE-CG170]].
- Autistic people say what matters most is everyday life: health, work, skills and being accepted [[Gotham2015,Roche2021]].

## Open questions and controversies
- **Is ABA effective?** Umbrella reviews of comprehensive ABA report consistent IQ and adaptive gains [[Reichow2026Umbrella]]. Project AIM finds that behavioural effects do not survive restriction to blinded, non-caregiver-rated RCT outcomes [[Sandbank2023BMJ]], and Cochrane rates EIBI evidence low or very low certainty [[Reichow2018Cochrane]]. Status: unresolved. The answer depends on which designs and outcome measures are accepted.
- **Is ABA harmful?** Autistic adults' qualitative accounts describe trauma and masking [[McGill2020,Anderson2023]]. The only quantitative study (Kupferstein 2018) is a self-selected, non-validated survey, now under an expression of concern [[Kupferstein2018,Leaf2018AiA,AiA2025EOC]]. Trials almost never measure harms [[BottemaBeutel2021AE]]. Status: harm is plausible and under-measured (Hypothesis), and needs prospective measurement.
- **Can ABA reform?** One group calls for neurodiversity-affirming ABA [[Mathur2024]]. Another defends current ABA as evidence-based and calls the criticisms myths [[Travers2025]]. A third proposes NDBIs as a bridge [[Schuck2022]]. Status: active debate.
- **What should count as a "problem" behaviour?** Trials often mix harmful behaviour with harmless autistic behaviour [[BottemaBeutel2025]]. NICE asks for quality-of-life-linked outcomes [[NICE-CG170]]. Status: no agreed definition.
- **How many hours?** Recommendations of 20–40 hours a week are common, but meta-regression and a randomised intensity trial found no added benefit from more hours [[Sandbank2024JAMAPed,Rogers2021]]. Status: high intensity is not supported by current evidence.
- **Pre-emptive intervention.** iBASIS-VIPP reduced the odds of diagnosis at age 3 in one trial using one-tailed tests [[Whitehouse2021]]. An earlier infant-sibling trial found no effect on diagnosis [[Green2017iBASIS]]. Status: needs replication. Whether "fewer diagnoses" is the right goal is itself debated (Hypothesis).
- **Sensory integration.** A small RCT supports it [[Schaaf2014]] and a review by proponents rates it evidence-based [[Schoen2019]]. The larger pragmatic SenITA RCT was negative [[Randell2022]], and the AAP calls the evidence inconclusive [[Zimmer2012]]. Status: unresolved, with independent evidence weaker.
- **Spelling to communicate/RPM.** Users report it lets them communicate. ASHA and systematic reviews find no authorship evidence and recommend against it [[ASHA2018RPM,Schlosser2026RPM]]. Status: controlled authorship testing is still lacking.
- **ESDs at JRC.** The 2020 ban was vacated in 2021, and the 2024 re-proposal had no final rule in the Federal Register as of 2026-10-25 [[FDA2024ESDProposed]].
