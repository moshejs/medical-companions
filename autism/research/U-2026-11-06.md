# U · Research update 2026-11-06

Pass date: 2026-11-06. Window: 2026-11-04 to 2026-11-06. Modules served: what (why the numbers rose), genes (female protective effect).

## Findings

### what · Who is diagnosed as an adult
- In UK primary care (2010–2023; 24,537,039 adults, 141,119 with an autism diagnosis) and Swedish national registers (2010–2021; 9,096,874 adults, 100,817 with a diagnosis), first-recorded adult autism diagnoses were consistently higher in Sweden than in the UK, but UK incidence rose rapidly after 2020 [[Sadik2026]].
- In both countries new adult diagnoses rose fastest at ages 16–20 and in females, and also in white ethnic groups (UK) and people with Swedish-born parents (Sweden) [[Sadik2026]].
- Among adults newly diagnosed in the UK, a prior epilepsy record fell from 10% (95% CI 7.6–13%) in 2010 to 4% (3.6–4.5%) in 2023, and a prior anxiety record rose from 28.7% (24.4–33.6%) to 58.3% (56.6–60.1%). Prior intellectual disability and psychosis also fell, while prior ADHD, depression and other mental illnesses rose; trends were similar in females and males (Established, observational) [[Sadik2026]].
- The authors state that whether these changes reflect "nuanced recognition of autism or broadening of diagnostic practice needs investigation". This fits, and does not change, the site's reading of the earlier UK rise [[Russell2022,Sadik2026]].

### genes · A proposed mechanism for the female protective effect
- In human neural progenitor cells, knocking down any of 18 autism genes produced overlapping changes in gene expression, including down-regulation of other autism genes; CHD8 and REST emerged as central regulators of this convergence (Early evidence) [[Andersen2026]].
- The X-linked transcription factor ZFX, expressed from both the active and inactive X chromosome in females, activated many autism genes. The authors propose that higher ZFX levels in the female brain "can buffer damaging mutations in diverse ASD genes", contributing to the female protective effect (Hypothesis: cell-model data only, not yet tested in people) [[Andersen2026]].
- Disclosure: four authors plan a patent application on ZFX and ASD [[Andersen2026]].

### Exam hooks
- Adults newly diagnosed with autism in the UK and Sweden since 2010 are younger-skewed (16–20), more often female, less often have epilepsy or intellectual disability, and more often have anxiety, depression or ADHD [[Sadik2026]].
- Female protective effect: family and genetic-load evidence is Early evidence [[Robinson2013,Antaki2022]]; ZFX buffering is a cell-model Hypothesis [[Andersen2026]].

### In plain words
- Adults getting an autism diagnosis today look different from those diagnosed in 2010: more are young women, and more already had anxiety or depression [[Sadik2026]].
- Lab work suggests a gene on the X chromosome might help explain why fewer girls are autistic. This has only been studied in cells so far [[Andersen2026]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Why adult diagnoses rose: better recognition of previously missed autistic adults [[Russell2022]]; broadening of diagnostic practice [[Sadik2026]]; status: the authors say it "needs investigation".
- Female protective effect: genetic-load and family-recurrence evidence [[Antaki2022,Ozonoff2024]]; a ZFX mechanism from cell models [[Andersen2026]]; diagnostic under-recognition of girls [[Loomes2017]]; status: open.
