An animated, cited guide for students, people who have had a heart attack, and their families and caregivers. Educational only, not medical advice. Your place is saved on this device.

Not medical adviceThe Heart Attack Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. It does not replace a doctor, nurse, pharmacist, paramedic or other clinician. ECG criteria, troponin algorithms, the HEART, TIMI and GRACE scores and the risk calculators are teaching aids, never for self-triage or for deciding whether to seek care, and doses, aspirin wording, CPR steps and guideline statements are quoted as published, never as instructions. Survival and mortality figures are population statistics, not predictions for one person. If you think you or someone else is having a heart attack, call your local emergency number now (911 in the US, 999 in the UK, 112 in the EU).

0 h/1 h algorithmThe ESC's preferred rapid troponin pathway: high-sensitivity troponin at presentation and 1 hour later sorts patients into rule-out, observe or rule-in. Rule-out NPV has been above 99% 46.
0 h/2 h algorithmThe ESC's "second-best option" rapid troponin pathway, with a second sample at 2 hours 46.
99th-percentile upper reference limit (URL)The troponin decision level for myocardial injury, set for each assay; UDMI4 recommends sex-specific values for hs-cTn assays 453.
ACE inhibitorA medicine that blocks angiotensin-converting enzyme. After MI with LV dysfunction, captopril cut mortality 19% in SAVE 342.
Acute coronary syndrome (ACS)The umbrella term for STEMI, NSTEMI and unstable angina; the ESC working diagnosis is classified as STEMI or NSTE-ACS using the ECG 46.
AdherenceTaking medicines as prescribed. Only about 4 in 10 older adults were still adherent to a statin 2 years after ACS 205.
AED (automated external defibrillator)A public device that analyses the rhythm and can deliver a shock. Survival to discharge was 38% when an AED delivered a shock before EMS arrived 475.
AlirocumabA PCSK9 antibody. In ODYSSEY OUTCOMES, given 1–12 months after ACS, it gave HR 0.85, with deaths 3.5% vs 4.1% 396.
AnticoagulantA drug that acts on clotting proteins (thrombin, fibrin) rather than platelets, such as heparin, enoxaparin, fondaparinux or bivalirudin. The ESC indicates a combination of antiplatelet and anticoagulant therapy acutely 46.
AntiplateletA drug that makes platelets less sticky: aspirin, the P2Y12 inhibitors and the GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors 131,459.
Aortic dissectionA tear in the wall of the aorta that can mimic MI. In IRAD, sudden severe sharp pain was commonest and the ECG was normal in 31.3% 176.
ApoBApolipoprotein B, carried by LDL and other atherogenic particles. Retention of apoB-containing lipoproteins starts atherosclerosis 433.
ApoB/ApoA1 ratioThe INTERHEART lipid measure with the largest population attributable risk (49.2%) 489.
ARC-HBRAcademic Research Consortium for High Bleeding Risk. One major or two minor criteria indicate high bleeding risk 46,460.
ARICAtherosclerosis Risk in Communities: US community surveillance used for AHA incidence estimates and long-term trends 280,378.
AspirinAn irreversible inhibitor of platelet COX-1 459. In ISIS-2 it cut 5-week vascular death from 11.8% to 9.4% 202. Doses are quoted from guidelines, never as instructions.
AtherosclerosisThe slow build-up of fatty plaque in artery walls, often starting in the teens or twenties 429,456.
Atrial fibrillation (AF) after MIAn irregular heart rhythm in 6–21% of acute MIs; it raises mortality and stroke risk, even when transient 391.
AV block (heart block)A conduction block between atria and ventricles. After inferior MI it usually reflects right coronary occlusion; in the PCI era its excess mortality is confined to the first 30 days 150.
Bempedoic acidAn oral LDL-lowering drug for statin intolerance; CLEAR Outcomes MACE HR 0.87, with gout and gallstones to watch for 331.
Beta-blockerA medicine that slows the heart. After MI with LVEF ≤40%, carvedilol lowered mortality (HR 0.77) 84; with preserved EF, recent trials disagree 312,486.
BivalirudinA direct thrombin inhibitor used during PCI instead of heparin. Trials disagree: in EUROMAX it cut death or major bleeding but raised acute stent thrombosis 420; in HEAT-PPCI ischaemic events were higher than with heparin 402.
Bystander CPRChest compressions by people at the scene before EMS arrives; linked with roughly doubled odds of 30-day survival (OR 2.15) 181.
CABG (coronary artery bypass grafting)Surgery that routes blood around blocked coronary arteries using a vein or artery graft 270.
Calcified noduleAn uncommon cause of coronary thrombosis, after rupture and erosion 468.
CangrelorAn intravenous, reversible P2Y12 inhibitor: onset within 2 minutes and offset within 1 hour 125.
Cardiac MRI (CMR)Magnetic resonance imaging of the heart; in MINOCA it often shows myocarditis (33%) 339, and it detects LV thrombus more sensitively than echo 259.
Cardiac rehabilitation (CR)A programme of exercise, education and support. Exercise-based CR cut MI (RR 0.72) and hospital admission at 6–12 months 98.
Cardiogenic shockEnd-organ hypoperfusion because the heart cannot pump enough; up to 10% after acute MI, with about 40% mortality at 30 days (population statistics) 388.
Case fatalityThe share of people with an event who die of it. About 14% of people who have an MI die of it (ARIC, 2005–2014) 280.
Chain of survivalThe linked steps of recognising arrest, calling, CPR and early defibrillation; the 2025 AHA guidelines emphasise high-quality compressions and early defibrillation 87,237.
Chest pain-equivalent symptomsESC 2023 lists dyspnoea, epigastric pain, and pain in the left or right arm or neck/jaw 46.
ClopidogrelA P2Y12 prodrug activated mainly by CYP2C19; its boxed warning concerns poor metabolisers 131. CURE: 20% fewer events with aspirin in NSTE-ACS 488.
ColchicineAn anti-inflammatory drug. COLCOT after MI was positive, CLEAR SYNERGY neutral Early evidence 216,436.
Complete revascularisationTreating non-culprit narrowings as well as the culprit. In stable STEMI with multivessel disease it reduced CV death or MI (COMPLETE) 296.
Coronary care unit (CCU)A ward for monitored care of MI patients, begun with Julian's 1961 paper 39,219.
COX-1Cyclooxygenase-1, the platelet enzyme that aspirin acetylates at serine 529 459.
CTT (Cholesterol Treatment Trialists)The collaboration whose 2010 meta-analysis found about 22% fewer major vascular events per 1 mmol/L LDL-C lowering 75.
Culprit lesionThe plaque responsible for the acute event. In shock with multivessel disease, culprit-only PCI first was better (CULPRIT-SHOCK) 442.
CYP2C19The liver enzyme that activates clopidogrel; some people's genes make it work less well 131.
DAPT (dual antiplatelet therapy)Aspirin plus a P2Y12 inhibitor. ESC 2023 default after ACS: aspirin plus prasugrel or ticagrelor for 12 months 46.
De-escalationSwitching from a potent P2Y12 inhibitor to clopidogrel; ESC 2023 does not recommend it in the first 30 days after ACS 46.
de Winter patternUpsloping precordial ST depression at the J point linked to proximal LAD occlusion without classic ST elevation 92,453.
Depression after MIAbout 1 in 5 people (19.8%) 447; it roughly doubles mortality risk (OR 2.25) 297. A treatable illness, not weakness.
DIDO (door-in to door-out)Time spent at a non-PCI hospital before transfer; ESC 2023 recommends ≤30 minutes 46.
Door-to-balloon timeTime from hospital arrival to the first device in the artery. US medians fell from 96 min (2005) to 64 min (2010) 251.
Door-to-ECG timeTime from arrival to the first ECG; the goal is ≤10 minutes. Women, Black patients and people without chest pain waited longer 485.
Dressler syndrome (post-cardiac injury syndrome)Immune-mediated pericarditis after injury to the heart; treatment is empirical anti-inflammatory therapy plus colchicine 200.
Driving after MIUK audits found written driving advice often missing from discharge summaries; teaching improved documentation 28,106.
Dyspnoea on ticagrelorBreathlessness in about 14% (PLATO), usually mild to moderate and often settling on treatment 121.
ECG (electrocardiogram)A recording of the heart's electrical activity; for suspected MI it should be done within 10 minutes of first medical contact 46.
EMS (emergency medical services)Ambulance care. EMS transport was linked with shorter onset-to-arrival (89 vs 120 min) and door-to-balloon times 281.
Erosion (plaque erosion)Loss of the plaque surface without rupture; more common in younger patients, women and NSTE-ACS 118,211.
EvolocumabA PCSK9 antibody; in FOURIER it cut LDL-C 59% to a median 30 mg/dL (HR 0.85) 383.
EzetimibeA pill that blocks cholesterol absorption; IMPROVE-IT: 2.0-point absolute reduction at 7 years after ACS 50.
Fatty streakAn AHA type II lesion, the first lesion visible to the naked eye; types I–II are usual in children 418.
Fibrinolysis (thrombolysis)Clot-dissolving drug treatment. ESC 2023: start within 10 min of STEMI diagnosis when primary PCI is not feasible within 120 min 46.
First medical contact (FMC)The moment a patient is first assessed by a paramedic or clinician; the ECG clock starts here 46.
FondaparinuxA factor Xa inhibitor; less bleeding than enoxaparin in NSTE-ACS (OASIS-5), but not recommended for primary PCI 46,491.
Framingham Heart StudyA US cohort that examined its first volunteer in 1948 and named "factors of risk" in 1961 221,276.
Free wall ruptureA tear in the outer wall of the ventricle, a rare mechanical complication usually within the first week 167.
Golden hourThe first hour of symptoms, when fibrinolysis saved the most lives (65 per 1000) 34.
GP IIb/IIIa inhibitorAn intravenous antiplatelet such as tirofiban; ESC 2023 reserves it for bail-out (no-reflow or thrombotic complication) 46,120.
GRACE scoreA model of 8 in-hospital variables, including age, Killip class, blood pressure and creatinine 168. GRACE >140 favours an early invasive strategy 46.
GTN (glyceryl trinitrate, nitroglycerin)A nitrate for angina; the label limits sublingual use to 3 tablets in 15 minutes and forbids use with PDE-5 inhibitors 130.
HEART scoreHistory, ECG, Age, Risk factors, Troponin, each 0–2, total 0–10 407. A clinician's teaching tool, not for self-triage.
Heart failure after MIA weaker pump after MI. In Framingham, 5-year HF incidence rose (27.6% to 31.9%) as more people survived the MI 467.
Hibernation (hibernating myocardium)Muscle that stops contracting during ongoing low flow and recovers with revascularisation 355.
High-sensitivity troponin (hs-cTn)Assays that detect troponin at very low levels, shortening the time to diagnosis to within 3 h in many patients 453.
Home-based cardiac rehabRehab at home with team support; outcomes similar to centre-based rehab 285.
Hyperacute T wavesTall, broad-based, symmetrical T waves that may come before ST elevation 453.
IABP (intra-aortic balloon pump)A balloon in the aorta that inflates in diastole. Routine use did not reduce mortality in MI with shock (IABP-SHOCK II) 440.
ICD (implantable cardioverter-defibrillator)A device that shocks dangerous rhythms. Early ICDs after MI did not cut total mortality 195; later, with LVEF ≤30%, they did (MADIT-II) 310.
InclisiranA small interfering RNA that lowers LDL-C about 50% with injections at day 1, day 90, then every 6 months 126,360.
Infarct sizeHow much muscle dies. Each 5% larger infarct was linked to HR 1.19 for 1-year death after primary PCI 427.
Influenza vaccinationA flu shot soon after MI reduced death, MI or stent thrombosis at 12 months (IAMI, HR 0.72) 148.
Internal mammary artery graftThe chest-wall artery grafted to the LAD in CABG; vein-only patients had 1.61 times the 10-year risk of death 270.
INTERHEARTA case-control study in 52 countries: nine modifiable factors explained 90% (men) and 94% (women) of the risk of a first MI 489.
IschaemiaToo little blood flow for the muscle's needs; prolonged ischaemia causes necrosis from the inner wall outward 365.
Ischaemic preconditioningBrief prior occlusions that protect muscle: four 5-minute occlusions cut infarct size to 25% of control in dogs 314.
J pointWhere the QRS ends and the ST segment begins; ST elevation is measured here 453.
Killip classA bedside heart-failure grading in MI; part of GRACE (OR 2.0 per class) 168. Named after Killip and Kimball's 1967 CCU report 230.
LAD (left anterior descending artery)The coronary artery down the front of the heart. The de Zwaan–Wellens pattern marks a critical stenosis high in the LAD 95.
LBBB (left bundle branch block)A conduction pattern that "precludes an accurate assessment" of ST elevation; with high suspicion, manage like STEMI 46.
LDL cholesterol (LDL-C)The cholesterol in low-density lipoproteins, a cause of atherosclerotic disease 135.
Left main diseaseNarrowing of the left main coronary artery; PCI and CABG gave similar 5-year death in pooled trials (10.7% vs 10.5%) 185.
Lp(a) (lipoprotein(a))An inherited LDL-like particle, more than 90% genetic 249. ≥50 mg/dL (≥125 nmol/L) is a risk-enhancing factor 13.
LV thrombusA clot in the left ventricle, up to 25% after anterior MI with optimal imaging; anticoagulation prevents embolism 284.
LVEF (left ventricular ejection fraction)The share of blood the left ventricle pumps out per beat; ≤30% after MI raised early sudden-death risk to 2.3% per month 415.
MACEMajor adverse cardiac events, a composite trial end point; HEART 0–3 carried 1.7% 6-week MACE 19.
Mechanical complicationsSeptal, free wall or papillary muscle rupture; 0.27% of STEMI but 42.4% in-hospital mortality 111.
Microaxial flow pumpA catheter pump (Impella CP); in DanGer Shock it lowered 180-day death in selected STEMI shock, with more complications 305.
MINOCAMI with non-obstructive coronary arteries: about 6% of MI; a working diagnosis that needs a cause 339,435.
Morphine and P2Y12 absorptionMorphine reduced ticagrelor exposure by about 36% and delayed its peak 252.
Myocardial infarction (MI)Heart attack: acute myocardial injury with clinical evidence of acute ischaemia 101,452.
Myocardial injuryA troponin above the 99th-percentile URL; acute if it rises and/or falls. Without ischaemia it is not MI 453.
Necrotic coreThe lipid-rich, dead centre of a plaque under the fibrous cap 468.
NSTE-ACSNon-ST-elevation acute coronary syndrome: NSTEMI and unstable angina; the ECG may be normal in more than a third 46.
NSTEMINon-ST-elevation MI; troponin testing raised its counts and it now makes up the majority of MIs 376.
Occlusion MI (OMI)A proposed paradigm based on whether the culprit artery is occluded; STEMI criteria were 41% sensitive for OMI Hypothesis as a replacement for STEMI/NSTEMI 303.
Oxygen therapyRoutine oxygen with SpO2 ≥90% did not lower mortality 194; continued SpO2 monitoring is needed 194.
P2Y12 inhibitorAn antiplatelet blocking the platelet ADP receptor: clopidogrel, prasugrel, ticagrelor or cangrelor 121,131.
Papillary muscle ruptureRupture of a mitral valve support muscle, causing acute mitral regurgitation; 36.3% in-hospital mortality 29.
Patent haemostasisCompression after radial access that keeps the artery open, confirmed by Barbeau's test; cut radial occlusion 75% at 30 days 336.
Patient delayTime from symptom onset to seeking care; an AHA statement calls it "the major factor limiting delivery of definitive treatment" 309.
PCI (percutaneous coronary intervention)Opening a coronary artery with a wire, balloon and stent through the wrist or groin 225,462.
PCSK9 inhibitorAn injected antibody (evolocumab, alirocumab) that sharply lowers LDL-C 383,396.
PericarditisInflammation of the sac around the heart: chest pain, a rub, ECG changes and effusion 201. Early post-MI pericarditis fell to 1.2% in the reperfusion era 254.
Pharmaco-invasive strategyFibrinolysis followed by angiography; in STREAM, similar outcomes to primary PCI when PCI could not be done within 1 hour 12.
Plaque ruptureA crack in a plaque's fibrous cap that exposes the core to blood; the most common cause of coronary thrombosis 115,468.
PlateletA tiny blood cell that sticks, activates and clumps on an exposed plaque, starting the clot 46.
PolypillOne tablet combining several heart medicines; SECURE (aspirin, ramipril, atorvastatin) cut MACE (HR 0.76) Early evidence 55.
PrasugrelA potent, irreversible P2Y12 inhibitor; beat clopidogrel in TRITON with more bleeding 478. Contraindicated after TIA or stroke 123.
PRECISE-DAPTA five-item bleeding score; ≥25 marks high bleeding risk on DAPT 71.
Prehospital ECGAn ECG recorded in the ambulance; median door-to-balloon 61 vs 75 min with it 100.
Primary PCIPCI as the first reperfusion treatment for STEMI; preferred if done within 120 min of the ECG diagnosis 46.
Proton pump inhibitor (PPI)A stomach-protecting drug; in COGENT omeprazole cut GI events on DAPT 30. The Plavix label advises avoiding omeprazole or esomeprazole 131.
PTSD after ACSTrauma-like stress symptoms in about 12%, doubling the risk of recurrence or death 108.
Pulmonary embolism (PE)A clot in the lung arteries that can mimic MI; the Wells score and PERC rule help assess probability 238,476.
Q wave (pathological)A deep, wide Q wave showing necrosis; prior-MI Q waves are most specific when >0.04 s or in several leads 453.
Radial accessPCI through the wrist artery; the ESC lists it as default access to reduce bleeding 46.
Radial artery occlusionClosure of the wrist artery after radial access: 7.7% within 24 h and 5.5% after a week 358.
Red-flag symptomsESC 2023: prolonged chest pain (>15 minutes) and/or pain that recurs within 1 hour should prompt contact with emergency services 46.
"Atypical" (a word to avoid)The ESC and AHA/ACC advise against calling chest pain "atypical"; describe it as cardiac, possibly cardiac or noncardiac 46,173.
Core components of cardiac rehabPatient assessment, nutritional counselling, risk-factor management, psychosocial support, and physical-activity counselling with exercise training; the 2024 update adds programme quality 41.
HIIT (high-intensity interval training)Short bursts of hard exercise; in cardiac rehab it improved fitness more than moderate continuous training (SMD 0.34) 179.
ReadmissionReturn to hospital. 19.9% of Medicare AMI stays were followed by readmission within 30 days, most not for MI 96.
ReperfusionRestoring blood flow in a blocked artery by PCI or fibrinolysis, within 12 hours of symptom onset in the ESC guideline 46.
Reperfusion injuryDamage at the moment flow returns; no drug or conditioning strategy has yet shown clinical benefit Hypothesis 80,183.
Rescue PCIUrgent PCI when fibrinolysis fails 46.
Retroperitoneal haemorrhageBleeding behind the abdomen after femoral access (OR 19.66), 0.06% of PCIs 253.
Return to workAbout 81% return to work after MI, ACS or angina 220.
Right ventricular infarctionInfarction of the right ventricle in inferior MI; ST elevation in V4R had 88% sensitivity 493.
SCAD (spontaneous coronary artery dissection)A tear inside a coronary artery, mostly in women (88.5%), linked to fibromuscular dysplasia 184,389.
SCAI shock stagesA to E: at risk, beginning, classic, deteriorating, extremis 22.
Sex-specific troponin thresholdsDifferent 99th-percentile limits for women and men (for example 16 vs 34 ng/L for one hs-cTnI assay) 137,256.
Sexual activity after MIA very small, transient extra risk (2–3 MIs per 10,000 person-years per extra hour a week), smaller with regular exercise 82.
Sgarbossa criteriaECG criteria for MI in LBBB: concordant ST elevation, ST depression in V1–V3 and discordant ST elevation ≥5 mm 399. The Smith modification uses an ST/S ratio ≤ −0.25 410.
SGLT2 inhibitorA diabetes and heart-failure drug class; after MI, EMPACT-MI and DAPA-MI did not cut death or HF hospitalisation as primary hard end points 45,208.
Silent MIAn MI without significant symptoms, found later on an ECG; more than 45% of incident MIs in ARIC 494.
Smoking cessationQuitting after a coronary diagnosis is associated with 36% lower mortality 73; quitting medicine started in hospital helps 110.
ST elevationJ-point elevation ≥1 mm in 2 contiguous leads, with higher V2–V3 thresholds 453.
StatinThe first-line LDL-lowering medicine; high-intensity atorvastatin beat pravastatin after ACS (PROVE IT) 49.
STEMIST-elevation MI, usually a fully blocked artery; 28% of US MI hospitalisations (2005–2019) 280.
StentA mesh tube that holds a coronary artery open after balloon dilatation; Sigwart reported coronary stents in 1987 405.
StreptokinaseA fibrinolytic drug; in GISSI it cut 21-day mortality to 10.7% vs 13% 161.
Stunning (stunned myocardium)Pump dysfunction that persists after blood flow returns and recovers over time 38.
Sudden cardiac deathUnexpected death from a rhythm disturbance; after MI, risk is highest in the first 30 days (1.4% per month) 415.
Takotsubo syndromeStress-related, usually apical, ballooning of the left ventricle that mimics MI; about 90% women 158,437.
Thin-cap fibroatheroma (TCFA)A vulnerable plaque with a cap <65 µm over a necrotic core 468.
Thrombus aspirationSucking out clot during primary PCI; no routine benefit (TOTAL, TASTE) 147,215.
TicagrelorA reversible P2Y12 inhibitor that needs no activation; PLATO: fewer events and deaths than clopidogrel 121,473.
TIMI risk scoreSeven one-point items for UA/NSTEMI, including aspirin in the prior 7 days 9.
Time is muscleThe principle that earlier reperfusion saves more muscle; each 30 min of PCI delay carried RR 1.075 for 1-year death 88,157.
TroponinA heart-muscle protein released when muscle is damaged; the blood test that confirms MI 223,453.
Type 1 MIAtherothrombotic MI: a clot on a ruptured or eroded plaque 453.
Type 2 MIMI from a supply/demand mismatch without acute atherothrombosis, about 20–26% of adjudicated MIs 57,382.
Universal Definition of MIThe consensus definitions of 2007, 2012 and 2018 (fourth, UDMI4) 450,451,452.
Ventricular septal ruptureA hole torn in the septum after MI: 0.2% in GUSTO-I, with 73.8% 30-day mortality 72.
VT/VFVentricular tachycardia or fibrillation; about 90% in primary-PCI STEMI occurs within 48 hours 291.
Waist-to-hip ratioA body-shape measure that predicts MI better than BMI (PAR 24.3% vs 7.7%) 490.
Wavefront phenomenonNecrosis spreading from the subendocardium to the subepicardium over hours (dogs) 365.
Wearable defibrillatorA vest that can shock; VEST did not significantly reduce arrhythmic death at 90 days 335.
Wellens (de Zwaan–Wellens) patternPrecordial ST-T changes in unstable angina marking critical proximal LAD stenosis; 75% of untreated patients had an anterior MI within weeks 95.

Heart Attack Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on heart attack (myocardial infarction) and acute coronary syndromes, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people who have had a heart attack, their families and caregivers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine or a care plan without your own care team. ECG criteria, troponin algorithms, the HEART, TIMI and GRACE scores and the risk calculators are for learning, never for self-triage or for deciding whether to seek care. Doses, aspirin wording, CPR steps and guideline statements are quoted as published in labels and guidelines, never as instructions, and survival and mortality figures are population statistics. If you think you or someone else is having a heart attack, call your local emergency number now (911 in the US, 999 in the UK, 112 in the EU).
