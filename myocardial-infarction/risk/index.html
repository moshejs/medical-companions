Part 1 · Understanding heart attack

Risk factors & prevention

In a study of first heart attacks in 52 countries, nine things that can be measured, and for many people changed, accounted for about 9 in 10 of them. Some risks, such as genes and family history, cannot be changed; knowing about them helps people and their clinicians plan. 249,269,489

How to read it. Odds ratios compare people with a factor to people without it (case-control study, so these are odds, not risks). The population attributable risk (PAR) is the share of heart attacks in the study population linked to a factor. PARs overlap, so they cannot be added; the meter combines them as 1 − (1 − PAR₁)(1 − PAR₂)…, a schematic that assumes independence. The published combined figures are marked. Population statistics, not a prediction for anyone. 489

The guideline recommends the Pooled Cohort Equations for adults aged 40–75, followed by a clinician–patient risk discussion before starting medicines, and advises at least 150 minutes a week of moderate-intensity (or 75 minutes of vigorous) physical activity, quoted as published. 13 The AHA PREVENT equations were derived from 3,281,919 people and validated in 3,330,085 more, US adults aged 30–79 without cardiovascular disease. They are sex-specific and race-free, add eGFR, and predict total cardiovascular disease including heart failure; median C-statistics were 0.794 in women and 0.757 in men. 229

Factors in focus

Established

LDL is causal

The European Atherosclerosis Society concluded that the evidence "unequivocally establishes that LDL causes ASCVD", with a dose-dependent, log-linear link that grows with longer exposure. 135 Lifelong, naturally lower LDL cholesterol was associated with 54.5% lower coronary risk per 1 mmol/L, about 3 times the reduction per unit seen with statins started later in life. 134 PCSK9 nonsense variants in Black participants meant 28% lower LDL and 88% lower coronary risk. 67 Familial hypercholesterolaemia affects about 1 in 500 to 1 in 200 people and is diagnosed in under 1% in most countries. 332 See Lowering LDL cholesterol.

Established

Lipoprotein(a)

Lp(a) levels are more than 90% set by genes at the LPA locus. High Lp(a) affects about 1.4 billion people, and the link with cardiovascular disease is causal and continuous, even when LDL is very low. Very high Lp(a) (>180 mg/dL or >430 nmol/L) carries a lifetime risk like untreated familial hypercholesterolaemia. The EAS panel recommends measuring Lp(a) at least once in adults; as of 2022 no specific Lp(a)-lowering therapy had been shown to reduce events. 249 Trials are in Research.

Established

Family history

In the Framingham Offspring Study, at least one parent with premature cardiovascular disease (father before 55, mother before 65) gave an adjusted OR of 2.0 (95% CI 1.2–3.1) in men and 1.7 (0.9–3.1) in women for an event within 8 years. 269 It is a risk-enhancing factor in the 2019 ACC/AHA guideline. 13

Established

Women-specific factors

Pre-eclampsia was associated with RR 2.16 for later ischaemic heart disease and 3.70 for hypertension. 23 A later meta-analysis of more than 6.4 million women reported RR 2.50 for coronary heart disease. 482 The AHA lists hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, preterm delivery, gestational diabetes, small-for-gestational-age delivery, placental abruption and pregnancy loss. 338 Premature surgical menopause carried HR 1.87. 198 Women had their first MI at a median 65 years vs 56 in men; hypertension and diabetes were more strongly linked to MI in women. 4

Established

South Asian ancestry

South Asian people in the US have higher proportional mortality from ASCVD than other Asian groups and non-Hispanic White people. The biology is "no different" from other groups: most of the excess is explained by known risk factors, especially insulin resistance. 470 The 2019 ACC/AHA guideline lists South Asian ancestry as a risk-enhancing factor. 13

Established

Tobacco, in every form

Risk rose 5.6% for each additional cigarette smoked. Chewing tobacco alone had OR 2.23, smoking plus chewing 4.09, and beedies 2.89, similar to cigarettes. After quitting, the OR fell to 1.87 within 3 years, with a small excess (1.22) after 20 years or more. The PAR of current smoking was 58.3% in young men and 6.2% in older women. 438 Quitting lowers heart attack risk within a few years, at any age. 438

Established

Stress and triggers

In INTERHEART, permanent stress at work had OR 2.14, permanent stress at home 2.12, severe financial stress 1.33 and depression 1.55. 379 In the hour before symptoms, heavy physical exertion (OR 2.31) and anger or emotional upset (OR 2.44) were more common, and both together OR 3.05. 414 Stress is not a personal failing.

Established

Inflammatory diseases and kidneys

Rheumatoid arthritis (pooled RR 1.68), HIV (adjusted RR 1.75; 2.98 in women) and severe psoriasis in younger people (RR 3.10 at age 30) raise MI risk; women aged 35–44 with lupus had a rate ratio of 52.43 against Framingham women of similar age. 17,154,278,454 Lower kidney function and raised urine albumin increase cardiovascular risk two- to four-fold after adjustment. 151

Trend

A changing landscape

Atherosclerosis now affects younger people, more women and more ethnic groups than before. Research attention has widened to triglyceride-rich lipoproteins and to non-traditional drivers such as disturbed sleep, inactivity, air pollution and environmental stress. 263 How plaque forms is in Plaque, rupture & clot.
