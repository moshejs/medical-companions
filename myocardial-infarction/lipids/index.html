Part 4 · Medicines & revascularisation

Lowering LDL cholesterol

LDL cholesterol (“bad” cholesterol) helps build the plaque that tears in a heart attack (myocardial infarction, MI). In large trials, the further LDL fell, the fewer heart attacks and strokes followed. Watch the trials land on that line, and one person's LDL step down as medicines are added.

Step 1 · Cause

LDL causes atherosclerosis

A European Atherosclerosis Society panel judged that LDL particles cause atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, from genetic studies, more than 200 cohorts, Mendelian randomisation and trials in more than 2 million people, showing a “dose-dependent log-linear” link. Any way of lowering LDL should cut events in proportion to how far and how long it is lowered. Established 135

In plain words“Bad” LDL cholesterol helps build the fatty plaque inside heart arteries. Lowering it, by any means, lowers the risk.
Exam hookLDL causality rests on genetics, cohorts, Mendelian randomisation and trials: risk is log-linear in LDL exposure. 135

The goal is “<55 mg/dL and ≥50% reduction” (<1.4 mmol/L), so from 3.6 mmol/L it is the lower of 1.4 mmol/L and half the baseline. 165,272,273,386

Reduction needed61%

On the average percentages cited, that is reached by statin, ezetimibe and a PCSK9 antibody (on average). The real response is measured: labels and ESC/EAS say to recheck LDL from 4 weeks (4–6 weeks for ESC/EAS). 127,133,386

US statements

2018 AHA/ACC: in very high-risk ASCVD, “use a LDL-C threshold of 70 mg/dL (1.8 mmol/L) to consider addition of nonstatins to statin therapy.” 170 2026 ACC/AHA: very high risk “should achieve LDL-C below 55 mg/dL” (per review). 33,386 Baseline 3.6 mmol/L is at or above 70 mg/dL.

The stepwise options, as published

  1. Statin first. 2018 AHA/ACC: “In patients with clinical ASCVD, reduce low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) with high-intensity statin therapy or maximally tolerated statin therapy.” High intensity means a daily dose that lowers LDL by about 50% or more: atorvastatin 40–80 mg or rosuvastatin 20 (40) mg. 170
  2. Check and add. ESC/EAS recommends “reassessment of LDL-C 4–6 weeks after treatment initiation or intensification, with further escalation when the goal has not been achieved” (as summarised in a 2026 review). 386 2018 AHA/ACC: “it is reasonable to add ezetimibe to maximally tolerated statin therapy when the LDL-C level remains ≥70 mg/dL”. 170
  3. Then a PCSK9 inhibitor. ESC 2023 ACS guideline: “if LDL-C targets are not reached within 4–6 weeks despite maximally tolerated statin therapy and ezetimibe, the addition of a PCSK9i is recommended” (as quoted in a 2026 review). 46,165
  4. Or combine early. The 2025 ESC/EAS focused update supports “intensive combination therapy during the index hospitalization when statin monotherapy is predictably insufficient” (per review). 273,386 Early evidence No outcome trial was found that tests upfront combination after ACS against the stepwise approach.
  5. US thresholds after ACS. The 2025 ACC/AHA ACS guideline recommends nonstatin therapy (ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitor, inclisiran or bempedoic acid) on maximally tolerated statin when LDL is ≥70 mg/dL, and says it “may be reasonable if LDL-C ≥55 mg/dL” (as summarised in two 2026 papers). 165,356,479 The 2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline restores goals: “In secondary prevention, patients at very high risk should achieve LDL-C below 55 mg/dL and non-HDL-C below 85 mg/dL”, and below 70 mg/dL for other ASCVD patients (per review). 33,386

Closer looks

Lp(a)

Lipoprotein(a): inherited, and hard to treat

Lp(a) levels are more than 90% set by genes, and high Lp(a) affects about 1.4 billion people; it is linked to heart disease even when LDL is very low. The EAS panel recommends measuring it at least once in adults. 249 The 2019 ACC/AHA guideline counts Lp(a) ≥50 mg/dL (≥125 nmol/L) as a risk-enhancing factor. 13

Lp(a)HORIZON's topline result (September 2026): pelacarsen lowered Lp(a) but did not reduce the primary cardiovascular end point; full data are awaited. 385 Early evidence OCEAN(a)-Outcomes (olpasiran) is due around 2028. 324

Bempedoic acid

For people who cannot take a statin

In CLEAR Outcomes, bempedoic acid lowered LDL by 21.1 percentage points more than placebo and cut major events (11.7% vs 13.3%) and MI (3.7% vs 4.8%), with no significant effect on death. Gout (3.1% vs 2.1%) and gallstones (2.2% vs 1.2%) were more common. 331 The Nexletol label is for adults “who are unable to take recommended statin therapy (including those not taking a statin)”. 129

Inclisiran

A twice-yearly injection

Inclisiran lowered LDL by about half (52.3% and 49.9% at day 510 in ORION-10 and -11). 360 The label gives 284 mg “as a single subcutaneous injection initially, again at 3 months, and then every 6 months”, “administered by a healthcare professional”, and its indication is LDL lowering, without a cardiovascular-event claim. 126 A 2026 review says it awaits outcome trials. 386 Early evidence

Muscle aches

Most aches on statins are not from the statin

In 19 blinded trials (123,940 people), muscle pain or weakness was reported by 27.1% on a statin and 26.6% on placebo. Statins added 11 reports per 1000 person-years in year 1 only, so more than 90% of muscle reports on statins were not caused by the statin. 78 If aches happen, talk with the care team before stopping.
