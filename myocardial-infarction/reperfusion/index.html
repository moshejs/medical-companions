Reperfusion: PCI & clot-busting drugs

A blocked coronary artery can be reopened with a balloon and stent (primary percutaneous coronary intervention, PCI) or with a clot-dissolving drug (fibrinolysis). Race the two strategies from a hospital without a cath lab, and see why the clock decides between them.

How PCI is done, and what the trials changed

Wrist, not groin

Radial access (MATRIX)

In 8404 patients with ACS, going in through the wrist (radial) rather than the groin (femoral) reduced net adverse clinical events (9.8% vs 11.7%), major bleeding (1.6% vs 2.3%) and all-cause death (1.6% vs 2.2%). 462 RIVAL found no difference in its primary outcome but fewer large haematomas with radial access. 214 ESC 2023 lists "Radial artery approach as default vascular access" among ways to reduce PCI bleeding. 46

More than one narrowing

Complete revascularisation (COMPLETE)

In 4041 STEMI patients with disease in several arteries, also treating the non-culprit narrowings after a successful culprit PCI reduced cardiovascular death or MI at a median of 3 years (7.8% vs 10.5%; HR 0.74). The benefit was similar whether the extra PCI was done during or after the hospital stay. 296 MULTISTARS AMI favoured doing it immediately rather than staged. 417 Choices between stents and bypass are in Stents or bypass?

Except in shock

Culprit artery only first (CULPRIT-SHOCK)

In 706 patients with MI, cardiogenic shock and multivessel disease, treating only the culprit lesion lowered 30-day death or kidney-replacement therapy (45.9% vs 55.4%) compared with immediate multivessel PCI. 442 ESC 2023: "Early revascularization with either PCI or CABG is recommended for patients with AMI complicated by CS". 46 More in Complications & cardiogenic shock.

NSTEMI timing

Early, immediate, or later?

ESC 2023 defines an early invasive strategy as "routine invasive angiography (and PCI if needed) within 24 h of presentation" and says it "should be considered when any high-risk features are present", including "A GRACE risk score >140". 46 TIMACS and VERDICT found no overall benefit from earlier angiography, but benefit in the highest-risk patients (GRACE >140 in VERDICT, HR 0.81). 241,292 A 17-trial meta-analysis found no mortality difference but less recurrent ischaemia. 236 See Risk scores.

A paradox

Faster door-to-balloon, flat mortality?

US median door-to-balloon time fell from 96 minutes (2005) to 64 minutes (2010), and the share treated within 90 minutes rose from 44.2% to 91.4%. 251 Yet in-hospital mortality in a registry of 96,738 primary PCIs did not change (4.8% vs 4.7%). 301 At the individual level, each 10 minutes saved still went with lower odds of death (adjusted OR 0.92); the treated population had grown by 55% and changed. 316 The current view: total ischaemic time, including delay before hospital, matters more than the in-hospital segment alone. 301,316

Did not help

Routine clot suction (TOTAL, TASTE)

Routinely sucking out the clot before stenting did not reduce cardiovascular death, MI, shock or severe heart failure at 180 days in 10,732 patients (6.9% vs 7.0%), and stroke within 30 days was higher (0.7% vs 0.3%). 215 TASTE found 30-day death of 2.8% vs 3.0%. 147

Late arrivals

After 12 hours

In BRAVE-2 (365 patients 12–48 hours after onset, without ongoing symptoms), an invasive strategy reduced final infarct size (median 8.0% vs 13.0% of the left ventricle). 392 In OAT, routine PCI of a still-blocked artery 3–28 days after MI in stable patients did not reduce death, reinfarction or severe heart failure at 4 years (17.2% vs 15.6%). 192

Pharmaco-invasive

Drug first, then the cath lab (STREAM)

In 1892 patients seen within 3 hours who could not get primary PCI within 1 hour, prehospital tenecteplase followed by angiography gave a 30-day composite of 12.4% vs 14.3% with primary PCI. 36.3% needed emergency angiography because the drug failed. Intracranial bleeding was 1.0% vs 0.2%, and 0.5% vs 0.3% after the dose was halved for people aged 75 and over. 12

Not a heart attack

Stable disease is different (ISCHEMIA)

In 5179 people with stable coronary disease, an initial invasive strategy did not reduce ischaemic events or death over a median 3.2 years (5-year event rates 16.4% vs 18.2%). This applies to stable patients, not to STEMI or high-risk NSTE-ACS, where timely reperfusion helps. 146,225,279
