# H · Cardiac rehabilitation, living after a heart attack & nursing care

Pass date: 2026-11-04. Modules served: rehab, living, nursing.

This pass covers cardiac rehabilitation (CR) after a heart attack (myocardial infarction, MI): what it does, who gets it, home versus centre programmes and exercise safety. It also covers life afterwards (work, driving, air travel, sexual activity, mood, memory and family) and the nursing care around an acute coronary syndrome (ACS). That includes the time to the first ECG, oxygen, pain relief, care of the radial and femoral access sites, bleeding after PCI and fibrinolysis, medicines adherence, discharge and nurse-led prevention clinics. Every number comes from a PubMed abstract or a US drug label (openFDA) that I read in this pass. Survival, mortality and event figures are population statistics from trials and registries. They do not predict what will happen to any one person.

## Findings

### rehab · What cardiac rehabilitation does (Cochrane and IPD evidence)
- Cochrane 2021 (85 RCTs, 23,430 people with coronary heart disease (CHD); exercise-based CR vs no exercise; follow-up 6–12 months): all-cause mortality RR 0.87 (95% CI 0.73–1.04; moderate certainty); MI RR 0.72 (0.55–0.93; NNTB 75); all-cause hospitalisation RR 0.58 (0.43–0.77; NNTB 12) [[Dibben2021]].
- Cochrane 2021, 6–12 months: little to no difference in cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.88, 0.68–1.14), CABG (RR 0.99) or PCI (RR 0.86). Cardiovascular hospitalisation was uncertain (RR 0.80, 0.41–1.59; low certainty) [[Dibben2021]].
- Cochrane 2021, medium term (>12–36 months): cardiovascular mortality RR 0.77 (0.63–0.93; 5 trials). Long term (>3 years): cardiovascular mortality RR 0.58 (0.43–0.78; 8 trials) and MI RR 0.67 (0.50–0.90; 10 trials) [[Dibben2021]].
- Cochrane 2021: women made up <15% of trial participants overall, and the median share rose from 11% to 17% in the last decade. 21 trials were in low- and middle-income countries [[Dibben2021]].
- Cochrane 2026 update (107 RCTs, 26,886 adults; search March 2026), 6–12 months: all-cause mortality RR 0.86 (0.74–1.00; NNTB 125; moderate certainty); cardiovascular mortality RR 0.87 (0.68–1.11); MI RR 0.72 (0.54–0.95; NNTB 71; high certainty); all-cause hospital admission RR 0.65 (0.51–0.82; NNTB 17) [[Dibben2026]].
- Cochrane 2026: women were included in 78% of trials but were only 17% of participants. Eight economic evaluations consistently found exercise-based CR cost-effective per QALY gained [[Dibben2026]].
- Cochrane 2026: about a quarter of the trials ran before contemporary optimal medical therapy was widespread. This is one reason the mortality effect is smaller in modern trials (Established) [[Dibben2026]].
- The CaReMATCH individual-participant meta-analysis covered trials published since 2010 (8 trials, 4,975 participants, 93.5% post-MI). Exercise-based CR lowered all-cause hospitalisation (HR 0.68, 0.53–0.87) and CVD hospitalisation (HR 0.62, 0.47–0.83). It showed no difference in all-cause mortality (HR 0.99, 0.74–1.32) or CVD mortality (HR 0.80, 0.32–2.04) [[Stens2026]].
- In CaReMATCH, hospitalisation fell more in people with lower LVEF, lower baseline exercise capacity, beta-blocker use or previous CVD. Quality-of-life gains were larger with lower baseline quality of life and lower education [[Stens2026]].
- Medicare dose–response (30,161 older adults attending ≥1 session): 36 sessions vs 24 sessions gave a 14% lower 4-year risk of death (HR 0.86, 0.77–0.97). 36 vs 12 sessions: HR 0.78 for death. 36 vs 1 session: HR 0.53 for death and 0.69 for MI. These are observational associations [[Hammill2010]].
- Secondary prevention programmes of any type (63 RCTs, 21,295 patients) reduced all-cause mortality (RR 0.85, 0.77–0.94) and recurrent MI (RR 0.83, 0.74–0.94). The mortality effect appeared by 24 months (RR 0.53) but not at 12 months (RR 0.97) [[Clark2005]].
- Programmes based only on education or counselling, without exercise, still reduced mortality (RR 0.87, 0.76–0.99) [[Clark2005]].
- Cochrane patient education (22 RCTs, 76,864 people): education within CR did not clearly reduce total mortality (3.6% vs 4.6%; RR 0.80, 0.60–1.05; moderate quality) [[Anderson2017Edu]].

### rehab · Components and exercise prescription
- The AHA/AACVPR 2007 core components are baseline patient assessment, nutritional counselling, risk-factor management (lipids, blood pressure, weight, diabetes, smoking), psychosocial interventions, and physical-activity counselling with exercise training [[Balady2007]].
- The 2024 AHA/AACVPR update keeps these components and lists separately: weight management and body composition, aerobic exercise training, and strength training. It adds a new core component, programme quality. It also covers virtual and remote delivery [[Brown2024]].
- The 2024 statement says high-quality programme performance is essential to improve "widely documented low enrollment and adherence rates" and to reduce disparities [[Brown2024]].
- Exercise safety (French registry, 25,420 patients, 743,471 patient-hours of supervised training): 1 severe cardiac event per 49,565 patient-hours of training and 1 per 8,484 exercise tests. Cardiac arrest rate was 1.3 per million patient-hours. No deaths [[Pavy2006]].
- High-intensity interval training vs moderate-intensity continuous training in CR (17 RCTs, 953 participants): HIIT improved cardiorespiratory fitness more (SMD 0.34, 0.20–0.48). No deaths or cardiac hospitalisations occurred during training in either arm (Early evidence) [[Hannan2018]].
- In the HIIT meta-analysis, programmes of 7–12 weeks gave the largest fitness gains in coronary artery disease [[Hannan2018]].

### rehab · Home-based vs centre-based CR
- Cochrane 2017 (23 RCTs, 2,890 participants after MI, revascularisation or heart failure): no difference in mortality up to 12 months (RR 1.19, 0.65–2.16; very low quality) or exercise capacity (SMD −0.13, −0.28 to 0.02). Completion was marginally higher at home (RR 1.04, 1.00–1.08) [[Anderson2017HB]].
- Cochrane 2023 update (24 RCTs, 3,046 participants; home-based included digital or telehealth): mortality RR 1.19 (0.65–2.16); exercise capacity SMD −0.10 (−0.24 to 0.04); completion RR 1.03 (0.99–1.08). Cost per patient was similar [[McDonagh2023]].
- In Cochrane 2023, 71 of 77 quality-of-life comparisons up to 24 months showed no significant difference between home and centre [[McDonagh2023]].
- The 2019 AACVPR/AHA/ACC home-based CR statement concluded that home-based CR "may be a reasonable option for selected clinically stable low- to moderate-risk patients who are eligible for CR but cannot attend a traditional center-based CR program" [[Thomas2019HBCR]].
- That statement rated the evidence that home-based and centre-based CR give similar 3- to 12-month outcomes as low to moderate strength, with gaps for older adults, women and underrepresented groups [[Thomas2019HBCR]].

### rehab · Referral and participation gaps
- US Medicare 2016 (366,103 eligible): 24.4% took part in CR (≥1 session). Of those, 24.3% started within 21 days and 26.9% completed ≥36 sessions [[Ritchey2020]].
- Medicare participation by group: women 18.9% vs men 28.6% (adjusted PR 0.91); Hispanic 13.2% and non-Hispanic Black 13.6% vs non-Hispanic White 25.8%. Participation fell with increasing age [[Ritchey2020]].
- Medicare participation by qualifying event: 7.1% after an acute MI without a procedure vs 55.3% after CABG alone. By region: 18.6% to 39.1% [[Ritchey2020]].
- The Million Hearts Cardiac Rehabilitation Collaborative goal is ≥70% participation by 2022 [[Ritchey2020]].
- Referral after PCI (US NCDR, 1,432,399 patients, 2009–2012): 59.2% referred overall and 66.0% in the AMI/Medicare subgroup. More than a quarter of hospitals referred <20% of patients [[Aragam2015]].
- In that registry, STEMI (OR 2.99) and NSTEMI (OR 1.99) raised the odds of referral compared with elective PCI [[Aragam2015]].
- Sex gap in referral (19 studies, 241,613 people): women 39.6% vs men 49.4% (OR 0.68, 0.62–0.74) [[Colella2015]].
- Sex gap in enrolment (26 studies, 297,719 people): women 38.5% vs men 45.0% (OR 0.64, 0.57–0.72). Systematic inpatient referral increased enrolment among women [[Samayoa2014]].
- In PREMIER, patients who stopped their P2Y12 inhibitor early were less likely to have been referred to CR or given medication discharge instructions [[Spertus2006]].

### living · Lifestyle after a coronary event
- EUROASPIRE V (8,261 coronary patients in 27 countries, interviewed ≥6 months after the event): 19% smoked, and 55% of those were persistent smokers. 38% were obese, 66% were physically active for less than 30 minutes 5 times a week, 42% had BP ≥140/90 mmHg and 71% had LDL-C ≥1.8 mmol/L (≥70 mg/dL) [[Kotseva2019]].
- Stopping smoking after a CHD diagnosis (20 cohorts): crude RR of death 0.64 (0.58–0.71) vs continuing to smoke, a 36% reduction. In 6 higher-quality studies the RR was 0.71 [[Critchley2003]].
- EUROACTION (nurse-coordinated, family-based programme; cluster RCT, 8 countries), coronary patients at 1 year: fruit and vegetable intake target met by 72% vs 35%; BP <140/90 mmHg in 65% vs 55%; smoking abstinence 58% vs 47% (p=0.06) [[Wood2008EUROACTION]].

### living · Return to work
- Meta-analysis (43 prospective studies, 34,964 patients after MI, ACS or angina): pooled return to work 81.1% (95% CI 75.8–85.8) [[Kai2022]].
- Return to work was lower with lower education (76.1% vs 85.6%), reduced LVEF (65.3% vs 77.8%), blue-collar work (65.0% vs 81.2% white-collar) and high physical workload (64.1% vs 78.3%) [[Kai2022]].
- Denmark, working-age people after a first MI (1997–2012): 91.1% had returned to work within 1 year. But 24.2% of those who returned were detached from employment 1 year after returning [[Smedegaard2017]].
- Predictors of later detachment from work included depression (OR 1.77), heart failure (OR 1.20) and diabetes (OR 1.13) [[Smedegaard2017]].
- VIRGO (18–55 years, full-time workers): at 12 months, 85% of young women and 89% of young men had returned to work (p=0.02). The gap disappeared after adjusting for sociodemographic, psychosocial and health factors [[Dreyer2016RTW]].

### living · Driving
- UK DVLA, Group 1 (car, motorcycle) after ACS: "You must not drive but you need not notify DVLA." Driving may resume 1 week after successful PCI if no other urgent revascularisation is planned, LVEF is at least 40% before discharge and there is no other disqualifying condition. Otherwise driving may resume 4 weeks after the acute event (unverified) [[DVLA2025]].
- UK DVLA, Group 2 (bus, lorry): the driver must not drive and must notify DVLA for all ACS. The licence will be refused or revoked. Relicensing is possible after at least 6 weeks if LVEF ≥40%, functional test requirements are met and there is no other disqualifying condition (unverified) [[DVLA2025]].
- UK audits found that written driving advice was often missing from discharge summaries, and targeted teaching improved documentation. The DVLA guidance places the duty to advise patients on clinicians [[Bharaj2021,Dyson2025]].

### living · Air travel
- RCT of 38 patients flying commercially 2 weeks after MI (oxygen 2 L/min vs none): 1 major end point, a brief, self-limiting, asymptomatic ischaemic episode on Holter. Minor end points occurred in 34%, with no difference between oxygen and no oxygen (5/13 vs 6/15) [[Roby2002]].
- The authors concluded that neither supplemental oxygen nor a medical escort was needed at 2 weeks after MI, provided patients did not overexert themselves before and after the flight. This is a small trial (Early evidence) [[Roby2002]].

### living · Sexual activity
- Case-crossover meta-analysis: sexual activity was associated with a transiently higher relative risk of MI (RR 2.70, 1.48–4.91). The absolute risk increase for 1 extra hour per week of sexual or physical activity was 2–3 MIs per 10,000 person-years [[Dahabreh2011]].
- The triggering effect was weaker in people with higher habitual physical activity. For each additional weekly session of habitual exercise, the RR of an activity-triggered MI fell by about 45% [[Dahabreh2011]].
- The AHA/ESC CCNAP sexual-counselling consensus reports that erectile dysfunction in men with cardiovascular disease is about twice as common as in the general population, with similar rates of sexual dysfunction in women. It recommends routine assessment and counselling for patients and their partners [[Steinke2013]].
- The nitroglycerin label: "Do not use NITROSTAT in patients who are taking PDE-5 Inhibitors, such as avanafil, sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil hydrochloride. Concomitant use can cause severe hypotension, syncope, or myocardial ischemia." [[FDA-Nitrostat]].

### living · Depression
- Major depression after MI on structured interview: 19.8% (95% CI 19.1–20.6; 8 studies, n=10,785). Depressive symptoms were found in 31.1% with BDI ≥10, 15.5% with HADS ≥8 and 7.3% with HADS ≥11 [[Thombs2006]].
- Formal DSM-diagnosed depression after acute MI (2025 meta-analysis): 23.58% overall; 19.46% when assessed within 3 months and 14.87% after 3 months [[Chong2025]].
- Post-MI depression (29 studies, 16,889 patients, about 16 months' follow-up): all-cause mortality OR 2.25 (1.73–2.93), cardiac mortality OR 2.71 (1.68–4.36), cardiac events OR 1.59 (1.37–1.85) [[Meijer2011]].
- Individual-patient meta-analysis (16 studies, 10,175 post-MI patients): HR 1.32 (1.26–1.38) for all-cause mortality and 1.19 (1.14–1.24) for cardiovascular events. Adjusting for disease severity reduced these by 28% and 25%. Per SD of depression score, adjusted risks were 22% higher for death and 13% higher for cardiovascular events [[Meijer2013]].
- In 2014 the AHA recommended that depression be elevated to the status of a risk factor for adverse medical outcomes after ACS, based on 53 studies and 4 meta-analyses [[Lichtman2014]].
- The 2008 AHA science advisory (endorsed by the American Psychiatric Association) recommends screening tests for depressive symptoms in CHD to identify patients who need further assessment and treatment [[Lichtman2008]].
- Counterpoint: a 2008 systematic review found no trial showing that depression screening improves depression or cardiac outcomes. Screening tools had median sensitivity 84% and specificity 79%, and treatment gave modest symptom improvement (effect size 0.20–0.38) [[Thombs2008]].
- SADHART (369 patients with major depression after MI or unstable angina; sertraline 50–200 mg/day vs placebo for 24 weeks): no effect on LVEF or arrhythmia measures. Severe cardiovascular adverse events occurred in 14.5% vs 22.4% (not significant). CGI-I responders were 67% vs 53% (p=0.01) [[Glassman2002]].
- ENRICHD (2,481 post-MI patients with depression or low perceived social support; CBT plus SSRI when indicated): depression and social support improved, but event-free survival did not differ at about 29 months (75.8% vs 75.9%) [[Berkman2003]].
- Cochrane psychological interventions (35 RCTs, 10,703 people with CHD): no reduction in total mortality (RR 0.90, 0.77–1.05). Cardiac mortality fell (RR 0.79, 0.63–0.98; low quality). Symptoms improved: depression SMD −0.27, anxiety SMD −0.24, stress SMD −0.56 [[Richards2017]].

### living · Anxiety, PTSD, cognition and family
- Anxiety after MI (12 studies, 5,750 patients, mean 2.6 years' follow-up): adverse outcomes OR 1.36 (1.18–1.56); all-cause mortality OR 1.47; cardiac mortality OR 1.23; new cardiac events OR 1.71 [[Roest2010]].
- DSM-diagnosed anxiety after acute MI: pooled prevalence 11.96% (7 studies). PTSD: 10.26% (3 studies) [[Chong2025]].
- PTSD caused by ACS (24 studies, n=2,383): 12% (95% CI 9–16%) overall; 16% on screening questionnaires vs 4% on clinical interview [[Edmondson2012]].
- Clinically significant ACS-induced PTSD symptoms were associated with a doubled risk of recurrent cardiac events or death (RR 2.00, 1.69–2.37; 3 studies, n=609) [[Edmondson2012]].
- English Longitudinal Study of Ageing (7,888 adults ≥50 years, median 12 years): incident CHD (MI or angina) was followed by faster decline in global cognition, verbal memory and orientation. There was no faster decline before the event and no short-term drop just after it (Early evidence) [[Xie2019]].
- HAT trial couples (460 medically stable post-MI patients and their spouses): at entry, 25% of patients and 15% of spouses were depressed, and 21% of patients and 19% of spouses were anxious. Prevalence did not change over 2 years [[Thomas2011HAT]].
- VIRGO: 21.2% of young MI patients had low perceived social support. This was associated with worse mental functioning, quality of life and depressive symptoms at 12 months, in both sexes [[Bucholz2014]].

### living · Women's and young adults' recovery (VIRGO)
- VIRGO enrolled 3,572 adults with AMI aged 18–55 years (67.1% women) at 103 US, 24 Spanish and 3 Australian hospitals between 2008 and 2012 [[Smolderen2015]].
- Lifetime history of depression: 48% of young women vs 24% of young men. Depressive symptoms at admission (PHQ ≥10): 39% vs 22% (adjusted OR 1.64) [[Smolderen2015]].
- Young women had lower health-status scores than men at baseline, 1 month and 12 months. Adjusted differences included SF-12 mental −2.36 points and Seattle Angina Questionnaire quality of life −3.51 points [[Dreyer2015]].
- In the year after discharge, 30.4% of 2,979 young patients were hospitalised at least once. Women had more coronary-related (SHR 1.33) and non-cardiac (SHR 1.51) hospitalisations than men [[Sawano2023]].

### nursing · First assessment: ECG within 10 minutes
- STEMI process measures target a door-to-ECG (D2E) time of 10 minutes. In 676 ED STEMI patients at 10 US sites, median D2E was 7 minutes, but 37.9% waited more than 10 minutes [[Yiadom2022]].
- Patients with D2E >10 minutes were more often female (32.8% vs 22.6%), Black, non-English speaking or diabetic, and less often reported chest pain (63.3% vs 87.4%) [[Yiadom2022]].
- Moving ECG acquisition from triage to intake would have met the ≤10-minute goal for 87.4% of STEMI patients [[Yiadom2022]].
- In an earlier ED registry, only 34% of NSTE-ACS and 40.9% of STEMI patients had an ECG within 10 minutes. A delayed ECG was linked to worse 30-day outcomes in STEMI (OR 3.95, 1.06–14.72) [[Diercks2006]].

### nursing · Oxygen only if hypoxaemic
- DETO2X-AMI (6,629 patients with suspected MI and SpO2 ≥90%; oxygen 6 L/min for 6–12 h vs room air): 1-year mortality 5.0% vs 5.1% (HR 0.97, 0.79–1.21). Rehospitalisation with MI 3.8% vs 3.3% [[Hofmann2017]].
- In DETO2X-AMI, hypoxaemia developed in 1.9% of the oxygen group vs 7.7% of the air group, so patients need continued SpO2 monitoring [[Hofmann2017]].
- AVOID (441 confirmed STEMI without hypoxia; oxygen 8 L/min vs none): recurrent MI 5.5% vs 0.9% (p=0.006); arrhythmia 40.4% vs 31.4%; mean peak CK 1,948 vs 1,543 U/L. Infarct size on 6-month CMR was 20.3 g vs 13.1 g (p=0.04) [[Stub2015]].
- Cochrane 2016 (5 RCTs, 1,173 participants) found no evidence supporting routine oxygen in AMI (mortality RR 0.99, 0.50–1.95) and could not rule out harm [[Cabello2016]].

### nursing · Pain relief, morphine and P2Y12 absorption
- IMPRESSION (70 MI patients; IV morphine 5 mg vs placebo before ticagrelor 180 mg): morphine lowered ticagrelor exposure by 36% (AUC0–12 6,307 vs 9,791 ng·h/mL) and delayed peak concentration (4 h vs 2 h) [[Kubica2016]].
- Meta-analysis of morphine in ACS (5 RCTs and 12 observational studies, 69,993 participants): higher in-hospital mortality (RR 1.45, 1.10–1.91; low confidence) and MACE (RR 1.21). There was high confidence that morphine reduces the antiplatelet effect of P2Y12 inhibitors [[Duarte2019]].
- CRUSADE (57,039 NSTE-ACS patients; observational): 29.8% received morphine within 24 h. Its use was associated with higher adjusted in-hospital death (OR 1.48, 1.33–1.64; propensity-matched OR 1.41). This is confounded observational data (Hypothesis for causation) [[Meine2005H]].

### nursing · Access-site care after PCI (radial and femoral)
- MATRIX (8,404 ACS patients): radial vs femoral access reduced net adverse clinical events (9.8% vs 11.7%; RR 0.83), BARC major bleeding (1.6% vs 2.3%) and all-cause mortality (1.6% vs 2.2%; RR 0.72) at 30 days [[Valgimigli2015]].
- RIVAL (7,021 ACS patients): the primary outcome was similar (3.7% vs 4.0%). Radial access cut large haematoma (42 vs 106 patients; HR 0.40) and pseudoaneurysm needing closure (7 vs 23; HR 0.30) [[Jolly2011]].
- Radial artery occlusion (66 studies, 31,345 participants): 7.7% within 24 h and 5.5% at >1 week. Higher-dose heparin, shorter compression and patent haemostasis reduce it [[Rashid2016]].
- PROPHET (436 transradial patients): compression with radial patency confirmed by Barbeau's test cut radial artery occlusion by 59% at 24 h and 75% at 30 days compared with conventional pressure [[Pancholy2008]].
- Faster TR band deflation (start at 1 h, 2 mL every 10 minutes, after diagnostic radial catheterisation; 173 patients) did not increase bleeding (8 vs 11 pulsatile bleeds). The band came off 129 minutes sooner, and more patients had a pain score of 0 at 1 h (89% vs 74%) [[Deuling2017]].
- Bed rest after transfemoral catheterisation (network meta-analysis, 28 studies, 9,217 participants): bed rest duration was not consistently linked to haematoma or bleeding. Back pain rose 8% per extra hour of bed rest. The authors state that ambulation as early as 2 h can be safely implemented [[Busca2023]].
- Retroperitoneal haemorrhage after PCI (UK, 511,106 procedures): 0.06% overall, falling from 0.09% to 0.03% between 2007 and 2014. Femoral access was the strongest predictor (OR 19.66). RH was linked to higher 30-day mortality (OR 3.59) [[Kwok2018]].
- After elective catheterisation or PCI, 68.6% of patients reported access-site bruising 5–7 days after discharge, 47% of them larger than 7.5 cm. Bruising was reported by 73% after femoral and 60% after radial access, and female sex predicted it [[Cosman2011]].
- After transradial PCI with intensive antiplatelet therapy (EASY trial, 1,348 patients), mean haemoglobin fell 0.6 g/dL within 24 h, and 30% developed anaemia. A fall >3 g/dL was linked to more 30-day MACE [[Bertrand2010]].

### nursing · After fibrinolysis
- FTT overview (9 trials, 58,600 patients): fibrinolysis caused about 4 extra strokes per 1,000 during days 0–1. Of these, 2 were linked to early death and 1 was moderately or severely disabling. Overall rates in the trials were 1.0% strokes and 0.7% major non-cerebral bleeds [[FTT1994]].
- In FTT, fibrinolysis showed an early excess of deaths on days 0–1, outweighed by a larger benefit on days 2–35. Absolute benefit was about 30 per 1,000 treated within 0–6 h of symptom onset and about 20 per 1,000 at 7–12 h [[FTT1994]].

### nursing · Medicines teaching: DAPT and GTN
- PARIS registry (5,018 PCI patients, 2 years): DAPT disruption (non-compliance or bleeding) raised MACE risk (HR 1.50, 1.14–1.97), with HR 7.04 in the first 7 days after disruption. Physician-guided discontinuation did not raise risk (HR 0.63) [[Mehran2013]].
- PREMIER: 13.6% of DES-treated MI patients had stopped their thienopyridine by 30 days. Their 11-month mortality was 7.5% vs 0.7% (adjusted HR 9.0, 1.3–60.6) [[Spertus2006]].
- Ontario, ≥65 years after AMI: 79% of prescriptions were filled within 120 days. Filling none of the discharge medicines vs all was linked to higher 1-year mortality (OR 1.80, 1.35–2.42). Discharge medication counselling was a factor associated with filling them [[Jackevicius2008]].
- Ticagrelor label, boxed warning: "If possible, manage bleeding without discontinuing BRILINTA. Stopping BRILINTA increases the risk of subsequent cardiovascular events." [[FDA-Brilinta]].
- Ticagrelor label: dyspnoea occurred in about 14% (PLATO, PEGASUS) to 21% (THEMIS) of patients, "usually mild to moderate in intensity" and often resolving with continued treatment [[FDA-Brilinta]].
- Sublingual nitroglycerin label: "Administer one tablet under the tongue or in the buccal pouch at the first sign of an acute anginal attack … One additional tablet may be administered every 5 minutes until relief is obtained. No more than three tablets are recommended within a 15-minute period. If the pain persists after a total of 3 tablets in a 15-minute period, or if the pain is different than is typically experienced, seek prompt medical attention." [[FDA-Nitrostat]].
- Nitroglycerin label: "Administer NITROSTAT at rest, preferably in the sitting position", because severe hypotension can occur, "particularly with upright posture" [[FDA-Nitrostat]].
- Nitroglycerin label storage: "keep in the original glass container and must be tightly capped after each use to prevent loss of tablet potency." [[FDA-Nitrostat]].

### nursing · Discharge, readmission and follow-up
- US Medicare 2007–2009: 19.9% of 548,834 AMI hospitalisations were followed by readmission within 30 days. Only 10.0% of readmissions were for AMI again. 67.6% occurred within 15 days, and the median time to readmission was 10 days [[Dharmarajan2013]].
- Meta-analysis of 42 RCTs of discharge interventions: 30-day readmission RR 0.82 (0.73–0.91). Interventions with many components, involving more people and supporting patient capacity for self-care were 1.3–1.4 times more effective [[Leppin2014]].

### nursing · Nurse-led secondary prevention
- Nurse-led secondary prevention clinics in Scottish general practice (RCT, 1,173 patients with CHD; 1 year): six of eight SF-36 domains improved, fewer patients reported worsening chest pain (OR 0.59) and fewer were admitted to hospital (OR 0.64) [[Campbell1998]].
- Four-year follow-up (1,343 patients): cumulative death 14.5% vs 18.9% (adjusted HR 0.75, 0.58–0.98) and coronary events 14.2% vs 18.2% (HR 0.76). This was despite controls later attending clinics [[Murchie2003]].
- EUROACTION, the nurse-coordinated, family-based programme, improved diet, BP control and statin prescribing (86% vs 80% in hospital patients) at 1 year compared with usual care [[Wood2008EUROACTION]].

### Exam hooks
- Exercise-based CR cuts MI (RR 0.72) and all-cause hospital admission (RR 0.65) at 6–12 months. The all-cause mortality effect is small (RR 0.86) [[Dibben2026]].
- The long-term benefit of CR shows in cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.58 at >3 years in Cochrane 2021) [[Dibben2021]].
- Home-based and centre-based CR give similar outcomes (mortality RR 1.19, CI crossing 1) [[McDonagh2023]].
- Only about 1 in 4 eligible US Medicare beneficiaries attends CR (24.4%). Women (18.9%) and Black and Hispanic patients (about 13%) attend less [[Ritchey2020]].
- Attending more CR sessions is linked to lower mortality: 36 vs 1 session, HR 0.53 for death at 4 years (observational) [[Hammill2010]].
- About 1 in 5 people have major depression after MI (19.8% on structured interview) [[Thombs2006]].
- Post-MI depression roughly doubles mortality risk (OR 2.25), and the association is partly explained by disease severity [[Meijer2011,Meijer2013]].
- About 1 in 8 people develop PTSD symptoms after ACS (12%), which doubles the risk of recurrence or death (RR 2.00) [[Edmondson2012]].
- Treating post-MI depression (ENRICHD CBT, SADHART sertraline) improves mood but has not been shown to improve cardiac survival [[Berkman2003,Glassman2002]].
- Routine oxygen in normoxaemic suspected MI (SpO2 ≥90%) does not lower 1-year mortality (5.0% vs 5.1%) [[Hofmann2017]].
- Oxygen in non-hypoxic STEMI was linked to larger infarcts on 6-month CMR (20.3 g vs 13.1 g) [[Stub2015]].
- Morphine reduces ticagrelor exposure by about 36% and delays its peak from 2 h to 4 h [[Kubica2016]].
- Radial access lowers major bleeding and mortality compared with femoral access in ACS (MATRIX) [[Valgimigli2015]].
- Femoral access is the strongest predictor of retroperitoneal bleeding (OR 19.66) [[Kwok2018]].
- Patent haemostasis (confirming radial flow during compression) cuts radial artery occlusion by 75% at 30 days [[Pancholy2008]].
- Unplanned DAPT disruption carries the highest risk in the first 7 days (HR 7.04) [[Mehran2013]].
- Nitroglycerin label: 1 tablet every 5 minutes, no more than 3 in 15 minutes, taken sitting. Contraindicated with PDE-5 inhibitors [[FDA-Nitrostat]].
- Door-to-ECG goal ≤10 minutes. Women, Black patients and people without chest pain are more likely to wait longer [[Yiadom2022]].
- Two-thirds of 30-day readmissions after AMI happen within 15 days, and most are not for MI [[Dharmarajan2013]].

### In plain words
- If you think you or someone else is having a heart attack, call your local emergency number now (911 in the US, 999 in the UK, 112 in the EU).
- Cardiac rehab is a programme of exercise, teaching and support after a heart attack. In studies, it lowered the chance of another heart attack and of going back into hospital [[Dibben2026]].
- Rehab at home, with support from the rehab team, worked about as well as rehab in a hospital gym in studies [[McDonagh2023]].
- Many people who could go to rehab never start. Women, older adults and people from some ethnic groups are offered it and attend less often. It is fine to ask your care team for a referral [[Ritchey2020,Colella2015]].
- Supervised rehab exercise is very safe: in France, serious heart problems happened about once in every 50,000 hours of exercise [[Pavy2006]].
- Feeling low, worried or on edge after a heart attack is common. About 1 in 5 people have depression, and about 1 in 8 have trauma-like stress symptoms. These are health problems, not weakness, and they can be treated [[Thombs2006,Edmondson2012]].
- Most people who worked before their heart attack go back to work, about 8 in 10 in studies [[Kai2022]].
- In the UK, car drivers usually stop driving for 1 to 4 weeks after a heart attack, depending on treatment and heart pump strength. Bus and lorry drivers must tell the DVLA. Ask your team what applies to you [[DVLA2025]].
- Sex causes only a very small extra heart risk, and regular exercise makes that risk smaller. It is okay to ask your team when it is right for you [[Dahabreh2011]].
- Never take nitroglycerin (GTN) with erection pills such as sildenafil or tadalafil. Together they can drop your blood pressure dangerously [[FDA-Nitrostat]].
- Do not stop your blood-thinning pills (antiplatelets) on your own, even for bruising. Talk to your heart team first [[FDA-Brilinta,Mehran2013]].
- Bruising where the tube went in (wrist or groin) is common in the week after a procedure [[Cosman2011]].
- Partners and families are often anxious or low too. Their wellbeing matters and support is available [[Thomas2011HAT]].
- Stopping smoking after a heart attack is linked to about a one-third lower chance of dying [[Critchley2003]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Does CR still reduce death in the modern era? The Cochrane 2026 review finds a likely small reduction in all-cause mortality (RR 0.86, CI up to 1.00) [[Dibben2026]]. The CaReMATCH IPD of post-2010 trials finds no mortality difference (HR 0.99) but a clear cut in hospitalisation [[Stens2026]]. Status: benefit on hospitalisation, MI and quality of life is Established; the mortality benefit in contemporary care is uncertain.
- How should hospitals close the referral and participation gap? Systematic inpatient (automatic) referral increased enrolment among women [[Samayoa2014]], and home-based or digital CR is as effective as centre-based CR [[McDonagh2023]]. US participation is still about 1 in 4 against a 70% goal [[Ritchey2020]]. Status: open implementation question.
- Should every post-MI patient be screened for depression? The AHA advises screening [[Lichtman2008,Lichtman2014]]. A systematic review found no trial showing that screening improves outcomes [[Thombs2008]], and depression treatment improves mood without proven cardiac benefit [[Berkman2003,Richards2017]]. Status: unresolved.
- Is the morphine–harm signal causal? IMPRESSION shows a pharmacological interaction [[Kubica2016]]. Mortality signals come from observational data with high risk of bias [[Meine2005H,Duarte2019]]. Status: interaction Established, clinical harm Hypothesis.
- What is the best TR band protocol? Faster deflation appeared safe after diagnostic catheterisation [[Deuling2017]], and patent haemostasis reduces occlusion [[Pancholy2008]]. Protocols after PCI with potent antithrombotics vary. Status: practice varies.
- Driving advice: UK rules are explicit [[DVLA2025]], but audits show advice is often not documented [[Bharaj2021,Dyson2025]]. Rules differ by country, and this pass did not verify non-UK rules.
- Sexual activity timing: absolute triggering risk is low [[Dahabreh2011]]. The AHA 2012 statement's specific timing and METs wording could not be read in this pass (publisher 403), so it is not quoted.
