# F · Complications, cardiogenic shock, prognosis and research toward better treatment

Pass date: 2026-11-04. Modules served: complications, frontier, what.

This pass covers what can go wrong after a heart attack (myocardial infarction, MI): rhythm problems, sudden death risk and defibrillator timing, heart failure, cardiogenic shock and its trials, mechanical complications, right ventricular infarction, left ventricular (LV) thrombus and aneurysm, and pericarditis. It also covers long-term outlook, readmission, and the research frontier (inflammation, lipoprotein(a), inclisiran outcome trials, heart-failure prevention after MI, protection against reperfusion injury, cell therapy, the polypill, AI reading of the ECG). Every number comes from a PubMed or Europe PMC abstract I read, or from a ClinicalTrials.gov record. All survival, mortality and incidence figures are population statistics from the named cohorts or trials. They cannot predict what will happen to any one person.

## Findings

### complications · Dangerous heart rhythms in the first hours and days (VT/VF)
- In APEX-AMI (5,745 people with ST-elevation MI, STEMI, having primary PCI at 296 hospitals in 17 countries), sustained ventricular tachycardia or fibrillation (VT/VF) occurred in 329 patients (5.7%) [[Mehta2009APEX]].
- 64% of these VT/VF episodes happened before the end of cardiac catheterisation, and 90% within 48 hours of symptom onset [[Mehta2009APEX]].
- 90-day mortality was 23.2% with VT/VF and 3.6% without (adjusted HR 3.63; 95% CI 2.59–5.09) [[Mehta2009APEX]].
- Late VT/VF (after catheterisation) carried a worse outlook than early VT/VF: 90-day mortality 33.3% (adjusted HR 5.59) versus 17.2% (adjusted HR 2.34), with 3.6% for no VT/VF [[Mehta2009APEX]].
- Factors linked to early VT/VF included TIMI flow grade 0 before PCI (HR 2.94), inferior infarction (HR 2.16) and Killip class above I (HR 1.88). Beta-blocker use within 24 hours went with less late VT/VF (HR 0.52; 95% CI 0.32–0.85) [[Mehta2009APEX]].
- A meta-analysis of 21 cohorts (57,158 people with acute MI) found 2,316 with validated primary VF (about 4%, my calculation). Primary VF was linked to ST elevation on the admission ECG (OR 3.35), earlier hospital arrival (2.62 hours earlier on average), male sex (OR 1.27), smoking (OR 1.26), AV block before VF (OR 2.02) and lower admission potassium (0.27 mEq/L lower) [[Gheeraert2006]].
- The same review found no firm risk factor for primary VF beyond ST elevation and time from symptom onset. It concluded that primary VF reflects abrupt coronary occlusion plus early arrival [[Gheeraert2006]].

### complications · Atrial fibrillation (AF) after MI
- A systematic review gives an AF incidence of 6–21% in acute MI. Predictors are older age, heart failure symptoms and depressed LV function [[Schmitt2009AF]].
- AF complicating acute MI raises in-hospital and long-term mortality and the risk of ischaemic stroke, both in hospital and afterwards. The stroke risk applies even when the AF was transient and sinus rhythm returned before discharge [[Schmitt2009AF]].
- In a meta-analysis of 43 studies (278,854 people), AF went with higher mortality after MI (OR 1.46; 95% CI 1.35–1.58). New AF with no prior history kept an OR of 1.37 (1.26–1.49) [[Jabre2011Meta]].
- In an Olmsted County community cohort (3,220 people with a first MI, 1983–2007), 729 developed AF after the MI: 30% within 2 days, 16% between days 3 and 30, and 54% after day 30 [[Jabre2011Community]].
- The 5-year cumulative incidence of AF after MI was 19% and did not change by calendar year of the MI [[Jabre2011Community]].
- In that cohort, AF was linked to higher mortality (HR 3.77; 95% CI 3.37–4.21). The risk was greatest when AF began more than 30 days after the MI (HR 2.58), compared with AF within 2 days (HR 1.63) [[Jabre2011Community]].

### complications · Heart block (atrioventricular block)
- In the fibrinolytic era, four pooled STEMI trials (about 76,000 patients) found high-degree AV block in 6.9%. Independent predictors included inferior MI, older age, worse Killip class, female sex, hypertension and diabetes [[Meine2005]].
- In those trials, AV block went with higher adjusted mortality at 30 days (OR 3.2; 95% CI 2.7–3.7), 6 months (OR 1.6) and 1 year (OR 1.5) [[Meine2005]].
- In the primary PCI era, high-degree AV block occurred in 67 of 2,073 people with STEMI (3.2%), lower than in the fibrinolytic era. Predictors included right coronary artery occlusion, age over 65, female sex, hypertension and diabetes [[Gang2012]].
- High-degree AV block tripled adjusted mortality (HR 3.14; 95% CI 2.04–4.84), but the excess was confined to the first 30 days. People who survived past day 30 had the same outlook as those without AV block [[Gang2012]].
- In the US National Inpatient Sample 2003–2012 (2,273,853 STEMI hospitalisations), complete heart block complicated 2.2% (2.1% in 2003, 2.3% in 2012) [[Harikrishnan2015]].
- In-hospital mortality was 20.4% with complete heart block versus 8.7% without (adjusted OR 2.47). The excess was largest in anterior STEMI, and permanent pacemaker rates in these patients fell over the decade [[Harikrishnan2015]].

### complications · Right ventricular infarction with inferior MI
- Among 200 consecutive people with acute inferior MI, 54% had ST elevation in lead V4R. This sign detected right ventricular infarction with 88% sensitivity and 78% specificity [[Zehender1993]].
- In-hospital mortality was 31% with V4R ST elevation versus 6% without, and major in-hospital complications occurred in 64% versus 28%. V4R elevation independently predicted in-hospital death (relative risk 7.7) [[Zehender1993]].

### complications · Sudden cardiac death risk and when defibrillators help
- VALIANT (14,609 people with LV dysfunction, heart failure or both after MI): 1,067 (7%) died suddenly or were resuscitated from cardiac arrest, at a median of 180 days after the MI [[Solomon2005VALIANT]].
- In VALIANT, risk was highest in the first 30 days at 1.4% per month (95% CI 1.2–1.6), falling to 0.14% per month after 2 years. With LV ejection fraction (LVEF) of 30% or less, the early rate was 2.3% per month [[Solomon2005VALIANT]].
- 19% of all sudden deaths or resuscitated arrests in VALIANT happened in the first 30 days. Each 5-point fall in LVEF raised the adjusted 30-day risk by 21% [[Solomon2005VALIANT]].
- DINAMIT randomised 674 people 6–40 days after MI (LVEF ≤0.35 plus impaired autonomic function) to an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) or no ICD. Overall mortality did not differ (HR 1.08; 95% CI 0.76–1.55) [[Hohnloser2004DINAMIT]].
- In DINAMIT, the ICD reduced arrhythmic death (12 vs 29 deaths; HR 0.42), but non-arrhythmic deaths rose (50 vs 29; HR 1.75), cancelling the benefit [[Hohnloser2004DINAMIT]].
- IRIS randomised 898 high-risk patients 5–31 days after MI to an ICD or medical therapy. Overall mortality was not reduced (HR 1.04; 95% CI 0.81–1.35) [[Steinbeck2009IRIS]].
- In IRIS, sudden cardiac deaths fell (27 vs 60; HR 0.55) and non-sudden cardiac deaths rose (68 vs 39; HR 1.92), the same pattern as DINAMIT [[Steinbeck2009IRIS]].
- MADIT-II (1,232 people with a prior MI and LVEF ≤0.30, not in the early post-MI window) showed that a prophylactic ICD lowered mortality: 14.2% versus 19.8% over an average 20 months (HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.51–0.93) [[Moss2002MADIT]].
- The VEST authors note that ICDs are contraindicated until 40 to 90 days after MI, despite the high early risk of sudden death with low ejection fraction. VEST tested a wearable defibrillator for this gap [[Olgin2018VEST]].
- VEST randomised 2,302 people with acute MI and LVEF ≤35% (2:1) to a wearable cardioverter-defibrillator plus guideline-directed therapy or therapy alone. Arrhythmic death at 90 days was 1.6% versus 2.4% (RR 0.67; 95% CI 0.37–1.21; P=0.18), not significant [[Olgin2018VEST]].
- In VEST, all-cause death was 3.1% versus 4.9% (RR 0.64; 95% CI 0.43–0.98; uncorrected P=0.04), a secondary outcome. Median wear time was 18.0 hours a day, and only 12 of the 48 device-group patients who died were wearing it at the time [[Olgin2018VEST]].
- In VEST, 1.3% of device-group patients received an appropriate shock and 0.6% an inappropriate shock [[Olgin2018VEST]].
- PROFID pooled cohort (32,214 people with LVEF ≤35% after MI, observational): the mortality reduction associated with primary-prevention ICDs shrank over time. HR 0.54 (0.47–0.62) in 1995–2004, 0.67 (0.62–0.72) in 2005–2014, and 0.89 (0.73–1.07, not significant) in 2015–2020 (Early evidence) [[SepehriShamloo2026]].
- The PROFID EHRA trial is testing whether optimal medical therapy without an ICD is non-inferior to therapy with an ICD. It plans to enrol about 3,595 people at least 3 months after MI with LVEF ≤35% and NYHA class II–III; the primary outcome is all-cause death. It is recruiting, with estimated primary completion in November 2027 [[Dagres2026PROFID,NCT05665608]].

### complications · Heart failure and LV dysfunction after MI
- In Framingham (676 people with a first MI, 1970–1999), 30-day heart failure incidence rose from 10% (1970s) to 23.1% (1990s), while 30-day mortality fell from 12.2% to 4.1% [[Velagaleti2008]].
- Over the same decades, 5-year heart failure incidence after MI rose from 27.6% to 31.9%, while 5-year mortality fell from 41.1% to 17.3%. More people now survive the MI and live on with a weakened heart [[Velagaleti2008]].
- In Olmsted County (2,596 people with a first MI and no prior heart failure, 1990–2010; mean follow-up 7.6 years), 902 (about 35%) developed heart failure [[Gerber2016]].
- Heart failure after MI was linked to higher mortality (age- and sex-adjusted HR 3.31; 2.49 after full adjustment). Mortality did not differ by ejection fraction, and delayed-onset heart failure carried a higher risk than early-onset [[Gerber2016]].
- Age- and sex-adjusted 5-year survival with heart failure after MI improved from 54% (1990–2000) to 61% (2001–2010), versus 81% and 82% without heart failure. Falling post-MI mortality was mainly driven by better survival with heart failure [[Gerber2016]].

### complications · Cardiogenic shock: what it is, how common, how deadly
- Cardiogenic shock is end-organ hypoperfusion from heart failure, often with multisystem organ failure. Despite improving survival, morbidity and mortality remain high and few interventions clearly improve outcomes. The AHA statement calls for regional systems of care [[vanDiepen2017]].
- In the US, cardiogenic shock affects 40,000 to 50,000 people a year and is the leading cause of in-hospital death after acute MI [[Samsky2021]].
- Cardiogenic shock occurs in up to 10% of people right after acute MI. Mortality is about 40% at 30 days and approaches 50% at 1 year (population statistics) [[Samsky2021]].
- Immediate revascularisation of the infarct-related artery is the only shock treatment supported by randomised trials. Mechanical support devices are often used without trial proof of benefit (review written before DanGer Shock) [[Samsky2021]].
- US National Inpatient Sample 2003–2010: 7.9% of 1,990,486 people aged 40 or older with STEMI had cardiogenic shock. The rate rose from 6.5% in 2003 to 10.1% in 2010 [[Kolte2014]].
- Over the same period, in-hospital mortality from STEMI shock fell from 44.6% to 33.8% (adjusted OR 0.71). Early mechanical revascularisation rose from 30.4% to 50.7% [[Kolte2014]].
- The SCAI classification stages shock from A to E: A "at risk", B "beginning" (without hypoperfusion), C "classic" (hypoperfusion present), D "deteriorating" (no stability after at least 30 minutes of initial treatment) and E "extremis" [[Baran2019SCAI]].

### complications · Cardiogenic shock trials
- SHOCK trial: 302 people with shock from LV failure after MI were randomised to emergency revascularisation (152, by CABG or angioplasty) or initial medical stabilisation (150). Intra-aortic balloon pump (IABP) was used in 86% of both groups [[Hochman1999SHOCK]].
- In SHOCK, the median onset of shock was 5.6 hours after infarction, and most infarcts were anterior [[Hochman1999SHOCK]].
- 30-day mortality, the primary endpoint, was 46.7% with revascularisation versus 56.0% with medical stabilisation (difference −9.3%; 95% CI −20.5 to 1.9; P=0.11), not significant [[Hochman1999SHOCK]].
- 6-month mortality was lower with early revascularisation: 50.3% versus 63.1% (P=0.027) [[Hochman1999SHOCK]].
- 1-year survival was 46.7% with early revascularisation versus 33.6% with medical stabilisation (absolute difference 13.2%; 95% CI 2.2–24.1%). The authors recommended rapid transfer to revascularisation-capable centres, especially for patients under 75 [[Hochman2001SHOCK]].
- At 6 years, survival was 32.8% versus 19.6% (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.57–0.97), a 13.2% absolute and 67% relative improvement [[Hochman2006SHOCK]].
- Among SHOCK patients who survived to discharge, 6-year survival was 62.4% with early revascularisation versus 44.4% (HR 0.59). Annualised death rates were 8.3% versus 14.3% [[Hochman2006SHOCK]].
- IABP-SHOCK II randomised 600 people with MI and cardiogenic shock (all planned for early revascularisation) to IABP or no IABP. 30-day mortality was 39.7% versus 41.3% (RR 0.96; 95% CI 0.79–1.17; P=0.69) [[Thiele2012IABP]].
- At 12 months in IABP-SHOCK II, 52% of the IABP group and 51% of controls had died (RR 1.01; 95% CI 0.86–1.18), and quality of life did not differ [[Thiele2013IABP]].
- CULPRIT-SHOCK (706 people with MI, cardiogenic shock and multivessel disease): treating the culprit lesion only, with optional staged PCI later, beat immediate multivessel PCI. Death or renal-replacement therapy at 30 days was 45.9% versus 55.4% (RR 0.83; 95% CI 0.71–0.96) [[Thiele2017CULPRIT]].
- In CULPRIT-SHOCK, the relative risk of death alone was 0.84 (95% CI 0.72–0.98) with culprit-only PCI [[Thiele2017CULPRIT]].
- ECLS-SHOCK (417 people analysed): early veno-arterial extracorporeal life support (ECLS) did not lower 30-day death, 47.8% versus 49.0% (RR 0.98; 95% CI 0.80–1.19) [[Thiele2023ECLS]].
- ECLS raised harm: moderate or severe bleeding 23.4% versus 9.6% (RR 2.44), and peripheral vascular complications needing intervention 11.0% versus 3.8% (RR 2.86) [[Thiele2023ECLS]].
- DanGer Shock (355 people with STEMI and cardiogenic shock analysed; median age 67; 79.2% men): a microaxial flow pump (Impella CP) plus standard care lowered death at 180 days, 45.8% versus 58.5% (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.55–0.99; P=0.04) [[Moller2024DanGer]].
- In DanGer Shock, a composite of severe bleeding, limb ischaemia, haemolysis, device failure or worsening aortic regurgitation occurred in 24.0% versus 6.2% (RR 4.74). Renal-replacement therapy was used in 41.9% versus 26.7% [[Moller2024DanGer]].
- Taken together: early revascularisation (SHOCK) and culprit-only PCI (CULPRIT-SHOCK) improved survival. Routine IABP and routine ECLS did not. The microaxial pump lowered 180-day death in a selected STEMI shock population, at the cost of more complications (Established) [[Hochman2006SHOCK,Thiele2017CULPRIT,Thiele2012IABP,Thiele2023ECLS,Moller2024DanGer]].

### complications · Mechanical complications (rupture of heart structures)
- The most common mechanical complications are acute mitral regurgitation from papillary muscle rupture, ventricular septal defect, pseudoaneurysm and free wall rupture. Each carries major risk of illness and death, and care needs a multidisciplinary team, including support for decisions about palliation [[Damluji2021]].
- With early reperfusion, mechanical complications now occur in fewer than 0.1% of people after acute MI. Mortality has not fallen in parallel [[Gong2021]].
- Mechanical complications most often occur within the first week after MI and often present as cardiogenic shock or acute pulmonary oedema. Echocardiography is usually the first test, and surgery is the definitive treatment, with percutaneous options emerging for people at prohibitive surgical risk [[Gong2021]].
- US National Inpatient Sample 2003–2015: mechanical complications occurred in 0.27% of 3,951,861 STEMI and 0.06% of 5,114,270 NSTEMI hospitalisations, with no change in trend [[Elbadawi2019]].
- In-hospital mortality with a mechanical complication was 42.4% after STEMI and 18.0% after NSTEMI, with no improvement over 2003–2015. Surgical repair predicted lower mortality in those with shock [[Elbadawi2019]].
- In people aged 75 or older with a first STEMI (1,393 patients, 1988–2008), mechanical complications fell from 11.1% to 4.3% as reperfusion spread. Their fatality stayed at 87.1% versus 82.4% [[Puerto2018]].
- In that elderly cohort, overall in-hospital mortality fell from 34.3% to 13.4%, but mechanical complications still caused about a quarter of deaths (28.1% to 24.5%) [[Puerto2018]].
- Ventricular septal rupture in GUSTO-I (fibrinolysis era): 84 of 41,021 patients (0.2%), at a median of 1 day from symptom onset, sooner than described before fibrinolysis [[Crenshaw2000]].
- 30-day mortality with septal rupture in GUSTO-I was 73.8% versus 6.8% without. Risk factors were older age, anterior infarction, female sex and no previous smoking [[Crenshaw2000]].
- Papillary muscle rupture in US hospitals (2005–2014): 932 of 3,244,799 acute MI admissions (0.029%). In-hospital mortality was 36.3% versus 5.3% without it, and 57.5% underwent mitral valve surgery [[Bhardwaj2020]].
- Predictors of death with papillary muscle rupture were age, inferior wall MI and cardiac arrest [[Bhardwaj2020]].
- In a series of 96 septal and 97 free wall ruptures, the two had similar clinical, angiographic and ECG features. In 20 patients, free wall rupture followed septal rupture, which supports early surgery [[Figueras1998]].

### complications · Left ventricular thrombus and aneurysm
- With optimal imaging, LV thrombus may occur in up to 15% of people with STEMI and up to 25% with anterior MI [[McCarthy2018LVT]].
- Standard transthoracic echo has low sensitivity for LV thrombus, so contrast echo or cardiac MRI is advised when suspicion is high. Once thrombus is found, anticoagulation is essential to prevent embolism [[McCarthy2018LVT]].
- The 2022 AHA scientific statement notes that cardiac MRI detects LV thrombus more sensitively than echocardiography. It addresses direct oral anticoagulants as an alternative to vitamin K antagonists, and the bleeding concerns of adding anticoagulation to dual antiplatelet therapy [[Levine2022LVT]].
- In a 1980s serial echo study of 158 people with a first MI (before routine reperfusion), LV aneurysm formed in 22%, mostly after anterior MI (27 of 35). No new aneurysms appeared after 3 months [[Visser1986]].
- In that study, aneurysm forming within the first 5 days carried 1-year mortality of 80% (12 of 15), versus 25% for later-forming aneurysms [[Visser1986]].

### complications · Pericarditis and Dressler (post-cardiac injury) syndrome
- In the Israeli ACSIS registry (6,282 people with STEMI, 2000–2013), early post-MI pericarditis occurred in 1.2%. The rate fell from 170 to 110 per 10,000 (a 35% reduction) [[Lador2018]].
- People with early post-MI pericarditis had longer time to reperfusion (225 vs 183 minutes), larger infarcts and longer hospital stays (7 vs 5 days). Their 30-day, 1-year and 5-year survival did not differ [[Lador2018]].
- Post-MI pericarditis belongs to the post-cardiac injury syndromes, which also include post-pericardiotomy and post-traumatic pericarditis. These are thought to be immune-mediated reactions after injury to the pericardium or myocardium [[Imazio2013]].
- Treatment of post-cardiac injury syndromes is empirical anti-inflammatory therapy plus colchicine. Colchicine has been shown to be safe and effective for preventing pericarditis [[Imazio2013]].

### what · Long-term outlook and readmission
- SWEDEHEART (105,674 people with STEMI in Sweden, 1995–2014): 1-year mortality fell from 22.1% to 14.1%. Cardiovascular death fell from 20.1% to 11.1% and recurrent MI from 11.5% to 5.8% [[Szummer2017]].
- Over the same years, primary PCI rose from 4.5% to 78.0% and statin use from 14.1% to 93.6%. Gains plateaued from about 2008, and after adjusting for treatment changes the improvement was no longer significant [[Szummer2017]].
- Compared with the general population, the standardised mortality ratio after STEMI fell from 5.54 to 3.74 [[Szummer2017]].
- In 97,254 Swedish MI survivors (2006–2011), 18.3% had non-fatal MI, non-fatal stroke or cardiovascular death in the first year. Among those event-free at 1 year, 20.0% had such an event in the next 36 months [[Jernberg2015]].
- Heart failure (HR 1.57) and no revascularisation for the index MI (HR 1.88) independently raised that long-term risk [[Jernberg2015]].
- In US Medicare (2007–2009; 548,834 acute MI hospitalisations), 19.9% were readmitted within 30 days, and only 10.0% of readmissions were for another MI [[Dharmarajan2013]].
- 67.6% of 30-day readmissions after MI happened within 15 days, and the median time to readmission was 10 days [[Dharmarajan2013]].

### frontier · Inflammation as a target
- CANTOS (10,061 people with prior MI and high-sensitivity CRP ≥2 mg/L): the IL-1β antibody canakinumab 150 mg every 3 months reduced MI, stroke or cardiovascular death (HR 0.85; 95% CI 0.74–0.98), without lowering lipids (Established proof of concept) [[Ridker2017CANTOS]].
- In CANTOS, fatal infections were more common with canakinumab, and all-cause mortality did not differ (HR 0.94; 95% CI 0.83–1.06) [[Ridker2017CANTOS]].
- COLCOT (4,745 people within 30 days of MI): colchicine 0.5 mg daily lowered the composite ischaemic endpoint over a median 22.6 months, 5.5% versus 7.1% (HR 0.77; 95% CI 0.61–0.96) [[Tardif2019]].
- In COLCOT, the HR for stroke was 0.26 and for urgent angina hospitalisation leading to revascularisation 0.50. Serious pneumonia was reported in 0.9% versus 0.4% [[Tardif2019]].
- LoDoCo2 (5,522 people with chronic coronary disease): colchicine 0.5 mg daily lowered the composite endpoint, 6.8% versus 9.6% (HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.57–0.83; median 28.6 months) [[Nidorf2020]].
- CLEAR SYNERGY (CLEAR; 7,062 people with MI in 14 countries): colchicine started soon after MI did not lower cardiovascular death, recurrent MI, stroke or ischaemia-driven revascularisation over a median 3 years, 9.1% versus 9.3% (HR 0.99; 95% CI 0.85–1.16) [[Jolly2025]].
- In CLEAR SYNERGY, CRP at 3 months was 1.28 mg/L lower with colchicine, and diarrhoea was more common (10.2% vs 6.6%) [[Jolly2025]].
- Net picture: colchicine after MI is supported by COLCOT and contradicted by the larger CLEAR SYNERGY trial, so its role early after MI is contested (Early evidence) [[Tardif2019,Jolly2025]].
- RESCUE (phase 2; 264 people with chronic kidney disease and CRP ≥2 mg/L): the IL-6 ligand antibody ziltivekimab cut median hsCRP at 12 weeks by 77%, 88% and 92% (7.5, 15 and 30 mg doses) versus 4% with placebo. These are biomarker results only (Early evidence) [[Ridker2021RESCUE]].
- ZEUS is the ziltivekimab outcome trial (6,385 people with cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease and inflammation; primary outcome 3-point MACE). ClinicalTrials.gov lists it as completed in June 2026, with no results posted as of 2026-11-04 (Hypothesis until reported) [[NCT05021835]].
- ARTEMIS is testing ziltivekimab in about 10,000 people with an acute heart attack (primary outcome 3-point MACE). It is active, not recruiting, with estimated primary completion in January 2027 (Hypothesis) [[NCT06118281]].

### frontier · Lipoprotein(a) and new LDL-lowering outcome trials
- OCEAN(a)-DOSE (phase 2; 281 people with atherosclerotic disease and Lp(a) >150 nmol/L): the small interfering RNA olpasiran lowered Lp(a) at 36 weeks by 70.5% (10 mg) to 101.1% (225 mg every 12 weeks), placebo-adjusted. This was a biomarker trial (Early evidence) [[ODonoghue2022]].
- OCEAN(a)-Outcomes (7,297 participants) is testing whether olpasiran reduces coronary death, MI or urgent coronary revascularisation. It is active, not recruiting, with estimated primary completion in March 2028 (Hypothesis) [[NCT05581303]].
- Lp(a)HORIZON (8,323 participants) tested the antisense drug pelacarsen. ClinicalTrials.gov lists it as completed (primary completion July 2026) with no results posted [[NCT04023552]].
- Topline Lp(a)HORIZON results, announced in September 2026, showed that pelacarsen lowered Lp(a) but did not reduce the primary composite cardiovascular endpoint. Full data are awaited (Early evidence: topline only, not yet peer-reviewed) [[Sabouret2026]].
- Inclisiran outcome trials are under way: ORION-4 (16,124 participants; primary completion listed as October 2026) and VICTORION-2P (17,004 participants; estimated primary completion May 2027). Both are active, not recruiting (Hypothesis until reported) [[NCT03705234,NCT05030428]].

### frontier · Preventing heart failure after MI
- EMPACT-MI (6,522 people at risk of heart failure after acute MI): empagliflozin 10 mg did not lower the composite of heart failure hospitalisation or death, 8.2% versus 9.1% (HR 0.90; 95% CI 0.76–1.06) [[Butler2024EMPACT]].
- In EMPACT-MI, first heart failure hospitalisation (a component) was 3.6% versus 4.7% (HR 0.77; 95% CI 0.60–0.98), and death was 5.2% versus 5.5% [[Butler2024EMPACT]].
- DAPA-MI (4,017 people with MI and impaired LV function, without diabetes or chronic heart failure): dapagliflozin improved a hierarchical cardiometabolic outcome (win ratio 1.34). Cardiovascular death or heart failure hospitalisation did not differ, 2.5% versus 2.6% (HR 0.95) [[James2024DAPAMI]].
- PARADISE-MI (5,661 people with MI plus reduced LVEF, congestion or both): sacubitril-valsartan was not superior to ramipril for cardiovascular death or incident heart failure, 11.9% versus 13.2% (HR 0.90; 95% CI 0.78–1.04) [[Pfeffer2021PARADISE]].

### frontier · Protecting the heart muscle from reperfusion injury
- CONDI-2/ERIC-PPCI (5,401 people with STEMI having primary PCI): remote ischaemic conditioning (four cycles of 5-minute arm-cuff inflation and deflation before PCI) did not reduce cardiac death or heart failure hospitalisation at 12 months, 9.4% versus 8.6% (HR 1.10; 95% CI 0.91–1.32) [[Hausenloy2019CONDI2]].
- CIRCUS (970 people with anterior STEMI and an occluded artery): intravenous cyclosporine 2.5 mg/kg before PCI did not improve the 1-year composite of death, heart failure or adverse LV remodelling, 59.0% versus 58.1% (OR 1.04) [[Cung2015CIRCUS]].
- Together these large neutral trials mean that no drug or conditioning strategy has yet shown clinical benefit against reperfusion injury on top of timely PCI. The concept remains a Hypothesis for clinical practice [[Hausenloy2019CONDI2,Cung2015CIRCUS]].

### frontier · Cell therapy and heart regeneration
- A Cochrane review (41 RCTs; 2,732 participants) found that bone marrow cell therapy after acute MI did not change long-term all-cause mortality (RR 0.93; 95% CI 0.58–1.50; moderate-quality evidence) [[Fisher2015Cochrane]].
- In that review, differences in LVEF were 2–5% and judged not clinically relevant, with no effect on morbidity or quality of life [[Fisher2015Cochrane]].
- BAMI, the largest trial of intracoronary bone marrow cells after MI (375 people with LVEF ≤45% after primary PCI), under-recruited and could not test its hypothesis. 2-year mortality was 3.26% versus 3.82% [[Mathur2020BAMI]].
- Rebuilding heart muscle after MI with cells remains a research Hypothesis, with no proven clinical benefit [[Fisher2015Cochrane,Mathur2020BAMI]].

### frontier · The polypill
- SECURE (2,499 people within 6 months of MI): a polypill of aspirin 100 mg, ramipril (2.5, 5 or 10 mg) and atorvastatin (20 or 40 mg) lowered major adverse cardiovascular events versus usual care, 9.5% versus 12.7% (HR 0.76; 95% CI 0.60–0.96; median 36 months) [[Castellano2022SECURE]].
- In SECURE, the key secondary composite was 8.2% versus 11.7% (HR 0.70), and self-reported adherence was higher with the polypill. This is a single positive phase 3 trial (Early evidence) [[Castellano2022SECURE]].

### frontier · Artificial intelligence reading the ECG (occlusion MI)
- About one-third of people with NSTEMI have an acutely occluded culprit artery (occlusion MI, OMI). Standard STEMI criteria miss many of them [[Herman2024]].
- An AI ECG model trained on 18,616 ECGs was tested on 3,254 ECGs from 2,222 patients (21.6% with OMI). It reached an AUC of 0.938, with sensitivity 80.6% versus 32.5% for STEMI criteria and specificity 93.7% versus 97.7% (Early evidence) [[Herman2024]].
- In the same test set, expert ECG readers had 73.0% sensitivity and 95.7% specificity [[Herman2024]].
- A machine-learning model derived and externally validated in 7,313 consecutive patients outperformed clinicians and commercial ECG software for OMI. Combined with clinician judgement, it correctly reclassified one in three patients with chest pain (Early evidence; observational) [[AlZaiti2023]].
- These are diagnostic accuracy studies. No randomised trial has yet shown that AI ECG triage improves patient outcomes (Early evidence) [[Herman2024,AlZaiti2023]].

### Exam hooks
- About 90% of VT/VF in STEMI treated with primary PCI occurs within 48 hours of symptom onset [[Mehta2009APEX]].
- Late VT/VF (after catheterisation) carries higher 90-day mortality than early VT/VF (33.3% vs 17.2%) [[Mehta2009APEX]].
- Heart block after inferior MI usually reflects right coronary artery occlusion. In the primary PCI era, its excess mortality is confined to the first 30 days [[Gang2012]].
- ST elevation in V4R points to right ventricular infarction in inferior MI (sensitivity 88%, specificity 78%) [[Zehender1993]].
- Sudden death risk after MI is highest in the first 30 days (1.4% per month; 2.3% per month if LVEF ≤30%) [[Solomon2005VALIANT]].
- Early ICDs (DINAMIT 6–40 days, IRIS 5–31 days) cut arrhythmic death but not total mortality, because non-arrhythmic deaths rose [[Hohnloser2004DINAMIT,Steinbeck2009IRIS]].
- MADIT-II: in prior MI with LVEF ≤30%, a prophylactic ICD lowered mortality (HR 0.69) [[Moss2002MADIT]].
- VEST: the wearable defibrillator did not significantly reduce arrhythmic death at 90 days [[Olgin2018VEST]].
- SHOCK: early revascularisation did not cut 30-day mortality, but survival was better at 6 months, 1 year and 6 years [[Hochman1999SHOCK,Hochman2006SHOCK]].
- IABP-SHOCK II: routine IABP did not reduce mortality at 30 days or 12 months [[Thiele2012IABP,Thiele2013IABP]].
- CULPRIT-SHOCK: in shock with multivessel disease, culprit-only PCI beat immediate multivessel PCI [[Thiele2017CULPRIT]].
- ECLS-SHOCK was neutral for 30-day death. DanGer Shock lowered 180-day death with a microaxial pump but raised complications [[Thiele2023ECLS,Moller2024DanGer]].
- SCAI shock stages run A (at risk), B (beginning), C (classic, hypoperfusion), D (deteriorating), E (extremis) [[Baran2019SCAI]].
- Septal rupture, free wall rupture and papillary muscle rupture are rare in the reperfusion era (all mechanical complications 0.27% of STEMI) but deadly (42.4% in-hospital mortality) [[Elbadawi2019]].
- Papillary muscle rupture causes acute mitral regurgitation. Its predictors of death include inferior wall MI [[Bhardwaj2020,Damluji2021]].
- LV thrombus is most common after anterior MI (up to 25% with optimal imaging). Cardiac MRI is more sensitive than echo [[McCarthy2018LVT,Levine2022LVT]].
- COLCOT was positive and CLEAR SYNERGY neutral for colchicine after MI [[Tardif2019,Jolly2025]].
- CANTOS proved the inflammation hypothesis without lipid lowering, at the cost of more fatal infections [[Ridker2017CANTOS]].

### In plain words
- If you think you or someone else is having a heart attack, call your local emergency number now (911 in the US, 999 in the UK, 112 in the EU).
- The first two days after a heart attack carry the highest risk of a dangerous heart rhythm. That is why people are watched on a heart monitor in hospital [[Mehta2009APEX]].
- A heart attack can disturb the heart's wiring and make the heartbeat too slow. This is more common when the lower (inferior) wall of the heart is affected [[Meine2005,Gang2012]].
- An irregular heartbeat called atrial fibrillation is common after a heart attack and raises stroke risk, so doctors look for it [[Schmitt2009AF,Jabre2011Community]].
- When the heart muscle is badly weakened, the risk of sudden cardiac arrest is highest in the first month. An implanted defibrillator is usually not fitted right away, because early implants did not help people live longer overall [[Solomon2005VALIANT,Hohnloser2004DINAMIT]].
- Some people develop heart failure after a heart attack, meaning the heart cannot pump as well as before. Because more people now survive the heart attack itself, more live with heart failure afterwards [[Velagaleti2008]].
- Cardiogenic shock means the heart is too weak to pump enough blood to the body. It is uncommon but serious, and the main treatment proven in trials is opening the blocked artery quickly [[Samsky2021]].
- Rarely, a part of the damaged heart tears, usually within the first week. This is an emergency that often needs surgery [[Gong2021]].
- A blood clot can form inside a weakened heart chamber. Doctors check with heart scans and use blood thinners if they find one [[McCarthy2018LVT]].
- Inflammation of the sac around the heart (pericarditis) can follow a heart attack, but it has become uncommon now that blocked arteries are opened quickly [[Lador2018]].
- In US Medicare data, about 1 in 5 people went back into hospital within a month of a heart attack, often for a different reason [[Dharmarajan2013]].
- Survival after a heart attack has improved a lot as treatments got better. These numbers describe large groups, not what will happen to you [[Szummer2017]].
- Researchers are testing medicines that calm inflammation, lower a blood fat called Lp(a), and AI tools that read the heart tracing. Some have worked in trials and some have not [[Tardif2019,Jolly2025,Sabouret2026,Herman2024]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Colchicine after MI: COLCOT showed fewer ischaemic events (HR 0.77) [[Tardif2019]]. The larger, longer CLEAR SYNERGY was neutral (HR 0.99) [[Jolly2025]]. LoDoCo2 was positive in chronic coronary disease [[Nidorf2020]]. Status: contested for the early post-MI period. Reasons proposed include trial size, follow-up length and background therapy, but these are interpretation, not data.
- Mechanical circulatory support in shock: DanGer Shock lowered 180-day death in selected STEMI shock with a microaxial pump [[Moller2024DanGer]]. ECLS-SHOCK (ECLS) and IABP-SHOCK II (IABP) were neutral [[Thiele2023ECLS,Thiele2012IABP]]. DanGer also raised bleeding, limb ischaemia and kidney replacement. Status: patient selection (who benefits) is unresolved.
- Defibrillators after MI in the modern era: MADIT-II established benefit for prior MI with LVEF ≤30% [[Moss2002MADIT]]. Early implantation did not lower total mortality [[Hohnloser2004DINAMIT,Steinbeck2009IRIS]]. The wearable device missed its primary endpoint [[Olgin2018VEST]]. PROFID registry data suggest the ICD benefit has shrunk (HR 0.89, not significant, in 2015–2020) [[SepehriShamloo2026]]. Status: the randomised PROFID EHRA trial is recruiting [[NCT05665608]].
- Lp(a) as a treatment target: Lp(a) is a genetically determined risk factor [[Sabouret2026]], and olpasiran nearly abolishes it [[ODonoghue2022]]. But Lp(a)HORIZON topline results were neutral for events [[Sabouret2026]]. Status: OCEAN(a)-Outcomes is due around 2028 [[NCT05581303]]. Whether a larger or earlier Lp(a) reduction is needed remains open.
- Timing of mechanical complications: classic teaching often says days 3–5. Abstracts here say "most commonly within the first week" [[Gong2021]], and septal rupture came at a median of 1 day after fibrinolysis [[Crenshaw2000]]. Status: timing depends on era and reperfusion; teach "usually within the first week".
- SGLT2 inhibitors and ARNI right after MI: EMPACT-MI, DAPA-MI and PARADISE-MI each missed their main hard endpoint [[Butler2024EMPACT,James2024DAPAMI,Pfeffer2021PARADISE]]. Fewer heart failure hospitalisations with empagliflozin were a component finding only [[Butler2024EMPACT]]. Status: no routine post-MI role for these drugs is established by these trials alone. Their heart-failure indications are separate.
- AI ECG triage: higher sensitivity for occlusion MI than STEMI criteria [[Herman2024,AlZaiti2023]]. Status: no outcome trials yet; specificity trade-offs and use across populations are untested.
