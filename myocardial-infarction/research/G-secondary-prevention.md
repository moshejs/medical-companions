# G · Secondary prevention medicines and LDL lowering

Pass date: 2026-11-04. Modules served: secondary, lipids.

This pass covers the medicines used after a heart attack (myocardial infarction, MI) to prevent another one: LDL-cholesterol lowering (statins, ezetimibe, PCSK9 antibodies, inclisiran, bempedoic acid) and its targets; beta-blockers; ACE inhibitors and ARBs; mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists; SGLT2 inhibitors; influenza vaccination; the polypill; smoking cessation; blood pressure and diabetes targets as published; and how often people keep taking these medicines. Antiplatelet therapy belongs to pass E. Colchicine and other anti-inflammatory therapy belong to pass F, and this file only points to them. Cardiac rehabilitation belongs to pass H.

All trial numbers come from PubMed or Europe PMC abstracts read in this pass. Trial results are population averages from the trial populations described. They are not predictions for any one person. Doses and guideline statements are quoted as published (US labels, guideline text) for teaching. They are not instructions. Any page built from this file that mentions symptoms carries the call-now box: "If you think you or someone else is having a heart attack, call your local emergency number now (911 in the US, 999 in the UK, 112 in the EU)."

## Findings

### lipids · Why LDL cholesterol matters (the causal case)
- A European Atherosclerosis Society consensus panel judged that low-density lipoproteins (LDL) cause atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD). It based this on genetic studies, more than 200 prospective cohorts, Mendelian randomisation studies and randomised trials covering more than 2 million participants, which show a "dose-dependent log-linear" link between LDL-C exposure and ASCVD risk. (Established) [[Ference2017EAS]]
- The same panel concluded that any mechanism that lowers LDL particle concentration should reduce ASCVD events in proportion to the absolute LDL-C reduction and to how long the reduction lasts. (Established) [[Ference2017EAS]]
- The CTT 2005 meta-analysis (90,056 participants, 14 statin trials, mean 5 years) found a 21% reduction in major vascular events (RR 0.79, 95% CI 0.77–0.81) and a 12% reduction in all-cause mortality (RR 0.88, 0.84–0.91) per 1.0 mmol/L LDL-C reduction [[CTT2005]].
- In CTT 2005, coronary mortality fell 19% per 1.0 mmol/L (RR 0.81, 0.76–0.85). MI or coronary death fell by RR 0.77, and coronary revascularisation by RR 0.76 [[CTT2005]].

### lipids · CTT 2010: the "22% per mmol/L" rule
- CTT 2010 pooled individual data from 26 trials and about 170,000 participants: 5 trials of more versus less intensive statin (39,612 people, median 5.1 years) and 21 trials of statin versus control (129,526 people, median 4.8 years) [[CTT2010]].
- Across all 26 trials, each 1.0 mmol/L (about 39 mg/dL) LDL-C reduction lowered major vascular events by about a fifth (RR 0.78, 95% CI 0.76–0.80). The reduction was similar in every type of patient studied, including those whose LDL-C was already below 2 mmol/L [[CTT2010]].
- More intensive statin regimens lowered LDL-C by a further 0.51 mmol/L at 1 year and reduced major vascular events by a further 15% (95% CI 11–18). This comprised coronary death or non-fatal MI −13%, coronary revascularisation −19% and ischaemic stroke −16% [[CTT2010]].
- All-cause mortality fell 10% per 1.0 mmol/L (RR 0.90, 0.87–0.93), mostly from fewer coronary deaths (RR 0.80). There was no effect on cancer incidence (RR 1.00) or non-vascular death (RR 0.97), "even at low LDL cholesterol concentrations" [[CTT2010]].
- CTT 2010 found "no evidence of any threshold" within the range studied and estimated that lowering LDL-C by 2–3 mmol/L would cut risk by about 40–50% [[CTT2010]].
- In a CTT analysis of 174,149 participants from 27 trials (27% women), the proportional reduction in major vascular events per 1.0 mmol/L was similar in women (RR 0.84, 99% CI 0.78–0.91) and men (RR 0.78, 0.75–0.81). All-cause mortality fell in both sexes (women RR 0.91, men RR 0.90) [[CTT2015Women]].
- In a CTT analysis of 186,854 participants from 28 trials, statins reduced major vascular events by 21% per 1.0 mmol/L (RR 0.79, 0.77–0.81) irrespective of age. The benefit was similar at all ages in people with pre-existing vascular disease, and direct evidence was weaker for people over 75 without known vascular disease [[CTT2019Older]].
- A meta-analysis of 29 trials (244,090 patients, 21,492 aged ≥75 years) found that LDL lowering reduced major vascular events in people aged 75 and older by 26% per 1 mmol/L (RR 0.74, 0.61–0.89). This was not significantly different from people under 75 (RR 0.85). Statin and non-statin therapies gave similar results in older people [[Gencer2020]].
- A meta-regression of 34 trials (270,288 patients) found all-cause mortality lower with more versus less intensive LDL lowering (7.08% vs 7.70%; RR 0.92, 0.88–0.96). The mortality benefit was larger at higher baseline LDL-C (RR change 0.91 per 40 mg/dL higher baseline) [[Navarese2018]].

### lipids · Statins after an acute coronary syndrome: early and high-intensity
- MIRACL randomised 3086 adults with unstable angina or non-Q-wave MI to atorvastatin 80 mg/day or placebo, started 24–96 hours after admission. At 16 weeks the primary end point occurred in 14.8% vs 17.4% (RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.70–1.00; P = .048), driven mostly by fewer rehospitalisations for recurrent symptomatic ischaemia (6.2% vs 8.4%) [[Schwartz2001MIRACL]].
- In MIRACL, mean LDL-C on atorvastatin fell from 124 mg/dL (3.2 mmol/L) to 72 mg/dL (1.9 mmol/L). Liver transaminases above 3 times the upper limit of normal were more common with atorvastatin (2.5% vs 0.6%) [[Schwartz2001MIRACL]].
- PROVE IT–TIMI 22 randomised 4162 patients hospitalised for ACS within the preceding 10 days to pravastatin 40 mg (standard) or atorvastatin 80 mg (intensive). Mean follow-up was 24 months [[Cannon2004PROVEIT]].
- In PROVE IT, median achieved LDL-C was 95 mg/dL (2.46 mmol/L) on pravastatin and 62 mg/dL (1.60 mmol/L) on atorvastatin 80 mg. Two-year rates of the primary composite were 26.3% vs 22.4%, a 16% hazard reduction (95% CI 5–26%; P = 0.005) [[Cannon2004PROVEIT]].
- PROVE IT was designed to show pravastatin was non-inferior. It instead showed that the intensive regimen was superior [[Cannon2004PROVEIT]].
- The 2018 AHA/ACC cholesterol guideline defines high-intensity statin therapy as a daily dose that lowers LDL-C by about ≥50%: atorvastatin 40–80 mg or rosuvastatin 20 (40) mg [[Grundy2019ACC]].
- Grundy2019ACC states: "In patients with clinical ASCVD, reduce low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) with high-intensity statin therapy or maximally tolerated statin therapy." It adds: "The more LDL-C is reduced on statin therapy, the greater will be subsequent risk reduction" [[Grundy2019ACC]].
- Grundy2019ACC defines clinical ASCVD to include acute coronary syndrome and a history of MI [[Grundy2019ACC]].
- The US Lipitor (atorvastatin) label lists indications that include reducing the risk of "Non-fatal MI, fatal and non-fatal stroke, revascularization procedures, hospitalization for congestive heart failure, and angina in adults with clinically evident CHD" [[FDA-Lipitor]].
- The Lipitor label gives an adult dosage range of 10 mg to 80 mg once daily and says to "Assess LDL-C when clinically appropriate, as early as 4 weeks after initiating LIPITOR" [[FDA-Lipitor]].
- The Lipitor label lists contraindications of acute liver failure or decompensated cirrhosis and hypersensitivity. It warns of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis, with risk factors that include "age 65 years or greater, uncontrolled hypothyroidism, renal impairment" and higher dosage [[FDA-Lipitor]].
- LODESTAR (4400 patients with coronary artery disease) compared treat-to-target statin dosing (LDL-C goal 50–70 mg/dL) with high-intensity statin. Mean 3-year LDL-C was 69.1 vs 68.4 mg/dL and the primary end point occurred in 8.1% vs 8.7%, which met non-inferiority [[Hong2023LODESTAR]].

### lipids · Statins and muscle symptoms
- A CTT individual-participant meta-analysis of 19 double-blind placebo-controlled trials (n = 123,940) found muscle pain or weakness reported by 27.1% on statin vs 26.6% on placebo (RR 1.03, 95% CI 1.01–1.06) over a median 4.3 years [[CTT2022Muscle]].
- In year 1, statins produced a 7% relative increase in muscle symptoms (RR 1.07), an excess of 11 events per 1000 person-years. This means about 1 in 15 statin-group muscle reports was due to the statin. After year 1 there was no significant excess (RR 0.99) [[CTT2022Muscle]].
- The CTT authors concluded that more than 90% of muscle symptom reports by people taking statins were not caused by the statin [[CTT2022Muscle]].
- More intensive regimens (atorvastatin 40–80 mg, rosuvastatin 20–40 mg) had a slightly higher muscle-symptom RR than moderate regimens (1.08 vs 1.03) [[CTT2022Muscle]].

### lipids · Ezetimibe
- IMPROVE-IT randomised 18,144 patients hospitalised for ACS within 10 days to simvastatin 40 mg plus ezetimibe 10 mg or simvastatin 40 mg plus placebo. Median follow-up was 6 years [[Cannon2015IMPROVEIT]].
- In IMPROVE-IT, time-weighted average LDL-C was 53.7 mg/dL (1.4 mmol/L) with the combination vs 69.5 mg/dL (1.8 mmol/L) with simvastatin alone [[Cannon2015IMPROVEIT]].
- The 7-year primary event rate was 32.7% vs 34.7% (absolute difference 2.0 percentage points; HR 0.936, 95% CI 0.89–0.99; P = 0.016). Muscle, gallbladder, liver and cancer adverse effects were similar [[Cannon2015IMPROVEIT]].
- IMPROVE-IT's authors concluded that "lowering LDL cholesterol to levels below previous targets provided additional benefit" [[Cannon2015IMPROVEIT]].
- RACING (3780 patients with ASCVD) compared moderate-intensity statin plus ezetimibe with high-intensity statin alone. The 3-year primary end point occurred in 9.1% vs 9.9%, which met non-inferiority [[Kim2022RACING]].
- In RACING, LDL-C was below 70 mg/dL at 3 years in 72% on combination therapy vs 58% on monotherapy. Discontinuation or dose reduction for intolerance occurred in 4.8% vs 8.2% [[Kim2022RACING]].
- The Zetia label gives ezetimibe 10 mg orally once daily, in combination with a statin or alone when more LDL-C lowering is not otherwise possible, and says to assess LDL-C "as early as 4 weeks after initiating" [[FDA-Zetia]].

### lipids · PCSK9 monoclonal antibodies
- FOURIER randomised 27,564 patients with ASCVD (prior MI, stroke or symptomatic peripheral artery disease) and LDL-C ≥70 mg/dL on statin therapy to evolocumab (140 mg every 2 weeks or 420 mg monthly) or placebo. Median follow-up was 2.2 years [[Sabatine2017FOURIER]].
- At 48 weeks evolocumab lowered LDL-C by 59% versus placebo, from a median of 92 mg/dL (2.4 mmol/L) to 30 mg/dL (0.78 mmol/L) [[Sabatine2017FOURIER]].
- FOURIER's primary end point occurred in 9.8% vs 11.3% (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.79–0.92). The key secondary end point (CV death, MI or stroke) occurred in 5.9% vs 7.4% (HR 0.80, 0.73–0.88) [[Sabatine2017FOURIER]].
- In FOURIER, adverse events (including new-onset diabetes and neurocognitive events) did not differ between groups, except injection-site reactions (2.1% vs 1.6%) [[Sabatine2017FOURIER]].
- A prespecified FOURIER analysis of 25,982 patients found a "highly significant monotonic relationship" between lower achieved LDL-C at 4 weeks and lower risk, down to LDL-C below 0.2 mmol/L. There was no association between achieved LDL-C and safety outcomes [[Giugliano2017FOURIERLDL]].
- In that analysis, 10% of patients reached LDL-C below 0.5 mmol/L (about 19 mg/dL) and 31% reached 0.5 to <1.3 mmol/L [[Giugliano2017FOURIERLDL]].
- FOURIER-OLE (6635 patients, median 5.0 years open label, up to 8.4 years total exposure) reported median LDL-C of 30 mg/dL. Rates of serious adverse events, muscle events, new diabetes, haemorrhagic stroke and neurocognitive events did not exceed placebo rates in the parent trial [[ODonoghue2022FOURIEROLE]].
- In FOURIER-OLE, patients first randomised to evolocumab had a 23% lower risk of CV death (HR 0.77, 0.60–0.99) and a 20% lower risk of CV death, MI or stroke (HR 0.80) than those first randomised to placebo. This "legacy" effect supports starting early [[ODonoghue2022FOURIEROLE]].
- ODYSSEY OUTCOMES randomised 18,924 patients 1 to 12 months after an ACS, on high-intensity or maximum tolerated statin, with LDL-C ≥70 mg/dL (or non-HDL-C ≥100 mg/dL or apoB ≥80 mg/dL), to alirocumab 75 mg every 2 weeks or placebo. Dosing was adjusted blind to target LDL-C 25–50 mg/dL [[Schwartz2018ODYSSEY]].
- Over a median 2.8 years, ODYSSEY's primary end point occurred in 9.5% vs 11.1% (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.78–0.93). Deaths were 3.5% vs 4.1% (HR 0.85, 0.73–0.98) [[Schwartz2018ODYSSEY]].
- In ODYSSEY, the absolute benefit was greater in patients with baseline LDL-C ≥100 mg/dL. Injection-site reactions occurred in 3.8% vs 2.1% [[Schwartz2018ODYSSEY]].
- EVOPACS gave evolocumab in hospital during ACS (308 patients, 78% statin-naive). At week 8, LDL-C fell from 3.61 to 0.79 mmol/L with evolocumab vs 3.42 to 2.06 mmol/L with placebo, and 95.7% vs 37.6% reached LDL-C <1.8 mmol/L [[Koskinas2019EVOPACS]].
- PACMAN-AMI started alirocumab 150 mg every 2 weeks within 24 hours of primary PCI, on top of rosuvastatin 20 mg (300 patients). At 52 weeks, percent atheroma volume changed by −2.13% vs −0.92% (difference −1.21%). This is an imaging (surrogate) result, not a clinical outcome. (Early evidence) [[Raber2022PACMAN]]
- The Praluent label indicates alirocumab "To reduce the risk of major adverse cardiovascular (CV) events (coronary heart disease death, myocardial infarction, stroke, or unstable angina requiring hospitalization) in adults at increased risk". Its listed starting dosage is 75 mg every 2 weeks or 300 mg every 4 weeks subcutaneously [[FDA-Praluent]].

### lipids · Inclisiran (small interfering RNA)
- ORION-10 (1561 patients with ASCVD) and ORION-11 (1617 patients with ASCVD or risk equivalent) gave inclisiran 284 mg or placebo by subcutaneous injection on day 1, day 90 and every 6 months, on top of maximum tolerated statin [[Ray2020ORION]].
- At day 510, inclisiran reduced LDL-C by 52.3% (95% CI 48.8–55.7) in ORION-10 and 49.9% (46.6–53.1) in ORION-11 versus placebo, from baselines of about 105 mg/dL. Injection-site adverse events were more frequent with inclisiran [[Ray2020ORION]].
- The ORION-10/11 trials measured LDL-C lowering, not cardiovascular outcomes. A 2026 review says inclisiran awaits "definitive results from dedicated cardiovascular outcome trials". (Early evidence) [[Ray2020ORION,Sabouret2026]]
- The Leqvio label gives 284 mg "as a single subcutaneous injection initially, again at 3 months, and then every 6 months", "administered by a healthcare professional" [[FDA-Leqvio]].
- The Leqvio label's indication is LDL-C reduction (adults with hypercholesterolemia, and HeFH/HoFH from age 12). It does not list a cardiovascular-event-reduction indication of its own [[FDA-Leqvio]].

### lipids · Bempedoic acid
- CLEAR Outcomes randomised 13,970 statin-intolerant patients with or at high risk of cardiovascular disease to bempedoic acid 180 mg daily or placebo. Median follow-up was 40.6 months [[Nissen2023CLEAR]].
- Baseline LDL-C was 139.0 mg/dL. At 6 months bempedoic acid lowered it by 29.2 mg/dL more than placebo (a 21.1-percentage-point greater percent reduction) [[Nissen2023CLEAR]].
- The four-component MACE end point occurred in 11.7% vs 13.3% (HR 0.87, 95% CI 0.79–0.96). Fatal or non-fatal MI occurred in 3.7% vs 4.8% (HR 0.77, 0.66–0.91). There was no significant effect on stroke, CV death or all-cause death [[Nissen2023CLEAR]].
- Gout (3.1% vs 2.1%) and gallstones (2.2% vs 1.2%) were more common with bempedoic acid, as were small rises in creatinine, uric acid and liver enzymes [[Nissen2023CLEAR]].
- The Nexletol label indicates bempedoic acid to reduce MACE risk in adults at increased risk "who are unable to take recommended statin therapy (including those not taking a statin)". The listed dose is 180 mg orally once daily, with lipids checked within 8 to 12 weeks [[FDA-Nexletol]].

### lipids · LDL-C targets and thresholds, as published
- ESC/EAS (2019, retained in the 2025 focused update), as summarised in a 2026 review: very-high-risk patients, including those with clinical ASCVD, have an LDL-C goal of "<55 mg/dL and ≥50% reduction". "A goal < 40 mg/dL may be considered after a recurrent vascular event within two years despite maximally tolerated therapy." [[Mach2019ESC,Mach2025ESC,Sabouret2026]]
- In mmol/L, the ESC very-high-risk goal is <1.4 mmol/L. A 2026 review describes it as the "stringent ESC/EAS LDL-C targets (≤1.4 mmol/L)" that statin monotherapy often misses after ACS [[Goel2026]].
- A 2026 review states that the ESC/EAS framework "recommends reassessment of LDL-C 4–6 weeks after treatment initiation or intensification, with further escalation when the goal has not been achieved" [[Sabouret2026]].
- The same review says the 2025 ESC/EAS focused update supports "intensive combination therapy during the index hospitalization when statin monotherapy is predictably insufficient" [[Mach2025ESC,Sabouret2026]].
- 2023 ESC ACS guideline, as summarised in a 2026 review: "if LDL-C targets are not reached within 4–6 weeks despite maximally tolerated statin therapy and ezetimibe, the addition of a PCSK9i is recommended" [[Byrne2023ESC,Goel2026]].
- 2018 AHA/ACC: "In very high-risk ASCVD, use a LDL-C threshold of 70 mg/dL (1.8 mmol/L) to consider addition of nonstatins to statin therapy." Very high risk "includes a history of multiple major ASCVD events or 1 major ASCVD event and multiple high-risk conditions" [[Grundy2019ACC]].
- 2018 AHA/ACC: "it is reasonable to add ezetimibe to maximally tolerated statin therapy when the LDL-C level remains ≥70 mg/dL". It goes on to say a PCSK9 inhibitor added after statin plus ezetimibe "is reasonable", noting uncertain long-term safety and low cost-effectiveness "at mid-2018 list prices" [[Grundy2019ACC]].
- 2025 ACC/AHA ACS guideline, as summarised in two 2026 papers: for patients on maximally tolerated statin, nonstatin therapy (ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitor, inclisiran or bempedoic acid) is recommended when LDL-C is ≥70 mg/dL and "may be reasonable if LDL-C ≥55 mg/dL" [[Rao2025ACC,Wong2026JACCAdv,Goel2026]].
- The 2026 ACC/AHA/multisociety dyslipidemia guideline restores explicit LDL-C goals. As summarised in a 2026 review: "In secondary prevention, patients at very high risk should achieve LDL-C below 55 mg/dL and non-HDL-C below 85 mg/dL". A goal below 70 mg/dL applies to ASCVD patients who do not meet very-high-risk criteria [[Blumenthal2026ACC,Sabouret2026]].
- That review concludes that US and European guidelines now "generally support LDL-C below 70 mg/dL in high-risk patients and below 55 mg/dL in very-high-risk patients". The main remaining difference is how patients are assigned to risk categories [[Sabouret2026]].
- ADA Standards of Care 2025 (recommendation 10.28): "For people with diabetes and ASCVD, treatment with high-intensity statin therapy is recommended to obtain an LDL cholesterol reduction of ≥50% from baseline and an LDL cholesterol goal of <55 mg/dL (<1.4 mmol/L)". It adds ezetimibe or a PCSK9 inhibitor if this goal is not reached (grade B) [[ADA2025SOC]].

### secondary · Beta-blockers: reduced ejection fraction
- CAPRICORN randomised 1959 patients with proven acute MI and LVEF ≤40% to carvedilol (6.25 mg, titrated to 25 mg twice daily) or placebo, on top of contemporary therapy that included ACE inhibitors [[Dargie2001CAPRICORN]].
- CAPRICORN's primary end point (death or CV hospitalisation) did not differ (35% vs 37%, HR 0.92, 0.80–1.07). All-cause mortality was lower with carvedilol (12% vs 15%; HR 0.77, 95% CI 0.60–0.98; p = 0.03) [[Dargie2001CAPRICORN]].
- The Coreg label indicates carvedilol "to reduce cardiovascular mortality in clinically stable patients who have survived the acute phase of a myocardial infarction and have a left ventricular ejection fraction of less than or equal to 40%". The post-MI titration starts at 6.25 mg twice daily [[FDA-Coreg]].

### secondary · Beta-blockers: preserved or mildly reduced ejection fraction (the 2024–2025 trials)
- REDUCE-AMI (5020 patients, 95.4% from Sweden; LVEF ≥50% after angiography; open label) compared long-term metoprolol or bisoprolol with no beta-blocker. Over a median 3.5 years, death or new MI occurred in 7.9% vs 8.3% (HR 0.96, 95% CI 0.79–1.16; P = 0.64) [[Yndigegn2024REDUCE]].
- In REDUCE-AMI, all-cause death was 3.9% vs 4.1% and MI 4.5% vs 4.7%. Safety events (bradycardia, AV block, hypotension, syncope, pacemaker) occurred in 3.4% vs 3.2% [[Yndigegn2024REDUCE]].
- REBOOT (Spain and Italy; 8438 patients analysed; LVEF >40%; open label) found the primary outcome (death, reinfarction or HF hospitalisation) in 22.5 vs 21.7 per 1000 patient-years (HR 1.04, 95% CI 0.89–1.22; P = 0.63) over a median 3.7 years [[Ibanez2025REBOOT]].
- BETAMI-DANBLOCK (Norway and Denmark; 5574 patients; LVEF ≥40%; beta-blocker started within 14 days) found death or MACE in 14.2% vs 16.3% (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.75–0.98; P = 0.03) over a median 3.5 years [[Munkhaugen2025BETAMI]].
- In BETAMI-DANBLOCK, new MI occurred in 5.0% vs 6.7% (HR 0.73, 0.59–0.92), while all-cause death was 4.2% vs 4.4% [[Munkhaugen2025BETAMI]].
- An individual-patient meta-analysis of 1885 patients with MI and mildly reduced LVEF (40–49%) and no heart failure, from REBOOT, BETAMI, DANBLOCK and CAPITAL-RCT, found the composite of death, new MI or heart failure at 32.6 vs 43.0 per 1000 patient-years (HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.58–0.97) [[Rossello2025IPD]].
- ABYSS (France; 3698 patients a median 2.9 years after MI, LVEF ≥40%, no event in 6 months) tested stopping versus continuing long-term beta-blockers. The primary outcome occurred in 23.8% vs 21.1% (HR 1.16, 95% CI 1.01–1.33), so stopping was not shown to be non-inferior [[Silvain2024ABYSS]].
- In ABYSS, stopping beta-blockers did not appear to improve quality of life [[Silvain2024ABYSS]].
- All four new trials (REDUCE-AMI, REBOOT, BETAMI-DANBLOCK, ABYSS) were open label, which is relevant when judging softer end points [[Yndigegn2024REDUCE,Ibanez2025REBOOT,Munkhaugen2025BETAMI,Silvain2024ABYSS]].
- The REDUCE-AMI and REBOOT authors note that earlier beta-blocker evidence came from an era before routine reperfusion, invasive care and modern secondary prevention [[Yndigegn2024REDUCE,Ibanez2025REBOOT]].

### secondary · ACE inhibitors, ARBs and ARNI
- SAVE randomised 2231 patients 3–16 days after MI with LVEF ≤40% and no overt heart failure to captopril or placebo. Over an average 42 months, all-cause mortality was 20% vs 25%, a 19% risk reduction (95% CI 3–32%; P = 0.019) [[Pfeffer1992SAVE]].
- In SAVE, captopril also reduced CV death (−21%), severe heart failure (−37%), HF hospitalisation (−22%) and recurrent MI (−25%) [[Pfeffer1992SAVE]].
- An individual-patient overview of three post-infarction ACE inhibitor trials (n = 5966) found mortality of 23.4% vs 29.1% (OR 0.74, 95% CI 0.66–0.83), with fewer HF readmissions (OR 0.73) and reinfarctions (OR 0.80) [[Flather2000]].
- In that overview, the benefits "were observed early after the start of therapy and persisted long term" and did not depend on age, sex or baseline aspirin or beta-blocker use [[Flather2000]].
- HOPE randomised 9297 high-risk patients aged ≥55 (vascular disease or diabetes plus one other risk factor, no known low EF or HF) to ramipril 10 mg daily or placebo for a mean 5 years. MI, stroke or CV death occurred in 14.0% vs 17.8% (RR 0.78, 95% CI 0.70–0.86) [[Yusuf2000HOPE]].
- In HOPE, ramipril reduced MI (9.9% vs 12.3%), stroke (3.4% vs 4.9%) and death from any cause (10.4% vs 12.2%) [[Yusuf2000HOPE]].
- VALIANT randomised 14,703 patients 0.5–10 days after MI complicated by HF and/or LV dysfunction to valsartan, captopril or both. Mortality was similar (valsartan vs captopril HR 1.00). Valsartan met non-inferiority, and the combination added adverse events without improving survival [[Pfeffer2003VALIANT]].
- VALIANT side-effect profiles differed: hypotension and renal dysfunction were more common with valsartan, and cough, rash and taste disturbance with captopril [[Pfeffer2003VALIANT]].
- PARADISE-MI (5661 patients with MI plus reduced LVEF and/or pulmonary congestion) compared sacubitril-valsartan with ramipril. The primary outcome was 11.9% vs 13.2% (HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.78–1.04; P = 0.17), which was not significant [[Pfeffer2021PARADISE]].

### secondary · Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists
- EPHESUS randomised 6632 patients with acute MI complicated by LV dysfunction and heart failure to eplerenone (25 mg titrated to 50 mg/day) or placebo. Over a mean 16 months there were 478 vs 554 deaths (RR 0.85, 95% CI 0.75–0.96; P = 0.008) [[Pitt2003EPHESUS]].
- In EPHESUS, eplerenone reduced sudden cardiac death (RR 0.79, 0.64–0.97). Serious hyperkalaemia was more common (5.5% vs 3.9%) and hypokalaemia less common (8.4% vs 13.1%) [[Pitt2003EPHESUS]].
- The Inspra label indicates eplerenone "to improve survival of stable adult patients with symptomatic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (≤40%) (HFrEF) after an acute myocardial infarction (MI)". It starts at 25 mg once daily, titrates to 50 mg within 4 weeks, and says to "Measure serum potassium before starting INSPRA and periodically thereafter" [[FDA-Inspra]].
- CLEAR SYNERGY (OASIS 9) randomised 7062 patients after MI and PCI to routine spironolactone or placebo, in a factorial design with colchicine. Over a median 3 years there was no reduction in CV death or new/worsening HF (1.7 vs 2.1 per 100 patient-years; HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.69–1.21; P = 0.51) [[Jolly2025CLEAR]].
- CLEAR SYNERGY's second primary composite (MI, stroke, HF or CV death) was 7.9% vs 8.3% (HR 0.96, 0.81–1.13). Serious adverse events were 7.2% vs 6.8% [[Jolly2025CLEAR]].
- Colchicine results from CLEAR SYNERGY, and colchicine more broadly (COLCOT, LoDoCo2), are covered in pass F.

### secondary · SGLT2 inhibitors after MI
- EMPACT-MI randomised 6522 patients hospitalised for acute MI and at risk of heart failure to empagliflozin 10 mg or placebo within 14 days of admission. The primary end point (first HF hospitalisation or death) was 8.2% vs 9.1% (HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.76–1.06; P = 0.21) over a median 17.9 months [[Butler2024EMPACT]].
- In EMPACT-MI, first HF hospitalisation was 3.6% vs 4.7% (HR 0.77, 0.60–0.98) and death from any cause 5.2% vs 5.5% (HR 0.96). Because the primary end point was neutral, the HF result is hypothesis-generating. (Early evidence) [[Butler2024EMPACT]]
- DAPA-MI randomised 4017 patients with acute MI and impaired LV function, without diabetes or chronic HF, to dapagliflozin 10 mg or placebo. The hierarchical win ratio was 1.34 (95% CI 1.20–1.50), driven mainly by cardiometabolic components. The analysis method was changed during the trial because few events accrued [[James2024DAPAMI]].
- In DAPA-MI, CV death or HF hospitalisation was 2.5% vs 2.6% (HR 0.95, 0.64–1.40), with no effect on hard outcomes [[James2024DAPAMI]].
- ADA 2025 (10.42a): "In people with type 2 diabetes and established ASCVD, multiple ASCVD risk factors, or chronic kidney disease (CKD), an SGLT2 inhibitor with demonstrated cardiovascular benefit is recommended" to reduce MACE and/or HF hospitalisation (grade A) [[ADA2025SOC]].
- ADA 2025 (10.42b): "In people with type 2 diabetes and established ASCVD or multiple risk factors for ASCVD, a GLP-1 RA with demonstrated cardiovascular benefit is recommended" [[ADA2025SOC]].
- SELECT randomised 17,604 people with pre-existing cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity, without diabetes, to semaglutide or placebo. The primary CV end point was 6.5% vs 8.0% (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.72–0.90). Discontinuation for adverse events was 16.6% vs 8.2% [[Lincoff2023SELECT]].

### secondary · Influenza vaccination
- IAMI randomised 2571 participants (99.7% shortly after an MI) to inactivated influenza vaccine or saline placebo. It was stopped early because of COVID-19. At 12 months, death, MI or stent thrombosis occurred in 5.3% vs 7.2% (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.52–0.99; P = 0.040) [[Frobert2021IAMI]].
- In IAMI, all-cause death was 2.9% vs 4.9% (HR 0.59, 0.39–0.89) and CV death 2.7% vs 4.5% (HR 0.59). MI alone did not differ significantly (2.0% vs 2.4%) [[Frobert2021IAMI]].
- An earlier meta-analysis of randomised trials (6735 patients) linked influenza vaccine with fewer major CV events (2.9% vs 4.7%; RR 0.64). The effect was larger in patients with a recent ACS (RR 0.45, 0.32–0.63) than in those without (RR 0.94) [[Udell2013]].

### secondary · Polypill
- SECURE randomised 2499 patients within 6 months of MI to a polypill (aspirin 100 mg, ramipril 2.5/5/10 mg, atorvastatin 20/40 mg) or usual care. Over a median 36 months, the primary end point occurred in 9.5% vs 12.7% (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.60–0.96; P = 0.02) [[Castellano2022SECURE]].
- SECURE's key secondary end point (CV death, type 1 MI or ischaemic stroke) was 8.2% vs 11.7% (HR 0.70, 0.54–0.90). Patient-reported adherence was higher with the polypill [[Castellano2022SECURE]].

### secondary · Smoking cessation
- A systematic review of 20 prospective cohort studies of people with coronary heart disease found a 36% reduction in crude relative risk of death for those who quit smoking compared with those who continued (RR 0.64, 95% CI 0.58–0.71) [[Critchley2003]].
- Restricting to 6 higher-quality studies gave RR 0.71 (0.65–0.77). The benefit was consistent across age, sex, type of index cardiac event, country and era [[Critchley2003]].
- These are observational cohort data, so confounding is possible. The authors note many studies did not adequately control for it [[Critchley2003]].
- EVITA randomised 302 smokers hospitalised with ACS to varenicline or placebo for 12 weeks, with low-intensity counselling. At 24 weeks, point-prevalence abstinence was 47.3% vs 32.5% (P = 0.012; number needed to treat 6.8) [[Eisenberg2016EVITA]].
- In EVITA, continuous abstinence was 35.8% vs 25.8% (P = 0.081). Major cardiovascular events within 30 days after the drug was stopped were 4.0% vs 4.6% [[Eisenberg2016EVITA]].
- EVITA's authors report that fewer than one-third of smokers hospitalised with ACS stay abstinent after discharge [[Eisenberg2016EVITA]].

### secondary · Blood pressure and diabetes targets, as published
- ADA 2025 (10.4): "The on-treatment blood pressure goal is <130/80 mmHg, if it can be safely attained" (grade A) for people with diabetes. The ADA notes this goal is consistent with ACC/AHA, ISH and ESC/ESH guidelines [[ADA2025SOC]].
- ADA 2025 also acknowledges "no randomized controlled trial to specifically demonstrate a decreased incidence of cardiovascular events in people with diabetes by achieving a blood pressure <130/80 mmHg" [[ADA2025SOC]].
- SPRINT randomised 9361 people without diabetes at increased cardiovascular risk (including clinical CVD) to a systolic target <120 vs <140 mmHg. The primary composite (MI, other ACS, stroke, HF or CV death) was 1.65% vs 2.19% per year (HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.64–0.89), and all-cause mortality HR was 0.73 [[Wright2015SPRINT]].
- In SPRINT, mean systolic pressure at 1 year was 121.4 vs 136.2 mmHg. Hypotension, syncope, electrolyte problems and acute kidney injury were more common with intensive treatment [[Wright2015SPRINT]].
- Blood pressure targets for people without diabetes after ACS from the 2024 ESC and 2025 AHA/ACC hypertension guidelines were not verified in this pass and are not given here.

### secondary · Adherence and drop-off after MI
- In Ontario adults aged ≥66 starting a statin (1994–1998), 2-year adherence was 40.1% after ACS, 36.1% with chronic CAD and 25.4% in primary prevention [[Jackevicius2002]].
- Among Medicare beneficiaries aged 66–75 who filled a high-intensity statin within 30 days of MI discharge (2007–2012), 58.9% were still taking it with high adherence at 6 months and 41.6% at 2 years. Discontinuation rose from 12.4% to 18.8% [[Colantonio2017]].
- In that Medicare cohort, African American and Hispanic patients and new statin users were less likely to stay on high-intensity statins with high adherence. These differences point to gaps in access and support, not to personal failings [[Colantonio2017]].
- In the PREMIER registry, 184 of 1521 MI patients discharged on aspirin, a beta-blocker and a statin had stopped all three by 1 month [[Ho2006]].
- Those who stopped all three had lower 1-year survival (88.5% vs 97.7%). Discontinuation was independently associated with higher mortality (HR 3.81, 95% CI 1.88–7.72). This is an observational association [[Ho2006]].
- A meta-analysis of 376,162 patients found overall adherence of 57% (95% CI 50–64) after a median 24 months: 66% in secondary prevention and 50% in primary prevention [[Naderi2012]].
- A meta-analysis of 44 prospective studies (1,978,919 participants) found good adherence (≥80%) in 60%. Good versus poor statin adherence was associated with RR 0.85 for CVD and RR 0.55 for all-cause mortality [[Chowdhury2013]].
- In 347,104 US veterans with ASCVD on stable statins, a medication possession ratio below 50% (vs ≥90%) was associated with higher mortality (HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.27–1.34). Patients on high-intensity statins were less adherent than those on moderate intensity (OR 1.18 for moderate) [[Rodriguez2019]].
- MI FREEE randomised insurance plan sponsors (5855 patients after MI) to full coverage of statins, beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors or ARBs. Adherence in the usual-coverage group was only 35.9–49.0%, and full coverage raised it by 4–6 percentage points [[Choudhry2011FREEE]].
- In MI FREEE, the primary outcome did not differ (HR 0.93, 0.82–1.04). First major vascular events fell (11.0 vs 12.8 per 100 person-years; HR 0.86) without higher total spending [[Choudhry2011FREEE]].

### Exam hooks
- CTT: each 1 mmol/L (about 39 mg/dL) LDL-C reduction lowers major vascular events by about 22% (RR 0.78), with no lower threshold seen [[CTT2010]].
- PROVE IT: after ACS, atorvastatin 80 mg beat pravastatin 40 mg (16% hazard reduction; LDL-C 62 vs 95 mg/dL) [[Cannon2004PROVEIT]].
- IMPROVE-IT: adding ezetimibe after ACS gave a 2.0-percentage-point absolute reduction at 7 years (32.7% vs 34.7%) [[Cannon2015IMPROVEIT]].
- FOURIER: evolocumab lowered LDL-C by 59% to a median 30 mg/dL. HR 0.85 for the primary end point [[Sabatine2017FOURIER]].
- ODYSSEY OUTCOMES: alirocumab 1–12 months after ACS, HR 0.85, with deaths 3.5% vs 4.1% [[Schwartz2018ODYSSEY]].
- Inclisiran: about 50% LDL-C lowering with injections at day 1, day 90 and then every 6 months. Its outcome trials were not reported in the sources read [[Ray2020ORION,FDA-Leqvio]].
- CLEAR Outcomes: bempedoic acid in statin intolerance, MACE HR 0.87. Watch for gout and gallstones [[Nissen2023CLEAR]].
- Statin muscle symptoms: more than 90% of muscle reports on statins are not due to the statin [[CTT2022Muscle]].
- ESC/EAS very-high-risk LDL-C goal: <1.4 mmol/L (<55 mg/dL) and ≥50% reduction from baseline [[Mach2019ESC,Sabouret2026]].
- 2018 AHA/ACC: in very-high-risk ASCVD, a 70 mg/dL LDL-C threshold triggers consideration of adding nonstatins [[Grundy2019ACC]].
- Post-MI LVEF ≤40%: carvedilol lowered all-cause mortality (CAPRICORN HR 0.77). SAVE captopril cut mortality 19%. EPHESUS eplerenone RR 0.85 for death [[Dargie2001CAPRICORN,Pfeffer1992SAVE,Pitt2003EPHESUS]].
- Preserved EF after MI: REDUCE-AMI (HR 0.96) and REBOOT (HR 1.04) were neutral for beta-blockers. BETAMI-DANBLOCK (LVEF ≥40%) favoured them (HR 0.85) [[Yndigegn2024REDUCE,Ibanez2025REBOOT,Munkhaugen2025BETAMI]].
- Beta-blockers in mildly reduced EF (40–49%): pooled HR 0.75 [[Rossello2025IPD]].
- VALIANT: valsartan is as effective as captopril after MI with HF/LV dysfunction. Combining them only adds side effects [[Pfeffer2003VALIANT]].
- EMPACT-MI and DAPA-MI: SGLT2 inhibitors after MI did not reduce death or HF hospitalisation as primary hard end points [[Butler2024EMPACT,James2024DAPAMI]].
- IAMI: influenza vaccination soon after MI reduced death, MI or stent thrombosis at 12 months (HR 0.72) and all-cause death (HR 0.59) [[Frobert2021IAMI]].
- SECURE: the aspirin–ramipril–atorvastatin polypill cut MACE versus usual care (HR 0.76) [[Castellano2022SECURE]].
- Quitting smoking after a coronary diagnosis is associated with 36% lower mortality (RR 0.64) [[Critchley2003]].
- Only about 4 in 10 older adults were still adherent to a statin 2 years after ACS (40.1%) [[Jackevicius2002]].

### In plain words
- After a heart attack (myocardial infarction, MI), most people are offered several medicines. Each one lowers the chance of another heart attack in a different way.
- "Bad" cholesterol (LDL cholesterol) helps build the fatty plaque inside heart arteries. In large studies, the lower it went, the fewer heart attacks people had [[CTT2010,Ference2017EAS]].
- A statin is usually the first cholesterol medicine. If cholesterol is still too high, other medicines can be added, such as ezetimibe (a pill) or injections like evolocumab, alirocumab or inclisiran [[Cannon2015IMPROVEIT,Sabatine2017FOURIER,Ray2020ORION]].
- Muscle aches are common in everyone. In careful studies, most aches in people taking statins were not caused by the statin. If you have aches, talk with your care team before stopping [[CTT2022Muscle]].
- Beta-blockers clearly help people whose heart pumps weakly after a heart attack. For people whose pump works normally, recent studies disagree, so your team will weigh your own situation [[Dargie2001CAPRICORN,Yndigegn2024REDUCE,Munkhaugen2025BETAMI]].
- ACE inhibitor medicines (or ARBs) help the heart heal and lower the chance of heart failure, especially after a large heart attack [[Pfeffer1992SAVE,Flather2000]].
- A yearly flu shot after a heart attack was linked to fewer deaths in the following year in a randomised trial [[Frobert2021IAMI]].
- Stopping smoking is one of the strongest steps you can take. In studies, people who quit had about a third lower risk of dying [[Critchley2003]].
- Help to quit works. In one trial, a quit-smoking medicine started in hospital helped more people stay smoke-free [[Eisenberg2016EVITA]].
- Many people stop their heart medicines within a year or two, often because of cost, side effects or confusion. Your team can help with all of these [[Colantonio2017,Choudhry2011FREEE]].
- A "polypill" combines several heart medicines in one tablet. In one trial it made taking medicines easier and lowered heart events [[Castellano2022SECURE]].
- These numbers come from large groups of people. They show what tends to happen on average, not what will happen to you.
- If you think you or someone else is having a heart attack, call your local emergency number now (911 in the US, 999 in the UK, 112 in the EU).

## Open questions and controversies
- Beta-blockers after MI with preserved EF: REDUCE-AMI (LVEF ≥50%, HR 0.96) and REBOOT (LVEF >40%, HR 1.04) found no benefit [[Yndigegn2024REDUCE,Ibanez2025REBOOT]]. BETAMI-DANBLOCK (LVEF ≥40%) found lower death or MACE (HR 0.85), driven by fewer MIs [[Munkhaugen2025BETAMI]]. Benefit looks clearer at LVEF 40–49% (HR 0.75) [[Rossello2025IPD]]. ABYSS found stopping long-term therapy was not non-inferior [[Silvain2024ABYSS]]. Status: unresolved for LVEF ≥50%. Guideline updates after these trials were not verified here.
- Treat-to-target versus "fire-and-forget" high-intensity statin: LODESTAR found treat-to-target (50–70 mg/dL) non-inferior [[Hong2023LODESTAR]]. Both ESC/EAS and the 2026 US guideline now use explicit LDL-C goals [[Sabouret2026]]. Status: converging on goals plus percentage reduction.
- How low and how early: FOURIER showed benefit down to LDL-C <0.2 mmol/L with no safety signal [[Giugliano2017FOURIERLDL]]. The 2025 ESC/EAS update supports in-hospital combination therapy when a statin alone is predictably insufficient [[Sabouret2026]]. No hard-outcome trial was found that tests upfront combination after ACS against a stepwise approach. Status: (Early evidence) for upfront combination.
- Inclisiran: robust LDL-C lowering but, in the sources read, no completed cardiovascular outcome trial. A 2026 review calls it "a practical alternative for selected patients rather than a routine replacement for PCSK9 monoclonal antibodies" [[Sabouret2026,Ray2020ORION]].
- SGLT2 inhibitors after MI in people without diabetes or HF: EMPACT-MI and DAPA-MI were neutral on hard primary end points. There was a signal for fewer HF hospitalisations (EMPACT-MI HR 0.77) and a win-ratio benefit driven by metabolic outcomes (DAPA-MI) [[Butler2024EMPACT,James2024DAPAMI]]. Status: not established for routine post-MI use outside HF, diabetes or CKD indications.
- Routine MRA after MI without HF: EPHESUS showed benefit with LVEF dysfunction and HF [[Pitt2003EPHESUS]]. CLEAR SYNERGY found no benefit from routine spironolactone after PCI for MI [[Jolly2025CLEAR]].
- Influenza vaccine: IAMI was stopped early (2571 of the planned sample), so its effect sizes may be overestimated. It is consistent with an earlier meta-analysis [[Frobert2021IAMI,Udell2013]].
- Adherence: full drug coverage raised adherence by only 4–6 percentage points [[Choudhry2011FREEE]]. The polypill improved adherence and outcomes in one trial [[Castellano2022SECURE]]. Status: which adherence strategies scale is open.
