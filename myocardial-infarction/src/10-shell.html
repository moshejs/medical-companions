Heart Attack Companion · animated and cited

Heart attack, explained

A heart attack happens when part of the heart muscle is starved of blood and oxygen long enough to be damaged, most often because a clot blocks a coronary artery. [[Thygesen2018UDMI4,Badimon2014]] In the US someone has one about every 40 seconds, yet far fewer people die after a heart attack than 30 years ago. [[Martin2025HDSS,Szummer2017STEMI]] This companion shows how it happens, how it is diagnosed and treated, and what recovery looks like, with a source for every number.

Every animation can be driven from the keyboard: Tab to its timeline, then arrow keys to scrub and Enter to play or pause.

Call nowIf you think you or someone else is having a heart attack, call your local emergency number now (911 in the US, 999 in the UK, 112 in the EU). Do not wait to see if it passes, and do not use this guide to decide.

Heart attack (myocardial infarction) at a glance

What it is
A heart attack happens when part of the heart muscle goes without enough blood and oxygen long enough to be damaged. [[Thygesen2018UDMI4]] Most often a clot forms on a fatty plaque inside a heart artery and blocks it. [[Badimon2014]]
Who gets it
The average age at a first heart attack is 65.6 years in men and 72.0 in women. [[Martin2025HDSS]] In the INTERHEART study, blood fats, smoking, stress and diabetes were linked to first heart attacks; many can be changed with support. [[Yusuf2004INTERHEART]]
Main symptoms
Pressure, tightness or discomfort in the chest count, and so does pain in the shoulders, arms, neck, back, upper belly or jaw. [[Gulati2021ChestPain]] About 1 in 5 heart attacks is silent. [[Martin2025HDSS]] The European guideline says the recommended action is to call emergency services rather than go to hospital on your own. [[Byrne2023ESC]]
How it is diagnosed
Guidelines call for an ECG within 10 minutes of first medical contact, but a normal ECG does not rule a heart attack out. [[Byrne2023ESC]] A blood test for troponin, a heart muscle protein, shows damage when it is high and rising or falling. [[Thygesen2018UDMI4,Domienik2021UDMI4]]
Main treatments
For a heart attack with ST elevation on the ECG, reopening the artery by catheter (primary PCI) is preferred within 120 minutes; otherwise clot-dissolving drugs are given. [[Byrne2023ESC]] Afterwards, one antiplatelet medicine usually continues long term [[ISIS2_1988,Yusuf2001CURE,Wallentin2009PLATO,Byrne2023ESC]], and guidelines advise a high-intensity statin. [[Schwartz2001MIRACL,Cannon2004PROVEIT,Grundy2019ACC]]
Outlook
Clot-dissolving drugs saved 65 lives per 1,000 treated in the first hour, against 26 to 29 at 2 to 6 hours. [[Boersma1996]] Survival has improved: in Sweden, death within a year fell from 22.1% to 14.1% between 1995 and 2014. [[Szummer2017STEMI]]

For students

Every module has an Exam hook box and a citation trail. ECG criteria, troponin algorithms and the HEART, TIMI and GRACE calculators are teaching aids, quoted as published. Finish with flashcards, memory aids and a quiz.

For patients, families and caregivers

Look for the In plain words boxes. Heart attacks are common and treatable, and the recovery modules show, with sources, what rehabilitation and the first year can look like. Survival figures are population statistics, not predictions for one person. Bring questions to your own care team: the app reports what labels, guidelines and trials say, not what you should take or change.
