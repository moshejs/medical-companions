Cardiac rehabilitation

Cardiac rehabilitation (CR) is a programme of supervised exercise, teaching and emotional support after a heart attack (myocardial infarction, MI). Follow one schematic programme week by week, see what the trials found, and see who never gets there.

Schematic programme: real programmes differ in length, format and number of sessions. The exercise-capacity curve has no units. What the trials found

Pick a review. Each line is a risk ratio (or hazard ratio) with its 95% confidence interval: left of 1 favours rehabilitation, and a line that crosses 1 means the trials cannot rule out no effect. These are population results from trials, not predictions for one person.

Who gets there? The participation funnel

In US Medicare data from 2016, 366,103 older adults were eligible for CR, after events such as a heart attack or bypass surgery. Choose a group to see how many attended even one session. The Million Hearts Cardiac Rehabilitation Collaborative set a goal of at least 70% participation by 2022. [[Ritchey2020]]

In plain words: many people who could go to rehab never start, and women, older adults and Black and Hispanic patients attend less often. It is fine to ask your care team for a referral. Formats, intensity and value

Home-based

Rehab at home works about as well

In the 2023 Cochrane review (24 trials, 3,046 people; home-based included digital and telehealth programmes), home and centre gave similar mortality (RR 1.19, 0.65–2.16), exercise capacity and completion, at a similar cost per patient. 71 of 77 quality-of-life comparisons showed no significant difference. [[McDonagh2023]]

A 2019 AACVPR/AHA/ACC statement concluded that home-based CR "may be a reasonable option for selected clinically stable low- to moderate-risk patients who are eligible for CR but cannot attend a traditional center-based CR program", with evidence gaps for older adults, women and underrepresented groups. [[Thomas2019HBCR]]

Exercise

How hard? Intensity concepts

The 2024 AHA/AACVPR core components list aerobic exercise training and strength training as separate components, alongside weight management and body composition. [[Brown2024]]

Across 17 trials (953 people), high-intensity interval training (HIIT: short hard bursts with recovery) improved cardiorespiratory fitness more than moderate-intensity continuous training (SMD 0.34, 0.20–0.48), with no deaths or cardiac hospitalisations during training in either arm. Programmes of 7–12 weeks gave the largest gains in coronary artery disease. Early evidence [[Hannan2018]]

Safety

Supervised exercise is very safe

A French registry followed 25,420 patients through 743,471 patient-hours of supervised training: 1 severe cardiac event per 49,565 patient-hours of training and 1 per 8,484 exercise tests, a cardiac arrest rate of 1.3 per million patient-hours, and no deaths. [[Pavy2006]]

Components

More than a gym

The AHA/AACVPR core components are baseline patient assessment, nutritional counselling, risk-factor management (lipids, blood pressure, weight, diabetes, smoking), psychosocial interventions, and physical-activity counselling with exercise training. [[Balady2007]] The 2024 update adds a new core component, programme quality, and covers virtual and remote delivery. It calls high-quality performance essential to improve "widely documented low enrollment and adherence rates". [[Brown2024]]

Modern care

Is rehab still worth it today?

About a quarter of the Cochrane trials ran before today's medicines were widespread, one reason the mortality effect is smaller in modern trials. [[Dibben2026]] Pooling individual data from trials since 2010 (4,975 people, 93.5% after MI), CaReMATCH found fewer hospital admissions (HR 0.68) but no difference in mortality (HR 0.99). [[Stens2026]] Eight economic evaluations consistently found exercise-based CR cost-effective. [[Dibben2026]] Established for fewer admissions and MIs; the mortality benefit in current care is uncertain.

Who gains most

Bigger gains for some

In CaReMATCH, admissions fell more in people with a weaker heart pump (lower LVEF), lower starting fitness, beta-blocker use or previous cardiovascular disease, and quality of life rose more in people who started lower or had less formal education. [[Stens2026]] Programmes of education or counselling without exercise also lowered mortality in a 2005 meta-analysis (RR 0.87). [[Clark2005]]
