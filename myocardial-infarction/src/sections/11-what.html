Part 1 · Understanding heart attack

What a heart attack is

A heart attack (myocardial infarction, MI) is heart muscle damaged because its blood supply failed. Here it is as numbers you can watch: how often it happens, which kinds there are, who it affects, and what happens afterwards.

Call nowIf you think you or someone else is having a heart attack, call your local emergency number now (911 in the US, 999 in the UK, 112 in the EU). Do not wait to see if it passes.

What the word means

In plain words

Muscle starved of blood

A heart attack happens when part of the heart muscle goes without enough blood and oxygen for long enough to be damaged. [[Thygesen2018UDMI4]] Most often a clot forms on a fatty plaque inside a coronary artery and blocks it (see plaque, rupture & clot). [[Badimon2014]]

The definition

Injury plus ischaemia

The Fourth Universal Definition (2018) defines MI as acute myocardial injury, detected by abnormal cardiac biomarkers, in the setting of evidence of acute myocardial ischaemia. Injury means a cardiac troponin value above the 99th-percentile upper reference limit; it is acute when values rise and/or fall. [[Thygesen2018UDMI4,Domienik2021UDMI4]]

Silent attacks

About 1 in 5 goes unnoticed

Of the 805,000 US heart attacks a year, an estimated 170,000 are silent. [[Martin2025HDSS]] In Framingham, more than a quarter of MIs were found only on routine ECGs, and unrecognised MIs were as likely as recognised ones to lead to death, heart failure or stroke. [[Kannel1984Unrecognized]] In ARIC, silent MI went with a higher risk of coronary death (HR 3.06). [[Zhang2016SilentMI]]

Injury or infarction?

A raised troponin shows the heart muscle has been injured. It does not say why. In 372,310 South Australian emergency episodes tested with high-sensitivity troponin, acute MI was the diagnosis in 5.12%, while acute or chronic injury without MI was far more common. Warning signs, women, and MINOCA

Warning signs

More than pain in the chest

The 2021 AHA/ACC chest pain guideline's first message is that chest pain means more than pain in the chest: pressure, tightness or discomfort, and pain in the shoulders, arms, neck, back, upper abdomen or jaw, all count. [[Gulati2021ChestPain]] The 2023 ESC guideline lists breathlessness, epigastric pain and arm, neck or jaw pain as chest pain-equivalent symptoms. [[Byrne2023ESC]] More in symptoms & who presents differently.

Women

Chest pain is the usual symptom for women too

In VIRGO (adults aged 18–55), 87.0% of women and 89.5% of men had chest pain, pressure, tightness or discomfort. More women reported three or more additional symptoms (61.9% vs 54.8%). Before the heart attack, 29.5% of women had sought care for similar symptoms, and 53% of those women said the provider did not think the symptoms were heart-related (37% of men). [[Lichtman2018VIRGO]] The ESC guideline states that the descriptor "atypical" should be avoided. [[Byrne2023ESC]]

MINOCA

A heart attack without a blocked artery

MI with non-obstructive coronary arteries (MINOCA) was found in 6% of MI patients in a review of 28 publications; median age was 55 and 40% were women. On cardiac MRI, only 24% showed a typical infarct and 33% had myocarditis. [[Pasupathy2015]] In the US ACTION registry, MINOCA made up 10.5% of MIs in women and 3.4% in men. [[Smilowitz2017ACTION]]

Younger adults

Not only an older man's problem

In ARIC surveillance, the share of heart attack hospitalisations aged 35–54 rose from 27% (1995–1999) to 32% (2010–2014), with the biggest increase in young women. Young women were less likely than young men to receive lipid-lowering therapy (RR 0.87) or revascularisation (RR 0.79). [[Arora2019ARICyoung]]

Fairness

Where you live shapes risk and care

Lower socioeconomic position is linked to more acute coronary syndrome, less optimal care and higher mortality. Black adults with heart attacks in 2012–2017 were less likely than White adults to have angiography (61.9% vs 73.1%). [[Martin2025HDSS]] Income, education, employment and neighbourhood are consistently linked to cardiovascular disease. [[Schultz2018SES]]

The long view

Far fewer deaths than 30 years ago

In Swedish STEMI care, 1-year mortality fell from 22.1% in 1995 to 14.1% in 2014 while primary PCI rose from 4.5% to 78.0%. [[Szummer2017STEMI]] US Medicare 30-day mortality after heart attack fell from 20.0% to 12.4% between 1995 and 2014. These are population statistics.
