The ECG: STEMI, NSTEMI & territories

The 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) is a tracing of the heart's electrical activity seen from twelve directions. When a coronary artery closes, the leads facing that wall change in a typical order. Pick a wall, then press Play to watch one heart attack (myocardial infarction, MI) evolve on paper.

Call nowIf you think you or someone else is having a heart attack, call your local emergency number now (911 in the US, 999 in the UK, 112 in the EU). The ECG is recorded by the ambulance crew or the hospital; nothing on this page can be used to check yourself.

Which leads look at which wall

Each lead views the heart from one direction, so a group of neighbouring ("contiguous") leads looks at one wall. The usual teaching convention pairs the inferior leads (II, III, aVF) with the right coronary artery, sometimes the circumflex; the anterior leads (V1–V4) with the left anterior descending artery; and the lateral leads (I, aVL, V5, V6) with the circumflex or diagonal branches. Treat this as a rule of thumb: coronary anatomy varies, and the culprit artery is confirmed at angiography.

Some walls are poorly seen by the standard 12 leads. For the back (posterior) wall, the 2023 ESC guideline says ST depression in V1–V3 and/or ST elevation in V7–V9 is "highly suggestive of posterior coronary artery occlusion". [[Byrne2023ESC]] For the right ventricle, UDMI4 treats ST elevation of at least 0.5 mm in the right-sided leads V3R/V4R (at least 1 mm in men under 30) as supportive, and notes that its absence does not exclude right ventricular infarction. [[Thygesen2018UDMI4C]] Among 200 people with an inferior MI, 54% had ST elevation in V4R; the sign detected right ventricular infarction with 88% sensitivity and 78% specificity. [[Zehender1993]]

STEMI criteria explainer: does this ST elevation meet the published threshold?

Teaching toolThis explainer shows how the ST-elevation thresholds in the Fourth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction (UDMI4) depend on the lead, age and sex. It is not a way to read a real ECG and must never be used for self-triage or to decide whether to seek care. Real ECG interpretation weighs the whole tracing, symptoms, old ECGs and repeat recordings.

Sex

Thresholds as published in UDMI4 (Table 2): new or presumed new J-point elevation in two contiguous leads, ≥1 mm in leads other than V2–V3; in V2–V3 ≥2 mm in men aged 40 or over, ≥2.5 mm in men under 40 and ≥1.5 mm in women of any age; V7–V9 0.5 mm (≥1 mm in men under 40); V3R/V4R ≥0.5 mm (≥1 mm in men under 30). 1 mm = one small square at standard calibration (10 mm = 1 mV). [[Thygesen2018UDMI4C]]

STEMI, NSTEMI and the limits of the ECG

Target

An ECG within 10 minutes

The 2023 ESC guideline says the 12-lead ECG should be "obtained immediately upon FMC [first medical contact] and interpreted ... within 10 min", and repeated as needed, especially if symptoms wane. Ambulance staff should record one as soon as possible and interpret or transmit it. UDMI4 sets the same 10-minute target. [[Byrne2023ESC,Thygesen2018UDMI4C]]

In 676 emergency department patients with STEMI at 10 US sites, the median door-to-ECG time was 7 minutes, but 37.9% waited longer than 10; those who waited were more often women and less often reported chest pain. [[Yiadom2022]] See Nursing care.

Classification

STEMI or NSTE-ACS: a working diagnosis

The ESC classifies the working diagnosis of acute coronary syndrome as STEMI or non-ST-elevation ACS using the clinical information available, including the ECG. [[Byrne2023ESC]] A normal ECG does not rule a heart attack out: the ECG may be normal in more than one-third of patients with NSTE-ACS, which is why troponin testing follows. [[Byrne2023ESC]]

Posterior MI

The wall the 12 leads miss

The back wall is seen "in the mirror" by V1–V3. The ESC calls ST depression in V1–V3 and/or ST elevation in V7–V9 "highly suggestive of posterior coronary artery occlusion". UDMI4 recommends a 0.5 mm ST-elevation cut-off in V7–V9 (≥1 mm in men under 40). [[Byrne2023ESC,Thygesen2018UDMI4C]]

LBBB & paced rhythm

When ST elevation cannot be judged: Sgarbossa

The ESC says bundle branch block or a paced rhythm "precludes an accurate assessment" of ST elevation; with high clinical suspicion these patients are managed like a clear STEMI. [[Byrne2023ESC]] UDMI4 says ST elevation ≥1 mm concordant with the QRS may indicate ischaemia in LBBB. [[Thygesen2018UDMI4C]]

  • Sgarbossa (GUSTO-1, 131 of 26,003 patients with LBBB, 0.5%): concordant ST elevation ≥1 mm, ST depression ≥1 mm in V1–V3, discordant ST elevation ≥5 mm. [[Sgarbossa1996]]
  • Smith's modification swaps the ≥5 mm rule for an ST/S ratio ≤ −0.25 and needs one criterion: sensitivity 91% vs 52%, specificity 90% vs 98% in derivation; 80% vs 49% and 99% vs 100% in validation. [[Smith2012,Meyers2015]]
Early evidence

Occlusion MI (OMI): beyond the millimetres

Established In a meta-analysis of 40,777 people with NSTEMI, 25.5% had a totally occluded culprit artery, mostly inferolateral (40% right coronary, 33% circumflex), with higher short-term mortality (RR 1.67). [[Khan2017]]

In 808 patients, STEMI criteria were 41% sensitive and 94% specific for acute occlusion; blinded reading for predefined OMI findings was 86% sensitive and 91% specific. [[Meyers2021OMI]] In DIFOCCULT, reviewers reclassified 28.2% of the NSTEMI group as acute occlusion, with higher mortality. [[Aslanger2020]] ST depression maximal in V1–V4 was 97% specific for OMI. [[Meyers2021STD]]

Hypothesis Replacing STEMI/NSTEMI with occlusion vs non-occlusion MI has not been adopted by any guideline as of November 2026. [[Meyers2021OMI,Byrne2023ESC]]
