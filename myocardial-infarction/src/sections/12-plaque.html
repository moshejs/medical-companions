Part 1 · Understanding heart attack

Plaque, rupture & clot

A heart attack (myocardial infarction, MI) usually starts decades before it happens, as cholesterol builds up in the wall of a coronary artery. Follow one plaque from a teenage fatty streak to the minutes when its surface breaks and a clot blocks the artery. [[Badimon2014,Strong1999PDAY]]

Rupture, erosion and calcified nodule

Two plaque events cause most coronary clots: rupture, the most common, and erosion. A third, the calcified nodule, is rare and is found in heavily calcified, tortuous arteries of older people. [[Falk2013,Bentzon2014]] How often each one is found depends on how it is looked for: autopsy after sudden death, or imaging inside the artery (optical coherence tomography, OCT) in living patients, where some lesions stay unclassified.

In men who died suddenly, the total-to-HDL cholesterol ratio was 8.5 with rupture, 5.0 with erosion and 5.5 with stable plaque, and smoking was a risk factor in 75% of those with an acute thrombus against 41% with stable plaques. The authors concluded that abnormal cholesterol predisposes to rupture and smoking to acute thrombosis. [[Burke1997]]

Inflammation, cholesterol and the debates

Established

Inflammation drives plaque

Atherosclerosis is an inflammatory disease in which macrophages and T lymphocytes play a dominant role. [[Falk2013]] In CANTOS, 10,061 people with a past MI and hs-CRP ≥2 mg/L were given canakinumab, an antibody against interleukin-1β, or placebo. At 150 mg every 3 months it lowered nonfatal MI, nonfatal stroke or cardiovascular death (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.74–0.98; 3.86 vs 4.50 events per 100 person-years) without lowering lipids. It raised fatal infections and did not change all-cause mortality. [[Ridker2017CANTOS]] More in Research toward better treatment.

Established

Colchicine trials

Low-dose colchicine 0.5 mg daily lowered ischaemic events after a recent MI (COLCOT: 5.5% vs 7.1%, HR 0.77) and in chronic coronary disease (LoDoCo2: 6.8% vs 9.6%, HR 0.69). Across 5 trials it reduced MI by 22% with no difference in all-cause death. Net benefit is still debated. [[Tardif2019COLCOT,Nidorf2020LoDoCo2,Fiolet2021Colchicine]] These are trial descriptions, not dosing advice.

Established

Why lowering LDL protects the artery

LDL retained in the wall is the starting point. [[Tabas2007]] Genetic, epidemiological and trial evidence "unequivocally establishes that LDL causes ASCVD". [[Ference2017EAS]] In 26 statin trials, each 1.0 mmol/L lower LDL cholesterol meant about 22% fewer major vascular events. [[CTT2010]] Early evidence After an MI, adding alirocumab to a high-intensity statin shrank plaque volume slightly more on imaging (PACMAN-AMI), a surrogate result. [[Raber2022PACMAN]] See Lowering LDL cholesterol.

Established

It starts young

Intravascular ultrasound of 262 young donor hearts found coronary atherosclerosis in 51.9%: 17% of donors under 20 and 85% of those 50 or older, with similar prevalence in men and women. [[Tuzcu2001]] PDAY concluded that prevention must begin in childhood or adolescence. [[Strong1999PDAY]] Risk factors are in Risk factors & prevention.

Debated

Beyond the "vulnerable plaque"

Autopsy and imaging link events to thin-cap fibroatheromas. [[Virmani2006,Narula2013]] Yet in PROSPECT, plaque burden and lumen area predicted events as strongly as thin-cap appearance, and half of later events came from the original culprit sites. [[Stone2011PROSPECT]] Libby argues that thinking has moved "beyond the 'vulnerable plaque' concept". [[Libby2021Nature]]

Early evidence

Clonal haematopoiesis (CHIP)

An expanded clone of blood cells carrying acquired mutations was linked to 1.9 times the risk of coronary heart disease and 4.0 times the risk of early-onset MI. In mice, Tet2-deficient bone marrow caused larger lesions. [[Jaiswal2017CHIP]] Libby calls CHIP a common, age-related contributor to risk that was not recognised before. [[Libby2021Nature]]

Early evidence

Bleeding inside the plaque

Red-cell membranes and iron build up in late-stage and thin-cap plaques, adding free cholesterol and macrophages and enlarging the core. [[Kolodgie2003]] Fragile, leaky new vessels growing into the plaque help enlarge the core, and a bleed can expand a plaque quickly. [[Badimon2014,Falk2013]]

Early evidence

Erosion without a stent?

In the single-centre, uncontrolled EROSION study, 60 patients with an OCT-diagnosed erosion had antithrombotic drugs and no stent; 78.3% had more than a 50% reduction in thrombus volume at 1 month. [[Jia2017EROSION]] Erosion is hard to diagnose without intracoronary imaging. Treatment of the blocked artery is in Reperfusion.
