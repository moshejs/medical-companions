Symptoms & who presents differently

A heart attack (myocardial infarction, MI) most often feels like pressure or discomfort in the chest, but it can spread, come with other symptoms, or arrive with no chest pain at all. Watch where it is felt, then switch the lens to see what the large studies found for women, men, older people and people with diabetes.

Chest pain means more than pain in the chest

The Fourth Universal Definition of MI describes ischaemic symptoms as "various combinations of chest, upper extremity, mandibular, or epigastric discomfort during exertion or at rest", and adds that these symptoms are not specific to the heart. [[Thygesen2018UDMI4C]] The 2023 ESC guideline lists breathlessness (dyspnoea), upper-belly (epigastric) pain, and pain in the left or right arm or neck and jaw as chest pain-equivalent symptoms. [[Byrne2023ESC]] The 2021 AHA/ACC chest pain guideline's first message makes the same point: pressure, tightness or discomfort count, and so does pain in the shoulders, arms, neck, back, upper abdomen or jaw. [[Gulati2021ChestPain]]

The ESC names two red-flag patterns: "prolonged chest pain (>15 min) and/or recurrent pain within 1 h". Its advice on what to do is quoted as published: "The recommended action should be to contact the EMS rather than to self-present." [[Byrne2023ESC]] What happens after the call is covered in The first minutes.

More to know

Silent MI

Heart attacks nobody noticed

In Framingham, more than 25% of heart attacks were found only on routine ECGs every two years, and almost half of those had been silent. They were as likely as recognised ones to lead to death, heart failure or stroke. [[Kannel1984Unrecognized]] In ARIC, silent MI made up more than 45% of new heart attacks and was linked to higher coronary death (HR 3.06). [[Zhang2016SilentMI]] Of an estimated 805,000 US heart attacks a year, about 170,000 are silent. [[Martin2025HDSS]]

Delay

Hours, not minutes

In the ARIC communities, 49.5% of people in 2000 took 4 hours or more from symptom onset to hospital arrival, with no real change since 1987; women and Black patients had longer delays, which reflects access and systems as well as individual choices. [[McGinn2005]] A review of 42 studies found women and older people more likely to arrive later. [[Nguyen2010]] The AHA calls patient delay "the major factor limiting delivery of definitive treatment". [[Moser2006]]

Awareness

Knowing to call, not knowing the signs

In US survey data, 94.9% of adults in 2017 knew to call 9-1-1 for a heart attack, but only 50.2% knew all five common symptoms: jaw, neck or back discomfort; weakness or light-headedness; chest discomfort; arm or shoulder discomfort; and shortness of breath. [[Fang2019MMWR]]

In the emergency department

When the system misses it

In 10,689 US emergency patients with possible cardiac ischaemia, 2.1% of the 889 with acute MI were sent home by mistake. Being sent home was more likely for women under 55 (OR 6.7), nonwhite patients (OR 2.2), people whose main symptom was breathlessness (OR 2.7) and people with a normal or nondiagnostic ECG (OR 3.3). [[Pope2000]] That is why the ECG and troponin tests matter: see the ECG and troponin.

Without chest pain

Which symptoms carried more risk

In the GRACE registry, 8.4% of people with acute coronary syndrome had no chest pain; 23.8% of them were not first recognised as having one, and their hospital mortality was 13% vs 4.3%. Among them, fainting or near-fainting (OR 2.0), nausea or vomiting (OR 1.6) and breathlessness (OR 1.4) as the main symptom were linked to higher mortality. [[Brieger2004]]

History alone

Symptoms cannot settle it

In a JAMA review, about 10% of people with acute chest pain turned out to have acute coronary syndrome, and history, examination and ECG alone could neither confirm nor exclude it. Pain spreading to both arms was among the more useful features (likelihood ratio 2.6). [[Fanaroff2015]] Other causes of chest pain are in heart attack or something else?
