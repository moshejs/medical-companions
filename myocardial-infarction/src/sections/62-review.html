Cards tagged memory aid are short phrases written for this guide to hold a published fact in mind; they are not official groupings from the ESC, ACC/AHA or a drug label. Cards tagged score acronym or official staging use the published names. None replaces the source. Doses, time targets and label wording are quoted as published, not as instructions.

Mnemonic in motion: ten, one-twenty, ten, thirty

For ST-elevation MI (STEMI), the 2023 ESC guideline sets a chain of time targets: an ECG within 10 minutes of first medical contact, primary PCI if it can be done within 120 minutes of the ECG diagnosis, otherwise clot-dissolving drugs within 10 minutes of diagnosis, and a door-in to door-out time of 30 minutes or less when a patient is transferred [[Byrne2023ESC]]. Play the animation to follow one blocked artery through each target, with the animal and trial data that explain why minutes matter. More detail is in and .

Schematic, not to scale. "Ten, one-twenty, ten, thirty" is a memory aid, not ESC wording. These are targets for health systems, quoted as published: they are not a test for deciding whether to call for help. [[Byrne2023ESC,Reimer1977,Boersma1996]]

These cards are for learning. ECG criteria, troponin algorithms and risk scores are teaching tools, never for self-triage or for deciding whether to seek care. Survival and mortality figures are population statistics from large groups, not predictions for any one person. Do not stop or change heart medicines without talking to the team who prescribed them [[FDA-Brilinta,Mehran2013]].

Sources

Every reference behind the Heart Attack Companion: journal articles, Cochrane and other systematic reviews, guidelines, drug labels and trial registrations. The small numbers throughout the app point here; filter by research area or search.

Eight research passes ran in parallel, one per area: epidemiology, burden, types and history; atherosclerosis, plaque rupture, thrombosis, ischaemia and risk factors; presentation, ECG, troponin, risk scores and the differential diagnosis; the first minutes, emergency services and reperfusion; antithrombotic therapy; complications, cardiogenic shock, prognosis and research; secondary prevention medicines and LDL lowering; and cardiac rehabilitation, living after a heart attack and nursing care. Before inclusion each source was checked, where possible, against PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, ClinicalTrials.gov or openFDA, and the green tag on an entry names the service that confirmed it. Works found by more than one pass appear once, with any extra keys listed beside them.

Entries marked %t:sources.check% are pages with no PMID or DOI, or works that could not be confirmed. They are not cited for any fact in this guide.
