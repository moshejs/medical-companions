Chest pain: heart attack or something else?

Several conditions can feel like a heart attack (myocardial infarction, MI), and some are just as dangerous. Step through a chest map of the main ones, what sets each apart, and which test helps, then compare the tests condition by condition.

Call nowIf you think you or someone else is having a heart attack, call your local emergency number now (911 in the US, 999 in the UK, 112 in the EU). Telling these conditions apart needs an emergency team and its tests.

More to know

Takotsubo · InterTAK

"Broken heart syndrome"

In the InterTAK registry of 1750 patients, 89.8% were women (mean age 66.8). Physical triggers (36.0%) were more common than emotional ones (27.7%), and 28.5% had no evident trigger. Compared with matched people with acute coronary syndrome, neurological or psychiatric disorders were more common (55.8% vs 25.7%) and ejection fraction lower (40.7% vs 51.5%), while severe in-hospital complications were similarly frequent. Over long-term follow-up, major cardiac and cerebrovascular events ran at 9.9% and death at 5.6% per patient-year. [[Templin2015]]

The InterTAK consensus estimates takotsubo at about 1–3% of everyone, and 5–6% of women, presenting with suspected STEMI. [[Ghadri2018]] Under the universal definition it is classed as another cause of myocardial injury, not as MI. [[Domienik2021UDMI4]]

SCAD

A tear inside a heart artery, often in younger women

The AHA describes spontaneous coronary artery dissection (SCAD) as an important cause of acute coronary syndrome, MI and sudden death, particularly in young women and people with few conventional risk factors, linked to pregnancy, stress triggers and fibromuscular dysplasia. [[Hayes2018]] In the Canadian cohort of 750 patients, mean age was 51.8, 88.5% were women and 33.9% had no cardiac risk factors; emotional stress was reported by 50.3%, and fibromuscular dysplasia was found in 31.1%. 84.3% were treated without a procedure, and 30-day major events occurred in 8.8%. [[Saw2019]]

MINOCA

A heart attack with open arteries

MI with no obstructive coronary arteries (MINOCA) was found in 6% of patients with MI in a systematic review (median age 55, 40% women), with 12-month mortality of 4.7% vs 6.7% in MI with obstructive disease. [[Pasupathy2015]] The AHA treats it as a working diagnosis with many causes that each need evaluation. [[TamisHolland2019MINOCA]] UDMI4 puts it at 6–8% of MI, more often in women and in NSTEMI. [[Thygesen2018UDMI4C]]

Not the heart

Most chest pain is something else

Among 10,689 US emergency patients with possible cardiac ischaemia, 55% had noncardiac problems, 21% other cardiac problems and 17% acute cardiac ischaemia. [[Pope2000]] In a JAMA review, about 10% of people with acute chest pain finally had an acute coronary syndrome, and history, examination and ECG alone neither confirmed nor excluded it. [[Fanaroff2015]] That is why the tests on the ECG and troponin pages matter.

Raised troponin, no MI

Injury is not always infarction

The 2023 ESC guideline lists myocarditis, sepsis, takotsubo cardiomyopathy and heart valve disease among causes of myocardial injury, and states that a single raised value alone is not MI. UDMI4 adds heart failure and kidney disease. [[Byrne2023ESC,Thygesen2018UDMI4C]]
