Living after a heart attack

The year after a heart attack (myocardial infarction, MI) brings going home, getting active again, travel, intimacy, work and a lot of feelings. Scroll through a first-year calendar, see how common low mood and worry are, and keep your own recovery checklist.

In plain words: feeling low, worried or on edge after a heart attack is common. These are health problems, not weakness, and they can be treated. [[Thombs2006,Edmondson2012]] In 2014 the American Heart Association recommended that depression be treated as a risk factor for poorer outcomes after an acute coronary syndrome. [[Lichtman2014]]

Recovery, one topic at a time

Depression

Why low mood matters

About 1 in 5 people have major depression after a heart attack, and it goes with higher mortality, partly explained by how severe the heart disease is. [[Thombs2006,Meijer2011,Meijer2013]] An AHA science advisory recommends screening for depressive symptoms in coronary heart disease. [[Lichtman2008]] A systematic review found no trial showing that screening itself improves outcomes. [[Thombs2008]]

Treatment helps mood: sertraline (SADHART) and cognitive behavioural therapy (ENRICHD) improved depression, but neither has been shown to improve cardiac survival. [[Glassman2002,Berkman2003]]

Family & carers

Partners feel it too

In the HAT trial, 15% of spouses were depressed and 19% anxious at entry, and this did not change over 2 years. [[Thomas2011HAT]] In young patients (VIRGO), 21.2% had low perceived social support, which went with worse mental health, quality of life and depressive symptoms at 12 months. [[Bucholz2014]] The family-based, nurse-coordinated EUROACTION programme improved diet and blood pressure control. [[Wood2008EUROACTION]]

Partners and families are often anxious or low too. Their wellbeing matters and support is available. [[Thomas2011HAT]]

Women

Women's recovery

VIRGO followed 3,572 people aged 18–55 after a heart attack (67.1% women). [[Smolderen2015]] Young women more often had a lifetime history of depression (48% vs 24%) and depressive symptoms on admission (39% vs 22%). [[Smolderen2015]] Their health status stayed lower than men's at 1 and 12 months [[Dreyer2015]], and they had more coronary-related and non-cardiac hospital stays in the first year. [[Sawano2023]] At 12 months, 85% of young women and 89% of young men were back at work, a gap that disappeared after adjustment. [[Dreyer2016RTW]]

Work

Going back to work

Across 43 studies, 81.1% returned to work after a coronary event; return was lower with less education, a weaker heart pump, manual work and heavy physical workload. [[Kai2022]] In Denmark, 91.1% of working-age people were back within a year of a first MI, but 24.2% of them had left work a year after returning; depression (OR 1.77) predicted leaving. [[Smedegaard2017]]

Driving

Driving: check the rules where you live

Whether and when you can drive again depends on where you live, the kind of licence you hold (a car, or a bus or lorry) and the treatment you had. This guide does not quote any country's rules: check your local licensing rules and ask your team what applies to you. UK audits found that written driving advice was often missing from discharge summaries, and targeted teaching improved it. [[Bharaj2021,Dyson2025]]

Travel

Flying

In a randomised trial of 38 people who flew commercially 2 weeks after a heart attack, there was 1 major end point (a brief, symptom-free ischaemic episode on a heart monitor), and supplemental oxygen made no difference to minor events. The authors concluded that neither oxygen nor a medical escort was needed, provided people did not overexert themselves around the flight. Early evidence [[Roby2002]]

Intimacy

Sex after a heart attack

Sexual activity carries a brief rise in relative risk of MI (RR 2.70), but the absolute increase is small: 2–3 MIs per 10,000 person-years for one extra hour a week. Each extra weekly session of habitual exercise cut the triggering risk by about 45%. [[Dahabreh2011]] An AHA and ESC nursing consensus recommends routine assessment and sexual counselling for patients and partners, noting that erectile dysfunction is about twice as common in men with cardiovascular disease. [[Steinke2013]]

The nitroglycerin label states: "Do not use NITROSTAT in patients who are taking PDE-5 Inhibitors, such as avanafil, sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil hydrochloride. Concomitant use can cause severe hypotension, syncope, or myocardial ischemia." [[FDA-Nitrostat]]

Habits

Smoking and everyday habits

Stopping smoking after a coronary diagnosis was linked to a 36% lower risk of death (RR 0.64) compared with continuing. [[Critchley2003]] In EUROASPIRE V, at least 6 months after their event, 19% of coronary patients still smoked, 38% were obese and 66% were active for less than 30 minutes 5 times a week. [[Kotseva2019]] Rehab helps with all of these: see Cardiac rehabilitation.

Memory

Memory and thinking

In 7,888 adults aged 50 and over followed for a median of 12 years, a new coronary event was followed by faster decline in global cognition, verbal memory and orientation over the years after it, with no sudden drop just after the event. Early evidence [[Xie2019]]
