Nursing care

What the literature says a nurse does for a person with gout: assess the joint, give the medicines safely, teach, and keep urate on target. Every figure here is quoted from guidelines, drug labels and trials, not from local practice. Where the literature is silent, this module says so.

The joint

What the literature describes

A self-report flare definition needs at least 3 of 4 items: the person says it is a flare, pain at rest above 3 on a 0–10 scale, at least one swollen joint, and at least one warm joint. In 509 patients at 17 sites it was 85% sensitive and 95% specific. Maximal pain within 24 hours and resolution within 14 days are both features scored by the 2015 classification criteria. 143,292

Exam hookA flare is 3 of 4: says it is a flare, pain at rest above 3/10, a swollen joint, a warm joint. 143
In plain wordsAsk the person, then look and feel: is the joint swollen, is it warm, and how bad is the pain when they are still?

Measuring it: tools with published validity

Validated
Flare definition
  • 3 of 4: the person says it is a flare; pain at rest above 3 on 0–10; at least one swollen joint; at least one warm joint.
  • 85% sensitive, 95% specific, accuracy 92%, in 509 patients at 17 sites.

143

OMERACT filter
Pain scales
  • 0–4 Likert, 0–10 numeric rating or 0–100 mm visual analogue scale all pass for acute gout.
  • Swelling and tenderness on a 4-point Likert scale pass too.

404

What to measure
OMERACT core domains
  • Acute gout: pain, joint swelling, joint tenderness, patient global assessment, activity limitation.
  • Chronic gout: serum urate, flares, tophus burden, health-related quality of life, activity limitation, pain, patient global assessment.

360

Patient-reported
Gout Impact Scale
  • 5 scales, each scored 0–100, where higher means worse.
  • Two-week test–retest reliability 0.77–0.89 in 308 patients.
  • The minimally important difference is 5–8 points.

164,202

Function
HAQ-DI
  • Valid for physical function in gout, with floor effects.
  • A 3-option rescoring fits the Rasch model better.
  • Scores correlated with days of sick leave (r² = 0.44).

402

Tophus size
Vernier caliper
  • Longest diameter; a mean 0.45 mm difference between observers.
  • Correlates with CT volume, r = 0.91.
  • Calipers and ultrasound met most of the OMERACT filter among 8 methods.

69,75

2 · Giving the medicines

Five checks come before any dose. Pick a medicine or a situation and see which checks it changes. The detail comes from the US labels and ACR 2020. 115,117,125,127,135

Situation

Five checks, and the ones this situation changes (marked !). Drawn from the labels and guidelines, not a local protocol.

Colchicine for a flare

What the label and guideline say

The US label gives 1.2 mg at the first sign of a flare, then 0.6 mg one hour later, a maximum of 1.8 mg in one hour. Someone already on prophylaxis can take the same flare dose, wait 12 hours, then restart the prophylactic dose. ACR 2020 strongly recommends low-dose over high-dose colchicine. Strong CYP3A4 or P-glycoprotein inhibitors raised exposure by more than 125%, so the published flare-dose cuts are 33–66%. 117,135,409

Exam hookGut effects were 26% at the low dose, 77% at the old high dose and 20% on placebo; diarrhoea (23%) was the commonest reaction. 117

3 · The nurse-led treat-to-target loop

In the Nottingham trial, 517 people with a recent flare were randomised to nurse-led care or usual GP care for 2 years. The nurses explained gout, shared the decision, and raised the dose until urate was on target. The loop below repeats until it is. 95

Month 0

A first visit, not a prescription

Trained nurses gave a holistic assessment, discussed what the person believed about gout, explained its nature, causes, associations, consequences and treatment options, shared the decision and gave a written booklet. Once fully informed, 99% chose urate-lowering therapy. 95

4 · What to teach, and what gets misunderstood

EULAR 2016 makes education an overarching principle: people with gout should be told about the disease and its treatments, and taught to treat a flare at the first warning symptoms. These are the misunderstandings the studies record. 333

What the literature says

It lowers urate over months; it does not treat a flare

ACR 2020 strongly recommends colchicine, an NSAID or a glucocorticoid for the flare itself, and treat-to-target dose titration of urate-lowering therapy for the long term. The two jobs use different medicines. 135

Ask: "Which tablet is for the pain, and which one is for the crystals?"

Staying on it

Points are the published values; the lines only join them.

Adherence

Most people stop

Among 48,280 UK people starting allopurinol, 38.5% had a first 90-day gap by 1 year and 56.9% by 5 years, so 61.5% and 43.1% were still persisting; 61% were non-adherent overall. After a gap, 43.3% restarted within a year. A meta-analysis of 13 studies pooled 46% adherent. 353,354

Exam hookPersistence falls fast: 61.5% still on allopurinol at 1 year, 43.1% at 5 years. 353
In plain wordsMore than half of people have stopped their urate tablet within five years. People who feel they understand gout are more likely to keep taking it.
What the effective model taught
  • The nature, causes, associations and consequences of gout, and the treatment options, with a written booklet and a shared decision.
  • What the person believes about gout, discussed rather than corrected.
  • ACR 2020's patient panel asked for a "medication-in-pocket" flare plan for people who recognise early signs.

95,135

What people do not know
  • In 240 US respondents, seafood, beef, pork and beer were recognised as triggers by only 23%, 22%, 7% and 43%.
  • Only 12% of those on urate-lowering therapy knew flares can worsen when it starts.
  • Poor health literacy (9.4% of 551 primary-care patients) was linked to 2 or more flares a year (adjusted OR 4.10).

159,429

Beliefs change outcomes
  • In 142 people with gout for under 10 years, adherence rose with perceived understanding and fell with perceived severity, and negative perceptions predicted worse disability at 1 year.
  • Beliefs about consequences predicted all-cause mortality (HR 1.18) in 242 people.

74,364

Asking about adherence
  • EULAR's points to consider: discuss adherence regularly, assess it without blame, build trust and tailor the solution. Non-adherence in rheumatic diseases runs at 30–80%.
  • EULAR's patient-education recommendations (eight, four with strength-A evidence) cover when, by whom and how education is given.

335,446

Other people who can run the loop
  • A 1463-patient site-randomised trial of pharmacist-led, mostly automated telephone care improved adherence (50% vs 37%) and urate below 6 mg/dL (30% vs 15%) at 1 year; the adherence gain faded in year 2.
  • A 77-patient pharmacist telephone trial reached urate at or below 6 mg/dL in 35% vs 13% at 26 weeks.
  • ACR 2020 says the key is that the treating provider, who could be the physician, educates and treats to target where such staff are not available.

135,148,264

Shame and stigma
  • A synthesis of 45 qualitative studies (1203 patients) found five themes: denying illness, negotiating uncertainty, juggling competing priorities, minimising symptoms and lifestyle restrictions, and developing strategies and empowerment.
  • ACR 2020 aimed to give lifestyle guidance "without implying any patient-blaming", given gout's strong genetic determinants.

135,416

5 · In hospital and after surgery

Risk
A tenfold rise
  • In 429 people with gout the flare rate rose about tenfold during a hospital stay (RR 10.2), and an in-hospital flare added 1.8 days to the stay.

458

Prediction
The GOUT-36 rule
  • Four pre-admission items: no flare prophylaxis, no urate-lowering therapy, a tophus, and urate above 0.36 mmol/L (6 mg/dL) in the past year.
  • 75% sensitive and 67% specific for inpatient flares; in validation, 80% of very-high-risk patients flared against 11% of low-risk patients.

183

After surgery
Days 1 to 17
  • 31 of 70 people (44.3%) with known gout flared a mean 3.7 days after surgery; 61.3% were monoarticular, 83.9% in the lower limb, and only 13% in the first MTP joint.
  • Pre-operative urate of 9 mg/dL (535 µmol/L) or more had OR 3.77; taking allopurinol had OR 0.15.
  • In an older series, 52 of 302 flared 1–17 days after admission, almost all with fever.

65,184

Evidence gap
Keep the urate tablet going?
  • Regular pre-admission urate-lowering therapy was linked to a 10% shorter stay in 36,047 New Zealand admissions (observational).
  • No trial has tested continuing or starting urate-lowering therapy during an admission.

182,183

6 · What goes in the notes

The nurse-led trial recorded flare diaries, urate at every titration contact, doses and changes, side-effects and reasons for stopping, tophus number, sites and largest diameter, the SF-36 and Gout Impact Scale, and every visit or call. The monitoring plan

Month 12 · For good. Urate-lowering therapy continued indefinitely (ACR 2020, conditional); after stopping, only 13% were flare-free at 5 years. 135

Measures with published validity
  • The 3-of-4 flare definition, pain on an NRS or VAS, the Gout Impact Scale, HAQ-DI and caliper tophus diameter all have published validity for the record.

Pregnancy
  • BSR 2023 asks that the risks and benefits of drug treatment in pregnancy be discussed and clearly documented by every professional involved (GRADE 1A).

What the record is for
