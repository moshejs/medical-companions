## Findings

### t2t · A smartphone-supported, physician-supervised treat-to-target model
- 280 men with gout were randomised 1:1 in a 12-month, single-centre (Shanghai), open-label superiority RCT. One arm used an integrated digital care model (the "1CARE" platform: automated behavioural reminders, tiered education, remote serum urate monitoring and weekly physician review); the other received usual care. (Early evidence) [[Gao2026]]
- At 12 months, 85.0% (119/140) of the app-led group reached serum urate < 360 µmol/L [≈ 6 mg/dL, converted], compared with 48.6% (68/140) on usual care (risk ratio 1.75, 95% CI 1.46–2.10; P < 0.0001). (Early evidence) [[Gao2026]]
- Annualised flare frequency was lower with the app-led model (0.02 vs 0.10 flares/year; P < 0.01). Medication adherence was higher, and exploratory glucose, HbA1c, total cholesterol and LDL fell more. Adverse event rates were comparable. (Early evidence) [[Gao2026]]
- Limits stated or evident: single centre, men only, open-label, and the authors call for multi-centre trials of generalisability and cost-effectiveness. No trial registration number is shown in the PubMed record. [[Gao2026]]
- Context: this agrees with the nurse-led treat-to-target RCT already cited [[Doherty2018]]. The common factor is structured follow-up with serial urate checks and dose titration; the delivery channel differs.

### links · Gout and lower-limb amputation in type 2 diabetes
- In a Danish nationwide case-control study nested in 601,541 people with type 2 diabetes, 4912 people with lower-extremity amputation were compared with 39,101 age- and sex-matched controls. Gout was identified by ICD-10 code or urate-lowering therapy dispensing. (Early evidence) [[Roikjer2026]]
- Gout was associated with amputation: OR 1.85 (95% CI 1.69–2.02) unadjusted and OR 1.53 (1.37–1.69) after adjustment for diabetes duration, peripheral arterial disease, myocardial infarction and late diabetic complications. (Early evidence) [[Roikjer2026]]
- There was a trend across amputation levels (p = 0.03), from OR 1.37 (1.12–1.68) for above-knee to OR 1.65 (1.32–2.05) for toe amputation, but individual levels did not differ significantly. (Early evidence) [[Roikjer2026]]
- Observational, with possible residual confounding (for example kidney disease severity and foot-ulcer care), so it shows association, not cause.

### Exam hooks
- Treat-to-target works through structured follow-up. Both a nurse-led model (UK) and a smartphone-supported, physician-supervised model (China, 85.0% vs 48.6% at SUA < 360 µmol/L at 12 months) beat usual care. [[Doherty2018,Gao2026]]
- In type 2 diabetes, coexisting gout carried an adjusted OR of 1.53 for lower-extremity amputation in a Danish nationwide study. [[Roikjer2026]]

### In plain words
- Regular reminders, home urate checks and a doctor who reviews the results every week helped far more people reach their urate goal than usual care did.
- People with both diabetes and gout had more foot and leg amputations than people with diabetes alone. Foot checks matter even more when both conditions are present.

## Open questions and controversies
- Generalisability of app-led T2T beyond a single urban Chinese centre and to women, and its cost-effectiveness: untested [[Gao2026]].
- Gout and amputation: is the association causal (tophaceous or inflamed foot tissue, impaired ulcer healing) or confounded by CKD and vascular disease? [[Roikjer2026]] Status: open.
