# H · Research & toward a cure

Pass date: 2026-10-20. Modules served: cure (with hand-offs to t2t and ult for the remission and implementation figures).

## Findings

### cure · What "cure" means in gout
- Gout is a disease of monosodium urate (MSU) crystal deposits, and those deposits are reversible: long-term urate-lowering therapy dissolves MSU crystals and so prevents flares and tophi (Established) [[Dalbeth2021,Dalbeth2019NRDP]].
- EULAR 2016 frames the aim explicitly as cure: keep serum urate below 6 mg/dL (360 µmol/L), and below 5 mg/dL (300 µmol/L) in severe gout, "to achieve the predefined urate target to cure the disease" (Established) [[Richette2017]].
- ACR 2020 strongly recommends treat-to-target dose titration guided by serial serum urate, with a target below 6 mg/dL (360 µmol/L) (Established) [[FitzGerald2020]].
- Crystal clearance from joint fluid: in 18 people with crystal-proven gout (11 knees, 7 first metatarsophalangeal joints) whose serum urate was brought to normal, MSU crystals disappeared from the synovial fluid of all 18 joints, taking 3 to 33 months; the time correlated with gout duration (rs = 0.71, p < 0.01). Median crystal count fell from 7.5 to 3 per 400× field within 3 months (Established) [[Pascual2007]].
- Tophus shrinkage: in 63 people with crystal-proven tophaceous gout, the velocity of tophus reduction was linearly related to the mean serum urate on treatment; the lower the urate, the faster the shrinkage. Benzbromarone alone or combined with allopurinol shrank tophi faster than allopurinol alone, and the two drugs were equally effective once optimal urate levels were reached (Established) [[PerezRuiz2002]].
- Cure in this sense is crystal clearance with sustained urate lowering. It is not a one-off event. Stopping urate-lowering therapy lets the urate pool rebuild (see "Recurrence after stopping") [[PerezRuiz2006,FitzGerald2020]].

### cure · Remission criteria and how often remission is reached
- Preliminary remission criteria (2016): 88 gout experts were invited and 49 responded (56%) to a three-round Delphi plus a 1000Minds discrete-choice exercise. They reached consensus on five domains: serum urate (98% agreement), flares (96%), tophi (92%), pain (83%) and patient global assessment (93%). Thresholds were serum urate < 0.36 mmol/L, pain < 2/10 and patient global < 2/10, each measured at least twice. The time window split 51% for 6 months and 49% for 1 year, and the discrete-choice exercise favoured 12 months (Established) [[deLautour2016]].
- Concurrent validity on dual-energy CT (DECT): among people taking allopurinol ≥ 300 mg/day, 23 (15.1%) met the preliminary criteria. DECT urate deposits were found in 44% of those in remission vs 73.6% of those not in remission (OR 0.28, p = 0.004). Median DECT urate volume was 0.00 vs 0.08 cm³. So some crystal deposition can remain in people who meet remission criteria (Established) [[Dalbeth2019ART]].
- The G-CAN definition of clinical remission (2025/2026) simplifies the criteria to three domains over 12 months: no gout flares, no subcutaneous tophi, and serum urate < 0.36 mmol/L (6 mg/dL). It was supported by 98% of G-CAN members and endorsed by the G-CAN Board. It is called "clinical" remission because no domain measures complete crystal dissolution (Established) [[TabiAmponsah2026]].
- Remission rises with years of treatment. In the CARES trial population (post hoc, simplified definition), remission went from 37.4% (1,593/4,259) at year 1 to 63.1% (322/510) at year 6, and 59.4% reached it at least once (Established) [[TabiAmponsah2025,White2018]].
- In the NOR-Gout treat-to-target cohort (n = 211; 95.3% male), the simplified definition was met by 7.7% at year 1, 45.4% at year 2 and 58.6% at year 5. The 2016 preliminary definition was met by 4.6%, 22.1% and 42.8% at the same points (Established) [[Uhlig2025]].
- In the nurse-led care trial (n = 517), the simplified definition was met by 17.6% at year 1 and 42.7% at year 2 across all participants. Nurse-led care raised the odds of remission at year 2 (OR 11.88, 95% CI 7.49–18.84, simplified definition; OR 7.92 with the 2016 definition) (Established) [[TabiAmponsah2024,Doherty2018]].
- A pragmatic RCT in the Netherlands (GO TEST Overture, n = 308) compared treat-to-target with symptom-driven care. Remission in months 18–24 was 39.4% (57/145) vs 24.0% (39/163), an absolute difference of 15.4 percentage points (95% CI 6.4–24.4). Adverse events were 42% vs 53% (Established) [[Moses2026]].
- Under intensive urate lowering with pegloticase (MIRROR post hoc), the three-criteria G-CAN-style definition was met by 36.7% (33/90) at week 24 and 70.3% (52/74) at week 52 among those still on treatment. A stricter six-criteria definition was met by 13.3% and 43.2% (Early evidence) [[Klionsky2025]].

### cure · Recurrence after stopping urate-lowering therapy
- In a prospective withdrawal study, people whose average serum urate was < 5.05 mg/dL (300 µmol/L) on treatment and < 8.75 mg/dL (520 µmol/L) after stopping had the longest symptom-free period, more than 4 years. The authors proposed 5-year intermittent rather than lifelong therapy (Early evidence) [[PerezRuiz2006]].
- ACR 2020 summarises the same case series this way: only 13% (27/211) of patients whose serum urate stayed < 7 mg/dL (420 µmol/L) off treatment were flare-free over 5 years, and higher off-treatment urate meant more flares. ACR therefore conditionally recommends continuing urate-lowering therapy indefinitely over stopping it (very low certainty) (Established guideline position) [[FitzGerald2020]].
- GO TEST Finale is a pragmatic RCT that plans to randomise at least 278 people who have been in remission for more than 12 months. One arm continues treat-to-target (serum urate < 0.36 mmol/L); the other tapers to stop and restarts on flare. The primary outcome is the proportion not in remission in the last 6 months of 24 months' follow-up. It is the first trial of its kind (Hypothesis under test) [[Peeters2023]].
- STING (France, NCT06956885) plans to randomise people to continue or withdraw urate-lowering therapy according to ultrasound features. It is listed as phase 2, not yet recruiting, estimated n = 450, with the primary outcome being the proportion with ≥ 1 flare at 2 years (Hypothesis under test) [[NCT06956885]].

### cure · Route 1, production: xanthine oxidase inhibitors
- Allopurinol is first-line urate-lowering therapy, started at ≤ 100 mg/day and titrated to target (Established) [[FitzGerald2020,Richette2017]].
- Tigulixostat (non-purine xanthine oxidase inhibitor, LG Chem), phase 2 (n = 143, 12 weeks, colchicine prophylaxis for all): serum urate < 5.0 mg/dL (300 µmol/L) at week 12 in 47.1% (50 mg), 44.7% (100 mg) and 62.2% (200 mg) vs 2.9% on placebo (P < 0.0001). Mean serum urate change was −38.8% to −61.8%. Flares needing rescue occurred in 9.4–13.2% and adverse events in 50.0–56.8% across groups (Early evidence) [[Terkeltaub2023]].
- The tigulixostat phase 3 programme (EURELIA) uses the same primary endpoint in both trials: serum urate < 6.0 mg/dL sustained at months 4, 5 and 6. EURELIA 1 is placebo-controlled (100, 200 or 300 mg, 6 months; planned n = 350). EURELIA 2 compares against allopurinol titrated up to 800 mg plus placebo, for up to 12 months (planned n = 2542) [[Saag2025]].
- Registry status: EURELIA 1 (NCT05586958) is completed with actual enrolment 354 and primary completion November 2024. EURELIA 2 (NCT05586971) is completed with actual enrolment 2202 and primary completion May 2025. Neither has posted results, and no peer-reviewed phase 3 results were found on 2026-10-20 (Early evidence) [[NCT05586958,NCT05586971]].
- Tigulixostat (as IBI128, Innovent) is in a phase 3 trial against febuxostat in China (NCT07414394). It is active and not recruiting, with actual enrolment 600, start March 2026, and primary endpoint serum urate < 360 µmol/L at week 24 (Early evidence) [[NCT07414394]].
- Topiroxostat (non-purine xanthine oxidoreductase inhibitor used in Japan), phase 3 (n = 206 randomised, 16 weeks): serum urate fell by 36.3% on topiroxostat 120 mg/day vs 34.3% on allopurinol 200 mg/day. This met non-inferiority (95% CI −5.3 to 1.3%), and adverse events were similar (Established in Japan) [[Hosoya2016]].

### cure · Route 2, excretion: selective URAT1 inhibitors (uricosurics)
- Lesinurad (first selective URAT1 inhibitor), CLEAR 1 (n = 603) and CLEAR 2 (n = 610), 12 months, added to allopurinol in people above target. Serum urate < 6.0 mg/dL at month 6 was reached by 54.2% and 59.2% (lesinurad 200 and 400 mg) vs 27.9% on allopurinol alone in CLEAR 1, and by 55.4% and 66.5% vs 23.3% in CLEAR 2. Lesinurad gave no significant benefit on flare rate or complete tophus resolution. Serum creatinine rose ≥ 1.5× baseline in 5.9% (200 mg), 15.0% (400 mg) and 3.4% (allopurinol alone) (Established, historical) [[Saag2017,Bardin2017]].
- FDA withdrew approval of Zurampic (lesinurad) 200 mg tablets (NDA 207988) in a March 2021 notice, after the applicant reported that the product was no longer marketed [[FR-2021-05368]].
- Dotinurad, Japan. Against febuxostat (n = 203, forced titration to 2 mg vs 40 mg over 14 weeks), serum urate fell by 41.82% vs 44.00% (difference −2.17%, 95% CI −5.26 to 0.92; non-inferior). Against benzbromarone (n = 201, dotinurad 2 mg vs benzbromarone 50 mg, 14 weeks), it fell by 45.9% vs 43.8% (non-inferior). Dotinurad was approved for hyperuricaemia and gout in Japan in 2020 (Established in Japan) [[Hosoya2020a,Hosoya2020b,Sun2025]].
- Dotinurad, China phase 3 (n = 451 randomised, 441 analysed). Serum urate ≤ 6.0 mg/dL at week 24 was reached by 73.6% on dotinurad 4 mg vs 38.1% on febuxostat 40 mg (adjusted difference 35.9%, 95% CI 27.4–44.4%). At week 12, dotinurad 2 mg was non-inferior to febuxostat 40 mg (55.5% vs 50.5%). Adverse event rates were similar (Established in China; registry NCT05007392, completed, results posted) [[Sun2025,NCT05007392]].
- Dotinurad, US and Europe (Crystalys Therapeutics), all recruiting. Against allopurinol, NCT07089875: phase 3, estimated n = 500, primary endpoint serum urate < 6.0 mg/dL at week 24. In tophaceous gout, NCT07089888: phase 3, estimated n = 250, primary endpoint serum urate < 5.0 mg/dL at week 24. In people intolerant of xanthine oxidase inhibitors or after uricase failure, NCT07535034: phase 2, estimated n = 90 (Early evidence outside Asia) [[NCT07089875,NCT07089888,NCT07535034]].
- AR882 (Arthrosi Therapeutics) has five gout trials registered:
  - Phase 2b (NCT05119686): completed, n = 140, primary endpoint serum urate < 6 mg/dL after 6 weeks.
  - Phase 2 in tophaceous gout, alone or with allopurinol (NCT05253833): completed, n = 42, primary endpoint serum urate < 5 mg/dL at month 3.
  - Two placebo-controlled phase 3 trials, both completed: AR882-301 (NCT06846515, actual n = 866, US) and AR882-302 (NCT06439602, actual n = 818, US, Australia, New Zealand, Taiwan, Hong Kong). Both use serum urate < 6 mg/dL at month 6 as the primary endpoint.
  - None has posted results, and no peer-reviewed efficacy results were found in PubMed or Europe PMC (Early evidence) [[NCT05119686,NCT05253833,NCT06846515,NCT06439602]].
- Epaminurad (URC102, JW Pharmaceutical), phase 2b (n = 169, 12 weeks). Serum urate < 0.36 mmol/L at week 4 was reached by 88.89% (9 mg), 71.79% (6 mg) and 54.05% (3 mg) vs 0.00% on placebo, with 84.21% in the febuxostat 80 mg reference arm. Adverse events and creatinine were no different from placebo. Phase 3 EPIC (NCT05815901, Korea, vs febuxostat) is completed with actual n = 612; its primary endpoint is serum urate < 6 mg/dL at the last 3 time points to week 24, and no results are posted or published (Early evidence) [[Jun2025,NCT05815901]].
- Verinurad (AstraZeneca) was combined with allopurinol in its later trials "to prevent verinurad-induced urate nephropathy". SAPPHIRE (CKD, n = 861, phase 2b) did not reduce albuminuria at 34 weeks or slow eGFR decline at 60 weeks, although serum urate fell dose-dependently. AMETHYST (heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, n = 159) lowered serum urate by 59.6% vs 37.6% (allopurinol) and 0.8% (placebo) without changing peak VO2 or symptoms. No gout phase 3 trial is registered (route shelved after negative non-gout trials; Early evidence for urate lowering only) [[Heerspink2024,Kitzman2024]].

### cure · Route 3, breakdown: uricase enzymes
- Humans lack a working uricase. Recombinant PEGylated uricase (pegloticase) converts urate to allantoin. In two replicate 6-month RCTs (n = 225; serum urate ≥ 8.0 mg/dL [476 µmol/L]; allopurinol intolerant or refractory), plasma urate < 6.0 mg/dL in months 3 and 6 was reached by 42% on pegloticase 8 mg every 2 weeks, 35% on monthly dosing and 0% on placebo (pooled) (Established) [[Sundy2011]].
- The pegloticase label carries a boxed warning for anaphylaxis, infusion reactions and G6PD-deficiency haemolysis and methaemoglobinaemia. In the pre-marketing trials, anaphylaxis occurred in 6.5% (every 2 weeks) and 4.8% (every 4 weeks), and infusion reactions in 26% and 41% vs 5% on placebo. The current recommended dose is 8 mg IV every 2 weeks co-administered with oral methotrexate 15 mg weekly, with pegloticase alone reserved for people in whom methotrexate is unsuitable (Established) [[FDA-Krystexxa]].
- MIRROR RCT (n = 152; pegloticase plus methotrexate 15 mg/week vs plus placebo, randomised 2:1). Month-6 response was 71.0% vs 38.5% (difference 32.3%, 95% CI 16.3–48.3%). Infusion reactions were 4.2% vs 30.6%, and antidrug antibodies were lower with methotrexate. Registry NCT03994731: phase 4, completed, results posted (Established) [[Botson2023,NCT03994731]].
- Pegloticase dosing studies (Amgen): every 4 weeks vs every 2 weeks, both with methotrexate (NCT06229145): phase 4, completed, actual n = 306, results posted. Subcutaneous vs IV, both with methotrexate (NCT07388498): phase 3, recruiting, estimated n = 270 [[NCT06229145,NCT07388498]].
- SEL-212 / NASP (pegadricase plus nanoencapsulated sirolimus, formerly "ImmTOR") aims to induce immune tolerance to the uricase. In the phase 2 COMPARE trial against pegloticase, SU < 6 mg/dL for ≥ 80% of months 3 and 6 was reached by 53.0% vs 46.0% (P = 0.181). Stomatitis occurred in 9.6% with SEL-212 and none with pegloticase (Early evidence) [[Baraf2024]].
- DISSOLVE I (NCT04513366, US, actual n = 112) and DISSOLVE II (NCT04596540, global, actual n = 153) are completed phase 3 trials with results posted. Pooled, 265 people received high-dose NASP, low-dose NASP or placebo every 4 weeks for 6 doses. Serum urate response in weeks 21–24 was 51% (high dose) and 43% (low dose) vs 8% on placebo (P < 0.0001). Infusion reactions within 1 hour occurred in 4% of NASP-treated people, and stomatitis in 9.2% (high dose), 3.4% (low dose) and 0% (placebo). Flares fell after week 12 with NASP (Early evidence: phase 3 positive, not approved as of this pass) [[Baraf2026,NCT04513366,NCT04596540]].
- PRX-115 (Protalix, PEGylated recombinant uricase) is in a phase 2 trial (RELEASE, NCT07280156), dosed with or without methotrexate vs placebo. It is recruiting in the US, Georgia and Israel, with estimated n = 150 and primary endpoint serum urate responders (< 6 mg/dL) during month 6 (Early evidence) [[NCT07280156]].
- ALLN-346 (oral engineered urate oxidase that acts in the gut): in uricase-deficient mice it lowered plasma urate by 44% and 28% under two feeding schedules and normalised urinary urate. The human phase 2a trial (NCT04987294) was terminated for "Company Financing" after enrolling 19 (Early evidence, stalled) [[Pierzynowska2020,NCT04987294]].
- Gene and RNA uricase replacement: lipid nanoparticles delivering circular RNA that encodes a secreted uricase gave sustained urate reduction, less tissue inflammation and fibrosis, and preserved kidney function in male mouse models. There are no human data (Hypothesis) [[Du2026]].

### cure · Route 4, inflammation: NLRP3 and IL-1
- MSU crystals activate the NALP3 (NLRP3) inflammasome. Caspase-1 then releases IL-1β, and mice lacking inflammasome parts or the IL-1 receptor show impaired crystal-induced neutrophil influx (Established) [[Martinon2006]].
- Canakinumab (anti-IL-1β), β-RELIEVED I/II (n = 230 vs 226, frequent flares, NSAIDs and colchicine unsuitable). 72-hour pain was 25.0 mm vs 35.7 mm with triamcinolone acetonide 40 mg (difference −10.7 mm). New-flare risk was 62% lower in the core studies (HR 0.38, 95% CI 0.26–0.57) and 56% lower over 24 weeks (HR 0.44). Serious adverse events were 8.0% vs 3.5% (Established) [[Schlesinger2012]].
- Canakinumab regulatory status:
  - US: FDA approved the gout flare indication as BLA 125319/S-107 in August 2023, for adults in whom NSAIDs and colchicine are contraindicated, not tolerated or inadequate, and in whom repeated corticosteroid courses are not appropriate. The dose is 150 mg SC, with at least 12 weeks before re-treatment (Established) [[FDA-BLA125319-S107,IlarisPIG]].
  - EU: the EPAR lists "gouty arthritis" among authorised indications (Established) [[EMA-Ilaris]].
- Firsekibart (GenSci048, earlier called genakumab; human anti-IL-1β IgG4) was approved by China's NMPA in June 2025 for gouty arthritis in adults who cannot use, do not tolerate or do not respond to NSAIDs and/or colchicine, and for whom repeated corticosteroids are unsuitable (Established in China) [[Crescioli2026]].
- Firsekibart phase 3 trial (NCT05983445): completed, n = 313, single 200 mg SC dose vs compound betamethasone. The co-primary endpoints were 72-hour pain and time to first new flare at 12 weeks.
  - Results: pain reduction at 72 hours was non-inferior (−57.09 vs −53.77 mm). New flares occurred in 10.9% vs 65.2% at 12 weeks and 14.7% vs 66.5% at 24 weeks (311 analysed) [[Crescioli2026,NCT05983445]].
  - By week 48 of the open-label extension, flare recurrence was 44.9% vs 74.8% (HR 0.30, 95% CI 0.223–0.408), and treatment-emergent adverse events were 89.7% vs 89.1% [[Zhu2026]].
- Firsekibart vs etoricoxib 120 mg (phase 2, n = 123, NCT05936268): firsekibart was superior on 72-hour pain change (difference −10.91 mm, 95% CI −18.11 to −3.72). Treatment-emergent adverse events were 77.0% vs 51.6%, most commonly hypertriglyceridaemia (Early evidence) [[Kong2025]].
- A second Chinese anti-IL-1β antibody, anflekitug (SSGJ-613), had its marketing application accepted by the NMPA in June 2025. Its phase 3 trial (about 500 people) was reported to meet both endpoints (Early evidence; reported in a review, primary paper not found) [[Crescioli2026]].
- Anakinra, phase 2 RCT (n = 165; 100 or 200 mg/day for 5 days vs triamcinolone 40 mg). It was not superior on the primary endpoint (pain change at 24–72 hours −41.2 vs −39.4, P = 0.688) but was favoured on most secondary endpoints. It is not labelled for gout in the US, and ACR 2020 conditionally recommends an IL-1 inhibitor when standard flare treatments fail or cannot be used (Early evidence; off-label) [[Saag2021,KineretPI,FitzGerald2020]].
- Rilonacept (IL-1 trap) as flare prophylaxis when starting allopurinol (phase 3, n = 241, 16 weeks): flares per patient were 1.06 on placebo vs 0.29 (80 mg) and 0.21 (160 mg), and ≥ 1 flare occurred in 46.8% vs 18.8% and 16.3%. Gout is not among its US labelled indications (Early evidence for gout; not approved for gout) [[Schumacher2012,ArcalystPI]].
- Dapansutrile (oral NLRP3 inhibitor, OLT1177), open-label phase 2a (34 enrolled, 29 per-protocol, 8 days). Target-joint pain fell by 52.4–68.4% by day 3 and by 68.9–84.2% by day 7 across doses of 100–2000 mg/day, with no placebo arm. The phase 2/3 placebo-controlled trial (NCT05658575) is recruiting (estimated n = 300; primary endpoint joint pain at 72 hours vs placebo; sites in the US, France, Israel, the Netherlands and Spain) (Early evidence) [[Kluck2020,NCT05658575]].
- DFV890 (Novartis oral NLRP3 inhibitor) has first-in-human safety data. Its registered phase 2 trials cover familial cold autoinflammatory syndrome, knee osteoarthritis, COVID-19, myeloid diseases and inflammatory markers, and none is in gout as of 2026-10-20 (Hypothesis for gout) [[DFV890]].
- IL-1 and NLRP3 blockade treat or prevent flares; they do not remove crystals. Only lowering urate dissolves the deposit (Established) [[Dalbeth2019NRDP,Dalbeth2021]].

### cure · Prevention research: before the first flare
- Hyperuricaemia alone rarely becomes gout quickly. In 18,889 gout-free people from four cohorts, the 15-year cumulative incidence of gout was 1.1% at baseline serum urate < 6 mg/dL (360 µmol/L) and 49% at ≥ 10 mg/dL (595 µmol/L). Adjusted hazard ratios rose from 2.7 (6.0–6.9 mg/dL) to 64 (≥ 10 mg/dL) (Established) [[Dalbeth2018ARD]].
- Silent crystal deposits are common in hyperuricaemia:
  - DECT found urate deposits in 6/25 (24%) of people with asymptomatic hyperuricaemia (≥ 540 µmol/L), 11/14 (79%) with early gout and 16/19 (84%) with late gout, and volumes were lower in hyperuricaemia [[Dalbeth2015]].
  - In the TIGER cohort (n = 269, serum urate ≥ 0.48 mmol/L, no prior flares), 38.7% had at least one definite double contour and/or tophus on ultrasound (Established) [[Stewart2026US]].
- FREED (Japan, n = 1070 elderly people with asymptomatic hyperuricaemia, serum urate > 7.0 to ≤ 9.0 mg/dL, 36 months). Febuxostat lowered the composite of cerebral, cardiovascular, renal events and death (HR 0.750, 95% CI 0.592–0.950), driven by renal impairment (16.2% vs 20.5%). Gout flares occurred in 6 (1.1%) vs 14 (2.6%) (P = 0.069) (Early evidence) [[Kojima2019]].
- Urate lowering did not protect the kidney in two RCTs (Established):
  - CKD-FIX (n = 369, stage 3–4 CKD, no gout): eGFR change was −3.33 vs −3.23 mL/min/1.73 m² per year with allopurinol vs placebo (P = 0.85) [[Badve2020]].
  - PERL (n = 530, type 1 diabetes): serum urate fell from 6.1 to 3.9 mg/dL with allopurinol, but GFR after washout did not differ (0.001 mL/min/1.73 m²) [[Doria2020]].
- ACR 2020 conditionally recommends against urate-lowering therapy for asymptomatic hyperuricaemia, judging that benefits would not outweigh costs or risks for most people unlikely to progress to gout (Established guideline position) [[FitzGerald2020]].
- SGLT2 inhibitors as gout-prevention candidates (Early evidence, observational):
  - Type 2 diabetes without prior gout: gout incidence was 4.9 vs 7.8 per 1000 person-years with SGLT2 inhibitors vs GLP-1 agonists (HR 0.64, 95% CI 0.57–0.72; n = 295,907) [[Fralick2020]].
  - People with gout and type 2 diabetes: recurrent flare rate was 52.4 vs 79.7 per 1000 person-years with SGLT2 inhibitors vs DPP-4 inhibitors (rate ratio 0.66, 95% CI 0.57–0.75) [[McCormick2023]].
  - The first randomised test in gout, SAVE-Care (NCT06674109, phase 4, recruiting, estimated n = 60), has serum urate change as its primary endpoint [[NCT06674109]].
- No gout vaccine or other preventive biological approach has a registered trial or published human data. Searches of ClinicalTrials.gov and Europe PMC on 2026-10-20 found none, so it is not listed as a route.

### cure · Implementation gap: the cure exists but rarely arrives
- UK (CPRD, 2012): of prevalent gout patients, only 48.48% were being consulted or treated for gout, and of these 37.63% received urate-lowering therapy. Of incident cases, 18.6% started urate-lowering therapy within 6 months and 27.3% within 12 months. Adherence was 39.66% in 2012 (Established) [[Kuo2015]].
- Catalonia (SIDIAP, 94,759 incident gout cases, 2012–2023, median follow-up 5.44 years): 37.30% never received urate-lowering therapy, and only 12.40% achieved good control (serum urate < 6 mg/dL for ≥ 80% of follow-up). There was no improvement over time (Established) [[Pou2026]].
- When care is organised the gap closes: nurse-led treat-to-target care reached serum urate < 360 µmol/L at 2 years in 95% vs 30% with usual GP care (RR 3.18), at £5066 per QALY gained (Established) [[Doherty2018]].
- The number of people with gout is projected to rise from 55.8 million in 2020 to 95.8 million in 2050, so the gap grows in absolute terms (Established) [[GBD2024Gout]].

### Exam hooks
- Gout is "curable" in the sense that keeping serum urate < 6 mg/dL (360 µmol/L) dissolves MSU crystals. Synovial-fluid crystals cleared in all 18 joints studied, within 3–33 months, and the time was longer with longer disease [[Pascual2007]].
- Lower urate means faster tophus shrinkage. The relation is linear [[PerezRuiz2002]].
- G-CAN clinical remission = 12 months with no flares, no tophi and serum urate < 6 mg/dL. It does not certify that crystals are gone [[TabiAmponsah2026,Dalbeth2019ART]].
- The four interception points are production (xanthine oxidase: allopurinol, febuxostat, tigulixostat, topiroxostat), excretion (URAT1: dotinurad, AR882, epaminurad; lesinurad withdrawn), breakdown (uricase: pegloticase plus methotrexate, NASP) and inflammation (NLRP3: dapansutrile; IL-1β: canakinumab, firsekibart). Only the first three remove urate [[Dalbeth2019NRDP,Martinon2006]].
- Methotrexate with pegloticase roughly doubles month-6 response (71.0% vs 38.5%) and cuts infusion reactions from 30.6% to 4.2% [[Botson2023]].
- Pegloticase is contraindicated in G6PD deficiency (haemolysis, methaemoglobinaemia) [[FDA-Krystexxa]].
- Canakinumab became the first FDA-approved biologic for gout flares in August 2023, in people for whom NSAIDs, colchicine and repeated steroids are unsuitable [[FDA-BLA125319-S107,IlarisPIG]].
- Allopurinol does not slow CKD progression in people without gout (CKD-FIX, PERL) [[Badve2020,Doria2020]].
- Only about half of people with serum urate ≥ 10 mg/dL develop gout over 15 years [[Dalbeth2018ARD]].

### In plain words
- Gout happens when urate builds up and forms tiny crystals in and around joints.
- If urate stays low enough for long enough, the crystals slowly melt away. This can take months to a few years.
- Being "in remission" means no flares, no lumps (tophi) and a low urate level for a whole year.
- Many people reach remission after a few years of steady treatment. Fewer reach it in the first year.
- Stopping the medicine often lets urate climb back, and flares can return.
- New medicines in testing work in three ways: some make less urate, some help the kidneys flush more out, and some break it down with an enzyme.
- Other medicines calm the flare by blocking the alarm signal (IL-1). They ease pain but do not remove crystals.
- The biggest problem today is not a missing drug. Most people with gout never get treated to a low enough urate level.

## Open questions and controversies
- Why do only some people with hyperuricaemia form crystals and flares? About half of people with serum urate ≥ 10 mg/dL stay gout-free for 15 years, which implies factors beyond urate [[Dalbeth2018ARD]]. Silent deposits appear in 24% on DECT and 38.7% on ultrasound [[Dalbeth2015,Stewart2026US]]. Status: open. The TIGER cohort is following people with asymptomatic hyperuricaemia to see who progresses [[Stewart2026US]].
- Lifelong therapy or a "holiday" once crystals clear? ACR conditionally favours indefinite urate-lowering therapy (very low certainty) [[FitzGerald2020]]. Perez-Ruiz proposed about 5 years of treatment then a pause, guided by urate [[PerezRuiz2006]]. Status: being tested in GO TEST Finale (n ≥ 278) and STING (n ≈ 450, not yet recruiting) [[Peeters2023,NCT06956885]].
- Is clinical remission the same as crystal-free cure? Of people meeting remission criteria, 44% still had DECT urate deposits [[Dalbeth2019ART]]. G-CAN explicitly calls its definition "clinical" remission for this reason [[TabiAmponsah2026]]. Status: open. No validated "imaging remission" endpoint exists.
- Treat to target or treat to symptoms? GO TEST Overture favoured treat-to-target (remission 39.4% vs 24.0%) [[Moses2026]], and nurse-led treat-to-target reached target in 95% vs 30% [[Doherty2018]]. Status: three published letters questioned whether the Overture evidence is sufficient for primary care (Lancet Rheumatol 2026;8(9):e686–e689; not individually validated here).
- Should asymptomatic hyperuricaemia be treated? FREED reported fewer composite events with febuxostat, driven by renal impairment [[Kojima2019]]. CKD-FIX and PERL found no kidney benefit from allopurinol [[Badve2020,Doria2020]]. ACR advises against routine treatment [[FitzGerald2020]]. Status: not established outside Japanese practice.
- Can new URAT1 and xanthine oxidase inhibitors beat allopurinol titrated to target? Dotinurad 4 mg beat febuxostat 40 mg, a low febuxostat dose [[Sun2025]]. EURELIA 2 (allopurinol up to 800 mg) and the dotinurad-vs-allopurinol trials are unreported or recruiting [[NCT05586971,NCT07089875]]. Status: open. The lesinurad history (creatinine rises, withdrawal) shows that uricosuric kidney safety must be shown, not assumed [[Bardin2017,FR-2021-05368]].
- Tolerance induction vs methotrexate for uricase immunogenicity: NASP reached 51% response vs 8% on placebo in phase 3, with infusion reactions in 4% [[Baraf2026]]. Pegloticase plus methotrexate reached 71.0% at month 6, with 4.2% infusion reactions [[Botson2023]]. Status: there is no head-to-head trial of the two strategies, and the trials used different comparators.
- NLRP3 inhibitors vs IL-1 antibodies for flares: canakinumab and firsekibart have phase 3 and regulatory evidence [[Schlesinger2012,Crescioli2026]]. Oral NLRP3 inhibition has only an open-label phase 2a without a placebo arm [[Kluck2020]]. Status: the placebo-controlled dapansutrile phase 2/3 is recruiting [[NCT05658575]].

## Provenance
- Access: PubMed E-utilities returned HTTP 429 repeatedly, because 8 parallel passes share one IP. So abstracts were read through Europe PMC (resultType=core), and every PMID was then confirmed in a single batched PubMed esummary call (authors, title, journal, year, volume, pages all matched). Full texts were read where open (PMC/Europe PMC) for the ACR 2020 guideline (ULT duration, asymptomatic hyperuricaemia, IL-1 sections), FREED (gout flare counts), dotinurad China (Japan 2020 approval statement), Crescioli 2026 (NMPA approvals) and the NASP review (DISSOLVE design).
- Trials: every NCT ID was fetched from the ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 on 2026-10-20 for phase, status, enrolment, primary endpoint and whether results were posted. Labels were read on DailyMed (Krystexxa SPL 1149, Ilaris, Kineret, Arcalyst). The canakinumab gout approval was read from the FDA approval letter for BLA 125319/S-107. The lesinurad withdrawal comes from Federal Register document 2021-05368. The canakinumab EU gout indication is from the EMA EPAR page.
- Reuse from FMF: IlarisPIG, KineretPI, ArcalystPI and DFV890 are copied verbatim from fmf/src/sources.json, with re-checks added to the Validated tail.
- Corrections to dossier hand-offs:
  - Pascual2007: the dossier anchors "knee ~18 months, first MTP ~33 months" are not in the abstract, and the full text is not open access. The source states a 3–33 month range across all 18 joints (11 knees, 7 first MTPs), correlated with gout duration (rs = 0.71). Use the range, not per-joint values.
  - PerezRuiz2002: confirmed. The relation is linear (lower urate, faster tophus shrinkage), but the abstract gives no mm/month figures, so the curve should be drawn as schematic.
  - Dalbeth2018ARD: confirmed (1.1% at < 6 mg/dL; 49% at ≥ 10 mg/dL over 15 years).
  - The MIRROR citation "Botson2023, to verify" is confirmed as Arthritis Rheumatol 2023;75(2):293-304, PMID 36099211.
  - Canakinumab FDA gout approval is confirmed as August 2023 (S-107, status date 2023-08-25). The EMA 2013 date for the gout indication was not confirmed; only the indication is confirmed on the EPAR.
  - The dossier labels asymptomatic hyperuricaemia treatment "Hypothesis (trials largely negative)". FREED was positive on its composite, driven by renal impairment, while CKD-FIX and PERL were negative on kidney outcomes. This file labels FREED "Early evidence" and the kidney-protection question "Established negative" for those populations.
