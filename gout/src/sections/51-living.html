Living with gout

Food matters a little and the urate-lowering medicine matters most, so living with gout is mostly a plan, a few blood tests and a flare kit. The gap to target is usually bigger than diet

ACR 2020 sets a serum urate below 6 mg/dL [360 µmol/L] for everyone on urate-lowering therapy. [[FitzGerald2020]] In the only DASH trial in people with gout, urate started at 8.1 mg/dL [482 µmol/L] and the result was inconclusive. [[Juraschek2021]] In 30 people already on treatment, detailed dietary teaching raised knowledge scores but did not change urate at 6 months. [[Holland2015]] In the DIRECT weight-loss trial urate fell by about 0.81 mg/dL [48 µmol/L] at 6 months and 0.30 mg/dL [18 µmol/L] at 2 years, whichever diet people followed. [[Yokose2020]]

Weight, more than single foods

A mean 5 kg weight loss lowered urate by 1.1 mg/dL, and a BMI rise of more than 5% carried 60% higher odds of a recurrent flare, a fall of more than 5% 40% lower odds. The first month after bariatric surgery may bring a short-lived rise in flares. [[FitzGerald2020]] In a review of 10 weight-loss studies, 6 of 8 showed fewer flares, at low certainty. [[Nielsen2017]] In US men, being overweight, alcohol, a low DASH score and diuretics together accounted for 77% of new gout, and in men with obesity weight came first. [[McCormick2020]]

Genes or diet?

16,760 US adults of European ancestry in 5 cohorts. Each diet score explained 0.3% or less of the variation in serum urate; common genome-wide variation explained 23.9%. [[Major2018]] That is a different question from what causes new gout, where weight and diuretics matter a lot. [[McCormick2020]]

Gout is not a diet failure. ACR 2020 treats diet and other medicines as areas for shared decision-making, and its panel aimed to give guidance "without implying any patient-blaming", because gout has strong genetic determinants. [[FitzGerald2020]] In interviews and surveys, people with gout describe shame and the belief that it is self-inflicted, and both get in the way of treatment. [[Lindsay2011,Spencer2012]]

Flares, hospital, pregnancy, work

A flare plan: "medication-in-pocket"

The ACR 2020 patient panel asked for an at-home strategy for people who recognise their early flare signs. ACR strongly recommends colchicine, an NSAID or a glucocorticoid as first-line flare treatment, and conditionally recommends ice as an add-on. [[FitzGerald2020]] The US colchicine label gives 1.2 mg at the first sign of a flare and 0.6 mg one hour later, at most 1.8 mg in that hour. [[FDA-Colcrys]] The allopurinol label says to keep taking allopurinol during a flare. [[FDA-Allopurinol]]

In plain wordsAgree a plan with your clinician before the next flare. Keep the medicine at home, start at the first twinge, and keep taking the daily urate pill.

Hospital and surgery

RR 10.2In a population cohort of 429 people with gout, the flare rate rose about tenfold in hospital, and an in-hospital flare lengthened the stay by 1.8 days. [[Zleik2018]] After surgery, 31 of 70 people with gout flared, a mean 3.7 days after the operation; the big toe was involved in only 13%. Taking allopurinol carried OR 0.15 and a preoperative urate ≥ 9 mg/dL OR 3.77. [[Jeong2019]] In an older series, almost all postoperative flares came with fever, and many people had tests for unexplained fever first. [[Craig1995]]

Predicting an inpatient flare

The GOUT-36 rule counts four things before admission: no flare prophylaxis, no urate-lowering therapy, a tophus, and a urate above 0.36 mmol/L [6 mg/dL] in the past year. It had 75% sensitivity and 67% specificity; 80% of very-high-risk patients flared against 11% of low-risk patients. [[Jatuworapruk2022Rheum]] Regular urate-lowering therapy before admission went with a 10% shorter stay in 36,047 admissions. [[Jatuworapruk2022IJRD]]

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

Gout is uncommon in younger women: 0.6 per 1000 person-years under 45, against 2.5 at 75 or older. [[Hak2010]] BSR 2023 says colchicine "may be considered during pregnancy" (1B) and may be used in breastfeeding (2C), based on 550 exposed pregnancies with no rise in malformations or miscarriage. [[Schreiber2023,Indraratna2018]] It advises NSAIDs only intermittently, weaning from 26 weeks and stopping by 30. [[Schreiber2023]] The allopurinol label notes possible fetal harm in animal data and advises against breastfeeding on it; febuxostat has insufficient human pregnancy data. [[FDA-Allopurinol,FDA-Uloric]]

Work, shoes and exercise

Among about 300,000 US employees, people with gout had 4.56 more health-related absence days a year. [[Kleinman2007]] Adding an athletic shoe to podiatry did not reduce foot pain at 6 months, though fit and comfort scores improved. [[Frecklington2019ART]] People with gout describe comfort first, knowing what to buy, and tophi and flares limiting shoe choice. [[Frecklington2019JFAR]] In interviews, uncertainty about whether exercise could set off flares held people back, "particularly in the absence of gout-specific advice". [[Stewart2026]]

Water, cherries, milk Early evidence

No trial of drinking more fluid in gout was found; the allopurinol label's urine output of at least 2 litres a day is there to prevent stones. [[FDA-Allopurinol]] Cherries over 2 days went with fewer flares (OR 0.65) in a case-crossover study, with no randomised trial behind it. [[Zhang2012Cherry]] Milk lowered urate acutely by about 10% in a crossover trial, and enriched skim milk powder reduced flares in a 3-month trial. [[Dalbeth2010Milk,Dalbeth2012]] Sugar-sweetened drinks raised gout risk; diet soft drinks did not. [[Choi2008BMJ]]

Monitoring and the clinic visit

The monitoring checklist in the guidelines and labels

  • Target serum urate below 6 mg/dL [360 µmol/L] on urate-lowering therapy (ACR 2020, strong); EULAR 2016 suggests below 5 mg/dL [300 µmol/L] in severe gout. [[FitzGerald2020,Richette2017]]
  • Urate checked every 2–5 weeks while the dose is being raised and after each dose change, with titration taking "weeks to months, not years". [[FitzGerald2020]]
  • Once at target, every 6 months (ACR 2012), partly as a check on adherence. [[Khanna2012P1]]
  • Before allopurinol, the label lists urate, a full blood count, a chemistry panel, liver tests and kidney function. Before febuxostat, a liver panel. [[FDA-Allopurinol,FDA-Uloric]]
  • Urate-lowering therapy continued indefinitely (ACR 2020, conditional): only 13% (27 of 211) stayed flare-free for 5 years after stopping, even with urate under 7 mg/dL. [[FitzGerald2020]]
  • A normal urate during a flare does not rule gout out: 14% of people in a flare had 6 mg/dL or less. [[Schlesinger2009]]

Questions people bring to a visit

Plain-words prompts built from what the guidelines say should be discussed. They start a conversation with a clinician; they are not a script and not advice.

  • What is my urate today, and how far is it from below 6 mg/dL?
  • When is the next dose step and the next blood test?
  • What is my flare plan, and do I keep my daily pill during a flare?
  • How long should I stay on flare prophylaxis?
  • Do any of my other medicines raise urate, and is there an alternative?
  • What should happen if I need an operation or a hospital stay?
  • Should my kidneys, blood pressure or heart be checked too?
In plain wordsTwo numbers do most of the work: your urate on the last test, and how many flares you had since the visit before.
