Part 2 · Treating a flare

Colchicine dosing & safety

Colchicine treats a gout flare at low doses but has a narrow safety margin, and a few common drugs can push its blood level up several-fold. Pick an interacting drug and press play to watch ten days of prophylaxis, then compare the published flare and prophylaxis schedules, the organs that are watched, and the kidney and liver rules.

Dosing for any person is set by their own clinician. This module reports what the US labels (COLCRYS tablets, MITIGARE capsules), EULAR 2016 and the ACR guidelines publish. It is a study aid, not a dosing guide. [[FDA-Colcrys,FDA-Mitigare,Richette2017,Khanna2012P2,FitzGerald2020]]

Interactions can be fatal

Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (such as clarithromycin, ketoconazole, itraconazole, ritonavir) and P-glycoprotein inhibitors (such as cyclosporine) stop the body clearing colchicine. The US label contraindicates them with colchicine in people with kidney or liver impairment, because fatal toxicity has occurred at therapeutic doses. EULAR 2016 says not to give colchicine with strong P-gp and/or CYP3A4 inhibitors. [[FDA-Colcrys,Richette2017]]

The clarithromycin study

10.2% vs 3.6%In a retrospective study, 9 of 88 inpatients (10.2%) given clarithromycin and colchicine at the same time died, versus 1 of 28 (3.6%) given them one after the other. A longer overlap (RR 2.16), baseline kidney impairment (RR 9.1) and pancytopenia (RR 23.4) predicted death. [[Hung2005]]

Pharmacokinetic studies support no adjustment with azithromycin. [[Terkeltaub2011]]

How big are the cuts?

33–66% · 50–75%A dose-reduction algorithm built from pharmacokinetic studies supports cutting the flare dose by 33–66% and the prophylaxis dose by 50–75% with most CYP3A4 or P-gp inhibitors. [[Terkeltaub2011]]

Statins and fibrates

The concern here is muscle. Statins, fibrates and cyclosporine can potentiate colchicine myopathy and rhabdomyolysis, and older people and people with kidney dysfunction are at higher risk. [[FDA-Colcrys]]

Grapefruit: a label paradox

The label lists grapefruit juice in its table of moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors, with the matching dose cuts, yet its own interaction study found colchicine AUC changed by −2%. Both sit in the same document, and it does not resolve the conflict. [[FDA-Colcrys]]

Two products, two wordings

COLCRYS contraindicates P-gp or strong CYP3A4 inhibitors in renal or hepatic impairment. MITIGARE contraindicates drugs that inhibit both P-gp and CYP3A4 in renal or hepatic impairment, and use at all in combined renal and hepatic impairment. [[FDA-Colcrys,FDA-Mitigare]]

2 · Published gout schedules

Each schedule is drawn as published, not as a dose for anyone; a dose for a person is set by their clinician. Tablet size shows the mg taken; the line shows the running total over the first 48 hours.

What the US label publishes

  • Flare: 1.2 mg at the first sign, then 0.6 mg 1 hour later; maximum 1.8 mg over 1 hour. "Higher doses have not been found to be more effective." [[FDA-Colcrys]]
  • Prophylaxis (adults and adolescents over 16): 0.6 mg once or twice daily, maximum 1.2 mg/day. [[FDA-Colcrys]]
  • Flare during prophylaxis: up to 1.2 mg, then 0.6 mg 1 hour later; wait 12 hours, then resume the prophylactic dose. [[FDA-Colcrys]]
  • Repeat flare courses: safety and efficacy have not been evaluated. [[FDA-Colcrys]]
  • MITIGARE capsules are labelled for prophylaxis only. [[FDA-Mitigare]]

What guidelines add

  • EULAR 2016: 1 mg, then 0.5 mg 1 hour later on day 1, within 12 hours of onset; prophylaxis 0.5–1 mg/day for the first 6 months of urate lowering, reduced in kidney impairment. [[Richette2017]]
  • ACR 2012: colchicine only if the flare began 36 hours or less earlier; after the loading doses, 0.6 mg once or twice daily from 12 hours until the flare resolves. [[Khanna2012P2]]
  • ACR 2020: low-dose over high-dose colchicine (strong recommendation); prophylaxis for 3–6 months rather than less than 3 when starting urate lowering. [[FitzGerald2020]]
  • Hong Kong 2023: prophylaxis 0.5 mg once or twice daily for 3–6 months. [[Yip2023]]

Why the low dose won

6.2 vs 6.8 ng/mLThe low-dose flare schedule (1.8 mg over 1 hour) reached a mean peak of 6.2 ng/mL; the old high dose (4.8 mg over 6 hours) reached 6.8 ng/mL. [[FDA-Colcrys]] In a Cochrane review, high dose gave no extra benefit but more adverse events (number needed to harm 2). [[vanEchteld2014]]

Prophylaxis works

0.52 vs 2.91mean flares with colchicine 0.6 mg twice daily vs placebo while starting allopurinol, in a trial of 43 people (P = 0.008). Months 3–6: 0 vs 1.05 flares. [[Borstad2004]] The label says prophylaxis "may be beneficial for at least the first six months" of urate lowering. [[FDA-Colcrys]]

3 · Toxicity and what is watched

Click an organ or a button. Schematic body map.

Narrow margin, serious overdose. Colchicine has a narrow therapeutic index, with no clear line between non-toxic, toxic and lethal doses; the lowest reported lethal oral doses are 7–26 mg. [[Finkelstein2010]] In a review of 150 overdoses in the label, people who took under 0.5 mg/kg survived with mostly gut effects, 0.5–0.8 mg/kg caused severe reactions such as myelosuppression, and over 0.8 mg/kg was fatal in every case. [[FDA-Colcrys]]

How toxicity unfolds. Acute toxicity typically begins within 24 hours with abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea; multi-organ failure follows at 24–72 hours. Gut symptoms are often the first sign. [[FDA-Colcrys,FDA-Mitigare]]

4 · Kidneys and liver

Creatinine clearance (CrCl) in mL/min. Categories and rules follow the COLCRYS label's gout sections.

Liver impairment

COLCRYS label (gout): no dose change in mild to moderate hepatic impairment, with close monitoring. In severe impairment, consider a lower prophylaxis dose, and repeat flare courses no more than once every 2 weeks. ACR 2012 lists hepatic disease as a limit for colchicine, NSAIDs and COX-2 inhibitors. [[FDA-Colcrys,Khanna2012P2]]

5 · Pregnancy and breastfeeding

What a 2023 guideline says

The British Society for Rheumatology says colchicine "may be considered during pregnancy" and "may be used in breastfeeding". It rests on a meta-analysis of 550 exposed pregnancies, mostly in FMF at 1–2 mg/day, against 1263 unexposed, with no rise in malformations or miscarriage. [[Schreiber2023,Indraratna2018]]

Exam hookColchicine toxicity = gut first, then muscle + nerve (myoneuropathy, high CK, usually with kidney impairment), then marrow. Danger rises with CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors and low CrCl. On dialysis the flare dose is a single 0.6 mg, no more than once every 2 weeks. [[FDA-Colcrys,Kuncl1987]]
In plain wordsColchicine is safe for most people at the published doses, but a few antibiotics, antifungals, HIV drugs and heart drugs make it build up. Tell every doctor and pharmacist that you take it, and keep the tablets away from children.
