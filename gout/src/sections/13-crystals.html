Crystals vs serum urate

Urate crystals grow only while serum urate stays above its saturation point of about 6.8 mg/dL, and they dissolve once it is kept below 6. Scrub through fifteen years of one imagined person, start treatment at year 9, then pick the urate level treatment reaches and how cool the joint is.

The fixed points are published: saturation at 6.8 mg/dL at 37 °C and 6.0 mg/dL at 35 °C, the < 6 mg/dL target, and tophus shrinkage speeding up in a straight line as on-treatment urate falls. The growth and shrink slopes are relative, because no study gives a rate in mm per month that applies here. [[Martillo2014,Loeb1972,Richette2017,PerezRuiz2002]]

Who forms crystals, and how fast they leave

Fifteen-year risk of gout by baseline urate

Individual-participant data from 4 US cohorts: 18,889 people and 212,363 person-years. Hazard ratios are adjusted and compared with a baseline urate < 6 mg/dL (< 360 µmol/L). The 15-year cumulative incidence was 1.1% (95% CI 0.9–1.4) below 6 mg/dL and 49% (31–67) at ≥ 10 mg/dL (≥ 595 µmol/L). Each dot is one person in 100. [[Dalbeth2018ARD]]

Crystals leaving joint fluid on treatment

18 men (11 knees, 7 first MTPs) were aspirated every 3 months after starting urate lowering. Median urate fell from 550 to 290 µmol/L (≈ 9.2 to 4.9 mg/dL) by 3 months. Crystals left every joint, after 3 to 33 months, and the time tracked how long the person had had gout (rs = 0.71, p < 0.01). The report does not give separate times for knees and toes.
