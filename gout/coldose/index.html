Part 2 · Treating a flare

Colchicine dosing & safety

Colchicine treats a gout flare at low doses but has a narrow safety margin, and a few common drugs can push its blood level up several-fold. Pick an interacting drug and press play to watch ten days of prophylaxis, then compare the published flare and prophylaxis schedules, the organs that are watched, and the kidney and liver rules.

Dosing for any person is set by their own clinician. This module reports what the US labels (COLCRYS tablets, MITIGARE capsules), EULAR 2016 and the ACR guidelines publish. It is a study aid, not a dosing guide. 117,124,135,204,332

Interactions can be fatal

Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (such as clarithromycin, ketoconazole, itraconazole, ritonavir) and P-glycoprotein inhibitors (such as cyclosporine) stop the body clearing colchicine. The US label contraindicates them with colchicine in people with kidney or liver impairment, because fatal toxicity has occurred at therapeutic doses. EULAR 2016 says not to give colchicine with strong P-gp and/or CYP3A4 inhibitors. 117,332

What the label publishes

Gout dose adjustments

When there is no alternative to the interacting drug, the US label cuts the prophylaxis dose (from 0.6 mg twice daily) to 0.3 mg once daily with strong CYP3A4 or P-gp inhibitors, and to 0.3 mg twice daily or 0.6 mg once daily with moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors. The flare dose falls too, and is not repeated within 3 days. The adjustments also apply within 14 days of stopping the interacting drug. Press "Apply the label's prophylaxis dose adjustment" to see the effect. 117,408

In plain wordsIf two drugs must be taken together, the label lowers the colchicine dose so that levels stay closer to normal.

The clarithromycin study

10.2% vs 3.6%In a retrospective study, 9 of 88 inpatients (10.2%) given clarithromycin and colchicine at the same time died, versus 1 of 28 (3.6%) given them one after the other. A longer overlap (RR 2.16), baseline kidney impairment (RR 9.1) and pancytopenia (RR 23.4) predicted death. 173

Pharmacokinetic studies support no adjustment with azithromycin. 408

How big are the cuts?

33–66% · 50–75%A dose-reduction algorithm built from pharmacokinetic studies supports cutting the flare dose by 33–66% and the prophylaxis dose by 50–75% with most CYP3A4 or P-gp inhibitors. 408

Statins and fibrates

The concern here is muscle. Statins, fibrates and cyclosporine can potentiate colchicine myopathy and rhabdomyolysis, and older people and people with kidney dysfunction are at higher risk. 117

Grapefruit: a label paradox

The label lists grapefruit juice in its table of moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors, with the matching dose cuts, yet its own interaction study found colchicine AUC changed by −2%. Both sit in the same document, and it does not resolve the conflict. 117

Two products, two wordings

COLCRYS contraindicates P-gp or strong CYP3A4 inhibitors in renal or hepatic impairment. MITIGARE contraindicates drugs that inhibit both P-gp and CYP3A4 in renal or hepatic impairment, and use at all in combined renal and hepatic impairment. 117,124

COLCRYS label

US label: flare

1.2 mg at the first sign of a flare, then 0.6 mg 1 hour later: a maximum of 1.8 mg over 1 hour. The label adds that "higher doses have not been found to be more effective", and that the safety and efficacy of repeat flare courses have not been evaluated. ACR 2020 says to follow this FDA dosing when colchicine is chosen. 117,135

What the US label publishes

  • Flare: 1.2 mg at the first sign, then 0.6 mg 1 hour later; maximum 1.8 mg over 1 hour. "Higher doses have not been found to be more effective." 117
  • Prophylaxis (adults and adolescents over 16): 0.6 mg once or twice daily, maximum 1.2 mg/day. 117
  • Flare during prophylaxis: up to 1.2 mg, then 0.6 mg 1 hour later; wait 12 hours, then resume the prophylactic dose. 117
  • Repeat flare courses: safety and efficacy have not been evaluated. 117
  • MITIGARE capsules are labelled for prophylaxis only. 124

What guidelines add

  • EULAR 2016: 1 mg, then 0.5 mg 1 hour later on day 1, within 12 hours of onset; prophylaxis 0.5–1 mg/day for the first 6 months of urate lowering, reduced in kidney impairment. 332
  • ACR 2012: colchicine only if the flare began 36 hours or less earlier; after the loading doses, 0.6 mg once or twice daily from 12 hours until the flare resolves. 204
  • ACR 2020: low-dose over high-dose colchicine (strong recommendation); prophylaxis for 3–6 months rather than less than 3 when starting urate lowering. 135
  • Hong Kong 2023: prophylaxis 0.5 mg once or twice daily for 3–6 months. 441

Why the low dose won

6.2 vs 6.8 ng/mLThe low-dose flare schedule (1.8 mg over 1 hour) reached a mean peak of 6.2 ng/mL; the old high dose (4.8 mg over 6 hours) reached 6.8 ng/mL. 117 In a Cochrane review, high dose gave no extra benefit but more adverse events (number needed to harm 2). 420

Prophylaxis works

0.52 vs 2.91mean flares with colchicine 0.6 mg twice daily vs placebo while starting allopurinol, in a trial of 43 people (P = 0.008). Months 3–6: 0 vs 1.05 flares. 26 The label says prophylaxis "may be beneficial for at least the first six months" of urate lowering. 117

3 · Toxicity and what is watched

Click an organ or a button. Schematic body map.

Toxicity map

Gut

What is reported
Cramping, nausea, diarrhoea, abdominal pain and vomiting are the most common effects. In the label's flare trial, gut adverse reactions occurred in 26% at the 1.8 mg dose, 77% at the old 4.8 mg dose and 20% on placebo, and diarrhoea (23%) was the most common. In CONTACT, diarrhoea affected 45.9% on colchicine 0.5 mg three times daily vs 20.0% on naproxen. 117,339
What to watch
The label says gut symptoms are often the first sign of toxicity, and that acute toxicity typically begins within 24 hours. 117,124

Narrow margin, serious overdose. Colchicine has a narrow therapeutic index, with no clear line between non-toxic, toxic and lethal doses; the lowest reported lethal oral doses are 7–26 mg. 133 In a review of 150 overdoses in the label, people who took under 0.5 mg/kg survived with mostly gut effects, 0.5–0.8 mg/kg caused severe reactions such as myelosuppression, and over 0.8 mg/kg was fatal in every case. 117

How toxicity unfolds. Acute toxicity typically begins within 24 hours with abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea; multi-organ failure follows at 24–72 hours. Gut symptoms are often the first sign. 117,124

4 · Kidneys and liver

Creatinine clearance (CrCl) in mL/min. Categories and rules follow the COLCRYS label's gout sections.

Kidney function

Above 80

Above 80 mL/min the label's kidney-impairment advice does not apply. 117

Across every category, the label contraindicates P-gp or strong CYP3A4 inhibitors with colchicine in kidney or liver impairment. Colchicine myopathy in gout typically appears when reduced kidney function raises drug levels on customary doses. 117,222

Liver impairment

COLCRYS label (gout): no dose change in mild to moderate hepatic impairment, with close monitoring. In severe impairment, consider a lower prophylaxis dose, and repeat flare courses no more than once every 2 weeks. ACR 2012 lists hepatic disease as a limit for colchicine, NSAIDs and COX-2 inhibitors. 117,204

5 · Pregnancy and breastfeeding

What a 2023 guideline says

The British Society for Rheumatology says colchicine "may be considered during pregnancy" and "may be used in breastfeeding". It rests on a meta-analysis of 550 exposed pregnancies, mostly in FMF at 1–2 mg/day, against 1263 unexposed, with no rise in malformations or miscarriage. 177,357

Exam hookColchicine toxicity = gut first, then muscle + nerve (myoneuropathy, high CK, usually with kidney impairment), then marrow. Danger rises with CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors and low CrCl. On dialysis the flare dose is a single 0.6 mg, no more than once every 2 weeks. 117,222
In plain wordsColchicine is safe for most people at the published doses, but a few antibiotics, antifungals, HIV drugs and heart drugs make it build up. Tell every doctor and pharmacist that you take it, and keep the tablets away from children.
