Crystals vs serum urate

Urate crystals grow only while serum urate stays above its saturation point of about 6.8 mg/dL, and they dissolve once it is kept below 6. Scrub through fifteen years of one imagined person, start treatment at year 9, then pick the urate level treatment reaches and how cool the joint is.

Years 0–4

Above saturation: nucleation

Plasma is saturated with urate at about 6.8 mg/dL (405 µmol/L) 250. Above that point urate can leave solution in three stages: solubility, then nucleation, then growth. A high urate concentration drives all three, and no other factor was found to promote growth specifically 44. Crystals deposit preferentially at the first MTP, the femoral condyle and the Achilles and patellar tendons 44.

Who forms crystals, and how fast they leave

Fifteen-year risk of gout by baseline urate

Individual-participant data from 4 US cohorts: 18,889 people and 212,363 person-years. Hazard ratios are adjusted and compared with a baseline urate < 6 mg/dL (< 360 µmol/L). The 15-year cumulative incidence was 1.1% (95% CI 0.9–1.4) below 6 mg/dL and 49% (31–67) at ≥ 10 mg/dL (≥ 595 µmol/L). Each dot is one person in 100. 82

Crystals leaving joint fluid on treatment

18 men (11 knees, 7 first MTPs) were aspirated every 3 months after starting urate lowering. Median urate fell from 550 to 290 µmol/L (≈ 9.2 to 4.9 mg/dL) by 3 months. Crystals left every joint, after 3 to 33 months, and the time tracked how long the person had had gout (rs = 0.71, p < 0.01). The report does not give separate times for knees and toes. 309

Why the big toe, and why at night?

A drop of only 2 °C, from 37 to 35 °C, lowers urate solubility from 6.8 to 6.0 mg/dL in vitro. The first MTP is cool, poorly perfused and has a high surface-to-volume ratio, which may partly explain why it is the commonest site of a first flare 237,250. Flares are 2.36 times as likely overnight (95% CI 2.05–2.73) 58. One proposed reason is that water leaves a resolving joint effusion about twice as fast as urate, so local urate rises for a while Hypothesis 369.

More than temperature

Across 35 studies, urate solubility was lower at lower temperature, at pH 7–9 and with ions including sodium, and higher at pH ≤ 6 or ≥ 10. Cartilage homogenates and healthy synovial fluid and serum all raised solubility, while synovial fluid or serum from people with gout increased nucleation 44. Lab figures for saturation differ a little: 6.6 mg/dL (392 µmol/L) at 37 °C and pH 7.4 in one review 39. This guide uses 6.8 mg/dL.

Necessary, not sufficient

Risk climbs steeply with urate, yet only about half of people at ≥ 10 mg/dL develop gout over 15 years 82. In the Normative Aging Study, annual incidence was 4.9% at ≥ 9 mg/dL, 0.5% at 7.0–8.9 mg/dL and 0.1% below 7.0 mg/dL 34. Many hyperuricaemic people have no deposits on imaging, and local tissue factors may decide where crystals form 44,79.

Stopping treatment

Cure in gout means clearing crystals and keeping urate low, not a one-off event 135,315. In a case series cited by ACR 2020, only 13% (27/211) of people whose urate stayed < 7 mg/dL off treatment were flare-free over 5 years 135. A withdrawal study found the longest flare-free spells, more than 4 years, when on-treatment urate had averaged < 5.05 mg/dL Early evidence 315.
