Step 1 · The frame

11 criteria, one 12-month window

DSM-5-TR defines alcohol use disorder as a problematic pattern of alcohol use causing clinically significant impairment or distress, shown by at least 2 of 11 criteria within a 12-month period. The work group chose 2 because it gave the best agreement with the combined prevalence of DSM-IV abuse and dependence. The criteria describe a dimensional condition with no natural cut-point, so the count itself is the severity. 16,169

In plain wordsAlcohol use disorder is a medical condition. A clinician diagnoses it by asking about 11 kinds of problems over the past year.
Exam hookAUD = ≥2 of 11 criteria within 12 months.

From DSM-IV abuse and dependence to one disorder

DSM-IV had two alcohol diagnoses, “alcohol abuse” (the historical term) and “alcohol dependence”, arranged in a hierarchy. DSM-5 merged them into one disorder, based on consistent findings from more than 200,000 study participants: the criteria formed one factor, or two highly correlated factors. DSM-IV · 1994

Two diagnoses in a hierarchy

DSM-IV split the criteria into “alcohol abuse” (role failure, hazardous use, legal problems, social problems) and “alcohol dependence” (the other seven). The hierarchy created “diagnostic orphans”: people with two dependence criteria and no abuse criterion received no diagnosis at all. 169,360

In plain wordsThe old system had two labels, and some people with real problems fell between them.
Exam hookDiagnostic orphan = 2 dependence criteria, 0 abuse criteria: no DSM-IV diagnosis, but mild AUD in DSM-5.

What the numbers say

Severity

Mild is the most common

In NESARC-III, 12-month prevalence was 7.3% mild, 3.2% moderate and 3.4% severe; lifetime, 8.6%, 6.6% and 13.9%. These are population estimates. 146

Onset

Earlier onset, more severe

Mean age at onset was 26.2 years overall: 30.1 for mild, 25.9 for moderate and 23.9 for severe AUD. 146

Treatment

Few seek help

Among people with 12-month AUD, treatment seeking was 2.7% (mild), 4.9% (moderate) and 21.3% (severe). Only 19.8% with lifetime AUD had ever been treated. 146

Crosswalk

Where old trials fit

DSM-IV dependence agreed best with DSM-5 AUD at 4 or more criteria, so treatments tested in DSM-IV dependence apply best to AUD of at least moderate severity. 64

Hypothesis

Why craving was added

Its psychometric benefit was equivocal; the view that craving may become a biological treatment target prevailed. Adding it did not substantially change diagnosis rates. 169,360

Not in the criteria

No blood test decides

Biomarkers were considered but left out of DSM-5, because they were not yet suitable as diagnostic tests. Lab tests can show recent or heavy drinking: see Blood and urine markers of drinking. 169

Recovery is common. As group statistics, not a promise for any one person: among US adults whose alcohol dependence began before the past year, 18.2% were abstinent and 17.7% low-risk drinkers in the past year, and in long-term studies 35.0–54.4% of people with substance use disorders reached remission over a mean of 17 years. AUD Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on alcohol use disorder and alcohol withdrawal, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people with alcohol use disorder, their families and friends.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine or a plan for stopping drinking without your own care team. The AUDIT, the criteria counter, CIWA-Ar, PAWSS and the blood alcohol and liver calculators are for learning, not self-diagnosis or reading your own results, and the blood alcohol calculator can never tell anyone whether it is safe to drive. Doses and regimens are quoted as published in labels and guidelines, never as instructions. Alcohol poisoning and severe withdrawal are emergencies.
