An animated, cited guide for students, people with alcohol use disorder, and their families and friends. Educational only, not medical advice. Your place is saved on this device.

Not medical adviceThe AUD Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. It does not replace a doctor, nurse, pharmacist, counsellor or other clinician. The AUDIT, the criteria counter, CIWA-Ar and the other calculators are teaching aids, not self-tests, and doses, benzodiazepine and thiamine regimens and CIWA-Ar thresholds are quoted as published, never as instructions. Stopping suddenly after long, heavy daily drinking can be dangerous: get medical help to stop safely. If you or someone else may have alcohol poisoning or severe withdrawal (seizure, confusion, hallucinations), call emergency services. For help with drinking in the US, SAMHSA's National Helpline is free and confidential: 1-800-662-4357. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

A history of alcohol use disorder

For more than 200 years, doctors have described problem drinking as a health condition. Walk the timeline from Benjamin Rush to DSM-5 and ICD-11, and watch the name for the condition change as the ideas behind it changed. 449,500

The scale changes at 1930 so the crowded 20th and 21st centuries have room. Dates as given by the cited histories and by Drugs@FDA; US Prohibition is shown only by its 1933 repeal, the date the sources give. 40,115,449,500

How the diagnosis changed

DSM-IV (1994) had two alcohol diagnoses, abuse and dependence. DSM-5 (2013) merged them into one alcohol use disorder, dropped legal problems and added craving. ICD-11 (2019) kept dependence separate from a harmful pattern of use. Switch between them and watch the criteria move. 169,414

Stigma and language over time

1780s onwards

From vice to disease

Rush described habitual heavy drinking as a disease caused by alcohol itself, and ran a public health campaign on the hazards of distilled spirits that listed more than a dozen alcohol-related health problems 223,449. Jellinek's 1960 book codified problem drinking as a disease of loss of control over drinking. 233,449

Names

Why the old names were retired

"Alcoholism", Huss's term in its Latin form, survives as the older, lay name 449. "Alcohol abuse" was a DSM-IV diagnosis, retired in 2013 169. Words still carry blame: clinicians who read "a substance abuser" rather than "having a substance use disorder" were more likely to judge the person culpable and to favour punishment. 226

Established

Stigma has not gone away

People with alcohol dependence are held more responsible for their condition than people with other mental disorders 417, and a 2010–2020 review found the public's wish for social distance from people with AUD stayed consistently higher 240. Stigma is one reason the treatment gap is so wide. 56,372

Mutual help

From temperance to AA

The temperance movement of the 1800s and early 1900s laid groundwork for later mutual-help organisations 500. By 2010, AA reported some 2 million members in more than 160 countries. 151 See Talking therapies and mutual help.

Disputed

Did Prohibition "work"?

Blocker argues the usual verdict of failure rests on a weak historical base: Prohibition did lower consumption, and after repeal yearly intake was about 1.2 US gallons per person, less than half the pre-Prohibition level. The Volstead Act defined "intoxicating" as 0.5% alcohol by volume or more. Historians still dispute it. 40

Debated

One disorder or two?

DSM-5 merged abuse and dependence on data from more than 200,000 participants 169. ICD-11 keeps dependence separate from harmful use 414. Stockwell argues DSM-5 mixes dependence with its consequences, departing from Edwards and Gross, whose syndrome shaped international classifications and underpins brief interventions. 442

AUD Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on alcohol use disorder and alcohol withdrawal, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people with alcohol use disorder, their families and friends.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine or a plan for stopping drinking without your own care team. The AUDIT, the criteria counter, CIWA-Ar, PAWSS and the blood alcohol and liver calculators are for learning, not self-diagnosis or reading your own results, and the blood alcohol calculator can never tell anyone whether it is safe to drive. Doses and regimens are quoted as published in labels and guidelines, never as instructions. Alcohol poisoning and severe withdrawal are emergencies. Please check the original sources, which are listed in full in the Sources module.
