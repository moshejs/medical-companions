Managing withdrawal safely

How the ASAM 2020 guideline walks from risk assessment to a setting, to medicines, to thiamine and fluids, and then on to treatment for the alcohol use disorder itself. Pick a person below and follow their token along the path.

SafetyStopping suddenly after long, heavy daily drinking can be dangerous: it can cause seizures or delirium tremens. Get medical help to stop safely. Everything on this page describes what guidelines and labels say, for study. None of it is a plan for any one person. [[ASAM2020Withdrawal,Mirijello2015]]

Withdrawal management is a start, not the treatment. ASAM 2020 says withdrawal management on its own "is not an effective treatment for alcohol use disorder" and should be used as a way into treatment, with AUD medicines started when thinking is clear enough and a "warm handoff" to treatment providers. Next: approved medicines, talking therapies and mutual help and recovery. [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]]

Benzodiazepines compared: longer- and shorter-acting

ASAM 2020 found no benzodiazepine more effective than another, but prefers longer-acting agents for their longer action, and asks for an agent with less liver metabolism in significant liver disease. Saitz's review explains why duration matters: long-acting agents give a smoother course with less rebound, while short-acting agents carry more risk of rebound symptoms, including late seizures. [[ASAM2020Withdrawal,Saitz1998AHRW]]

Never with opioids or alcoholEvery benzodiazepine label checked (Valium, chlordiazepoxide, oxazepam) carries a boxed warning that use with opioids "may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death". ASAM asks that patients and caregivers be taught the danger of combining benzodiazepines with alcohol or other sedatives, not to drive for the first few days, and to reduce the dose if drowsy. And alcohol itself is never a treatment: "Oral or intravenous alcohol should not be used for the prevention or treatment of alcohol withdrawal". What the trials show

Other medicines and harder cases

Mild to moderate

Carbamazepine and gabapentin

ASAM 2020: appropriate for mild withdrawal; for moderate withdrawal, benzodiazepines come first and these are alternatives. Gabapentin is a favourable choice when it will also be used for ongoing AUD treatment. In one trial of 100 outpatients, high-dose gabapentin was statistically better than lorazepam on CIWA-Ar but clinically similar, with less drinking during and just after withdrawal. The 2010 Cochrane review of all anticonvulsants did not find enough evidence to support them overall. [[ASAM2020Withdrawal,Myrick2009,Minozzi2010Cochrane]]

Early evidence

Phenobarbital

ASAM: only for clinicians experienced with it, "given its narrow therapeutic window", and given by vein only in highly supervised settings such as the ICU. In one emergency trial, a single dose added to a lorazepam protocol cut ICU admissions from 25% to 8%; an ED meta-analysis of 8 studies found no significant difference from benzodiazepines (ICU admission RR 0.92). An ICU meta-analysis of 16 non-randomised studies found ICU stays 0.60 days shorter, with very low certainty. [[ASAM2020Withdrawal,Rosenson2013,Lee2024AEM,Rizwan2026]]

Adjuncts only

Medicines that mask, not prevent

Clonidine, dexmedetomidine and beta-blockers can calm a racing heart and blood pressure, but ASAM says they "should not be used alone" to prevent or treat seizures or delirium. Antipsychotics are adjuncts only for hallucinations or delirium not controlled by benzodiazepines; used alone in delirium they carried a relative risk of death of 6.6 against sedative-hypnotics. Phenytoin is not used unless there is a separate seizure disorder. [[ASAM2020Withdrawal,MayoSmith2004Delirium]]

ICU

Resistant withdrawal

ASAM: resistant withdrawal "is not well defined"; reviews used symptoms uncontrolled despite more than 150–200 mg diazepam or 30–40 mg lorazepam in the first 3–4 hours (quoted, not instructions). Phenobarbital may be added with close monitoring, propofol used if a person already needs a ventilator, and dexmedetomidine used in the ICU. In one ICU before-and-after study, escalating benzodiazepines plus phenobarbital went with fewer people on ventilators (47% to 22%). [[ASAM2020Withdrawal,Gold2007CCM]]

Delirium tremens

Care in delirium

Close nursing observation, often in an ICU; continuous one-to-one observation for agitated, disoriented people; immediate IV access; restraints "only when required to prevent injuries"; and a sedation target of "light somnolence". Very large benzodiazepine doses may be needed. In a case-control study, restraint use was associated with death (OR 5.8): an association, not proof of cause. See nursing care. [[ASAM2020Withdrawal,Khan2008AEM]]

Established

With modern care, most people survive

Reported death rates in delirium tremens range from about 1% or less with early detection and appropriate treatment, through 1–5%, to about 5% in older series. In a meta-analysis of delirium trials, no deaths were reported in 217 patients treated with benzodiazepines or barbiturates. These are group statistics, not a promise for any one person. [[Jesse2017,Mirijello2015,Saitz1998AHRW,MayoSmith2004Delirium]]

Get helpIf you or someone else may have alcohol poisoning or severe withdrawal (seizure, confusion, hallucinations), call emergency services. For help with drinking in the US, SAMHSA's National Helpline is free and confidential: 1-800-662-4357. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).
