Part 2 · Screening & diagnosis

Screening: AUDIT, AUDIT-C & brief intervention

Unhealthy alcohol use is common and easy to miss, so primary care asks everyone. Follow one person through the SBIRT pathway, score a teaching copy of the AUDIT, and see how many people each screening question flags correctly and wrongly.

The USPSTF recommendation (2018, grade B). The US Preventive Services Task Force recommends screening for unhealthy alcohol use in primary care in adults 18 years or older, including pregnant women, and providing people engaged in risky or hazardous drinking with brief behavioural counselling. For adolescents aged 12 to 17 it found the evidence insufficient (an I statement). [[USPSTF2018Alcohol]]

Try it: score a teaching copy of the AUDIT

A teaching tool, not a self-test or a diagnosis. Use it to learn how the AUDIT is built and scored, not to interpret your own drinking or anyone else's. A screening score only says “let's talk more”: a score of 20 or more, or any dependence symptom, signals that a diagnostic assessment is needed, not that alcohol use disorder is present. If you are worried about drinking, talk with a health professional. [[HigginsBiddle2018USAUDIT]]

Adolescents: CRAFFT and frequency screens

For people aged 12 to 17 the USPSTF found the evidence insufficient to recommend for or against screening, and brief-intervention trials in this age group gave mixed results. Tools built for adolescents do better than adult problem questions. [[USPSTF2018Alcohol,OConnor2018JAMA]]

The usual adolescent cut-off

In 538 clinic patients aged 14 to 18, a score of 2 or more was optimal: sensitivity 0.76 and specificity 0.94 for any problem, 0.80 and 0.86 for any disorder, 0.92 and 0.80 for dependence. About one in four scored 2 or more, and validity did not differ by age, sex or race. [[Knight2002CRAFFT]]

Compared

CAGE misses most adolescents

For adolescent alcohol problems, sensitivities were CRAFFT 0.92, AUDIT 0.88, POSIT 0.84 and CAGE 0.37; CAGE is not recommended in this age group. Frequency screens (S2BI, BSTAD, TAPS) reached AUCs of 0.89 to 1, and S2BI was 100% sensitive for moderate or severe AUD. [[Knight2003ACER,Levy2023Adolescent,Levy2021S2BI]]

What the evidence says, in four cards

Established

Brief intervention helps on average

Across 69 trials, people given a brief intervention (five or fewer sessions, under an hour in total) drank about 20 g a week less after a year (95% CI −28 to −12), from an average of 244 g a week. Longer counselling added little. Both men and women cut down. [[Kaner2018Cochrane]]

Pregnancy

Plan with the care team

Screening and counselling in pregnancy are part of the USPSTF B recommendation, and counselling increased reported abstinence (OR 2.26). If you are pregnant or planning a pregnancy, your care team will ask about alcohol as routine, to help you plan. [[USPSTF2018Alcohol,OConnor2018JAMA]]

Hypothesis

Population-level benefit

More than a hundred trials support short-term individual benefit of screening and brief intervention; long-term effects on whole populations have not been demonstrated. No study directly tested a screening programme itself. [[Babor2007SBIRT,OConnor2018JAMA]]

The gap

Most people are never treated

Only 19.8% of US adults with lifetime DSM-5 alcohol use disorder had ever been treated. AUD is a treatable medical condition, and stigma plus too little systematic screening keep many people from care. Next: how it is diagnosed, in Diagnosis: DSM-5-TR criteria.
