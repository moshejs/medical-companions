Flashcards & memory aids

Flashcards on alcohol use disorder, from standard drinks and the GABA–glutamate balance to AUDIT-C, DSM-5-TR criteria, CIWA-Ar, thiamine, the approved medicines, recovery and the liver. Tap a card or press Space to flip it; arrow keys move through the deck. Filter by part, or shuffle.

These cards are for study. The scores and thresholds on them (AUDIT, CIWA-Ar, PAWSS, Maddrey and others) are teaching facts, not self-tests, and the doses are quoted as published, not as instructions.

Cards tagged well known are acronyms in common use; cards tagged memory aid were written for this guide to hold a published fact in mind. Neither replaces the source.

Memory aid in motion: the withdrawal clock, 6 · 12 · 24 · 48

Play the clock to watch the four numbers light up as the hours pass after the last drink: shakes from 6 hours, hallucinosis from 12, seizures peaking near 24, and delirium tremens from 48, with both published onset windows shown. The person's signs are drawn schematically.

Schematic timeline, not to scale for any one person; not everyone passes through every stage, and a seizure can occur without other prominent signs. [[ASAM2020Withdrawal,Mirijello2015,Jesse2017]]

Safety. Stopping suddenly after long, heavy daily drinking can be dangerous (seizures, delirium tremens): get medical help to stop safely. If someone may have alcohol poisoning or severe withdrawal (seizure, confusion, hallucinations), call emergency services. Mixing alcohol with opioids, benzodiazepines or other sedatives is dangerous [[FDA-OxyContin,FDA-Xanax]].

Sources

Every reference behind the AUD Companion: journal articles, guidelines, drug labels, trial registrations and survey reports. The small numbers throughout the app point here; filter by research area or search.

Eight research passes ran in parallel, one per area: epidemiology, drinking patterns, burden and history; alcohol in the body and brain; screening, diagnosis and biomarkers; alcohol withdrawal and Wernicke; medicines for AUD; psychosocial treatment, mutual help and recovery; medical harms; and nursing, harm reduction, special groups and the research frontier. Before inclusion each source was checked, where possible, against PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, ClinicalTrials.gov or openFDA, and the green tag on an entry names the service that confirmed it. Works found by more than one pass appear once, with any extra keys listed beside them.

Entries marked %t:sources.check% could not be confirmed against a database, so treat them with extra care.
