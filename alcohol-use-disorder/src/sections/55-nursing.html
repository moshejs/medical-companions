Nursing care in hospital

Many people on a medical or surgical ward drink heavily, and a hospital stay is a chance to find withdrawal early, keep people safe and start treatment. Follow one illustrative 72-hour admission, then try the priority questions.

UK inpatients, harmful use19.76%

About 1 in 5 inpatients in a meta-analysis of 124 studies (1,657,614 patients); GRADE quality very low. [[Roberts2019]]

UK inpatients, dependence10.25%

About 1 in 10. Age and type of ward explained most of the variation between studies. [[Roberts2019]]

Medically ill inpatients>40%

The PAWSS validation paper puts AUD prevalence among hospitalised medically ill patients above 40%, with complicated withdrawal in up to 20% of cases. [[Maldonado2015]]

Get medical help to stop safelyAfter long, heavy daily drinking, stopping suddenly can cause seizures or delirium tremens. This page describes published hospital protocols for teaching. It is not a set of instructions, and its scores and quiz are not self-tests. [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]]

Monitoring and red flags, as published

What ASAM's 2020 guideline asks nursing teams to watch during inpatient withdrawal, with the warning signs named in the sources. This is a teaching summary of published recommendations; local protocols govern real care. See also CIWA-Ar: scoring withdrawal and Managing withdrawal safely.

Withdrawal severityModerate to severe withdrawal, or anyone needing medication: "re-assess every 1–4 hours for 24 hours, as clinically indicated." Once stable ("e.g., CIWA-Ar score < 10 for 24 hours"), monitoring "can be extended to every 4–8 hours for 24 hours" (V.1). [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]]Example ASAM bands: mild <10, moderate 10–18, severe ≥19. Complicated: ≥19 with seizure or signs of delirium. Classification "is ultimately up to the judgment of clinicians". [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]]

[[ASAM2020Withdrawal]]Over-sedation; slowed breathing. Opioids or other sedatives on the chart: the opioid boxed warning names alcohol among the CNS depressants that may cause "profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death". [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]]A second seizure "generally happens within 6 hours of the first". Phenytoin should not be used unless there is a separate seizure disorder. [[Saitz1998AHRW,ASAM2020Withdrawal]]

Close nursing observation, often in ICU, and continuous one-to-one observation for agitated, disoriented patients (VI.7–8). [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]]Acute, fluctuating inattention with disorganised thinking or altered consciousness (CAM). In one case-control study, hyperthermia in the first 24 h (OR 6.1) and restraint use (OR 5.8) were associated with death in delirium tremens: association, not cause. [[Inouye1990,Khan2008AEM]]
Restraint"Should only be used when required to prevent injuries due to agitation or violence, and in compliance with state laws" (VI.12). [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]]See the delirium row.
Fluids, electrolytes, nutritionFluid intake and output and serum electrolytes as indicated (VII.15). Magnesium for hypomagnesaemia, arrhythmias, electrolyte disturbances or past withdrawal seizures (V.9); phosphorus if below 1 mg/dL (V.10). [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]]Low potassium that does not correct often reflects low magnesium (renal potassium wasting). Lower potassium and platelets predicted delirium tremens in a meta-analysis. [[Elisaf2002,Goodson2014]]
ThiamineThiamine "should be provided to prevent Wernicke encephalopathy"; "Typical dosing is 100 mg IV/IM per day for 3–5 days" (V.7, quoted, not an instruction). [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]]Confusion, unsteadiness or eye-movement problems: Caine criteria need 2 of 4 signs. See Thiamine, Wernicke and Korsakoff. [[Caine1997]]

[[ASAM2020Withdrawal]]In 505 older adults admitted with a fall-related fracture, PEth showed hazardous drinking in 13%. [[Rogmark2026]]

Priority questions

Exam-style scenarios about the illustrative ward. Pick the answer the published guidance supports; each explanation gives the source. These are teaching questions, not advice for any real patient.

Starting treatment before discharge

ASAM 2020: "When feasible, alcohol use disorder (AUD) treatment should be initiated concurrently with alcohol withdrawal management as cognitive status permits." [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]] Pick a study to see what happened when hospitals did this. Every study here is observational or before-and-after; there is no large randomised trial yet. [[Wei2015,Wakeman2017,Lambert2025]]

Words, attitudes and special groups

Communication

Stigma-free language changes judgements

alcoholic→person with alcohol use disordersubstance abuser→person with a substance use disorder

In a randomised study of 516 clinicians, a vignette about "a substance abuser" drew more agreement that the person was to blame and should be punished than one about a person "having a substance use disorder". [[Kelly2010Language]]

Attitudes

Negative attitudes worsen care

A review of 28 studies found health professionals generally held negative attitudes towards patients with substance use disorders. Those professionals were "less involved", more task-oriented and less empathic, and patients' empowerment and outcomes suffered. Public stigma towards AUD "remains comparatively high". [[vanBoekel2013,Kilian2021]]

Hope

Recovery is common

AUD is a treatable medical condition, not a moral failing. In a US survey, 9.1% of adults said they once had an alcohol or drug problem and no longer do: a group statistic, not a promise for any one person. ASAM asks that patients be offered "hope and the expectation of recovery". [[Kelly2017Recovery,ASAM2020Withdrawal]]

Older adults

Medicines that interact

Among US drinkers aged 65 and over, 77.8% used a prescription medicine that interacts with alcohol. Older age was linked to a lower chance of AUD medicine being started in hospital. [[Breslow2015,Anderson2026,Bernstein2026]]

Mental health

Ask about mood and suicidal thoughts

AUD is associated with major depression (OR 2.42) and anxiety disorders (OR 2.11), suicidal ideation (OR 1.86) and suicide attempt (OR 3.13): population statistics. ASAM's monitoring list includes "suicidal thoughts". [[Lai2015,Darvishi2015,ASAM2020Withdrawal]]

Sedatives

Opioids, benzodiazepines and alcohol

Both the opioid and the benzodiazepine boxed warnings name combined use with CNS depressants, including alcohol, as a cause of respiratory depression and death. Alcohol was co-involved in 14.7% of US opioid overdose deaths in 2017. [[FDA-OxyContin,FDA-Xanax,Tori2020]]

Need help now?If you or someone else may have alcohol poisoning or severe withdrawal (seizure, confusion, hallucinations), call emergency services. For help with drinking in the US, SAMHSA's National Helpline is free and confidential: 1-800-662-4357. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).
