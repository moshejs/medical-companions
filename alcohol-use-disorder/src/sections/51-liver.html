The liver: fatty liver to cirrhosis

Alcohol-associated liver disease runs from fat in the liver cells, through inflammation and scarring, to cirrhosis. Scrub through the stages, see how many people who drink heavily reach each one, and switch on “stops drinking” to see what changes. [[Singal2018ACG,Seitz2018NRDP]]

Schematic, not to scale. Time is not drawn to a scale: the pace differs greatly between people, and genetic and other factors explain much of that variation. Icon counts are our rounding of the ACG percentages; they are population statistics, not a forecast for any one person. [[Singal2018ACG,Seitz2018NRDP]]

Stopping helps at every stage, and stopping safely matters. Abstinence is the most effective way to prevent progression and the best treatment for all stages of alcohol-associated liver disease. [[Singal2018ACG,Seitz2018NRDP]] A person who has been drinking heavily every day should not stop suddenly alone: get medical help to stop safely, because withdrawal can cause seizures or confusion (see the withdrawal timeline). Medicines for alcohol use disorder can be used in many people with liver disease, as set out in approved medicines. [[Jophlin2024ACG]]

Who gets advanced disease, and why

Pattern

Daily drinking

In a Danish cohort of 55,917 adults aged 50–64, men who drank daily had a hazard ratio for alcohol-related cirrhosis of 3.65 (95% CI 2.39–5.55) versus drinking 2–4 days a week; no cases occurred among lifetime abstainers. [[Askgaard2015JHep]] A 2023 meta-analysis found daily drinking (same amount) had a pooled RR of 1.71 in men and 1.56 in women. [[LlamosasFalcon2023]]

Sex

Women at the same intake

At the same average intake, women have a higher risk of cirrhosis than men, and the dose-response is steeper for death than for illness. [[Rehm2010Cirrhosis]]

Weight

Obesity

Obesity is one of the most important environmental risk factors for cirrhosis in people who drink heavily: being overweight for at least 10 years roughly doubles the risk. [[Singal2018ACG]]

Diagnosis

Often found late

Most people are diagnosed at advanced stages. In a biopsy cohort, half of people with clinically “early” disease already had severe fibrosis or cirrhosis, with 45% 10-year mortality versus 0% for milder fibrosis. [[Singal2018ACG,Lackner2017JHep]] An AST:ALT ratio above 2 points towards advanced alcohol-related liver disease, not heavy drinking itself. [[Cohen1979SGOTSGPT,Nyblom2004ASTALT]]

Alcohol-associated hepatitis

Alcohol-associated hepatitis (AH; older name “alcoholic hepatitis”) is a sudden, serious flare of inflammation on top of heavy drinking. It presents with rapid onset or worsening of jaundice. [[Singal2018ACG,Crabb2020AASLD]]

The trials behind today’s care

Doses below are quoted as the ACG guideline published them, not as instructions: “Prednisolone is used in a dose of 40 mg per day for a total duration of 4 weeks”, with methylprednisolone 32 mg/day by vein for people unable to take tablets. [[Singal2018ACG]]

1989 · RCT

Methylprednisolone trial

In severe AH (Maddrey >32 or spontaneous encephalopathy), 28-day mortality was 35% (11/31) with placebo versus 6% (2/35) with methylprednisolone. [[Carithers1989]]

placebo 35%methylprednisolone 6%
2015 · STOPAH

Prednisolone or pentoxifylline

1,103 people, 2×2 factorial. 28-day mortality: 17% placebo–placebo, 14% prednisolone, 19% pentoxifylline, 13% both. Prednisolone OR 0.72 (95% CI 0.52–1.01; P=0.06), with no benefit at 90 days or 1 year; pentoxifylline OR 1.07. Serious infections: 13% with prednisolone versus 7% without. [[Thursz2015STOPAH]]

2011 · early transplant

French–Belgian pilot

26 highly selected people with a first episode of severe AH not responding to treatment (median Lille 0.88): 6-month survival 77% with early transplant versus 23% in matched controls. Fewer than 2% of people admitted with severe AH were selected; 3 recipients resumed drinking at 720–1,140 days. [[Mathurin2011NEJM]]

early transplant 77%matched controls 23%
2018 · ACCELERATE-AH

US early-transplant consortium

12 centres, 147 recipients, median 55 days’ abstinence before transplant: 1-year survival 94%, 3-year 84%. Any alcohol use reached 25% at 1 year and 34% at 3 years; sustained use (10% and 17%) was linked to higher mortality (HR 4.59). [[Lee2018Gastro]] Most centres still require 6 months of abstinence before evaluation, a rule the ACG calls a conflicting predictor of return to drinking. [[Singal2018ACG]]

Liver deaths are rising

US deaths from cirrhosis rose 65% from 1999 to 2016, to 34,174 a year, and people aged 25–34 had the fastest rise from 2009 (10.5% a year), “driven entirely by alcohol related liver disease”. [[Tapper2018BMJ]] Deaths with alcohol-associated liver disease as the underlying cause rose from 24,106 in 2019 to 29,504 in 2020. [[White2022JAMA]] Worldwide, cirrhosis caused more than 1.32 million deaths in 2017. [[GBD2017Cirrhosis]] Even so, abstinence remains the best treatment at every stage, and people with cirrhosis who stop drinking live longer as a group. [[Singal2018ACG,Xie2014HepatolRes]]
