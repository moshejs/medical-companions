A history of alcohol use disorder

For more than 200 years, doctors have described problem drinking as a health condition. Walk the timeline from Benjamin Rush to DSM-5 and ICD-11, and watch the name for the condition change as the ideas behind it changed. [[Witkiewitz2019,Tabakoff2013]]

The scale changes at 1930 so the crowded 20th and 21st centuries have room. Dates as given by the cited histories and by Drugs@FDA; US Prohibition is shown only by its 1933 repeal, the date the sources give. [[Witkiewitz2019,Tabakoff2013,Blocker2006,FDA-DrugsFDA-AUD]]

How the diagnosis changed

DSM-IV (1994) had two alcohol diagnoses, abuse and dependence. DSM-5 (2013) merged them into one alcohol use disorder, dropped legal problems and added craving. ICD-11 (2019) kept dependence separate from a harmful pattern of use. Switch between them and watch the criteria move. [[Hasin2013,Saunders2019ICD11]]

Criteria paraphrased and shortened. DSM-IV grouping as described in the DSM-5 work group's rationale; DSM-5-TR wording in APA 2022. [[Hasin2013,APA2022,Degenhardt2019ICD11]]

Stigma and language over time

1780s onwards

From vice to disease

Rush described habitual heavy drinking as a disease caused by alcohol itself, and ran a public health campaign on the hazards of distilled spirits that listed more than a dozen alcohol-related health problems [[Tabakoff2013,Katcher1993]]. Jellinek's 1960 book codified problem drinking as a disease of loss of control over drinking. [[Tabakoff2013,Kelly2019Jellinek]]

Names

Why the old names were retired

"Alcoholism", Huss's term in its Latin form, survives as the older, lay name [[Tabakoff2013]]. "Alcohol abuse" was a DSM-IV diagnosis, retired in 2013 [[Hasin2013]]. Words still carry blame: clinicians who read "a substance abuser" rather than "having a substance use disorder" were more likely to judge the person culpable and to favour punishment. [[Kelly2010Language]]

Established

Stigma has not gone away

People with alcohol dependence are held more responsible for their condition than people with other mental disorders [[Schomerus2011]], and a 2010–2020 review found the public's wish for social distance from people with AUD stayed consistently higher [[Kilian2021]]. Stigma is one reason the treatment gap is so wide. [[Carvalho2019Lancet,Probst2015]]

Mutual help

From temperance to AA

The temperance movement of the 1800s and early 1900s laid groundwork for later mutual-help organisations [[Witkiewitz2019]]. By 2010, AA reported some 2 million members in more than 160 countries. [[Gross2010AA]] See Talking therapies and mutual help.

Disputed

Did Prohibition "work"?

Blocker argues the usual verdict of failure rests on a weak historical base: Prohibition did lower consumption, and after repeal yearly intake was about 1.2 US gallons per person, less than half the pre-Prohibition level. The Volstead Act defined "intoxicating" as 0.5% alcohol by volume or more. Historians still dispute it. [[Blocker2006]]

Debated

One disorder or two?

DSM-5 merged abuse and dependence on data from more than 200,000 participants [[Hasin2013]]. ICD-11 keeps dependence separate from harmful use [[Saunders2019ICD11]]. Stockwell argues DSM-5 mixes dependence with its consequences, departing from Edwards and Gross, whose syndrome shaped international classifications and underpins brief interventions. [[Stockwell2015]]
