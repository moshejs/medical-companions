Alcohol and the whole body: cancer, heart, brain

Alcohol harms far more than the liver. Raise the daily amount and watch the organs light up with the relative risks reported in large studies, while an all-cause mortality curve builds beside them. Then look at why the old idea that “a little is good for the heart” has not held up.

Relative risks are population statistics versus people who do not drink, from the studies named in each step; they are not a forecast for any one person. Cancer bands follow Bagnardi 2015 (light ≤12.5 g/day, moderate ≤50, heavy >50). A “standard drink” here is the GBD’s 10 g; national definitions range from 8 to 20 g. Body map schematic. [[Bagnardi2015BJC,GBD2016Alcohol,Kalinowski2016]]

Alcohol is a Group 1 carcinogen. In 2007 the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) concluded that ethanol in alcoholic beverages is carcinogenic to humans, and added the female breast and the colorectum to the alcohol-related cancer sites. Established [[Baan2007IARC,Brooks2009]] The main suspect is acetaldehyde, the first and most toxic product of alcohol breakdown; alcohol may also disrupt DNA methylation and retinoid metabolism, raise oxidative stress, change oestrogen regulation, and act as a solvent for tobacco carcinogens. [[Seitz2007,Rumgay2021LancetOncol]] ASCO calls alcohol “an established risk factor for several malignancies” that can be changed. [[LoConte2018ASCO]]

Breast

About 7% per 10 g a day

In a pooled reanalysis of 53 studies (58,515 women with breast cancer), risk rose 7.1% for each extra 10 g of alcohol a day, in women who did and did not smoke. [[Hamajima2002BJC]] In the Million Women Study, each extra regular daily drink added about 11 breast cancers per 1,000 women up to age 75, and about 15 cancers in total. [[Allen2009JNCI]]

World burden

741,300 cancers in 2020

About 4.1% of new cancers worldwide in 2020 were attributable to alcohol. 46.7% came from heavy drinking (>60 g/day), 39.4% from risky drinking (20–60 g/day) and 13.9% (103,100 cases) from moderate drinking (<20 g/day). [[Rumgay2021LancetOncol]]

Flushing

ALDH2 and the oesophagus

About 36% of East Asians show a flushing response to alcohol, mainly from ALDH2 deficiency, which slows acetaldehyde clearance. In studies from Japan and Taiwan, people with low-activity ALDH2 who drank had odds ratios for oesophageal squamous cell cancer of 3.7 to 18.1. [[Brooks2009]] More in genes and risk.

Early evidence

After stopping

There is some evidence that the risk of laryngeal, pharyngeal and liver cancers falls after drinking stops. [[Connor2017Addiction]]

The J-curve debate: is a little alcohol good for you?

Older studies often drew a “J”: light drinkers seemed to die less often than non-drinkers. Switch between three ways of looking at the same question. The short answer, as the research stands: the research does not support drinking alcohol for health. [[Stockwell2016JSAD,Zhao2023JAMANO,Millwood2019]]

Heart rhythm and blood pressure

RCT · NEJM 2020

Abstinence and atrial fibrillation

140 people with atrial fibrillation (AF) who drank at least 10 drinks a week were randomised to abstinence or usual drinking. The abstinence group cut from 16.8 to 2.1 drinks a week. [[Voskoboinik2020NEJM]]

Recurrence over 6 months: 53% versus 73% (HR 0.55, 95% CI 0.36–0.84); median time in AF 0.5% versus 1.2%. [[Voskoboinik2020NEJM]]

Holiday heart

Rhythm after a heavy bout

“Holiday heart” was the 1978 name for heart rhythm disorders after heavy drinking; AF is the most common. [[Ettinger1978,Day2019Addiction]] In people with paroxysmal AF wearing alcohol sensors, one drink in the previous 4 hours doubled the odds of an episode (OR 2.02) and two or more more than tripled it (OR 3.58). [[Marcus2021AnnIntMed]]

Blood pressure

Cutting down lowers it

In 36 trials, cutting drinking lowered blood pressure in people who drank more than 2 drinks a day; people drinking 6 or more a day who halved intake lowered it by 5.50/3.97 mm Hg. [[Roerecke2017LPH]] Important reductions can be expected after as little as 1 month of abstinence. [[Day2019Addiction]]

Heart muscle

Alcohol-related cardiomyopathy

Generally considered to need more than 80 g a day for over 5 years; gene variants may add a “double hit” Early evidence. In people who stop drinking, recovery of heart pumping is linked with an excellent prognosis. [[Dominguez2024EHJ]]

Brain, injuries and medicines

Dementia

The strongest modifiable risk

In over 1.1 million people with dementia in French hospitals, alcohol use disorders were the strongest modifiable risk factor (adjusted HR 3.34 in women, 3.36 in men); 38.9% of early-onset dementia was alcohol-related by definition. [[Schwarzinger2018LPH]] People with alcohol-related brain damage can partly recover if abstinence is maintained. [[Ridley2013AlzResTher]] See also thiamine and Wernicke.

Brain structure

No protection from light drinking

Over 30 years, 14–21 UK units a week went with three times the odds of right hippocampal atrophy (OR 3.4) and more than 30 units with OR 5.8, versus abstinence. [[Topiwala2017]] In UK Biobank, lower brain volumes were apparent at an average of one to two daily units. [[Daviet2022]]

Injuries

Risk climbs per occasion

For motor-vehicle crashes the odds ratio rises 1.24 per 10 g and reaches 52.0 at 120 g on one occasion; the authors state that no level of drinking is safe when driving. [[Taylor2010DAD]]

Danger

Alcohol with sedatives

The US opioid boxed warning states that use with benzodiazepines or other CNS depressants, “including alcohol, may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death”. [[FDA-OxyContin]] The acetaminophen label warns of severe liver damage with 3 or more drinks every day. [[FDA-Tylenol]] If someone cannot be woken, is breathing very slowly or has a seizure after drinking, call emergency services.
