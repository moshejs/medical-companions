Standard drinks, blood alcohol & metabolism

What a "drink" means in grams of ethanol, how blood alcohol rises and then falls in a straight line, and what the liver makes from it. Scrub twelve hours of one illustrative evening, then try the teaching calculators.

How many standard drinks is it?

A "standard drink" is a fixed amount of pure ethanol, not a glass. Of 75 national governments examined, 37 had a definition: most often 10 g, ranging from 8 to 20 g. [[Kalinowski2016]] The US standard drink is 0.6 oz of ethanol, for example 12 oz of 5% beer, 5 oz of 12% wine or 1.5 oz of 40% spirits; 0.6 fl oz is about 14 g (the research team's arithmetic: 0.6 × 29.57 mL × 0.789 g/mL). [[KranzlerSoyka2018]] GBD and the WHO AUDIT assume 10 g. [[GBD2016Alcohol,HigginsBiddle2018USAUDIT]] The UK counts in 8 g units (the Cochrane brief-intervention review gives 244 g a week as 30.5 UK units). [[Kaner2018Cochrane]] The sum is always the same: volume (mL) × ABV ÷ 100 × 0.789 g/mL = grams of ethanol.

Binge, heavy and high-intensity drinking: the published definitions

TermDefinition as publishedSource
US standard drink0.6 oz of ethanol: 12 oz of 5% beer, 5 oz of 12% wine or 1.5 oz of 40% spirits.[[KranzlerSoyka2018]]
Binge drinking (NIAAA)A pattern that brings blood alcohol to about 0.08 g%: 5 or more drinks for men or 4 or more for women. An NIAAA task force recommended adding "within about 2 hours"; in one comparison, the older measure without a time limit predicted negative consequences at least as well.[[Corbin2014]]
Binge drinking (NSDUH surveys)5 or more standard drinks for males, or 4 or more for females, on the same occasion.[[KranzlerSoyka2018]]
Heavy alcohol use (NSDUH surveys)Binge drinking on 5 or more days in the past month.[[KranzlerSoyka2018]]
High-intensity drinking8 or more drinks for women, 10 or more for men: two or more times the binge thresholds.[[Patrick2023HID]]

In 2016, 26.2% of US adults reported at least one binge-drinking day in the past month and 6.6% reported heavy alcohol use. [[KranzlerSoyka2018]] These are population statistics. Most people who drink heavily do not have alcohol dependence: in NSDUH 2009–2011, 10.5% of binge drinkers met DSM-IV dependence criteria. [[Esser2014PCD]] That is not reassurance. Binge drinking still accounted for 76.7% of the US cost of excessive drinking in 2010. [[Sacks2015]] For how alcohol use disorder itself is defined, see Diagnosis: DSM-5-TR criteria.

A teaching blood alcohol calculator

This calculator cannot tell anyone whether it is safe to drive or to drink

It draws a textbook curve from published average values. Real people differ: elimination rates vary about four-fold between healthy people [[Edenberg2007]], five published Widmark-type models give different answers for the same evening [[Posey2007Widmark]], and one proposed error band is ±21% (others argue the error must be worked out case by case) [[Searle2015]]. It is not for working out your own level, and no number here means "fine". Fatal-crash risk rises steadily with BAC in every age and sex group [[Zador2000]], and a 2014 review found virtually all drivers impaired on at least some driving measures at 0.05. [[Fell2014BAC]]

Alcohol poisoning is an emergency: call emergency services. Signs progress from poor judgement, slurred speech, poor coordination and vomiting to stupor, coma and death. [[Kanny2015]] Call if someone cannot be woken, is breathing slowly or irregularly, or has a seizure. [[Kanny2015,Abrahao2017]] Do not leave them alone; cold can contribute (hypothermia was a contributing cause in 6.0% of US alcohol poisoning deaths). [[Kanny2015]] Mixing alcohol with opioids, benzodiazepines or other sedatives is dangerous. [[Tori2020]]

r values: fixed Widmark factors 0.68 and 0.55 [[Terpstra2020]]; average volume of distribution 0.70 and 0.60 L/kg [[Jones2019WIREs]]. Elimination rates from a forensic evidence review [[Jones2010]]. Absorption: about 30 minutes per drink, as in Terpstra's fixed 0.5 h. Shaded band: ±21%. [[Searle2015]]

What changes the curve, and what does not

Established

Food in the stomach

Ten men drank 0.80 g/kg once fasting and once straight after breakfast. Peak BAC was 104 mg/dL fasting and 67 mg/dL fed; the 0–6 h area under the curve was 398 against 241 mg/dL·h, and the dose was cleared about 2 h sooner after the meal. [[Jones1994Food]] Eating lowers the peak; it does not make heavy drinking safe.

Established

Body water and sex

Ethanol does not bind to plasma proteins and spreads in total body water [[Norberg2003]]: about 55–60% of body weight in non-obese men and 50–55% in women [[Jones2019WIREs]]. After the same amount, women reach higher BACs than men even allowing for body size. [[Frezza1990]]

Debated

First-pass metabolism in the stomach

Some alcohol is oxidised by gastric ADH before it reaches the blood. In women without alcohol dependence, first-pass metabolism was 23% and gastric ADH activity 59% of men's; in women with alcohol dependence it was "virtually abolished". [[Frezza1990]] How much this matters overall is debated; body water is the better-established factor. [[Cederbaum2012,Norberg2003]]

Established

Heavy drinking speeds clearance

CYP2E1 is inducible, so people who drink heavily clear ethanol faster [[Norberg2003]]: 25–35 mg/100 mL per hour during detoxification against about 15 in moderate drinkers. [[Jones2010]] In 20 people admitted for detoxification, blood ethanol on arrival was 238–489 mg/dL. Tolerance does not remove danger. [[Jones1992Detox]]

Established

Acetaldehyde: the toxic step

Acetaldehyde is a mutagen and an animal carcinogen that damages DNA; saliva holds 10–20 times more than blood because mouth bacteria make it. [[Brooks2009]] About 3.6% of cancers worldwide derive from chronic drinking. [[Seitz2007]] See Genes, family & risk for the flushing variant, and the whole body for cancer risk.

Established

No amount is good for health

GBD 2016 found the level that minimised harm across health outcomes was zero standard drinks a week. [[GBD2016Alcohol]] After bias adjustment, low-volume drinkers had no lower all-cause mortality than lifetime non-drinkers (RR 0.93). [[Zhao2023JAMANO]] Genetic evidence calls the apparent stroke protection "largely non-causal". [[Millwood2019]] The research does not support drinking for health.
