Genes, family & risk

About half of the risk for alcohol use disorder is inherited, yet no gene decides it and no test can predict it. Follow one family from population risk to twin studies, two well-known alcohol-metabolising variants and the newest genome scans.

[[Li2009ALDH2]] Inactive ALDH2 in 15–40% of East Asian populations [[Cederbaum2012]]; at least 540 million people worldwide, about 8%. [[Brooks2009]]Flushing, nausea and a fast heart rate after drinking; 5- to 20-fold higher blood acetaldehyde. Homozygotes are "virtual abstainers". [[Cederbaum2012,Brooks2009]] Oesophageal cancer: OR 3.19 for heterozygotes who drink, 0.36 for homozygotes. [[Lewis2005]]

If your face turns red when you drink, your body may be slow to clear acetaldehyde, and drinking then raises the risk of cancer of the food pipe even more. [[Brooks2009,Lewis2005]] A flushing questionnaire identified ALDH2 deficiency in Japanese men with 90% sensitivity and 88% specificity. [[Brooks2009]] Protection is not fixed: the share of Japanese people with alcohol dependence who were ALDH2 heterozygotes rose from 3% in 1979 to 13% in 1992, as drinking rose. [[Brooks2009]]

What the genome scans add, and what they cannot do

Established

Heavy drinking is not the whole story

In 274,424 Million Veteran Program participants, 18 loci were found: 5 shared by AUDIT-C and AUD, 8 for AUDIT-C only and 5 for AUD only. Heavy drinking is a key risk factor for AUD but not a sufficient cause. [[Kranzler2019]] AUD genetics only partly overlap with drinking-amount genetics. [[Walters2018]]

Established

Shared roots with other conditions

Problematic alcohol use correlated genetically with 138 phenotypes; among psychiatric disorders, depression had the highest correlation (rg 0.39), and cognitive traits correlated negatively. [[Zhou2020NatNeurosci]] AUD also correlates genetically with schizophrenia, ADHD and cigarette and cannabis use. [[Walters2018]]

Established

More ancestries, better scores

The 2023 meta-analysis of 1,079,947 people (903,147 European, 122,571 African, 38,962 Latin American, 13,551 East Asian, 1,716 South Asian ancestry) found 110 variants with similar architecture across ancestries; cross-ancestry polygenic scores beat single-ancestry ones. [[Zhou2023NatMed]]

Cannot diagnose

Polygenic scores

Combined scores explained about 2.5–3.5% of the variance in AUD and added only slightly beyond age, sex and ancestry: "modest, at best" for finding people at higher risk. [[Barr2020]] A genetic test or risk score cannot tell anyone whether they have, or will get, alcohol use disorder.

Risk is not destiny. About half of the risk comes from genes, but family environment and life events matter too, and genetic and environmental risk add up. [[Verhulst2015,Kendler2015Adoption]] Families are not to blame for a relative's AUD. Alcohol use disorder is treatable: see medicines, talking therapies and support for families.
