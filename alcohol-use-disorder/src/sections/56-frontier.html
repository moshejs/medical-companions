Evidence labels used here. Established consistent findings across several good studies. Early evidence a few small or short trials, or observational data that cannot rule out confounding. Policy research: price, availability and labels

Some of the strongest evidence for reducing alcohol harm is about populations, not pills. "Alcohol: No Ordinary Commodity" concludes that the most effective policies are taxes that reduce affordability and limits on availability. More of the frontier

Established, small effect

Digital CBT

A meta-analysis of 65 randomised trials found a small effect on consumption when digitally delivered cognitive behavioural interventions were used on their own against minimal control (g = 0.27). A single RCT of the A-CHESS recovery app (n = 349) found fewer risky drinking days over 12 months (1.39 vs 2.75). [[Kiluk2026,Gustafson2014]]

Hypothesis

Gene-matched ondansetron

In an 11-week RCT (n = 283 European Americans), certain HTR3A/HTR3B and SLC6A4 genotype combinations predicted response to ondansetron. This awaits independent replication. [[Johnson2013]]

Early evidence

Brain stimulation and craving

A meta-analysis of 20 sham-controlled RCTs of non-invasive brain stimulation found a small fall in craving (SMD −0.211); by type, only transcranial direct current stimulation reached significance. [[Kim2025NIBS]]

What research does not support

Drinking for health

Research does not support drinking for health. In a 2023 meta-analysis of 107 cohort studies that corrected for biases such as counting former drinkers as abstainers, occasional and low-volume drinkers had no significant reduction in all-cause mortality compared with lifetime non-drinkers. GBD 2016 found that the level of drinking that minimised health loss was zero. [[Zhao2023JAMANO,GBD2016Alcohol]]
