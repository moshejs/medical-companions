Safety first. Talking therapies can start at any point, but if someone has been drinking heavily every day for a long time, stopping suddenly can be dangerous (seizures, delirium tremens). Get medical help to stop safely: see the withdrawal timeline and managing withdrawal.

Goals, combinations and special situations

Goal

Drinking less, or not at all

Among very-high-risk drinkers in the US population, dropping one, two or three WHO risk levels went with lower odds of alcohol dependence 3 years later (adjusted ORs 0.27, 0.17 and 0.07), and with less liver disease, depression and anxiety. After the COMBINE trial, 85.5% of people who had cut down by at least one level still had that reduction a year later. [[Hasin2017WHO,Knox2018Liver,Knox2019Depression,Witkiewitz2019WHO]]

In people with AUD, cutting below heavy drinking went with lower mortality (OR 0.61 for substantial reduction, 0.35 for abstinence): both goals help, stopping helps most. These are observational links in people who drank heavily; they do not show that any amount of drinking is good for health or set a "safe" level. [[Roerecke2013Reduction]]

Definition

Recovery does not have to mean abstinence

The 2022 NIAAA research definition of recovery is remission from DSM-5 AUD plus cessation of heavy drinking, with weight on functioning and quality of life. In a 10-year follow-up of Project MATCH participants, high functioning at 3 years predicted the best psychological functioning at 10 years, whatever the drinking level. Early evidence [[Hagman2022,Witkiewitz2021Nonabstinent]] See course and recovery.

Combine

Medicine and therapy together

Across 30 RCTs, CBT combined with medication did better than usual care (Hedges g 0.18–0.28); the authors recommend medication plus CBT or another evidence-based therapy. In COMBINE, percent days abstinent during treatment was 80.6 with naltrexone plus medical management, 79.2 with the combined behavioural intervention, 77.1 with both and 75.1 with placebo; therapy alone with no pills or medical visits did worse (66.6). [[Ray2020CBT,Anton2006COMBINE]] See approved medicines.

Limits

What brief advice cannot do

Of 16 primary-care trials, 14 excluded some or all people with dependence or very heavy drinking, and the two that included them showed no effect. Brief interventions did not raise the use of specialist services. On hospital wards they cut drinking at 6 and 9 months, not at a year, but deaths were fewer at 6 months (RR 0.42) and 1 year (RR 0.60). [[Saitz2010,McQueen2011]]

Co-occurring

Depression and PTSD

AUD often comes with depression and other conditions (NESARC-III odds ratios 1.2–6.4). Combined CBT and MI gave small gains for drinking (g = 0.17) and depression (g = 0.27). In 165 people with alcohol dependence and PTSD, prolonged exposure therapy did not make drinking worse; all groups cut drinking days by about 61–70%. [[Grant2015NESARC,Riper2014Depression,Foa2013]] If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

Digital

Apps and websites

Personalised digital programmes cut drinking by about 23 g/week compared with minimal help. Internet programmes with a human guide beat fully automated ones, and adults over 55 responded better. After residential treatment, the A-CHESS app cut risky drinking days over 12 months (1.39 vs 2.75). [[Kaner2017Digital,Riper2018IPD,Gustafson2014]]
