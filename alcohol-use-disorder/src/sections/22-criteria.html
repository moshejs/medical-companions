From DSM-IV abuse and dependence to one disorder

DSM-IV had two alcohol diagnoses, “alcohol abuse” (the historical term) and “alcohol dependence”, arranged in a hierarchy. DSM-5 merged them into one disorder, based on consistent findings from more than 200,000 study participants: the criteria formed one factor, or two highly correlated factors. What the numbers say

Severity

Mild is the most common

In NESARC-III, 12-month prevalence was 7.3% mild, 3.2% moderate and 3.4% severe; lifetime, 8.6%, 6.6% and 13.9%. These are population estimates. [[Grant2015NESARC]]

Onset

Earlier onset, more severe

Mean age at onset was 26.2 years overall: 30.1 for mild, 25.9 for moderate and 23.9 for severe AUD. [[Grant2015NESARC]]

Treatment

Few seek help

Among people with 12-month AUD, treatment seeking was 2.7% (mild), 4.9% (moderate) and 21.3% (severe). Only 19.8% with lifetime AUD had ever been treated. [[Grant2015NESARC]]

Crosswalk

Where old trials fit

DSM-IV dependence agreed best with DSM-5 AUD at 4 or more criteria, so treatments tested in DSM-IV dependence apply best to AUD of at least moderate severity. [[Compton2013Crosswalk]]

Hypothesis

Why craving was added

Its psychometric benefit was equivocal; the view that craving may become a biological treatment target prevailed. Adding it did not substantially change diagnosis rates. [[Hasin2013,Peer2013DSM5]]

Not in the criteria

No blood test decides

Biomarkers were considered but left out of DSM-5, because they were not yet suitable as diagnostic tests. Lab tests can show recent or heavy drinking: see Blood and urine markers of drinking. [[Hasin2013]]

Recovery is common. As group statistics, not a promise for any one person: among US adults whose alcohol dependence began before the past year, 18.2% were abstinent and 17.7% low-risk drinkers in the past year, and in long-term studies 35.0–54.4% of people with substance use disorders reached remission over a mean of 17 years. See Course, relapse and recovery and Approved medicines. [[Dawson2005,Fleury2016]] Stopping suddenly after long, heavy daily drinking can be dangerous: get medical help to stop safely.
