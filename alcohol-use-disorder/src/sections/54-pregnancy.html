Pregnancy and fetal alcohol spectrum disorders

Alcohol in pregnancy can affect a developing baby at any stage. Scrub through the 40 weeks to see what the research and guidance say, the facial features doctors look for in fetal alcohol syndrome, and why early diagnosis and steady support matter. This page is about information and support, not blame: anyone who is pregnant and finding it hard to stop drinking deserves care, and it is never too late to get it.

“There is no known safe amount of alcohol consumption during pregnancy.”CDC, MMWR 2022 [[Gosdin2022MMWR]]

The American Academy of Pediatrics adds that there is no safe trimester, that beer, wine and liquor pose similar risk, and that alcohol-related birth defects and developmental disabilities are completely preventable when no alcohol is used in pregnancy. [[Williams2015AAP]] If you are pregnant or planning a pregnancy, plan with your care team; it is never too late to get support.

Diagnosing FASD: the 2016 clinical guidelines

FASD is the umbrella term for the range of effects of prenatal alcohol exposure; fetal alcohol syndrome (FAS) was first described in 1973. [[Williams2015AAP]] The 2016 guidelines draw on the evaluation of more than 10,000 children. [[Hoyme2016Pediatrics]]

Category (Institute of Medicine)What the 2016 criteria require
Fetal alcohol syndrome (FAS)≥2 of 3 facial features: short palpebral fissures (≤10th centile), thin vermilion border of the upper lip, smooth philtrum (rank 4 or 5 on a racially normed lip/philtrum guide); plus growth deficiency (height and/or weight ≤10th centile), deficient brain growth or abnormal brain structure or function (for example head circumference ≤10th percentile), and neurobehavioural impairment. Can be diagnosed with or without documented exposure. [[Hoyme2016Pediatrics]]
Partial FAS (PFAS)With documented prenatal exposure: the facial pattern plus neurobehavioural impairment. [[Hoyme2016Pediatrics]]
Alcohol-related neurodevelopmental disorder (ARND)One of the four Institute of Medicine categories. [[Hoyme2016Pediatrics]]
Alcohol-related birth defects (ARBD)One of the four Institute of Medicine categories. [[Hoyme2016Pediatrics]]

FASD rarely comes alone: a meta-analysis found 428 co-occurring conditions across 18 of 22 ICD-10 chapters, with conduct disorder, language disorders, chronic middle-ear fluid and sensory problems each in 50–91% of people with FAS. [[Popova2016Lancet]] In 9,719 children aged 9–10, any reported prenatal exposure was linked with more attention problems and impulsiveness Early evidence for low-level exposure; these were retrospective parental reports. [[Lees2020AJP]]

Support in pregnancy

Screening

Asking is routine

The USPSTF recommends screening adults 18 and older for unhealthy alcohol use in primary care, including pregnant women, with brief counselling for risky drinking (B recommendation). [[USPSTF2018Alcohol]] In pregnancy, counselling increased reported abstinence (OR 2.26). [[OConnor2018JAMA]] AUDIT-C at 3 or more had 96% sensitivity for alcohol use disorder in pregnancy. [[Burns2010Pregnancy]] See screening.

Withdrawal

Stopping safely

Stopping suddenly after long, heavy daily drinking can be dangerous: get medical help to stop safely. ASAM 2020 says to consider inpatient care for every pregnant patient who needs withdrawal management, offer it at CIWA-Ar ≥10, and consult an obstetrician (quoted as published, not as instructions). [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]] See managing withdrawal.

Medicines

Plan with the care team

The oral naltrexone label says to use it in pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk; there are no adequate human studies. [[FDA-Naltrexone]] Medicine choices in pregnancy are made with the care team.

Access

Care, not blame

In US data for 2018–2020, 13.5% of pregnant adults reported current drinking and 5.2% binge drinking. Those with frequent mental distress were 2.3 times as likely to drink, and those without a usual provider 1.7 times: access to care and mental health support are the levers. [[Gosdin2022MMWR]] Worldwide, an estimated 9.8% of pregnancies involve alcohol use. [[Popova2017LancetGH]]

Children

Early diagnosis, steady homes

In 415 people with FAS or fetal alcohol effects, being diagnosed younger and growing up in a stable, nurturing home raised the odds of avoiding adverse life outcomes 2- to 4-fold. [[Streissguth2004JDBP]] Early recognition and therapy improve outcomes. [[Williams2015AAP]]

Help

Where to start

For help with drinking in the US, SAMHSA’s National Helpline is free and confidential: 1-800-662-4357. Families can find support in families, friends and everyday life.

Milk levelsBreast milk alcohol levels closely follow blood levels and peak 30 to 60 minutes after a drink; food delays the peak. [[LactMed-Alcohol]]
Effects on feedingNursing after 1 or 2 drinks can reduce the infant’s milk intake by 20–23% and cause agitation and poor sleep; 5 or more drinks can reduce let-down. [[LactMed-Alcohol]]
