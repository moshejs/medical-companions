Not medical adviceThe AUD Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. It does not replace a doctor, nurse, pharmacist, counsellor or other clinician. The AUDIT, the criteria counter, CIWA-Ar and the other calculators are teaching aids, not self-tests, and doses, benzodiazepine and thiamine regimens and CIWA-Ar thresholds are quoted as published, never as instructions. Stopping suddenly after long, heavy daily drinking can be dangerous: get medical help to stop safely. If you or someone else may have alcohol poisoning or severe withdrawal (seizure, confusion, hallucinations), call emergency services. For help with drinking in the US, SAMHSA's National Helpline is free and confidential: 1-800-662-4357. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

Part 1 · Understanding AUD

What alcohol use disorder is

Alcohol use disorder (AUD) is a medical condition defined by heavy alcohol use that a person finds hard to control, kept up despite harm. It is common, it is treatable, and most people who have it never receive treatment. Press Play to fill 100 US adults from the national survey. 56,146

Step 1 · The spectrum

A spectrum, not a switch

Drinking runs along a spectrum. The USPSTF uses "unhealthy alcohol use" for everything from risky drinking to alcohol use disorder 470. In 2012–2013, 72.7% of US adults drank alcohol in the past year 148. Binge drinking in US federal surveys means 5 or more drinks (males) or 4 or more (females) on one occasion 261. Most people who drink heavily do not have dependence: in NSDUH 2009–2011, 10.2% of excessive drinkers met DSM-IV dependence criteria and about 90% did not 102. "Lower risk" is not "no risk": GBD 2016 found the level that minimised harm was zero drinks a week. 137

In plain wordsDrinking is not simply "fine" or "a disorder". Most people who sometimes drink too much do not have AUD, but heavy drinking still carries risk.

Who is affected

AUD can begin or continue at any age and in any group, but the survey numbers are not even. Pick a view. Group figures describe populations, never a single person. 146

Who is affected · sex

The gap between men and women is closing

In NESARC-III, 12-month AUD was 17.6% in men and 10.4% in women, and severe AUD 4.5% and 2.3% 146. Across 68 studies of birth cohorts from 1891 to 2001, the male-to-female ratio for problematic alcohol use fell from 3.0 in cohorts born in the early 1900s to 1.2 in those born in the late 1900s, most clearly among young adults 435. In the US, alcohol-related deaths rose 12.5% a year in men and 14.7% a year in women from 2018 to 2020. 222

In plain wordsMen still have AUD more often, but women are catching up, especially among younger people.

How much harm alcohol causes

Burden studies count the harm from all alcohol use, not only from the disorder. Different modelling groups give different totals for similar years: always read a figure with its source. 137,430,431

Totals differ because the models and risk curves differ, not because deaths fell that fast. 137,139,430,431

Burden · the world

Millions of deaths a year, by any model

GBD 2016 attributed 2.8 million deaths in 2016 to alcohol use 137. The WHO comparative risk model gave 3.0 million for 2016, 5.3% of all deaths, with 52.4% of alcohol-attributable deaths in people under 60 and 17.7% of all injury deaths caused by alcohol 430; its update gave 2.6 million (4.7%) for 2019 431. GBD 2020 estimated 1.78 million for 2020, and 1.34 billion people drinking harmful amounts. 139

In plain wordsAlcohol causes millions of deaths every year around the world. Experts disagree on the exact number, not on the scale.

Established

The most common substance use disorder

GBD 2016 counted 100.4 million people with AUD worldwide, more than any other substance use disorder, and attributed 99.2 million DALYs (4.2% of all DALYs) to alcohol use. 136

Established

The leading risk factor at ages 15–49

Globally alcohol was the 7th leading risk factor for deaths and DALYs in 2016, and the leading one at ages 15–49, causing 12.2% of male and 3.8% of female deaths in that age group. 137

Not for health

No level was best for health

GBD 2016 found the level of drinking that minimised harm across health outcomes was zero standard drinks a week. GBD 2020 put the minimum-risk level at 0–0.6 drinks a day for people aged 15–39. The research does not support drinking for health, and neither study supports starting to drink. 137,139

Trend

More drinking, fewer deaths per drinker?

Adult consumption worldwide rose from 5.9 L of pure alcohol a year in 1990 to 6.5 L in 2017 312. A WHO-funded model found alcohol-attributable deaths fell 31.0% from 2000 to 2019, with the burden falling hardest on Eastern Europe, Central and Southern Sub-Saharan Africa, and young people. 431

A medical condition, and the words we use

Medical, not moral

AUD is a treatable medical condition, not a moral failing

A Lancet review defines AUD by compulsive heavy alcohol use and loss of control over intake, and notes that effective, cost-effective psychosocial and drug treatments exist 56. Drug dependence has been compared with other chronic illnesses such as diabetes, hypertension and asthma, which have similar adherence and relapse rates and benefit from long-term care. 325

Recovery is common at population level: in a US probability survey, 9.1% of adults said they used to have an alcohol or drug problem and no longer do. That is a group figure, not a promise for any one person. 230

Person-first language

Why this guide says "a person with AUD"

In a randomised study of 516 mental health clinicians, a vignette calling someone "a substance abuser" made readers more likely to judge the person personally culpable and to favour punishment than "having a substance use disorder" 226. In 1,288 people, "alcoholic", "addict" and "substance abuser" carried strongly negative associations. 21

"Alcoholism" is the older, lay name. "Alcohol abuse" appears here only as the historical DSM-IV diagnosis: see the history module.

Established

Stigma is higher for AUD

Population studies show people with alcohol dependence are held more responsible for their condition than people with other mental disorders, and face more social rejection and structural discrimination 417. A 2010–2020 update of 16 studies found the wish for social distance stayed consistently higher for AUD. 240

Established

Why people do not seek treatment

Of 1,008 European primary-care patients with AUD, 810 had no treatment. Of those who gave a reason, 55.3% named lack of problem awareness, 28.6% stigma or shame, 22.8% barriers to care and 20.9% wanting to cope alone 372. In NESARC, people with AUD who perceived more stigma were less likely to use alcohol services (OR 0.37). 237

Established

Medicines are rarely used

In NSDUH 2019 only 10.9% of adults in alcohol treatment received a medicine for AUD, against 40.9% of adults in opioid treatment who received a medicine for opioid use disorder 35. In a US retail study, fewer than 9% of patients who needed AUD treatment received a single prescription for an approved medicine. 261 See Approved medicines.

Depression and anxiety

Pooling 22 population surveys (1990–2014), AUD was associated with major depression (OR 2.42) and any anxiety disorder (OR 2.11), more strongly for dependence than for abuse. These pooled odds ratios are higher than NESARC-III's because most surveys adjusted less. 146,270

Other conditions

AUD was the most common substance use disorder in clinical samples of people with bipolar disorder, at 42% 192. In treatment populations for substance use disorders, 23.1% met criteria for ADHD 474. In US veterans with lifetime PTSD, the adjusted OR for AUD was 2.1. 438

AUD and suicide

In longitudinal studies, AUD was associated with higher suicide mortality (pooled OR 2.68 in males, 2.39 in females); the authors conclude that treating AUD belongs in suicide prevention 273. In Nordic registers, people hospitalised with AUD died by suicide at 9.3–35.9 times the general-population rate. 494

SafetyStopping suddenly after long, heavy daily drinking can be dangerous (seizures, delirium tremens): get medical help to stop safely. 20,332 Alcohol poisoning is an emergency: call emergency services. Mixing alcohol with opioids, benzodiazepines or other sedatives is dangerous: together they can slow or stop breathing. 20,122 See Alcohol withdrawal.

AUD Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on alcohol use disorder and alcohol withdrawal, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people with alcohol use disorder, their families and friends.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine or a plan for stopping drinking without your own care team. The AUDIT, the criteria counter, CIWA-Ar, PAWSS and the blood alcohol and liver calculators are for learning, not self-diagnosis or reading your own results, and the blood alcohol calculator can never tell anyone whether it is safe to drive. Doses and regimens are quoted as published in labels and guidelines, never as instructions. Alcohol poisoning and severe withdrawal are emergencies.
