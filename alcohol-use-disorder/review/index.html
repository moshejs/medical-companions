Not medical adviceThe AUD Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. It does not replace a doctor, nurse, pharmacist, counsellor or other clinician. The AUDIT, the criteria counter, CIWA-Ar and the other calculators are teaching aids, not self-tests, and doses, benzodiazepine and thiamine regimens and CIWA-Ar thresholds are quoted as published, never as instructions. Stopping suddenly after long, heavy daily drinking can be dangerous: get medical help to stop safely. If you or someone else may have alcohol poisoning or severe withdrawal (seizure, confusion, hallucinations), call emergency services. For help with drinking in the US, SAMHSA's National Helpline is free and confidential: 1-800-662-4357. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

Flashcards & memory aids

Flashcards on alcohol use disorder, from standard drinks and the GABA–glutamate balance to AUDIT-C, DSM-5-TR criteria, CIWA-Ar, thiamine, the approved medicines, recovery and the liver. Tap a card or press Space to flip it; arrow keys move through the deck. Filter by part, or shuffle.

These cards are for study. The scores and thresholds on them (AUDIT, CIWA-Ar, PAWSS, Maddrey and others) are teaching facts, not self-tests, and the doses are quoted as published, not as instructions.

Mnemonics

Cards tagged well known are acronyms in common use; cards tagged memory aid were written for this guide to hold a published fact in mind. Neither replaces the source.

Memory aid in motion: the withdrawal clock, 6 · 12 · 24 · 48

Play the clock to watch the four numbers light up as the hours pass after the last drink: shakes from 6 hours, hallucinosis from 12, seizures peaking near 24, and delirium tremens from 48, with both published onset windows shown. The person's signs are drawn schematically.

AUD Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on alcohol use disorder and alcohol withdrawal, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people with alcohol use disorder, their families and friends.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine or a plan for stopping drinking without your own care team. The AUDIT, the criteria counter, CIWA-Ar, PAWSS and the blood alcohol and liver calculators are for learning, not self-diagnosis or reading your own results, and the blood alcohol calculator can never tell anyone whether it is safe to drive. Doses and regimens are quoted as published in labels and guidelines, never as instructions. Alcohol poisoning and severe withdrawal are emergencies. Please check the original sources, which are listed in full in the Sources module.
