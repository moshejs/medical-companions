# H · Nursing, harm reduction, special groups & the research frontier

Pass date: 2026-11-04. Modules served: nursing, families, frontier, recovery.

This pass covers four areas. The first is hospital nursing care for people with alcohol use disorder (AUD): how common AUD is on the wards, screening on admission, monitoring during withdrawal, delirium, nutrition and electrolytes, stigma-free communication, and linking people to treatment at discharge. The second is special groups: adolescents, older adults, women, and people with co-occurring mental disorders and suicide risk. The third is harm reduction and policy: mixing alcohol with opioids or benzodiazepines, alcohol poisoning, drink-driving, managed alcohol programmes, pricing and warning labels. The fourth is the research frontier: GLP-1 receptor agonists, psilocybin, ketamine, brain stimulation, mifepristone, pharmacogenetics, digital therapeutics and the alcohol-associated hepatitis pipeline. Every frontier item gives the study type, n, effect and an evidence label.

## Findings

### nursing · How common AUD is in hospital
- A UK meta-analysis of 124 studies (1,657,614 patients) found that 19.76% (95% CI 15.61–24.26%) of inpatients had harmful alcohol use and 10.25% (95% CI 7.06–13.96%) had alcohol dependence. That is about one in five and one in ten. GRADE rated both estimates very low quality [[Roberts2019]].
- In the same meta-analysis, mean patient age and type of inpatient setting were the main sources of variation in prevalence. Date of data collection was not [[Roberts2019]].
- The PAWSS validation paper states that the prevalence of AUD among hospitalised medically ill patients "exceeds 40%" and that complicated withdrawal (seizures, delirium tremens) occurs in up to 20% of cases [[Maldonado2015]].
- Most people with AUD in hospital have uncomplicated withdrawal that needs only supportive care. The smaller group at risk of complicated withdrawal is the one that screening on admission aims to find [[Maldonado2015]].

### nursing · Screening on admission (teaching tools, not self-tests)
- AUDIT-C is three consumption questions. In the original primary-care validation (n = 243), its AUROC for detecting heavy drinking was 0.891, against 0.881 for the full 10-item AUDIT [[Bush1998AUDITC]].
- The full AUDIT did better than AUDIT-C for active DSM-IV abuse or dependence (AUROC 0.811 vs 0.786). The two performed alike for "heavy drinking and/or active abuse or dependence" (0.880 vs 0.881) [[Bush1998AUDITC]].
- The Prediction of Alcohol Withdrawal Severity Scale (PAWSS) has 10 items, built from a systematic review of 233 articles on risk factors for complicated withdrawal (hallucinosis, seizures, delirium tremens) [[Maldonado2014]].
- In the 68-patient PAWSS pilot, sensitivity, specificity, PPV and NPV were all 100% at a threshold score of 4 [[Maldonado2014]].
- In the prospective validation (n = 403 medical and surgical inpatients) at a cutoff of 4, PAWSS had:
  - sensitivity 93.1% (95% CI 77.2–99.2%)
  - specificity 99.5% (95% CI 98.1–99.9%)
  - PPV 93.1% and NPV 99.5%
  - inter-rater concordance 0.963 [[Maldonado2015]]
- ASAM 2020 states that, among hospitalised patients, PAWSS "can be used for predicting risk of developing severe or complicated alcohol withdrawal in the medically ill" (Recommendation VII.12) [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- Screening itself may help. A Cochrane review of hospital brief interventions noted that "screening, asking participants about their drinking patterns, may also have a positive impact on alcohol consumption levels" [[McQueen2011]].
- Midlife and older adults in the emergency department: 39,912 of 698,308 encounters (5.7%) among people aged ≥55 screened positive on AUDIT-C (≥4 for men, ≥3 for women). Brief intervention or referral happened in 30% of these encounters. Medicine for AUD was prescribed in only 3% [[Lebin2026]].

### nursing · Withdrawal monitoring as published (CIWA-Ar)
- Get medical help to stop drinking safely: after long, heavy daily drinking, stopping suddenly can cause seizures or delirium tremens. Everything in this section describes published hospital protocols, not instructions [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- The CIWA-Ar is a shortened 10-item scale for measuring how severe alcohol withdrawal is. It was designed to fit into usual clinical care and into drug trials [[Sullivan1989CIWA]].
- ASAM 2020 Recommendation V.1 (inpatient) gives this monitoring schedule as published:
  - Moderate to severe withdrawal, or anyone needing medication: "re-assess every 1–4 hours for 24 hours, as clinically indicated."
  - Once stable ("e.g., CIWA-Ar score < 10 for 24 hours"): monitoring "can be extended to every 4–8 hours for 24 hours." [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]]
- ASAM 2020 states that patients with mild withdrawal and low risk of complicated withdrawal "may be observed for up to 36 hours, after which more severe withdrawal is unlikely to develop" [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- ASAM 2020 gives example CIWA-Ar severity bands as published: mild "<10", moderate "10–18" and severe "≥19" (the ≥ sign was lost in PDF text extraction and is restored from context) [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- ASAM 2020 Recommendation V.2 lists what nurses should monitor: vital signs, hydration, orientation, sleep, and emotional status "including suicidal thoughts" [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- ASAM 2020 Recommendation V.3: monitor patients who receive withdrawal medication "for signs of over-sedation and respiratory depression" [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- After an alcohol withdrawal seizure, ASAM 2020 says patients should be admitted where close monitoring is available and "re-assessed every 1-2 hours for 6–24 hours." They should also be watched for delirium and the need for IV fluids (Recommendation VI.2) [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- Symptom-triggered dosing: in a double-blind RCT (n = 101, a VA detoxification unit), symptom-triggered chlordiazepoxide was compared with a fixed four-times-daily schedule. Median treatment duration was 9 h vs 68 h. Total chlordiazepoxide was 100 mg vs 425 mg. Withdrawal severity, seizures and delirium tremens did not differ [[Saitz1994]].
- Restraints: ASAM 2020 says they "should only be used when required to prevent injuries due to agitation or violence, and in compliance with state laws" (Recommendation VI.12) [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].

### nursing · Supportive care, falls and delirium
- ASAM 2020 Recommendation V.5 lists the non-drug interventions: frequent reassurance, re-orientation to time and place, and nursing care. Patients should be told what to expect during withdrawal. Patients with severe withdrawal should be cared for in "an evenly lit, quiet room" and offered "hope and the expectation of recovery" [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- ASAM 2020 Recommendation V.6: supportive care includes safety measures and protocols, "e.g., assess risk for fall/syncope." Existing hospital delirium protocols can be used for patients with severe withdrawal [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- The Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) takes less than 5 minutes. Its algorithm requires (1) acute onset and fluctuating course and (2) inattention, plus either (3) disorganised thinking or (4) altered level of consciousness [[Inouye1990]].
- In the CAM validation (56 patients aged 65–98), sensitivity was 100% and 94% at the two sites, and specificity 95% and 90%, against psychiatrist diagnosis [[Inouye1990]].
- Falls in older adults: in a Swedish cohort of 505 people aged ≥65 admitted with a fall-related fracture, the blood marker PEth showed hazardous drinking in 13% and intermediate drinking in 8.9% [[Rogmark2026]].
- In that cohort, 18% of the non-frail patients had hazardous-range PEth. Alcohol use was associated with hyponatraemia (OR 2.3, 95% CI 1.1–4.5) [[Rogmark2026]].

### nursing · Nutrition, thiamine, electrolytes and refeeding
- Wernicke encephalopathy is an acute neuropsychiatric syndrome caused by thiamine deficiency. Autopsy studies suggest it is still "greatly underdiagnosed" [[Sechi2007]].
- ASAM 2020 Recommendation V.7, as published: thiamine "should be provided to prevent Wernicke encephalopathy." IV or IM is preferred for people with poor nutrition, malabsorption or severe complications. "Typical dosing is 100 mg IV/IM per day for 3–5 days." This is quoted from the guideline, not given as instructions [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- On glucose, ASAM 2020 says patients also receiving glucose "can be administered thiamine and glucose in any order or concurrently." Glucose should not be delayed in a nutritionally compromised patient. This differs from the older teaching that thiamine must always come first (see Open questions) [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- ASAM 2020 Recommendation V.8: give thiamine to patients admitted to the ICU to treat alcohol withdrawal [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- ASAM 2020 Recommendation V.9: give magnesium to patients with hypomagnesaemia, cardiac arrhythmias, electrolyte disturbances or a history of withdrawal seizures. Recommendation IV.44/V.40 adds that magnesium given only as prophylaxis or treatment for withdrawal itself "has no supporting evidence" [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- ASAM 2020 Recommendation V.10: supplement phosphorus if it is below 1 mg/dL. For moderate hypophosphataemia (1–2 mg/dL), "correction through proper nutrition is recommended" [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- In a hospital study of 127 people admitted for alcohol-related causes, mean serum potassium was lower than in controls (3.8 vs 4.6 mmol/L). Inappropriate potassium loss in the urine, mainly from co-existing hypomagnesaemia, was the main mechanism [[Elisaf2002]].
- A 2025 systematic review of 30 studies found that a previous history of alcohol misuse was one of the risk factors for refeeding syndrome reported by two or more studies after adjustment [[Zheng2025]].
- In a small prospective cohort (n = 36) admitted for managed withdrawal, no patient developed refeeding syndrome after 3 days, although 44% had hypomagnesaemia. Low thiamine levels were found in 3/29 (10%) [[Manning2014]].

### nursing · Stigma-free communication
- In a randomised study of 516 clinicians, those who read a vignette describing "a substance abuser" agreed more that the person was personally to blame and should face punishment than those who read "having a substance use disorder" [[Kelly2010]].
- A systematic review of 28 studies found that health professionals generally held negative attitudes towards patients with substance use disorders. These attitudes reduced patients' sense of empowerment and worsened treatment outcomes [[vanBoekel2013]].
- The same review found that professionals with negative attitudes were "less involved," took a more task-oriented approach and showed less empathy [[vanBoekel2013]].
- Compared with people with other mental disorders, the public is less likely to see people with alcohol dependence as mentally ill. They are held more responsible for their condition, meet more social rejection and face more structural discrimination [[Schomerus2011]].
- An updated review (16 unique studies, 9 countries) found that public stigma towards people with AUD "remains comparatively high." The desire for social distance was consistently higher than for substance-unrelated mental disorders [[Kilian2021]].

### nursing · Starting treatment in hospital and linking at discharge
- ASAM 2020 Recommendation IV.12: "When feasible, alcohol use disorder (AUD) treatment should be initiated concurrently with alcohol withdrawal management as cognitive status permits." Clinicians should offer to start medicine for AUD or link patients to evidence-based services and recovery support groups [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- San Francisco General Hospital, before and after a resident-led discharge-planning protocol for patients admitted with alcohol dependence:
  - medicine for AUD rose from 0% to 64% (p < 0.001)
  - all-cause 30-day readmission fell from 23.4% to 8.2% (p = 0.042)
  - 30-day ED visits fell from 18.8% to 6.1% (p = 0.056) [[Wei2015]]
- In a quasi-experimental study of 399 inpatients, an addiction consult team offered medicine, motivational counselling and direct linkage. At 30 days after discharge, the consulted group gained more days of abstinence (+12.7 vs +5.6 days, p < 0.001) and had a larger fall in ASI-alcohol score (0.24 vs 0.08) [[Wakeman2017]].
- In 19,697 admissions for substance use disorder (2019–2023), addiction consultation was linked to more use of medicine for AUD during the stay (33.4% vs 6.0%) and a much higher AUD medicine discharge prescription rate (RR 9.48, 95% CI 8.05–11.16) [[Lambert2025]].
- In the same study, addiction consultation was associated with lower 30-day readmission (aRR 0.82, 95% CI 0.75–0.89). For AUD, discharge medicine (aRR 0.84) and newly started medicine (aRR 0.78, 95% CI 0.629–0.977) were each associated with fewer readmissions. This is an observational design [[Lambert2025]].
- US Veterans Health Administration, 29,041 hospitalisations for AUD (2022–2023):
  - medicine for AUD was started in hospital or within 7 days in 30.8%
  - the medicines were naltrexone 57.9%, acamprosate 16.5% and injectable naltrexone 13.9%
  - starting was less likely in people aged ≥65, frail veterans and ICU patients, and more likely with an addiction consult [[Anderson2026]]
- Across 12 hospitals (5,993 alcohol withdrawal hospitalisations, 2022–2024), 19.8% started medicine for AUD (range 5.1–43.2% by hospital). The only hospital with an addiction consult service was one of the two high-rate sites [[Bernstein2026]].
- Cochrane review of hospital brief interventions (14 studies, 4,041 mainly male participants): alcohol consumption fell more than in controls at 6 and 9 months, but the effect was not maintained at 1 year. Deaths were fewer at 6 months (RR 0.42, 95% CI 0.19–0.94) and 1 year (RR 0.60, 95% CI 0.40–0.91) [[McQueen2011]].
- Discharge safety with naltrexone (label wording as published): people being treated for alcohol dependence who also use opioids "should be opioid-free before starting VIVITROL." The label recommends a minimum 7–10 days opioid-free to avoid severe precipitated withdrawal [[FDA-Vivitrol]].
- The Vivitrol label also warns that after treatment, opioid tolerance is reduced, so patients are "vulnerable to potentially fatal overdose" at the end of a dosing interval, after a missed dose or after stopping. It advises strongly considering an opioid reversal agent such as naloxone [[FDA-Vivitrol]].

### nursing · Adolescents and early onset
- In the 1992 NLAES (27,616 current and former drinkers), lifetime alcohol dependence was more than 40% among people who started drinking at 14 or younger, against roughly 10% among those who started at 20 or older [[Grant1997]].
- In the same survey, after adjustment, the odds of dependence fell by 14% for each later year of age at first drink [[Grant1997]].
- In NESARC 2001–2002 (43,093 adults), people who started drinking before 14, compared with those who started at 21 or older, had:
  - an adjusted HR of 1.78 (95% CI 1.51–2.11) for alcohol dependence ever
  - an adjusted OR of 3.09 (95% CI 2.19–4.35) for multiple dependence episodes [[Hingson2006Arch]]
- Of people who were ever alcohol dependent, 15% were diagnosable before age 18, 47% before 21 and two-thirds before 25. Those first dependent at younger ages were less likely ever to seek help [[Hingson2006Peds]].
- The USPSTF (2018) found "insufficient" evidence (I statement) to assess benefits and harms of screening and brief counselling in primary care for adolescents aged 12–17. For adults aged 18 and over, including pregnant women, it gave a B recommendation [[USPSTF2018Alcohol]].

### nursing · Older adults
- In US NSDUH data for adults aged ≥50 (2005/2006 to 2013/2014), past-month binge drinking rose by a relative 19.2% and AUD by a relative 23.3%, with significant rises among women [[Han2017]].
- In NHANES 1999–2010, 41.5% of current drinkers used a prescription medicine that interacts with alcohol. Among drinkers aged ≥65 it was 77.8%, mostly cardiovascular, central nervous system and metabolic agents [[Breslow2015]].
- Between NESARC (2001–2002) and NESARC-III (2012–2013), 12-month DSM-IV AUD rose from 8.5% to 12.7% of US adults. The increases were greatest among women, older adults, racial and ethnic minorities, and people with lower education and income [[Grant2017JAMAPsychiatry]].
- In the VA hospital cohort, people aged ≥65 were less likely to have medicine for AUD started [[Anderson2026]]. In the 12-hospital study, age ≥65 meant an adjusted −6.89 percentage points in starting medicine compared with ages 18–29 [[Bernstein2026]].

### nursing · Women
- A narrative review describes "telescoping": women seem to progress faster to AUD and its health consequences. Proposed reasons include greater biological vulnerability to alcohol toxicity and an internalising (negative-emotion) pathway. The review frames this as a hypothesis with supporting evidence, not a settled fact (Early evidence) [[Kirsch2025]].
- In a meta-analysis of 107 cohort studies, female drinkers had a significantly higher risk of all-cause mortality than female lifetime non-drinkers (RR 1.22) [[Zhao2023JAMANetwOpen]].

### nursing · Co-occurring mental disorders and suicide risk (safe messaging)
- If you or someone else is in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact a crisis line: in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland, Samaritans 116 123. This section gives population statistics only.
- In a meta-analysis of 22 epidemiological surveys, AUD was associated with:
  - major depression: pooled OR 2.42 (95% CI 2.22–2.64)
  - any anxiety disorder: OR 2.11 (95% CI 2.03–2.19) [[Lai2015]]
- A meta-analysis of 31 observational studies (420,732 participants) found that AUD was associated with:
  - suicidal ideation: OR 1.86 (95% CI 1.38–2.35)
  - suicide attempt: OR 3.13 (95% CI 2.45–3.81)
  - death by suicide: OR 2.59 (95% CI 1.95–3.23); RR 1.74 (95% CI 1.26–2.21) [[Darvishi2015]]
- Drinking in the hours before is also a risk factor. Across seven case-control and case-crossover studies, any acute alcohol use was associated with suicide attempt (OR 6.97, 95% CI 4.77–10.17). Heavy acute use carried a much larger OR (37.18) than low levels (2.71) [[Borges2017]].
- In practice, the ASAM 2020 nursing monitoring list includes "emotional status including suicidal thoughts" during withdrawal [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].

### families · Mixing alcohol with opioids or sedatives
- The opioid label boxed warning (OxyContin, current US label) states that using opioids together with benzodiazepines or other CNS depressants, "including alcohol, may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death" [[FDA-OxyContin]].
- The benzodiazepine boxed warning (Xanax label) says co-use with opioids may cause the same outcomes. It adds that abuse and misuse of benzodiazepines "commonly involve concomitant use of other medications, alcohol, and/or illicit substances" [[FDA-Xanax]].
- Of 399,230 US opioid overdose deaths (1999–2017), alcohol was co-involved in 12.4% in 1999 and 14.7% in 2017. Benzodiazepines were co-involved in 8.7% and 21.0% respectively [[Tori2020]].
- States with higher binge-drinking rates had more alcohol co-involvement in opioid overdose deaths (r = 0.34) [[Tori2020]].

### families · Alcohol poisoning is an emergency
- If someone may have alcohol poisoning, call emergency services. In 2010–2012, an average of 2,221 people aged ≥15 died of alcohol poisoning each year in the US, about six a day [[Kanny2015]].
- 75.7% of those deaths were in adults aged 35–64 and 76.4% were in men. The highest age-adjusted rate was among American Indian and Alaska Native people (49.1 per million) [[Kanny2015]].

### families · Family support and couples approaches
- In a meta-analysis of four RCTs (264 concerned significant others), Community Reinforcement and Family Training (CRAFT) got the family member into treatment more often than Al-Anon/Nar-Anon (RR 3.25, 95% CI 2.11–5.02; NNT 2) and the Johnson Institute intervention (RR 2.15; NNT 3) [[Roozen2010]].
- CRAFT helped about two-thirds of treatment-resistant people start treatment, usually after 4–6 sessions. Family members improved psychologically whichever approach they received [[Roozen2010]].
- A meta-analysis of 12 RCTs (n = 754) found behavioural couples therapy better than individual-based treatment overall (Cohen's d = 0.54). At follow-up the gain covered frequency of use (d = 0.45), consequences (d = 0.50) and relationship satisfaction (d = 0.51) [[Powers2008]].

### recovery · Recovery is common (population statistics)
- In a US probability survey (n = 39,809 contacted), 9.1% of adults said they used to have a problem with alcohol or drugs but no longer do. Of those, 46% called themselves "in recovery" [[Kelly2017Recovery]].
- About half (53.9%) of those who resolved a problem used an "assisted" pathway: mutual help 45.1%, treatment 27.6% and recovery support services 21.8%. The rest resolved without formal help [[Kelly2017Recovery]].
- In the same sample, quality of life, happiness, self-esteem and recovery capital rose steeply in the first 6 years after resolving a problem, then more gradually. Self-esteem and happiness dipped in the first year before rising [[Kelly2018QoL]].

### recovery · Harm reduction: managed alcohol programmes
- Managed alcohol programmes (MAPs) give regulated doses of alcohol through the day to people with alcohol dependence who are homeless or unstably housed. They also offer housing, health and social support [[Carver2026]].
- A realist review of 60 sources concluded that MAPs work best when they enable autonomy, meet clients' needs, create hope and purpose, and give access to healthcare (Early evidence) [[Carver2026]].
- In the first multisite controlled study (59 MAP participants in six Canadian cities vs 116 local controls), both groups cut drinks per day by similar amounts over 12 months (−8.11 vs −8.54). MAP participants reported significantly fewer harms at 0–2 and 6 months (Early evidence) [[Stockwell2021]].
- In that study, liver markers (AST, bilirubin) got worse after participants left a MAP [[Stockwell2021]].
- Qualitative interviews describe MAPs as breaking a "revolving" cycle through health, justice and shelter systems where help depends on abstinence [[Pauly2019]].

### recovery · Drink-driving
- In a meta-analysis, the odds of a motor vehicle crash rose by 1.24 (95% CI 1.18–1.31) for every 10 g of alcohol, reaching an OR of 52.0 at 120 g. The authors conclude that "No level of consumption is safe when driving" [[Taylor2010]].
- Meta-analysis of international studies: lowering the legal BAC limit to 0.05 g/dL or lower was associated with an 11.1% decline in fatal alcohol-related crashes. The authors estimated 1,790 US lives saved each year if all states adopted 0.05 [[Fell2017]].
- A Community Guide review found strong evidence that ignition interlocks cut re-arrest for alcohol-impaired driving while they are installed. Re-arrest rates went back to comparison levels once they were removed [[Elder2011]].

### recovery · Policy: price, availability, labels
- A meta-analysis of 1,003 estimates from 112 studies found simple mean price elasticities of −0.46 for beer, −0.69 for wine and −0.80 for spirits. Price also affected heavy drinking (mean elasticity −0.28) [[Wagenaar2009]].
- Scotland introduced minimum unit pricing (MUP, £0.50 per unit) on 1 May 2018. Compared with England, it was associated with:
  - a 13.4% reduction (95% CI −18.4 to −8.3) in deaths wholly attributable to alcohol
  - a 4.1% (non-significant) reduction in such hospitalisations [[Wyper2023]]
- The MUP effects were driven by chronic outcomes, especially alcohol-related liver disease. They were largest in the four most deprived deciles [[Wyper2023]].
- A synthesis of 40 MUP evaluation reports found no strong evidence of harm to the drinks industry or of social harm at population level. Some qualitative evidence suggested worse outcomes for financially vulnerable people with alcohol dependence [[Patterson2023]].
- In Whitehorse, Yukon, labels carrying a cancer warning, drinking guidelines and standard-drink information were associated with a 6.31% fall in total per capita alcohol sales during the intervention. Sales of labelled products fell 6.59% [[Zhao2020Labels]].
- In a global cost-effectiveness model, raising excise taxes cost less than I$0.10 per capita and less than I$100 per healthy life year gained. Restrictions on availability and marketing were also highly cost-effective [[Chisholm2018]].
- The third edition of "Alcohol: No Ordinary Commodity" concludes that the most effective policies are taxes that reduce affordability and limits on availability. It adds that total marketing bans, drink-driving measures, brief interventions and treatment are also effective [[Babor2022]].
- GBD 2016 found that the level of drinking that minimised health loss was zero standard drinks per week (95% UI 0.0–0.8) [[GBD2016Alcohol]].
- Research does not support drinking for health. A 2023 meta-analysis of 107 cohort studies (4.8 million participants) adjusted for biases such as counting former drinkers as abstainers. It found no significant reduction in all-cause mortality for occasional drinkers (RR 0.96) or low-volume drinkers (1.3–24 g/day; RR 0.93) compared with lifetime non-drinkers [[Zhao2023JAMANetwOpen]].
- In that meta-analysis, risk rose at 45–64 g/day (RR 1.19) and ≥65 g/day (RR 1.35) [[Zhao2023JAMANetwOpen]].

### frontier · GLP-1 receptor agonists
- Semaglutide, phase 2 RCT, outpatient (n = 48, non-treatment-seeking adults with AUD, 9 weeks, low doses):
  - less alcohol consumed in a laboratory self-administration task (β −0.48, 95% CI −0.85 to −0.11)
  - fewer drinks per drinking day (β −0.41) and less weekly craving (β −0.39)
  - no change in drinking days or average drinks per day
  - (Early evidence) [[Hendershot2025]]
- Exenatide, RCT (n = 127, treatment-seeking, 2 mg weekly for 26 weeks plus CBT): no reduction in heavy drinking days compared with placebo. It did reduce alcohol cue reactivity in the ventral striatum, and an exploratory analysis found fewer heavy drinking days in people with BMI >30 (Early evidence) [[Klausen2022]].
- Semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly vs placebo for 26 weeks, double-blind RCT (n = 108, treatment-seeking, moderate to severe AUD with obesity, plus CBT):
  - heavy drinking days fell 41.1 percentage points vs 26.4 with placebo
  - estimated difference −13.7 percentage points (95% CI −22.0 to −5.4; p = 0.0015)
  - gastrointestinal side effects were more common with semaglutide
  - (Early evidence; the strongest RCT to date) [[Klausen2026Lancet]]
- Oral semaglutide (3 then 7 mg/day for 8 weeks) vs placebo, phase 2 RCT (n = 50, treatment-seeking): no effect on the primary outcome of laboratory cue craving. Heavy drinking days (b −0.580) and drinks per drinking day (b −1.177) fell significantly (Early evidence) [[Schacht2026]].
- Electronic-record cohort of 83,825 patients with obesity: semaglutide, compared with other anti-obesity medicines, was associated with a 50–56% lower risk of new and recurrent AUD diagnoses over 12 months. The finding replicated in 598,803 patients with type 2 diabetes (Early evidence; observational) [[Wang2024]].
- Swedish national within-person cohort (227,866 people with AUD): periods on semaglutide (aHR 0.64, 95% CI 0.50–0.83) and liraglutide (aHR 0.72) were associated with fewer AUD hospitalisations than periods off them. For approved AUD medicines the aHR was 0.98 (Early evidence; observational) [[Lahteenvuo2025]].
- Target-trial emulation in type 2 diabetes:
  - semaglutide vs SGLT2 inhibitors: hospital or ED visits for AUD HR 0.54 (95% CI 0.45–0.64)
  - tirzepatide vs SGLT2 inhibitors: HR 0.61
  - no difference between semaglutide and tirzepatide (HR 0.98)
  - (Early evidence; observational) [[Tang2026]]
- A VA phase 3 double-blind trial of semaglutide vs placebo (CSP #2041, estimated n = 622) started on 28 July 2026. Its primary endpoint is a two-level fall in WHO risk drinking level, with primary completion estimated for 2029 [[NCT07218354]].
- NIDA's phase 2 STAR trial (semaglutide vs placebo, n = 63, drinks per week) is active and not recruiting. Primary completion is estimated for March 2027 [[NCT06015893]].
- GLP-1 receptor agonists are not approved for AUD in the US. The completed RCTs are small or short, and the cohort data cannot rule out confounding. Status: (Early evidence) [[Klausen2026Lancet,Wang2024]].

### frontier · Psilocybin-assisted therapy
- Psilocybin vs active placebo, double-blind RCT (n = 95, adults aged 25–65, two sessions with psilocybin 25–40 mg/70 kg vs diphenhydramine, within 12 weeks of manualised psychotherapy):
  - percentage of heavy drinking days over 32 weeks: 9.7% vs 23.6%
  - mean difference 13.9% (95% CI 3.0–24.7)
  - no serious adverse events with psilocybin
  - (Early evidence) [[Bogenschutz2022]]
- That trial excluded people with major psychiatric and drug use disorders, and people with medical conditions that ruled out the study drugs. This limits how far the result applies [[Bogenschutz2022]].
- In the open-label extension (n = 59, one extra psilocybin session), people who were drinking more heavily at entry cut down at month 1, but drinking returned to entry levels afterwards (Early evidence) [[Pagni2026]].
- A 2026 meta-analysis (6 trials, N = 202, 3 RCTs) found:
  - moderate short-term advantages for drinks per day (SMD 0.48) and heavy drinking days (SMD 0.39)
  - only percent drinking days still better at mid- to long-term follow-up (SMD 0.41)
  - authors' summary: evidence is "mixed" (Early evidence) [[Florineth2026]]

### frontier · Ketamine
- KARE, phase 2 RCT (n = 96, severe AUD; three weekly ketamine infusions 0.8 mg/kg vs saline, with relapse-prevention therapy or education):
  - days abstinent at 6 months: 10.1% more with ketamine (95% CI 1.1–19.0)
  - ketamine plus therapy vs saline plus education: 15.9% more
  - no difference in relapse rate
  - (Early evidence) [[Grabski2022]]
- Pilot RCT (n = 40): one ketamine infusion (0.71 mg/kg) during motivational enhancement therapy, compared with midazolam, increased the likelihood of abstinence and delayed relapse over 21 days (Early evidence) [[Dakwar2020]].

### frontier · Brain stimulation (TMS and deep TMS)
- Deep TMS (H7 coil to medial prefrontal and anterior cingulate cortex), double-blind sham-controlled RCT (n = 51, recently abstinent):
  - heavy drinking days during follow-up: 2.9% active vs 10.6% sham (p = .037)
  - craving was also lower
  - (Early evidence) [[Harel2022]]
- A meta-analysis of 20 RCTs of non-invasive brain stimulation found a small reduction in craving compared with sham (SMD −0.211, 95% CI −0.379 to −0.042). By type, only transcranial direct current stimulation reached significance (Early evidence) [[Kim2025NIBS]].

### frontier · Mifepristone (glucocorticoid receptor antagonist)
- In alcohol-dependent rats, mifepristone reduced drinking. In a double-blind study of 56 people with alcohol dependence, 600 mg daily for 1 week reduced alcohol-cued craving and naturalistic drinking compared with placebo (Early evidence) [[Vendruscolo2015]].
- A later crossover laboratory study (N = 32, non-treatment-seeking, 600 mg/day for 1 week) found that mifepristone reduced stress-induced craving but not alcohol consumption, in the laboratory or in daily life (Early evidence; mixed) [[HaassKoffler2023]].

### frontier · Pharmacogenetics
- In COMBINE, people with an OPRM1 Asp40 allele who received medical management alone had fewer heavy drinking days on naltrexone than on placebo (P = .04). This was a secondary pharmacogenetic analysis (Hypothesis) [[Anton2008]].
- The prospective genotype-stratified RCT (n = 221, naltrexone 50 mg) found no genotype × treatment interaction (P = .32). The authors concluded it is "premature" to use Asn40Asp as a biomarker [[Oslin2015]].
- A meta-analysis of seven RCTs found a nominal moderating effect of rs1799971 only on drinks per day (d = −0.18), and it was not significant after correction for multiple comparisons (Hypothesis; not supported) [[Hartwell2020]].
- Ondansetron: in an 11-week RCT (n = 283 European Americans), certain HTR3A/HTR3B and SLC6A4 genotype combinations predicted response. This awaits independent replication (Hypothesis) [[Johnson2013]].

### frontier · Digital therapeutics
- A-CHESS smartphone app after residential treatment, unmasked RCT (n = 349): fewer risky drinking days over 12 months (1.39 vs 2.75; mean difference 1.37, 95% CI 0.46–2.27) (Early evidence; single RCT) [[Gustafson2014]].
- A meta-analysis of 65 randomised trials of digitally delivered cognitive behavioural interventions found a small positive effect on consumption when they were used as stand-alone treatment vs minimal control (g = 0.27) (Established, small effect) [[Kiluk2026]].

### frontier · Alcohol-associated hepatitis pipeline
- IL-22 agonist F-652, open-label phase 2 dose-escalation study (n = 18, moderate and severe alcohol-associated hepatitis): no serious adverse events leading to discontinuation, and MELD fell by days 28 and 42. There was no randomised control (Early evidence) [[Arab2020]].
- Larsucosterol (DUR-928), phase 2b RCT AHFIRM (n = 307, 30 or 90 mg vs placebo, primary endpoint 90-day mortality or transplant): completed in 2023, with no results posted on ClinicalTrials.gov [[NCT04563026]].
- A confirmatory phase 3 trial of larsucosterol 30 mg vs placebo (RE-AHFIRM, estimated n = 350, primary endpoint transplant-free survival to day 90) started recruiting in January 2026 (Early evidence; pending) [[NCT07428538]].

### Exam hooks
- About one in five UK inpatients uses alcohol harmfully and about one in ten is alcohol dependent [[Roberts2019]].
- PAWSS has 10 items, and a score of ≥4 flags high risk of complicated withdrawal in medically ill inpatients [[Maldonado2014,Maldonado2015]].
- PAWSS predicts risk before symptoms appear. CIWA-Ar measures how severe withdrawal is once it starts [[ASAM2020Withdrawal,Sullivan1989CIWA]].
- ASAM 2020 says to re-assess moderate to severe withdrawal every 1–4 h for 24 h, then every 4–8 h once CIWA-Ar is <10 for 24 h [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- Symptom-triggered dosing cut median treatment time from 68 h to 9 h and benzodiazepine dose from 425 mg to 100 mg, with no rise in seizures or delirium tremens [[Saitz1994]].
- CAM diagnoses delirium when there is acute onset with a fluctuating course, plus inattention, plus disorganised thinking or an altered level of consciousness [[Inouye1990]].
- ASAM 2020 says thiamine and glucose may be given in any order or together, so glucose should not be delayed [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- Hypokalaemia in AUD that does not respond to treatment often reflects co-existing hypomagnesaemia (renal potassium wasting) [[Elisaf2002]].
- Starting AUD medicine before discharge was linked to fewer 30-day readmissions (23.4% → 8.2% in one pre-post study) [[Wei2015]].
- Naltrexone needs 7–10 opioid-free days first, and reduced tolerance afterwards raises overdose risk [[FDA-Vivitrol]].
- Saying "substance abuser" rather than "person with a substance use disorder" makes clinicians more likely to favour punishment [[Kelly2010]].
- Starting to drink at 14 or younger: lifetime dependence >40%, against about 10% for people who start at 20 or older [[Grant1997]].
- AUD is associated with suicide attempt (OR 3.13) and death by suicide (OR 2.59) [[Darvishi2015]].
- Opioids combined with alcohol or benzodiazepines carry an FDA boxed warning for respiratory depression and death [[FDA-OxyContin,FDA-Xanax]].
- CRAFT gets about two-thirds of family members who refused treatment into care, three times more than Al-Anon [[Roozen2010]].
- Scotland's minimum unit pricing was associated with 13.4% fewer deaths wholly attributable to alcohol [[Wyper2023]].
- The OPRM1 Asn40Asp genotype did not predict naltrexone response in a prospective RCT [[Oslin2015]].
- Semaglutide 2.4 mg cut heavy drinking days by 13.7 percentage points more than placebo in people with AUD and obesity [[Klausen2026Lancet]].

### In plain words
- Many people in hospital have a drinking problem, about one in five in the UK. Being asked about drinking is a normal part of care [[Roberts2019]].
- If you drink heavily every day, do not stop suddenly on your own. Get medical help to stop safely, because withdrawal can cause seizures or confusion [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- In hospital, nurses check withdrawal signs often, every 1 to 4 hours at first, and give medicine when the scores call for it [[ASAM2020Withdrawal,Saitz1994]].
- A calm, quiet, evenly lit room and simple reminders of the day and place help people through withdrawal [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]].
- Vitamin B1 (thiamine) protects the brain in people who drink heavily [[ASAM2020Withdrawal,Sechi2007]].
- Hospital is a good place to start treatment. Leaving with a medicine and a plan was linked to fewer trips back to hospital [[Wei2015,Lambert2025]].
- Words matter. "A person with alcohol use disorder" leads to fairer treatment than labels like "abuser" [[Kelly2010]].
- Mixing alcohol with opioid painkillers or sleeping and anxiety pills can stop your breathing [[FDA-OxyContin,FDA-Xanax]].
- Alcohol poisoning is an emergency. Call emergency services [[Kanny2015]].
- Families can learn skills (CRAFT) that help a loved one accept treatment. It is not their fault, and support helps them too [[Roozen2010]].
- Recovery is common. About 1 in 11 US adults say they once had an alcohol or drug problem and no longer do [[Kelly2017Recovery]].
- Life often gets steadily better in the first years of recovery [[Kelly2018QoL]].
- No amount of alcohol is safe before driving [[Taylor2010]].
- Research does not show that drinking is good for your health [[Zhao2023JAMANetwOpen,GBD2016Alcohol]].
- New treatments, such as semaglutide-type medicines and psilocybin with therapy, are being tested. They are not approved for alcohol use disorder yet [[Klausen2026Lancet,Bogenschutz2022]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Thiamine before glucose? Classic teaching, reflected in the Wernicke review literature, is that thiamine must come before glucose [[Sechi2007]]. ASAM 2020 says the two may be given in any order or together, so that glucose is not delayed [[ASAM2020Withdrawal]]. Status: guideline consensus has moved towards "don't delay either."
- How much repeat electrolyte testing does withdrawal need? Alcohol misuse is a recognised refeeding risk factor [[Zheng2025]]. A small detox-unit cohort found no refeeding syndrome and called routine repeat electrolytes unnecessary [[Manning2014]]. Status: risk-based testing rather than universal testing. The evidence is thin.
- Do hospital brief interventions last? Consumption fell at 6–9 months but not at 1 year. Deaths were lower, though from small numbers [[McQueen2011]]. Status: still uncertain.
- Does an addiction consult or starting medicine in hospital cause fewer readmissions? The data are pre-post [[Wei2015]], quasi-experimental [[Wakeman2017]] or retrospective [[Lambert2025]], and there is no large RCT. Starting medicine varies 5–43% between hospitals [[Bernstein2026]].
- Managed alcohol programmes: they reduce harms and give people stability [[Stockwell2021,Pauly2019]], but total consumption fell about equally in controls [[Stockwell2021]]. The evidence is quasi-experimental and qualitative [[Carver2026]]. Status: early evidence; it is debated whether these programmes fit within an abstinence-oriented health system.
- MUP and people with dependence: population deaths fell [[Wyper2023]], but qualitative evidence suggests financially vulnerable people with alcohol dependence may have been harmed [[Patterson2023]]. Status: the policy is effective overall, but specific services are needed alongside it.
- Telescoping in women: supported by biopsychosocial evidence, but still a hypothesis [[Kirsch2025]].
- GLP-1 receptor agonists: a large observational signal [[Wang2024,Lahteenvuo2025,Tang2026]] meets mixed RCT results. Exenatide missed its primary endpoint [[Klausen2022]]. Oral semaglutide missed its craving primary endpoint but improved heavy drinking [[Schacht2026]]. Semaglutide 2.4 mg in people with obesity met its endpoint [[Klausen2026Lancet]]. Open questions: does it work in people without obesity, and how durable is it? Phase 3 is under way [[NCT07218354]].
- Psilocybin: one positive phase 2 RCT [[Bogenschutz2022]], but its benefit faded after an open-label dose [[Pagni2026]], and a meta-analysis calls the evidence "mixed" [[Florineth2026]]. Status: early evidence. Blinding is hard with psychedelics, and a phase 3 trial is needed.
- OPRM1-guided naltrexone: a post hoc COMBINE signal [[Anton2008]] did not hold in a prospective trial [[Oslin2015]] or a meta-analysis [[Hartwell2020]]. Status: not ready for clinical use.
- Alcohol-associated hepatitis: larsucosterol's phase 2b results are not peer-reviewed or posted [[NCT04563026]], and a phase 3 trial is recruiting [[NCT07428538]]. Status: pending.
