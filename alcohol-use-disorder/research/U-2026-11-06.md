## Findings

### frontier · Kudzu extract (herbal)
- Phase 2b double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT (A-HACK; 120 adults with AUD who binge drink, randomised 1:1, 12 weeks, weekly counselling in both arms). The active arm took oral kudzu extract, 2 g on days when binge drinking was anticipated (targeted, as-needed use, as published) [[Santos2026,NCT03709043]].
  - primary outcome, binge-drinking days in the past week (intention to treat): IRR 0.73 (95% CI 0.54–0.99) vs placebo
  - as-treated analysis (taking it ≥2.7 days a week): IRR 0.58 (95% CI 0.38–0.89)
  - secondary: fewer binge-drinking weeks (ITT RR 0.79, 95% CI 0.62–0.99); PEth positivity lower in the as-treated analysis (RR 0.82, 95% CI 0.67–0.99); the abstract gives no ITT PEth result
  - binge-drinking days still lower at the 3-month post-treatment visit (RR 0.68, 95% CI 0.47–0.97)
  - adverse events similar between groups; no serious adverse events related to kudzu
  - 88% completed the trial; 86% of weekly visits completed
  - (Early evidence; single phase 2b trial, self-reported primary outcome, upper CI bound close to 1) [[Santos2026]]
- Registry: NCT03709043, UCSF (sponsor-investigator G-M Santos), NIAAA collaborator, phase 2, completed (primary completion 2024-04-01); primary outcome registered as number of binge-drinking days, matching the paper [[NCT03709043]].

### Exam hooks
- Kudzu (herbal extract) taken as needed reduced binge-drinking days vs placebo in one phase 2b RCT (n = 120; IRR 0.73); it is not an approved AUD medicine and awaits replication [[Santos2026]].

### In plain words
- In one study, people who took a plant extract called kudzu on days they expected to binge drink had somewhat fewer binge days than people who took a dummy pill. It is one study, and kudzu is not an approved treatment for alcohol use disorder [[Santos2026]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Kudzu: one positive phase 2b RCT [[Santos2026]]; no confirmatory trial registered; supplement products are not standardised like approved medicines (not tested in this trial). Status: early evidence.
