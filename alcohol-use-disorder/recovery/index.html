Not medical adviceThe AUD Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. It does not replace a doctor, nurse, pharmacist, counsellor or other clinician. The AUDIT, the criteria counter, CIWA-Ar and the other calculators are teaching aids, not self-tests, and doses, benzodiazepine and thiamine regimens and CIWA-Ar thresholds are quoted as published, never as instructions. Stopping suddenly after long, heavy daily drinking can be dangerous: get medical help to stop safely. If you or someone else may have alcohol poisoning or severe withdrawal (seizure, confusion, hallucinations), call emergency services. For help with drinking in the US, SAMHSA's National Helpline is free and confidential: 1-800-662-4357. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

Everyone starts with AUD

Start with 100 people who have alcohol use disorder. Most of them will never be treated: only 25.5% of US adults with past alcohol dependence in NESARC had ever had treatment, 19.8% of people with lifetime AUD in NESARC-III, and about 1 in 6 worldwide (17.3%; 9.3% in low and lower-middle-income countries). 78,146,328

Lapse or relapse? The road back

A lapse is a single return to drinking; a relapse is a return to the old pattern. Relapse-prevention therapy treats the moment after a lapse as a turning point: the abstinence violation effect pulls one way, a plan and a reframe pull the other. People also respond better to the phrase "recurrence of use" than to "relapse". 21,274

Schematic of the Marlatt relapse-prevention model and its later dynamic version; distances are not to scale. 274,498

The road

Recovery is a process, not a switch

The later version of Marlatt's model treats relapse as a dynamic process in which many risk factors and situations interact, rather than a fixed chain of events. Long-term care with continued monitoring, as for diabetes, hypertension or asthma, gives lasting benefit. 325,498

In plain wordsRecovery has bends and hills. Planning for them is part of the treatment.
Exam hookAUD fits a chronic-illness model: adherence and relapse rates are similar to diabetes, hypertension and asthma. 325

Stopping safely. If someone has been drinking heavily every day for a long time, stopping suddenly can be dangerous (seizures, delirium tremens). Get medical help to stop safely, including after a return to heavy drinking. Alcohol poisoning is an emergency: call emergency services. See the withdrawal timeline.

What the long view shows

Recovery is common

9.1%

of US adults have resolved an alcohol or drug problem

In a national probability survey (39,809 adults), 9.1% said they used to have a problem with alcohol or drugs but no longer do. Of them, 46% called themselves "in recovery" and 53.9% had used an assisted pathway: mutual help 45.1%, treatment 27.6%, recovery support services 21.8%. Group statistics, not a promise for any one person. 230

Attempts

2

median serious attempts before resolving a problem

Among 2,002 people who had resolved a problem, the median number of serious recovery attempts was 2 (IQR 1–4); the mean of 5.35 is pulled up by a few people with many attempts. The authors advise quoting the median, because describing AUD as endlessly relapsing may weaken hope and help-seeking. 232

Definitions

Remission and recovery

DSM-5 early remission: at least 3 but less than 12 months without meeting any AUD criterion except craving; sustained remission: 12 months or more. The 2022 NIAAA research definition of recovery adds cessation of heavy drinking, and does not require abstinence. 16,157,169 Count criteria in the criteria teaching counter.

Mortality

Risk falls when drinking falls

In clinical samples, people with AUD had higher all-cause mortality than people without (RR 3.38 in men, 4.57 in women). In 16 cohorts of people with AUD, cutting below heavy drinking went with lower mortality than continued heavy drinking (OR 0.41); abstinence carried the lowest (OR 0.35). In a 60-year Boston cohort, by age 70 of 72 men with alcohol dependence, 54% had died, 32% were abstinent and 12% still had alcohol problems. 395,396,471

Treatment gap

Most people never get treatment

Only 25.5% of NESARC adults with past dependence had ever been treated; 19.8% of people with lifetime AUD in NESARC-III. Worldwide about 1 in 6 receive treatment (17.3%), 9.3% in low and lower-middle-income countries. High stigma and too little screening are part of the reason. 56,78,146,328

Abstinence or moderation

Paths after resolution

In the recovery survey, 20.3% reported continuous abstinence since resolving their problem and 33.7% current abstinence; about half still used alcohol or drugs in some form. Lower-risk use, including abstinence, went with more self-esteem, happiness and recovery capital, and people moved toward abstinence as time passed. 95 Low-risk drinking was an outcome in 78.6% of natural-recovery alcohol samples. 439 The research does not support drinking for health.

Controversy

A chronic relapsing illness?

Treatment cohorts show long careers: median 27 years from first use to last use, 9 years from first treatment to last use, and 47% reaching a year of abstinence within 3 years. AUD is best managed like diabetes or asthma, with continued monitoring. Population surveys show many people remit, often untreated. Both are true for different groups: clinical samples are more severe. 78,85,232,325

Early evidence

Managed alcohol programmes

For people with alcohol dependence who are homeless or unstably housed, managed alcohol programmes give regulated doses through the day with housing, health and social support. In six Canadian cities both programme participants and controls cut drinks per day by similar amounts, but participants reported fewer harms; liver markers worsened after people left. 57,444

Health in recovery

Keep primary care in the plan

People in recovery had higher rates of hepatitis C, COPD, heart disease and diabetes than the general population, which supports joining addiction care with primary care. 94 Recovery housing helped too: 15.6% of people who lived in an Oxford House for 6 months or more reported substance use at 24 months, against 64.8% with usual aftercare. 198

AUD Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on alcohol use disorder and alcohol withdrawal, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people with alcohol use disorder, their families and friends.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine or a plan for stopping drinking without your own care team. The AUDIT, the criteria counter, CIWA-Ar, PAWSS and the blood alcohol and liver calculators are for learning, not self-diagnosis or reading your own results, and the blood alcohol calculator can never tell anyone whether it is safe to drive. Doses and regimens are quoted as published in labels and guidelines, never as instructions. Alcohol poisoning and severe withdrawal are emergencies.
