An animated, cited guide for students, people with alcohol use disorder, and their families and friends. Educational only, not medical advice. Your place is saved on this device.

Not medical adviceThe AUD Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. It does not replace a doctor, nurse, pharmacist, counsellor or other clinician. The AUDIT, the criteria counter, CIWA-Ar and the other calculators are teaching aids, not self-tests, and doses, benzodiazepine and thiamine regimens and CIWA-Ar thresholds are quoted as published, never as instructions. Stopping suddenly after long, heavy daily drinking can be dangerous: get medical help to stop safely. If you or someone else may have alcohol poisoning or severe withdrawal (seizure, confusion, hallucinations), call emergency services. For help with drinking in the US, SAMHSA's National Helpline is free and confidential: 1-800-662-4357. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

Abstainer biasCounting former drinkers, who are often ill, as "non-drinkers", which makes light drinkers look healthier; after adjustment the J-curve's benefit disappeared (RR 0.97) 443.
AcamprosateAn approved AUD medicine for "maintenance of abstinence" in people abstinent at the start. It is excreted unchanged by the kidneys and does not treat withdrawal (label) 113.
AcetaldehydeThe first and most toxic product of ethanol metabolism; a mutagen and the main focus of research on how alcohol causes cancer 50,425.
Acetaminophen and alcoholThe US OTC label warns that severe liver damage may occur with 3 or more alcoholic drinks every day 121. Chronic drinking induces CYP2E1, which makes more toxic acetaminophen metabolites 291.
ADH (alcohol dehydrogenase)The cytosolic enzyme that oxidises ethanol to acetaldehyde. Its low Km means it saturates after a few drinks, so BAC falls at a constant rate 212,512.
ADH1BA gene for a fast alcohol dehydrogenase. The rs1229984 variant protects against alcohol dependence and reaches genome-wide significance 288,488.
Adverse childhood experiences (ACEs)Childhood adversities that were 2 to 13 times more likely when a parent had alcohol problems (retrospective reports) 92.
Al-AnonThe most widely used mutual-help group for people concerned about someone else's drinking 462.
Alcohol dependence syndromeEdwards and Gross's 1976 description of dependence as a syndrome not defined by its consequences 97,315.
Alcohol poisoningA medical emergency: call emergency services. Signs progress from slurred speech and vomiting to stupor, coma and death 221.
Alcohol use disorder (AUD)A problematic pattern of alcohol use causing clinically significant impairment or distress, shown by 2 or more of 11 criteria in 12 months 16,169. A treatable medical condition 56.
Alcohol withdrawalSymptoms when a physically dependent person stops or cuts down, from a sudden reversal of alcohol's depressant effects; the most severe results are seizures and delirium 20.
Alcohol-associated hepatitis (AH)Rapid onset or worsening of jaundice in heavy drinking; 1-month mortality can reach 20–50% in severe cases (population statistics) 433.
Alcohol-associated liver disease (ALD)The spectrum of steatosis, alcohol-associated hepatitis and cirrhosis; the AASLD term replacing "alcoholic liver disease" 69,433.
Alcoholics Anonymous (AA)A mutual-help fellowship founded in 1935 in Akron, Ohio 151. Its effect works mainly through social-network change 228.
"Alcoholism"The older, lay name for alcohol use disorder, introduced by Magnus Huss 449.
Alcohol-related brain damageAn umbrella term covering alcohol-related dementia and Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome; partial recovery is possible with abstinence 388.
ALDH2Mitochondrial aldehyde dehydrogenase, which turns acetaldehyde into acetate 512.
ALDH2*2A dominant-negative variant that causes flushing. Heterozygotes who drink have about 3 times the oesophageal cancer risk (OR 3.19) 285,287.
AllostasisIn Koob's model, a chronic shift in the reward set point kept going by stress systems, which may explain lasting relapse risk Hypothesis 255.
Alpha-2 agonistsClonidine and dexmedetomidine: adjuncts for autonomic signs in withdrawal, never used alone, because they do not prevent seizures or DT 20.
ARNDAlcohol-related neurodevelopmental disorder, one of the four Institute of Medicine FASD categories 187.
AST:ALT ratioA ratio above 2 suggests advanced alcohol-related liver disease rather than heavy drinking itself 62,348.
Atrial fibrillation (AF)The commonest alcohol-triggered arrhythmia. Risk rises about 8% per drink a day, and abstinence cut recurrence from 73% to 53% 275,483.
AUDITThe WHO 10-item Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test, scored 0–40; 8 or more flags hazardous or harmful use 413.
AUDIT-CThe first three AUDIT questions, scored 0–12; positive at 4 or more in men and 3 or more in women 47.
BaclofenA GABA-B agonist used off-label; approved for AUD in France in 2018 500. It has a cirrhosis RCT (71% vs 29% abstinent) 2.
Behavioural couples therapy (BCT)Treatment with the partner; it beat individual treatment (d = 0.54) 369.
BenzodiazepinesFirst-line medicines for alcohol withdrawal, reducing both seizures and delirium 20,321.
Binge drinkingIn US surveys, 5 or more drinks (males) or 4 or more (females) on one occasion 261; it reflects drinking to a BAC of about 0.08 g% 68.
Blood alcohol concentration (BAC)The amount of ethanol in the blood, often in g/dL. It peaks 10–60 minutes after a dose 213. A BAC calculation is a teaching estimate with real uncertainty 368.
Brief interventionFive or fewer sessions totalling under 60 minutes, for hazardous and harmful drinkers who are not seeking help; about 20 g a week less drinking at one year 220.
CAGEFour questions: Cut down, Annoyed, Guilty, Eye-opener 107; of limited value for screening 3.
Caine criteriaWernicke encephalopathy in people who drink heavily: 2 of dietary deficiency, oculomotor abnormality, cerebellar dysfunction, altered mental state 54,134.
CAM (Confusion Assessment Method)Delirium = acute onset with fluctuating course, plus inattention, plus disorganised thinking or an altered level of consciousness 193.
CAM-ICUA delirium tool for ventilated ICU patients (sensitivity 93–100%, about 2 minutes) 101, used where CIWA-Ar cannot be 20.
CarbamazepineAn anticonvulsant that ASAM 2020 lists as appropriate for mild withdrawal and an alternative for moderate withdrawal 20.
Carbohydrate-deficient transferrin (CDT)A specific marker of chronic heavy drinking that needs about 50–80 g a day for weeks to rise 346; half-life about 15 days 18.
ChlordiazepoxideA long-acting benzodiazepine labelled for "withdrawal symptoms of acute alcoholism" (label wording) 114.
CirrhosisAdvanced scarring of the liver. It follows steatohepatitis in 8–20% 433; stopping drinking improves survival 508.
CIWA-ArThe 10-item Clinical Institute Withdrawal Assessment for Alcohol, revised; maximum 67 332,447. ASAM bands: <10, 10–18, ≥19 20.
COMBINEA 1,383-person US trial: naltrexone with medical management helped; acamprosate did not 11.
Community reinforcement approach (CRA)A behavioural treatment that makes sober life more rewarding; first described by Hunt and Azrin in 1973 191,400.
ConfabulationA feature of Korsakoff syndrome, alongside severe anterograde episodic memory loss 257.
Contingency management (CM)Rewards for verified abstinence, for example EtG-negative urine; 3.1 times more negative samples in one trial 323.
Corticotropin-releasing factor (CRF)A brain stress transmitter recruited in the extended amygdala during the withdrawal/negative affect stage 256.
CRAFFTAn adolescent screen: Car, Relax, Alone, Forget, Friends, Trouble; positive at 2 or more 248,429.
CRAFTCommunity reinforcement and family training: it helps family members get a loved one into treatment, about three times as often as Al-Anon 401.
CravingA strong desire to drink; added as a DSM-5 criterion 169.
CYP2E1An inducible liver enzyme (part of the MEOS) with a higher Km than ADH; it raises clearance in heavy drinkers 59,347.
Delirium tremens (DT)Withdrawal delirium with fluctuating attention and autonomic overactivity, beginning 48–72 h (Mirijello) or 72–96 h (ASAM) after the last drink 20,332.
Diagnostic orphansPeople with two DSM-IV dependence criteria and no abuse criteria, who received no diagnosis 169.
DisulfiramAn ALDH inhibitor (Antabuse) that causes an aversive reaction to alcohol 112. It works in supervised or open-label trials 434.
Disulfiram–alcohol reactionFlushing, throbbing headache, nausea, vomiting, palpitations and hypotension after alcohol on disulfiram, possible up to 14 days after the last dose 112.
DopamineA reward transmitter released by alcohol in the human ventral striatum 43.
DSM-5-TRThe 2022 text revision of DSM-5, which defines AUD by 11 criteria 16.
DynorphinA stress-related opioid peptide recruited in the extended amygdala in withdrawal 256.
Early liver transplantationTransplant for a first episode of severe AH not responding to treatment, in highly selected people: 6-month survival 77% vs 23% 319.
Early remission3 to under 12 months with no AUD criteria except craving 169.
Ethyl glucuronide (EtG)A minor ethanol metabolite in urine, detectable for a median of about 78 h after heavy drinking 173. Incidental exposure can make it positive 455.
Ethyl sulfate (EtS)A minor ethanol metabolite with a urinary time course similar to EtG 173.
Extended amygdalaThe brain hub of the withdrawal/negative affect stage 256.
Extended-release naltrexoneVivitrol: 380 mg by deep gluteal injection every 4 weeks, given by a healthcare provider (label) 123.
Fetal alcohol spectrum disorder (FASD)The umbrella term for the effects of prenatal alcohol exposure 497; about 7.7 per 1,000 children and youth worldwide 272.
Fetal alcohol syndrome (FAS)The most severe FASD form, first described in 1973 497: facial features, growth deficiency, brain effects and neurobehavioural impairment 187.
First-pass metabolismOxidation of some alcohol in the stomach before it reaches the blood; lower in women and when fasting 59,132.
Front loadingGiving large early doses of a long-acting benzodiazepine; ASAM 2020 recommends it for severe withdrawal (CIWA-Ar ≥19) 20.
GABA-A receptorThe main inhibitory receptor; ethanol enhances it acutely, and chronic drinking changes its subunits 264.
GabapentinAn anticonvulsant used off-label, with a dose-response for abstinence (17.0% at 1,800 mg vs 4.1% placebo) 318.
Gamma-glutamyltransferase (GGT)A liver enzyme that rises with drinking but is not specific; it normalises 2–3 weeks after stopping 346.
Genome-wide association study (GWAS)A scan of the genome for common variants; in AUD, ADH1B variants reach genome-wide significance 488.
GLP-1 receptor agonistsDiabetes and obesity medicines under study for AUD, not approved for it Early evidence 247,490.
Hair EtGEtG in hair, reflecting months of drinking; 30 pg/mg or more strongly suggests chronic excessive drinking (SoHT 2026) 111.
Hallucinosis (withdrawal)Hallucinations with a clear sensorium at 12–24 h, distinct from delirium tremens; the older name is alcoholic hallucinosis 20,410.
Harm reductionApproaches that lower harm without requiring abstinence, such as cutting down by WHO risk levels or managed alcohol programmes 57,170.
Harmful pattern of use (ICD-11)Drinking that has damaged physical or mental health without reaching dependence 84.
Harmful useWHO term used with the AUDIT for a drinking pattern already damaging health 179.
Hazardous useWHO term for a drinking pattern that raises the risk of harm, even without a current disorder 179.
Heavy alcohol useIn US surveys, binge drinking on 5 or more days in the past month 261.
HeritabilityThe share of variation in a trait due to genes; about 0.49 for AUD 478.
High-intensity drinking8 or more drinks for women and 10 or more for men, at least twice the binge thresholds 356.
Holiday heartAlcohol-triggered heart rhythm disorder, classically AF after binge drinking, named in 1978 81,105.
HypomagnesaemiaLow magnesium, found in 44% of one small withdrawal cohort 311; it can keep potassium low through renal wasting 100.
ICD-11 alcohol dependence2 of 3 features: impaired control, increasing priority, physiological features 84.
Ignition interlockInterlocks cut re-arrest for alcohol-impaired driving while installed; re-arrest returned to comparison levels once removed 99.
J-curveThe apparent lower mortality of light drinkers in cohort studies, largely explained by bias 443,516.
KetamineAn anaesthetic under study with therapy for AUD Early evidence 74.
KindlingRepeated withdrawals making later withdrawal worse Early evidence 34,51.
Korsakoff syndromeSevere anterograde episodic memory loss with confabulation, usually after Wernicke encephalopathy 19,257.
Lille modelA day-7 steroid-response score; 0.45 or more means nonresponse (6-month survival 25% vs 85%) 299.
Maddrey discriminant function4.6 × (PT − control) + bilirubin (mg/dL); 32 or more marks severe AH 55,433.
Mammillary bodiesSmall brain structures damaged in Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome 257.
Managed alcohol programme (MAP)Regulated doses of alcohol with housing and health support for people with alcohol dependence who are homeless Early evidence 57,444.
Mean corpuscular volume (MCV)Red cell size; it rises with drinking and takes 2–4 months to normalise 346.
MELDA score from bilirubin, creatinine and INR; above 20 flags severe AH 218,433.
Mendelian randomisationUsing gene variants as natural experiments; ALDH2/ADH1B studies show no protective U-shape for stroke 330.
MEOSThe microsomal ethanol-oxidising system (CYP2E1), induced by chronic drinking 291.
Minimum unit pricing (MUP)A floor price per unit of alcohol; Scotland's £0.50 MUP was associated with 13.4% fewer wholly attributable deaths 507.
Motivational interviewing (MI)A counselling approach tested in 93 studies: a small-to-moderate effect vs no intervention right afterwards, shrinking at later follow-up 421.
Mutual-help groupsPeer groups such as AA, SMART Recovery, LifeRing and Women for Sobriety; deeper involvement in each was linked with similar outcomes 513.
NADHA reduced coenzyme made by ADH and ALDH2; its surplus drives fatty liver 59,290.
NalmefeneA mu- and delta-opioid antagonist and kappa partial agonist 261, approved in the EU and taken as needed to reduce drinking 473.
NaltrexoneA mu-opioid antagonist approved for AUD; it reduces heavy drinking and craving 117,305. Opioids must be stopped 7–10 days first (label) 117.
Negative reinforcementDrinking to relieve a negative emotional state 254.
NMDA receptorA glutamate receptor that ethanol inhibits acutely and that is up-regulated with tolerance 300,466.
Nucleus accumbensA reward hub in the ventral striatum where alcohol releases dopamine and endogenous opioids 43,333.
Number needed to treat (NNT)How many people must be treated for one to benefit; acamprosate 12 and naltrexone 20 to prevent any drinking 209.
OndansetronStudied in early-onset AUD (70.1% vs 50.2% days abstinent) and by genotype Early evidence 204,207.
OPRM1 Asn40AspA mu-opioid receptor variant once thought to predict naltrexone response; a prospective RCT found no interaction 353.
PAWSSThe Prediction of Alcohol Withdrawal Severity Scale: 10 items, 4 or more flags high risk of complicated withdrawal 307,308.
PhenobarbitalA barbiturate for withdrawal, used only by experienced clinicians "given its narrow therapeutic window" 20.
Phosphatidylethanol (PEth)A blood marker formed only when ethanol is present; average half-life about 4 days 158.
Polygenic scoreA sum of many small genetic effects; for AUD it explains about 2.5–3.5% of the variance and cannot diagnose 32.
Prefrontal cortexFront of the brain; with the insula, the hub of the preoccupation/anticipation stage 256.
Project MATCHA large trial in which CBT, MET and twelve-step facilitation gave similar outcomes 373.
Psilocybin-assisted therapyPsilocybin sessions with psychotherapy; heavy drinking days 9.7% vs 23.6% in one RCT Early evidence 42.
RASSThe Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale, from +4 combative to −5 unarousable 428.
RecompensationRecovery from decompensated cirrhosis; 31.1% of people who stopped drinking achieved it in one cohort 184.
Recovery (NIAAA 2022)Remission from DSM-5 AUD plus cessation of heavy drinking; abstinence is not required 157.
Recovery capitalThe personal, social and community resources that support recovery 277.
Refeeding syndromeDangerous shifts in electrolytes when feeding restarts after malnutrition; prior alcohol misuse is a reported risk factor 517.
Relapse preventionMarlatt's model built on high-risk situations, coping, self-efficacy, outcome expectancies and the abstinence violation effect 274.
Resistant alcohol withdrawalWithdrawal uncontrolled despite large benzodiazepine doses; ASAM 2020 notes it "is not well defined" 20.
SBIRTScreening, Brief Intervention, and Referral to Treatment: a public-health approach built on universal screening 27.
SemaglutideA GLP-1 receptor agonist; in AUD with obesity it cut heavy drinking days 13.7 percentage points more than placebo Early evidence 247.
SMART RecoveryA mutual-help alternative to AA; deeper involvement was linked with similar outcomes 513.
Sodium oxybate (GHB)Approved in Italy and Austria for alcohol withdrawal and abstinence; it carries misuse risk and is dangerous with alcohol or other sedatives 153,282.
Standard drinkA set amount of ethanol: 0.6 oz in the US 261; 10 g in GBD, and 8–20 g across countries 217.
Steatohepatitis (ASH)Fatty liver with inflammation; about one-third of people with steatosis who keep drinking heavily 433.
SteatosisFatty liver; seen in about 90% of people who drink heavily and reversible with abstinence 433.
StigmaNegative labels and treatment. "Substance abuser" made clinicians more likely to favour punishment 226; stigma towards AUD stays high 240.
STOPAHA 1,103-person trial: prednisolone gave no significant mortality benefit and pentoxifylline none 460.
Sustained remission12 months or more with no AUD criteria except craving 169.
Symptom-triggered dosingGiving withdrawal medicine only when a CIWA-Ar score calls for it; less drug for a shorter time 409. ASAM 2020's preferred inpatient method 20.
T-ACEA pregnancy screen: Tolerance, Annoyed, Cut down, Eye-opener; 2 or more is positive 440.
TelescopingWomen's apparently faster progression to AUD and its health consequences 245.
ThiamineVitamin B1; deficiency causes Wernicke encephalopathy 424. Regimens are quoted as published by EFNS and ASAM 20,134.
ToleranceNeeding more alcohol for the same effect; a DSM-5 criterion 16, driven partly by receptor adaptation 264.
TopiramateAn anticonvulsant used off-label; it reduced heavy drinking days, with paresthesia in 50.8% 206.
Twelve-step facilitation (TSF)Manualised therapy that encourages AA involvement; it beat other treatments for 12-month continuous abstinence 234.
UKATTA UK trial in which social behaviour and network therapy matched MET; both saved about five times their cost 467,468.
Unhealthy alcohol useThe USPSTF term for a spectrum from risky drinking to AUD 470.
USPSTFThe US Preventive Services Task Force; it recommends alcohol screening and brief counselling for adults (B) 470.
Volume of distribution (Widmark r)The body water ethanol spreads in: about 0.70 L/kg in men and 0.60 in women 213; fixed Widmark factors 0.68 and 0.55 454.
Warm handoffA direct, personal link from withdrawal care to AUD treatment providers (ASAM 2020) 20.
Wernicke encephalopathy (WE)An acute neuropsychiatric syndrome caused by thiamine deficiency, greatly underdiagnosed 166,424.
WHO risk drinking levelsFour levels of average daily intake; dropping 1–2 levels is linked with less dependence and liver disease 170,250.
Withdrawal managementThe term that replaced "detoxification"; on its own "not an effective treatment for alcohol use disorder" 20.
Zero-order kineticsElimination at a constant amount per hour, because ADH is saturated; BAC falls in a straight line 212.

AUD Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on alcohol use disorder and alcohol withdrawal, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people with alcohol use disorder, their families and friends.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine or a plan for stopping drinking without your own care team. The AUDIT, the criteria counter, CIWA-Ar, PAWSS and the blood alcohol and liver calculators are for learning, not self-diagnosis or reading your own results, and the blood alcohol calculator can never tell anyone whether it is safe to drive. Doses and regimens are quoted as published in labels and guidelines, never as instructions. Alcohol poisoning and severe withdrawal are emergencies.
