What the trigger evidence actually shows

Questionnaire percentages are what patients report, which is not the same as what causes attacks. The case-crossover design compares each patient with themselves, so it is the stronger evidence. [[Karadag2013,Parlar2024,Yenokyan2012]]

Diet is the weakest link in this literature: a review found only 7 eligible studies, with conflicting results on fatty and salty food and a possible role for wheat, and no robust trial showing that any diet prevents attacks. [[Mansueto2022]]

Prognosis, monitoring and people

The monitoring checklist in the 2016 guideline

  • Response, toxicity and adherence reviewed every 6 months, more often in the first year or when acute-phase markers are raised, and yearly once stable.
  • Tests listed: SAA and/or CRP, blood count, liver enzymes, kidney function, CPK, and urinalysis for proteinuria.
  • Urinalysis at every clinic visit, to catch proteinuria early.
  • Colchicine is reduced if liver enzymes go above twice the upper limit of normal.
  • High SAA values are shared with the patient, deliberately, to support adherence.
  • The 2024 update sets no fixed interval, and asks for monitoring more often at diagnosis and when disease is uncontrolled, against a minimum outcome set. [[Ozen2016,Ozen2025,RheumNow2025]]

Questions people bring to a clinic visit

Plain-words versions of what the guidelines themselves say should be discussed. They are prompts for a conversation with a clinician, not a script and not advice.

  • What is my SAA or CRP between attacks, and is it in the normal range?
  • Was there any protein in my urine today?
  • Am I on the dose the guidance would call maximum tolerated for me, and is there room to go up?
  • How many attacks have I had in the last 3 months, counted rather than remembered?
  • If attacks continue at this dose, what would the next step be?
  • What should happen to my treatment if I plan a pregnancy, or need an operation, or need vaccinations?
  • How often should I be seen, and which tests each time?
In plain wordsTwo numbers do most of the work at a visit: how many attacks since last time, and what the inflammation markers say between them.
