Toward a cure

No approved therapy cures FMF today. Colchicine and IL-1 blockade control it, for life. This module takes the question seriously anyway: where in the pathway could you intervene, what evidence exists for each route, and what stands in the way. Every claim carries an evidence label. What a cure would have to achieve. Two things at once, not one. It would have to stop attacks, and it would have to switch off the quiet inflammation between them, because AA amyloidosis is driven by chronically raised SAA rather than by the attacks themselves. The 2024 EULAR/PReS update makes control of subclinical inflammation an explicit treatment target. A cure would also have to reach the right cells: MEFV messenger RNA in blood is restricted to myeloid cells, and the gene is switched on as CD34+ progenitors differentiate into granulocytes, which puts the target inside the bone-marrow stem and progenitor compartment. Pyrin protein has also been reported in dendritic cells and in synovial fibroblasts, so a purely blood-cell fix might leave a reservoir behind. [[Ozen2025,Centola2000,Diaz2004]]

What a next trial might look like

Read this part carefully. The three designs below are illustrations written for this page. They are not registered trials, not proposals from any investigator, and not advice. No trial of gene editing, RNA therapeutics, HSCT or a pyrin-targeted small molecule in FMF is registered as of the October 2026 sweep of ClinicalTrials.gov. [[CTgovSweep2026]]

The statin caution, and its limits. Statins deplete geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate, which stops RhoA from sitting in the membrane, and in cell experiments that activates the pyrin inflammasome; statin-induced IL-1β release was reversed by adding geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate back or by activating PKN. This is in-vitro mechanism. Its clinical relevance is unknown: no clinical study has looked at whether statins affect FMF, and patients with cardiovascular indications routinely receive them. Nobody should change or stop a medicine on the strength of this, and any question about a statin belongs with the prescriber. It appears here as one of the open questions, not as a recommendation.
