The differential

Recurrent fever with pain has many causes. The rhythm is often the first clue: how long each episode lasts, how regularly it comes back and whether it ends by itself. Press play to sweep through 60 days, then click a disease to see how it is told apart.

Across the hereditary recurrent fevers, how long an episode lasts is the single most discriminating variable: FCAS under a day, FMF 1–3 days, PFAPA 3–6 days, MKD 3–7 days, TRAPS 7 days or more. Fever + abdominal pain: decision points

Pick a clue from the story. The matching rows light up in the animation above and the box below names the test that separates them.

Exam hookFever + abdominal pain, four questions: Does it stop by itself in 1–3 days? (FMF). Is it getting worse and moving to the right lower quadrant? (appendicitis, even in FMF). Is there fever at all? (porphyria usually has none). What is the rhythm? FMF irregular, PFAPA clockwork every ~4 weeks, TRAPS a week or more, Still's disease daily spikes. Then: ferritin flat in FMF, very high in Still's. [[Lidar2008,Anderson2005,Thomas1999,Korkmaz2002,Yamaguchi1992]]
In plain wordsMany illnesses cause fever and tummy pain. Doctors tell them apart mainly by how long each bout lasts, how often it comes back, what else happens at the same time, and a few targeted tests. An FMF attack stops on its own within a few days; appendicitis keeps getting worse.
