Familial Mediterranean fever at a glance

What it is
Familial Mediterranean fever (FMF) is the most common inherited autoinflammatory disease. It causes short attacks of fever with inflammation of the lining of the belly, chest or joints, and the attacks stop on their own. [[Tufan2020]]
Who gets it
Turkey has the most patients, then Israel and Armenia. In those countries about 1 in 500 to 1 in 1,000 people have it. [[Tufan2020]] Most people have their first symptoms before age 20. [[Tufan2020,Sohar1967]] Many healthy people carry one changed copy of the gene, which usually causes no illness. [[Papadopoulos2008,GeneReviews]]
Main symptoms
In a Turkish registry of 2,838 patients, about 94% had belly pain attacks, 93% fever, 47% arthritis, usually in one knee, ankle or hip, and 31% one-sided chest pain on breathing. [[Tunca2005]] Between attacks most people feel completely well. [[Tunca2005,GeneReviews]]
How it is diagnosed
FMF remains a clinical diagnosis. A gene test can support it but cannot rule it out. [[Giancane2015]] Blood markers of inflammation such as CRP and SAA rise during an attack. [[Livneh1997,SoonLaxer2017,ARUP2023]]
Main treatments
Colchicine, taken every day to prevent attacks, fully prevents them in about 60 to 70% of patients and partly in another 20 to 30%. [[SoonLaxer2017,Ozen2016,Ozen2025,Cush2025]] When attacks continue at the highest tolerated dose, guidelines add a drug that blocks IL-1. [[Ozen2016]]
Outlook
The main long-term risk is AA amyloidosis: an inflammation protein builds up in the kidneys over years. [[Ozen2016,Mirioglu2024]] Before colchicine, more than half of patients died of it. [[Tufan2020]] In one large cohort, protein in the urine appeared in 1.7% of patients who took colchicine regularly, against 48.9% of those who did not. [[Lachmann2006,Duzova2003,Zemer1986]] Treatment is lifelong. [[Duzova2003,Zemer1986,Lachmann2007,Ozen2017]]
