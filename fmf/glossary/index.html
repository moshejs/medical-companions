Glossary

Every specialist term used in the app. Type to filter; the arrow on each entry jumps to the module where it matters most.

90 of 90 terms

14-3-3 proteinsScaffold proteins that bind phosphorylated Ser208/Ser242 of pyrin and hold it inactive.
AA amyloidosisDeposition of fibrils made from serum amyloid A in organs, especially the kidney; the main long-term danger of uncontrolled FMF.
Acute-phase reactantsBlood proteins that rise with inflammation: CRP, SAA, fibrinogen; ESR rises too.
AnakinraRecombinant IL-1 receptor antagonist injected daily; used in colchicine-resistant FMF.
Apple-green birefringenceThe color Congo red–stained amyloid shows between crossed polarizers.
ASCAdaptor protein that links pyrin's PYD to caspase-1 and polymerizes into a single 'speck'.
Autosomal recessiveInheritance in which disease needs two variant copies, one from each parent.
B30.2 (PRY/SPRY) domainThe C-terminal pyrin domain encoded by exon 10, where most FMF variants cluster.
Base editingGene editing that changes a single DNA letter without cutting both strands; in the clinic for sickle cell disease, untested for MEFV.
Behçet diseaseSystemic vasculitis with recurrent oral and genital ulcers, uveitis and pathergy; part of the differential.
CanakinumabMonoclonal antibody against IL-1β given every 4 weeks; tested in the CLUSTER trial.
CAPSCryopyrin-associated periodic syndromes from NLRP3 variants: urticarial rash, cold triggers, deafness.
CarrierA person with one variant copy of a recessive gene; usually healthy.
Caspase-1The protease activated in the inflammasome; cleaves pro-IL-1β, pro-IL-18 and gasdermin D.
CLUSTER trialPhase 3 randomized trial of canakinumab in colchicine-resistant FMF, MKD and TRAPS (2018).
ColchicineAlkaloid from the autumn crocus that binds tubulin; lifelong first-line FMF treatment.
Colchicine resistanceOngoing attacks or inflammation despite the maximum tolerated dose in an adherent patient.
Compound heterozygoteTwo different variants of the same gene, one on each copy (for example M694V/V726A).
Congo redDye used to detect amyloid in biopsy tissue.
CRPC-reactive protein, an acute-phase protein made in the liver.
CYP3A4Liver and gut enzyme that metabolizes colchicine; its inhibitors raise colchicine levels.
E148QCommon exon 2 MEFV variant, classed as of uncertain significance in Infevers as of October 2026; low penetrance.
Enterohepatic recyclingDrug excreted in bile and reabsorbed from the gut, prolonging its stay in the body.
Erysipelas-like erythemaTender red plaque on the lower leg or foot during FMF attacks.
Eurofever/PRINTO criteria2019 evidence-based classification criteria for FMF and other periodic fevers.
Exon 10The MEFV exon encoding the B30.2 domain; home of M694V, M680I, M694I and V726A.
Founder mutationA variant inherited from a common ancestor and spread in a population.
Gasdermin DProtein cut by caspase-1; its N-terminal fragment forms membrane pores that cause pyroptosis.
GEF-H1RhoA activator held on microtubules and released when colchicine disrupts them.
Gene dosage effectMore variant copies give stronger effects: two-variant cells respond more than one-variant cells.
Heterozygote advantageThe idea that carriers of one variant gained a survival benefit, which would explain why the variants stayed common. For FMF the proposed benefit is resistance to plague. The evidence is suggestive, not settled, and drift in isolated populations is the competing explanation.
HSCTHematopoietic stem cell transplantation; replaces the blood-forming system.
IL-1βKey inflammatory cytokine and endogenous pyrogen released by the inflammasome.
IL-18Inflammasome-processed cytokine of the IL-1 family.
InflammasomeMultiprotein complex that activates caspase-1; pyrin forms one.
InfeversOnline registry of variants in autoinflammatory disease genes, with consensus pathogenicity classes.
Livneh criteria1997 Tel Hashomer diagnostic criteria with major, minor and supportive items.
M694VThe most frequent and most severe MEFV variant in many populations.
MarenostrinThe French consortium's name for pyrin, from mare nostrum.
MEFVThe FMF gene on chromosome 16p13.3.
MicrotubuleHollow polymer of tubulin forming the cell's scaffold; disrupted by colchicine.
MKD (HIDS)Mevalonate kinase deficiency: infant-onset periodic fever; RhoA is functionally off, releasing pyrin.
NLRP3A different inflammasome sensor responding to many stress signals; mutated in CAPS.
NSAIDsNon-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, used for symptoms during attacks.
P-glycoprotein (P-gp, ABCB1)Efflux pump that pushes colchicine out of cells; low in neutrophils.
PAANDDominant pyrin disease from S242R/E244K with neutrophilic dermatosis; not FMF.
PenetranceThe share of people with a genotype who actually show the disease.
PeritonitisInflammation of the peritoneum; the most common FMF attack.
PFAPAPeriodic fever, aphthous stomatitis, pharyngitis, adenitis; clockwork fevers in young children.
Phenotype IIAA amyloidosis as the first sign of FMF without prior attacks.
Phenotype IIITwo MEFV variants without any disease.
PKN1/PKN2RhoA-activated kinases that phosphorylate pyrin to keep it off.
PleuritisInflammation of the pleura causing one-sided chest pain on breathing.
ProdromeWarning phase before an attack, felt by about half of patients.
Protracted febrile myalgiaRare weeks-long fever with severe muscle pain; responds to steroids.
ProteinuriaProtein in the urine; the first sign of renal amyloidosis.
PseudodominanceA recessive disease looking dominant because carriers are common in the population.
Punnett squareGrid showing the possible allele combinations of offspring.
PYD (pyrin domain)N-terminal domain of pyrin that binds ASC.
PyrinThe MEFV protein: an innate immune sensor of RhoA inactivation.
PyroptosisInflammatory lytic cell death through gasdermin D pores.
RhoASmall GTPase switch; GTP-bound = on. Its inactivation releases pyrin.
RilonaceptIL-1 trap given weekly; tested in a small randomized trial in FMF.
SAASerum amyloid A, an acute-phase protein and the precursor of AA amyloid.
SerosaSmooth membranes lining body cavities: peritoneum, pleura, pericardium, tunica vaginalis. FMF also inflames the synovium of joints.
AIDAIAutoinflammatory Diseases Activity Index: a one-month patient or parent diary scoring 12 items a day. Nine or more means active disease.
Amyloid stormA rapid deterioration in AA amyloidosis, usually triggered by infection, with proteinuria and creatinine climbing over days to weeks.
FMF50A response score: at least 50% improvement in 5 of 6 items with none worsening, judged after 3 to 6 months.
ISSFInternational Severity Scoring System for FMF: nine domains, maximum 10, with bands for mild, intermediate and severe.
MASIFAn 18-item scale measuring how closely colchicine is actually taken, developed in children.
Minimum outcome setThe 2024 guideline's list of what every FMF service should record: attack frequency, patient-reported measures, and CRP or SAA.
Teach-backAsking someone to say back in their own words what they will do, to check understanding rather than agreement.
AdherenceHow closely someone takes a medicine as prescribed. Poor adherence is a common reason attacks continue and is measured in studies by prescription refills or questionnaires.
Amyloid stormA rapid deterioration in AA amyloidosis, often set off by infection, with creatinine and proteinuria at least doubling and CRP rising more than tenfold within two weeks.
ASC speckThe single large clump of ASC protein that forms in a cell when an inflammasome assembles. One per cell, visible under a microscope.
BirefringenceSplitting of light by an ordered material. Congo red-stained amyloid turns apple-green between crossed polarizers.
BusulfanA chemotherapy drug used to clear bone marrow before a stem cell transplant. Its toxicity is the main obstacle to gene-corrected stem cell routes.
ChimerismThe proportion of a person’s blood cells that come from donor or gene-corrected stem cells after a transplant.
EprodisateA drug designed to interfere with amyloid fibril formation. A phase 3 trial did not meet its primary endpoint.
MyeloablationDestroying the bone marrow with drugs or radiation to make room for transplanted stem cells.
Nephrotic syndromeHeavy protein loss in the urine with low blood albumin and swelling. A stage of renal amyloidosis.
Odds ratio (OR)A measure of how much more likely an outcome is in one group than another. An OR of 3.2 means about three times the odds, not three times the risk.
PrenylationAttaching a lipid tail to a protein so it can anchor to a membrane. RhoA needs it to work, which links mevalonate kinase deficiency to pyrin.
PresenteeismBeing at work or school but working below normal capacity because of illness.
SAA1 genotypeA variation in the gene for serum amyloid A. The alpha/alpha form is associated with higher amyloidosis risk.
Subclinical inflammationRaised CRP/SAA between attacks without symptoms; drives amyloid risk.
Tel Hashomer criteriaClassic clinical FMF criteria from the Israeli Tel Hashomer hospital.
TRAPSTNF receptor-associated periodic syndrome: long attacks, migratory rash, periorbital edema.
TubulinProtein building block of microtubules; colchicine's binding target.
VUSVariant of uncertain significance: not enough evidence to call it pathogenic or benign.
