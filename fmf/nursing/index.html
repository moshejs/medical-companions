Abdomen

What the literature describes

Peritonitis is the commonest feature, in 93.7% of the 2,838-patient Turkish registry. Guarding, rebound tenderness and ileus all occur in ordinary FMF peritonitis, so peritoneal signs do not separate it from a surgical abdomen. The published discriminator for appendicitis is a shift of pain from diffuse to the right lower quadrant. Symptoms resolve completely within 24–72 hours. 152,268,269

Exam hookAbout 80% of appendices removed from FMF patients were not inflamed, against about 20% in the general population. In one 971-patient cohort a quarter had had an appendectomy. 23,152

Measuring how active the disease is

Validated
AIDAI
  • A patient or parent diary kept for one month before the visit.
  • Twelve items scored 0 or 1 each day: fever ≥38 °C, overall symptoms, abdominal pain, nausea or vomiting, diarrhoea, headache, chest pain, painful nodes, arthralgia or myalgia, joint swelling, eye signs, skin rash. A thirteenth item, pain relief taken, is recorded but not scored.
  • Total 9 or more means active disease: sensitivity 89%, specificity 92%, AUC 98% against blinded expert consensus.
  • The 2024 guideline calls it the only validated activity score in FMF, and says it has problems reflecting activity.

206,214

Validated
ISSF
  • A severity score built by Delphi and validated in 281 patients, AUC 0.825.
  • Nine domains, scored as ten items because attack frequency splits in two; maximum 10.
  • Bands: 2 or less mild, 3–5 intermediate, 6 or more severe.
  • In 160 treated Turkish children it classed 21.3% mild, 70.5% intermediate, 8.1% severe.

51,199

Response, not activity
FMF50
  • Six items: change in attack frequency, change in attack duration, patient or parent global severity, physician global severity, change in arthritis attacks, and change in CRP, ESR or SAA.
  • Response is at least 50% improvement in 5 of the 6 with none worsening, judged after 3–6 months, and only once the maximum tolerated dose has been reached.
  • It separated responders poorly in the rilonacept randomised trial.

94,201,203

Simplest
Attack count
  • In 239 patients, the number of attacks in the past 12 months correlated moderately with quality-of-life and pain scores, which supports it as a single activity measure.
  • The 2024 minimum outcome set uses attacks per 3 months.

206,215

Adherence
MASIF
  • An 18-item, four-subscale adherence scale developed in 150 Turkish children aged 2 to 18.
  • Correlated with the Morisky scale at r = 0.515, Cronbach alpha 0.728, test-retest r = 0.843, parent–child agreement r = 0.781.

285

Multidimensional
JAIMAR
  • Sixteen measures across functional status, pain, adherence and quality of life, parent-proxy or self-report from age 8, tested in 250 children with FMF.
  • About 15 minutes to complete and score.

2 · Giving the medicines

Colchicine has a narrow therapeutic index, and most of what goes wrong with it goes wrong at the point of giving it. Pick a situation and see which check changes. 64,68

Situation

Five checks, and which one the situation changes. The checks are drawn from the label and the guideline, not from a local protocol.

A routine daily dose

What changes

The label gives colchicine for FMF by mouth, once daily or in two divided doses, without regard to meals. The 2024 guideline allows either, chosen by adherence and tolerance, and notes a single dose is accepted as effective but may bring more gut upset. Dose increases are made gradually and no more often than weekly. 64,206

Exam hookOverdose can be fatal and there is no antidote. The 2024 guideline asks that intentional or accidental overdosing be carefully assessed, with suicidal ideation or intent evaluated, and that families be told about the narrow therapeutic index. The label advises keeping it out of reach of children. 52,64,206

Injectable IL-1 blockers

Sites shown are the ones the labels name for subcutaneous injection. Rotation is standard practice for repeated subcutaneous injections.

Subcutaneous sites

Rotating them

These are the sites the labels name. Anakinra's instructions list the outer upper arms, the abdomen except the 2-inch area around the navel, the front of the middle thighs and the upper outer buttocks, and tell the patient to choose a new site each time, which may reduce the risk of injection-site amyloid deposits. Skin that is bruised, red, scarred, hard, thickened or lumpy is avoided. No minimum distance from the last site is given. 129

Off-label in FMF (US)
Anakinra
  • 100 mg a day, or 2 mg/kg, subcutaneously daily.
  • Sites named by the label: outer upper arms, abdomen except the 2-inch area around the navel, front of the middle thighs, upper outer buttocks. A new site each time, which may reduce the risk of injection-site amyloid deposits. No minimum distance from the last site is given.
  • Refrigerate at 2–8 °C, do not freeze or shake, protect from light, and let the syringe stand at room temperature for 30 minutes first.
  • Injection-site reactions in 71% of the rheumatoid arthritis trials, usually in the first 4 weeks; 16.3% in NOMID in the first 6 months, none after year 2.
  • Neutrophils before starting, monthly for 3 months, then quarterly for up to a year.
  • Its US indications are rheumatoid arthritis, NOMID and DIRA, so FMF use is off-label.

129,206

FMF is in the US label
Canakinumab
  • 150–300 mg, or 2–4 mg/kg, subcutaneously every 4–8 weeks.
  • Store unopened vials at 2–8 °C in the original carton, do not freeze. Preservative-free.
  • The label says to avoid injecting into scar tissue, because it may give insufficient exposure.
  • Injection-site reactions in 10.1% in the TRAPS, HIDS/MKD and FMF study, all mild or moderate, none leading to stopping.
  • It reports neutropenia and other cytopenias but gives no blood-count interval.

106,206

Off-label in FMF (US)
Rilonacept
  • 2.2 mg/kg, maximum 160 mg, subcutaneously weekly. A 320 mg loading dose is given as two 160 mg injections the same day at different sites.
  • Reconstituted with 2.3 mL preservative-free sterile water, shaken about a minute, left to stand a minute, used within 3 hours. Vials are single-use.
  • Sites: abdomen, thigh or upper arm, avoiding within 2 inches of the navel and skin that is bruised, red, tender or hard, rotating each time.
  • Injection-site reactions in 48% in the CAPS placebo-controlled phase, against 13% on placebo.
  • The label advises monitoring lipids, and growth and development in children.
  • Its US indications are CAPS, maintenance of remission of DIRA and recurrent pericarditis.

3 · What to teach, and what gets misunderstood

The 2024 guideline makes adherence the cornerstone of management and says the goals cannot be reached without a lifelong therapeutic alliance. It also names poor information about colchicine as a barrier to implementation. These are the six things the literature records people getting wrong. 206

What the literature says

Colchicine prevents attacks; it does not treat one under way

The patient-information source states plainly that raising the dose during an attack does not help. Recommendation 11 of the 2024 guideline says the same from the clinician side: continue the usual dose, use NSAIDs for symptoms. 206,219

Ask: "If an attack starts tonight, what will you do about your tablets?"

What the guidelines ask of education
  • Overarching principle 3, agreed by everyone on the panel: FMF needs lifetime management, so adherence is the cornerstone, and education of patient and family is crucial.
  • Education should cover disease, medication, adherence, prevention and self-management, and the research agenda lists tools to improve it as an open need.
  • The panel noted patients preferred the word "holistic" to "patient-centred".

206

How often it is not taken
  • 72.8% of 324 Turkish children scored poorly adherent on the MASIF scale, many of whom reported taking it regularly.
  • Among 104 Turkish adults, adherence was low in 28.8% and moderate in 45.2%.
  • In an Israeli cohort of 4,526 patients the medication possession ratio was below 80% in more than half, and being on an IL-1 blocker did not improve it.
  • Self-report overestimates it: most of 179 patients were non-adherent on a validated questionnaire while far fewer said so.

24,61,150,260

What has been tried, and worked
  • A randomised trial gave 70 adolescents individual disease-management education with a booklet: depression scores fell and quality of life rose, while the anxiety change was not significant. The authors describe it as a first study of nurses educating adolescents with FMF.
  • A second randomised trial in 43 children compared a purpose-built mobile game, a booklet and nothing. The game group gained in self-efficacy, symptom management and quality of life, and the effect lasted longer than the booklet's.
  • No randomised trial of a nurse-led intervention with colchicine adherence as its primary outcome, and no FMF study of teach-back or motivational interviewing, was found in an October 2026 search.

117,122,222

Asking about adherence
  • The guideline directs teams to the 2020 EULAR points to consider on non-adherence, summarising the method as setting up a safe environment and using open questions.
  • Those points report that 30 to 80% of people with rheumatic diseases do not take treatment as agreed, and organise the response into preventing non-adherence, removing structural barriers, assessing it without blame, and tailoring the fix to the cause.
  • Measuring colchicine in hair as an objective check is described in the guideline as experimental and costly.

206,228

Family, school and genetics
  • Telling the school about the condition, including what to do during an attack, is described as useful, and children on long-term colchicine can take part in any sport. No published template school or work letter was found.
  • FMF is mostly autosomal recessive, so parents are usually unaffected carriers; if one parent has FMF and the other is a carrier, there is a 50% chance a child is affected. Testing finds variants in only about 70 to 80% of patients.
  • Among 90 caregivers of Turkish children, most reported mild care burden and a substantial minority moderate or severe.

139,219

Where to send people
  • The PRINTO and PReS family information pages cover treatment, follow-up tests, school, sport, pregnancy, inheritance and prognosis.
  • The FMF & AID Global Association runs a helpline, educational materials and an international patient survey.
  • The Autoinflammatory Alliance is a US charity promoting awareness, diagnosis and care.

The monitoring year

Month 12 · Urinalysis. Urinalysis at least yearly, and more often if the disease is poorly controlled. The 2016 version asked for one at every clinic visit. 202,206

The 2024 minimum outcome set
  • Recommendation 12, agreed by everyone on the panel: a minimum outcome set should include clinical disease activity, for example attack frequency per 3 months and physician assessment; patient experience, for example patient-reported outcome and experience measures; and biochemical markers, for example CRP and SAA.
  • Measures used in FMF studies: number of attacks per 3 months, attack site, quality of life, AIDAI, modified FMF50, physician global score, patient visual analogue score, CRP, SAA, SF-36, the Child Health Questionnaire and SF-12.
  • The panel recorded that no accepted definition of minimal disease activity existed, and that the existing tools lack the patient perspective.

206

Toxicity bloods, and a disagreement
  • Toxicity is assessed with complete blood counts and liver function tests, with dose reduction and investigation if liver enzymes exceed twice the upper limit of normal.
  • Creatine kinase is monitored carefully, with signs of toxicity, where renal function is reduced.
  • The panel recorded a real split: most paediatricians read a liver-enzyme rise as a colchicine effect in children subject to extra insults, while adult rheumatologists read it as inadequately controlled inflammation.
  • Children may show toxicity especially during viral infections, when raised enzymes or leukopenia may reflect the infection, concurrent NSAIDs and possibly antibiotics as well as the drug.

206

Amyloidosis follow-up
  • In AA amyloidosis the aim is complete and sustained control of biochemical inflammation measured by SAA, or a sensitive CRP where SAA is unavailable or costly.
  • Subclinical inflammation is monitored by SAA and proteinuria. A repeat biopsy for follow-up is not recommended.

206

Where the data goes
  • The Eurofever registry, hosted on the PRINTO website, collects baseline and cross-sectional clinical plus genetic data across the monogenic fevers and several related conditions. In its first 15 months it enrolled 1,360 patients from 64 centres in 28 countries, 376 of them with FMF.
  • The AIDA Network registry runs on REDCap with 3,769 fields in 23 instruments, across 110 centres in 23 countries.
  • A single registry across diseases was a deliberate choice, so manifestations can be compared and patients of uncertain classification included.

47,264

Growth in children
  • Neither guideline version specifies an interval or method for growth monitoring, so this app does not state one.
  • What is published is reassurance about the outcome: in children treated over 10 years growth stayed within normal percentiles, and the patient-information source states that growth retardation is not a major problem.
