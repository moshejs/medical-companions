Part 4 · Treatment & the future

The treatment ladder

Published guidance climbs one rung at a time: colchicine from the moment of clinical diagnosis, then the maximum tolerated dose, then IL-1 blockade added on top if attacks and inflammation persist. Press play to light the rungs in order, or click a rung to read what the guidelines say about it. This describes guidelines and trials, not advice for any person.

Rung 1 · Start

Colchicine at clinical diagnosis

EULAR 2016 recommendation 3 says colchicine should start as soon as a clinical diagnosis of FMF is made (level of evidence 1b). The 2024 update keeps this, and both versions allow the daily amount to be given once a day or split through the day. A Cochrane review of 10 randomised trials in 312 participants, and the 2025 systematic review written for the update, found once-daily and divided dosing equivalent. 199,203,222,229,283

In plain wordsTreatment does not wait for the gene test. Once the clinical picture fits FMF, published guidance starts colchicine.
Exam hookColchicine is prophylaxis, started on clinical grounds, taken for life. Genetics support the diagnosis; they do not gate the treatment.

2016 vs the 2024 update

  • Fewer, consolidated statements: 12 recommendations instead of 18, plus 4 overarching principles, with agreement above 9/10 on every statement.
  • More weight on adherence, which sits at the core of lifelong prophylaxis.
  • A new statement on colchicine overdose, intentional or accidental, which can be fatal and must be assessed carefully.
  • New statements on optimising biologic doses and tapering them once remission is reached.
  • A minimum outcome set: attack frequency per 3 months, physician assessment, patient-reported outcome and experience measures, and CRP or SAA, plus quality indicators.
  • No fixed monitoring interval is given; monitor more often at diagnosis and when disease is uncontrolled. 199,203,222

How CLUSTER was built

A phase 3 randomised trial across three diseases at once: colchicine-resistant FMF, mevalonate kinase deficiency and TRAPS. Patients were randomised at the time of a flare to canakinumab 150 mg under the skin every 4 weeks or placebo. The primary endpoint was resolution of that flare with no new flare through week 16. The FMF cohort had 63 participants. A 72-week open-label extension showed sustained flare control with CRP and SAA normalised, which is durable suppression rather than cure: attacks return when the drug is withdrawn. 44,201,283

The canakinumab label dose

The US prescribing information gives 150 mg every 4 weeks for FMF patients over 40 kg, or 2 mg/kg for 40 kg and under, increasable to 300 mg (4 mg/kg) if the response is not adequate. In CLUSTER, 71% of colchicine-resistant FMF patients responded at 300 mg every 4 weeks, and 46% stayed controlled when dosing was stretched to every 8 weeks. Quoted here as published label and trial figures, not as a dose for anyone: the dose for any individual is set by their clinician. 44,105

Colchicine does not stop

When a biologic is added, guidelines continue colchicine alongside it, because colchicine carries the hard long-term outcome data on preventing AA amyloidosis: cumulative proteinuria of 1.7% at 11 years in adherent patients against 48.9% at 9 years in non-adherent ones, in 1,070 patients. The IL-1 blockers control attacks and acute-phase markers; they have no comparable multi-decade amyloid outcome data. 199,229,286

In plain wordsThe new injection is added on top of the old tablet. It does not replace it.
Evidence check

What the Cochrane review concluded

Re-analysing the randomised trials, Cochrane rated the evidence for each IL-1 blocker as moderate certainty. Canakinumab reduced the number of patients with attacks (RR 0.41, 95% CI 0.26–0.65, 63 participants). For anakinra (RR 0.76, 0.54–1.07) and rilonacept (RR 0.87, 0.59–1.26) the confidence intervals crossed 1, so on that outcome the review found no clear difference from placebo, although anakinra did lower CRP. The individual trials still reported fewer attacks on drug; the bars above are those trial figures, and this re-analysis is the more cautious reading of the same data. The trials are small; the reviewers asked for more. 283

Intolerance is not the same as resistance

Resistance means attacks or inflammation persist at the maximum tolerated dose. Intolerance means side effects, most often diarrhoea and other gut symptoms, stop the dose from reaching that point. Roughly 5 to 10% of patients are described as resistant and another 2 to 5% as intolerant; both groups are the population every biologic trial recruits. 16,200,202

What a trial counted as success

Different trials used different yardsticks, which is why the bars above cannot be compared across groups. CLUSTER used complete response at week 16. The anakinra trial counted attacks per month (1.7 vs 3.5, P=0.037) and how many patients fell below one attack a month (6 of 12 vs 0 of 13). The rilonacept trial compared median attack rates within the same patients on and off drug (0.77 vs 2.00, P=0.027), and 29% versus 0% of treatment courses were attack-free. A 2025 systematic review of 42 studies and 1,798 patients, including 6 randomised trials of IL-1 blockers, concluded they are effective and relatively safe in colchicine-unresponsive FMF. 20,44,93,94,229
