Part 3 · Complications & diagnosis

Diagnosis & criteria

There is no single test that proves FMF. The diagnosis rests on the attack pattern, blood markers in and between attacks, the gene test and the response to colchicine. Tick features in the calculators below, then press play on the lab timeline and the diagnostic pathway.

Eurofever/PRINTO 2019 calculator

These are classification criteria, built so that research studies enrol comparable patients. They are not a diagnostic test for one person, and a doctor can diagnose FMF in someone who does not meet them. 76

The diagnostic pathway

A simplified teaching sequence drawn from published reviews and guidelines. Real work-ups overlap and loop back.

Step 1

Recurrent, self-limited fever + serositis

A typical attack is recurrent (at least 3 of the same type), febrile (≥38 °C) and short (12 h to 3 days), with peritonitis, pleuritis or arthritis, and the person is well between attacks. 147,161

In plain wordsThe story comes first: short, repeated bouts of fever and pain that stop on their own.
Exam hookTel Hashomer: 2 major, or 1 major + 2 minor. Livneh: ≥1 major or ≥2 minor. Yalçınkaya–Özen (children): ≥2 of 5. Eurofever/PRINTO: confirmatory genotype + ≥1 of 4, non-confirmatory + ≥2 of 4, or clinical-only ≥6 of 9. In plain wordsDoctors recognise FMF from the story: short bouts of fever with belly, chest or joint pain that stop by themselves, with blood tests that are high during an attack and calmer in between. A gene test helps but can be negative in people who really have FMF. Sometimes the clearest answer is whether daily colchicine stops the attacks.

Why the gene test can mislead

About 10–20% of people with FMF carry no MEFV variant, usually with a milder picture. Single variants are common: in a Turkish referral cohort, 415 of 1,291 patients (32%) were heterozygous. Healthy carriers also run slightly higher CRP and SAA than non-carriers. 19,143,257

Criteria travel badly

In Eurofever children whose fevers had other causes, the Livneh criteria had a specificity of only 41.1% and the paediatric criteria 40.7%. Criteria built in high-prevalence populations over-call FMF where other periodic fevers are common. 50

Colchicine response helps, but is not proof

In daily practice a good response and the right ancestry can support FMF in someone with a non-confirmatory genotype, but the Eurofever/PRINTO panel left both out of the criteria. 76

One pathogenic variant is not "confirmatory"

A single heterozygous pathogenic MEFV variant counts as a non-confirmatory genotype in the 2019 criteria, so it needs 2 of the 4 clinical items, not 1. The same goes for one pathogenic variant plus a VUS in trans, and for two VUS. Benign and likely-benign variants do not count at all. 76,147

Counts, not points

Both FMF sets are plain counts: every item scores 1 and there are no weights, so the 4-item and 9-item thresholds are all there is. The TRAPS clinical set in the same paper is point-weighted with a threshold of 5, so the two models should not be mixed up. 76

Who gets the gene test?

The clinical-only Eurofever/PRINTO items can also be used to pick who should be tested. Earlier provisional Eurofever clinical scores (2015) separated FMF from other periodic fevers with an AUC of 0.98. 66,76
