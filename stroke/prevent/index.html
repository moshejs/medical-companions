Part 4 · Preventing another stroke

Risk factors and prevention

How much of stroke is linked to things that can change? Build up the INTERSTROKE figures, switch factors on and off, and see why those percentages cannot simply be added. Then open the trials behind blood pressure and lipid treatment, and look at diabetes, smoking, diet and salt.

This page reports what studies and guidelines say. It is not advice for any one person. Figures describe groups of people, not what will happen to you.

Step 1 · one factor

Hypertension alone

INTERSTROKE was a standardised case-control study in 32 countries from 2007 to 2015: 13 447 people with an acute first stroke and 13 472 controls. Hypertension (a history of it, or a reading of 140/90 mm Hg or higher) had an odds ratio of 2.98 and the largest population attributable risk (PAR), 47.9% 751.

In plain wordsMost strokes are linked to things that can be changed, and high blood pressure is the biggest one 305,751.

A second way of counting: GBD 2021

The Global Burden of Disease study attributed 84.1% (77.8 to 88.8) of stroke disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) to the 23 risks it analysed 305. This does not contradict 90.7%. INTERSTROKE gives PAR from a case-control comparison of first strokes. GBD gives attributable DALYs from modelled exposure 305,751.

GBD also reports that the fall in stroke incidence stalled from 2015, with some increases in people younger than 70 and in countries with lower socio-demographic index 305.

Blood pressure and lipid trials

Choose a set, then choose a row to read the trial. A ratio below 1 means fewer events with the more intensive treatment.

Outcomes differ between rows (stroke, or a wider composite), so rows are not directly comparable. Rows given as relative risk reduction are plotted as 1 minus RRR.

Blood pressure · 123 studies

Each 10 mm Hg

In 123 studies and 613 815 participants, a 10 mm Hg reduction in systolic BP reduced stroke by 27%. Coronary disease RR was 0.83 and heart failure RR 0.72. Calcium channel blockers were superior to other drugs for preventing stroke and beta blockers inferior 289.

How low for blood pressure after a stroke? SPS3 and RESPECT were individually not significant at their lower targets 92,507. Pooled data support below 130/80 mm Hg 507, and ESPRIT supports below 120 mm Hg with no heterogeneity by prior stroke 588. This guide found no stroke-only trial below 120 mm Hg. The question is open.

How low for LDL? Treat Stroke to Target supports below 70 mg/dL 33. FOURIER observational data suggest benefit below 40 mg/dL 687. SPARCL showed more haemorrhagic stroke with high-dose statin 29, while later data show no clear excess 33,687. The question is open.

Exam hookA 10 mm Hg fall in systolic BP reduces stroke by about 27% 289. PROGRESS: combination therapy reduced stroke, single-agent perindopril did not, and benefit included non-hypertensive people 814. SPARCL: fewer strokes overall but more haemorrhagic strokes 29.

Aspirin with no previous cardiovascular disease

13 trials, 164 225 people: the cardiovascular composite fell (HR 0.89, NNT 265) and major bleeding rose (HR 1.43, NNH 210) 1094. The 2009 ATT meta-analysis found no significant net effect on stroke in primary prevention, 0.20% vs 0.21% per year 61.

Aspirin after a stroke

In secondary prevention, total stroke fell from 2.54% to 2.08% per year 61. Module 4.2 covers early aspirin, dual antiplatelets and anticoagulants.

Diabetes, weight and insulin resistance

IRIS: pioglitazone

3876 people with insulin resistance but no diabetes (HOMA-IR above 3.0) after stroke or TIA. Stroke or MI 9.0% vs 11.8% at 4.8 years (HR 0.76, 0.62 to 0.93). Diabetes 3.8% vs 7.7% (HR 0.48) 493.

Harms: weight gain over 4.5 kg 52.2% vs 33.7%, oedema 35.6% vs 24.9%, fracture needing surgery or admission 5.1% vs 3.2%. Mortality did not differ 493.

GLP-1 receptor agonists

8 trials, 60 080 people with type 2 diabetes: MACE HR 0.86 (0.80 to 0.93), all-cause mortality HR 0.88 (0.82 to 0.94) 890.

A later meta-analysis of 11 trials and 85 373 people gives stroke HR 0.88 (0.80 to 0.96), and 0.73 after a prior stroke 13.

SELECT: semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly

17 604 people with cardiovascular disease and BMI of 27 or more, no diabetes. Composite 6.5% vs 8.0% (HR 0.80, 0.72 to 0.90). Stopping for adverse events 16.6% vs 8.2% 579.

Two figures for GLP-1 and stroke. The guideline review quotes a 27% relative reduction (0.7% absolute) in non-fatal stroke 828; the larger meta-analysis gives 12% 13. Different trial sets and outcome definitions may explain the spread. Both are shown here rather than choosing one.

Smoking: one cigarette a day

Published anchors at 1 and 20 cigarettes a day; the curve between them is schematic 382.

Stopping after a stroke

In the IRIS cohort, 450 of 1072 smokers (42%) quit between the event and randomisation. Five-year stroke, MI or death was 15.7% in those who quit vs 22.6% in those who continued (adjusted HR 0.66, 0.48 to 0.90). This was an observational, non-prespecified analysis 285.

Dose and past smoking

Jackson Heart Study, 4410 Black adults: current smoking HR 2.48 (1.60 to 3.83) vs never smoking, 2.28 at 1 to 19 and 2.78 at 20 or more cigarettes a day. Past smoking HR 1.10 (0.74 to 1.64) 764.

In plain wordsEven one cigarette a day raises stroke risk. Cutting down helps less than people expect, and stopping is the goal 382. People who stopped after a stroke or TIA had fewer further problems than those who kept smoking 285.

Salt substitute: SSaSS

A cluster randomised trial in 600 villages in rural China, with 20 995 people (72.6% with prior stroke, 88.4% with hypertension). The substitute was 75% sodium chloride and 25% potassium chloride 728.

Events per 1000 person-years 728.

The size of the effect does not match between sources. The trial reports a 14% lower stroke rate (rate ratio 0.86) 728. A review of the 2024 guideline quotes a 22% relative reduction (0.34% absolute) for this trial 828. The review's source could not be traced. This guide uses the trial's own number.

Diet, activity and alcohol

PREDIMED: Mediterranean diet

Republished in 2018 after randomisation deviations were found. 7447 people at high risk: primary composite HR 0.69 (0.53 to 0.91) with olive oil and 0.72 (0.54 to 0.95) with nuts vs reduced-fat advice. Results were similar after omitting 1588 people whose assignment departed from protocol 287. The guideline review quotes a 42% relative reduction in stroke (1.5% absolute) 828.

Physical activity

14 cohorts, 2 639 086 people: moderate-to-vigorous activity had an L-shaped association with stroke. The greatest reduction (19%) was at 19 MET-hours a week, followed by a gradual rise. Evidence in men and for haemorrhagic stroke is limited 570.

Alcohol by dose

27 prospective studies, ischaemic stroke RR: 0.90 under 1 drink a day, 0.92 at 1 to 2, 1.08 at over 2 to 4, 1.14 over 4. Heavy drinking RR 1.67 for intracerebral and 1.82 for subarachnoid haemorrhage 550.

A real controversy: is moderate drinking protective? Observational analyses show a U shape in China Kadoorie Biobank men and lower risk at low intake in cohort data 550,677. Genotype-predicted alcohol gave a continuously positive association with stroke (RR per 280 g a week 1.58 for intracerebral haemorrhage, 1.27 for ischaemic stroke). The authors conclude the apparent protection is largely non-causal 677.

Sleep apnoea and lipoprotein(a)

Established

SAVE: CPAP

2717 adults with moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnoea and coronary or cerebrovascular disease: 17.0% vs 15.4% (HR 1.10, 0.91 to 1.32). Mean use was 3.3 hours a night. Snoring, sleepiness, quality of life and mood improved 644.

Early evidence

Sleep SMART: pending

Tests auto-CPAP after acute stroke or high-risk TIA 139. It randomised 1892 people, below the 3062 planned 139,141. The registry lists no posted results, with estimated completion in November 2026 714.

Established association

Lp(a) and risk

Above 93 mg/dL vs below 10 mg/dL: adjusted ischaemic stroke HR 1.60 (1.24 to 2.05) in Copenhagen. Genetic causal risk ratios per 50 mg/dL were 1.20 and 1.27 545. In BIOSIGNAL it was linked to large-artery stroke, most strongly under age 60 49.

Hypothesis

Lowering Lp(a)

Not yet shown to help. Pelacarsen lowered Lp(a) but did not reduce the primary composite in Lp(a)HORIZON 873. Extended analyses found no benefit (on-treatment HR 0.98, 0.85 to 1.12) 739. Whether earlier or stronger lowering would help is still being tested 873.

In plain wordsBlood pressure is the factor with the largest share. Trials also support lowering cholesterol after a stroke linked to narrowed arteries 33. These are findings about groups. Your own targets are a matter for you and your care team.
