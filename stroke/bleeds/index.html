Part 1 · Understanding stroke

Bleeding strokes

A bleeding stroke (haemorrhagic stroke, US spelling hemorrhagic) has two main forms. Blood can burst into the brain itself, or spread over its surface from a burst aneurysm. Scrub through both, then try the rules doctors use to spot them.

Step 1 · onset

A small artery gives way

ICH affects about 3.4 million people worldwide each year and causes about 2.8 million deaths 943. It is about 10% of strokes in the United States and 6.5 to 19.6% worldwide 622. Hypertensive bleeds occur in deep areas supplied by small vessels 622. The slice shows the person's left on the viewer's right, as a scan is read.

In plain wordsA brain bleed happens when a blood vessel inside the head breaks and blood leaks out. Bleeds are less common than clots but are more often fatal 998.

A sudden headache that is the worst of your life, peaking in moments, is an emergency 192. Stroke signs of any kind: call emergency services.

The 6-hour CT rule

CT sensitivity was higher within 6 hours of onset than overall 786. Move the time since the headache began, and add a new neurological deficit, to see which route the 2023 AHA/ASA guideline describes 433.

Teaching tool only, never for an individual's diagnosis or care. Sensitivity axis starts at 85%.

Under 6 hours, no new deficit

A high-quality CT, read by a board-certified neuroradiologist

AHA/ASA 2023 gives this route Class 2a, B-NR 433. Within 6 hours one cohort found sensitivity 100% (97.0 to 100.0) and specificity 100% (99.5 to 100) on third-generation scanners read by a qualified radiologist 786.

In plain wordsA very early scan picks up nearly all of these bleeds, when the scanner and the reader meet the standard.

Disagreement on the early scan

Within 6 hours, one cohort found 100% sensitivity 786. A meta-analysis gave 98.7%, with 13 missed SAH, 1.46 per 1000 257. A review quotes 95.5% for an implementation study of 3672 people, so SAH might be missed in 5% 192. The figures are not reconciled here. The first study's conditions were third-generation scanners read by a qualified radiologist 786.

After CT

After lumbar puncture in 1739 alert patients, fewer than 2000 x 106/L red cells plus no xanthochromia excluded aneurysmal SAH: sensitivity 100% (74.7 to 100), specificity 91.2%, based on only 15 cases 788. If CT angiography is negative or inconclusive with high concern for an aneurysm, AHA/ASA 2023 says digital subtraction angiography is indicated (Class 1, B-NR) 433.

Causes of ICH: SMASH-U and where bleeds sit

SMASH-U sorted 1013 consecutive ICH cases in Helsinki into six groups 665. Pick a group. Deep bleeds point to hypertensive disease and lobar bleeds to amyloid angiopathy 37,622.

Schematic. Bleed sizes only hint at the cited medians. Percentage shares by exact site could not be verified, so none is given.

SMASH-U

Hypertensive angiopathy

35% of the 1013 cases 665. Hypertensive bleeds occur in deep areas supplied by small vessels: putamen, caudate, thalamus, brainstem and deep cerebellar nuclei 622.

High blood pressure

Long-standing hypertension degenerates the media and smooth muscle of the responsible small arteries. Lipohyalinosis is most often found in non-lobar ICH 37. Bleeds occur in the putamen, caudate, thalamus, brainstem and deep cerebellar nuclei 622. The most common site is the putamen 37.

Cerebral amyloid angiopathy

Amyloid builds up in arterial walls with loss of smooth muscle cells. It occurs mainly in lobar locations or the subarachnoid space over the cortical convexities, with microbleeds or superficial siderosis 622.

Anticoagulants

In Greater Cincinnati, anticoagulant-associated ICH rose from 5% of ICH in 1988 to 17% in 1999, as US per-capita warfarin distribution quadrupled 322. Warfarin did not raise baseline volume, but raised the risk of expansion (OR 6.2, 1.7 to 22.9) 324.

Other risk factors

One review lists current smoking, excess alcohol, low cholesterol and drugs, plus older age, male sex, Asian ethnicity, chronic kidney disease and microbleeds 37.

Disputed outside ICH In a community and memory clinic cohort of 54, v2.0 probable CAA had sensitivity 28.6% and specificity 65.3% (AUC 0.47), and was not superior to v1.5 955.

WFNS and Hunt and Hess describe how ill the person is. AHA/ASA 2023 recommends clinical scales like these to set initial severity and predict outcome (Class 1, B-NR) 433. The Fisher family describes the blood on CT, which predicts spasm. In the original Fisher work, no detected or diffuse blood was almost never followed by severe vasospasm (1 of 18), while thick clots or layers almost always were (23 of 24) 318. In 1355 placebo-arm patients, 33% developed symptomatic vasospasm, and the modified scale's crude ORs against grade 0 to 1 were 1.6, 1.6 and 2.2 for grades 2, 3 and 4 337.
