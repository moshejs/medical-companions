Part 3 · Acute treatment

Clot-dissolving treatment

Thrombolysis means giving a medicine that dissolves a clot. Watch it work inside an artery, move the delay slider to see what time does to the benefit, then compare alteplase with tenecteplase. This page reports what trials, labels and guidelines say. It is not medical advice.

If you think someone is having a stroke, call your local emergency number. In the data below, earlier treatment brought more benefit 278,895.

Step 1 · the blockage

A clot blocks the artery

Most strokes happen because a clot blocks a blood vessel in the brain. Reperfusion means getting blood flowing again 364,738. Red cells pile up behind the clot and none get past.

In plain wordsA clot is like a plug in a pipe. Brain tissue beyond it is short of blood until the plug clears.
Within 3 hours

10 in 100

Disability-free survival rose by about 10% with alteplase, in a pool of 9 trials and 6756 people 278.

From 3 to 4.5 hours

5 in 100

The same analysis found about 5% for treatment from 3.1 to 4.5 hours 278.

The cost

2 in 100

The early excess of fatal bleeding was about 2%, offset by the gains above 278.

One minute saved

1.8 days

Modelling: about 1.8 days of extra healthy life on average for each minute of onset-to-treatment time saved (95% prediction interval 0.9 to 2.7) 668.

Where does benefit end? The two pools differ. Lees 2010 saw no benefit after around 270 minutes. Emberson 2014 placed zero effect at 6.3 hours and loss of statistical confidence at 5.1 hours. Both support treatment up to 4.5 hours 278,564.

Bleeding: the number depends on the definition

Pooled data from the same patients give very different headline rates, because "symptomatic" can be defined in several ways. Switch views to see the definitions, the effect of severity, and the trade-off.

Same drug, different number

Definitions drive the headline

Across the same pooled patients symptomatic bleeding reads 6.8% or 3.7% by definition, and NINDS reported 6.4% under its own definition 738,1046. Odds ratios were 5.55 (4.01 to 7.70), 6.67 (4.11 to 10.84) and 7.14 (3.98 to 12.79) 1046.

Other doses tested

Low-dose alteplase in ENCHANTED (3310 people, 63% Asian): 0.6 versus 0.9 mg/kg did not meet noninferiority for death or disability (53.2% versus 51.1%; OR 1.09, 0.95 to 1.25). Major symptomatic haemorrhage was 1.0% versus 2.1% 39.

FDA approval of TNKase for stroke in adults was reported on 4 March 2025, resting on AcT 298,349.

Windows: what labels say, what trials tested

Licence and guideline differ. Both US labels say within 3 hours, whereas ESO guidance and the 2026 AHA/ASA guideline as summarised work to 4.5 hours 96,293,298,698. The TNKase label analysed the 0 to 3 hour AcT subset because the comparator is approved for 0 to 3 hours 298.

Raised risk to weigh

Both labels list recent major surgery, recent gastrointestinal or genitourinary bleeding, recent trauma, and blood pressure above 175 systolic or 110 diastolic 293,298.

Clotting tests

Without recent oral anticoagulant or heparin use, treatment can start before coagulation results. The Activase label says to discontinue if pretreatment INR is above 1.7 or aPTT is elevated 293.

Blood pressure, ESO 2025

Below 185/110 mmHg before the bolus. Below 180/105 mmHg during and for 24 hours after thrombolysis (low certainty). These thresholds come from early pilot studies and the NINDS protocol, not from trials testing the thresholds 885.

Where it is given

Both labels limit stroke use to facilities with timely access to evaluation and management of intracranial haemorrhage 293,298.

Lower blood pressure targets

ENCHANTED (2196 people): systolic 130 to 140 versus below 180 mmHg gave no difference in mRS (OR 1.01, 0.87 to 1.17). Any intracranial haemorrhage was 14.8% versus 18.7% (OR 0.75, 0.60 to 0.94) 41.

Nursing points from the labels

  • Monitor and control blood pressure often during and after treatment
  • Avoid intramuscular injections and non-essential trauma
  • Arterial and venous punctures only as required; avoid internal jugular and subclavian punctures
  • Compress an arterial puncture for at least 30 minutes 293,298

Open questions

Early evidence

Recent DOAC: a controversy

The usual position: Meinel 2023 states that international guidelines recommend avoiding thrombolysis after recent DOAC intake 657.

The challenge: in its retrospective cohort (64 centres, 33,207 people, 832 with a DOAC within 48 hours) symptomatic haemorrhage was 2.5% with a DOAC versus 4.1% without anticoagulation (adjusted OR 0.57, 0.36 to 0.92). 30.3% had reversal, 27.0% had levels measured and 42.7% had neither 657. A single-centre emulation (98 people) found 0 of 49 versus 2 of 49 145.

Both are observational, with selection bias acknowledged. Bücke and colleagues call for randomised trials 145,657.

Early evidence

Adding another drug: opposite results

MOST (514 people): utility-weighted mRS 5.2 with argatroban, 6.3 with eptifibatide, 6.8 with placebo. Mortality 24%, 12% and 8%. Neither reduced disability 9.

ASSET-IT (China, 832 people, non-cardioembolic, not thrombectomy candidates): tirofiban within 60 minutes after thrombolysis gave mRS 0 to 1 of 65.9% versus 54.9% (RR 1.20, 1.07 to 1.34). Symptomatic haemorrhage 1.7% versus 0 964.

Different drugs, populations and countries. The 2026 guideline is summarised as not recommending argatroban or eptifibatide 376,812.

Women and older people

In 18 studies women had 30% lower odds of receiving rt-PA (OR 0.70, 0.55 to 0.88). Among those arriving within 3 hours without contraindications, the difference was not significant (OR 0.81, 0.58 to 1.13) 833.

By age: at 80 or under, mRS 0 to 1 was 39% versus 34% (OR 1.25, 1.10 to 1.42); above 80, 18% versus 13% (OR 1.56, 1.17 to 2.08). Age did not change the effect (p=0.53) 278.

Swelling of the lips and tongue

Orolingual angioedema: 42 of 530 (7.9%) at one UK centre, 5 to 189 minutes after starting tPA. 12% of cases needed urgent advanced airway care. Prior ACE inhibitor use was the only independent predictor (OR 2.3, 1.1 to 4.7) 452.

The rate is disputed: trial and label figures are near 1% 298,695, against 7.9% in consecutive patients with retrospective grading 452.

If a bleed happens

An AHA/ASA statement says treatment of symptomatic haemorrhage rests on expert opinion and small case series; prompt diagnosis and early correction of the coagulopathy remain the mainstay 1074. A separate guideline covers reversal of thrombolytics in intracranial haemorrhage 338.

In plain wordsA few people get swelling of the lips or tongue after the clot-buster. Staff watch for this because it can affect breathing. It is more likely in people taking ACE inhibitor blood pressure tablets 452.

What the 2026 guideline changed

The 2026 AHA/ASA guideline replaces the 2018 guideline and 2019 update 810,812. Its stated key updates include thrombolytic choice and eligibility, thrombectomy eligibility and the approach to contraindications. Its literature search ran to December 2024, with high-impact additions to March 2025 812. So trials published later, such as OPTION, HOPE and ASSET-IT, are unlikely to be reflected. That is an inference from the search dates 613,812,964,1097.

The full text could not be read for this guide, so no class of recommendation is given. Secondary sources summarise it as: tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg up to 25 mg or alteplase within 4.5 hours; 0.4 mg/kg not recommended; no delay for CTA, MRA or perfusion imaging within 4.5 hours; imaging-selected thrombolysis for unknown onset or 4.5 to 9 hours 376,698. Intensive systolic lowering below 140 mmHg is summarised as not improving function after thrombolysis 376,698. Mobile stroke units are recommended where available 177,376.

A correction has been issued, and a separate group of authors published proposed corrections in 2026. Their content was not available for this guide, so check the original before relying on any statement 18,813. Grotta's 2026 review frames the new directions: vascular targets for lysis versus thrombectomy, tenecteplase replacing alteplase, broader indications and ancillary drugs 375.
