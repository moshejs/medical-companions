Not medical adviceThe Stroke Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. If you think someone is having a stroke, call your local emergency number. It does not replace a doctor, nurse, physiotherapist, occupational therapist, speech and language therapist or the rest of a stroke team. Scores such as the NIHSS, ICH score, ABCD2, CHA2DS2-VASc and ASPECTS are teaching aids, never for an individual's diagnosis, prognosis or treatment. Doses, thresholds and guideline statements are quoted as published, never as instructions. Outcomes are population statistics; they are not predictions for any person.

Part 2 · Recognition & assessment

Imaging & the first hour

From the door to the needle or the groin, every scan answers one question and costs minutes. Walk the pathway with the clock running, score a teaching ASPECTS, and test core and penumbra volumes against the late-window trial criteria as they are quoted.

Schematic. The order and the running minutes are illustrative and vary between centres. Only the marks at 25, 45 and 60 minutes are published benchmarks 274,577,1067. No door-to-groin benchmark appears in the sources read here.

Non-contrast CT

CT

Registry analyses use door-to-imaging of 25 minutes or less as the quality measure 274,577. CT matched MRI for acute haemorrhage but was far less sensitive for acute ischaemia: 26% vs 83% for any acute stroke, in 356 people 172.

In plain wordsCT is the fast first look. It shows bleeding well, but a normal CT does not rule out a stroke 172.

CT or MRI: what each one sees early

CT is fast and sees blood well, but it often misses a very early infarct. A normal CT does not rule out ischaemic stroke 172. Pick a comparison.

Any acute stroke. Prospective comparison in 356 people with suspected stroke, 217 with a final diagnosis of stroke. MRI 83% (95% CI 78 to 88), CT 26% (20 to 32) 172.

Blood: a draw

For acute haemorrhage MRI and CT were equivalent (96% concordance), and MRI found far more chronic haemorrhage, mostly microbleeds 498. MRI was similar to CT for acute intracranial haemorrhage in the Chalela series too 172.

Early signs on CT are hard

Mean sensitivity for early infarction signs 66% (range 20 to 87%), specificity 87%, with interobserver kappa 0.14 to 0.78. Experience improved detection; knowing the symptoms did not 1029. In 258 readers, slower reading and later CT helped 1030.

What early signs mean

Any early sign raised the risk of poor outcome (OR 3.11, 95% CI 2.77 to 3.49; 3468 people). Two studies found no evidence that thrombolysis in the presence of early signs worsened outcome beyond the effect of the signs themselves 1029.

The hyperdense artery

Pooled risk ratio for poor outcome after thrombolysis 1.56 (1.50 to 1.62) in 11,818 people, with no significant effect on symptomatic haemorrhage 948. A second meta-analysis found 1.43 after reperfusion therapy but called its prognostic sensitivity and specificity poor 921. Every one of 62 people who recanalised with thrombolysis had a thrombus of 8 mm or less 844.

In plain wordsA normal first CT scan does not rule out a stroke. CT shows bleeding well but can miss early stroke damage 172.
Exam hookNon-contrast CT has low sensitivity for early ischaemia: 26% vs 83% for MRI for any acute stroke 172, and 41.8% in the posterior fossa 452.

ASPECTS: a clickable teaching scorer

ASPECTS divides the middle cerebral artery territory into 10 regions. Start at 10 and take off one point for each region showing early ischaemic change; 0 means diffuse involvement of the whole territory 84,791. Click or tab to a region and press Enter, or use the buttons. The regions are caudate (C), lentiform (L), internal capsule (IC), insular ribbon (I), and cortex M1 to M3, with M4 to M6 about 2 cm above them 791.

Teaching toolThis scorer only shows how the published score adds up. It gives a number, not a prediction, and is never for an individual's diagnosis, prognosis or treatment. Real ASPECTS reading needs a real scan and a trained reader.

Derivation

In 156 people with anterior ischaemia treated with alteplase within 3 hours, ASPECTS correlated inversely with NIHSS (r=-0.56). Kappa was 0.71 to 0.89 when the affected hemisphere was known. Sensitivity and specificity were 0.78 and 0.96 for functional outcome, and 0.90 and 0.62 for symptomatic haemorrhage 84.

The 7 line

Dependence and death rise sharply at an ASPECTS of 7 or less, in the study populations 791.

Not a hard stop

SELECT2 enrolled ASPECTS 3 to 5 or core of at least 50 mL: thrombectomy gave generalised OR 1.51 (95% CI 1.20 to 1.89), functional independence 20% vs 7% 886. RESCUE-Japan LIMIT: mRS 0 to 3 in 31.0% vs 12.7%, any intracranial haemorrhage 58.0% vs 31.4% 1087.

Unknown onset: the DWI-FLAIR mismatch

Some people wake up with a stroke, so nobody knows when it began. A lesion visible on DWI but not on FLAIR suggests onset within about 4.5 hours 974. Scrub the hours.

Door-to-needle: how fast it can go

Helsinki. In 2011, 31% of admitted people with ischaemic stroke had tPA, 94% of those within 60 minutes 664.

The baseline

Only 26.6% of 25,504 people treated within 3 hours had door-to-needle of 60 minutes or less (2003 to 2009), varying by hospital from 0% to 79.2% 326.

What it bought in hospital

Target: Stroke phase II vs preintervention: alteplase use 11.7% vs 5.6%, in-hospital mortality 6.0% vs 10.0% (adjusted OR 0.69), bleeding 3.4% vs 5.5%, discharge home 49.6% vs 35.7% 1067.

One year on

In 61,426 people, door-to-needle above 60 vs within 60 minutes: mortality 35.8% vs 32.1% (adjusted HR 1.11) 627. Yet Target: Stroke's own mortality benefit dissipated after adjustment at one year (HR 0.98, 0.94 to 1.02), a useful caution 626.

Every 15 minutes

In 58,353 people, each 15 minutes faster went with lower in-hospital mortality (OR 0.96) and more independent walking at discharge (OR 1.04) 893. Each 15 minutes of delay in people having both treatments raised the odds of never returning home in a year (OR 1.12) 628.

The value of a minute

Each minute saved on onset-to-treatment gave on average 1.8 days of healthy life (95% prediction interval 0.9 to 2.7), in a model 667.

Early evidence

AI reads the scan

A cluster trial of 243 people: AI LVO detection with phone alerts cut door-to-groin by 11.2 minutes, with no difference in functional independence 636. Observational data: CTA-to-team-notification 7 vs 26 minutes 276; in England the thrombectomy rate rose faster in evaluation sites (funded through NHS England, not randomised) 700. Algorithms could not be compared in a 2020 review 696.

Guidelines and open questions

The 2026 AHA/ASA guideline replaces the 2018 guideline and its 2019 update. Its stated key updates cover thrombolytic choice and eligibility, thrombectomy eligibility, hyperglycaemia, dysphagia, paediatric stroke and thrombolysis contraindications 809,811. Its full text could not be read here, so its class and level statements are not quoted from it. As reported in a 2026 review: AHA/ASA recommends thrombectomy to 16 hours (Class I) and considers it reasonable to 24 hours (Class IIa) in people meeting DAWN or DEFUSE 3 criteria 762. ESO-ESMINT 2019 found high-quality evidence for thrombectomy with best medical care within 6 hours for LVO, and moderate-quality evidence at 6 to 24 hours for people meeting trial criteria 987. A recent review describes imaging strategy as evolving as eligibility expands 1085.

QuestionEvidence each way
Who should get MRI first?Chalela argues MRI should be preferred 172. Helsinki found that added vascular or perfusion imaging doubled in-hospital delay 664. Cochrane questions how far the DWI data generalise: sensitivity 0.99 with a CI of 0.23 to 1.00, on a narrow patient spectrum, small samples and incorporation bias 126. An AAN guideline, since retired, rated DWI as more useful than non-contrast CT within 12 hours (Level A) 900.
How low an ASPECTS?The original score identified people unlikely to recover independently despite thrombolysis 84. Recent thrombectomy trials in large core still show benefit, with low absolute rates of independence and more haemorrhage in one 886,1087.
Door-to-imaging target?US registry analyses use 25 minutes or less 274,577. A survey of national guidelines found the USA and Qatar put weight on aiming for under 20 minutes 1048.
