Part 2 · Recognition & assessment

The NIH Stroke Scale

The NIHSS is a set of short bedside checks: 15 items, 0 to 42. Watch a score build item by item, try every item yourself with teaching notes, see what it misses, and meet the modified Rankin Scale that trials use for the end result.

Teaching toolThis calculator explains how the published scale adds up. It is never for an individual's diagnosis, prognosis or treatment, and it does not teach or certify examination. Results are shown as population figures from the cited studies, not predictions for a person.

Where the wording comes from. Item wording and score options here are taken from two secondary summaries: a 2022 narrative review 146 and a 2014 Continuum appendix 199. The official NINDS form and booklet could not be fetched for this guide (HTTP 403), so administration rules, such as which response to score, are not stated here and need checking against the official NIH Stroke Scale from NINDS.

Schematic. The three examples are invented score sets, not people. The slice sits opposite the weaker side of the body; lesion size follows the total only as a picture, and the next sections show that the link is loose.

Item 11, and the total

Attention and total

Extinction and inattention closes the scale. A commonly used banding is minor 1 to 4, moderate 5 to 15, moderate to severe 16 to 20, severe 21 to 42: a convention, not validated thresholds 328,699. In clinical practice the NIHSS is useful for early prognosis and serial assessment 488.

In plain wordsThe total describes today. It is not a promise about the future 6,488.
Exam hookNIHSS 16 or more forecasts death or severe disability; 6 or less forecasts good recovery 6.

Score every item

Pick one option for each item. Choosing an option moves the picture above to the full total. An untestable (UN) choice adds nothing to the total; one summary codes the same situations as 9 199. The maximum of 42 is the sum of the item maxima and agrees with the published maximum 146.

1aLevel of consciousness

Tests alertness. Maximum 3.

The modified NIHSS removes level of consciousness 608. In the original scale the most reliable item, pupillary response, had low validity, while less reliable motor items were more valid 134.

1bLOC questions

Tests month and age. Maximum 2.

Uses month and age 199. A high LVO signal: LOC questions independently predicted central occlusion 313.

1cLOC commands

Tests close eyes and hand grip. Maximum 2.

Two simple tasks: closing the eyes and gripping the hand 146.

2Best gaze

Tests eye movement. Maximum 2.

A high LVO signal: gaze independently predicted central occlusion 313, and gaze deviation was a significant LVO indicator across tools 45.

3Visual

Tests visual fields. Maximum 3.

One of only two subscores that run higher in posterior stroke 891. Hemianopia explained 10.8% of EQ-5D utility variance at 90 days 999.

4Facial palsy

Tests the face. Maximum 3.

Historically one of the hardest items to agree on, kappa below 0.40 606. One summary words 2 as "partial (lower only)" and 3 as "complete (upper and lower)" 199. The modified NIHSS removes it 608.

5aMotor arm, left

Tests drift, held for 10 s. Maximum 4.

Limb paresis explained most of the variance in quality of life at 90 days (17.5 to 24.1% of EQ-5D utility) 999. A right-hemisphere stroke shows here with no language points to add 1064.

5bMotor arm, right

Tests drift, held for 10 s. Maximum 4.

Same options as the left arm. In the 4-factor fit, motor function on each side separates from cortical function 607,609.

6aMotor leg, left

Tests drift, held for 5 s. Maximum 4.

A high LVO signal: motor leg independently predicted central occlusion 313.

6bMotor leg, right

Tests drift, held for 5 s. Maximum 4.

Same options as the left leg, and the same LVO signal 313.

7Limb ataxia

Tests finger-nose-finger and heel-knee-shin. Maximum 2.

Kappa below 0.40 historically 606. Runs higher in posterior stroke 891. Truncal ataxia, the commonest sign in people who scored 0 with an infarct on DWI, is not on the scale 639.

8Sensory

Tests pinprick. Maximum 2.

Uses pinprick 199. The modified NIHSS collapses it to 2 responses 608.

9Best language

Tests for aphasia. Maximum 3.

Language adds points that right-sided signs do not, so left hemisphere strokes score higher for the same volume 609,1064. Aphasia explained 10.0% of EQ-5D utility variance at 90 days 999.

10Dysarthria

Tests articulation. Maximum 2.

Option 2 reads "near to unintelligible or worse" in one summary and "severe" in the other 146,199. The modified NIHSS removes it 608.

11Extinction and inattention

Formerly neglect. Maximum 2.

Reads "partial" and "complete" neglect in one summary, "mild" and "severe" in the other 146,199. A high LVO signal 313. Neglect explained 8.9% of EQ-5D utility variance at 90 days 999. At most 2 points, against 3 for language.

16 / 42Commonly used band: moderate to severe (16 to 20), a convention

In 33,102 Medicare beneficiaries with NIHSS 14 to 21, 30-day mortality was 31.6% 328. That is a population figure from one older US cohort, never a prediction for a person.

In plain wordsA higher number means a more severe stroke now. It is not a promise about the future 6,488. A low score does not always mean a small stroke, especially with problems of balance or on the right side of the brain 309,639.
Exam hookNIHSS: 15 elements, maximum 42. Left hemisphere bias and poor capture of posterior strokes are the two named weaknesses 146,891,1064. A score of 16 or more forecasts death or severe disability; 6 or less forecasts good recovery 6.

"Mild" has no single meaning

About a third of people with mild stroke were not functionally independent at 90 days, whatever the treatment or definition used, including NIHSS 0 to 5 934. PRISMS (minor nondisabling deficits, median NIHSS 2): favourable outcome 78.2% with alteplase vs 81.5% with aspirin, symptomatic haemorrhage 3.2% vs 0. Stopped very early, so not definitive 497.

How much it explains

NIHSS explained 11.3 to 25.0% of variation in outcome models, against 0.7 to 4.0% for a comorbidity index 857. Lacunar subtype was the only one predicting outcome independently of NIHSS (OR 3.1) 6. 24-hour NIHSS and 90-day mRS gave the same trial conclusion in 61 of 73 trials 847.

NIHSS and large vessel occlusion

At 10 or more, PPV for arterial occlusion was 97% in carotid and 96% in vertebrobasilar stroke, in selected people having arteriography 313. In 2152 people the best anterior cut-offs were 9 or more within 3 hours and 7 or more at 3 to 6 hours 420. Unselected data disagree: 11 or more misses over 25% of LVOs, and keeping misses at or below 10% needs a cut-off of 5 988.

What limb weakness means later

Limb paresis explained most of the variance in quality of life at 90 days (17.5 to 24.1% of EQ-5D utility), far more than neglect (8.9%), aphasia (10.0%) or hemianopia (10.8%) 999.

The left-hemisphere bias

Language adds points that right-sided signs do not, so left hemisphere strokes score higher for the same volume 609,1064. Factor analysis finds left and right brain factors 607,609. Choose a study to see the median volumes behind the same score.

Same band, nearly three times the volume. At NIHSS 16 to 20, median infarct volume 133 mL right vs 48 mL left (P<0.001). For the same NIHSS band below 20, right volume was about double the left 1064. Lesion areas follow the volumes only as a picture.

The posterior blind spot

Medians from 1569 people 459 and from people with occlusion 420. Cut-offs 891.

Cranial nerves are not tested in detail, and the scale underestimates brainstem or cerebellar severity 146. In posterior stroke the total is lower, and only the ataxia and visual subscores are higher 891.

In 1569 people, 71% of those with posterior stroke scored 4 or less, and 15% of them had a poor outcome at 3 months. The authors argue thrombolysis should not be withheld on the NIHSS alone in posterior stroke 459.

A score of zero does not exclude stroke. In one series 20 people (0.76% of stroke service admissions) scored 0 with an infarct on DWI, with headache, vertigo and nausea common, and truncal ataxia the commonest sign. Truncal ataxia is not on the scale, nor is isolated hand or finger palsy; 30% had a cardioembolic cause, which matters for prevention 639,929.

The e-NIHSS adds posterior elements while keeping the same total and item count, raising posterior scores by an average of 2 points in early testing Early evidence 758. The original remains standard for comparability 565,608.

Where the scale came from, and how far raters agree

1989

Designed as a 15-item examination for acute stroke trials. Interrater kappa averaged 0.69 in 24 people; test-retest kappa 0.66 to 0.77, with no difference among a neurologist, a resident, a neurology nurse and an emergency nurse. Correlation with infarct size r=0.68, with 3-month outcome r=0.79 134.

Training helps

Video training of 162 investigators gave moderate to excellent agreement on most items, though facial paresis and ataxia stayed below 0.40 606. Non-neurologists reached intraclass correlations of 0.93 and 0.95 360. A 2025 animated certification system: overall 0.979, while item kappas ran 0.25 to 0.90 42.

The modified NIHSS

Removes level of consciousness, face weakness, ataxia and dysarthria, and collapses sensory to 2 responses. Items with poor kappa fell from 8 to 3 608.

Scoring from the notes

Agreement with prospective scores r²=0.94 1052 and R²=0.991 in severe stroke 583, but interrater agreement fell to 0.48 at a hospital without neurologist assessment, with 62% of items missing 149.

The modified Rankin Scale

The NIHSS measures the deficit now. The mRS measures the result in daily life: how much help a person needs. It descends from Rankin's 1957 work 828. The 1988 interobserver study used six grades, 0 to 5 1004; modern usage is a 7-level scale that adds 6 for death 899,977. Definitions below are verbatim as listed in a 2026 clinical study 604, because the 1988 abstract does not state them.

A structured interview

Agreement across multiple raters rose from 43% to 81%, kappa 0.25 to 0.74 1054. In an earlier study, 57% to 78%, weighted kappa 0.78 to 0.93 1053.

Reliability disagrees

Original: the same grade in 65 of 100 people, weighted kappa 0.91 1004. A systematic review found weighted kappa from 0.25 to 0.95, overall kappa 0.46 823. Another synthesis reports moderate inter-rater reliability, improved by structured interviews 83.

Quick versions

A simplified questionnaire took 1.67 minutes on average, with kappa 0.72 (95% CI 0.58 to 0.86) 143.

Why trials use it

mRS at 3 months or later is the preferred primary outcome for acute trials 565. Comorbidities and socioeconomic factors affect how it should be read 83. A roundtable recommends uniform names, because trials had used "a bewildering variety" of labels 899.

In plain wordsThe mRS is a 0 to 6 scale of how much help a person needs in daily life 604.
Exam hookmRS 0 to 6, where 6 is death. Grade 3 walks unaided with some help; grade 4 cannot walk unaided 604.
