Part 3 · Acute treatment

Thrombectomy

Endovascular thrombectomy pulls the clot out through a thin tube. Watch a stent retriever do it, explore the trials by window and by who was treated, and see what delay costs. This page reports what trials and guidelines say. It is not medical advice.

Schematic, not to scale. Lesion size and reperfused share are illustrative, not measured.

Step 1 · the blockage

A large artery is blocked

A clot sits in the first part of the middle cerebral artery (M1). Everything beyond it goes pale. Tissue with no flow dies (the core, red), and a wider rim is at risk (the penumbra, amber).

In plain wordsThrombectomy is for clots in larger arteries 77,364,896. A drug alone often does not open them: in EXTEND-IA TNK, 22% with tenecteplase and 10% with alteplase had reperfused by the first angiogram 154.

One minute saved

4.2 days

Modelling: 4.2 days of extra healthy life per minute (95% prediction interval 2.3 to 5.4). 20 minutes equalled about 3 months of disability-free life 669.

In plain wordsThrombectomy is a procedure where a doctor passes a thin tube up an artery, often from the groin, and pulls out the clot. It is for clots in larger arteries 77,364,896. For every 3 or so people who have thrombectomy, about one is left with less disability 364.

In plain wordsEvery minute counts. Saving one minute before thrombectomy is estimated to give about 4 days of extra healthy life on average 669.

Reperfusion of more than half of the territory. The consensus settled a discrepancy here: 2b had meant more than half (modified) or more than two thirds (original), and it chose more than half 1092.

Tenecteplase first: BRIDGE-TNK

China, 550 people. Tenecteplase then thrombectomy gave mRS 0 to 2 of 52.9% versus 44.1% with thrombectomy alone (RR 1.20, 1.01 to 1.43; P=0.04). Symptomatic haemorrhage 8.5% versus 6.7%; mortality 22.3% versus 19.9% 822.

ESO 2023 expert consensus

For eligible people with large-vessel occlusion within 4.5 hours, tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg is preferable to skipping thrombolysis, even after direct admission to a thrombectomy-capable centre 14.

Basilar, late: ATTENTION LATE

China, 330 people, NIHSS 10 or more, 4.5 to 24 hours: tenecteplase before thrombectomy gave no gain in functional independence (30% versus 30%; adjusted rate ratio 0.92, 0.67 to 1.25) 572.

Open question

When delay or transfer differ

Individual participant data could not establish noninferiority of skipping the lytic, and BRIDGE-TNK favours tenecteplase first. Whether this holds when delay or transfer differ is open 625,822.

Anaesthesia: the data point opposite ways

Observational: in HERMES (797 procedures, non-randomised choice) outcomes were better without general anaesthesia (covariate-adjusted cOR 1.53, 1.14 to 2.04) 155.

Randomised: 7 trials, 980 participants: general anaesthesia improved recanalisation (84.6% versus 75.6%) and functional recovery (44.6% versus 36.2%; OR 1.43, 1.04 to 1.98), with no difference in haemorrhage or mortality 160. GOLIATH also shifted towards a lower mRS with it (OR 1.91, 1.03 to 3.56) 927.

Confounding by indication is the obvious explanation, since the HERMES choice was not randomised 155,160.

Technique: aspiration or stent retriever

ASTER (France, 381 people): successful revascularisation 85.4% with first-line contact aspiration versus 83.1% with a stent retriever (OR 1.20, 0.68 to 2.10; P=.53) 548.

COMPASS (270 people, ASPECTS over 6, within 6 hours): mRS 0 to 2, 52% versus 50%, non-inferior. Mortality 22% each 992.

Complications

A narrative review puts complications with sequelae at about 15% across RCTs: access-site problems, vasospasm, arterial perforation and dissection, device detachment, symptomatic and subarachnoid haemorrhage, new-territory embolisation, anaesthetic and contrast effects, pseudoaneurysm 77.

Procedural complications were 14% in ATTENTION and 11% in BAOCHE 477,963. TESLA reported subarachnoid haemorrhage in 16.2% versus 6.2% 1088.

Who can reach it

Median 2.79% rounded to whole dots; IQR 0.70 to 11.74. MT-GLASS survey, 887 responses, 67 countries, 2020 to 2021 56.

Access was under 1.0% in 18 countries (27%) and 0 in 7 (10%), with a 460-fold gap between the highest and lowest nonzero regions. Low-income countries were 88% lower than high-income ones 56.

Operators reached 16.5% and centres 20.8% of optimal availability. Access rose with a prehospital bypass protocol (OR 4.00, 1.70 to 9.42) 56. Each 5% rise in rural population share had 13% lower odds of access after adjustment (OR 0.87, 0.78 to 0.96) 57.

In plain wordsMany people in the world cannot reach thrombectomy. In some countries it is not available at all 56.
