An animated, cited guide for students, people who have had a stroke, and their families and carers. Educational only, not medical advice. Your place is saved on this device.

Not medical adviceThe Stroke Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. If you think someone is having a stroke, call your local emergency number. It does not replace a doctor, nurse, physiotherapist, occupational therapist, speech and language therapist or the rest of a stroke team. Scores such as the NIHSS, ICH score, ABCD2, CHA2DS2-VASc and ASPECTS are teaching aids, never for an individual's diagnosis, prognosis or treatment. Doses, thresholds and guideline statements are quoted as published, never as instructions. Outcomes are population statistics; they are not predictions for any person.

ABC/2A bedside method for estimating intracerebral haematoma volume from CT 514.
ABCD2A 7-point TIA score: Age, Blood pressure, Clinical features, Duration, Diabetes. 2-day stroke risk 8.1% at 6-7 466. NICE says do not use it to decide urgency 734.
ABCD3-IABCD2 with dual TIA, carotid stenosis of 50% or more, and a DWI lesion added, each +2 672.
AbuliaLoss of will or initiative. With leg-predominant weakness it points to the anterior cerebral artery 939.
ACA (anterior cerebral artery)An anterior circulation artery. Its stroke gives leg-predominant weakness and abulia 939.
ADL (activities of daily living)Everyday self-care tasks. 80% reached best ADL function by 6 weeks and 95% by 12.5 weeks in Copenhagen 472.
AF (atrial fibrillation)An irregular heart rhythm that can throw clots to the brain. In Framingham it raised stroke risk nearly fivefold 1059.
Alteplase (t-PA)A clot-dissolving drug for stroke. Labelled 0.9 mg/kg, maximum 90 mg, 10% as a bolus 293.
AndexanetA factor Xa inhibitor reversal agent. ANNEXA-I: haemostatic efficacy 67.0% against 53.1%, thrombotic events 10.3% against 5.6% 198.
Angio-oedemaSwelling of the lips and tongue, a complication of lysis. Prior ACE inhibitor use is the predictor (OR 2.3) 452.
AphasiaA language disorder. Median frequency 30% in mixed acute stroke 325.
ApoplexyThe old word for stroke, meaning struck down with violence. It covered non-vascular events too 280.
ASPECTSA 10-region CT score of the MCA territory. 10 is normal, with 1 point off per affected region 84,792.
AspirationFood or fluid entering the airway. Confirmed aspiration raises pneumonia risk (RR 11.56) 638.
AsundexianAn oral factor XIa inhibitor. It failed against apixaban in AF, and reduced ischaemic stroke against placebo after non-cardioembolic stroke; the US FDA approved it for that use in November 2026 297,796,918.
Atrial septal aneurysmA floppy bulge of the wall between the atria. With a PFO it counts as a high-risk feature in PASCAL 173,493.
AVERTA trial of very early, higher-dose mobilisation. Favourable outcome 46% against 50% 98.
Basilar arteryThe main artery of the posterior circulation. Occlusion can bring decreased consciousness, quadriparesis and coma 595.
BE-FASTBalance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time. Cut missed ischaemic strokes from 14.1% to 4.4% in a retrospective study 50.
Boston criteriaCriteria for cerebral amyloid angiopathy. Version 2.0 adds white matter features, and needs age 50 or over for probable CAA 176.
Botulinum toxinAn injected treatment for spasticity. It reduces resistance to passive movement and aids self-care, but not arm capacity 44.
BridgingGiving a clot-dissolving drug before thrombectomy, rather than going direct. Tested in DIRECT-MT, DEVT and SKIP 951,1080,1098.
CAA (cerebral amyloid angiopathy)Amyloid in arterial walls with loss of smooth muscle, mainly in lobar locations, with microbleeds or superficial siderosis 622.
CardioembolismA stroke from a clot formed in the heart. One TOAST subtype; pooled 22% of ischaemic strokes 4,764.
Carotid endarterectomySurgery to clean out a narrowed carotid artery. Benefit depends on stenosis and on timing 860,861.
Carotid stentingOpening a carotid narrowing with a stent. CREST: more periprocedural stroke than surgery, less MI 136.
CASTA trial of 21,106 people given aspirin 160 mg/day or placebo within 48 hours: deaths 3.3% against 3.9% 167.
Central post-stroke painPain with sensory abnormalities in body parts matching the injured area 511. Pooled prevalence 11% 573.
CHA2DS2-VAScAn AF stroke-risk score from 0 to 9. Age 75 or over and previous thromboembolism score 2 757.
ChameleonA stroke that looks like something else. Young people, women and those with nonspecific symptoms are at higher risk of misdiagnosis 76,1017.
Circle of WillisThe anastomosis joining the anterior and posterior circulations 856. Complete in only 11.9% in one population 431.
ClazosentanAn endothelin receptor antagonist tried in SAH. It reduced vasospasm without better function 25.
Clinical-core mismatchA severe deficit with a small core on imaging. DAWN used it for selection at 6 to 24 hours 741.
ClippingSurgery that closes an aneurysm with a clip at its neck. Compared with coiling in ISAT 686.
CoilingTreating a ruptured aneurysm from inside with coils. ISAT: about 7% less death or dependence at 1 year than clipping 686.
CollateralsBack-up vessels that keep tissue alive during occlusion 576,630. Growth was 52.3 cm3 with poor and 0.9 cm3 with good collaterals in one series 995.
Constraint-induced movement therapyRestraining the stronger arm to drive practice of the weaker one. EXCITE was positive 1060.
CoreTissue already irreversibly damaged. Core below 70 mL was a DEFUSE 3 criterion 16.
CPSS (Cincinnati Prehospital Stroke Scale)A prehospital scale with three items: arm, speech and face 515.
Craniectomy, decompressiveRemoving part of the skull to relieve pressure in swollen infarction. Number needed to treat 2 for survival, age 60 or under within 48 hours 996.
CRESTA trial of carotid stenting against surgery in 2502 people 136.
Cryptogenic strokeA stroke with no cause found, about 25% of strokes, the group from which ESUS was proposed 406.
CT angiography (CTA)CT of the vessels, showing clot and collaterals 958.
CT perfusionCT mapping of blood flow to separate core from penumbra. No single threshold avoids false positives 152.
DALYDisability-adjusted life year, a measure of healthy life lost. 160.5 million for stroke in 2021 306.
DAPT (dual antiplatelet therapy)Two antiplatelet drugs together. The benefit after minor stroke sits in the first 21 days 772.
DAWNA late-window thrombectomy trial, 6 to 24 hours: functional independence 49% against 13% 741.
DCI (delayed cerebral ischaemia)Deterioration after aneurysmal SAH, mostly days 4 to 14, peak days 6 to 10 433.
DEFUSE 3A late-window thrombectomy trial at 6 to 16 hours, with perfusion selection 16.
DissectionA tear in an artery wall. 15% of stroke at ages 15-49 in one cohort 819; head or neck pain is common 561.
DOACDirect oral anticoagulant. Against warfarin, intracranial haemorrhage RR 0.48 868.
Door-to-needle timeMinutes from arrival to thrombolysis. Helsinki reached a median of 20 minutes 666.
DWI-FLAIR mismatchA lesion on diffusion MRI that does not yet show on FLAIR, suggesting onset within about 4.5 hours 976.
DysarthriaSlurred speech from weak speech muscles. Scored on the NIHSS (item 10) 146.
DysphagiaSwallowing difficulty. Present in more than 50% of people with acute stroke, says one guideline 264.
Early brain injuryThe name for the first 24 to 48 hours after SAH 244.
Early supported dischargeGoing home sooner with a specialist team. Hospital stay about 6 days shorter 543.
ECASS IIIThe trial that extended alteplase to 3 to 4.5 hours: favourable outcome 52.4% against 45.2% 381.
EmbolismA clot carried from elsewhere. Virchow created the term 70,280.
EmotionalismA problem with emotional control after stroke. 12% in one cohort 541.
ENRICHA trial of minimally invasive ICH surgery. The benefit appeared in lobar bleeds 814.
ESUSEmbolic stroke of undetermined source: a non-lacunar infarct with no proximal stenosis or cardioembolic source 406.
EVD (external ventricular drain)A tube draining CSF, used in acute hydrocephalus after SAH 433.
ExcitotoxicityInjury from excess glutamate overstimulating receptors 243.
EXPRESSThe Oxford study of urgent TIA care: 90-day stroke 10.3% before and 2.1% after 863.
EXTENDA trial of lysis at 4.5 to 9 hours or on waking, selected by perfusion imaging 611.
Factor XI inhibitorsAnticoagulants tested with the hypothesis of less bleeding for similar effect 462.
FASTFace, Arm, Speech, Time. Missed 30.6% of haemorrhages in one series 509.
FatigueTiredness not relieved by rest 844. Pooled prevalence 48% 20.
FeSSFever, Sugar, Swallow protocols, tested in QASC 676.
FFP (fresh frozen plasma)Plasma given to correct clotting. Four-factor PCC beat it in INCH 942.
Find-AFA trial of repeated 10-day Holter monitoring after stroke: AF found in 14% against 5% at 6 months 1013.
Fisher scale, modifiedA grading of SAH by clot thickness and intraventricular blood, 0 to 4 192,337.
FluoxetineAn antidepressant tried for recovery. No functional benefit, less depression, more fractures 238,566.
Fugl-Meyer (FMA-UE)An arm impairment scale from 0 to 66 1057.
GCS (Glasgow Coma Scale)A bedside scale of conscious level. Its authors advise describing people by its three components 969.
Haematoma expansionGrowth of a bleed after onset. 26% had substantial growth in the first hour in one series 135.
Haemorrhagic transformationBleeding into an infarct. Only PH2 clearly changes the clinical course 312.
HeadPoSTA trial of lying flat against head up 30 degrees: no difference in outcome 40.
Helsinki modelDo as much as possible before arrival. Median door-to-needle 20 minutes 666.
HemianopiaLoss of half the visual field, scored on the NIHSS (item 3) 146.
Hemiplegic shoulder painShoulder pain on the weak side, about 29% in one population study, with onset peaking at 4 months 10.
HERMESA pooled analysis of five thrombectomy trials: common OR 2.49, number needed to treat 2.6 364.
Horner syndromePart of Wallenberg syndrome on the same side as the lesion 595, and a sign of dissection 222.
Hunt and HessA clinical grading of SAH from 1 (mild headache) to 5 (coma) 192,450.
HydrocephalusEnlarged ventricles from blocked CSF flow. About 25% after aneurysmal SAH 1009.
ICH (intracerebral haemorrhage)Bleeding into the brain. 28.8% of incident strokes worldwide 306.
ICH scoreA 0 to 6 score from GCS, age, location, volume and intraventricular blood 423.
IdarucizumabReverses dabigatran: median maximum reversal 100% in RE-VERSE AD 802.
InfarctDead tissue after blocked blood supply. Cohnheim introduced the word in 1872 280.
INTERACT2A blood pressure trial in ICH: under 140 systolic within 1 hour against under 180 38.
INTERSTROKEA case-control study in 32 countries: ten factors, about 90% of population attributable risk 752.
ISATThe coiling against clipping trial in ruptured aneurysm 685,686.
Ischaemic cascadeThe chain of injury after blood flow falls: excitotoxicity, peri-infarct depolarisations, inflammation and apoptosis 243.
IVH (intraventricular haemorrhage)Blood in the ventricles, an ICH score item 423.
Lacunar strokeA small deep infarct, about one quarter of ischaemic strokes, with five classic syndromes 47.
LacuneA small deep cavity. Dechambre introduced the word in 1838 280; Fisher linked it to penetrating artery disease 163,317.
LAMSLos Angeles Motor Scale: face, arm and grip, 0 to 5, with 4 or more the LVO threshold 592,709.
Large coreExtensive early infarct. Trials used ASPECTS 3 to 5, and SELECT2 also core 50 mL or more 888,1089.
Left atrial appendage closureA device or surgery to seal the appendage where clots form in AF. LAAOS III: 4.8% against 7.0% 1047.
LipohyalinosisDisease of small penetrating arteries, tied to lacunes 319 and most often found in non-lobar ICH 37.
Locked-in syndromeParalysis with preserved consciousness from pontine injury, sparing vertical eye movement and blinking 214.
LVO (large vessel occlusion)A blocked main brain artery. No clinical scale identifies it with both high sensitivity and high specificity 931.
MCA (middle cerebral artery)The artery most commonly involved in ischaemic stroke, with segments M1 to M4 94.
MimicA condition that looks like a stroke but is not. About 30% of those seen by a stroke team 671.
Mobile stroke unitAn ambulance with a CT scanner. BEST-MSU: onset-to-t-PA 72 against 108 minutes 374.
mRS (modified Rankin Scale)A disability scale from 0 (no symptoms) to 6 (death) 604.
mTICIThe reperfusion grade after thrombectomy. 2b means more than half of the territory 1092.
NeglectInattention to one side. About 29% overall, more after right brain damage 286.
NerinetideA neuroprotectant tested in ESCAPE-NA1. A subgroup signal did not replicate in ESCAPE-NEXT 428,429.
NeuroplasticityThe nervous system's ability to reorganise structure, function and connections 207.
NIHSSThe NIH Stroke Scale: 15 elements, maximum 42 146.
NimodipineA calcium channel blocker for SAH. BRANT: 60 mg orally every 4 hours for 21 days 797.
NINDS trialThe 1995 t-PA trial within 3 hours 738.
NNT (number needed to treat)How many people must be treated for one extra to benefit. 2.6 in HERMES 364.
No-reflowFailure of microvessels to refill after the main artery opens 72,212.
OCEANIC-STROKEA phase 3 asundexian trial after stroke or TIA: ischaemic stroke 6.2% against 8.4% 918.
Ottawa SAH RuleSix items to decide who needs tests for SAH: sensitivity 100%, specificity about 15% 787.
Oxfordshire classificationTACI, PACI, POCI and LACI: 17%, 34%, 24% and 25% 78.
PAR (population attributable risk)The share of cases linked to a factor. Hypertension 47.9% in INTERSTROKE 752.
PASCALA PFO classification combining RoPE with high-risk PFO features 173,493.
PCA (posterior cerebral artery)Its stroke can give hemianopia, memory loss and alexia without agraphia 939.
PCC (prothrombin complex concentrate)A clotting factor concentrate. Four-factor PCC beat FFP for INR correction 942.
PEGA feeding tube through the stomach wall. Not supported early by FOOD 232.
PenumbraTissue in the range between two flow thresholds, which can recover if flow returns 419.
PFO (patent foramen ovale)A small opening between the atria, in about 1 in 4 adults 673.
PH2Parenchymal haematoma type 2, the haemorrhagic transformation grade that clearly changes the course 312.
Posterior circulationThe vertebral and basilar system. Stroke here is misdiagnosed about twice as often as anterior stroke 48.
Proportional recoveryThe idea that people regain about 0.7 of initial impairment; statistical coupling may inflate it 438,811.
QASCA cluster trial of FeSS protocols in 19 acute stroke units 676.
RACEAn LVO scale of face, arm, leg, gaze and aphasia or agnosia with a threshold of 5 or more 785.
Reperfusion injuryDamage after flow returns. No study has quantified its share in people with stroke 72,748.
RoPE scoreA 0 to 10 score of how likely a PFO is to be the cause. Higher means younger with fewer risk factors 492,746.
ROSIERA recognition scale from -2 to +5, positive above 0 742.
SAH (subarachnoid haemorrhage)Bleeding around the brain, usually from an aneurysm. 5.8% of incident strokes 306,1009.
SAMMPRISIntracranial stenting against aggressive medical therapy: 14.7% against 5.8% at 30 days 184.
SeLECTA score for late seizures: Severity, Large-artery, Early seizures, Cortical involvement, Territory of MCA 343.
Sentinel headacheA warning headache before SAH, reported in 10 to 43% across studies 805.
SMASH-UA classification of ICH causes: structural lesions, anticoagulation, amyloid, systemic disease, hypertensive angiopathy, undetermined 665.
SO2SA trial of routine oxygen in non-hypoxic people: no benefit 853.
SPARCLHigh-dose atorvastatin after stroke: fewer strokes overall, more haemorrhagic 29.
SpasticityStiff, resistant muscles. Prevalence 17% to 42.6% beyond 3 months 1058.
Spot signContrast leak in a haematoma on CT angiography, predicting expansion: specificity 85% 228.
Stent retrieverA mesh device that grips a clot and pulls it out. 81.5% of the MR CLEAN intervention group 97.
Stroke unitOrganised inpatient care. 6 more per 100 live independently 545.
TenecteplaseA clot-dissolving drug given as one bolus: 0.25 mg/kg, maximum 25 mg 298,663.
ThrombectomyPulling the clot out through a catheter 364.
ThrombolysisDissolving a clot with a drug, given within time and imaging limits 278.
Thunderclap headacheA sudden severe headache from onset, the commonest presentation of SAH 192.
TIA (transient ischaemic attack)A transient neurological episode from focal ischaemia, without acute infarction 265.
TmaxA perfusion delay measure. Above 6 s marks hypoperfused tissue in common use 16,760.
TOASTA five-subtype classification of the cause of ischaemic stroke 4.
Vagus nerve stimulationStimulation paired with arm rehabilitation. Vivistim is FDA-approved, and the between-group difference was 2.6 FMA-UE points 218,299.
VasospasmNarrowing of arteries after SAH, with peak risk on post-bleed days 6 to 10 433.
Visual field lossPart of the visual field gone. In a referred sample at follow-up: 7.5% full recovery, 39% improvement, 52% no recovery 866.
Wallenberg syndromeLateral medullary stroke, from PICA or vertebral artery disease 595.
WFNS gradeA SAH grading from 1 (GCS 15) to 5 (GCS 3 to 6) 192,968.

Stroke Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on stroke, made for medical, nursing and therapy courses and for people who have had a stroke, their families and carers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose. If you think someone is having a stroke, call your local emergency number. Nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine, a therapy or a care plan without your own doctor or stroke team. The NIHSS, ICH score, ABCD2, CHA2DS2-VASc, ASPECTS and other scales are for learning, never for an individual's diagnosis, prognosis or treatment. Doses, thresholds and guideline statements are quoted as published by the AHA/ASA, ESO, NICE and drug labels, never as instructions, and outcome figures are population statistics, not predictions for any person.
