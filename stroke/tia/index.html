Part 1 · Understanding stroke

TIA: the warning stroke

A transient ischaemic attack (TIA, often called a mini stroke; US: ischemic) passes, but the risk does not pass with it. See how the definition moved from time to tissue, how the ABCD2 score adds up and where it fails, how the risk clusters in the first days, and what fast clinics changed.

This page reports what studies and guidelines say. It is not advice for any one person, and the figures describe groups of people. If you think someone is having a stroke, call your local emergency number.

A clot that clears: time or tissue?

The older definition drew the line at 24 hours. The 2009 AHA/ASA definition, following a 2002 proposal, draws it at tissue: a transient episode of focal brain, spinal cord or retinal ischaemia "without acute infarction" 15,265. Compare three cases.

How the ABCD2 score adds up

ABCD2 sums five items to a score from 0 to 7 466. Choose answers and watch the score and the published band change.

Score 0. Low band, 0-3. In Johnston 2007, 34% of patients fell here and 1.0% had a stroke within 2 days 466. Population figures from the published score, never a prediction for one person.

Misses people who need care

A score of 4 or more had sensitivity 86.7% and specificity 35.4% across 29 studies and 13,766 people. 20% of those under 4 had carotid stenosis over 50% or AF 1035.

Mimics score high too

35 to 41% of mimics and 66% of true TIAs scored 4 or more. 45% of those assessed were mimics 1035.

Moves risk only a little

Sensitivity 0.89, specificity 0.34, AUC 0.70. At a 5% baseline, a score over 3 adds 2.0% and a score of 3 or under lowers it by 2.9% 882.

What NICE NG128 says

  • Aspirin 300 mg daily, unless contraindicated, started immediately after a suspected TIA (1.1.4, 2019) 734.
  • Immediate referral, seen within 24 hours of onset (1.1.5, 2019) 734.
  • Secondary prevention as soon as possible after diagnosis is confirmed (1.1.7) 734.
  • No CT unless an alternative diagnosis is suspected; consider MRI after specialist assessment, on the same day (1.2.1, 1.2.2, 2019) 734.

Quoted as guidance for clinicians, not instructions for any one person.

Why the first days of treatment matter

Memory loss pointed to a mimic, as did headache (OR 3.71); one-sided paresis pointed away (OR 0.35). 303 people; 18.2% were mimics 36.

Agreement is hard even for experts: GPs were confirmed in 19% and emergency departments in 13% in one comparison 308, and stroke neurologists agree poorly 169. Mimics also reach acute services: 24.2% of 2,305 hyperacute stroke unit admissions 217, 31% in a brain attack series 391. An overview is available in 602. NICE lists excluding hypoglycaemia and using ROSIER among community and emergency steps 734.

Two open questions. The ESO keeps a pragmatic clinical definition of under 24 hours 330, while others say the 24-hour limit should be abandoned 700. Early risk also spreads widely: 5.2% pooled at 7 days 354, 2.1% in a modern registry of urgently treated people 31, 8.0% in an older population cohort 202; setting and treatment explain much of it.
