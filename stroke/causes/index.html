Part 1 · Understanding stroke

Causes of ischaemic stroke

Ischaemic (US: ischemic) stroke means an artery in the brain is blocked. The question that shapes prevention is where the blockage came from. Pick a source and follow it, then see how the shares change between populations, and why some causes stay unknown.

This page reports what studies and classifications say. It is not advice for any one person, and the figures describe groups of people. If you think someone is having a stroke, call your local emergency number.

Heart · cardioembolism

The atria quiver

In Framingham, AF raised stroke risk nearly fivefold, and the risk attributable to it rose from 1.5% at age 50-59 to 23.5% at 80-89 1057. A later review gives a 3 to 5 fold rise and 33 million people affected 483.

In plain wordsMany strokes come from clots made in the heart 763.

TOAST and the other ways of sorting causes

TOAST defines five subtypes, each possible or probable. In its first test, two neurologists disagreed in one of 20 patients, with a specific diagnosis in 11 and undetermined in 9 4. The original paper's full text could not be read for this guide, so the cutoffs below come from a later review that restates them 501. Other criteria are not listed here.

A real disagreement. Large-artery disease leads in pooled Asian populations at 33% (31 to 36) 763, and one review calls intracranial large-artery disease the most common subtype worldwide 361. A pooled Chinese vs white comparison found no clear evidence of different ischaemic subtypes, though intracerebral haemorrhage was 33% vs 12% 984. Annual trends differ too: cardioembolic in white populations +2.4%, large artery in Asian populations +5.7%, small artery in white populations -4.7% 763.

Large arteries

  • Autopsy prevalence of intracranial atherosclerosis was 43%, 65% and 80% with increasing age 80.
  • In the Rotterdam Study, calcification contributed to 75% of strokes at the intracranial carotid, 45% at the aortic arch and 25% at the extracranial carotid 119.
  • SAMMPRIS, stenting vs aggressive medical management: stroke or death 14.7% vs 5.8% at 30 days and 20.0% vs 12.2% at 1 year 184. See

Small vessels

  • Small vessel disease involves endothelial dysfunction and barrier leak 772,1034.
  • Lacunar infarcts are put at about a quarter 47 or "nearly one-third" 833 of ischaemic strokes.
  • At 5 years, 25% had died, 20% had recurrence and 30% were impaired 360.

ESUS and cryptogenic stroke

ESUS stands for embolic stroke of undetermined source. It was proposed in 2014 for a non-lacunar infarct with no proximal stenosis and no cardioembolic source, a defined part of the cryptogenic group 405. ESUS accounts for 9 to 25% of ischaemic strokes, on average 17%, with mean age 65, NIHSS 5 and recurrence of 4.5% per year 406. ESUS is not a synonym for cryptogenic stroke 748.

Rates per year as reported. NAVIGATE ESUS bleeding: 1.8% vs 0.7%, HR 2.72 407. RE-SPECT ESUS HR 0.85 (0.69 to 1.03) 242. ARCADIA HR 1.00 484.

Exam hookESUS is a non-lacunar infarct with no proximal stenosis or cardioembolic source 405. The three anticoagulant ESUS trials were neutral 242,407,484.

How big is "unknown"? It depends on definition and workup: about 25% 405, 30 to 40% 1073, 20 to 40% 884. Active cancer is present in 5 to 10% of ESUS 707.

Stroke in younger people

Young stroke incidence is rising 273. In Helsinki (1,008 people aged 15 to 49), incidence was 10.8 per 100,000. Traditional factors were still common: dyslipidaemia 60%, smoking 44%, hypertension 39%. Silent infarcts appeared in 13% 818. Choose a cause to explore.

Dissection

A tear in a neck artery wall. It presents with headache, neck pain, Horner syndrome or cranial nerve palsy, and most people then have a stroke or TIA 222.

  • Population incidence 2.6 per 100,000: carotid 1.72, vertebral 0.97. Good outcome in 92% 560. CADISP compared carotid and vertebral disease in 982 people 223.
  • Age 60 or over made up 7.4%, with less pain 981.
  • CADISS: stroke recurrence 2% (4 of 250), 3 of 126 on antiplatelet vs 1 of 124 on anticoagulant; imaging failed to confirm dissection in 52 632.
  • TREAT-CAD: composite 23% with aspirin vs 15%, so aspirin was not shown non-inferior; ischaemic strokes 7 vs 0 282. Which antithrombotic is best remains a controversy.
Exam hookDissection accounts for 15% of stroke at ages 15-49 in one large cohort 818.
