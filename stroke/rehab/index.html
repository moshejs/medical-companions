Part 5 · Recovery & care

Rehabilitation & recovery

How function comes back after a stroke, how the brain builds new routes, and what the trials say helps. Scrub the recovery curve, try the proportional recovery rule, then open the part of recovery that matters to you.

In plain wordsMost recovery happens in the first weeks and months, and it is fastest early on. Bigger strokes take longer 471. Getting better can continue after that with the right practice, even long after the stroke. Not everyone gains the same amount 810,1028. The numbers on this page describe groups of people. They are not a forecast for you.

The current guideline is from 2026. The AHA/ASA guideline by Richards and colleagues replaces the 2016 guideline. Its search ran from June to October 2025. Updates include medical comorbidities, fracture risk, fall prevention, technology-enabled rehabilitation, caregiver support, participation, and safe return to work and driving 843,1053. It says rehabilitation should begin in the acute phase, and that it combines restoring function with learning new ways to do things. It is also frank: "uncertainty remains regarding the best treatment approaches, their timing, and dosing" 843.

Open question

When does recovery stop?

The Copenhagen authors wrote that recovery "should not be expected after the first 5 months" 471. Later work reports more: language recovery continues, though less, beyond 6 months 836, and intensive therapy long after stroke brings gains in the arm and in speech 128,218,1028.

One reading is that the apparent plateau partly reflects the therapy dose delivered as well as biology. Hypothesis The question is unresolved.

Worth knowing

Function can also slip

In the Swedish Stroke Register (64,746 people; 35,064 followed-up survivors), dependency in daily activities rose from 16.2% at 3 months to 28.3% at 12 months 992. So the 2026 guideline names periodic reassessment and re-engagement in therapy as important to prevent functional decline 843.

NICE says care and rehabilitation should continue after hospital "for as long as it continues to help them achieve their treatment goals" 734.

The proportional recovery rule, and the argument about it

In 2008, researchers noticed that many people regained about 70% of the arm movement they had lost. Is that biology, or partly arithmetic? Move the start score, then read each side of the argument.

The rule

About 70% of what was lost

In 41 people with ischaemic stroke, clinical variables explained 47% of variance in arm recovery. With a set of severe, poorly recovering outliers removed, this rose to 89%, and recovery approximated 0.70 x initial impairment 810. A replication gave predicted change = 0.7 x (66 - initial score) + 0.4. In 211 people, about 70% (n=146) showed recovery of about 78%, and 65 did worse than predicted 1055.

Exam hookProportional recovery: about 0.7 of initial impairment, except a subgroup with severe impairment 810,1055.

Predicting the arm in practice: PREP2

PREP2 (207 people recruited within 3 days) combines arm impairment, age, motor evoked potentials on TMS, and MRI lesion load or NIHSS, to predict arm function at 3 months. It was correct for 75%, and TMS was needed for one third 943.

Where the argument stands

Three positions remain: a real mechanism in non-severe weakness 810,1055; a figure inflated by mathematical coupling 437; valid for populations but not for one person 522. Hawe and colleagues published a further critique, "Taking Proportional Out of Stroke Recovery" 411; we could not read its content, so it is not summarised here.

Timing: is there a window? CPASS

Animal work suggests a time-limited window of plasticity opens after stroke, with the greatest gains during it 695. The Critical Period After Stroke Study tested that in people. Play it through.

Bars are published values, whiskers the published ±. The shaded hump is schematic: it draws the idea of a window, not data.

In people

CPASS: 20 extra hours

20 extra hours of self-selected task-specific therapy. By 1 year, the Action Research Arm Test difference versus controls was +5.25 ± 2.59 (P=0.043) when begun at 30 days or less, +6.87 ± 2.63 (P=0.009) at 2 to 3 months, and +2.41 ± 2.25 (P=0.29) at 6 months or later 256.

Exam hookCPASS: extra therapy worked best at 2 to 3 months 256.

Dose: how much, and how early?

AVERT changed practice

2104 people at 56 stroke units in five countries. Very early, higher dose mobilisation lowered favourable outcome: adjusted OR 0.73 (0.59 to 0.90; p=0.004). Deaths 8% versus 7%, OR 1.34 (0.93 to 1.93) 98.

Within the trial, each extra out-of-bed session per day had OR 1.13 (1.09 to 1.18) for a favourable outcome, while more minutes per day had OR 0.94 (0.91 to 0.97). This is observational, Class III 99.

In plain wordsIn hospital, short and frequent times out of bed seem better than long sessions very early on 99.

2026 guideline: a graduated mobilisation protocol commencing 24 to 48 hours after stroke onset (COR 1, LOE A). High-dose mobilisation within the first 24 hours "should not be performed" (COR 3: Harm, LOE A). After 24 hours, moderate to high-intensity exercise and task practice should be performed 843. AVERT-DOSE, a phase III adaptive trial of regimens begun within 48 hours, is planned for 1300 and 1400 participants in mild and moderate stroke; only its protocol was found 100.

More helps

The case for more time

  • Meta-analysis of more versus less scheduled therapy: g=0.35 (0.26 to 0.45) 595.
  • At least 16 hours more in the first 6 months was needed for a significant ADL difference (20 studies, 2686 people) 526.
  • Extra rehabilitation of the same content: SMD 0.39 (0.07 to 0.71); doubling or more SMD 0.59 (0.23 to 0.94) 902.
  • Rehabilitation versus none: independence in ADL SMD 0.78 (0.58 to 0.97; 27 studies, 3423 participants). Evidence on dose "does not support robust conclusions" 802.
  • NICE: "for at least 3 hours a day, on at least 5 days of the week", needs-based. If someone is unable or does not wish to, needed therapy for a minimum of 5 days per week 734.
Not so simple

The case for better, not just more

  • 85 people at least 6 months after stroke got 3200, 6400, 9600 or individualised maximum repetitions over 8 weeks. Overall effects were small, with no evidence of a dose-response effect 538.
  • VECTORS (52 people): high-intensity constraint therapy early on gave less improvement at day 90 than dose-matched therapy 255.
  • ICARE was neutral at equal dose 1054.
  • 467 physical therapy RCTs: intensive, highly repetitive, task-specific training has the strongest support in all phases, and effects are mostly limited to what was trained 1003.
  • Several large motor trials were neutral, which may reflect no added benefit or the difficulty of running such trials Established 944.

Fluoxetine: a promising signal that large trials did not confirm

This is one of the clearest lessons in stroke research. A small trial raised real hope. Three large trials then asked the same question, and gave a different answer.

Published values. Odds ratio axis is logarithmic. Bars and rows are positioned from the cited figures.

The lesson

Why three trials agree

Established FOCUS, AFFINITY and EFFECTS were designed collaboratively with the same basic protocol, so their agreement is strong evidence 238,392,602,651. Cochrane 2021 (76 studies, 13,029 participants): in low risk of bias studies, no difference in disability (SMD -0.0, -0.05 to 0.05) or independence (RR 0.98, 0.93 to 1.03), less depression (RR 0.75, 0.65 to 0.86), more fractures (RR 2.35, 1.62 to 3.41) and seizures (RR 1.40, 1.00 to 1.98). "When all studies, irrespective of risk of bias, were included", SSRIs reduced disability scores but not the proportion independent 565. The 2026 guideline cites a meta-analysis of the three trials (n=5907): no effect on mRS, with very low absolute risks of the harms 843.

Going home early, with a team

Early supported discharge (17 trials, 2422 participants): hospital stay about 6 days shorter (MD -5.5 days). Death or dependency OR 0.80 (0.67 to 0.95), five fewer adverse outcomes per 100. No adverse impact on mood or carer health. Benefits were greatest with a coordinated team and mild to moderate disability 542.

Therapy at home by screen

Home telerehabilitation versus clinic (124 people, 36 sessions): Fugl-Meyer change 7.86 versus 8.36, within the margin, so not inferior 208. Cochrane (22 trials, 1937 participants): no difference in ADL, low to moderate evidence 553.

Shoulder pain

In a population study: 10% at baseline, 21% at 4 and 12 months, 29% overall. Peak onset and severity at 4 months, after most rehabilitation stays end 10.

Bladder

Urinary incontinence, Cochrane (20 trials, 1338 participants): insufficient evidence to guide continence care in rehabilitation 978.
