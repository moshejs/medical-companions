# C · Haemorrhagic stroke

Pass date: 2026-11-08. Modules served: bleeds, ich, sah.

## Findings

### bleeds · ICH: how common and how deadly
- ICH affects about 3.4 million people worldwide each year and causes about 2.8 million deaths [[Steiner2025]].
- ICH is about 10% of all strokes in the United States and 6.5 to 19.6% worldwide [[Magid2022]]. Hemphill's 2001 paper gave 10 to 15% of all strokes [[Hemphill2001]].
- Pooled incidence across 36 population studies (8145 patients) was 24.6 per 100 000 person-years (95% CI 19.7 to 30.7), and it did not fall between 1980 and 2008 [[vanAsch2010]].
- Incidence by ethnic group per 100 000 person-years: white 24.2, black 22.9, Hispanic 19.6, Asian 51.8 (95% CI 38.8 to 69.3) [[vanAsch2010]].
- Using age 45 to 54 years as reference, the incidence ratio rose from 0.10 under 45 years to 9.6 over 85 years [[vanAsch2010]].
- Median 1-month case fatality was 40.4% (range 13.1 to 61.0) and did not fall over time. It was lower in Japan (16.7%) than elsewhere (42.3%) [[vanAsch2010]].
- Six studies reported functional outcome, with independence in 12 to 39% [[vanAsch2010]].
- Population-based pooled survival was 46% at 1 year (95% CI 43 to 49) and 29% at 5 years (95% CI 26 to 33) [[Poon2014]].
- A review quotes case fatality of 40% at 1 month and 54% at 1 year [[An2017]]. Another puts 30-day mortality as high as 50%, in-hospital mortality roughly stable at 35 to 40%, and one study's 30-day case fatality as falling from 40 to 33% between 1985 and 2011 with no change in 48-hour case fatality [[Magid2022]].
- In Greater Cincinnati in 1988, 30-day mortality was 44%, and half of the deaths came in the first 2 days [[Broderick1993]].

### bleeds · ICH: causes
- SMASH-U classified 1013 consecutive ICH cases in Helsinki: hypertensive angiopathy 35%, undetermined 21%, amyloid angiopathy 20%, anticoagulation 14%, structural lesions 5%, systemic disease 5% [[Meretoja2012]].
- Structural lesions (cavernomas and arteriovenous malformations) had the smallest bleeds (median 2.8 mL) and lowest 3-month mortality (4%). Anticoagulation-related bleeds were largest (median 13.4 mL) and most often fatal (54%) [[Meretoja2012]].
- Hypertensive bleeds occur in deep areas supplied by small vessels: putamen, caudate, thalamus, brainstem and deep cerebellar nuclei [[Magid2022]].
- The most common site of ICH is the putamen [[An2017]].
- Long-standing hypertension causes degeneration of the media and smooth muscle of the responsible arteries. Lipohyalinosis is most often found in non-lobar ICH, and cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA) is relatively more common in lobar ICH [[An2017]].
- In CAA, amyloid builds up in arterial walls with loss of smooth muscle cells. It occurs mainly in lobar locations or the subarachnoid space over the cortical convexities, and goes with microbleeds or superficial siderosis [[Magid2022]].
- Risk factors listed in one review: hypertension, current smoking, excess alcohol, low cholesterol and drugs. Older age, male sex, Asian ethnicity, chronic kidney disease, CAA and microbleeds also raise risk [[An2017]].
- Anticoagulant-associated ICH in Greater Cincinnati rose from 5% of ICH in 1988 to 9% in 1993/94 and 17% in 1999. Annual incidence rose from 0.8 to 4.4 per 100 000, and in people aged 80 or over from 2.5 to 45.9 per 100 000. US per-capita warfarin distribution quadrupled over 1988 to 1999 [[Flaherty2007]].
- Warfarin did not increase baseline ICH volume but raised the risk of expansion (OR 6.2, 95% CI 1.7 to 22.9), which was detected later in the hospital course than in people not on warfarin [[Flibotte2004]].

### bleeds · Boston criteria version 2.0 for CAA
- Definite CAA: full post-mortem showing a presentation with spontaneous ICH, transient focal neurological episodes, convexity SAH or cognitive impairment/dementia; severe CAA with vasculopathy; no other diagnostic lesion [[Charidimou2022]].
- Probable CAA with supporting pathology: clinical data plus evacuated haematoma or cortical biopsy showing some degree of CAA, and no other diagnostic lesion [[Charidimou2022]].
- Probable CAA: age 50 or over; presentation with spontaneous ICH, transient focal neurological episodes or cognitive impairment/dementia; at least 2 strictly lobar haemorrhagic lesions on T2*-weighted MRI in any combination (ICH, cerebral microbleed, cortical superficial siderosis or convexity SAH foci), OR 1 lobar haemorrhagic lesion plus 1 white matter feature (severe visible perivascular spaces in the centrum semiovale, or white matter hyperintensities in a multispot pattern); no deep haemorrhagic lesion; no other cause [[Charidimou2022]].
- Possible CAA: same age and presentation, with a single strictly lobar haemorrhagic lesion OR a single white matter feature, no deep haemorrhagic lesion and no other cause. A cerebellar lesion counts as neither lobar nor deep [[Charidimou2022]].
- Other causes to exclude include head trauma, haemorrhagic transformation of an ischaemic stroke, arteriovenous malformation, haemorrhagic tumour and CNS vasculitis [[Charidimou2022]].
- Probable CAA by v2.0 in the autopsy group: sensitivity 74.5% (95% CI 65.4 to 82.4), specificity 95.0% (83.1 to 99.4), AUC 0.848. The earlier criteria had sensitivity 64.5%, specificity 95.0%, AUC 0.798 (p=0.0005) [[Charidimou2022]].
- Performance is weak outside ICH. In a community and memory clinic cohort of 54, v2.0 probable CAA had sensitivity 28.6% and specificity 65.3% (AUC 0.47), and was not superior to v1.5 [[Switzer2024]].
- The earlier modification added superficial siderosis, found in 60.5% of histologically proven CAA and in none of 22 non-CAA ICH controls [[Linn2010]].

### bleeds · Haematoma growth and expansion
- In 103 people scanned within 3 hours of onset, substantial growth occurred in 26% between baseline and 1-hour CT, and in another 12% between 1 and 20 hours. Early growth went with clinical deterioration, and no baseline feature predicted it [[Brott1997]].
- In a pooled analysis of 218 people scanned within 3 hours, 72.9% had some growth. Each 10% of growth carried a hazard ratio for death of 1.05 (95% CI 1.03 to 1.08), and predicted worse mRS (cumulative OR 0.84). IVH carried a hazard ratio of 2.23 [[Davis2006]].
- In individual patient data from 5076 people not on anticoagulants, 20% had growth of more than 6 mL. Independent predictors: time from onset to baseline imaging (OR 0.50), baseline volume (7.18), antiplatelet use (1.68), anticoagulant use (3.48). Adding the spot sign (OR 4.46) raised the C-index by 0.05 [[AlShahiSalman2018]].
- Across commonly used definitions, 13 to 32% of 531 patients met criteria for expansion. All definitions predicted poor outcome, and absolute growth cut-offs predicted severe outcomes better than relative ones [[Dowlatshahi2011]].
- In PREDICT (268 patients, within 6 hours), 30% were spot-sign positive. Median expansion was 8.6 mL versus 0.4 mL. Spot sign for expansion: positive predictive value 61%, negative predictive value 78%, sensitivity 51%, specificity 85%. Three-month mortality was 43.4% versus 19.6% (HR 2.4) [[Demchuk2012]].
- Volume can be estimated at the bedside as ABC/2: A is the greatest diameter on CT, B the diameter at 90 degrees to A, C the number of slices with haemorrhage times slice thickness. Time taken averaged 38 seconds in 118 patients [[Kothari1996]].
- Volume of 60 cm3 or more with GCS 8 or less predicted 30-day mortality of 91%. Volume under 30 cm3 with GCS 9 or more predicted 19% [[Broderick1993]].
- Haematoma expansion occurs predominantly within the first 3 hours of onset [[Sandset2026]], and typically within 24 hours [[Greenberg2022]].

### bleeds · Perihaematomal oedema
- In 219 people with supratentorial ICH (mean volume 35.7 mL), mean absolute oedema volume rose from 32.6 mL to 63.7 mL at days 7 to 11, with no significant change later. It roughly doubles in that window [[Staykov2011]].
- Oedema volume correlated strongly with ICH volume (rho 0.8). Growth between days 1 and 3 predicted in-hospital death [[Staykov2011]].
- In pooled INTERACT data (2549 people), intensive BP lowering reduced 24-hour oedema growth by 1.13 mL (95% CI 0.33 to 1.94), with the greatest benefit when started within 2.2 hours and no effect on 7-day measures (Early evidence) [[Ren2026]].

### bleeds · SAH: who and how often
- Aneurysmal SAH results from rupture of an intracranial aneurysm. About half of those affected are under 50 and about two-thirds are women, per the ESO lay summary; the same guideline gives a median age of 52 [[Vergouwen2026]]. UK data (3341 people): median age 55, 68.5% female, 70% in WFNS grade 1 or 2, 75% coiled [[Galea2017]].
- Crude worldwide SAH incidence was 7.9 per 100 000 person-years (95% CI 6.9 to 9.0). It fell from 10.2 in 1980 to 6.1 in 2010, by 1.7% a year. It fell 40.6% in Europe, 46.2% in Asia and 14.0% in North America, and rose 59.1% in Japan [[Etminan2019]].
- SAH incidence fell 7.1% per mm Hg fall in population SBP and 2.4% per percentage point fall in smoking prevalence [[Etminan2019]].
- An earlier review found about 9 per 100 000 person-years overall, 22.7 in Japan and 19.7 in Finland, 1.24 times higher in women, with the female excess starting at 55 [[deRooij2007]]. US incidence between 2007 and 2017 is reported as rising to 11.4 per 100 000 person-years [[Hoh2023]]. Incidence and regional figures therefore depend on period and method.
- Angiography finds no aneurysm in about 15%, half of whom have perimesencephalic SAH. Perimesencephalic SAH has an incidence of about 0.5 per 100 000 person-years, and rebleeding, hydrocephalus and DCI are rare [[Claassen2022]].

### bleeds · Unruptured aneurysms
- Prevalence is about 3.2% (95% CI 1.9 to 5.2) in a population with no comorbidity, mean age 50 and 50% men (68 studies, 94 912 patients) [[Vlak2011]].
- Prevalence ratios: autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease 6.9 (3.5 to 14); family history 3.4 (1.9 to 5.9); women versus men 1.61 (1.02 to 2.54), rising to 2.2 above mean age 50 [[Vlak2011]].
- Finland and Japan had similar prevalence to the USA. Their higher SAH incidence is therefore not explained by prevalence, which points to higher rupture risk [[Vlak2011]].
- PHASES pooled 8382 people in six cohorts: 230 ruptures, 1-year risk 1.4% (1.1 to 1.6), 5-year risk 3.4% (2.9 to 4.0) [[Greving2014]].
- PHASES predictors: population (region), hypertension, age, size of aneurysm, earlier SAH, site. Finnish people had 3.6 times and Japanese people 2.8 times the rupture risk of North American and other European populations [[Greving2014]].
- PHASES 5-year risk ranged from 0.25% (under 70, no vascular risk factors, internal carotid aneurysm under 7 mm) to over 15% (70 or over, hypertension, earlier SAH, giant posterior aneurysm over 20 mm) [[Greving2014]].
- ISUIA 5-year cumulative rupture without previous SAH, anterior circulation: 0%, 2.6%, 14.5%, 40% for under 7, 7 to 12, 13 to 24, and 25 mm or more. Posterior circulation and posterior communicating: 2.5%, 14.5%, 18.4%, 50% [[Wiebers2003]].

### bleeds · SAH: thunderclap headache and sentinel bleeds
- The commonest presentation is sudden thunderclap headache, very painful and intense from onset. It is the only symptom in half of SAH cases [[Claassen2022]].
- In Dutch general practice, sudden severe headache was SAH in 25% (95% CI 18 to 32) and any serious neurological disorder in 37%. With headache as the only symptom, 12% had SAH [[Linn1994]].
- Sentinel headache rates in nine good-quality studies ranged 10 to 43%. True incidence may vary from near zero to about 40% depending on misdiagnosis in the community [[Polmear2003]].
- In 1102 people with SAH, 14% reported sentinel headache in the prior 2 weeks. It carried no prognostic significance: rebleeding 10% versus 8%, DCI 18% versus 20% [[Viarasilpa2020]]. Disagreement: Linn's prospective series recorded previous bouts in only 2 of 103 headache-only patients and questioned the idea of warning leaks as a less serious variant [[Linn1994]].

### bleeds · SAH: ruling it in or out
- Ottawa SAH Rule applies to alert people over 15 with new severe nontraumatic headache peaking within 1 hour. Investigate if any: age 40 or over; neck pain or stiffness; witnessed loss of consciousness; onset during exertion; thunderclap headache (instantly peaking pain); limited neck flexion on examination [[Hoh2023,Perry2013]].
- Derivation (2131 adults, 132 with SAH): sensitivity 100% (97.2 to 100.0), specificity 15.3% (13.8 to 16.9) [[Perry2013]].
- Validation (1153 adults, 67 with SAH): sensitivity 100% (94.6 to 100), specificity 13.6% (13.1 to 15.8), with neuroimaging rates similar at about 87% [[Perry2017]].
- CT overall (3132 patients, 240 with SAH): sensitivity 92.9% (89.0 to 95.5). Within 6 hours (953 patients, 121 with SAH): sensitivity 100% (97.0 to 100.0), specificity 100% (99.5 to 100), on third-generation scanners read by a qualified radiologist [[Perry2011]].
- A meta-analysis of 5 studies (8907 patients) gave CT within 6 hours a sensitivity of 0.987 (0.971 to 0.994), with 13 missed SAH, 1.46 per 1000 [[Dubosh2016]].
- A later implementation study of 3672 patients combining the Ottawa rule with CT within 6 hours is quoted as sensitivity 95.5% and specificity 100%, so SAH might be missed in 5% [[Claassen2022]]. See open questions.
- After lumbar puncture in 1739 alert patients, fewer than 2000 x 10^6/L red cells plus no xanthochromia excluded aneurysmal SAH: sensitivity 100% (74.7 to 100), specificity 91.2% (88.6 to 93.3), based on 15 cases [[Perry2015]].
- AHA/ASA 2023: more than 6 hours from onset or a new neurological deficit, non-contrast CT and, if negative, lumbar puncture (COR 1, LOE B-NR). Under 6 hours with no new deficit, high-quality CT read by a board-certified neuroradiologist (2a, B-NR). Ottawa rule (2b, B-NR) [[Hoh2023]].
- If CT angiography is negative or inconclusive with high concern for an aneurysm, digital subtraction angiography is indicated (1, B-NR) [[Hoh2023]].

### bleeds · SAH grading scales
- WFNS: grade 1 GCS 15; grade 2 GCS 13 to 14, no motor deficit; grade 3 GCS 13 to 14, with motor deficit; grade 4 GCS 7 to 12; grade 5 GCS 3 to 6 [[Claassen2022,Teasdale1988]].
- Hunt and Hess: 1 mild headache; 2 moderate to severe headache, cranial nerve palsy, nuchal rigidity; 3 lethargy or confusion, mild focal deficit; 4 stupor, hemiparesis; 5 coma, decerebrate posturing [[Claassen2022,Hunt1968]].
- Modified Fisher: 0 no SAH; 1 thin SAH, no IVH; 2 thin SAH, with IVH; 3 thick SAH, no IVH; 4 thick SAH, with IVH [[Claassen2022,Frontera2006]].
- Original Fisher work (47 cases): no detected or diffuse blood was almost never followed by severe vasospasm (1 of 18). Clots over 5 x 3 mm or layers 1 mm or more thick were followed by it almost invariably (23 of 24) [[Fisher1980]].
- In 1355 placebo-arm patients, 33% developed symptomatic vasospasm. Modified Fisher crude ORs versus grade 0 to 1: grade 2 1.6, grade 3 1.6, grade 4 2.2. It stayed predictive after adjustment (OR 1.28) while the original scale did not [[Frontera2006]].
- Thick clot filling a cistern or fissure and blood in both lateral ventricles independently predicted DCI (ORs 2.3 and 4.1), and their risk adds up [[Claassen2001]].
- AHA/ASA 2023 recommends clinical scales such as Hunt and Hess or WFNS to determine initial severity and predict outcome (1, B-NR) [[Hoh2023]].

### ich · Guidelines at a glance
- AHA/ASA 2022 replaces the 2015 guideline [[Greenberg2022,Hemphill2015]]. Its take-home messages cover regional systems, expansion markers, smooth BP control, updated reversal, and therapies that give no benefit or harm [[Greenberg2022]].
- ESO/EANS 2025 addressed 37 PICO questions and 208 studies, updating the ESO 2014 guideline. Strong for: organised stroke units and secondary prevention with BP lowering. Strong against: anti-inflammatory drugs outside trials [[Steiner2025]].
- ESO/EANS 2025 weak for: acute BP lowering, open craniotomy for supratentorial ICH, minimally invasive surgery, decompressive surgery for deep supratentorial ICH, evacuation of cerebellar ICH over 15 mL, external ventricular drainage with thrombolysis for IVH, intermittent pneumatic compression, antiplatelet therapy for a licensed indication, and care bundles [[Steiner2025]].
- ESO/EANS 2025 weak against: routine rFVIIa, platelet transfusion for antiplatelet-associated ICH, general policies limiting treatment within 24 hours of onset, temperature and glucose management as single measures outside bundles, and prophylactic anti-seizure medicines [[Steiner2025]].

### ich · ICH score (calculator)
- Points: GCS 3 to 4 = 2, GCS 5 to 12 = 1, GCS 13 to 15 = 0; age 80 or over = 1; infratentorial origin = 1; volume 30 cm3 or more = 1; IVH present = 1 [[Hemphill2001]].
- Maximum score by addition is 6. The derivation abstract reports outcomes for scores 0 to 5 only [[Hemphill2001]].
- 30-day mortality in the derivation cohort: score 0, all 26 survived; score 5, all 6 died; mortality rose steadily with score (P<0.005) [[Hemphill2001]].
- The intermediate rates in that cohort are quoted as 13% (score 1), 26% (score 2), 72% (score 3), 97% (score 4), 100% (score 5) [[Alkhars2026]].
- Prospective validation (243 people): the score stratified 12-month functional outcome, and many people kept improving even after 6 months [[Hemphill2009]].
- In INTERACT2 (2556 people), discrimination for 90-day poor outcome was 0.68 for the original ICH score. All scores had poor sensitivity and positive predictive value for death [[Heeley2015]].
- AHA/ASA 2022: a baseline severity measure is recommended at first evaluation (1, B-NR). A score should not be the sole basis for forecasting individual prognosis or limiting life-sustaining treatment (3: No Benefit, B-NR) [[Greenberg2022]].
- AHA/ASA 2022: postponing new DNAR orders or withdrawal of support until at least the second full day of hospitalisation is reasonable (2a, B-NR). Limiting other interventions because someone has DNAR status is 3: Harm [[Greenberg2022]].

### ich · Blood pressure trials
- INTERACT2 (2839 people, within 6 hours): target SBP under 140 within 1 hour versus under 180. Death or major disability 52.0% versus 55.6% (OR 0.87, 95% CI 0.75 to 1.01; P=0.06). Ordinal mRS OR 0.87 (0.77 to 1.00; P=0.04). Mortality 11.9% versus 12.0% [[Anderson2013]].
- ATACH-2 (1000 people, volume under 60 cm3, GCS 5 or more, nicardipine within 4.5 hours): SBP target 110 to 139 versus 140 to 179. Death or disability 38.7% versus 37.7% (RR 1.04, 0.85 to 1.27). Stopped for futility. Renal adverse events 9.0% versus 4.0% (P=0.002) [[Qureshi2016]].
- Pooled INTERACT2 and ATACH-2 (3829 people): mean early reduction 29 mm Hg, mean achieved SBP 147 mm Hg, and achieved SBP continuously associated with function (per 10 mm Hg, as reported, aOR 0.90, 0.87 to 0.94) [[Moullaali2019]].
- J-shape: adjusted odds of poor outcome were lower for 1-hour reductions up to 60 mm Hg, with an inflection between 32 and 46 mm Hg, and significantly higher above 70 mm Hg [[Wang2022]].
- INTERACT3 stepped wedge cluster trial (121 hospitals, 7036 people, within 6 hours). Bundle: SBP target under 140; glucose 6.1 to 7.8 mmol/L without diabetes and 7.8 to 10.0 with diabetes; temperature 37.5 C or less; warfarin reversal to INR under 1.5 within 1 hour. Poor functional outcome common OR 0.86 (0.76 to 0.97; p=0.015). Serious adverse events 16.0% versus 20.1% [[Ma2023]].
- INTERACT4 (2404 people, China, ambulance, within 2 hours, median 61 min): target SBP 130 to 140 versus usual care. Hospital arrival SBP 159 versus 170 mm Hg. Overall common OR 1.00 (0.87 to 1.15). Haemorrhagic stroke (46.5% of confirmed strokes) 0.75 (0.60 to 0.92); ischaemic 1.30 (1.06 to 1.60) [[Li2024]].
- Pooled INTERACT and ATACH-2 trajectories (11 269 people): compared with the lowest SBP group, adjusted ORs for poor outcome across the other groups in INTERACT were 1.16, 1.44, 1.46, 1.90 and 1.28 (Early evidence) [[Wang2026]].
- INTERACT pooled data (11 312 people) as quoted by ESO: intensive lowering OR 0.85 (0.78 to 0.91) for poor outcome [[Sandset2026]].
- AHA/ASA 2022 acute BP: smooth sustained control (2a, B-NR); start within 2 hours and reach target within 1 hour (2a, C-LD); SBP 150 to 220 in mild to moderate ICH, target 140 keeping 130 to 150 (2b, B-R); large or severe ICH or surgical decompression, not well established (2b, C-LD); lowering to under 130 potentially harmful (3: Harm, B-R) [[Greenberg2022]].
- ESO 2025 BP update: advises against routine pre-hospital lowering in suspected stroke (moderate certainty). In acute ICH the net benefit of intensive lowering remains uncertain, but expert consensus supports early reduction to under 140 in small to moderate haematomas [[Sandset2026]].

### ich · Anticoagulant and antiplatelet reversal
- INCH (50 treated, INR 2.0 or more, within 12 hours): 20 mL/kg FFP versus 30 IU/kg four-factor PCC. INR 1.2 or lower within 3 hours: 9% versus 67% (aOR 30.6, 4.7 to 197.9). Deaths 35% versus 19%, stopped early for safety [[Steiner2016]].
- German cohort (1176 people): INR under 1.3 within 4 hours was associated with less enlargement (19.8% versus 41.5%). SBP under 160 at 4 hours likewise (33.1% versus 52.4%). Both together: 18.1% versus 44.2%, in-hospital mortality 13.5% versus 20.7% [[Kuramatsu2015]].
- Idarucizumab (RE-VERSE AD, 503 people, 5 g IV): median maximum reversal 100%. Among 301 with bleeding, 98 (32.6%) had intracranial haemorrhage; median time to cessation of bleeding 2.5 hours (among those assessable). At 90 days thrombotic events 6.3% and mortality 18.8% in that group. No control arm [[Pollack2017]]. US label: 5 g, given as two 2.5 g vials [[FDA-Praxbind]].
- ANNEXA-I (530 people, factor Xa inhibitor within 15 hours; 85.5% of usual care received PCC): haemostatic efficacy 67.0% versus 53.1% (difference 13.4 points, 4.6 to 22.2; P=0.003). Thrombotic events 10.3% versus 5.6%. Ischaemic stroke 6.5% versus 1.5%. No appreciable difference in mRS or 30-day death [[Connolly2024]].
- FDA stated in December 2025 that it considers the risks of Andexxa to outweigh its benefits, quoting ANNEXA-I through day 30: thrombosis 14.6% versus 6.9%, thrombosis-related death 2.5% versus 0.9%. AstraZeneca sought voluntary licence withdrawal for commercial reasons, with US sales ending 22 December 2025 [[FDA-Andexxa2025]].
- AHA/ASA 2022 reversal: stop and reverse as soon as possible (1, C-LD); VKA with INR 2.0 or more, four-factor PCC over FFP (1, B-R); IV vitamin K directly after factor replacement (1, C-LD); INR 1.3 to 1.9, PCC may be reasonable (2b, C-LD) [[Greenberg2022]].
- AHA/ASA 2022 direct agents: andexanet reasonable for factor Xa inhibitors (2a, B-NR); idarucizumab reasonable for dabigatran (2a, B-NR); four-factor or activated PCC may be considered for factor Xa inhibitors (2b, B-NR); protamine for unfractionated heparin (2a, C-LD) and LMWH (2b, C-LD) [[Greenberg2022]]. The andexanet statement predates ANNEXA-I and the FDA action.
- PATCH (190 people on antiplatelets): platelet transfusion raised odds of death or dependence (aOR 2.05, 1.18 to 3.56). Serious adverse events 42% versus 29% [[Baharoglu2016]]. AHA/ASA 2022 allows it only as might be considered with aspirin and emergency neurosurgery (2b, C-LD) [[Greenberg2022]].

### ich · Haemostatic drugs
- FAST (841 people, within 4 hours): rFVIIa 80 microg/kg reduced growth (11% versus 26%, 3.8 mL less) but not poor outcome (29% versus 24%). Arterial events 9% versus 4% [[Mayer2008]].
- Pooled four trials (721 people): significant expansion reduced (acOR 0.57, 0.43 to 0.75) [[Yogendrakumar2023]].
- FASTEST (626 people at 93 sites, aged 18 to 80, 2 to 60 mL, mean 100 min from onset; rFVIIa 80 microg/kg over 2 min) met futility at the second interim analysis. mRS at 180 days aOR 1.09 (0.79 to 1.51; p=0.61). Thromboembolic complications within 4 days 15 versus 4 (RR 3.41, 1.14 to 10.15). ICH growth 3.7 mL less [[Broderick2026]]. Testing in those at greatest risk of continued bleeding is ongoing (Early evidence) [[Broderick2026]].
- TICH-2 (2325 people, within 8 hours): tranexamic acid 1 g bolus then 1 g over 8 h. Day 90 function aOR 0.88 (0.76 to 1.03). Day 7 deaths 9% versus 11% (aOR 0.73). Day 90 case fatality 22% versus 21% [[Sprigg2018]].
- Tranexamic acid individual patient data (2860 people): no change in 90-day function (acOR 0.93, 0.81 to 1.07), lower day 7 mortality (0.70) and expansion (0.81) [[Menon2026]].

### ich · Surgery and drainage
- STICH (1033 people): favourable outcome 26% versus 24% (OR 0.89, 0.66 to 1.19) [[Mendelow2005]].
- STICH II (601 conscious people, superficial lobar 10 to 100 mL, no IVH): unfavourable outcome 59% versus 62% (absolute difference 3.7%, -4.3 to 11.6; p=0.367) [[Mendelow2013]].
- MISTIE III (506 people, 30 mL or more; alteplase 1 mg every 8 h up to nine doses): mRS 0 to 3 at 365 days 45% versus 41% (adjusted difference 4%, -4 to 12; p=0.33). Death at 7 days 1% versus 4%, at 30 days 9% versus 15% [[Hanley2019]].
- ENRICH (300 people, 30 to 80 mL, within 24 hours): utility-weighted mRS 0.458 versus 0.374 (difference 0.084; posterior probability 0.981). Lobar difference 0.127 (0.035 to 0.219); anterior basal ganglia -0.013 (-0.147 to 0.116), and enrolment there stopped by adaptation rule. 30-day death 9.3% versus 18.0% [[Pradilla2024]].
- SWITCH (197 people, severe deep ICH, median 57 mL, stopped early for funding): mRS 5 to 6 at 180 days 44% versus 58% (aRR 0.77, 0.59 to 1.01; p=0.057). Authors call this weak evidence, with survival linked to severe disability in both groups [[Beck2024]].
- Meta-analysis to 2019 (21 trials, 4145 people, only 4 of highest quality): good outcome RR 1.40 (1.22 to 1.60) for any surgery, 1.47 (1.26 to 1.72) for minimally invasive. Death RR 0.77 and 0.68. Surgery looked better earlier (p=0.04) [[Sondag2020]].
- INTERACT3 China secondary analysis (5772 people, 24.4% operated): surgery associated with lower poor outcome (OR 0.71) and mortality (OR 0.55). This is observational [[Hu2025]].
- CLEAR III (500 people, IVH blocking the third or fourth ventricle, ICH under 30 mL): good outcome 48% versus 45% (RR 1.06). Case fatality 18% versus 29% (HR 0.60), but mRS 5 in 17% versus 9% [[Hanley2017]].
- AHA/ASA 2022: cerebellar ICH with deterioration, brainstem compression and/or hydrocephalus, or volume 15 mL or more, immediate removal (1, B-NR). Large IVH with impaired consciousness, EVD (1, B-NR) [[Greenberg2022]].
- AHA/ASA 2022: minimally invasive evacuation for supratentorial over 20 to 30 mL, GCS 5 to 12, can reduce mortality (2a, B-R), and effect on function uncertain (2b, B-R). Craniotomy for most is uncertain (2b, A), lifesaving if deteriorating (2b, C-LD). Decompressive craniectomy may be considered (2b, C-LD) [[Greenberg2022]]. ENRICH and SWITCH were published after this guideline.

### ich · Restarting antithrombotics and preventing recurrence
- RESTART (537 people, median 76 days after ICH): recurrent ICH 4% on antiplatelet versus 9% avoiding (aHR 0.51, 0.25 to 1.03; p=0.060). Major occlusive events 15% versus 14% [[RESTART2019]].
- COCROACH (4 trials, 412 people with AF): any stroke or cardiovascular death 14% versus 22% (HR 0.68, 0.42 to 1.10). Ischaemic events 4% versus 19% (HR 0.27). Haemorrhagic events 7% versus 5% (HR 1.80, 0.77 to 4.21) [[AlShahiSalman2023]].
- PRESTIGE-AF (319 people, median age 79, median follow-up 1.4 years): first ischaemic stroke HR 0.05 (0.01 to 0.36), 0.83 versus 8.60 per 100 patient-years. Recurrent ICH did not meet non-inferiority (HR 10.89, 90% CI 1.95 to 60.72), 5.00 versus 0.82 per 100 patient-years. Deaths 10% versus 13% [[Veltkamp2025]].
- ENRICH-AF (registered 948 participants; edoxaban 60/30 mg daily versus no antithrombotic or antiplatelet monotherapy) is complete [[NCT03950076]].
- ENRICH-AF results from the coordinating institute's press release on the ESC 2026 presentation: stroke or systemic embolism 11.8% versus 12.8%; major bleeding 11.6% versus 5.2%. No peer-reviewed paper was found (unverified) [[PHRI2026]].
- AHA/ASA 2022: restart anticoagulation early with a mechanical valve or LVAD (2a, C-LD); antiplatelet may be reasonable (2b, B-R); anticoagulation in non-valvular AF may be considered (2b, B-NR), at about 7 to 8 weeks (2b, C-LD); left atrial appendage closure if ineligible (2b, C-LD) [[Greenberg2022]].
- Observational: OAC resumption went with fewer ischaemic complications (5.2% versus 15.0%) and similar haemorrhagic ones (8.1% versus 6.6%) [[Kuramatsu2015]].
- Recurrence: annual risk 1.3 to 7.4% across nine studies, higher after lobar in two of three hospital studies [[Poon2014]].
- Single-centre 1145 survivors: recurrence per 1000 person-years, lobar 84 with inadequate versus 49 with adequate BP control; non-lobar 52 versus 27 [[Biffi2015]].
- AHA/ASA 2022: BP control to prevent recurrence (1, B-R); reasonable to aim for SBP 130 and DBP 80 mm Hg long term (2a, B-NR) [[Greenberg2022]].

### sah · Guidelines at a glance
- AHA/ASA 2023 replaces the 2012 guideline [[Hoh2023,Connolly2012]]. Neurocritical Care Society guidance appeared the same year: five PICO questions with strong recommendations, one conditional, six with insufficient evidence [[Treggiari2023]].
- ESO/EANS/ESMINT 2026: 15 evidence-based recommendations and 37 expert consensus statements. High-level evidence backs only oral nimodipine and regular coiling when both options are equally suitable in good-grade patients [[Vergouwen2026]].
- ESO 2026 recommends against routine antifibrinolytics before aneurysm treatment, and against tirilazad, statins, magnesium sulphate and endothelin receptor antagonists. It makes no evidence-based recommendation on external lumbar drainage, induced hypertension, endovascular treatment of vasospasm, or non-coil devices [[Vergouwen2026]].
- ESO 2026 consensus: treat in a dedicated neuro-ICU or high care unit in a centre with at least 70 aSAH patients a year (35 in remote areas), by a physician treating at least 30 aneurysms a year [[Vergouwen2026]]. AHA/ASA 2023 recommends timely transfer to higher-volume centres (1, B-NR) with no numeric threshold in the recommendation [[Hoh2023]].

### sah · Rebleeding
- Rebleeding occurs in about 40% if the ruptured aneurysm stays untreated [[Vergouwen2026]].
- Most rebleeding occurs within 24 hours of onset [[Claassen2022]]. No day-by-day rate curve could be verified, so none is given here. The rate is highest in the first 6 hours [[Larsen2013]]. Rebleeding under 6 hours had OR 3.22 (1.46 to 7.12) in a meta-analysis of 14 studies and 5693 patients [[Tang2014]].
- In 273 people admitted within 24 hours, 13.6% rebled before arrival, 77% of episodes within 2 hours. SBP over 160 mm Hg had OR 3.1 [[Ohkuma2001]].
- Other risk factors: high SBP (OR 1.93), Hunt-Hess III to IV (3.43), intracerebral or intraventricular haematoma (1.65), posterior circulation (2.15), size over 10 mm (1.70) [[Tang2014]].
- Rebleeding is among the strongest predictors of poor outcome: in-hospital death aOR 7.69 (3.00 to 19.71) in Switzerland [[Stienen2018]], unfavourable discharge outcome OR 7.41 (4.48 to 12.30) in the UK [[Galea2017]].
- ULTRA (955 people, tranexamic acid up to 24 h): good outcome 60% versus 64% (OR 0.86, 0.66 to 1.12); rebleeding 10% versus 14% (OR 0.71, 0.48 to 1.04) [[Post2021]].
- Antifibrinolytics reduced rebleeding (Cochrane OR 0.55, 0.42 to 0.71), raised cerebral ischaemia (OR 1.39), and did not improve poor outcome (OR 1.12) [[Roos2003]]. Later meta-analyses: rebleeding RR 0.61, mortality RR 0.99, hydrocephalus RR 1.12 [[AlSalihi2025]]; ESO: rebleeding RR 0.56, poor outcome OR 1.03 [[Vergouwen2026]].
- AHA/ASA 2023: BP monitoring and control with short-acting agents to avoid severe hypotension, hypertension and variability (1, C-EO); emergency anticoagulant reversal (1, C-EO); routine antifibrinolytics not useful (3: No Benefit, A) [[Hoh2023]].
- EVD or lumbar puncture before securing did not clearly raise rebleeding: HR 1.0 (0.4 to 2.7) and 0.7 (0.1 to 6.4) [[Hellingman2007]].

### sah · Securing the aneurysm
- AHA/ASA 2023: as early as feasible, preferably within 24 hours of onset (1, B-NR); complete obliteration whenever feasible (1, B-NR) [[Hoh2023]].
- ESO 2026 moved from the 2013 target of within 72 hours to a consensus of within 24 hours with the most dedicated team available. The evidence for under 24 hours is low-quality observational, and very early treatment might worsen early brain injury and DCI [[Vergouwen2026]].
- The 1980s International Cooperative Study (3521 people): at 6 months 26% had died and 58% had fully recovered [[Kassell1990]].
- ISAT 2002 (2143 people): dependent or dead at 1 year 23.7% coiling versus 30.6% clipping (ARR 6.9%, 2.5 to 11.3) [[Molyneux2002]].
- ISAT 2005: 23.5% versus 30.9% (ARR 7.4%, 3.6 to 11.2). Survival advantage held up to 7 years. Epilepsy lower and late rebleeding higher with coiling [[Molyneux2005]].
- ISAT UK cohort, 10 to 18.5 years (1644 people): alive at 10 years 83% versus 79% (OR 1.35). Alive and independent OR 1.34 (1.07 to 1.67). 33 recurrent SAH after 1 year, 17 from the target aneurysm [[Molyneux2015]]. Target aneurysm recurrent aSAH quoted as 1.6% coiling and 0.5% clipping [[Vergouwen2026]].
- AHA/ASA 2023: anterior circulation good-grade, equally suitable: primary coiling over clipping for 1-year outcome (1, A), both reasonable for long-term outcome (2a, B-R). Posterior circulation amenable: coiling (1, B-R). Under 40 years: clipping might be preferred (2b, C-LD). Over 70: not well established (2b, B-R). Large haematoma with depressed consciousness: emergency evacuation (1, B-R) [[Hoh2023]].
- ESO 2026 suggests reserving stent-assisted coiling for when other options are not feasible, and flow diversion as a last line. ISAT-2 stopped early for poor enrolment [[Vergouwen2026]].
- A non-randomised 24-centre comparison (n=9159) linked coiling to higher 14-day case fatality and non-superior 90-day outcome, as quoted in a review [[Claassen2022]].

### sah · Vasospasm and delayed cerebral ischaemia
- DCI occurs in about 30%, mostly between days 4 and 14 after aSAH [[Hoh2023]]. Peak risk for DCI and vasospasm is post-bleed days 6 to 10 [[Hoh2023]].
- Reported frequencies differ: symptoms in about 20% [[Vergouwen2026]]; DCI in 20% of 276 [[Claassen2001]]; symptomatic vasospasm in 33% of 1355 [[Frontera2006]].
- The first 24 to 48 hours are called the early brain injury phase. DCI is now understood as multifactorial: microcirculatory dysfunction, glymphatic impairment, inflammation, and neuroelectric disruption alongside vasospasm (Early evidence) [[Dodd2021]].
- Research definition: main outcomes are cerebral infarction on CT, MRI or autopsy, after excluding procedure-related infarcts, and functional outcome. Vasospasm can be proof of concept only [[Vergouwen2010]]. Clinical deterioration is focal impairment or a fall of at least 2 GCS points per AHA text [[Hoh2023]].
- BRANT: British aneurysm nimodipine trial (554 people, 60 mg orally every 4 hours for 21 days, started within 96 hours): cerebral infarction 22% versus 33% (34% reduction, 13 to 50). Poor outcome 20% versus 33% (40% reduction, 20 to 55) [[Pickard1989]].
- Cochrane (16 trials, 3361 people): overall RR 0.81 (0.72 to 0.92). Oral nimodipine RR 0.67 (0.55 to 0.81). Results depend largely on one large trial. IV calcium antagonists are not recommended for routine use [[DorhoutMees2007]]. ESO: oral nimodipine poor outcome OR 0.56 (0.40 to 0.76), case fatality OR 0.74 (0.53 to 1.02) [[Vergouwen2026]].
- US label: indicated across Hunt and Hess I to V. Boxed warning: capsules must not be given intravenously or by other parenteral routes, as deaths have occurred [[FDA-Nimodipine]].
- AHA/ASA 2023: early enteral nimodipine (1, A); euvolaemia (2a, B-NR); raising SBP for symptomatic vasospasm (2b, B-NR); intra-arterial vasodilators and angioplasty for severe vasospasm (2b, B-NR); statins and IV magnesium not recommended (3: No Benefit, A); prophylactic haemodynamic augmentation (3: Harm, B-R) [[Hoh2023]].
- Monitoring in AHA/ASA 2023: CT angiography or perfusion (2a), transcranial Doppler (2a), continuous EEG in high grade (2a), invasive monitoring may be considered (2b) [[Hoh2023]].
- Induced hypertension trial stopped at 41 people: aRR for poor outcome 1.0 (0.6 to 1.8), serious adverse events RR 2.1 (0.9 to 5.0) [[Gathier2018]]. ESO notes three rescue therapy trials of under 70 patients each were underpowered [[Vergouwen2026]].
- Clazosentan: CONSCIOUS-1 reduced moderate or severe vasospasm from 66% to 23% at 15 mg/h [[Macdonald2008]]. CONSCIOUS-2 (5 mg/h, clipping): primary 21% versus 25% (p=0.10), poor outcome 29% versus 25% [[Macdonald2011]]. CONSCIOUS-3 (coiling, 15 mg/h): primary 15% versus 27% (OR 0.474), poor outcome 28% versus 24% [[Macdonald2012]].
- Japanese trials: vasospasm morbidity and mortality 13.6% versus 28.8% after coiling, 16.2% versus 39.6% after clipping [[Endo2022]]. REACT (409 people, thick and diffuse clot): deterioration from DCI 15.8% versus 17.2% (p=0.734), poor mRS and GOSE 24.8% versus 20.1% [[Mayer2025]].
- Intrathecal nimodipine microparticles (EG-1962): the NEWTON-2 cisternal phase 2 study randomised only 6 people and was halted when the phase 3 intraventricular study was judged unlikely to meet its primary endpoint [[Macdonald2020]]. A review records no benefit for EG-1962 [[Claassen2022]].
- EARLYDRAIN (287 people, lumbar drain 5 mL/h within 72 hours): unfavourable outcome 32.6% versus 44.8% (RR 0.73, 0.52 to 0.98). Secondary infarction 28.5% versus 39.9% (Early evidence) [[Wolf2023]].
- Transfusion trial (742 people): liberal versus restrictive strategy, unfavourable outcome 33.5% versus 37.7% (RR 0.88, 0.72 to 1.09; P=0.22) [[English2025]].

### sah · Hydrocephalus and hyponatraemia
- Hydrocephalus affects about 25% [[Vergouwen2026]]. AHA text reports 15 to 87% in the acute stage [[Hoh2023]]. A review gives 6 to 67% overall, about 20 to 30% in recent studies; one fifth early (acute within 3 days, subacute days 4 to 14) and 10 to 20% chronic after 2 weeks [[Chen2017]].
- Shunt dependence 17.4% in one meta-analysis and ventriculoperitoneal shunt 12.7% in 41 789 patients, both as quoted [[Chen2017]].
- AHA/ASA 2023: acute symptomatic hydrocephalus, urgent CSF diversion (1, B-NR); EVD bundle (1, B-NR); chronic symptomatic, permanent diversion (1, B-NR); routine lamina terminalis fenestration not indicated (3: No Benefit, C-LD) [[Hoh2023]].
- Hyponatraemia pooled incidence 37.0% (31.7 to 42.4; 52 studies, 10 512 people). Linked to vasospasm (OR 2.93) and longer stay (16.4 versus 8.0 days), not to poor outcome (OR 1.15, 0.44 to 3.02) [[Gillespie2025]].
- In a prospective series of 100, 49 developed sodium under 135 mmol/L. Cause: SIAD in 36 of 49 (the abstract prints 71.4%; 36 of 49 is 73.5%), glucocorticoid insufficiency in 4 (8.2%), incorrect IV fluids in 5 (10.2%), hypovolaemia in 5 (10.2%), and no cases of cerebral salt wasting [[Hannon2014]]. A review treats SIAD or cerebral salt wasting as causes that are hard to separate, says fluid restriction can be harmful, and focuses management on euvolaemia [[Claassen2022]].
- AHA/ASA 2023: goal-directed euvolaemia (2a, B-R); mineralocorticoids reasonable for natriuresis and hyponatraemia (2a, B-R); induced hypervolaemia potentially harmful (3: Harm, B-R) [[Hoh2023]].

### sah · Outcomes over time
- Case fatality in 33 studies ranged 8.3 to 66.7%, falling 0.8% a year (0.2 to 1.3), 17% over 1973 to 2002, despite mean age rising from 52 to 62. Japan was 11.8% lower [[Nieuwkamp2009]].
- Earlier review, 1960 to 1992: 32 to 67%, falling 0.5% a year [[Hop1997]].
- Greater Cincinnati 1988 to 2010: incidence stable (8.8 to 7.7 per 100 000); case fatality fell at 5 days 32 to 18%, 30 days 46 to 25%, 90 days 49 to 29%. For proven or highly likely aneurysmal SAH alone the declines were no longer significant [[Mackey2016]].
- AHA text: prehospital mortality 22 to 26%; US inpatient mortality 13.7% (2006) to 13.1% (2018); population case fatality down 1.5% a year over 1980 to 2020 [[Hoh2023]].
- ESO lay summary: about 40% die within 3 months and roughly one third regain independence [[Vergouwen2026]].
- Swiss registry: in-hospital mortality 20% (373 of 1866) overall, 13.9% once those with active treatment discontinued were excluded [[Stienen2018]].
- 20-year excess mortality in 3078 one-year survivors was 17%, including young people and those with good recovery [[Huhtakangas2015]].

### sah · Long-term cognition, fatigue and work
- Survivors commonly have deficits in memory, executive function and language, compounded by depression, anxiety, fatigue and sleep disturbance [[AlKhindi2010]].
- People who look normal at discharge may have hidden cognitive deficits that interfere with daily function [[Nussbaum2021]].
- In ISAT, 32.1% of 474 otherwise non-disabled survivors had cognitive impairment at 12 months, less often after coiling (OR 0.58, 0.38 to 0.87) [[Scott2010]].
- At 3 months, 94.6% of 111 people reported at least one cognitive or emotional complaint that hampered daily life. Most frequent: mental slowness, short-term memory, attention [[Passier2010]].
- Of 88 working at the time, 61.4% returned to work 2 to 4 years later and only 35.2% resumed completely [[Passier2011]].
- Among 96 good-outcome people with fatigue, it ranked in their three most disabling symptoms, described as low motivation, mental fatigue and sensitivity to stress [[Western2021]].
- AHA text: depression in about one-third of survivors, anxiety and post-traumatic stress disorder in about 15 to 20%, cognitive impairment in 40 to 70% [[Hoh2023]].
- Long-term data remain limited on life expectancy and on the course of cognitive deficits [[Rinkel2011]].
- AHA/ASA 2023: validated screening for physical, cognitive, behavioural and quality-of-life deficits before discharge (1, B-NR); depression and anxiety screening (1, B-NR); early multidisciplinary team-based treatment and rehabilitation (1, B-NR); early rehabilitation once the aneurysm is secured (2a, B-NR); follow-up cerebrovascular imaging after repair (1, B-NR) [[Hoh2023]].

### Exam hooks
- ICH score items: GCS (0 to 2), age 80 or over, infratentorial, volume 30 cm3 or more, IVH [[Hemphill2001]].
- ABC/2 estimates ICH volume [[Kothari1996]].
- Spot sign predicts expansion: specificity 85%, sensitivity 51% [[Demchuk2012]].
- Deep bleeds point to hypertensive disease and lobar bleeds to CAA [[Magid2022,An2017]].
- Probable CAA in Boston v2.0 adds white matter features, and needs age 50 or over [[Charidimou2022]].
- AHA/ASA 2022 calls lowering SBP below 130 in mild to moderate ICH potentially harmful [[Greenberg2022]].
- Four-factor PCC beats FFP for INR correction in warfarin-related ICH [[Steiner2016]].
- Platelet transfusion worsened outcome in PATCH [[Baharoglu2016]].
- rFVIIa slows growth without improving function, in FAST and FASTEST [[Mayer2008,Broderick2026]].
- Cerebellar ICH 15 mL or more, or with brainstem compression or hydrocephalus, is a Class 1 indication for surgery in AHA/ASA 2022 [[Greenberg2022]].
- Ottawa rule has six items and specificity about 15% [[Perry2013]].
- CT within 6 hours, on a modern scanner, read by a qualified radiologist [[Perry2011]].
- Oral nimodipine 60 mg every 4 hours for 21 days in BRANT; never IV from capsules [[Pickard1989,FDA-Nimodipine]].
- DCI mostly days 4 to 14, peak days 6 to 10 [[Hoh2023]].
- Prophylactic hypervolaemia and augmentation are harmful [[Hoh2023]].
- ISAT: 1-year death or dependence about 7% less with coiling, late rebleeding more common [[Molyneux2005]].
- Hyponatraemia after SAH is mostly SIAD in the best prospective series [[Hannon2014]].

### In plain words
- A brain bleed happens when a blood vessel inside the head breaks and blood leaks out. Bleeds are less common than clots but are more often fatal [[vanAsch2010]].
- Long-term high blood pressure can damage tiny deep vessels. In older people, a protein called amyloid can weaken vessels near the brain surface [[Magid2022]].
- Bleeds often keep growing in the first hours, which is why speed matters even without a clot-busting drug [[Brott1997]].
- Swelling around the bleed can keep growing for about a week or more [[Staykov2011]].
- Blood thinners usually can be reversed in hospital. Tell staff what you take and when you last took it [[Greenberg2022]].
- A risk score is a starting point. It should not be the only thing used to decide about someone's care [[Greenberg2022]].
- Restarting a blood thinner after a bleed is a careful choice between preventing clots and risking another bleed. Trials are still settling this [[Veltkamp2025]].
- A sudden headache that is the worst of your life, peaking in moments, is an emergency [[Claassen2022]].
- About 3 in 100 adults may have a small swelling on a brain artery, and most never burst [[Vlak2011,Greving2014]].
- After an aneurysm bleeds, doctors try to seal it fast because it may bleed again, most often soon after the first bleed [[Claassen2022]].
- Blood around the brain can cause trouble days later, so people often stay in hospital for up to two weeks and take a medicine called nimodipine [[Hoh2023,Pickard1989]].
- Many people who survive still feel tired or find thinking and memory harder, even when they look well. This is real, common and worth raising with the care team [[AlKhindi2010,Western2021]].

## Open questions and controversies
- BP floor in ICH: AHA/ASA 2022 gives target 140 keeping 130 to 150 and warns below 130 [[Greenberg2022]]; ESO 2025 calls net benefit uncertain and relies on consensus for under 140 [[Sandset2026]]; pooled data point to harm from very large early falls [[Wang2022]]. Status: unresolved on floor and speed.
- Prehospital BP lowering: benefit in haemorrhagic stroke and harm in ischaemic stroke in INTERACT4 [[Li2024]]; ESO advises against it routinely [[Sandset2026]]. Status: needs prehospital differentiation of stroke type.
- Factor Xa inhibitor reversal: AHA/ASA 2022 calls andexanet reasonable [[Greenberg2022]]; ANNEXA-I shows better haemostasis, more thrombosis, no functional gain [[Connolly2024]]; the FDA now considers risks to outweigh benefits [[FDA-Andexxa2025]]. Thrombosis figures differ, 10.3% versus 5.6% in the paper and 14.6% versus 6.9% through day 30 in the FDA notice. Status: US withdrawal; no direct functional benefit shown.
- Haemostasis as a strategy: reducing growth has not translated into better function [[Mayer2008,Broderick2026,Menon2026]]. Status: further testing in selected people, for example spot-sign positive or very early (Early evidence) [[Broderick2026]].
- Surgery: neutral STICH and STICH II [[Mendelow2005,Mendelow2013]] and MISTIE III [[Hanley2019]]; positive ENRICH driven by lobar bleeds [[Pradilla2024]]; weak SWITCH [[Beck2024]]; favourable meta-analysis with few high-quality trials [[Sondag2020]]. Status: selection and timing unsettled.
- Anticoagulation after ICH in AF: ischaemic benefit is large, recurrent bleeding raised [[Veltkamp2025,AlShahiSalman2023]]; ENRICH-AF press results neutral on efficacy with more bleeding (unverified) [[PHRI2026]]. Status: full ENRICH-AF publication pending as of this pass.
- CT within 6 hours: 100% sensitivity [[Perry2011]]; 98.7% pooled [[Dubosh2016]]; 95.5% quoted for an implementation study [[Claassen2022]]. Status: AHA/ASA 2023 gives Class 2a under defined conditions [[Hoh2023]].
- Antifibrinolytics and rebleeding: AHA text reads ULTRA as no significant reduction [[Hoh2023,Post2021]]; meta-analyses show reduction without functional gain [[AlSalihi2025,Vergouwen2026]]. Status: agreed not to use routinely.
- Timing within 24 hours: both guidelines favour it [[Hoh2023,Vergouwen2026]], yet ESO stresses low-quality evidence and possible harm from very early treatment [[Vergouwen2026]]. Status: no randomised trial.
- Lumbar drain: EARLYDRAIN positive [[Wolf2023]]; ESO makes no recommendation until more trial data exist [[Vergouwen2026]]. Status: open.
- Clazosentan: lowers vasospasm without consistent functional gain, conflicting Japanese and REACT results [[Endo2022,Mayer2025]]; ESO recommends against endothelin receptor antagonists [[Vergouwen2026]].
- Induced hypertension and rescue therapy: widely used, but with low-level evidence [[Vergouwen2026,Gathier2018]]; AHA/ASA gives 2b [[Hoh2023]].
- DCI window: this pass's brief assumed days 3 to 14; AHA/ASA 2023 states mostly days 4 to 14 with peak days 6 to 10 [[Hoh2023]]. Status: the AHA figures are used here.
- SAH in Japan: Etminan reports a 59.1% rise in incidence [[Etminan2019]]; a Lancet review words the same region as a decrease of 59% [[Claassen2022]]. Status: the primary analysis is used here.
- Boston v2.0 outside ICH: accuracy drops in non-haemorrhagic cohorts [[Switzer2024]] compared with validation in mixed presentations [[Charidimou2022]].

- All PubMed sources had their record fetched by E-utilities in this pass, and at least the abstract was read. Hunt1968 and Teasdale1988 have no abstract on the record, so they are cited only as the originators of the scales; the scale wording comes from the table in Claassen2022.
- Full text read through PubMed Central: Vergouwen2026, Sandset2026, Charidimou2022, Claassen2022, Magid2022, An2017, Chen2017, Dodd2021, Alkhars2026. The PMC copy of Steiner2025 held the abstract and summary only, so its recommendation list comes from that text.
- ahajournals.org refused automated access. Greenberg2022 and Hoh2023 were read from publicly hosted PDF copies; the Hoh2023 copy is the pre-print layout, final citation Stroke 2023;54(7):e314-e370. COR and LOE were taken from those PDFs.
- The per-score ICH score mortality for scores 1 to 4 is a secondary quotation from Alkhars2026, because the Hemphill2001 abstract gives only scores 0 and 5. The original full text was not accessed.
- The FDA safety communication on Andexxa was read directly on fda.gov. The drug labels were read through openFDA.
- Marked (unverified): ENRICH-AF results, from press material only. The hazard ratio of 0.88 and a reported lobar enrolment halt appeared only in news coverage and were left out.
- Dropped because they could not be verified: percentage shares of ICH by location, the PHASES point table (only predictors and risk range are given), and a day-by-day rebleeding curve.
- The Hannon2014 abstract's 71.4% for SIAD does not match its own count of 36 of 49; both are shown.
