## Findings

### lysis · The founding trials
- NINDS rt-PA (1995): randomised, double-blind trial within 3 hours of onset. Part 1 had 291 patients and part 2 had 333. At 24 hours, part 1 showed no significant difference in neurological improvement [[NINDS1995]].
- In NINDS part 2 the global odds ratio for a favourable 3-month outcome was 1.7 (95% CI 1.2 to 2.6). Patients given t-PA were at least 30 percent more likely to have minimal or no disability at 3 months [[NINDS1995]].
- NINDS safety: symptomatic intracerebral haemorrhage within 36 hours was 6.4% with t-PA versus 0.6% with placebo (P<0.001). Three-month mortality was 17% versus 21% (P=0.30) [[NINDS1995]].
- The Activase label reports NINDS part 2 (labelled Study 2, n=333) by scale. Placebo versus Activase: modified Rankin Scale (mRS) 0 to 1, 26.1% versus 38.7% (difference 12.6%, 95% CI 3.7 to 21.6). Barthel 95 or more, 37.6% versus 50.0%. Glasgow Outcome Scale 1, 31.5% versus 44.0%. NIHSS 1 or less, 20.0% versus 31.0% [[FDA-Activase]].
- Combined label safety for both parts (n=624, placebo versus Activase): 90-day mortality 20.5% versus 17.3% (p=0.36); any intracranial haemorrhage 6.4% versus 15.4%; symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage within 36 hours 0.6% versus 6.4% [[FDA-Activase]].
- ECASS III (2008): 821 patients, 3 to 4.5 hours, alteplase 0.9 mg/kg versus placebo, median treatment time 3 hours 59 minutes. mRS 0 to 1 at 90 days was 52.4% versus 45.2% (OR 1.34, 95% CI 1.02 to 1.76; P=0.04) [[Hacke2008]].
- ECASS III safety: any intracranial haemorrhage 27.0% versus 17.6% (P=0.001); symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage 2.4% versus 0.2% (P=0.008); mortality 7.7% versus 8.4% (P=0.68) [[Hacke2008]].
- IST-3 (2012): 3035 patients within 6 hours, open treatment, and 1617 (53%) were older than 80. Alive and independent (Oxford Handicap Score 0 to 2) at 6 months was 37% versus 35% (adjusted OR 1.13, 95% CI 0.95 to 1.35; p=0.181), missing its primary outcome. The ordinal shift was significant (common OR 1.27, 95% CI 1.10 to 1.47; p=0.001) [[Sandercock2012]].
- IST-3 harm timing: fatal or non-fatal symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage within 7 days was 7% versus 1%. Deaths within 7 days were 11% versus 7%, but by 6 months deaths were equal at 27% in each group [[Sandercock2012]].
- Cochrane 2014 (27 trials, 10,187 participants, any thrombolytic, mostly up to 6 hours): death or dependency (mRS 3 to 6) OR 0.85 (95% CI 0.78 to 0.93); symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage OR 3.75 (3.11 to 4.51); early death OR 1.69 (1.44 to 1.98). Within 3 hours, death or dependency OR 0.66 (0.56 to 0.79) with no increase in death (OR 0.99, 0.82 to 1.21) [[Wardlaw2014]].

### lysis · Time is benefit
- Stroke Thrombolysis Trialists (9 trials, 6756 patients): mRS 0 to 1 at 3 to 6 months was 32.9% versus 23.1% within 3.0 hours (OR 1.75, 95% CI 1.35 to 2.27); 35.3% versus 30.1% from over 3.0 to 4.5 hours (OR 1.26, 1.05 to 1.51); 32.6% versus 30.6% beyond 4.5 hours (OR 1.15, 0.95 to 1.40) [[Emberson2014]].
- The same analysis estimated that alteplase has no effect at 6.3 hours (95% CI 5.0 to 13.8), and that the lower 95% CI first crosses 1.0 at 5.1 hours. The trend was p=0.016 [[Emberson2014]].
- Overall, the early excess of fatal haemorrhage (about 2%) was offset by a rise in disability-free survival of about 10% for treatment within 3.0 hours and about 5% for treatment from 3.1 to 4.5 hours [[Emberson2014]].
- Derived by this pass, not stated by the source: NNT for one extra mRS 0 to 1 is about 10 within 3 hours (1/0.098), about 19 at 3 to 4.5 hours (1/0.052) and about 50 beyond 4.5 hours (1/0.020, not statistically significant) [[Emberson2014]].
- Earlier pooled analysis (3670 patients within 360 minutes): adjusted odds of mRS 0 to 1 were 2.55 (95% CI 1.44 to 4.52) at 0 to 90 minutes, 1.64 (1.12 to 2.40) at 91 to 180, 1.34 (1.06 to 1.68) at 181 to 270 and 1.22 (0.92 to 1.61) at 271 to 360 [[Lees2010]].
- Adjusted odds of mortality in that pool were 0.78, 1.13, 1.22 and 1.49 across the same bands. Large parenchymal haemorrhage was 5.2% versus 1.0%, with no clear relation to onset-to-treatment time (p=0.4140) [[Lees2010]].
- Disagreement on where benefit ends: Lees2010 saw no benefit after around 270 minutes; Emberson2014 placed zero effect at 6.3 hours and loss of statistical confidence at 5.1 hours. Both support treatment up to 4.5 hours [[Lees2010,Emberson2014]].
- Real-world registry (Get With The Guidelines-Stroke, 58,353 patients, 1395 US hospitals, 2003 to 2012): median onset to treatment 144 minutes. Per 15 minutes faster: in-hospital mortality OR 0.96 (0.95 to 0.98), symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage OR 0.96 (0.95 to 0.98), independent walking at discharge OR 1.04 (1.03 to 1.05), discharge home OR 1.03 (1.02 to 1.04) [[Saver2013]].
- Modelling: each minute of onset-to-treatment time saved granted on average 1.8 days of extra healthy life (95% prediction interval 0.9 to 2.7); 0.6 day in an 80-year-old with NIHSS 20 and 3.5 days in a 50-year-old with NIHSS 20. A 15-minute reduction equalled about 1 month of disability-free life [[Meretoja2014]].
- Mobile stroke units (BEST-MSU, observational alternating-week design, 1047 t-PA-eligible patients): median onset to t-PA 72 versus 108 minutes; 97.1% versus 79.5% received t-PA; mRS 0 to 1 at 90 days 55.0% versus 44.4% [[Grotta2021]].

### lysis · Bleeding risk
- Type 2 parenchymal haemorrhage within 7 days: 6.8% versus 1.3% (OR 5.55, 95% CI 4.01 to 7.70; absolute excess 5.5%, 4.6 to 6.4) [[Whiteley2016]].
- SITS-MOST haemorrhage within 24 to 36 hours (type 2 parenchymal plus at least 4 NIHSS points worse): 3.7% versus 0.6% (OR 6.67, 4.11 to 10.84; absolute excess 3.1%, 2.4 to 3.8) [[Whiteley2016]].
- Fatal intracerebral haemorrhage within 7 days: 2.7% versus 0.4% (OR 7.14, 3.98 to 12.79; absolute excess 2.3%, 1.7 to 2.9) [[Whiteley2016]].
- However defined, the proportional increase did not depend on delay, age or severity. The absolute excess rose with severity: for SITS-MOST haemorrhage, 1.5% (0.8 to 2.6) at NIHSS 0 to 4 and 3.7% (2.1 to 6.3) at NIHSS 22 or more (p=0.0101) [[Whiteley2016]].
- Within 4.5 hours, the absolute gain in mRS 0 to 1 (6.8%, 4.0 to 9.5) exceeded the rise in fatal haemorrhage (2.2%, 1.5 to 3.0) and in any death within 90 days (0.9%, -1.4 to 3.2) [[Whiteley2016]].
- Stroke Thrombolysis Trialists 90-day mortality overall was 17.9% versus 16.5% (HR 1.11, 0.99 to 1.25; p=0.07) [[Emberson2014]].
- Definitions drive the headline number. Across the same patients symptomatic bleeding reads 6.8% or 3.7% by definition, and NINDS reported 6.4% under its own definition [[Whiteley2016,NINDS1995]].

### lysis · Dose, label and approval
- Activase label: 0.9 mg/kg, not exceeding 90 mg total, with 10% as an initial intravenous bolus over 1 minute and the rest infused over 60 minutes; "as soon as possible but within 3 hours after onset of symptoms" [[FDA-Activase]].
- TNKase label: stroke dose is a single intravenous bolus over 5 seconds, weight-tiered, maximum 25 mg; "as soon as possible and within 3 hours". Tiers: under 60 kg 15 mg; 60 to under 70 kg 17.5 mg; 70 to under 80 kg 20 mg; 80 to under 90 kg 22.5 mg; 90 kg or more 25 mg. The heart attack (STEMI) maximum is 50 mg, so the two dose tables differ [[FDA-TNKase]].
- FDA approval of TNKase for acute ischaemic stroke in adults was reported on 4 March 2025, resting on AcT, and made it Genentech's second approved stroke medicine [[Genentech2025,FDA-TNKase]].
- The label analysed the 0 to 3 hour AcT subset (n=1147, median onset to needle 108 minutes) because the comparator is approved for 0 to 3 hours: mRS 0 to 1, 36.6% versus 35.9% (risk difference 0.7%, 95% CI -4.9 to 6.3). Death 15.0% versus 15.0%, symptomatic intracerebral haemorrhage 3.4% versus 3.1%, orolingual angioedema 1.0% versus 1.4% [[FDA-TNKase]].
- Licence and guideline differ: both US labels say within 3 hours, whereas ESO guidance and the 2026 AHA/ASA guideline as summarised work to 4.5 hours [[FDA-Activase,FDA-TNKase,Berge2021,Musmar2026]].
- Low-dose alteplase (ENCHANTED, 3310 patients, 63% Asian): 0.6 versus 0.9 mg/kg did not meet noninferiority for death or disability (53.2% versus 51.1%; OR 1.09, 95% CI 0.95 to 1.25). Major symptomatic haemorrhage was 1.0% versus 2.1% (P=0.01) [[Anderson2016]].

### lysis · Tenecteplase versus alteplase
- EXTEND-IA TNK (202 patients with large-vessel occlusion before thrombectomy, tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg): reperfusion over 50% of territory at first angiogram 22% versus 10% (P=0.03 for superiority). Median 90-day mRS 2 versus 3 (common OR 1.7, 1.0 to 2.8). Symptomatic haemorrhage 1% in each group [[Campbell2018]].
- EXTEND-IA TNK part 2 (300 patients): 0.40 versus 0.25 mg/kg gave identical reperfusion (19.3% versus 19.3%). Symptomatic haemorrhage was 4.7% versus 1.3% [[Campbell2020JAMA]].
- AcT (Canada, 1577 in analysis, median age 74, 47.9% female): mRS 0 to 1, 36.9% versus 34.8% (risk difference 2.1%, 95% CI -2.6 to 6.9, meeting noninferiority). 24-hour symptomatic haemorrhage 3.4% versus 3.2%. 90-day death 15.3% versus 15.4% [[Menon2022]].
- TRACE-2 (China, 1430 enrolled, NIHSS 5 to 25, not eligible for thrombectomy): mRS 0 to 1, 62% versus 58% (RR 1.07, 0.98 to 1.16, noninferior). Symptomatic haemorrhage 2% versus 2%. Mortality 7% versus 5% [[Wang2023Lancet]].
- ATTEST-2 (UK, 39 centres, 1777 analysed, median NIHSS 7): noninferior but not superior on mRS distribution (OR 1.07, 0.90 to 1.27; p=0.43 for superiority). Deaths 8% versus 8%, symptomatic haemorrhage 2% versus 2%, parenchymal haematoma type 2 4% versus 3%, angioedema 1% in both [[Muir2024]].
- ORIGINAL (China, 1465 analysed, NIHSS 1 to 25): mRS 0 to 1, 72.7% versus 70.3% (RR 1.03, 0.97 to 1.09, noninferior). Symptomatic haemorrhage 1.2% each. 90-day mortality 4.6% versus 5.8% [[Meng2024]].
- Why the absolute rates differ so much (36.9% in AcT and 72.7% in ORIGINAL): the populations differ, with AcT at median age 74 and ORIGINAL allowing NIHSS from 1. The within-trial comparison is what counts [[Menon2022,Meng2024]].
- ESO 2023: tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg "can be used as a safe and effective alternative" under 4.5 hours (moderate evidence, strong recommendation); against 0.40 mg/kg (low evidence, strong); tenecteplase over alteplase in large-vessel occlusion under 4.5 hours (moderate evidence, strong) [[Alamowitch2023]].
- Position difference: ATTEST-2 authors conclude tenecteplase "should be preferred"; the 2026 AHA/ASA guideline as summarised endorses either agent; a pharmacy review describes it as "equally preferred" [[Muir2024,Musmar2026,HelixTalk2026]].

### lysis · Extended and unknown-onset windows
- WAKE-UP (unknown onset, DWI-FLAIR mismatch, thrombectomy excluded; 503 of a planned 800, stopped when funding ended): mRS 0 to 1 at 90 days 53.3% versus 41.8% (adjusted OR 1.61, 1.09 to 2.36; P=0.02). Deaths 4.1% versus 1.2%; symptomatic haemorrhage 2.0% versus 0.4% [[Thomalla2018]].
- EXTEND (perfusion selected, 4.5 to 9 hours or wake-up; 225 of a planned 310): mRS 0 to 1, 35.4% versus 29.5% (adjusted RR 1.44, 1.01 to 2.06; P=0.04). Symptomatic haemorrhage 6.2% versus 0.9% [[Ma2019]].
- Individual patient meta-analysis (WAKE-UP, EXTEND, THAWS, ECASS-4; 843 patients): mRS 0 to 1, 47% versus 39% (adjusted OR 1.49, 1.10 to 2.03). Death 6% versus 3% (adjusted OR 2.06, 1.03 to 4.09). mRS 4 to 6, 21% versus 25%. Symptomatic haemorrhage 3% versus under 1% (adjusted OR 5.58, 1.22 to 25.50) [[Thomalla2020]].
- TIMELESS (458 patients, MCA or ICA occlusion, 4.5 to 24 hours, 77.3% had thrombectomy): neutral (common OR 1.13, 0.82 to 1.57; P=0.45). Mortality 19.7% versus 18.2%; symptomatic haemorrhage 3.2% versus 2.3% [[Albers2024]].
- TRACE-III (China, 516 patients, large-vessel occlusion, no access to thrombectomy, under 2% rescued): mRS 0 to 1, 33.0% versus 24.2% (relative rate 1.37, 1.04 to 1.81). Mortality 13.3% versus 13.1%. Symptomatic haemorrhage 3.0% versus 0.8% [[Xiong2024]].
- CHABLIS-T II (224 patients; primary outcome major reperfusion over 50% of territory without symptomatic haemorrhage): 33.3% versus 10.8% (adjusted RR 3.0, 1.6 to 5.7), with no change in 90-day clinical outcomes [[Cheng2025]].
- OPTION (China, 566 patients, non-large-vessel occlusion, 4.5 to 24 hours): mRS 0 to 1, 43.6% versus 34.2% (RR 1.28, 1.04 to 1.57). Symptomatic haemorrhage 2.8% versus 0%. Mortality 5.0% versus 3.2% [[Ma2026]].
- HOPE (China, 372 patients, perfusion selected, alteplase, 4.5 to 24 hours): mRS 0 to 1, 40% versus 26% (adjusted RR 1.52, 1.14 to 2.02). Symptomatic haemorrhage 3.8% versus 0.51%. Mortality 11% each. The paper labels mRS 0 to 1 "functional independence", whereas most trials use 0 to 2 for that term [[Zhou2025]].
- EXPECTS (China, 234 patients, posterior circulation, median NIHSS 3, alteplase): mRS 0 to 2, 89.6% versus 72.6% (adjusted RR 1.16, 1.03 to 1.30). Symptomatic haemorrhage 1.7% versus 0.9%. Death 5.2% versus 8.5% [[Yan2025]].
- Meta-analysis of tenecteplase at 4.5 to 24 hours (4 RCTs, 1278 patients): mRS 0 to 1 OR 1.34 (1.06 to 1.71). The benefit held in the non-thrombectomy subgroup (OR 1.46, 1.02 to 2.08), while the thrombectomy-permitted subgroup improved only on recanalisation [[Wang2026]].
- Pattern worth teaching: where thrombectomy is available and used (TIMELESS), adding a late lytic did not help; where it is not (TRACE-III), it did. (Early evidence) [[Albers2024,Xiong2024,Wang2026]].
- ESO 2021 recommends alteplase for people who wake with stroke, last seen well more than 4.5 hours earlier, with DWI-FLAIR mismatch and no planned thrombectomy (high quality evidence). ESO 2023 recommends against tenecteplase in wake-up or unknown-onset stroke selected by non-contrast CT alone (low evidence, strong) [[Berge2021,Alamowitch2023]].

### lysis · Minor stroke
- PRISMS (NIHSS 0 to 5 and not clearly disabling, within 3 hours, stopped early at 313 of 948): mRS 0 to 1, 78.2% alteplase versus 81.5% aspirin (adjusted risk difference -1.1%, -9.4 to 7.3). Symptomatic haemorrhage 3.2% versus 0 [[Khatri2018]].
- ARAMIS (China, 760 patients, within 4.5 hours, NIHSS 5 or less): dual antiplatelet therapy (clopidogrel 300 mg then 75 mg daily, aspirin 100 mg daily, 12 ± 2 days) was noninferior to alteplase: mRS 0 to 1, 93.8% versus 91.4% (risk difference 2.3%, -1.5 to 6.2). Symptomatic haemorrhage 0.3% versus 0.9% [[Chen2023]].
- TEMPO-2 (886 patients, NIHSS 0 to 5 with proven occlusion or perfusion abnormality, within 12 hours, stopped for futility): return to baseline 75% control versus 72% tenecteplase (RR 0.96, 0.88 to 1.04). Deaths 1% versus 5% (adjusted HR 3.8, 1.4 to 10.2). Symptomatic haemorrhage under 1% versus 2% [[Coutts2024]].
- The 2026 AHA/ASA guideline as summarised: thrombolysis should not be given for mild non-disabling symptoms, where dual antiplatelet therapy is preferred. "Non-disabling" means not impairing daily activities or return to work. Faster treatment is stated to help disabling deficits regardless of NIHSS [[GuidelineCentral2026,HelixTalk2026,Musmar2026]].
- A pharmacy review reports dual antiplatelet criteria widening to NIHSS 4 or 5 (previously 3 or less) and starting up to 72 hours (previously 24) (secondary source) [[HelixTalk2026]].

### lysis · Adjuncts
- MOST (514 patients, thrombolysis within 3 hours): utility-weighted mRS 5.2 with argatroban, 6.3 with eptifibatide and 6.8 with placebo. Mortality 24%, 12% and 8%. Symptomatic haemorrhage 4%, 3% and 2%. Neither adjunct reduced disability [[Adeoye2024]].
- ASSET-IT (China, 832 patients, non-cardioembolic, not thrombectomy candidates): 24-hour tirofiban within 60 minutes after thrombolysis gave mRS 0 to 1 of 65.9% versus 54.9% (RR 1.20, 1.07 to 1.34). Symptomatic haemorrhage 1.7% versus 0. Mortality 4.1% versus 3.8% (Early evidence) [[Tao2025]].
- Disagreement: MOST and ASSET-IT reach opposite conclusions on glycoprotein IIb/IIIa adjuncts, with different drugs, populations and countries. The 2026 AHA/ASA guideline, whose literature closed by March 2025, is summarised as not recommending argatroban or eptifibatide [[Adeoye2024,Tao2025,Prabhakaran2026,GuidelineCentral2026]].

### lysis · Eligibility, contraindications and blood pressure
- Activase contraindications for stroke: current intracranial haemorrhage; subarachnoid haemorrhage; active internal bleeding; intracranial or intraspinal surgery or serious head trauma within 3 months; intracranial conditions that raise bleeding risk (some neoplasms, arteriovenous malformations, aneurysms); bleeding diathesis; current severe uncontrolled hypertension [[FDA-Activase]].
- TNKase contraindications for stroke: active internal bleeding; intracranial or intraspinal surgery or trauma within 2 months; known bleeding diathesis; current severe uncontrolled hypertension; intracranial conditions that raise bleeding risk; active intracranial haemorrhage. The surgery and trauma window (2 months versus 3 months) differs between the labels [[FDA-TNKase,FDA-Activase]].
- Both labels list raised-risk situations to weigh, including recent major surgery, recent gastrointestinal or genitourinary bleeding, recent trauma, and blood pressure above 175 systolic or 110 diastolic [[FDA-Activase,FDA-TNKase]].
- Coagulation: without recent oral anticoagulant or heparin use, treatment can start before coagulation results; the label says to discontinue if pretreatment INR is above 1.7 or aPTT is elevated [[FDA-Activase]].
- Both labels limit stroke use to facilities with timely access to evaluation and management of intracranial haemorrhage [[FDA-Activase,FDA-TNKase]].
- ESO 2025 blood pressure update: below 185/110 mmHg before the thrombolysis bolus, and below 180/105 mmHg during and for 24 hours after thrombolysis (low certainty) and/or thrombectomy (moderate certainty) [[Sandset2026]].
- These thresholds originate from early non-randomised pilot studies and the NINDS protocol, not from trials testing the thresholds [[Sandset2026]].
- ENCHANTED (2196 patients): intensive target of systolic 130 to 140 versus below 180 mmHg, mean achieved 144.3 versus 149.8. No difference in mRS (OR 1.01, 0.87 to 1.17). Any intracranial haemorrhage 14.8% versus 18.7% (OR 0.75, 0.60 to 0.94) [[Anderson2019]].
- Nursing hooks from the labels: frequently monitor and control blood pressure during and after treatment; avoid intramuscular injections and non-essential trauma; do arterial and venous punctures only as required; avoid internal jugular and subclavian punctures; compress an arterial puncture for at least 30 minutes [[FDA-Activase,FDA-TNKase]].

### lysis · Recent direct oral anticoagulant: the controversy
- Meinel 2023 states that international guidelines recommend avoiding thrombolysis after recent direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) intake [[Meinel2023]].
- Meinel 2023 (retrospective, 64 centres, 33,207 patients, 832 with DOAC ingestion within 48 hours): symptomatic haemorrhage 2.5% (1.6 to 3.8) with DOAC versus 4.1% (3.9 to 4.4) without anticoagulation (adjusted OR 0.57, 0.36 to 0.92) [[Meinel2023]].
- Selection in that cohort: 30.3% had reversal (all idarucizumab), 27.0% had DOAC levels measured and 42.7% had neither [[Meinel2023]].
- Single-centre target-trial emulation (98 patients): thrombolysis regardless of level or timing, no reversal; symptomatic haemorrhage 0 of 49 versus 2 of 49 [[Bucke2024]].
- Status: both are observational and selection bias is acknowledged. Bücke and colleagues call for randomised trials (Early evidence) [[Meinel2023,Bucke2024]].

### lysis · Sex and age
- Meta-analysis of 18 studies: women had 30% lower odds of receiving rt-PA (OR 0.70, 0.55 to 0.88). Among those arriving within 3 hours without contraindications, the difference was not significant (OR 0.81, 0.58 to 1.13) [[Reeves2009]].
- Asian registries (4453 patients): women had a worse mRS shift (adjusted OR 1.14, 1.02 to 1.28), with no significant difference in death or symptomatic haemorrhage, and the gap widened with age [[Wang2021IJS]].
- French tenecteplase cohort (1339 patients): no difference in good outcome (adjusted OR 0.85, 0.63 to 1.13), less symptomatic haemorrhage in women (adjusted OR 0.35, 0.18 to 0.69), and more unknown-onset stroke in women (adjusted OR 1.44, 1.07 to 1.95) [[Yger2025]].
- Age above 80: pooled RCTs show mRS 0 to 1 of 19.1% versus 13.1% (P=0.0109), with mortality 29.5% versus 30.2% [[Bluhmki2020]].
- Stroke Thrombolysis Trialists: at 80 or under, 39% versus 34% (OR 1.25, 1.10 to 1.42); above 80, 18% versus 13% (OR 1.56, 1.17 to 2.08). Age did not change the effect (p=0.53) [[Emberson2014]].

### lysis · Complications: angioedema and haemorrhage management
- Orolingual angioedema in a UK centre: 42 of 530 patients (7.9%, 95% CI 5.5 to 10.6), 5 to 189 minutes after starting tPA (median 65). 12% of cases were severe, needing urgent advanced airway care. Prior ACE inhibitor use was the only independent predictor (OR 2.3, 1.1 to 4.7) [[Hurford2015]].
- Disagreement on rate: Hurford found 7.9% in consecutive patients at one centre, with retrospective grading; trial and label figures are near 1% (1.0% to 1.4% in the label subset, 1% in ATTEST-2) [[Hurford2015,FDA-TNKase,Muir2024]].
- Activase label: angioedema seen during and up to 2 hours after infusion, in many cases with an ACE inhibitor. TNKase label: if hypersensitivity occurs, start therapy such as antihistamines, corticosteroids or epinephrine [[FDA-Activase,FDA-TNKase]].
- Case report: tenecteplase-related angioedema progressed through dexamethasone, diphenhydramine, famotidine and C1 esterase inhibitor to intubation, with extubation after 30 hours. The authors state no single agent or algorithm has shown significant efficacy [[Pitts2024]].
- AHA/ASA statement on symptomatic haemorrhage: treatment rests on expert opinion and small case series; prompt diagnosis and early correction of the coagulopathy remain the mainstay [[Yaghi2017]].
- A Neurocritical Care Society and Society of Critical Care Medicine guideline covers reversal of thrombolytics in intracranial haemorrhage [[Frontera2016]].

### lysis · What the 2026 guideline changed
- Citation verified: Prabhakaran S, Gonzalez NR, Zachrison KS, et al., Stroke 2026;57(8):e316-e436. It replaces the 2018 guideline and 2019 update [[Prabhakaran2026,Powers2019]].
- Stated key updates: thrombolytic choice and eligibility, eligibility for thrombectomy, hyperglycaemia and dysphagia, a focused paediatric section, and "modification of the approach to thrombolysis contraindications". Literature search September to December 2024, with high-impact additions to March 2025 [[Prabhakaran2026]].
- So trials published after March 2025 are unlikely to be reflected, for example OPTION, HOPE, ASSET-IT and LATE-MT by date. This is an inference from the search dates [[Prabhakaran2026,Ma2026,Zhou2025,Tao2025,Yang2026]].
- As summarised by secondary sources (not read in the original): tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg up to 25 mg or alteplase within 4.5 hours; 0.4 mg/kg not recommended; no delay for CTA, MRA or perfusion imaging within 4.5 hours; imaging-selected thrombolysis for unknown onset or 4.5 to 9 hours [[Musmar2026,GuidelineCentral2026]].
- Secondary sources disagree on the outer limit of extended-window thrombolysis in the guideline; those read here give 9 hours. The original should be checked before stating a limit [[Musmar2026,HelixTalk2026]].
- Blood pressure as summarised: intensive systolic lowering below 140 mmHg does not improve function after thrombolysis, and is harmful or not recommended after successful thrombectomy [[Musmar2026,GuidelineCentral2026]].
- Other items as summarised: tight glucose control at 80 to 130 mg/dL not recommended; mobile stroke units recommended where available; direct transport to the closest thrombectomy-capable hospital endorsed where rapid transfer systems are not established, where previously the nearest thrombolysis-capable facility was recommended [[GuidelineCentral2026,Chauhan2026]].
- A correction has been issued, and a separate group of authors published "Proposed Corrections" in Stroke in 2026. Their content was not available to this pass [[Prabhakaran2026Correction,Albers2026]].
- Radiology-focused review: the guideline expands guidance on medium-vessel occlusion and adds paediatric recommendations [[Rai2026]].
- Grotta's 2026 NEJM review frames the new directions: vascular targets for lysis versus thrombectomy, tenecteplase replacing alteplase, broader indications and ancillary drugs [[Grotta2026]].

### evt · HERMES and the five 2015 trials
- HERMES (5 trials, 1287 patients, 634 versus 653): adjusted common OR for reduced disability 2.49 (95% CI 1.76 to 3.53). NNT 2.6 to reduce disability by at least one mRS level for one patient [[Goyal2016]].
- HERMES subgroups: age 80 or over cOR 3.68 (1.95 to 6.92); randomised after 300 minutes 1.76 (1.05 to 2.97); not eligible for alteplase 2.43 (1.30 to 4.55). No heterogeneity overall (p interaction=0.43). 90-day mortality and symptomatic haemorrhage did not differ [[Goyal2016]].
- MR CLEAN (Netherlands, 500 patients, within 6 hours, 89.0% had alteplase, 81.5% stent retrievers): cOR 1.67 (1.21 to 2.30); mRS 0 to 2, 32.6% versus 19.1% (difference 13.5 points, 5.9 to 21.2) [[Berkhemer2015]].
- ESCAPE (316 patients, up to 12 hours, small core and good collaterals, stopped early, CT to reperfusion median 84 minutes): cOR 2.6 (1.7 to 3.8); mRS 0 to 2, 53.0% versus 29.3%; mortality 10.4% versus 19.0% (P=0.04); symptomatic haemorrhage 3.6% versus 2.7% [[Goyal2015]].
- EXTEND-IA (70 patients, stopped early, core under 70 ml on CT perfusion): reperfusion at 24 hours median 100% versus 37%; early improvement 80% versus 37%; mRS 0 to 2, 71% versus 40% [[Campbell2015]].
- SWIFT PRIME (196 patients, within 6 hours, stopped early, imaging to puncture median 57 minutes, 88% substantial reperfusion): mRS 0 to 2, 60% versus 35%; mortality 9% versus 12%; symptomatic haemorrhage 0% versus 3% [[Saver2015]].
- REVASCAT (206 patients, within 8 hours): adjusted OR for 1-point improvement 1.7 (1.05 to 2.8); mRS 0 to 2, 43.7% versus 28.2%; symptomatic haemorrhage 1.9% each; death 18.4% versus 15.5% [[Jovin2015]].
- Derived by this pass, not stated by the sources, NNT for one more person at mRS 0 to 2 (1 divided by the absolute difference): MR CLEAN about 7, REVASCAT about 6.5, ESCAPE about 4, SWIFT PRIME 4, EXTEND-IA about 3, DAWN about 3, DEFUSE 3 about 3.6 [[Berkhemer2015,Jovin2015,Goyal2015,Saver2015,Campbell2015,Nogueira2018,Albers2018]].

### evt · Late window
- DAWN (206 patients, 6 to 24 hours, clinical-core mismatch by age under or over 80): utility-weighted mRS 5.5 versus 3.4 (adjusted difference 2.0, 1.1 to 3.0); mRS 0 to 2, 49% versus 13% (adjusted difference 33 points, 24 to 44); symptomatic haemorrhage 6% versus 3%; mortality 19% versus 18% [[Nogueira2018]].
- DEFUSE 3 (182 patients, 6 to 16 hours, core under 70 ml, mismatch ratio 1.8 or more, 38 US centres): common OR 2.77 (1.63 to 4.70); median mRS 3 (IQR 1 to 4) versus 4 (3 to 6); mRS 0 to 2, 45% versus 17%; mortality 14% versus 26% (P=0.05); symptomatic haemorrhage 7% versus 4% [[Albers2018]].
- DEFUSE 3 reperfusion above 90% at 24 hours was 79% versus 18% [[Albers2018]].
- LATE-MT (China, 336 patients, 24 to 72 hours, median 38.5 hours, salvageable tissue on imaging): common OR 1.74 (1.19 to 2.55). Symptomatic haemorrhage 4.8% versus 0. Deaths 6.0% versus 1.8%. (Early evidence, a single trial, not yet reflected in the 2026 guideline) [[Yang2026]].

### evt · Time to reperfusion
- HERMES timing: median onset to randomisation 196 minutes, to puncture 238, to reperfusion 286. Mean 90-day mRS 2.9 versus 3.6 [[Saver2016]].
- Benefit by onset to puncture: 3 hours cOR 2.79 (1.96 to 3.98), absolute risk difference for lower disability 39.2%; 6 hours cOR 1.98 (1.30 to 3.00), 30.2%; 8 hours cOR 1.57 (0.86 to 2.88), 15.7%. Significance held through 7 hours 18 minutes [[Saver2016]].
- Among 390 with substantial reperfusion, each 1-hour delay gave worse disability (cOR 0.84, 0.76 to 0.93; absolute difference -6.7%) and less functional independence (OR 0.81, 0.71 to 0.92; -5.2%), with no change in mortality (OR 1.12, 0.93 to 1.34) [[Saver2016]].
- Real-world (6756 patients, Get With The Guidelines, 2015 to 2016): median onset to puncture 230 minutes, door to puncture 87, substantial reperfusion 85.9%, symptomatic haemorrhage 6.7%, in-hospital death or hospice 19.6%, mRS 0 to 2 at discharge 23.0% [[Jahan2019]].
- Per 15 minutes faster in the 30 to 270 minute range: independent walking +1.14% (0.75 to 1.53), death or hospice -0.77% (-1.07 to -0.47), symptomatic haemorrhage -0.22% (-0.40 to -0.03) [[Jahan2019]].
- Modelling: each minute saved gave 4.2 days of extra healthy life (95% prediction interval 2.3 to 5.4); 20 minutes equalled about 3 months of disability-free life [[Meretoja2017]].
- Tension to teach: early-window results are strongly time-dependent, yet DAWN and DEFUSE 3 show large effects late in people selected by imaging. Selection for slow progressors is the usual reconciliation (Hypothesis, not tested by these abstracts) [[Saver2016,Nogueira2018,Albers2018]].

### evt · Large core
- RESCUE-Japan LIMIT (203 patients, ASPECTS 3 to 5): mRS 0 to 3, 31.0% versus 12.7% (RR 2.43, 1.35 to 4.37). Any intracranial haemorrhage 58.0% versus 31.4% [[Yoshimura2022]].
- SELECT2 (352 patients, ASPECTS 3 to 5 or core 50 ml or more, within 24 hours): generalised OR 1.51 (1.20 to 1.89); mRS 0 to 2, 20% versus 7% (RR 2.97, 1.60 to 5.51). Access-site complications 5, dissection 10, perforation 7, vasospasm 11. Symptomatic haemorrhage 1 versus 2 patients [[Sarraj2023]].
- SELECT2 at 1 year: generalised OR 1.43 (1.14 to 1.78); died 45% versus 52% (RR 0.89, 0.71 to 1.11) [[Sarraj2024]].
- ANGEL-ASPECT (China, 456 patients, ASPECTS 3 to 5 or core 70 to 100 ml): generalised OR 1.37 (1.11 to 1.69). Symptomatic haemorrhage 6.1% versus 2.7%; any haemorrhage 49.1% versus 17.3% [[Huo2023]].
- TENSION (253 patients, ASPECTS 3 to 5, up to 12 hours, mainly non-contrast CT): common OR 2.58 (1.60 to 4.15); mortality HR 0.67 (0.46 to 0.98); symptomatic haemorrhage 6% versus 5%. At 12 months common OR 2.39 (1.47 to 3.90), with survival HR 0.70 (0.50 to 0.99) [[Bendszus2023,Thomalla2024]].
- LASTE (333 patients, ASPECTS 5 or less, within 6.5 hours, unrestricted size): median mRS 4 versus 6 (generalised OR 1.63, 1.29 to 2.06). Death 36.1% versus 55.5% (adjusted RR 0.65, 0.50 to 0.84). Symptomatic haemorrhage 9.6% versus 5.7% [[Costalat2024]].
- TESLA (US, 300 patients, ASPECTS 2 to 5, non-contrast CT, within 24 hours): utility-weighted mRS 2.93 versus 2.27 (adjusted difference 0.63, 95% credible interval -0.09 to 1.34; posterior probability 0.96). This did not demonstrate improvement, and the interval includes both no effect and clinically relevant benefit. Mortality 35.3% versus 33.3%; symptomatic haemorrhage 4.0% versus 1.3% [[Yoo2024]].
- Pooled patient-level data (6 trials, 1886 patients): median mRS 4 versus 5 (adjusted generalised OR 1.63, 1.42 to 1.88). Mortality 31.1% versus 37.3% (adjusted RR 0.82, 0.70 to 0.97). Symptomatic haemorrhage 1.1% versus 1.0%. A core laboratory readjudicated ASPECTS and core volume. Evidence remains limited for core 150 ml or more beyond 6 hours [[Sarraj2026]].
- Disagreement on bleeding: single trials report symptomatic haemorrhage from 1 patient to 9.6%, while the pooled analysis gives 1.1%. The abstracts do not explain the gap. Any haemorrhage was much higher with thrombectomy in ANGEL-ASPECT and RESCUE-Japan LIMIT [[Sarraj2023,Costalat2024,Sarraj2026,Huo2023,Yoshimura2022]].
- SVIN 2025: ASPECTS 0 to 5 at 0 to 6 hours (age 18 to 80, mRS 0 to 1) class 1, level A; ASPECTS 3 to 5 at 6 to 24 hours class 1, level A; ASPECTS 0 to 2 at 6 to 24 hours "uncertain", class 2b, level B-R [[Mokin2025]].
- 2026 AHA/ASA as summarised: within 6 hours ASPECTS 3 to 10; at 6 to 24 hours ASPECTS 3 to 5 with age under 80, NIHSS 6 or more, mRS 0 to 1, no significant mass effect; within 6 hours ASPECTS 0 to 2 "reasonable" in selected patients; prestroke mRS 2 "reasonable" in selected patients [[Musmar2026]].

### evt · Basilar occlusion
- ATTENTION (China, 340 patients, within 12 hours): mRS 0 to 3, 46% versus 23% (adjusted rate ratio 2.06, 1.46 to 2.91). Mortality 37% versus 55% (adjusted RR 0.66, 0.52 to 0.82). Symptomatic haemorrhage 5% versus 0. Procedural complications 14% [[Tao2022]].
- BAOCHE (China, 217 patients, 6 to 24 hours, median 663 minutes): mRS 0 to 3, 46% versus 24% (adjusted rate ratio 1.81, 1.26 to 2.60). The primary outcome was changed from mRS 0 to 4, which gave 55% versus 43% (1.21, 0.95 to 1.54). Mortality 31% versus 42%. Symptomatic haemorrhage 6% versus 1%. Procedural complications 11% [[Jovin2022]].
- Basilar trials use mRS 0 to 3 as "good", reflecting how severe the condition is, unlike the mRS 0 to 2 used in anterior trials [[Tao2022,Jovin2022]].
- 2026 AHA/ASA as summarised: within 24 hours with mRS 0 to 1, NIHSS 10 or more and PC-ASPECTS 6 or more; not well established for NIHSS 6 to 9 [[Musmar2026,GuidelineCentral2026]].
- ATTENTION LATE (China, 330 patients, NIHSS 10 or more, 4.5 to 24 hours at thrombectomy-capable centres): tenecteplase before thrombectomy gave no gain in functional independence (30% versus 30%; adjusted rate ratio 0.92, 0.67 to 1.25) [[Li2026JAMA]].

### evt · Medium and distal vessels
- ESCAPE-MeVO (530 patients, within 12 hours, 84.7% MCA branch): mRS 0 to 1, 41.6% versus 43.1% (adjusted rate ratio 0.95, 0.79 to 1.15). Mortality 13.3% versus 8.4% (adjusted HR 1.82, 1.06 to 3.12). Symptomatic haemorrhage 5.4% versus 2.2% [[Goyal2025]].
- DISTAL (543 participants, within 24 hours, median NIHSS 6, 65.4% had thrombolysis): common OR 0.90 (0.67 to 1.22). Mortality 15.5% versus 14.0%. Symptomatic haemorrhage 5.9% versus 2.6% [[Psychogios2025]].

### evt · Bridging or direct to thrombectomy
- DIRECT-MT (China, 656 patients): thrombectomy alone noninferior within a 20% margin (adjusted common OR 1.07, 0.81 to 1.40; P=0.04). Reperfusion before thrombectomy 2.4% versus 7.0%; overall 79.4% versus 84.5%. Mortality 17.7% versus 18.8% [[Yang2020]].
- DEVT (China, 234 of a planned 970, stopped early): mRS 0 to 2, 54.3% alone versus 46.6% combined (noninferiority P=.003). Symptomatic haemorrhage 6.1% versus 6.8% [[Zi2021]].
- SKIP (Japan, 204 patients, alteplase 0.6 mg/kg): mRS 0 to 2, 59.4% versus 57.3%, failing noninferiority (P=.18). Any haemorrhage 33.7% versus 50.5% [[Suzuki2021]].
- MR CLEAN-NO IV (Europe, 539 patients): median mRS 3 versus 2; adjusted common OR 0.84 (0.62 to 1.15), neither superior nor noninferior. Mortality 20.5% versus 15.8% [[LeCouffe2021]].
- SWIFT DIRECT (Europe and Canada, 408 analysed): mRS 0 to 2, 57% versus 65% (adjusted risk difference -7.3%, -16.6 to 2.1), crossing the -12% margin. Reperfusion 91% versus 96% [[Fischer2022]].
- DIRECT-SAFE (Australia, New Zealand, China, Vietnam; 295 patients): functional independence 55% versus 61%; noninferiority not shown [[Mitchell2022]].
- Individual participant meta-analysis (6 trials, 2313 participants): median mRS 3 versus 2 (adjusted common OR 0.89, 0.76 to 1.04); noninferiority not established. Any intracranial haemorrhage less with thrombectomy alone (0.82, 0.68 to 0.99); symptomatic haemorrhage and mortality not significantly different [[Majoie2023]].
- BRIDGE-TNK (China, 550 patients): tenecteplase then thrombectomy gave mRS 0 to 2 of 52.9% versus 44.1% (RR 1.20, 1.01 to 1.43; P=0.04). Symptomatic haemorrhage 8.5% versus 6.7%; mortality 22.3% versus 19.9% [[Qiu2025]].
- ESO 2023 expert consensus: for eligible patients with large-vessel occlusion within 4.5 hours, tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg is preferable to skipping thrombolysis, even after direct admission to a thrombectomy-capable centre [[Alamowitch2023]].

### evt · Adjunct intra-arterial thrombolysis
- CHOICE (Spain, 121 randomised, stopped early): alteplase 0.225 mg/kg after reperfusion gave mRS 0 to 1 of 59.0% versus 40.4% (adjusted risk difference 18.4%, 0.3 to 36.4). Authors call it preliminary (Early evidence) [[Renu2022]].
- POST-TNK (China, 540 patients, eTICI 2c to 3, no prior intravenous lysis, tenecteplase 0.0625 mg/kg): mRS 0 to 1, 49.1% versus 44.1% (adjusted RR 1.15, 0.97 to 1.36; P=.11). Symptomatic haemorrhage 6.3% versus 4.4% [[Huang2025]].
- POST-UK (China, 535 patients, urokinase 100,000 IU): survival without disability 45.1% versus 40.2% (adjusted RR 1.13, 0.94 to 1.36; P=.19). Symptomatic haemorrhage 4.1% each [[Liu2025]].
- ANGEL-TNK (China, 256 patients, 4.5 to 24 hours, eTICI 2b or more, tenecteplase 0.125 mg/kg): mRS 0 to 1, 40.5% versus 26.4% (RR 1.44, 1.06 to 1.95). None of the 7 secondary efficacy endpoints was significant, and authors call for further trials (Early evidence) [[Miao2025]].
- Disagreement: two larger China trials were neutral while CHOICE and ANGEL-TNK were positive. Doses, windows and thresholds differ, so the question remains open [[Renu2022,Huang2025,Liu2025,Miao2025]].

### evt · Anaesthesia
- HERMES analysis (797 procedures, 30% under general anaesthesia, non-randomised choice): outcomes better without general anaesthesia (covariate-adjusted cOR 1.53, 1.14 to 2.04) [[Campbell2018GA]].
- GOLIATH (single centre, 128 patients): reperfusion 76.9% versus 60.3% favouring general anaesthesia; infarct growth not significantly different (8.2 versus 19.4 ml, P=.10); shift to a lower mRS with general anaesthesia (OR 1.91, 1.03 to 3.56) [[Simonsen2018]].
- RCT meta-analysis (7 trials, 980 participants): general anaesthesia improved recanalisation (84.6% versus 75.6%; OR 1.75, 1.26 to 2.42) and functional recovery (44.6% versus 36.2%; OR 1.43, 1.04 to 1.98), with no difference in haemorrhage or mortality [[CampbellD2023]].
- Disagreement: observational pooled data favour avoiding general anaesthesia; randomised data favour it. Confounding by indication is the obvious explanation, since the HERMES choice was not randomised [[Campbell2018GA,CampbellD2023]].

### evt · Blood pressure after reperfusion
- OPTIMAL-BP (South Korea, 305 eligible, stopped early for safety): systolic target below 140 versus 140 to 180 mmHg for 24 hours. mRS 0 to 2, 39.4% versus 54.4% (risk difference -15.1%, -26.2 to -3.9) [[Nam2023]].
- ENCHANTED2/MT (China, 821 randomised, stopped early): below 120 versus 140 to 180 mmHg for 72 hours. Poor outcome common OR 1.37 (1.07 to 1.76); early deterioration 1.53 (1.18 to 1.97); major disability 2.07 (1.47 to 2.93) [[Yang2022]].
- BEST-II (US, 120 patients, phase 2 futility design): below 140 or below 160 versus 180 or less. Mean utility-weighted mRS 0.51, 0.47, 0.58. Infarct volume 32.4, 50.7 and 46.4 ml. Lower targets did not meet futility criteria, but predicted success of a future trial was only 25% and 14% [[Mistry2023]].
- ESO 2025: recommends against intensive lowering below 140 mmHg systolic in the first 24 hours after successful thrombectomy (high certainty) [[Sandset2026]].

### evt · Technique and the TICI score
- ASTER (France, 381 patients): first-line contact aspiration versus stent retriever. Successful revascularisation 85.4% versus 83.1% (OR 1.20, 0.68 to 2.10; P=.53) [[Lapergue2017]].
- COMPASS (270 patients, ASPECTS over 6, within 6 hours): mRS 0 to 2, 52% aspiration versus 50% stent retriever (non-inferiority p=0.0014, margin 0.15). Intracranial haemorrhage 36% versus 34%. Mortality 22% each [[Turk2019]].
- Modified TICI per the 2013 consensus: grade 0 no perfusion; grade 1 antegrade reperfusion past the occlusion but limited distal filling; grade 2a less than half of the territory; grade 2b more than half; grade 3 complete, with no visualised occlusion in distal branches [[Zaidat2013]].
- The consensus settled a discrepancy: 2b had meant more than half (modified) or more than two thirds (original). It chose more than half, and suggested testing a 2c grade (over 90% but less than complete) [[Zaidat2013]].

### evt · Complications
- Narrative review: complications with sequelae in about 15% of thrombectomy patients across RCTs. Listed: access-site problems; vasospasm; arterial perforation and dissection; device detachment; symptomatic haemorrhage; subarachnoid haemorrhage; new-territory embolisation; anaesthetic and contrast effects; pseudoaneurysm [[Balami2018]].
- Trial rates: procedural complications 14% in ATTENTION and 11% in BAOCHE, and 11 procedure-related complications in LASTE [[Tao2022,Jovin2022,Costalat2024]].
- TESLA reported subarachnoid haemorrhage in 16.2% versus 6.2% and parenchymal haematoma type 2 in 9.5% versus 3.4% [[Yoo2024]].

### evt · Access and systems
- MT-GLASS (survey, 887 responses, 67 countries, 2020 to 2021): median global thrombectomy access 2.79% of large-vessel occlusions (IQR 0.70 to 11.74). Under 1.0% in 18 countries (27%) and 0 in 7 (10%). A 460-fold gap between highest and lowest nonzero regions. Low-income countries 88% lower than high-income [[Asif2023]].
- Operators reached 16.5% and centres 20.8% of optimal availability. Access rose with a prehospital bypass protocol (OR 4.00, 1.70 to 9.42) [[Asif2023]].
- Each 5% rise in rural population share had 13% lower odds of access after adjustment (OR 0.87, 0.78 to 0.96) [[Asif2026]].
- ESO-ESMINT: high quality evidence for thrombectomy plus best medical management within 6 hours, and moderate quality evidence at 6 to 24 hours in patients meeting trial criteria [[Turc2023]].

### Exam hooks
- Alteplase dose as published: 0.9 mg/kg, maximum 90 mg, 10% bolus over 1 minute, rest over 60 minutes [[FDA-Activase]].
- Tenecteplase stroke dose as published: 0.25 mg/kg, maximum 25 mg, single bolus; 0.40 mg/kg gave no reperfusion gain [[Menon2022,Campbell2020JAMA,FDA-TNKase]].
- ESO blood pressure thresholds: below 185/110 before and below 180/105 during and for 24 hours after [[Sandset2026]].
- NINDS window 3 hours, ECASS III extended to 4.5 hours [[NINDS1995,Hacke2008]].
- WAKE-UP uses DWI-FLAIR mismatch; EXTEND uses perfusion imaging [[Thomalla2018,Ma2019]].
- Symptomatic haemorrhage after alteplase: the proportional rise is constant, while the absolute rise grows with severity [[Whiteley2016]].
- Non-disabling minor stroke: PRISMS, ARAMIS and TEMPO-2 did not show benefit from a lytic [[Khatri2018,Chen2023,Coutts2024]].
- HERMES NNT 2.6 for at least a one-level mRS improvement [[Goyal2016]].
- DAWN 6 to 24 hours selects by clinical-core mismatch; DEFUSE 3 6 to 16 hours selects by perfusion mismatch [[Nogueira2018,Albers2018]].
- Each hour of delay to reperfusion: functional independence falls 5.2 percentage points [[Saver2016]].
- ESO recommends against intensive systolic lowering below 140 mmHg after successful thrombectomy; in OPTIMAL-BP it reduced functional independence [[Sandset2026,Nam2023]].
- mTICI 2b means more than half of the territory reperfused [[Zaidat2013]].
- Angioedema predictor: prior ACE inhibitor (OR 2.3) [[Hurford2015]].
- Medium-vessel trials ESCAPE-MeVO and DISTAL were neutral on function [[Goyal2025,Psychogios2025]].

### In plain words
- Most strokes happen because a clot blocks a blood vessel in the brain. Reperfusion means getting blood flowing again [[Goyal2016,NINDS1995]].
- A clot-busting medicine is given through a drip or a single injection into a vein. It can only be given to some people, and timing matters [[FDA-Activase,FDA-TNKase]].
- Out of 100 people treated within 3 hours, about 10 more recover with no real disability than if they had not had the medicine [[Emberson2014]].
- The medicine can cause bleeding in the brain. Fatal bleeding goes up by about 2 in 100, yet more people overall come out with no real disability [[Emberson2014,Whiteley2016]].
- Thrombectomy is a procedure where a doctor passes a thin tube up an artery, often from the groin, and pulls out the clot. It is for clots in larger arteries [[Goyal2016,Saver2015,Balami2018]].
- For every 3 or so people who have thrombectomy, about one is left with less disability [[Goyal2016]].
- Every minute counts. Saving one minute before thrombectomy is estimated to give about 4 days of extra healthy life on average [[Meretoja2017]].
- If you do not know when your stroke began, for example if you woke up with it, brain scans can sometimes show whether treatment could still help [[Thomalla2018,Thomalla2020]].
- If symptoms are mild and do not get in the way of daily life, trials found a clot-buster did not help more than tablets [[Chen2023,Khatri2018]].
- A few people get swelling of the lips or tongue after the clot-buster. Staff watch for this because it can affect breathing. It is more likely in people taking ACE inhibitor blood pressure tablets [[Hurford2015]].
- Many people in the world cannot reach thrombectomy. In some countries it is not available at all [[Asif2023]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Tenecteplase or alteplase: ATTEST-2 authors say it should be preferred; AHA/ASA as summarised treats them as alternatives; US labels and the guidelines still differ on the window [[Muir2024,Musmar2026,FDA-TNKase]].
- Extended-window lysis when thrombectomy is available: TIMELESS was neutral, while TRACE-III, OPTION and HOPE were positive in settings with little or no planned thrombectomy. Most positive data come from China, which limits generalisation [[Albers2024,Xiong2024,Ma2026,Zhou2025]].
- Recent DOAC intake: observational data show no excess symptomatic haemorrhage, with selection bias acknowledged, and no randomised answer in the sources read [[Meinel2023,Bucke2024]].
- Bridging: individual participant data could not establish noninferiority of skipping the lytic, and BRIDGE-TNK favours tenecteplase first. Open whether this holds when delay or transfer differ [[Majoie2023,Qiu2025]].
- Very large core and very late presentation: limited evidence at 150 ml or more beyond 6 hours; LATE-MT is the first report at 24 to 72 hours among those read [[Sarraj2026,Yang2026]].
- Adjunct intra-arterial lysis: conflicting trials [[Renu2022,Huang2025,Liu2025,Miao2025]].
- Adjunct antiplatelet with lysis: MOST showed no benefit and more deaths with eptifibatide or argatroban; ASSET-IT showed benefit with tirofiban [[Adeoye2024,Tao2025]].
- Anaesthesia: randomised and observational data point in opposite directions [[CampbellD2023,Campbell2018GA]].
- Medium and distal vessels: neutral trials despite a plausible rationale; the 2026 guideline has "expanded guidance", whose wording was not read [[Goyal2025,Psychogios2025,Rai2026]].
- 2026 AHA/ASA guideline accuracy: a correction exists and outside authors proposed further corrections [[Prabhakaran2026Correction,Albers2026]].

- Validation: journal records were fetched from Europe PMC (core result type) on 2026-11-08, and every key, PMID, PMCID and DOI on the source lines was generated from those records by script. PubMed esummary returned HTTP 429 during this pass, so Europe PMC is the named service.
- Labels were fetched from openFDA by set_id. A second TNKase set_id (739a3c53-caf6-40d6-a6af-84ce733a948b, also dated 2025-12-15) still shows a STEMI-only indication; the stroke label cited is e647640d-c395-4b4b-a0be-1162f9c21d84. - Could not access: full text of Prabhakaran2026 (HTTP 403 at ahajournals.org and professional.heart.org). It is therefore cited directly only for what its abstract says. Class of recommendation, level of evidence, the contraindication table and its DOAC wording are not recorded here because they were not read. A later reader with access should add them.
