## Findings

### unit · Stroke unit care (the foundation)
- The 2020 Cochrane review included 29 trials (5902 participants) comparing organised inpatient stroke unit care with an alternative service; 20 of them (4127 participants) compared it with a general ward [[Langhorne2020]].
- At the end of scheduled follow-up (median one year), stroke unit care was associated with less poor outcome (OR 0.77, 95% CI 0.69 to 0.87), death (OR 0.76, 0.66 to 0.88), death or institutional care (OR 0.76, 0.67 to 0.85) and death or dependency (OR 0.75, 0.66 to 0.85), all moderate-quality evidence [[Langhorne2020]].
- In absolute terms, for every 100 people receiving stroke unit care there were two extra survivors, six more living at home and six more living independently [[Langhorne2020]].
- Poor outcome in the review means death, dependency (Rankin 3 to 5) or requiring institutional care [[Langhorne2020]].
- Outcomes appeared independent of age, sex, initial stroke severity, stroke type and duration of follow-up [[Langhorne2020]].
- There was no indication of a longer hospital stay, but length of stay was measured inconsistently (I² = 85%) [[Langhorne2020]].
- The model of care matters. In the network meta-analysis against a general ward, the odds of poor outcome were: stroke ward OR 0.74 (0.62 to 0.89, moderate quality), mixed rehabilitation ward OR 0.70 (0.52 to 0.95, low quality), mobile stroke team OR 0.88 (0.58 to 1.34, low quality) [[Langhorne2020]].
- Benefits were most obvious in units based in a discrete stroke ward [[Langhorne2020]].
- In a national audit in England and Wales of 143,578 people with acute stroke in 154 hospitals (2013 to 2015), 14.4% died within 30 days [[Paley2018]].
- Admission to hospitals with higher ratios of nurses trained in swallow screening was associated with reduced odds of death (P=0.004) [[Paley2018]].
- Daily physician ward rounds were associated with 10% lower odds of mortality than less frequent rounds (95% CI 0.82 to 0.98) [[Paley2018]].
- Number of admissions and the overall weekend registered-nurse ratio were not independently associated with mortality [[Paley2018]].
- The authors estimated, if the associations are causal, an extra 1332 deaths saved each year in England and Wales with at least 3 swallow-trained nurses per 10 beds and daily stroke physician rounds [[Paley2018]].

### unit · Complications: how often and when
- In a three-site cohort, 265 (85%) people with stroke had a complication during admission [[Langhorne2000]].
- Rates in that cohort: urinary tract infection 24%, chest infection 22%, falls 25%, falls with serious injury 5%, pressure sores 21%, shoulder pain 9%, other pain 34%, depression 16%, anxiety 14%, emotionalism 12%, confusion 56%, recurrent stroke 9%, epileptic seizure 3%, deep vein thrombosis 2%, pulmonary embolism 1% [[Langhorne2000]].
- In a comprehensive stroke unit with early supported discharge, 312 of 489 people (63.8%) had one or more complications in the first week. The commonest were pain 23.9%, temperature of 38 degrees C or more 23.7%, progressing stroke 18.4%, urinary tract infection 16.0%, chest infection 11.2% and nonserious falls 7.4% [[Indredavik2008]].
- Most of those complications occurred in the first 4 days, and stroke severity was the most important risk factor [[Indredavik2008]].
- At 3 months, 201 of 244 people (82.4%) had at least one complication, led by pain (53.3%), urinary tract infection (27.9%) and nonserious falls (25.0%) [[Indredavik2008]].
- Complications differ in how much they matter. Recurrent stroke (OR 7.45, 2.83 to 20.96) and chest infection (OR 3.28, 1.16 to 9.29) were strongly tied to worse 90-day outcome; other infections (OR 1.59) and falls (OR 1.43) less so; myocardial infarction, urinary tract infection and pain showed no association [[Rohweder2015]].
- In inpatient rehabilitation, 75% of people had at least one medical complication and 19% had one requiring transfer to an acute facility [[Roth2001]].
- Disagreement: pressure sores were reported in 21% of inpatients in a 2000 cohort [[Langhorne2000]], in 2.5% or fewer in the first week of a 2008 comprehensive stroke unit [[Indredavik2008]], and in 22% over 12 months after severely disabling stroke [[Sackley2008]]. The populations and time frames differ, so these are not one rate.

### unit · The FeSS bundle: QASC
- QASC was a single-blind cluster randomised trial in 19 acute stroke units in New South Wales (10 intervention, 9 control), with 1696 patients analysed (687 before and 1009 after the intervention) [[Middleton2011]].
- Intervention units received protocols for fever, hyperglycaemia and swallowing dysfunction plus multidisciplinary team-building workshops. Control units received an abridged version of existing guidelines [[Middleton2011]].
- At 90 days, death or dependency (mRS 2 or more) was 42% (236 of 558) in intervention units versus 58% (259 of 449) in control units (p=0.002; number needed to treat 6.4; adjusted absolute difference 15.7%, 95% CI 5.8 to 25.4) [[Middleton2011]].
- SF-36 physical summary was better (45.6 vs 42.5, p=0.002). Mortality (4% vs 5%, p=0.36), SF-36 mental summary and Barthel index were not different at 90 days [[Middleton2011]].
- At a median of 4.1 years in 1076 participants, 264 (24.5%) had died. The survival gain was above 20% but significant only after adjustment: unadjusted HR 0.79 (0.58 to 1.07, P=0.13), adjusted HR 0.77 (0.59 to 0.99, P=0.045) [[Middleton2017]].
- Summarised FeSS protocol elements, as listed for a later QASC translation trial: temperature monitored and recorded at least four times per day for the first 72 h, and "If temperature => 37.5°C treat with paracetamol or other anti-pyretic" [[Fasugba2023]].
- Sugar element as listed: formal venous glucose on admission; readings at least four times per day for the first 48 h, continuing for 72 h if unstable; "If blood glucose level >10 mmol/L (180mg/dl) treat with insulin" [[Fasugba2023]].
- Swallow element as listed: "Swallow screen or swallow assessment within 4 h of admission and prior to being given oral food, drink, or medications", with referral to a speech pathologist for those who fail [[Fasugba2023]].
- The same paper cites a 2021 Australian national audit: 50% of people with fever of 37.5°C or more had paracetamol within an hour, 29% had insulin within 1 h of a first elevated finger-prick glucose of 10 mmol/L or more, and 60% had a swallow screen or assessment before oral intake [[Fasugba2023]].
- QASC Germany, a before-and-after implementation study (771 and 679 patients), raised overall FeSS adherence from 20% to 28%, antipyretic within one hour from 29% to 59%, insulin within one hour from 41% to 60%, and swallow screen within 24 h from 65% to 74% [[CassierWoidasky2024]].
- The European scale-up used a cascading facilitation model, with nurse champions as a key factor [[McInnes2024]].

### unit · Early aspirin
- CAST randomised 21,106 people in 413 Chinese hospitals to aspirin 160 mg/day or placebo, started within 48 h and continued in hospital for up to 4 weeks. Mean start was 25 h after onset [[CAST1997]].
- CAST results: deaths 3.3% vs 3.9% (2p=0.04); recurrent ischaemic stroke 1.6% vs 2.1% (2p=0.01); haemorrhagic stroke 1.1% vs 0.9% (2p>0.1); death or dependence at discharge 30.5% vs 31.6% (2p=0.08) [[CAST1997]].
- IST randomised 19,435 people in 467 hospitals in 36 countries within 48 h, in a factorial design: unfractionated heparin (5000 or 12,500 IU twice daily) or none, and aspirin 300 mg daily or none, for up to 14 days [[IST1997]].
- IST aspirin results: recurrent ischaemic stroke at 14 days 2.8% vs 3.9%; haemorrhagic stroke 0.9% vs 0.8%; death or non-fatal recurrent stroke 11.3% vs 12.4%; dead or dependent at 6 months 62.2% vs 63.5% (2p=0.07) [[IST1997]].
- IST heparin results: fewer recurrent ischaemic strokes (2.9% vs 3.8%) offset by more haemorrhagic strokes (1.2% vs 0.4%), and no difference in dead or dependent at 6 months (62.9% in both groups) [[IST1997]].
- The combined analysis of 40,000 people found 7 fewer recurrent ischaemic strokes per 1000 (1.6% vs 2.3%), about 4 fewer deaths without further stroke per 1000, and about 2 more haemorrhagic strokes or transformations per 1000. The net was 9 fewer further strokes or deaths per 1000 (8.2% vs 9.1%, 2P=0.001) [[Chen2000]].
- Benefit did not differ substantially by age, sex, consciousness, atrial fibrillation, CT findings, blood pressure, stroke subtype or heparin use [[Chen2000]].
- About 9000 people (22%) were randomised without a prior CT and 800 (2%) inadvertently after a haemorrhagic stroke, with no evidence of net hazard in the latter group (further stroke or death 63 vs 67) [[Chen2000]].
- Cochrane 2022: 11 studies, 42,226 participants. Two trials of aspirin 160 mg to 300 mg once daily within 48 h supplied 96% of the data. Death or dependency OR 0.95 (0.91 to 0.99), 13 fewer per 1000, number needed to treat 79 [[Minhas2022]].
- The review states aspirin may be given orally, by nasogastric tube or per rectum in people who cannot swallow [[Minhas2022,Sandercock2014]].

### unit · Head position
- HeadPoST assigned 11,093 people (85% ischaemic) in nine countries to lying flat or to sitting up with the head elevated to at least 30 degrees for 24 h, by hospital, in a cluster crossover design [[Anderson2017]].
- There was no shift in 90-day disability (unadjusted OR 1.01, 0.92 to 1.10; P=0.84). Mortality was 7.3% vs 7.4%. Serious adverse events, including pneumonia, did not differ [[Anderson2017]].
- The position started a median of 14 h after onset (IQR 5 to 35), and fewer people kept to lying flat for 24 h (87% vs 95%, P<0.001) [[Anderson2017]].

### unit · Blood pressure
- CATIS randomised 4071 non-thrombolysed people within 48 h with raised systolic pressure to antihypertensives (10% to 25% systolic reduction in the first 24 h, under 140/90 mm Hg within 7 days) or to stopping all antihypertensives during admission [[He2014]].
- Systolic pressure at 24 h fell to 144.7 vs 152.9 mm Hg. Death or major disability did not differ at 14 days or discharge (683 vs 681 events; OR 1.00, 0.88 to 1.14) or at 3 months (OR 0.99, 0.86 to 1.15) [[He2014]].
- SCAST (2029 people, candesartan vs placebo for 7 days) found no benefit: vascular composite HR 1.09 (0.84 to 1.41); functional outcome common OR 1.17 (1.00 to 1.38; p=0.048, not significant at the trial's threshold of p≤0.025). The authors wrote that, if anything, the evidence suggested harm [[Sandset2011]].
- ENCHANTED (2196 alteplase-eligible people) compared intensive systolic lowering (target 130 to 140 mm Hg within 1 h) with guideline care. Mean 24 h systolic was 144.3 vs 149.8 mm Hg [[Anderson2019]].
- Functional outcome did not differ (OR 1.01, 0.87 to 1.17), though any intracranial haemorrhage fell (14.8% vs 18.7%; OR 0.75, 0.60 to 0.94) [[Anderson2019]].
- ESO 2021 recommends "early and modest blood pressure control (avoiding blood pressure levels >180/105 mm Hg)" in people with ischaemic stroke undergoing reperfusion therapies, and describes the overall evidence for lowering in ischaemic stroke as generally low quality [[Sandset2021]].

### unit · Glucose
- Admission hyperglycaemia predicts poor outcome. Admission glucose above 6.1 to 7.0 mmol/L (110 to 126 mg/dL) after ischaemic stroke carried a relative risk of in-hospital or 30-day death of 3.28 (2.32 to 4.64) in people without diabetes [[Capes2001]].
- In TOAST (1259 people), higher admission glucose was associated with worse 3-month outcome in nonlacunar stroke but not in lacunar stroke, and not with symptomatic haemorrhagic transformation [[Bruno1999]].
- Treating the number harder has not helped. GIST-UK (933 of a planned 2355 people, stopped for slow enrolment) targeted capillary glucose 4 to 7 mmol/L with glucose-potassium-insulin: mortality OR 1.14 (0.86 to 1.51), underpowered [[Gray2007]].
- SHINE randomised 1151 people within 12 h to continuous intravenous insulin (target 80 to 130 mg/dL, 4.4 to 7.2 mmol/L) or sliding-scale subcutaneous insulin (target 80 to 179 mg/dL, 4.4 to 9.9 mmol/L) for up to 72 h [[Johnston2019]].
- Mean glucose was 118 vs 179 mg/dL. Favourable outcome was 20.5% vs 21.6% (adjusted RR 0.97, 0.87 to 1.08), and the trial stopped for futility [[Johnston2019]].
- Severe hypoglycaemia occurred only with intensive treatment (15 of 581, 2.6%), and treatment stopped early for hypoglycaemia or other adverse events in 11.2% vs 3.2% [[Johnston2019]].
- The 2026 AHA/ASA guideline names management of hyperglycaemia and dysphagia among its key updates; the recommendation wording was not read in this pass [[Prabhakaran2026]].

### unit · Fever
- A meta-analysis of 14,431 people with stroke and other brain injury found fever or higher temperature associated with worse outcome on every measure: relative risk 1.5 for mortality, 2.2 for modified Rankin, 3.2 for hospital stay [[Greer2008]].
- PAIS randomised 1400 people (temperature 36 to 39 degrees C) to paracetamol 6 g daily or placebo within 12 h: improved beyond expectation 37% vs 33% (adjusted OR 1.20, 0.96 to 1.50), not supporting routine high-dose use [[denHertog2009]].
- A post hoc subgroup with baseline temperature 37 to 39 degrees C appeared to benefit (OR 1.43, 1.02 to 1.97), flagged by the authors as needing further study [[denHertog2009]].
- PRECIOUS (paracetamol 1000 mg four times daily for 4 days in people aged 66 or older with NIHSS 6 or more) showed no benefit: adjusted common OR 1.19 (0.96 to 1.47) [[deJonge2024]].
- Disagreement: routine fever prevention with paracetamol alone did not improve outcome [[denHertog2009,deJonge2024]], whereas the bundle that included treating temperature of 37.5°C and above did [[Middleton2011]]. The bundle also treated glucose and swallowing, so its effect cannot be credited to fever control alone.

### unit · Oxygen
- SO2S randomised 8003 non-hypoxic adults in 136 UK centres to continuous oxygen for 72 h, nocturnal oxygen for 3 nights, or oxygen only if clinically indicated. Oxygen was given at 3 L/min if baseline saturation was 93% or less and 2 L/min if above 93% [[Roffe2017]].
- No benefit: OR for better outcome 0.97 (0.89 to 1.05) for oxygen vs control, and 1.03 (0.93 to 1.13) for continuous vs nocturnal. No subgroup benefited [[Roffe2017]].

### unit · Dysphagia and pneumonia
- Reported dysphagia depends on the method: 37% to 45% with cursory screening, 51% to 55% with clinical testing, 64% to 78% with instrumental testing [[Martino2005]].
- Dysphagia raises pneumonia risk (RR 3.17, 2.07 to 4.87) and confirmed aspiration raises it further (RR 11.56, 3.36 to 39.77) [[Martino2005]].
- ESO/ESSD 2021 states post-stroke dysphagia is present in more than 50% of people with acute stroke, and finds moderate-quality evidence to recommend screening in all people with stroke to prevent pneumonia and early mortality [[Dziewas2021]].
- In Ontario (7171 people, 2010 to 2013), 19.2% of those eligible had no documented screen, and people with mild stroke were less likely to be screened (adjusted OR 0.51, 0.41 to 0.64) [[Joundi2017]].
- Failing the screen was associated with pneumonia (adjusted OR 4.71), severe disability (5.19), long-term care (2.79) and 1-year death (HR 2.42), including in mild stroke [[Joundi2017]].
- Across 63,650 people in a national cohort in England and Wales, 88% had a dysphagia screen and 39% a comprehensive assessment. The longest delays carried more pneumonia: screening adjusted OR 1.14 (1.03 to 1.24), speech and language therapy assessment adjusted OR 2.01 (1.76 to 2.30) [[Bray2017]].
- Pneumonia incidence rose in absolute terms by 1% per day of delay to specialist assessment [[Bray2017]].
- In a multicentre audit of 2532 cases, six sites with a formal screening protocol had higher adherence (78% vs 57%) and less pneumonia (2.4% vs 5.4%, P=0.0016) [[Hinchey2005]].
- Training nurses in one neurology department to screen 24/7 cut median time to screen from 20 h to 7 h and pneumonia from 11.6% to 3.8% (P=0.004), in a before-and-after comparison [[Palli2017]].
- Screening does not stop all pneumonia. In one stroke unit using GUSS, 30 of 216 people (13.9%) developed pneumonia while nil by mouth [[Teuschl2018]].
- A review of 12 studies (87,824 people) found formal and early screening and speech and language assessment associated with less pneumonia, but heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis [[Eltringham2018]].
- Cochrane 2021 (25 studies, 3953 participants, 37 tests): no single swallow screening tool had high and precisely estimated sensitivity and specificity. Water-only tools ranged 46% to 100% sensitivity and 43% to 100% specificity [[Boaden2021]].
- In that review, 21 of the 37 screens were carried out by nurses [[Boaden2021]].
- The 2026 Cochrane update of swallowing therapy (181 trials, 11,500 participants) found behavioural interventions may reduce chest infection or pneumonia (OR 0.45, 0.27 to 0.76; low certainty) and acupuncture may too (OR 0.47, 0.25 to 0.85; low certainty) [[Wilkinson2026]].
- Pneumonia frequency: 10% (95% CI 9 to 10) in a 2011 meta-analysis of 87 studies and 137,817 people, with overall infection 30% and urinary tract infection 10%. ICU rates were 45%, 28% and 20% [[Westendorp2011]].
- Later pooled estimate from prospective consecutive cohorts (47 studies, 139,432 people): pneumonia 12.3%, stroke units 8%, any infection 21%, urinary tract infection 7.9% [[Badve2019]].
- Disagreement: a microbiology review pooled pneumonia at 23% (14 to 34), with individual studies ranging 2% to 63% [[Kishore2018]]. Definitions drive the range, which is why a consensus group proposed "stroke-associated pneumonia" for lower respiratory infection in the first 7 days, using modified CDC criteria [[Smith2015]].
- Where timing was reported, 78% of pneumonia occurred within 1 week. Aerobic Gram-negative bacilli (38%) and Gram-positive cocci (16%) were most often cultured [[Kishore2018]].
- Pneumonia was associated with death (OR 3.62, 2.80 to 4.68) [[Westendorp2011]].

### unit · Preventive antibiotics
- PASS (2538 people, ceftriaxone 2 g intravenously every 24 h for 4 days vs standard care) did not change 3-month outcome: adjusted common OR 0.95 (0.82 to 1.09) [[Westendorp2015]].
- STROKE-INF (1217 people with dysphagia in 37 UK units, 7 days of antibiotics) did not reduce pneumonia: 13% vs 10% (adjusted OR 1.21, 0.71 to 2.08) by algorithm, and 16% vs 15% by physician diagnosis [[Kalra2015]].
- PRECIOUS (ceftriaxone 2000 mg once daily and metoclopramide 10 mg thrice daily for 4 days): no functional benefit, ceftriaxone acOR 0.99 (0.77 to 1.27), metoclopramide acOR 1.01 (0.81 to 1.25). The trial reached 1493 of a target 3800 people [[deJonge2024]].
- Antibiotics reduced infections other than pneumonia. In PRECIOUS, ceftriaxone reduced any infection (21.9% vs 33.1%; aOR 0.55, 0.39 to 0.78), mainly urinary tract infection (2% vs 12.3%), with pneumonia not significantly changed (17.3% vs 18.5%) [[deJonge2024]].

### unit · Venous thromboembolism prevention
- CLOTS 1 (2518 immobile people): proximal DVT 10.0% with thigh-length stockings vs 10.5% without (absolute reduction 0.5%, 95% CI -1.9 to 2.9). Skin problems were commoner with stockings (5% vs 1%; OR 4.18, 2.40 to 7.27) [[Dennis2009]].
- CLOTS 2 (3114 people): proximal DVT 6.3% with thigh-length vs 8.8% with below-knee stockings (absolute difference 2.5 percentage points, 0.7 to 4.4) [[Dennis2010]].
- CLOTS 3 (2876 immobile people, 94 UK centres, median age 76): proximal DVT 8.5% with intermittent pneumatic compression vs 12.1% without, absolute reduction 3.6% (1.4 to 5.8), adjusted OR 0.65 (0.51 to 0.84) [[Dennis2013]].
- CLOTS 3 defined immobile as unable to walk to the toilet without another person's help. Deaths during treatment were 11% vs 13% (p=0.057). Skin breaks on the legs were 3% vs 1% (p=0.002) [[Dennis2013]].
- At 6 months, compression showed no difference in disability (adjusted OR 0.98, 0.80 to 1.19), institutional care or quality of life. Cost was £64.10 per patient, and the direct cost of preventing a DVT was £1282 [[Dennis2014]].

### unit · Nutrition (FOOD)
- Routine oral supplements (4023 people who could swallow, only 8% undernourished at baseline) changed death by an absolute 0.7% reduction (-1.4 to 2.7) and death or poor outcome by a 0.7% increase (-2.3 to 3.8), not supporting routine supplementation [[Dennis2005Oral]].
- Early tube feeding vs avoiding it for 7 days or more (859 dysphagic people): absolute reduction in death 5.8% (-0.8 to 12.5, p=0.09), and in death or poor outcome 1.2% (-4.2 to 6.6) [[Dennis2005]].
- Gastrostomy vs nasogastric (321 people): increased death or poor outcome by 7.8% (0.0 to 15.5, p=0.05). The authors do not support early gastrostomy [[Dennis2005]].
- Cochrane 2012 found nutritional supplementation associated with fewer pressure sores (OR 0.56, 0.32 to 0.96; two studies, 4125 people) [[Geeganage2012]].
- Malnutrition ranged 8.2% to 49.0% across 8 studies, and the odds were higher with dysphagia (OR 2.425, 1.264 to 4.649) [[Foley2009]].
- ESPEN's neurology guideline has 88 recommendations covering stroke and three other conditions, with oropharyngeal dysphagia management described as pivotal [[Burgos2018ESPEN]].

### unit · Space-occupying infarction and decompressive craniectomy
- Warning signs in swollen hemispheric infarction: new or further impairment of consciousness, cerebral ptosis and changes in pupil size [[Wijdicks2014]].
- Warning signs in swollen cerebellar infarction: falling consciousness from brainstem compression, possibly with early loss of corneal reflexes and miosis [[Wijdicks2014]].
- The pooled analysis of DECIMAL, DESTINY and HAMLET (93 people aged 18 to 60, surgery within 48 h) found at 1 year: mRS 4 or less 75% vs 24%, mRS 3 or less 43% vs 21%, survival 78% vs 29% [[Vahedi2007]].
- Numbers needed to treat were two for survival with mRS 4 or less, four for survival with mRS 3 or less, and two for survival regardless of function [[Vahedi2007]].
- DECIMAL (38 people) reported a 52.8% absolute reduction in death [[Vahedi2007DECIMAL]]. DESTINY (32 people) reported 88% vs 47% survival at 30 days [[Juttler2007]].
- HAMLET (64 people, up to 96 h) showed no effect on poor outcome (absolute risk reduction 0%, -21 to 21) but reduced death (38%, 15 to 60). There was no evidence of functional benefit when surgery is delayed up to 96 h [[Hofmeijer2009]]. The picture held at 3 years, with 16 surgical patients and 8 controls living at home [[Geurts2013]].
- DESTINY II (112 people aged 61 or older, median 70, within 48 h): survival without severe disability 38% vs 18% (OR 2.91, 1.06 to 7.49), mortality 33% vs 70% [[Juttler2014]].
- In DESTINY II no one reached mRS 0 to 2. mRS 3 was 7% vs 3%, mRS 4 32% vs 15%, mRS 5 28% vs 13%. The majority of survivors needed help with most bodily needs [[Juttler2014]].
- Individual-patient meta-analysis (488 people, 7 trials): death adjusted OR 0.16 (0.10 to 0.24), favourable outcome (mRS 3 or less) adjusted OR 2.95 (1.55 to 5.60), with no heterogeneity across subgroups. Benefit after day 2 and in older people remains uncertain [[Reinink2021]].
- ESO 2021: high-quality evidence for surgery in adults up to and including 60 years treated within 48 h, low-quality evidence in older people, and continued uncertainty after 48 h and in selecting people with cerebellar infarction [[vanderWorp2021]].
- The AHA statement advises expecting that one third of people will be severely disabled and fully dependent on care even after craniectomy [[Wijdicks2014]].
- Posterior fossa: the AHA statement says suboccipital craniectomy with dural expansion should be performed in people who deteriorate neurologically, and that ventriculostomy should be accompanied by it to avoid upward cerebellar displacement [[Wijdicks2014]].
- Evidence for that is observational. Pooled across 11 studies (283 people), moderate to severe disability was 28%, mortality 20% and adverse events 23% [[Ayling2018]].
- One matched study suggested preventive surgery might beat medical treatment alone in selected people without brainstem infarction (OR 4.815 for favourable outcome) (Early evidence) [[Kim2016]].

### unit · Haemorrhagic transformation
- ECASS I classified 24 to 36 h CT into no transformation, haemorrhagic infarction types 1 and 2, and parenchymal haematoma types 1 and 2 [[Fiorelli1999]].
- Against no bleeding, HI1, HI2 and PH1 did not change the risk of deterioration, death or disability. PH2 did: OR 32.3 (13.4 to 77.7) for early deterioration and OR 18.0 (8.05 to 40.1) for 3-month death, in both placebo and alteplase groups [[Fiorelli1999]].
- In ECASS II, PH and symptomatic haemorrhage were associated with alteplase but HI was not. Risk factors for PH were alteplase, extent of hypoattenuation on CT, heart failure, older age and baseline systolic pressure [[Larrue2001]].
- Definition changes the rate. In the alteplase meta-analysis, symptomatic haemorrhage was 6.8% vs 1.3% by the type 2 parenchymal definition and 3.7% vs 0.6% by the SITS-MOST definition; fatal intracranial haemorrhage within 7 days was 2.7% vs 0.4% [[Emberson2014]].
- SITS-MOST in routine practice: 1.7% by its protocol definition at 24 h, and 7.3% by the Cochrane definition at 7 days [[Wahlgren2007]]. ECASS III reported 2.4% vs 0.2% symptomatic [[Hacke2008]].
- Comparing ECASS III and Heidelberg criteria after thrombectomy (768 people, 156 bleeds): ECASS III could assign 101 bleeds unambiguously, and Heidelberg categorised 55 more [[Neuberger2017]].
- The AHA statement on post-alteplase haemorrhage names prompt diagnosis and early correction of the coagulopathy as the mainstay of treatment [[Yaghi2017]].

### unit · Finding atrial fibrillation
- A meta-analysis of 50 studies (11,658 people) found new AF in 7.7% on the admission ECG, 5.1% with in-hospital monitoring, 10.7% with ambulatory Holter and 16.9% with later ambulatory methods. The yield after all phases was 23.7% (17.2 to 31.0) [[Sposato2015]].
- Find-AF randomised (398 people aged 60 or older): 14% vs 5% at 6 months with repeated 10-day Holter vs standard care, number needed to screen 11 [[Wachter2017]].
- EMBRACE (cryptogenic stroke or TIA, 55 or older): AF of 30 s or more in 16.1% vs 3.2% with a 30-day event recorder, number needed to screen 8; anticoagulation 18.6% vs 11.1% [[Gladstone2014]].
- CRYSTAL AF (cryptogenic stroke): insertable monitor detected AF in 8.9% vs 1.4% at 6 months (HR 6.4) and 12.4% vs 2.0% at 12 months (HR 7.3) [[Sanna2014]].
- Yield rises with duration: 12.2% at 3 months, 16.0% at 6, 18.7% at 12, 22.8% at 24 and 28.5% at 36 months with implantable monitors [[Noubiap2021]].
- A German expert paper proposes a structured analysis of ECG monitoring on the stroke unit and a staged diagnostic scheme, and notes that the optimal duration is not established [[Haeusler2018]].

### unit · Early mobilisation
- AVERT (2104 people, 56 units, 5 countries) tested very early, higher-dose mobilisation against usual care. In the early group 92% vs 59% were mobilised within 24 h [[Bernhardt2015]].
- Favourable outcome (mRS 0 to 2) was lower with very early mobilisation: 46% vs 50% (adjusted OR 0.73, 0.59 to 0.90; p=0.004). Deaths were 8% vs 7% (OR 1.34, 0.93 to 1.93), with no reduction in immobility-related complications [[Bernhardt2015]].
- The prespecified dose analysis found each extra out-of-bed session per day improved the odds of favourable outcome by 13% (OR 1.13, 1.09 to 1.18), keeping timing and amount constant [[Bernhardt2016]].
- More minutes per day reduced the odds (OR 0.94, 0.91 to 0.97), and longer time to first mobilisation also slightly reduced them (OR 0.99, 0.98 to 1.00) [[Bernhardt2016]].
- This is Class III observational evidence by the authors' own classification, analysed irrespective of treatment group, and it cannot prove that short frequent sessions cause better recovery [[Bernhardt2016]].
- In the AVERT protocol, low-arousal dependent people had active sitting with assistance, sessions of 10 to 30 minutes. Passive sitting in a chair did not count, and sitting for more than 50 minutes at one time was discouraged [[Bernhardt2016]].
- Cochrane 2018 (9 trials, 2958 people): early mobilisation did not increase good recovery (poor outcome 51% vs 49%; OR 1.08, 0.92 to 1.26), may shorten stay by about a day (low quality), and within 24 h may carry increased risk in some people [[Langhorne2018]].
- Exploratory network analysis suggested first mobilisation at around 24 h had the lowest odds of death or poor outcome [[Langhorne2018]].

### unit · Seizures
- In a prospective cohort of 1897 people, seizures occurred in 8.9% (10.6% haemorrhagic, 8.6% ischaemic). Epilepsy (recurrent seizures) occurred in 2.5% [[Bladin2000]].
- Risk factors after ischaemic stroke were cortical location (HR 2.09) and disability (HR 2.10) [[Bladin2000]].
- The SeLECT score uses severity, large-artery cause, early seizures, cortical involvement and MCA territory. Overall late seizure risk was 4% at 1 year and 8% at 5 years [[Galovic2018]].
- SeLECT 0 predicted 0.7% at 1 year and 1.3% at 5 years; SeLECT 9 predicted 63% and 83% [[Galovic2018]].
- ESO 2017: evidence for all recommendations is very low, and "in most instances" the guideline suggests not giving antiseizure drugs, while secondary prophylaxis needs to be considered after one unprovoked seizure [[Holtkamp2017]].

### unit · Delirium
- Pooled across 32 papers (6718 people), delirium occurred in 25% (20 to 30) in the first 6 weeks; with the Confusion Assessment Method it was 21%, and with clinical diagnosis 27% [[Shaw2019]].
- A cohort of 527 people found 11.8% in the first week, with risk from prior cognitive decline, infection, right hemispheric stroke, anterior large-vessel stroke, severity and atrophy [[Oldenbeuving2011]].
- Delirium went with inpatient death (OR 4.71, 1.85 to 11.96), death at 12 months (OR 4.91, 3.18 to 7.6), stay longer by 9.39 days, and discharge to an institution (OR 3.39) [[Shi2012]].
- Disagreement: 56% had "confusion" in an older cohort [[Langhorne2000]], against 11.8% to 25% in delirium-specific studies [[Oldenbeuving2011,Shaw2019]]. Confusion and formally diagnosed delirium are not the same measure.

### unit · Palliative care and goals of care
- The 2014 AHA statement says primary palliative care should be available to all people with serious or life-threatening stroke and their families, throughout the illness [[Holloway2014]].
- It lists nine tasks for teams, including family-centred care, estimating prognosis, setting goals of care, managing symptoms, care coordination, and bereavement resources [[Holloway2014]].
- The 2025 AHA statement adds that needs are insufficiently addressed by current models, and highlights inequities across sociodemographic and regional strata [[Creutzfeldt2025]].
- In US hospitalisations (2016 to 2022), 5.83% of ischaemic stroke admissions received palliative care. The odds were lower for Black (aOR 0.71) and Hispanic (aOR 0.73) people than for White people, and higher with severity (NIHSS 30 to 39 aOR 12.22) [[Asghar2026]].
- 2026 neurocritical care guidance advises avoiding premature prognostication and confounders, and using multimodal assessment. Early neurological improvement within 24 h of revascularisation was the only moderately reliable predictor of good outcome; no other single predictor was considered reliable [[Mainali2026]].
- Early care limitations can shape outcomes. In intracerebral haemorrhage, an early do-not-resuscitate order was associated with a doubled hazard of death at 30 days (HR 2.17, 1.38 to 3.41) after adjustment [[Zahuranec2007]]. This is observational and in haemorrhage, not ischaemic stroke.
- The HAMLET authors wrote that the decision to operate should depend on the emphasis people and relatives place on survival and dependency [[Hofmeijer2009]].

### nursing · What the nursing statements cover
- The AHA replaced its 2009 nursing statement [[Summers2009]] with three updates in 2021: prehospital and acute care [[Ashcraft2021]], endovascular and ICU postinterventional care [[Rodgers2021]], and posthyperacute care up to discharge [[Green2021]].
- The prehospital and acute statement describes nurses as coordinating care between professions, working in telestroke, ambulance services and mobile stroke units, and ensuring stroke education through transitions home [[Ashcraft2021]].
- The posthyperacute statement notes that knowledge gaps remain in many areas and calls for continued nurse-led research [[Green2021]].
- The 2026 AHA/ASA guideline replaces the 2018 guideline and its 2019 update, covering prehospital care through early complications and secondary prevention [[Prabhakaran2026,Powers2019]].

### nursing · Neurological observations and GCS
- The Glasgow Coma Scale was designed for practical bedside assessment of conscious level in general and specialist units [[Teasdale2014]].
- Its authors advise describing individuals by the three components, keeping the total score for characterising groups, and not making predictions from it alone [[Teasdale2014]].
- Neurological decline signs nurses watch for after large infarcts are new impaired consciousness and pupil changes, and in cerebellar infarction falling consciousness [[Wijdicks2014]].
- Early decline is common: progressing stroke occurred in 18.4% in the first week of one stroke unit [[Indredavik2008]].

### nursing · Monitoring during and after thrombolysis
- The standard post-thrombolysis schedule, as described by OPTIMISTmain: neurological and vital signs every 15 min for 2 h, every 30 min for 6 h, and every 1 h until 24 h after thrombolysis [[Anderson2025]].
- The low-intensity protocol: every 15 min for 2 h, every 2 h for 8 h, and every 4 h until 24 h [[Anderson2025]].
- Arithmetic from those published intervals (derived, not stated in the paper): the standard schedule is 36 assessments in 24 h, and the low-intensity schedule about 15 to 16.
- OPTIMISTmain enrolled 4922 people at 114 hospitals in 8 countries, who were clinically stable with NIHSS under 10 within 2 h of starting thrombolysis [[Anderson2025]].
- mRS 2 to 6 at 90 days was 31.7% vs 30.9% (RR 1.03, 0.92 to 1.15; p for non-inferiority 0.057). Symptomatic haemorrhage was 0.2% vs 0.4%. Serious adverse events were 11.1% vs 11.3% [[Anderson2025]].
- The non-inferiority margin was an RR of 1.15, which the upper confidence limit reached. The authors call this "weak evidence" of non-inferiority and say hospitals could consider the approach according to local circumstances [[Anderson2025]].
- The Activase label states: "During and following Activase administration for the treatment of acute ischemic stroke, frequently monitor and control blood pressure" [[FDA-Activase]].
- The label reports angioedema "during and up to 2 hours after" infusion, and says to stop the infusion and treat if anaphylactoid reaction or angioedema develops [[FDA-Activase]].
- In a UK series of 530 people, orolingual angio-oedema occurred in 7.9% (5.5 to 10.6), starting 5 to 189 min after alteplase began (median 65 min) [[Hurford2015]].
- 12% of those cases were severe (1% of all treated) and needed urgent advanced airway management. Prior ACE inhibitor use was the only independent predictor (OR 2.3, 1.1 to 4.7) [[Hurford2015]].
- Disagreement: the 189-minute latest onset in that series starts the clock at initiation of alteplase [[Hurford2015]], whereas the label counts up to 2 hours after infusion [[FDA-Activase]]. The authors say angio-oedema may be delayed and that it occurs more often than previously reported.

### nursing · Swallow screen before oral intake
- The FeSS wording is a screen or assessment within 4 h of admission and before oral food, drink or medications [[Fasugba2023]].
- Medicines are part of the risk. In a FEES study of 60 people with dysphagia, all fully swallowed whole tablets were safe with no penetration or aspiration, while 12.1% of crushed tablets were rated unsafe, and crushed forms left more vallecular residue (Early evidence) [[TraplGrundschober2025]].
- That study challenges the routine crushing of tablets, but its whole tablets were given with endoscopic guidance, and 20 of 174 trials were not swallowed [[TraplGrundschober2025]].

### nursing · Bladder and catheters
- Urinary incontinence affected 36% fully and 11% partly on admission in Copenhagen, falling to 8% and 11% at 6 months; faecal incontinence went from 34% and 6% to 5% and 4% [[Nakayama1997]].
- In a series of 235 people, incontinence was 40% at first, then 19% at 3 months, 15% at 1 year and 10% at 2 years [[Patel2001]].
- People with initial incontinence had higher 2-year case fatality (67% vs 20%) and institutional care (39% vs 16%) [[Patel2001]].
- Urinary tract infection affected 15.8% of 412 people, at a median of 14 days, and was associated with catheterisation (OR 3.03, 1.41 to 6.52), disability and age [[Stott2009]].
- One retrospective study found catheter-associated infection commoner in people with stroke than without (6.0% vs 1.7%; OR 3.53, 1.24 to 10.03) [[Retelski2017]].
- A mixed-methods study in three teaching hospitals found catheters placed for output monitoring, retention or continence issues, with no policy or standard tool, poor documentation and people not fully involved in decisions [[Cowey2012]].
- A programme in three Japanese tertiary hospitals cut inappropriate catheter use from 50.1% to 22.5% and all symptomatic urinary infection from 9.5% to 4.9%, without more retention. The per-catheter-day rate did not change (8.81 to 8.28 per 1000 catheter-days) [[IkedaSakai2022]].
- Cochrane 2019 (20 trials, 1338 people) found insufficient evidence to guide continence care in rehabilitation after stroke [[Thomas2019]].

### nursing · Bowel
- New-onset faecal incontinence was 30% at 7 to 10 days, 11% at 3 months, 11% at 1 year and 15% at 3 years [[Harari2003]].
- At 3 months it was tied to anticholinergic drugs (OR 3.1) and needing help with the toilet (OR 3.5), both potentially changeable, and it increased long-term placement (28% vs 6%) and death within a year (20% vs 8%) [[Harari2003]].
- In a cohort of 600 people, 46.3% had constipation after stroke, with risk factors including bedpan use, depression, poor sleep and NIHSS. New-onset constipation was independently associated with poor discharge outcome (Early evidence) [[Lv2026]].

### nursing · Skin
- Pressure sores: 21% of inpatients in one cohort [[Langhorne2000]], 2.5% or fewer in the first week of one stroke unit [[Indredavik2008]], and 22% in a year after severely disabling stroke [[Sackley2008]].
- More dependent people in the Sackley cohort had more complications, and nursing home residents had the highest rates [[Sackley2008]].
- Devices cause skin harm too: leg skin breaks were 3% vs 1% with pneumatic compression [[Dennis2013]] and 5% vs 1% with stockings [[Dennis2009]].
- Nutrition supplements were associated with fewer pressure sores in Cochrane's pooled data [[Geeganage2012]].

### nursing · Falls
- Falls occur at all stages: acute, rehabilitative and chronic [[Batchelor2012]].
- 25% fell during hospital admission, 5% with serious injury, in one cohort [[Langhorne2000]].
- In Auckland, 37% of 1104 survivors reported a fall: 14% of survivors had an injury needing treatment and 3% a fracture. Depressive symptoms, disability, prior falls and older age were associated [[Kerse2008]].
- 73% of 108 older people with residual disability fell within 6 months of discharge, with 270 falls, and carers of repeat fallers were more stressed (53% vs 18%) [[Forster1995]].
- 73% had a fall within 12 months after severely disabling stroke [[Sackley2008]].
- A fall in hospital or rehabilitation combined with poor balance predicted recurrent falls, with sensitivity and specificity above 80%, in a small study of 55 people [[Mackintosh2006]].
- In the AVERT database, 4% of 2104 people had a serious fall in the first year. 60% of those falls caused a fracture, 42% of fractures were hip fractures, and two falls were fatal [[Wang2025]].
- Cochrane 2019 (14 studies, 1358 people): exercise may reduce the rate of falls (rate ratio 0.72, 0.54 to 0.94; low quality) but not the number of fallers (RR 1.03, 0.90 to 1.19; very low quality) [[Denissen2019]].

### nursing · Mobility and positioning
- Lying flat for 24 h did not improve outcome over sitting up with the head at 30 degrees or more, and fewer people kept to it [[Anderson2017]].
- The AVERT dose signal favours shorter, more frequent out-of-bed sessions, and nurses recorded session data alongside physiotherapists in the trial [[Bernhardt2016]].
- Immobility is the CLOTS entry criterion: unable to walk to the toilet without help [[Dennis2013]].

### nursing · Communication with aphasia
- Aphasia frequency was a median 30% in mixed acute stroke and 34% in rehabilitation, and 62% for acute ischaemic stroke with arrival within 3 h [[Flowers2016]].
- In Swiss first ischaemic stroke, 30% had aphasia [[Engelter2006]].
- Aphasia carried greater odds of in-hospital death (OR 2.7, 2.4 to 3.1) and longer stay (mean 1.6 days) [[Flowers2016]].
- In general acute care, people with communication problems were three times more likely to have a preventable adverse event (OR 3.00, 1.43 to 6.27), and more likely to have several (46% vs 20%) [[Bartlett2008]].
- Supported Conversation training rests on revealing the competence of the person with aphasia through a skilled partner. Trained volunteers scored higher than untrained on acknowledging and revealing competence [[Kagan2001]].
- All 25 studies in a 2016 review reported positive changes from partner training, mostly in chronic aphasia, with more research needed for acute settings [[SimmonsMackie2016]].
- Speech and language therapy improved functional communication compared with none (SMD 0.28, 0.06 to 0.49), and benefit was not evident at follow-up [[Brady2016]].

### nursing · Mood screening
- Depression affected 31% (28 to 35) pooled across 61 studies and 25,488 people, 25% between one and five years, and 23% at five years [[Hackett2014]].
- Another synthesis found 29% prevalence, stable up to 10 years, with cumulative incidence 39% to 52% within 5 years [[Ayerbe2013]].
- The AHA statement says depression affects about one third of survivors at any time and raises risks of poor recovery, recurrent events, poor quality of life and death [[Towfighi2017]].
- For screening, PHQ-9 had sensitivity 0.86 and specificity 0.79, CES-D 0.75 and 0.88, and HDRS 0.84 and 0.83. None was satisfactory for case-finding alone, and PHQ-2 performed less well [[Meader2014]].
- The Stroke Aphasic Depression Questionnaire Hospital version was the only tool meeting all psychometric and utility criteria, and two tools use visual prompts for people with communication problems [[Burton2015]].

### nursing · Hemiplegic shoulder
- Shoulder pain onset within 4 months was reported by 22% of 327 people in Sweden, with almost one third by 16 months, 79% of scorers having moderate to severe pain [[Lindgren2007]].
- In a population study, 10% had pain at baseline and 21% at each follow-up, and 29% had it at some point in 12 months. Peak onset and severity were at 4 months, after most rehabilitation ends [[AdeyWakeling2015]].
- Passive range-of-motion testing elicited more pain than self-report and predicted later pain (crude RR 3.22, 1.01 to 10.27) [[AdeyWakeling2015]].
- Impaired arm motor function and high NIHSS predicted pain, and pain restricted dressing often or constantly in 51% at 4 months and 31% at 16 months [[Lindgren2007]].
- Supports: Cochrane found insufficient evidence on slings and wheelchair attachments. Strapping delayed the onset of pain by 14 days (9.7 to 17.8) without reducing its severity [[Ada2005]].

### nursing · Oral care
- Cochrane 2020 (15 trials, 3631 participants, data for 1546 people with stroke): multi-component oral care reduced denture plaque (DMS -1.31, -1.96 to -0.66; low quality) and improved staff and survivor knowledge [[Campbell2020]].
- It showed no difference in pneumonia in the one trial reporting it: 5 of 99 vs 1 of 105 (OR 4.17, 0.82 to 21.11; low quality) [[Campbell2020]].
- A European review concludes there is an association but only weak evidence about whether improved oral care reduces pneumonia or death after stroke [[Lyons2018]].
- ESO/ESSD lists oral health care among dysphagia treatment options, with low to moderate quality evidence across those options [[Dziewas2021]].
- Disagreement: oral care is widely taught as pneumonia prevention, but trial evidence specifically in stroke is thin, and the only pooled pneumonia estimate does not show benefit [[Campbell2020,Lyons2018]].

### nursing · Teaching: risk factors, FAST, medicines
- Face Arm Speech Test: paramedics using it correctly diagnosed stroke in 144 of 183 (79%) people, with similar non-stroke rates to other routes [[Harbison2003]].
- FAST missed 11.1% of 3498 stroke and TIA presentations: 8.9% of ischaemic strokes, 8.2% of TIAs, and 30.6% of haemorrhages [[Kleindorfer2007]].
- Information provision improves patient knowledge (SMD 0.29, 0.12 to 0.46) and carer knowledge (SMD 0.74, 0.06 to 1.43), with a small effect on depression scores. Active information did better than passive on mood [[Forster2012]].
- Knowledge does not guarantee risk factor control. Cochrane 2018 (42 studies, 33,840 people): educational and behavioural interventions showed no clear differences on blood pressure, lipids, adherence or recurrent events. Organisational interventions improved reaching a blood pressure target (OR 1.44, 1.09 to 1.90) [[Bridgwood2018]].

### nursing · Discharge planning and care transitions
- COMPASS was a pragmatic cluster trial at 40 US hospitals with 6024 adults with stroke or TIA discharged home (2016 to 2018) [[Duncan2020]].
- Its primary outcome, Stroke Impact Scale-16 at 90 days, was 80.6 vs 79.9, not significantly different. Home blood pressure monitoring rose (72% vs 64%; aOR 1.43, 1.21 to 1.70), and no other secondary outcome differed [[Duncan2020]].
- Delivery was the limit: 58% of intervention hospitals delivered it without interruption, 35% of patients attended a clinic visit, and the primary outcome was measured in 59% [[Duncan2020]].
- Early supported discharge (14 trials, 1957 people) cut stay by about seven days and death or dependency (OR 0.80, 0.67 to 0.97), mainly with a co-ordinated team and mild to moderate disability. Benefits were no longer significant at five years [[Fearon2012]].
- A 2010 review found no risk-standardised model of readmission after stroke, and few characteristics consistently associated with it [[Lichtman2010]].
- Caregiver and dyad interventions: 18 studies, 11 with significant caregiver outcomes (most often burden) and 6 with significant survivor outcomes, too limited for firm conclusions [[Bakas2022]].

### nursing · Documentation items (drawn from the findings above)
- Time of swallow screen and result, before any oral intake or medicines, since delay carries pneumonia risk [[Bray2017,Fasugba2023]]. Mild stroke is the group most often missed [[Joundi2017]].
- Temperature and glucose at the FeSS frequency, with time to treatment [[Fasugba2023,CassierWoidasky2024]].
- Catheter indication and review, since documentation was often lacking [[Cowey2012]], and appropriateness programmes reduced infection [[IkedaSakai2022]].
- GCS components rather than the total alone [[Teasdale2014]].
- Observation times after thrombolysis, ACE inhibitor status and any tongue or lip swelling [[Anderson2025,Hurford2015]].
- Prior falls in hospital, since they predict later falls [[Forster1995,Mackintosh2006]].
- Mood and communication tools used, because standard self-report tools may not suit people with aphasia [[Burton2015]].

### Exam hooks
- Stroke unit: for every 100 people treated, 2 more survive, 6 more live at home, 6 more live independently, and benefit is independent of age, sex and severity [[Langhorne2020]].
- QASC tested nurse-initiated protocols for Fever, Sugar and Swallow, with number needed to treat 6.4 for death or dependency at 90 days [[Middleton2011]].
- Aspirin within 48 h: net 9 fewer further strokes or deaths per 1000 in hospital [[Chen2000]]; number needed to treat 79 for death or dependency [[Minhas2022]].
- Lying flat vs head up 30 degrees for 24 h: no difference in outcome or pneumonia (HeadPoST) [[Anderson2017]].
- SHINE: intensive insulin gave no benefit and 2.6% severe hypoglycaemia [[Johnston2019]].
- SO2S: routine oxygen in non-hypoxic people did not help [[Roffe2017]].
- PASS and STROKE-INF: prophylactic antibiotics did not improve outcome or pneumonia [[Westendorp2015,Kalra2015]].
- CLOTS: stockings failed (CLOTS 1), thigh-length beat below-knee (CLOTS 2), and intermittent pneumatic compression worked (CLOTS 3) [[Dennis2009,Dennis2010,Dennis2013]].
- FOOD: no routine supplements and no early gastrostomy [[Dennis2005Oral,Dennis2005]].
- Craniectomy within 48 h, age 60 or under: number needed to treat 2 for survival [[Vahedi2007]]. Over 60: 38% vs 18%, and most survivors need help with bodily needs [[Juttler2014]].
- Only PH2 clearly changes the clinical course [[Fiorelli1999]].
- AVERT: higher dose earlier was worse (46% vs 50%), while shorter, more frequent sessions were associated with better outcome [[Bernhardt2015,Bernhardt2016]].
- ACE inhibitor use more than doubles the odds of angio-oedema with alteplase (OR 2.3) [[Hurford2015]].
- Standard post-lysis observations: every 15 min for 2 h, every 30 min for 6 h, then hourly until 24 h [[Anderson2025]].
- Late seizures: SeLECT uses Severity, Large-artery, Early seizures, Cortical involvement, Territory of MCA [[Galovic2018]].
- FAST misses about 30.6% of haemorrhages [[Kleindorfer2007]].

### In plain words
- A stroke unit is a ward where a whole team looks after people with stroke. People treated there are more likely to live, and to get home [[Langhorne2020]].
- Three simple things matter in the first days: checking for fever, checking blood sugar, and checking swallowing. Units that did this well had fewer people left dead or dependent [[Middleton2011]].
- You may not be allowed to eat, drink or take tablets until a nurse has checked your swallow. Food or drink going down the wrong way can cause a chest infection [[Martino2005,Fasugba2023]].
- Lying flat does not help more than sitting up with your head raised [[Anderson2017]].
- Extra oxygen does not help if your oxygen level is normal [[Roffe2017]].
- Very tight control of blood sugar did not help, and sugar levels can drop too low [[Johnston2019]].
- Machines that gently squeeze your legs can lower the risk of clots in people who cannot walk alone [[Dennis2013]].
- Getting up matters, but more is not always better in the first day. Short and frequent sessions looked better than long ones [[Bernhardt2016]].
- After the clot-busting drip, nurses check you often through the first day. Tell them at once if your lips or tongue feel swollen [[Anderson2025,FDA-Activase]].
- Trouble passing urine or holding it is common and often gets better over time. A catheter can raise the chance of infection, so it should be there only if needed [[Patel2001,Stott2009]].
- Many people fall after a stroke, even at home. A fall in hospital is a warning sign to plan for [[Forster1995,Kerse2008]].
- If you have trouble speaking, it does not mean you cannot think. Staff and family can learn ways to help you get your message across [[Kagan2001]].
- Feeling low is common after a stroke: about 3 in 10 people. Tell someone, because it can be treated [[Hackett2014,Towfighi2017]].
- Shoulder pain often starts some months after a stroke, so keep mentioning it after you go home [[AdeyWakeling2015]].
- If a stroke is very severe, the team should talk with you and your family about what matters most [[Holloway2014,Creutzfeldt2025]].
- FAST stands for Face, Arm, Speech, Time. It catches most strokes but not all, and sudden symptoms still need urgent help [[Kleindorfer2007]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Why did the QASC bundle work when single components did not? Fever trials (PAIS, PRECIOUS) and glucose trials (GIST-UK, SHINE) were neutral [[denHertog2009,deJonge2024,Gray2007,Johnston2019]]; the bundle lowered death or dependency [[Middleton2011]]. Status: unresolved. The bundle changed team behaviour as well as targets (Hypothesis).
- Observation intensity after thrombolysis: OPTIMISTmain calls low-intensity monitoring weakly non-inferior, with p for non-inferiority of 0.057 [[Anderson2025]]; the angio-oedema window is a reason for caution [[Hurford2015]]. Status: applies only to stable people with NIHSS under 10 (Early evidence).
- Early mobilisation: AVERT showed harm from higher early dose [[Bernhardt2015]]; the dose analysis favours frequency [[Bernhardt2016]]; Cochrane voices concern about the first 24 h [[Langhorne2018]]. Status: frequency-over-duration is observational (Early evidence).
- Oral care and pneumonia: association exists, trial evidence is weak [[Lyons2018,Campbell2020]]. Status: unresolved.
- Crushing tablets in dysphagia: long-standing practice challenged by one small FEES study [[TraplGrundschober2025]]. Status: (Early evidence).
- Decompression in older people and after 48 h: survival gain is clear but survivors have high dependency [[Juttler2014]], and evidence after day 2 is uncertain [[Reinink2021,vanderWorp2021]]. Status: decided with the person and family.
- Post-discharge care: COMPASS was negative, but only 35% attended a clinic visit [[Duncan2020]]. Status: whether the model or the delivery failed is unresolved.
- Blood pressure in non-thrombolysed ischaemic stroke: neutral trials [[He2014]] and a possible harm signal [[Sandset2011]]. Status: ESO grades the evidence generally low [[Sandset2021]].
- Early care limitations: an observational doubling of death risk in haemorrhage [[Zahuranec2007]] against the guidance to set goals of care early [[Holloway2014]]. Status: the guidance also warns against premature prognostication [[Mainali2026]].
