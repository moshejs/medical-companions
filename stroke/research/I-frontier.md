# I · Frontier: neuroprotection, factor XI, recovery science, prevention, systems of care, ICH and SAH

Pass date: 2026-11-08. Modules served: frontier.

Evidence labels used here (applied to the trial finding, whether it is positive or neutral):
- (Established): primary result of a large or adequately powered phase 3 RCT, regulator decision, or high-certainty synthesis. A single positive phase 3 trial is flagged as such in the bullet.
- (Early evidence): phase 1 or 2 work, a single non-replicated or underpowered trial, stopped-early trials, post hoc or subgroup analyses, uncontrolled cohorts, low-certainty syntheses.
- (Hypothesis): preclinical or mechanistic rationale, or a claim awaiting a trial that has not reported.

Corrections to the commissioning list, from the fetched records:
- MARVEL tested intravenous methylprednisolone, not intra-arterial [[Yang2024MARVEL]].
- BAST was published in 2023, so its key is Wang2023 [[Wang2023]].
- The 2-year SB623 paper carries a 2019 journal date, so its key is Steinberg2019 [[Steinberg2019]].

## Findings

### frontier · Coagulation background for the factor XI drawing
- The most used anticoagulants inhibit thrombin or factor Xa, or lower prothrombin and factor X; they do not separate thrombin generation in thrombosis from that needed for haemostasis, so they raise bleeding risk. FXII starts contact activation and FXI is the zymogen of a protease in that system; in animal studies these components contribute to induced thrombosis while playing little or no role in haemostasis (Early evidence) [[Gailani2015]].

### frontier · Neuroprotection: nerinetide
- Nerinetide is an eicosapeptide that interferes with post-synaptic density protein 95. It was given as a single 2.6 mg/kg intravenous dose, up to 270 mg, in the thrombectomy trials [[Hill2020,Hill2025]].
- ESCAPE-NA1 (1105 people, large vessel occlusion, thrombectomy, within 12 h): mRS 0-2 at 90 days in 61.4% with nerinetide versus 59.2% with placebo (adjusted RR 1.04, 95% CI 0.96-1.14; p=0.35). (Established) [[Hill2020]]
- ESCAPE-NA1 reported effect modification, with the treatment effect inhibited in people receiving alteplase. (Early evidence) [[Hill2020]]
- ESCAPE-NEXT tested that idea directly in 850 people not given a plasminogen activator: mRS 0-2 in 45% versus 46% (OR 0.97, 95% CI 0.72-1.30; p=0.82). The confirmation failed. (Established) [[Hill2025]]
- FRONTIER (paramedics, within 3 h of suspected severe stroke, 532 treated): treatment began a median of 64 min after onset. Favourable outcome in 57% versus 58% (adjusted OR 1.05, 95% CI 0.73-1.51). (Early evidence, phase 2) [[Christenson2025]]
- The FRONTIER population shows the prehospital problem: 63% had ischaemic stroke, 18% intracranial haemorrhage, 9% TIA and 10% a stroke mimic [[Christenson2025]].
- In FRONTIER participants who had reperfusion therapy, nerinetide was associated with better outcome (adjusted OR 1.84, 95% CI 1.03-3.28). The authors call for confirmation. (Early evidence) [[Christenson2025]]
- A post hoc pooled analysis of the three trials, restricted to 690 of 2487 people treated within 3 h and selected for reperfusion: responders 56% versus 48% (adjusted OR 1.48, 95% CI 1.07-2.06). Authors say the criteria should be tested in a future trial. (Early evidence) [[Tymianski2025]]
- ESCAPE-NEXT at 1 year: functional independence in 48.0% versus 47.9% overall (adjusted OR 1.12, 95% CI 0.74-1.71). In the early-window post hoc group (n=163), 52.6% versus 45.5% (adjusted OR 2.80, 95% CI 1.18-6.66). (Early evidence) [[Adams2026]]

### frontier · Neuroprotection: cerebral oedema and glibenclamide (glyburide)
- CHARM (large hemispheric infarction, ASPECTS 1-5 or core 80-300 mL; intravenous glibenclamide 8.6 mg over 72 h started within 10 h; 431 people aged 18-70 in the efficacy population): no favourable mRS shift at 90 days (common OR 1.17, 95% CI 0.80-1.71; p=0.42). (Early evidence, stopped early and underpowered) [[Sheth2024]]
- The trial was stopped by the sponsor for strategic and operational reasons, before unblinding [[Sheth2024,NCT02864953]].
- CHARM 90-day mortality was 32% with glibenclamide versus 29% with placebo (HR 1.20, 95% CI 0.85-1.70). Hypoglycaemia was a serious adverse event in 6% versus 2% [[Sheth2024]].
- A post hoc CHARM analysis of 147 people with core under 125 mL found a favourable shift (adjusted common OR 2.11, 95% CI 1.10-4.01). In those who also had thrombectomy the estimate was 8.19 (95% CI 1.60-42.0). Authors call this hypothesis-generating. (Hypothesis) [[Kimberly2025]]
- Cochrane 2025 (GAMES-RP plus CHARM, 621 people): little to no difference in function (RR 1.08, 95% CI 0.89-1.32), low certainty, and more hypoglycaemia (RR 4.66, 95% CI 1.59-13.67), moderate certainty. (Early evidence) [[Fan2025]]

### frontier · Neuroprotection: multi-target agents tested mostly in China
- TASTE (1165 people within 48 h; edaravone dexborneol versus edaravone alone): mRS 0-1 at 90 days in 67.18% versus 58.97% (OR 1.42, 95% CI 1.12-1.81). (Early evidence) [[Xu2021]]
- TASTE-2 (1362 people, thrombectomy, anterior large vessel occlusion, within 24 h; edaravone 30 mg plus (+)-dexborneol 7.5 mg twice daily for 10-14 days versus placebo): mRS 0-2 in 55.0% versus 49.6% (RR 1.11, 95% CI 1.00-1.23; P=0.05; risk difference 5.4%). (Early evidence) [[Wang2026TASTE2]]
- In TASTE-2 the effect seemed to be driven by people with clinical-imaging mismatch at admission (55.5% versus 42.9%; RR 1.29, 95% CI 1.10-1.52). The authors suggest a dedicated trial. (Hypothesis) [[Wang2026TASTE2]]
- BAST (1216 people in 59 Chinese centres, given intravenous thrombolysis and/or endovascular treatment, drug within 6 h): favourable severity-adjusted outcome at 90 days in 56.7% with butylphthalide versus 44.0% with placebo (OR 1.70, 95% CI 1.35-2.14; P<.001). Serious adverse events 10.1% versus 12.0%. (Early evidence, single-country trial not yet replicated) [[Wang2023]]

### frontier · Adjuncts to reperfusion: antiplatelets, steroids, intra-arterial lysis, cooling
- RESCUE BT (948 people; intravenous tirofiban versus placebo before thrombectomy): median 90-day mRS 3 in both (adjusted common OR 1.08, 95% CI 0.86-1.36). Symptomatic ICH 9.7% versus 6.4%. Authors say findings do not support this use. (Established) [[Qiu2022]]
- RESCUE BT2 (1177 people without large or medium vessel occlusion; tirofiban versus aspirin 100 mg for 2 days): mRS 0-1 in 29.1% versus 22.2% (adjusted RR 1.26, 95% CI 1.04-1.53). Symptomatic ICH 1.0% versus 0%. Secondary end points were generally not consistent. (Early evidence) [[Zi2023]]
- ASSET-IT (832 people; tirofiban infusion within 60 min after thrombolysis, not eligible for thrombectomy): mRS 0-1 in 65.9% versus 54.9% (RR 1.20, 95% CI 1.07-1.34). Symptomatic ICH 1.7% versus none. (Early evidence, single-country phase 3) [[Tao2025]]
- MARVEL (1680 people; intravenous methylprednisolone 2 mg/kg/day for 3 days with thrombectomy): no significant shift in disability (generalised OR 1.10, 95% CI 0.96-1.25; P=.17). Secondary safety outcomes favoured the drug: death 23.2% versus 28.5% and symptomatic ICH 8.6% versus 11.7%. (Established for the neutral primary; Early evidence for mortality) [[Yang2024MARVEL]]
- Intra-arterial lysis after successful thrombectomy gives mixed results. CHOICE (113 treated, alteplase): mRS 0-1 in 59.0% versus 40.4% (adjusted difference 18.4%, 95% CI 0.3-36.4). (Early evidence) [[Renu2022]]
- POST-TNK (540 people): 49.1% versus 44.1% (adjusted RR 1.15, 95% CI 0.97-1.36; not significant). (Early evidence) [[Huang2025]]
- POST-UK (535 people, urokinase): 45.1% versus 40.2% (adjusted RR 1.13, 95% CI 0.94-1.36; not significant). (Early evidence) [[Liu2025]]
- ANGEL-TNK (256 people, 4.5-24 h): mRS 0-1 in 40.5% versus 26.4% (RR 1.44, 95% CI 1.06-1.95). None of 7 secondary efficacy end points supported this. (Early evidence) [[Miao2025]]
- EuroHYP-1 (cooling to 34.0-35.0 C within 6 h, for 12 or 24 h): stopped at 98 of 1500 planned people. Only 31% reached the cooling target. Outcome OR 1.01 (95% CI 0.48-2.13). Authors judged the regimen not feasible for most and the trial underpowered. (Early evidence) [[vanderWorp2019]]

### frontier · Remote ischaemic conditioning (RIC)
- RIC means repeated cycles of cuff inflation and deflation on a limb, for example 5 cycles of 5 min on and 5 min off at about 200 mm Hg [[Chen2022,Blauenfeldt2023]].
- RICAMIS (1893 people in China, moderate stroke, within 48 h, open label, bilateral arms for 10-14 days): mRS 0-1 in 67.4% versus 62.0% (risk difference 5.4%, 95% CI 1.0-9.9; OR 1.27). Authors say this needs replication. (Early evidence) [[Chen2022]]
- RESIST (1500 people in Denmark, started in the ambulance, sham controlled; 902 with confirmed stroke): no improvement (OR 0.95, 95% CI 0.75-1.20; P=.67). Arm pain or skin petechiae in 7.2% versus 1.5%. (Established) [[Blauenfeldt2023]]
- SERIC-EVT (488 analysed in China, after thrombectomy, sham at 60 mm Hg, twice daily for 7 days): mRS 0-2 in 61.1% versus 48.9% (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.06-1.47). (Early evidence) [[Guo2026]]
- SERIC-IVT (547 analysed, after thrombolysis): excellent outcome 62.7% versus 56.8% (RR 1.10, 95% CI 0.96-1.27; P=0.169). (Early evidence) [[Guo2025]]
- A 2026 meta-analysis (35 studies, 6304 people) favoured RIC for favourable outcome (OR 1.28, 95% CI 1.05-1.57). It reported limited quality: mostly Chinese studies, small samples and heterogeneity. (Early evidence) [[Lam2026]]

### frontier · Factor XI and XIa inhibitors
- Rationale in trial terms: testing whether anticoagulant effect can be separated from bleeding. Milvexian "may offer similar anticoagulant efficacy with less bleeding risk" is the stated trial hypothesis. (Hypothesis) [[Jain2024]]
- PACIFIC-Stroke (phase 2b, 1808 people, asundexian 10, 20 or 50 mg versus placebo): composite of covert infarct or ischaemic stroke at 26 weeks in 19%, 22% and 20% versus 19%. Major or clinically relevant non-major bleeding pooled HR 1.57 (90% CI 0.91-2.71). (Early evidence) [[Shoamanesh2022]]
- AXIOMATIC-SSP (phase 2, 2366 people, milvexian on dual antiplatelet therapy): composite of symptomatic stroke or covert infarct at 90 days 16.8% with placebo versus 15.3-16.7% across doses, with no dose-response. Major bleeding showed no apparent dose-response. (Early evidence) [[Sharma2024]]
- OCEANIC-AF (14,810 people with AF; asundexian 50 mg daily versus apixaban): stopped early. Stroke or systemic embolism 1.3% versus 0.4% (HR 3.79, 95% CI 2.46-5.83). Major bleeding 0.2% versus 0.7% (HR 0.32, 95% CI 0.18-0.55). In AF, asundexian was clearly worse at preventing stroke. (Established) [[Piccini2025]]
- OCEANIC-STROKE (12,327 people, non-cardioembolic ischaemic stroke or high-risk TIA, on antiplatelet therapy; asundexian 50 mg daily versus placebo): ischaemic stroke 6.2% versus 8.4% (cause-specific HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.65-0.84; P<0.001). (Established, single positive phase 3 trial) [[Sharma2026]]
- OCEANIC-STROKE major bleeding was 1.9% versus 1.7% (cause-specific HR 1.10, 95% CI 0.85-1.44). Serious adverse events were 19.2% versus 19.5% [[Sharma2026]].
- OCEANIC-STROKE was the first completed trial of factor XIa inhibition for prevention after non-cardioembolic stroke or TIA. Mean age was 68, 67% were male, 43% of index strokes were large artery, 22% small vessel, 30% undetermined and 2% cardioembolic; dual antiplatelet therapy was planned in 63% [[Sharma2026Design]].
- Entry was within 72 h of the index event, per the registry record, which lists no posted results [[NCT05686070]].
- Abelacimab, a fully human monoclonal antibody that binds factor XI (AZALEA-TIMI 71, 1287 people with AF, versus rivaroxaban): free factor XI fell by a median 99% with 150 mg and 97% with 90 mg. Major or clinically relevant non-major bleeding was 3.2 and 2.6 versus 8.4 events per 100 person-years (HR 0.38 and 0.31). Stopped early for the bleeding reduction. (Established for bleeding; stroke efficacy is not reported as a result in the abstract) [[Ruff2025]]
- Status from registries on the date of this pass:
  - LIBREXIA-STROKE (milvexian versus placebo after stroke or high-risk TIA): active, not recruiting, 12,523 enrolled, primary completion listed 2026-10-15, no results posted [[NCT05702034]].
  - LIBREXIA-AF (milvexian versus apixaban): active, not recruiting, 20,284 enrolled, primary completion listed 2026-12-08, no results posted [[NCT05757869]]. The design paper planned 15,500 [[Jain2024]].
  - LILAC-TIMI 76 (abelacimab versus placebo in AF unsuitable for oral anticoagulation): recruiting, 2500 estimated, primary completion listed 2027-12-30 [[NCT05712200]]. A second record under the same name, sponsored by Novartis, is not yet recruiting with completion listed 2030-12-30 [[NCT07739888]].

### frontier · Recovery: paired vagus nerve stimulation
- VNS-REHAB (108 people with chronic moderate-to-severe arm impairment, randomised, blinded, rehabilitation plus active or control stimulation): FMA-UE rose 5.0 versus 2.4 points (difference 2.6, 95% CI 1.0-4.2; p=0.0014). (Established) [[Dawson2021]]
- At 90 days a clinically meaningful FMA-UE response occurred in 47% versus 24% (difference 24%, 95% CI 6-41) [[Dawson2021]].
- The FDA approved the Vivistim System (PMA P210007) on 27 August 2021, to stimulate the vagus nerve during rehabilitation to reduce arm motor deficits and improve motor function in chronic ischaemic stroke with moderate to severe arm impairment. (Established) [[FDA-Vivistim]]
- Subgroup analyses found the VNS effect similar across the subgroups examined. (Early evidence) [[Dawson2023]]
- At 1 year (74 people, 69% of those enrolled, pooled and without a control comparison): FMA-UE improved by 5.23 points from baseline (95% CI 4.08-6.39). (Early evidence) [[Kimberley2025]]
- An uncontrolled real-world programme reported a mean FMA-UE gain of 10.8 points, with 28 of 35 classed as responders (at least 6 points). This is well above the randomised trial and should not be read as an effect size. (Early evidence) [[Saylor2026]]
- The Vivistim GRASP registry (a post-market registry, up to 3 years of follow-up) is recruiting, with 1000 estimated [[Doherty2026,NCT05301140]].

### frontier · Recovery: neurotechnology
- Brain-computer interface (BCI) coupled to functional electrical stimulation gave more recovery than sham stimulation in chronic stroke survivors, lasting 6-12 months. (Early evidence) [[Biasiucci2018]]
- An uncontrolled BCI feasibility study reported an FMA-UE gain of 4.68 points. People with motor imagery accuracy above 80% gained 3.16 points more. (Early evidence) [[SebastianRomagosa2020]]
- Cochrane 2026: BCI versus conventional therapy may slightly improve arm function (MD 4.67, 95% CI 2.25-7.09; 10 studies, 611 people; low certainty). Against sham BCI it may make little to no difference. (Early evidence) [[Qin2026]]
- Dentate nucleus deep brain stimulation with rehabilitation (open-label phase 1, 12 people, 1-3 years after stroke): median FMA-UE gain 7 points, 15 points in those with some preserved distal function. No serious perioperative or stimulation-related events. (Early evidence) [[Baker2023]]
- Cervical epidural spinal cord stimulation was first reported in 2 people, with grip force up 40% and 108% [[Powell2023]]. (Early evidence)
- The final feasibility report covered 7 people with 4 weeks of implantation: no serious adverse events; average FMA gain 5.6 points with stimulation on and 6.6 at study end. (Early evidence) [[deFreitas2026]]
- NICHE (199 people, navigated 1 Hz rTMS with rehabilitation, 3-12 months after stroke): at least a 5-point FMA gain in 67% versus 65% with sham (P=0.76). Rehabilitation helped both groups. (Established) [[Harvey2018]]
- E-FIT (60 people) with NICHE, in Bayesian analysis: active stimulation not superior to sham (posterior mean OR 1.94, 96% credible interval 0.61-4.80). (Established) [[Edwards2023]]
- RATULS (770 people, moderate or severe arm limitation): ARAT success 44% with robot training, 50% with enhanced therapy and 42% with usual care. Neither beat usual care. Authors do not support the robot as provided in routine care. (Established) [[Rodgers2019]]
- GRASP, the self-directed Graded Repetitive Arm Supplementary Program (not the Vivistim registry of the same name): greater arm improvement at 4 weeks (CAHAI mean difference 6.2, 95% CI 3.4-9.0), held at 5 months. (Early evidence) [[Harris2009]]

### frontier · Recovery: cell therapy and drugs
- MASTERS (phase 2, MultiStem cells intravenously, 126 in the ITT population): no difference in global recovery at day 90 (OR 1.08, 95% CI 0.55-2.09). Safe. (Early evidence) [[Hess2017]]
- TREASURE (phase 2/3 in Japan, 206 people, 18-36 h after stroke): excellent outcome 11.5% versus 9.8% (adjusted risk difference 0.5%, 95% CI -7.3 to 8.3). Safe, no short-term benefit. (Early evidence) [[Houkin2024]]
- MASTERS-2 (phase 3) is listed as terminated by sponsor's decision at 206 people, with no results posted [[NCT03545607]].
- SB623 modified mesenchymal cells implanted into the brain (phase 1/2a, open label, 18 people): FMA motor score up 11.40 at 12 months and 10.4 at 24 months, with no change in mRS [[Steinberg2016,Steinberg2019]]. (Early evidence)
- The sham-controlled phase 2 trial ACTIsSIMA posted results: at least a 10-point FMA motor gain at 6 months in 7 of 55 (2.5 million cells), 9 of 56 (5.0 million cells) and 7 of 52 (sham), p=0.6743 for combined versus sham. This shows why open-label gains can mislead. (Early evidence) [[NCT02448641]]
- Maraviroc rationale: CCR5 is expressed in cortical neurons after stroke, blocking it improved motor recovery in animals, and human carriers of the loss-of-function CCR5-delta32 variant recovered better. (Hypothesis) [[Joy2019]]
- A preclinical systematic review (5 studies) found smaller infarcts with CCR5 antagonists, and high risk of bias in every study. (Hypothesis) [[Sharif2025]]
- A small proof-of-concept study in post-stroke depression reported a mean MADRS fall of 16.4 points on maraviroc 300 mg daily, returning partly after stopping. Not placebo controlled. (Early evidence) [[Tene2024]]
- CAMAROS (phase 2, Calgary, 120 estimated): the record has unknown status, last verified 2024-07, with no results [[NCT04789616]]. MASTER (Geneva, 80 people, 300 mg twice daily for 90 days) is recruiting [[Broc2026,NCT07080567]].
- Fluoxetine shows how promising recovery drugs can fail. EFFECTS (1500 people): no effect on mRS (common OR 0.94, 95% CI 0.78-1.13), with fewer new depression diagnoses but more fractures and hyponatraemia. (Established) [[EFFECTS2020]]
- Cochrane 2021, low risk of bias studies: little to no difference in disability (SMD 0.0, 95% CI -0.05 to 0.05; 5436 people), high-quality evidence. (Established) [[Legg2021]]

### frontier · Prevention: polypills
- TIPS-3 (5713 people without cardiovascular disease at intermediate risk, 4.6 years): polypill plus aspirin 4.1% versus double placebo 5.8% (HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.50-0.97). Polypill alone 4.4% versus 5.5% (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.63-1.00). (Established) [[Yusuf2021]]
- SECURE (2499 people within 6 months after myocardial infarction, aspirin plus ramipril plus atorvastatin): major cardiovascular events 9.5% versus 12.7% (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.60-0.96), with better reported adherence. (Established) [[Castellano2022SECURE]]
- SECURE enrolled people after heart attack, not after stroke. Extending this to people who have had a stroke is an inference. (Hypothesis) [[Castellano2022SECURE]]

### frontier · Prevention: colchicine and inflammation
- CONVINCE (3144 people, non-cardioembolic stroke or TIA, open label add-on): primary end point 9.8% versus 11.7% (HR 0.84, 95% CI 0.68-1.05; p=0.12). CRP was lower on colchicine. Ended before the planned 367 events because of budget constraints. (Early evidence, underpowered) [[Kelly2024]]
- CHANCE-3 (8343 people, minor-to-moderate stroke or TIA with hs-CRP at least 2 mg/L): stroke within 90 days 6.3% versus 6.5% (HR 0.98, 95% CI 0.83-1.16). (Established) [[Li2024CHANCE3]]
- A meta-analysis (6 trials, 14,934 people with prior stroke or coronary disease): ischaemic stroke 1.8% versus 2.5% (RR 0.73, 95% CI 0.58-0.90) and MACE 6.8% versus 9.4% (RR 0.73, 95% CI 0.65-0.81), with no difference in death. (Early evidence for people with prior stroke, because the pooled population is mixed) [[Fiolet2024]]
- A trial named CASPER could not be verified in PubMed, Europe PMC or ClinicalTrials.gov in this pass, so it is not described here. A related record is CoVasc-ICH2, colchicine after intracerebral haemorrhage, not yet recruiting, 1125 planned [[NCT06587737]].

### frontier · Prevention: lipoprotein(a) and GLP-1 receptor agonists
- Lp(a)HORIZON (8323 people with established cardiovascular disease including ischaemic stroke, Lp(a) at least 70 mg/dL; monthly pelacarsen 80 mg versus placebo; nonfatal stroke within the composite) is the first large outcomes test of Lp(a) lowering [[Cho2025,Sabouret2026]].
- A 2026 review reports that topline results announced in September 2026 showed lower Lp(a) but no reduction in the primary composite. Full data are awaited, and the registry lists the trial as completed with no results posted. (Early evidence, secondary report of topline data) [[Sabouret2026,NCT04023552]]
- Lepodisiran 400 mg lowered Lp(a) by 93.9 percentage points over days 60-180 (ALPACA, 320 people). This is a biomarker result, not an outcome result. (Early evidence) [[Nissen2025]]
- Outcome trials still running: OCEAN(a)-Outcomes, olpasiran, 7297 people, primary completion 2028-03-31 [[NCT05581303]], and ACCLAIM-Lp(a), lepodisiran, 17,300 people, primary completion 2029-03 [[NCT06292013]].
- GLP-1 receptor agonists and stroke, two syntheses disagree. Seven trials in type 2 diabetes (56,004 people): fatal or non-fatal stroke HR 0.84 (95% CI 0.76-0.93) [[Kristensen2019]]. Twenty-one trials across wider populations (99,599 people): MACE IRR 0.87 and no difference in stroke [[Galli2025]]. (Early evidence for stroke specifically)
- SELECT (17,604 people with cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity, no diabetes): composite primary end point 6.5% versus 8.0% (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.72-0.90) [[Lincoff2023SELECT]]. SOUL (9650 people, type 2 diabetes, oral semaglutide): 12.0% versus 13.8% (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.77-0.96) [[McGuire2025]]. (Established for composite outcomes; the abstracts do not give stroke separately)

### frontier · Prevention: finding atrial fibrillation early
- LOOP (6004 people aged about 75 with stroke risk factors; implanted loop recorder versus usual care, 64.5 months): AF found in 31.8% versus 12.2% (HR 3.17) and anticoagulation started in 29.7% versus 13.1%. Stroke or systemic embolism 4.5% versus 5.6% (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.61-1.05; p=0.11). (Established) [[Svendsen2021]]
- LOOP authors suggest "not all atrial fibrillation is worth screening for" [[Svendsen2021]].
- STROKESTOP (28,768 randomised in Sweden, 51.3% of those invited attended, 6.9 years): primary end point events 31.9% versus 33.0% (HR 0.96, 95% CI 0.92-1.00; p=0.045), a small net benefit. (Established) [[Svennberg2021]]

### frontier · Systems: AI imaging and mobile stroke units
- AI could reduce variation between readers and help non-expert centres, but any system should let experts catch errors (narrative review). (Hypothesis) [[Bivard2020]]
- A stepped-wedge cluster trial of automated large vessel occlusion detection in 243 people: door-to-groin time fell by 11.2 min (95% CI 4.2-18.22), with no clear difference in functional independence (OR 1.3, 95% CI 0.42-4.0). (Early evidence) [[Martinez-Gutierrez2023]]
- A test of one CT angiography AI tool against experts in 668 people: software processed 82% of scans, with sensitivity, specificity and accuracy each 72%. Users need to remain competent to read CT angiography. (Early evidence) [[Mair2023]]
- These two AI papers differ in what they measure: one improved workflow times, the other found modest accuracy. Neither shows better function. [[Martinez-Gutierrez2023,Mair2023]]
- BEST-MSU (1047 eligible for t-PA): onset to t-PA 72 versus 108 min; mRS 0-1 at 90 days 55.0% versus 44.4% (adjusted OR on utility-weighted mRS 2.43, 95% CI 1.75-3.36). (Established) [[Grotta2021]]
- B_PROUD (Berlin, non-randomised, 1543 people): lower mRS with mobile unit dispatch (common OR for worse outcome 0.71, 95% CI 0.58-0.86). (Early evidence) [[Ebinger2021]]
- A meta-analysis: excellent outcome adjusted OR 1.64 (95% CI 1.27-2.13), and thrombolysis 31 min sooner, with no increase in mortality or symptomatic ICH. (Established) [[Turc2022]]
- INTERACT4 (2404 people, blood pressure lowered in the ambulance, before imaging): no overall benefit (common OR 1.00, 95% CI 0.87-1.15). Benefit in haemorrhagic stroke (0.75, 95% CI 0.60-0.92), harm in cerebral ischaemia (1.30, 95% CI 1.06-1.60). (Established overall; Early evidence for subgroups) [[Li2024INTERACT4]]

### frontier · Intracerebral haemorrhage frontier
- FASTEST (626 people, recombinant factor VIIa within 2 h, mean 100 min): stopped for futility. mRS at 180 days common OR 1.09 (95% CI 0.79-1.51). Haematoma growth 3.7 mL smaller, life-threatening thromboembolic events RR 3.41. (Established) [[Broderick2026]]
- ANNEXA-I (530 people on factor Xa inhibitors with ICH): haemostatic efficacy 67.0% with andexanet versus 53.1% with usual care. Thrombotic events 10.3% versus 5.6%, ischaemic stroke 6.5% versus 1.5%, no appreciable difference in mRS or 30-day death. (Established) [[Connolly2024]]
- TICH-2 (2325 people): day 90 function aOR 0.88 (95% CI 0.76-1.03; p=0.11). Deaths by day 7 were 9% versus 11% (aOR 0.73), with no difference at 90 days. (Established) [[Sprigg2018]]
- STOP-AUST (100 people, spot sign): ICH growth 44% with tranexamic acid versus 52% with placebo (OR 0.72, 95% CI 0.32-1.59). (Early evidence) [[Meretoja2020]]
- Tranexamic acid individual patient data meta-analysis (5 trials, 2860 people): worse outcome 53.2% versus 53.6% (aOR 0.93, 95% CI 0.81-1.07). Day 7 mortality aOR 0.70 and expansion OR 0.81. (Established for no 90-day functional benefit) [[Menon2026]]
- ENRICH (300 people, surgery within 24 h): utility-weighted mRS 0.458 versus 0.374 (posterior probability of superiority 0.981). The effect appeared to be in lobar bleeds (difference 0.127), not anterior basal ganglia bleeds (-0.013). (Established, single trial) [[Pradilla2024]]
- MISTIE III (506 people, minimally invasive surgery with thrombolysis): mRS 0-3 at 365 days 45% versus 41% (adjusted risk difference 4%, 95% CI -4 to 12; p=0.33). (Established) [[Hanley2019]]
- SWITCH (201 people, deep ICH, decompressive craniectomy, stopped early): mRS 5-6 at 180 days 44% versus 58% (aRR 0.77, 95% CI 0.59-1.01). Authors call this weak evidence and note severe disability in survivors of both groups. (Early evidence) [[Beck2024]]
- INTERACT3 (7036 people, care bundle in 121 hospitals): lower odds of poor outcome (common OR 0.86, 95% CI 0.76-0.97), with fewer serious adverse events (16.0% versus 20.1%). (Established) [[Ma2023]]
- Middle meningeal artery embolisation is for subdural haematoma, not ICH, but it belongs to the same neurovascular frontier. EMBOLISE (400 people): repeat surgery for recurrence or progression 4.1% versus 11.3% (RR 0.36). (Established) [[Davies2024]]
- STEM (310 people): treatment failure 16% versus 36% (OR 0.36, 95% CI 0.20-0.66). (Established) [[Fiorella2025]]
- MAGIC-MT (722 people): recurrence or progression 6.7% versus 9.9% (P=0.10, not significant), with fewer serious adverse events (6.7% versus 11.6%). (Established) [[Liu2024]]

### frontier · Subarachnoid haemorrhage frontier: clazosentan
- REACT (phase 3, 409 randomised; clazosentan 15 mg/hour for up to 14 days on top of nimodipine, thick and diffuse clot): clinical deterioration due to delayed cerebral ischaemia 15.8% versus 17.2% (relative risk reduction 7.2%, 95% CI -42.6 to 39.6; p=0.734). (Established) [[Mayer2025]]
- REACT poor outcome at week 12 was numerically higher, 24.8% versus 20.1% (not significant), though rescue therapy use was lower, 10.4% versus 18.1% [[Mayer2025]].
- A 2026 meta-analysis (7 RCTs against placebo, 9 cohorts against fasudil) found less angiographic vasospasm and rescue therapy but no significant difference in function or death. Hypotension, anaemia, pleural effusion, pulmonary oedema and pneumonia increased. (Established for the vasospasm-function gap) [[Alsakti2026]]

### Exam hooks
- A drug that reduces a marker or the time to treatment may still not improve function: FASTEST slowed haematoma growth without functional benefit [[Broderick2026]]; AI cut door-to-groin time with no shown functional gain [[Martinez-Gutierrez2023]]; clazosentan reduced vasospasm without better function [[Alsakti2026]].
- Asundexian failed against apixaban in AF but reduced ischaemic stroke against placebo after non-cardioembolic stroke: mechanism of stroke and comparator matter [[Piccini2025,Sharma2026]].
- Nerinetide subgroup signal in ESCAPE-NA1 did not replicate in ESCAPE-NEXT: treat subgroups as hypotheses [[Hill2020,Hill2025]].
- Open-label gains can vanish with sham control: SB623 open-label versus ACTIsSIMA [[Steinberg2019,NCT02448641]].
- Vivistim is FDA-approved for chronic ischaemic stroke with moderate to severe arm impairment [[FDA-Vivistim]]; the VNS-REHAB between-group difference was 2.6 FMA-UE points [[Dawson2021]].
- Three times more AF found and twice as much anticoagulation did not significantly reduce stroke in LOOP [[Svendsen2021]].
- Prehospital blood pressure lowering helped haemorrhagic stroke and harmed cerebral ischaemia in INTERACT4, which shows why stroke type matters before lowering pressure [[Li2024INTERACT4]].
- ENRICH's benefit appeared in lobar bleeds [[Pradilla2024]].
- MARVEL was intravenous methylprednisolone, with a neutral primary [[Yang2024MARVEL]].
- Fluoxetine reduces depression but does not improve disability after stroke, and raises fracture risk [[EFFECTS2020,Legg2021]].

### In plain words
- Scientists are trying to protect brain cells during a stroke. Many drugs looked good in animals but have not clearly helped people yet [[Hill2025,Christenson2025]].
- A newer type of blood thinner, asundexian, lowered the chance of another clot-related stroke in one very large study, without more serious bleeding. It is one study, and a similar medicine is still being tested [[Sharma2026,NCT05702034]].
- The same medicine did worse than a standard blood thinner for people with an irregular heartbeat called AF. So a medicine that helps one group may not help another [[Piccini2025]].
- A small implant that stimulates a nerve in the neck during arm therapy is approved in the US for some people with long-term arm weakness. The average gain in the trial was modest [[Dawson2021,FDA-Vivistim]].
- Stem cell treatments have been safe in trials so far, but they have not clearly improved recovery in controlled studies [[Hess2017,Houkin2024,NCT02448641]].
- Early results from brain and spinal cord implants are hopeful. They come from very small groups of people and need bigger studies [[Baker2023,deFreitas2026]].
- An ambulance with a CT scanner can start clot-busting treatment sooner, and people treated this way tended to recover better [[Grotta2021,Turc2022]].
- For a bleed in the brain, keeping several things under control at once in the first hours, such as blood pressure, improved recovery in one large study [[Ma2023]].
- Taking part in a trial is a personal choice. A care team can explain whether a trial may fit. This guide does not recommend any treatment.

## Open questions and controversies
- Is there a real nerinetide effect in very early treatment with reperfusion? For: post hoc pooled analysis and 1-year early-window data [[Tymianski2025,Adams2026]]. Against: primary results of all three trials were neutral [[Hill2020,Hill2025,Christenson2025]]. Status: needs a prospective trial.
- Does RIC work? For: RICAMIS and SERIC-EVT [[Chen2022,Guo2026]]. Against: the sham-controlled Danish RESIST and SERIC-IVT [[Blauenfeldt2023,Guo2025]]. The meta-analysis found better favourable outcomes only without reperfusion [[Lam2026]], which conflicts with SERIC-EVT [[Guo2026]]. Status: unresolved, with differences in setting, timing, dose and blinding.
- Can Chinese neuroprotection trials be generalised? BAST and TASTE-2 are positive or borderline in Chinese centres [[Wang2023,Wang2026TASTE2]]. Lam and colleagues raise regional generalisability for RIC [[Lam2026]]. Status: replication elsewhere has not been found in this pass.
- Will factor XI inhibition become standard after non-cardioembolic stroke? For: OCEANIC-STROKE [[Sharma2026]]. Caution: both phase 2 trials missed their composite outcome [[Shoamanesh2022,Sharma2024]]. Status: LIBREXIA-STROKE has no reported result [[NCT05702034]]. Regulatory and guideline status were not established in this pass.
- Could a factor XI drug still work in AF? Asundexian failed [[Piccini2025]]; LIBREXIA-AF and LILAC have not reported [[NCT05757869,NCT05712200]].
- Is colchicine useful after stroke? For: pooled reduction in ischaemic stroke [[Fiolet2024]]. Against: CONVINCE missed significance and CHANCE-3 was neutral [[Kelly2024,Li2024CHANCE3]]. Note CHANCE-3 measured 90 days, while CONVINCE reported long-term follow-up.
- Does lowering Lp(a) cut stroke? Topline HORIZON report is neutral, full data awaited [[Sabouret2026]]; two further outcome trials continue [[NCT05581303,NCT06292013]].
- Do GLP-1 receptor agonists prevent stroke? Position A: yes in diabetes [[Kristensen2019]]. Position B: no difference in the wider analysis [[Galli2025]].
- Should people be screened for AF, and how hard? STROKESTOP found a small net benefit [[Svennberg2021]], and LOOP did not reach significance [[Svendsen2021]].
- Is intra-arterial lysis after thrombectomy useful? CHOICE and ANGEL-TNK were positive with small samples [[Renu2022,Miao2025]], while POST-TNK and POST-UK were not significant [[Huang2025,Liu2025]].
