## Findings

### prevent · OPTIMAL-STROKE: a stroke-only trial of a systolic target below 120 mm Hg
- OPTIMAL-STROKE was a multicentre, open-label, randomised trial at 30 sites in Brazil. People with raised blood pressure and a previous ischaemic stroke or TIA were assigned to intensive treatment (systolic target below 120 mm Hg) or standard treatment (below 140 mm Hg). 4368 people were enrolled (2172 intensive, 2196 standard); mean age 63.9 years, 47.9% women. [[SampaioSilva2026]]
- Mean systolic blood pressure during follow-up, excluding the first 3 months, was 122.0 mm Hg (median 119.6) vs 135.2 mm Hg (median 135.0), a mean difference of 13.1 mm Hg (95% CI 12.5 to 13.8). [[SampaioSilva2026]]
- Over a median 3.9 years, the primary outcome (cardiovascular death, non-fatal MI, non-fatal stroke, or hospitalisation for unstable angina or heart failure) occurred in 236 people (10.9%; 3.25 per 100 person-years) vs 297 (13.5%; 4.11 per 100 person-years): HR 0.79 (95% CI 0.67 to 0.94; P = 0.007). (Early evidence: one open-label trial in one country) [[SampaioSilva2026]]
- A composite kidney outcome (sustained fall in eGFR of 50% or more, or progression to end-stage kidney disease) was more common with intensive treatment. Overall serious adverse events were similar. The abstract gives no numbers for these. [[SampaioSilva2026]]
- This answers the gap stated on the site ("no stroke-only trial below 120 mm Hg"). Earlier evidence: SPS3 and RESPECT were individually not significant [[Benavente2013,Kitagawa2019]]; ESPRIT included 3022 people with previous stroke [[Liu2024]].

### frontier · FDA approval of asundexian (Sunharbo)
- On 9 November 2026 the US FDA approved Sunharbo (asundexian) 50 mg tablets, the first factor XIa inhibitor, "for use in combination with antiplatelet therapy to reduce the risk of stroke in adults following a recent mild-to-moderate non-cardioembolic ischemic stroke ... or a high-risk transient ischemic attack". (Established: regulatory fact) [[FDA-Sunharbo2026]]
- The FDA cites OCEANIC-STROKE (12,327 adults, NIHSS 15 or lower, randomised within 72 hours, both groups on antiplatelet therapy): ischaemic stroke risk was reduced by 26% over a median treatment of 14.2 months. [[FDA-Sunharbo2026,Sharma2026]]
- FDA safety statements: the overall rate of major bleeding was similar to placebo but was increased with the drug in patients older than 75 years and during the first 90 days; gastrointestinal bleeding was more common; the most frequent adverse reaction (3% or more) was diarrhoea. [[FDA-Sunharbo2026]]
- Not recommended with combined P-gp and strong CYP3A4 inducers or in moderate or severe hepatic impairment. To be withheld at least 24 hours before elective surgery or invasive procedures and for at least 24 hours after thrombolysis for acute ischaemic stroke. Not approved for stroke prevention in atrial fibrillation. [[FDA-Sunharbo2026]]
- The approval was granted to Bayer HealthCare Pharmaceuticals Inc., with Fast Track and Priority Review designations. [[FDA-Sunharbo2026]]

### af · Left atrial appendage closure papers (recorded, not added to the site)
- An individual participant data meta-analysis of CLOSURE-AF and PRAGUE-17 (1290 people, median age 77.3) found no significant difference between closure and medical therapy for stroke, systemic embolism or cardiovascular death (21.9% vs 20.7%; adjusted HR 0.98, 95% CI 0.77-1.24) or for major bleeding (13.3% vs 12.4%; adjusted HR 1.03, 95% CI 0.75-1.39). [[Skurk2026]]
- ASAP-TOO (closure vs single antiplatelet or no therapy in people unsuitable for anticoagulation) stopped early for slow accrual with 481 of 888 planned. Ischaemic stroke or systemic embolism at up to 5 years: 7.8% vs 11.4% (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.35-1.52). The authors call the findings exploratory. [[Reddy2026ASAPTOO]]

### Exam hooks
- OPTIMAL-STROKE: after ischaemic stroke or TIA, systolic target below 120 vs below 140 mm Hg, major cardiovascular events 10.9% vs 13.5%, HR 0.79. [[SampaioSilva2026]]
- Asundexian is the first factor XIa inhibitor approved by the FDA (9 November 2026): with antiplatelets after non-cardioembolic stroke or high-risk TIA, not for AF. [[FDA-Sunharbo2026]]

### In plain words
- In a large study in Brazil, people who had already had a stroke and kept their upper blood pressure number near 120 had fewer heart attacks, strokes and heart-related deaths than people kept near 135. Kidney problems were more common with the lower goal.
- A new type of blood thinner, asundexian, is now approved in the United States to help prevent a second stroke in some adults. It is taken with an antiplatelet medicine. It is not approved for people whose strokes come from atrial fibrillation.

## Open questions and controversies
- How low for blood pressure after stroke: OPTIMAL-STROKE supports below 120 mm Hg [[SampaioSilva2026]]; SPS3 and RESPECT were individually not significant [[Benavente2013,Kitagawa2019]]. Status: one positive open-label trial; guidelines have not been checked for a response.
- Will factor XI inhibition become standard after non-cardioembolic stroke? Now FDA-approved on one phase 3 trial [[FDA-Sunharbo2026,Sharma2026]]; LIBREXIA-STROKE has no reported result [[NCT05702034]]. Guideline status not established.
