# G · Stroke prevention: risk factors, antithrombotics, atrial fibrillation, carotid and intracranial disease, PFO

Pass date: 2026-11-08. Modules served: prevent, af, carotid.

Doses, thresholds and guideline statements below are recorded as published. They are teaching material, not advice, and none of the scores is a treatment tool.

## Findings

### prevent · How much stroke is preventable
- INTERSTROKE was a standardised case-control study in 32 countries, 2007 to 2015: 26 919 participants (13 447 people with acute first stroke, 10 388 ischaemic and 3059 intracerebral haemorrhage, and 13 472 controls) [[ODonnell2016]].
- Ten potentially modifiable factors accounted for 90.7% of the population attributable risk (PAR) for all stroke worldwide: 91.5% for ischaemic stroke and 87.1% for intracerebral haemorrhage [[ODonnell2016]].
- The collective PAR ranged from 82.7% in Africa to 97.4% in southeast Asia. It was 90.6% in both men and women, 92.2% at age 55 or under and 90.0% above 55 [[ODonnell2016]].
- INTERSTROKE odds ratios and PARs, each with 99% CI, for all stroke:
  - Hypertension (history or BP of 140/90 mm Hg or higher): OR 2.98 (2.72 to 3.28); PAR 47.9% (45.1 to 50.6)
  - Regular physical activity: OR 0.60 (0.52 to 0.70); PAR 35.8% (27.7 to 44.7)
  - ApoB/ApoA1 ratio, highest vs lowest tertile: OR 1.84 (1.65 to 2.06); PAR 26.8% (22.2 to 31.9)
  - Diet (mAHEI), highest vs lowest tertile: OR 0.60 (0.53 to 0.67); PAR 23.2% (18.2 to 28.9)
  - Waist-to-hip ratio, highest vs lowest tertile: OR 1.44 (1.27 to 1.64); PAR 18.6% (13.3 to 25.3)
  - Psychosocial factors: OR 2.20 (1.78 to 2.72); PAR 17.4% (13.1 to 22.6)
  - Current smoking: OR 1.67 (1.49 to 1.87); PAR 12.4% (10.2 to 14.9)
  - Cardiac causes: OR 3.17 (2.68 to 3.75); PAR 9.1% (8.0 to 10.2)
  - Alcohol, high or heavy episodic intake vs never or former: OR 2.09 (1.64 to 2.67); PAR 5.8% (3.4 to 9.7, current drinker vs never or former)
  - Diabetes: OR 1.16 (1.05 to 1.30); PAR 3.9% (1.9 to 7.6) [[ODonnell2016]]
- Hypertension was more strongly associated with intracerebral haemorrhage than with ischaemic stroke. Smoking, diabetes, apolipoproteins and cardiac causes were more strongly associated with ischaemic stroke (p<0.0001) [[ODonnell2016]].
- GBD 2021: 11.9 million (10.7 to 13.2) incident strokes, 93.8 million prevalent strokes, 7.3 million deaths (10.7% of all deaths, third leading cause) and 160.5 million DALYs in 2021 [[GBD2021Stroke]].
- GBD 2021: 84.1% (77.8 to 88.8) of stroke DALYs were attributed to the 23 risk factors analysed [[GBD2021Stroke]].
- Leading attributable fractions of stroke DALYs in GBD 2021: high systolic BP 56.8% (42.5 to 68.0), ambient particulate matter 16.6% (11.5 to 20.9), smoking 13.8% (2.5 to 26.0) and high LDL cholesterol 13.1% (4.6 to 21.3) [[GBD2021Stroke]].
- DALYs attributable to several risks rose from 1990 to 2021, including high BMI (88.2%), high ambient temperature (72.4%), high fasting plasma glucose (32.1%) and high systolic BP (6.7%) [[GBD2021Stroke]].
- GBD reports a stagnation in the decline of stroke incidence from 2015 onwards, with some increases in people younger than 70 and in countries with lower socio-demographic index [[GBD2021Stroke]].
- The 90.7% and 84.1% figures measure different things. INTERSTROKE gives PAR from a case-control comparison of first strokes, and GBD gives attributable DALYs from modelled exposure across 23 risks [[ODonnell2016,GBD2021Stroke]].

### prevent · Guidelines and what changed
- The 2024 AHA/ASA primary prevention guideline replaces the 2014 version, aligns with Life's Essential 8 and adds sex-specific screening and prevention recommendations that are new compared with 2014 [[Bushnell2024]].
- As summarised in an open review of the 2024 guideline (the guideline's full text could not be read in this pass), recommendations include:
  - Mediterranean diet: Class 1, LOE B-R, intermediate to high CVD risk
  - Potassium salt substitution: Class 2a, LOE B-R, age 60 or over with SBP of 140 mm Hg or higher on medication or 160 mm Hg or higher without it
  - BP target below 130/80 mm Hg: Class 1, LOE A, stage 2 hypertension or stage 1 with high CVD risk
  - Statin: Class 1, LOE A, for the intermediate-risk, high-risk, type 2 diabetes and LDL-C of 190 mg/dL or higher groups
  - PCSK9 antibody: Class 2b, LOE A
  - Omega-3 fatty acids: Class 3 (no benefit), LOE A
  - Aspirin in people without clinical CVD: Class 2b, LOE A
  - GLP-1 RA in type 2 diabetes: Class 1, LOE A, flagged as a new indication for stroke risk reduction
  - Low-dose colchicine after recent MI on a statin: Class 2b, LOE B-R [[Razavi2025]]
- Primary prevention aspirin: in 13 trials and 164 225 participants, aspirin lowered the cardiovascular composite (HR 0.89, 95% credible interval 0.84 to 0.95; NNT 265) and raised major bleeding (HR 1.43, 1.30 to 1.56; NNH 210) [[Zheng2019]].
- The 2009 ATT meta-analysis found no significant net effect of aspirin on stroke in primary prevention (0.20% vs 0.21% per year, p=0.4). In secondary prevention, total stroke fell from 2.54% to 2.08% per year (p=0.002) [[ATT2009]].
- The 2021 AHA/ASA secondary prevention guideline is the reference for people who have had a stroke or TIA. PubMed carries no abstract and the full text was blocked, so its statements on BP below 130/80 mm Hg and LDL-C below 70 mg/dL in atherosclerotic disease are not confirmed here (unverified) [[Kleindorfer2021]]. The trial evidence behind them is below.

### prevent · Blood pressure
- A 10 mm Hg reduction in systolic BP reduced stroke by 27% (RR 0.73, 0.68 to 0.77) in a meta-analysis of 123 studies and 613 815 participants. Coronary disease RR was 0.83 and heart failure RR was 0.72 [[Ettehad2016]].
- In that analysis, calcium channel blockers were superior to other drugs for preventing stroke and beta blockers were inferior [[Ettehad2016]].
- In 344 716 trial participants, each 5 mm Hg lower systolic BP reduced major cardiovascular events by about 10%. The HR was 0.91 (0.89 to 0.94) without previous cardiovascular disease and 0.89 (0.86 to 0.92) with it, including at normal or high-normal BP [[BPLTTC2021Levels]].
- Relative benefit per 5 mm Hg held to age 84: HR 0.82 under 55, 0.91 at 55 to 64, 0.91 at 65 to 74 and 0.91 at 75 to 84. At 85 and over the HR was 0.99 (0.87 to 1.12) [[BPLTTC2021Age]].
- PROGRESS: 6105 people with prior stroke or TIA; a perindopril-based regimen lowered BP by 9/4 mm Hg over 4 years. Stroke occurred in 10% vs 14% (RRR 28%, 95% CI 17 to 38), in hypertensive and non-hypertensive subgroups alike [[PROGRESS2001]].
- In PROGRESS, perindopril plus indapamide lowered BP by 12/5 mm Hg and stroke by 43% (30 to 54). Single-drug therapy lowered BP by 5/3 mm Hg with no discernible reduction in stroke [[PROGRESS2001]].
- SPS3: 3020 people with recent lacunar stroke; systolic target below 130 vs 130 to 149 mm Hg (achieved 127 vs 138 at 1 year). All stroke HR 0.81 (0.64 to 1.03, not significant); intracerebral haemorrhage HR 0.37 (0.15 to 0.95) [[Benavente2013]].
- RESPECT (Japan): 1263 people with prior stroke analysed; below 120/80 vs below 140/90 mm Hg (achieved 126.7/77.4 vs 133.2/77.7). Recurrent stroke HR 0.73 (0.49 to 1.11). Pooled with 3 earlier trials: RR 0.78 (0.64 to 0.96), absolute difference 1.5%, NNT 67. The authors concluded this supports a target below 130/80 mm Hg [[Kitagawa2019]].
- ESPRIT: 11 255 people at high cardiovascular risk in China, 3022 with previous stroke; target below 120 vs below 140 mm Hg (achieved 119.1 vs 134.8). Composite 9.7% vs 11.1%, HR 0.88 (0.78 to 0.99), with no heterogeneity by history of stroke. Serious syncope 0.4% vs 0.1% [[Liu2024]].
- BPROAD: 12 821 people with type 2 diabetes in China; below 120 vs below 140 mm Hg. Primary composite 1.65 vs 2.09 events per 100 person-years, HR 0.79 (0.69 to 0.90). Symptomatic hypotension and hyperkalaemia were more frequent [[Bi2025]].

### prevent · Lipids
- SPARCL: 4731 people 1 to 6 months after stroke or TIA without known coronary disease; atorvastatin 80 mg vs placebo. Mean LDL was 73 vs 129 mg/dL. Stroke occurred in 11.2% vs 13.1% over a median 4.9 years (adjusted HR 0.84, 0.71 to 0.99; 5-year absolute reduction 2.2%) [[Amarenco2006]].
- SPARCL's trade-off: 218 vs 274 ischaemic strokes and 55 vs 33 haemorrhagic strokes (atorvastatin vs placebo). Overall mortality was similar [[Amarenco2006]].
- Treat Stroke to Target: 2860 people with recent ischaemic stroke or TIA and atherosclerosis; LDL below 70 mg/dL (1.8 mmol/L) vs 90 to 110 mg/dL. Achieved 65 vs 96 mg/dL. Events 8.5% vs 10.9%, adjusted HR 0.78 (0.61 to 0.98). Intracranial haemorrhage did not differ significantly. The trial stopped early with 277 of 385 planned events [[Amarenco2020]].
- More intensive statin regimens reduced ischaemic stroke by 16% (5 to 26) compared with less intensive regimens [[CTT2010]].
- IMPROVE-IT, stroke analysis: ezetimibe added to simvastatin after acute coronary syndrome reduced ischaemic stroke (3.4% vs 4.1%, HR 0.79, 0.67 to 0.94). Haemorrhagic stroke was not significantly increased (0.8% vs 0.6%, HR 1.38, 0.93 to 2.04) [[Bohula2017]].
- In IMPROVE-IT participants with prior stroke, any stroke occurred in 10.2% vs 18.8% (HR 0.60, 0.38 to 0.95; NNT 12) [[Bohula2017]].
- FOURIER: evolocumab lowered LDL by 59% to a median of 30 mg/dL (0.78 mmol/L) on a statin; primary endpoint HR 0.85 (0.79 to 0.92) [[Sabatine2017FOURIER]].
- FOURIER stroke analysis: all stroke 1.5% vs 1.9% (HR 0.79, 0.66 to 0.95), ischaemic stroke HR 0.75 (0.62 to 0.92), haemorrhagic stroke 0.21% vs 0.18% (HR 1.16, 0.68 to 1.98). In the 5337 with prior ischaemic stroke the all-stroke HR was 0.90 (0.68 to 1.19), with no significant interaction [[Giugliano2020]].
- FOURIER and FOURIER-OLE, 5291 people with prior ischaemic stroke: risk fell monotonically with achieved LDL-C. Below 40 vs 70 mg/dL or higher, rate ratios were 0.69 (0.57 to 0.84) for the primary endpoint and 0.73 (0.53 to 0.99) for all stroke. Haemorrhagic strokes were unrelated to achieved LDL-C. This is observational within a trial (Early evidence) [[Monguillon2026]].

### prevent · Diabetes, weight and insulin resistance
- IRIS: 3876 people without diabetes but with insulin resistance (HOMA-IR above 3.0) after stroke or TIA; pioglitazone vs placebo. Stroke or MI 9.0% vs 11.8% at 4.8 years (HR 0.76, 0.62 to 0.93). Diabetes 3.8% vs 7.7% (HR 0.48) [[Kernan2016]].
- IRIS harms: weight gain over 4.5 kg 52.2% vs 33.7%, oedema 35.6% vs 24.9%, fracture needing surgery or admission 5.1% vs 3.2%. Mortality did not differ [[Kernan2016]].
- GLP-1 receptor agonists, 8 trials and 60 080 people with type 2 diabetes: MACE HR 0.86 (0.80 to 0.93), all-cause mortality HR 0.88 (0.82 to 0.94). The authors state a reduction in individual MACE components, but the abstract gives no stroke-specific HR [[Sattar2021]].
- A later meta-analysis of 11 trials and 85 373 participants gives stroke HR 0.88 (0.80 to 0.96), and 0.73 in people with a prior stroke [[Alammari2025]].
- The 2024 guideline review quotes a 27% relative reduction (0.7% absolute) in non-fatal stroke with GLP-1 RA in type 2 diabetes. Different trial sets and outcome definitions may explain the spread from the 12% above [[Razavi2025,Alammari2025]].
- SELECT: 17 604 people with cardiovascular disease and BMI of 27 or more without diabetes; semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly reduced the composite (6.5% vs 8.0%, HR 0.80, 0.72 to 0.90). Stopping for adverse events was 16.6% vs 8.2% [[Lincoff2023SELECT]].

### prevent · Smoking
- One cigarette a day carries much more risk than expected. Pooled stroke RR for 1 and 20 cigarettes a day was 1.25 and 1.64 in men, and 1.31 and 2.16 in women. One a day carries 41% (men) and 34% (women) of the excess risk of 20 a day [[Hackshaw2018]].
- In the IRIS cohort, 450 of 1072 smokers (42%) quit between the index event and randomisation. Five-year stroke, MI or death was 15.7% in those who quit vs 22.6% in those who continued (adjusted HR 0.66, 0.48 to 0.90). This is an observational, non-prespecified analysis [[Epstein2017]].
- Jackson Heart Study, 4410 Black adults: current smoking HR 2.48 (1.60 to 3.83) vs never smoking, with a dose response (2.28 at 1 to 19 and 2.78 at 20 or more cigarettes a day). Past smoking HR was 1.10 (0.74 to 1.64) [[Oshunbade2020]].

### prevent · Diet, salt, activity and alcohol
- PREDIMED, republished in 2018 after randomisation deviations were found: 7447 people at high risk; primary composite HR 0.69 (0.53 to 0.91) with olive oil and 0.72 (0.54 to 0.95) with nuts vs reduced-fat advice. Results were similar after omitting 1588 participants whose assignment departed from protocol [[Estruch2018]].
- The 2024 guideline review quotes a 42% relative reduction in stroke (1.5% absolute) from PREDIMED [[Razavi2025]].
- SSaSS: cluster randomised, 600 villages in rural China, 20 995 people (72.6% with prior stroke, 88.4% with hypertension); salt substitute of 75% sodium chloride and 25% potassium chloride. Stroke 29.14 vs 33.65 per 1000 person-years (rate ratio 0.86, 0.77 to 0.96), death 39.28 vs 44.61 (rate ratio 0.88). Serious hyperkalaemia events were not significantly higher (3.35 vs 3.30, rate ratio 1.04) [[Neal2021]].
- Physical activity, 14 cohorts and 2 639 086 people: moderate-to-vigorous activity had an L-shaped association with stroke, with the greatest reduction (19%) at 19 MET-hours a week, and then a gradual rise. Evidence in men and for haemorrhagic stroke is limited [[Li2026]].
- Alcohol, 27 prospective studies: ischaemic stroke RR 0.90 (under 1 drink a day), 0.92 (1 to 2), 1.08 (over 2 to 4) and 1.14 (over 4). Heavy drinking RR was 1.67 for intracerebral and 1.82 for subarachnoid haemorrhage [[Larsson2016BMCMed]].
- Observational and genetic evidence disagree on moderate drinking. Conventional analysis in China Kadoorie Biobank men gave a U shape, but genotype-predicted alcohol gave a continuously positive association with stroke (RR per 280 g a week 1.58 for intracerebral haemorrhage, 1.27 for ischaemic stroke). The authors conclude the apparent protection is largely non-causal [[Millwood2019,Larsson2016BMCMed]].

### prevent · Sleep apnoea and lipoprotein(a)
- SAVE: 2717 adults with moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnoea and coronary or cerebrovascular disease. CPAP plus usual care vs usual care: 17.0% vs 15.4% (HR 1.10, 0.91 to 1.32). Mean use was 3.3 hours a night. Snoring, sleepiness, quality of life and mood improved [[McEvoy2016]].
- Sleep SMART tests auto-CPAP after acute stroke or high-risk TIA, with two primary outcomes (recurrent events at 6 months, modified Rankin at 3 months) [[Brown2020]].
- Sleep SMART randomised 1892 participants from 2019 to 2025, below the 3062 planned, with outcome assessments ongoing [[Brown2026,Brown2020]]. ClinicalTrials.gov lists it as active, not recruiting, with no posted results and estimated completion in November 2026 [[NCT03812653]].
- Lp(a): levels above 93 mg/dL vs below 10 mg/dL had an adjusted ischaemic stroke HR of 1.60 (1.24 to 2.05) in Copenhagen. Genetic causal risk ratios per 50 mg/dL were 1.20 and 1.27 (Established as an association; causal support from genetics) [[Langsted2019]].
- BIOSIGNAL, 1733 people with acute ischaemic stroke: Lp(a) was linked to large-artery aetiology, most strongly under age 60 (adjusted OR 4.04 at 100 nmol/L or higher). Recurrence HR was 2.40 (1.05 to 5.47) under 60 [[Arnold2021]].
- Lowering Lp(a) has not yet been shown to help. Lp(a)HORIZON topline results in September 2026 reported pelacarsen lowered Lp(a) but did not reduce the primary composite [[Sabouret2026]]. Extended analyses found no benefit (on-treatment HR 0.98, 0.85 to 1.12; total events rate ratio 1.00, 0.87 to 1.14) [[Nissen2026]].
- The registry entry for that trial shows completion on 2026-07-16 with no results posted [[NCT04023552]]. Other agents are still in outcome trials (Hypothesis that earlier or stronger lowering would help) [[Sabouret2026]].

### prevent · Antiplatelets after stroke or TIA
- Aspirin acts early. Pooling 12 trials and 15 778 people, aspirin reduced 6-week recurrent ischaemic stroke by about 60% (HR 0.42, 0.32 to 0.55) and disabling or fatal ischaemic stroke by about 70% (HR 0.29, 0.20 to 0.42). There was no benefit beyond 12 weeks (OR 0.97, 0.84 to 1.12) in the aspirin-only comparison [[Rothwell2016]].
- CAPRIE: 19 185 people; clopidogrel 75 mg vs aspirin 325 mg. Annual risk 5.32% vs 5.83% (RRR 8.7%, 0.3 to 16.5), with no major safety differences [[CAPRIE1996]].
- PRoFESS: 20 332 people; aspirin with extended-release dipyridamole vs clopidogrel. Recurrent stroke 9.0% vs 8.8% (HR 1.01, 0.92 to 1.11), intracranial haemorrhage HR 1.42 (1.11 to 1.83). Neither was superior [[Sacco2008]].
- CHANCE (China): within 24 hours of minor stroke or high-risk TIA, clopidogrel plus aspirin for 21 days, then clopidogrel alone, vs aspirin. Stroke at 90 days 8.2% vs 11.7% (HR 0.68, 0.57 to 0.81). Moderate or severe haemorrhage 0.3% in each group [[Wang2013]].
- POINT (international): clopidogrel plus aspirin for 90 days vs aspirin. Major ischaemic events 5.0% vs 6.5% (HR 0.75, 0.59 to 0.95). Major haemorrhage 0.9% vs 0.4% (HR 2.32, 1.10 to 4.87). Most events occurred in the first week [[Johnston2018]].
- CHANCE and POINT pooled, 10 051 people: 6.5% vs 9.1% at 90 days (HR 0.70, 0.61 to 0.81), and 5.2% vs 7.8% in the first 21 days (HR 0.66, 0.56 to 0.77), with no benefit from day 22 to 90 [[Pan2019]].
- BMJ meta-analysis of 3 trials and 10 447 people: non-fatal recurrent stroke RR 0.70 (0.61 to 0.80), about 20 fewer strokes per 1000 and possibly 2 more moderate or severe extracranial bleeds per 1000. Stopping within 21 days, and possibly as early as 10 days, is likely to maximise benefit and minimise harm [[Hao2018]].
- INSPIRES: 6100 people, 87.2% treated after 24 hours and within 72 hours, presumed atherosclerotic cause. New stroke 7.3% vs 9.2% (HR 0.79, 0.66 to 0.94); moderate-to-severe bleeding 0.9% vs 0.4% (HR 2.08, 1.07 to 4.04) [[Gao2023]].
- THALES: 11 016 people, NIHSS 5 or less or TIA; ticagrelor plus aspirin for 30 days vs aspirin. Stroke or death 5.5% vs 6.6% (HR 0.83, 0.71 to 0.96). Severe bleeding 0.5% vs 0.1%. Disability did not differ [[Johnston2020]].
- CHANCE-2: 6412 CYP2C19 loss-of-function carriers in China, with aspirin for 21 days in both groups. Stroke at 90 days 6.0% with ticagrelor vs 7.6% with clopidogrel (HR 0.77, 0.64 to 0.94). Severe or moderate bleeding 0.3% in each; any bleeding 5.3% vs 2.5% [[Wang2021]].
- Long-term dual therapy harms. In SPS3, 3020 people with lacunar stroke over a mean 3.4 years: recurrent stroke HR 0.92 (0.72 to 1.16), major haemorrhage HR 1.97 (1.41 to 2.71), all-cause mortality HR 1.52 (1.14 to 2.04) [[Benavente2012]].

### af · Why atrial fibrillation matters and how risk is scored
- Atrial fibrillation is described as the causal mechanism of about 20% of acute ischaemic strokes in a state public health review [[Tai2023]].
- CHA2DS2-VASc was refined from the 2006 Birmingham/NICE schema and tested in 1084 Euro Heart Survey patients. The C-statistic was modest (0.606), and those classed as low risk had no thromboembolic events [[Lip2010]].
- CHA2DS2-VASc items, scored 0 to 9: heart failure 1, hypertension 1, age 75 or over 2, diabetes 1, previous thromboembolism 2, vascular disease 1, age 65 to 74 1, female sex 1 [[Olesen2011]].
- Danish cohort of people admitted with AF and not on a vitamin K antagonist: thromboembolism per 100 person-years at 1 year by score (the outcome includes peripheral embolism, ischaemic stroke and pulmonary embolism): 0 = 0.78, 1 = 2.01, 2 = 3.71, 3 = 5.92, 4 = 9.27, 5 = 15.26, 6 = 19.74, 7 = 21.50, 8 = 22.38, 9 = 23.64 [[Olesen2011]].
- Rates vary between cohorts. Olesen records 0.78 per 100 person-years at score 0 where Lip recorded no events, and 1-year rates in Olesen exceed its own 10-year rates at every score (for example 9.27 vs 6.46 at score 4) [[Olesen2011,Lip2010]].
- Swedish cohort of 182 678 people with AF: C-statistics for thromboembolism were 0.67 for CHA2DS2-VASc and 0.66 for CHADS2. Peripheral artery disease (HR 1.22), vascular disease (HR 1.14), prior MI (HR 1.09) and female sex (HR 1.17) were independent predictors [[Friberg2012]].
- The 2024 ESC/EACTS guideline indicates CHA2DS2-VA as a replacement for CHA2DS2-VASc when no locally validated tool exists, removing sex from the score. This is taken from a paper describing the guideline, as the guideline itself was blocked [[ClueEspuny2025,VanGelder2024]].
- Removing the sex point lowers the maximum score to 8 (derived from the item list) [[Olesen2011,ClueEspuny2025]].
- Evidence for the change: in 229 565 Finns with new AF, female sex was linked to a 20% to 30% higher ischaemic stroke rate in 2007 to 2008, but this attenuated to non-significance by 2018. Crude incidence was 21.1 vs 14.9 per 1000 patient-years [[Teppo2024]].
- HAS-BLED was developed from Euro Heart Survey data (53 major bleeds, 1.5%, during 1-year follow-up) with a C-statistic of 0.72 [[Pisters2010]].
- The 2023 ACC/AHA/ACCP/HRS guideline added new recommendations on thromboembolic risk assessment, anticoagulation and left atrial appendage occlusion [[Joglar2024]].

### af · Anticoagulant evidence
- Adjusted-dose warfarin reduced stroke by 64% (49 to 74) vs control in 6 trials and 2900 participants. Antiplatelets reduced it by 22% (6 to 35). Warfarin beat antiplatelets by 39% (22 to 52) [[Hart2007]].
- DOACs vs warfarin, 71 683 participants: stroke or systemic embolism RR 0.81 (0.73 to 0.91), haemorrhagic stroke RR 0.49 (0.38 to 0.64), intracranial haemorrhage RR 0.48 (0.39 to 0.59), mortality RR 0.90 (0.85 to 0.95), gastrointestinal bleeding RR 1.25 (1.01 to 1.55) [[Ruff2014]].
- RE-LY, per year, warfarin vs dabigatran 110 mg vs 150 mg: stroke or embolism 1.69% vs 1.53% vs 1.11%; major bleeding 3.36% vs 2.71% vs 3.11%; haemorrhagic stroke 0.38% vs 0.12% vs 0.10% [[Connolly2009]].
- ARISTOTLE, apixaban vs warfarin per year: stroke or embolism 1.27% vs 1.60% (HR 0.79), major bleeding 2.13% vs 3.09% (HR 0.69), death 3.52% vs 3.94% (HR 0.89) [[Granger2011]].
- ROCKET AF, rivaroxaban vs warfarin: 1.7% vs 2.2% per year (HR 0.79, noninferior); intention-to-treat HR 0.88 (P=0.12 for superiority); intracranial haemorrhage 0.5% vs 0.7% [[Patel2011]].
- ENGAGE AF, high-dose edoxaban vs warfarin: 1.18% vs 1.50% per year (HR 0.79); major bleeding 2.75% vs 3.43% (HR 0.80) [[Giugliano2013]].
- AVERROES, people unsuitable for warfarin: apixaban vs aspirin 1.6% vs 3.7% per year (HR 0.45, 0.32 to 0.62); major bleeding 1.4% vs 1.2% (HR 1.13, not significant) [[Connolly2011]].

### af · Timing after a stroke
- ELAN: 2013 people. Early start was within 48 hours for minor or moderate stroke and day 6 or 7 for major. Later start was day 3 or 4 (minor), day 6 or 7 (moderate) and day 12 to 14 (major). Composite at 30 days 2.9% vs 4.1% (risk difference -1.18 points, -2.84 to 0.47). Symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage 0.2% in both [[Fischer2023]].
- TIMING (registry-randomised, Sweden): 888 people; 4 days or fewer vs 5 to 10 days. Composite at 90 days 6.89% vs 8.68% (P=0.004 for noninferiority); no symptomatic intracerebral haemorrhage in either group [[Oldgren2022]].
- OPTIMAS (UK): 3621 people; 4 days or fewer vs 7 to 14 days. Composite at 90 days 3.3% in each group (noninferior; p=0.0003). Symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage 0.6% vs 0.7% [[Werring2024]].
- START: 200 people; response-adaptive design. No clearly superior day was identified, but the evidence favoured earlier starts in the first 2 weeks [[Warach2025]].
- CATALYST pooled TIMING, ELAN, OPTIMAS and START, 5441 participants: starting within 4 days vs day 5 or later reduced the 30-day composite (2.1% vs 3.0%, OR 0.70, 0.50 to 0.98) and recurrent ischaemic stroke (1.7% vs 2.6%, OR 0.66, 0.45 to 0.96). Symptomatic intracerebral haemorrhage was 0.4% in each group (OR 1.02, 0.43 to 2.46) [[Dehbi2025]].

### af · Closing the left atrial appendage
- PROTECT AF: 707 people, device vs warfarin. Primary event rate 2.3 vs 3.8 per 100 patient-years (rate ratio 0.60); cardiovascular mortality HR 0.40 (0.21 to 0.75) [[Reddy2014]].
- PREVAIL and PROTECT AF at 5 years: composite HR 0.820 and all stroke or embolism HR 0.961, both not significant. Ischaemic stroke or embolism was numerically higher with closure (HR 1.71, p=0.080). Haemorrhagic stroke (HR 0.20) and post-procedure bleeding (HR 0.48) favoured closure [[Reddy2017]].
- LAAOS III, closure added during cardiac surgery, most people staying on anticoagulation: stroke or embolism 4.8% vs 7.0% (HR 0.67, 0.53 to 0.85) [[Whitlock2021]].
- OPTION, after catheter ablation: 1600 people. Non-procedural bleeding 8.5% vs 18.1% (superior); death, stroke or embolism 5.3% vs 5.8% (noninferior) at 36 months [[Wazni2025]].
- CHAMPION-AF: 3000 people suitable for anticoagulation, device vs NOAC. Cardiovascular death, stroke or embolism 5.7% vs 4.8% at 3 years (difference 0.9 points, -0.8 to 2.6; noninferior with a 4.8-point margin). Non-procedural bleeding 10.9% vs 19.0% (HR 0.55) [[Doshi2026]].
- CLOSURE-AF (Germany): 912 people, mean age 77.9, CHA2DS2-VASc 5.2, HAS-BLED 3.0. Primary composite 16.8 vs 13.3 per 100 patient-years with device vs best medical care. Closure was not noninferior (P=0.44). Serious adverse events 82.5% vs 77.4% [[Landmesser2026]].
- The closure trials disagree by population. OPTION and CHAMPION-AF, in people who could take anticoagulants, were noninferior on efficacy with less later bleeding; CLOSURE-AF, in older people at high risk of both stroke and bleeding, failed noninferiority [[Wazni2025,Doshi2026,Landmesser2026]].

### af · Device-detected AF and embolic stroke of undetermined source
- NOAH-AFNET 6: 2536 people with atrial high-rate episodes, median 2.8 hours. Edoxaban vs placebo: composite HR 0.81 (0.60 to 1.08); safety composite HR 1.31 (1.02 to 1.67). Stroke was about 1% per patient-year in both groups [[Kirchhof2023]].
- ARTESiA: 4012 people with subclinical AF of 6 minutes to 24 hours. Apixaban vs aspirin: stroke or embolism 0.78% vs 1.24% per patient-year (HR 0.63, 0.45 to 0.88); major bleeding 1.71% vs 0.94% (HR 1.80, 1.26 to 2.57) [[Healey2024]].
- Combined, these two trials give ischaemic stroke RR 0.68 (0.50 to 0.92) and major bleeding RR 1.62 (1.05 to 2.50), with no reduction in cardiovascular death (RR 0.95) or all-cause mortality (RR 1.08). The results were consistent (I2 = 0%) [[McIntyre2024Meta]].
- In ARTESiA, neither frequency nor longest duration of subclinical AF predicted stroke risk or changed apixaban's effect [[McIntyre2024]].
- LOOP: 6004 older people with risk factors. Loop recording found AF in 31.8% vs 12.2% (HR 3.17) but stroke or embolism was not significantly reduced (4.5% vs 5.6%, HR 0.80, 0.61 to 1.05) [[Svendsen2021]].
- CRYSTAL AF: after cryptogenic stroke, an implantable monitor found AF in 8.9% vs 1.4% by 6 months and 12.4% vs 2.0% by 12 months [[Sanna2014]].
- ESUS was proposed in 2014 for non-lacunar infarcts without proximal stenosis or a cardioembolic source. Cryptogenic strokes were put at about 25% of ischaemic strokes, and the authors predicted anticoagulants would work better than antiplatelets [[Hart2014]].
- That prediction failed:
  - NAVIGATE ESUS: rivaroxaban 15 mg vs aspirin, recurrence 5.1% vs 4.8% per year (HR 1.07), major bleeding 1.8% vs 0.7% (HR 2.72) [[Hart2018]]
  - RE-SPECT ESUS: dabigatran vs aspirin, 4.1% vs 4.8% per year (HR 0.85, 0.69 to 1.03) [[Diener2019]]
  - ARCADIA: apixaban vs aspirin in atrial cardiopathy without AF, 4.4% vs 4.4% per year (HR 1.00), stopped for futility [[Kamel2024]]

### carotid · Symptomatic stenosis
- NASCET, 659 people with 70 to 99% stenosis: any ipsilateral stroke at 2 years 26% medical vs 9% surgical (absolute reduction 17 ± 3.5%). Major or fatal ipsilateral stroke 13.1% vs 2.5% [[Barnett1991]].
- NASCET, moderate stenosis: at 50 to 69%, 5-year ipsilateral stroke 15.7% surgical vs 22.2% medical (NNT 15). Below 50%, 14.9% vs 18.7% (P=0.16, no benefit). Benefit in severe stenosis lasted to 8 years [[Barnett1998]].
- ECST, 3024 people: surgical major stroke or death risk 7.0%, not varying by stenosis severity. On ECST's own measure, surgery was worthwhile above about 80%: 3-year major stroke or death 26.5% control vs 14.9% surgery [[ECST1998]].
- NASCET and ECST used different measurement methods. Re-measuring ECST angiograms the NASCET way gave highly consistent results [[Rothwell2003]].
- Pooled 5-year absolute reduction in ipsilateral ischaemic stroke, 6092 people: below 30% -2.2% (harm), 30 to 49% 3.2%, 50 to 69% 4.6%, 70% or more without near-occlusion 16.0%. Near-occlusion 5.6% at 2 years and -1.7% at 5 years [[Rothwell2003]].
- Timing and subgroup NNT over 5 years for 50% or more stenosis: within 2 weeks of last event 5, after more than 12 weeks 125; men 9, women 36; age 75 or over 5, under 65 18. The authors state that surgery should ideally occur within 2 weeks [[Rothwell2004]].

### carotid · Asymptomatic stenosis
- ACAS: 60% or more stenosis; 5-year ipsilateral stroke plus perioperative stroke or death 5.1% vs 11.0% (risk reduction 53%, 22 to 72), conditional on surgery with under 3% perioperative risk [[ACAS1995]].
- ACST-1 at 5 years: stroke risk 3.8% vs 11% excluding perioperative events. Net 6.4% vs 11.8% including the 3.1% perioperative risk [[Halliday2004]].
- ACST-1 at 10 years: net 13.4% vs 17.9% (gain 4.6%, 1.2 to 7.9). Benefit was seen under age 75 but not in older people [[Halliday2010]].
- Stenosis degree matters in contemporary cohorts: OxVasc 5-year ipsilateral stroke was 14.6% at 70 to 99% and none in 154 people at 50 to 69%. Cohorts show severity dependence (OR 2.9) where trials did not (OR 0.8) [[Howard2021]].
- SPACE-2 stopped early at 513 of 3640 planned: primary endpoint 2.5% endarterectomy, 4.4% stenting, 3.1% medical therapy alone, not significantly different [[Reiff2022]].
- ECST-2 interim at 2 years: 429 people with 50% or more stenosis and low-to-intermediate predicted risk. Win ratio 1.01 (0.60 to 1.70), no benefit shown from adding revascularisation. The trial is ongoing [[Donners2025]].
- CREST-2 (NEJM 2026): 70% or more asymptomatic stenosis, intensive medical management with or without revascularisation. Stenting trial (1245 people): 4-year events 6.0% medical vs 2.8% stenting (P=0.02). Endarterectomy trial (1240 people): 5.3% vs 3.7% (P=0.24). Periprocedural events: 8 with stenting vs 0 medical; 9 with endarterectomy vs 3 medical [[Brott2026]].
- Modern trials disagree on the size of the gain. CREST-2's stenting arm showed benefit, whereas its endarterectomy arm, SPACE-2 and ECST-2 did not. Absolute medical-arm risks are far below those of ACST-1 (11% at 5 years) [[Brott2026,Reiff2022,Donners2025,Halliday2004]].

### carotid · Stenting vs endarterectomy
- CREST, 2502 people: 4-year composite 7.2% stenting vs 6.8% surgery (HR 1.11, 0.81 to 1.51). Periprocedural stroke 4.1% vs 2.3%; MI 1.1% vs 2.3% [[Brott2010]].
- CREST at 10 years: composite 11.8% vs 9.9% (HR 1.10, 0.83 to 1.44); post-procedural ipsilateral stroke 6.9% vs 5.6% [[Brott2016]].
- ICSS, 1713 people with symptomatic stenosis: 5-year fatal or disabling stroke 6.4% vs 6.5% (HR 1.06). Any stroke 15.2% stenting vs 9.4% surgery (HR 1.71), mainly non-disabling. Function did not differ [[Bonati2015]].
- ICSS restenosis of 50% or more: 40.7% stenting vs 29.6% surgery at 5 years (HR 1.43) [[Bonati2018]].
- ACST-2, 3625 asymptomatic people: procedural disabling stroke or death about 1% with either. Five-year non-procedural fatal or disabling stroke 2.5% in each group [[Halliday2021]].

### carotid · Vertebral and intracranial stenosis
- VAST: 115 people with vertebral stenosis of 50% or more; 30-day event 5% stenting vs 2% medical. The medical-arm stroke risk was low [[Compter2015]].
- Pooled VIST, VAST and SAMMPRIS, 354 people: any stroke HR 0.81 (0.45 to 1.44) with stenting. Periprocedural stroke or death 16% intracranial vs 1% extracranial [[Markus2019]].
- WASID: warfarin vs aspirin 1300 mg for 50 to 99% intracranial stenosis. Primary endpoint 21.8% vs 22.1%, death 9.7% vs 4.3%, major haemorrhage 8.3% vs 3.2% [[Chimowitz2005]].
- SAMMPRIS: 451 people with 70 to 99% stenosis, Wingspan stent plus aggressive medical therapy vs medical therapy alone. Thirty-day stroke or death 14.7% vs 5.8%; 1-year primary endpoint 20.0% vs 12.2% [[Chimowitz2011]].
- SAMMPRIS final: median 32.4 months, primary endpoint 23% vs 15%. Absolute difference 9.0% at year 3 (1.5 to 16.5) [[Derdeyn2014]].
- CASSISS: 358 people with refined selection, beyond 3 weeks from symptoms. Primary endpoint 8.0% vs 7.2% (HR 1.10, 0.52 to 2.35); 3-year mortality 4.4% vs 1.3% (not significant) [[Gao2022]].
- Intracranial large-artery disease has been described as likely the most common stroke subtype worldwide [[Gorelick2008]].

### carotid · PFO
- PFO occurs in about 1 in 4 adults [[Messe2020]].
- RoPE score (0 to 10): no hypertension 1, no diabetes 1, no prior stroke or TIA 1, non-smoker 1, cortical infarct on imaging 1, plus age 18 to 29 = 5, 30 to 39 = 4, 40 to 49 = 3, 50 to 59 = 2, 60 to 69 = 1, 70 or over = 0 [[Chambers2026,Nouh2016]].
- RoPE strata: PFO prevalence rose from 23% (score 0 to 3) to 73% (9 to 10), attributable fraction from about 0% to 90%, while 2-year stroke or TIA recurrence fell from 20% to 2% [[Kent2013]].
- By stratum, PFO-attributable fraction and 2-year recurrence: 0 to 3 = 0%, 20%; 4 = 38%, 12%; 5 = 34%, 7%; 6 = 62%, 8%; 7 = 72%, 6%; 8 = 84%, 6%; 9 to 10 = 88%, 2% [[Nouh2016]].
- PASCAL combines high RoPE (7 or more) with high-risk PFO features (large shunt and/or atrial septal aneurysm). Neither present = unlikely; one present = possible; both = probable [[Chambers2026,Kent2021]].
- RESPECT extended, 980 people: recurrent ischaemic stroke 0.58 vs 1.07 per 100 patient-years (HR 0.55, 0.31 to 0.999); venous thromboembolism more common with closure [[Saver2017]].
- CLOSE, 663 people with high-risk features: stroke 0 of 238 vs 14 of 235 (HR 0.03, 0 to 0.26); AF 4.6% vs 0.9% [[Mas2017]].
- REDUCE, 664 people: clinical stroke 1.4% vs 5.4% (HR 0.23, 0.09 to 0.62); AF 6.6% after closure [[Sondergaard2017]].
- DEFENSE-PFO, 120 people with high-risk PFO: primary events 0 vs 6 of 60; 2-year event rate 12.9% in the medication-only group [[Lee2018]].
- SCOPE pooled 6 trials and 3740 people aged 18 to 60: annual stroke 1.09% medical vs 0.47% closure (adjusted HR 0.41, 0.28 to 0.60). Median follow-up was 57 months [[Kent2021]].
- SCOPE by PASCAL: unlikely HR 1.14 (0.53 to 2.46), possible 0.38 (0.22 to 0.65), probable 0.10 (0.03 to 0.35). Two-year absolute reduction: -0.7%, 2.1%, 2.1%. Added AF beyond day 45: 4.41%, 1.53%, 0.65% [[Kent2021]].
- SCOPE at 5 years: probable (37.0% of people) 2.5% fewer strokes vs 1.3% more late AF; possible (48.4%) 3.4% fewer vs 1.1% more; unlikely (14.6%) 0.4% more strokes and 4.6% more late AF. The authors read this as net benefit in 4 of every 5 and net harm in 1 of every 5 [[Saver2026]].
- AAN 2020 (level C): under 60 with an embolic-appearing infarct and no other mechanism, clinicians may recommend closure after discussing an absolute 5-year stroke reduction of 3.4%, a periprocedural complication rate of 3.9% and added non-periprocedural AF of 0.33% per year [[Messe2020]].
- ANZ consensus 2026: PASCAL should be incorporated into decisions and high RoPE alone should not be the principal basis; decisions by a multidisciplinary heart-brain team [[Chambers2026]].
- Older people remain open. A trial-feasibility study estimated 5-year recurrence of 13.7% (Early evidence) [[Wang2024]]. Observational data at age 60 or over gave RR 0.49 for recurrent stroke or TIA (Early evidence) [[deDeus2026]].

### Exam hooks
- INTERSTROKE: ten factors, about 90% of PAR, hypertension the largest at 47.9% [[ODonnell2016]].
- A 10 mm Hg fall in systolic BP reduces stroke by about 27% [[Ettehad2016]].
- PROGRESS: combination therapy reduced stroke, single-agent perindopril did not, and benefit included non-hypertensive people [[PROGRESS2001]].
- SPARCL: fewer strokes overall but more haemorrhagic strokes [[Amarenco2006]].
- Dual antiplatelet benefit sits in the first 21 days after minor stroke or high-risk TIA [[Pan2019]]; long-term therapy in lacunar stroke raised bleeding and death [[Benavente2012]].
- CHA2DS2-VASc has two 2-point items: age 75 or over and previous thromboembolism [[Olesen2011]].
- Warfarin 64%, antiplatelets 22% stroke reduction in AF [[Hart2007]]; DOACs halve intracranial haemorrhage vs warfarin [[Ruff2014]].
- Early DOAC (within 4 days) is safe and reduces recurrent ischaemic stroke in pooled data [[Dehbi2025]].
- No empirical anticoagulant has beaten aspirin in ESUS: NAVIGATE, RE-SPECT, ARCADIA [[Hart2018,Diener2019,Kamel2024]].
- Symptomatic carotid: 70% or more gives 16% absolute 5-year gain, 50 to 69% gives 4.6%, below 30% is harmed [[Rothwell2003]].
- Timing: NNT 5 within 2 weeks vs 125 after 12 weeks [[Rothwell2004]].
- CREST trade-off: more periprocedural stroke with stenting, more MI with surgery [[Brott2010]].
- SAMMPRIS: stenting worse than aggressive medical therapy at 30 days (14.7% vs 5.8%) [[Chimowitz2011]].
- PASCAL: RoPE of 7 or more plus large shunt or atrial septal aneurysm = probable [[Chambers2026]].

### In plain words
- Most strokes are linked to things that can be changed, and high blood pressure is the biggest one [[ODonnell2016,GBD2021Stroke]].
- Even one cigarette a day raises stroke risk. Cutting down helps less than people expect, and stopping is the goal [[Hackshaw2018]].
- People who stopped smoking after a stroke or TIA had fewer further problems than those who kept smoking [[Epstein2017]].
- After a small stroke or TIA, the first days carry the most risk, so early treatment matters [[Rothwell2016,Johnston2018]].
- Two blood-thinning tablets can help for a short time after a small stroke, but staying on both for years raises bleeding risk [[Pan2019,Benavente2012]].
- An irregular heartbeat called atrial fibrillation can form clots that travel to the brain. Anticoagulants lower this risk a lot, more than aspirin does [[Hart2007]].
- If a neck artery is badly narrowed and has caused symptoms, surgery works best when done soon, ideally within 2 weeks [[Rothwell2004]].
- If a narrowed neck artery has caused no symptoms, medicines work much better than they used to, and doctors are still working out who gains from a procedure [[Brott2026,Donners2025]].
- About 1 in 4 adults has a small hole between the upper heart chambers. Often it did not cause the stroke, so doctors look at the whole picture before closing it [[Messe2020,Kent2021]].

## Open questions and controversies
- How low for BP after stroke: SPS3 and RESPECT were individually non-significant at below 130 and below 120 mm Hg targets [[Benavente2013,Kitagawa2019]]; pooled data support below 130/80 mm Hg [[Kitagawa2019]]; ESPRIT supports below 120 mm Hg with no heterogeneity by prior stroke [[Liu2024]]. No stroke-only trial below 120 mm Hg is reported here; status open.
- How low for LDL: Treat Stroke to Target supports below 70 mg/dL [[Amarenco2020]]; FOURIER observational data suggest benefit below 40 mg/dL [[Monguillon2026]]; SPARCL showed more haemorrhagic stroke with high-dose statin [[Amarenco2006]], while later data show no clear excess [[Amarenco2020,Monguillon2026]]. Status open.
- Salt substitute size of effect: the trial reports a 14% lower stroke rate [[Neal2021]]; a guideline review quotes a 22% relative reduction (0.34% absolute) for this trial [[Razavi2025]]. The figures do not match and the review's source could not be traced here; the trial's own number is preferred.
- Which DAPT and for whom: clopidogrel-based [[Wang2013,Johnston2018]], extended window [[Gao2023]], ticagrelor in carriers [[Wang2021]]; CHANCE, INSPIRES and CHANCE-2 all enrolled in China. CHANCE-2's authors limit their conclusion to Chinese carriers [[Wang2021]].
- Carotid revascularisation in asymptomatic people: CREST-2 stenting benefit [[Brott2026]] vs neutral SPACE-2 and ECST-2 [[Reiff2022,Donners2025]]. Severity may be the key: cohort data show high risk only at severe stenosis [[Howard2021]].
- Left atrial appendage closure vs DOAC: positive in OPTION and CHAMPION-AF [[Wazni2025,Doshi2026]], negative in CLOSURE-AF [[Landmesser2026]]. Numerically more ischaemic stroke with closure in earlier trials [[Reddy2017]]. Status open.
- Device-detected AF: anticoagulation lowers ischaemic stroke and raises bleeding, with no mortality gain [[McIntyre2024Meta]]; screening raised detection without a significant stroke reduction [[Svendsen2021]].
- PFO closure above age 60: not tested in the pooled trials [[Kent2021]]; observational and modelling data only [[deDeus2026,Wang2024]]. Atrial arrhythmia after closure may be mostly early and transient in one meta-analysis (RR 2.27 overall; 0.60 after the first month, CI 0.02 to 19.88) [[Spyropoulou2026]], yet SCOPE reports excess AF beyond day 45 in the unlikely group [[Kent2021]].
- CPAP after stroke: SAVE was neutral with 3.3 hours a night of use [[McEvoy2016]]; Sleep SMART results pending [[NCT03812653]].
- Lp(a): strong genetic link to ischaemic stroke [[Langsted2019]] but neutral first outcome trial of lowering [[Nissen2026,Sabouret2026]]; full stroke-specific data not yet seen.
