Stroke, explained

A stroke happens when part of the brain loses its blood supply, or when a blood vessel in or around the brain bleeds, and brain cells are hurt or die [[Sacco2013]]. About 2 in 3 strokes come from a blocked artery and roughly 1 in 3 are bleeds; there were 11.9 million new strokes worldwide in 2021, and about 1 in 4 adults will have a stroke at some point in life [[Feigin2024,Feigin2018]]. Many people live for years afterwards: almost 94 million people are alive today after having one [[Feigin2025]].

For students

Every module has an Exam hook box and a citation trail. The NIHSS, ICH score, ABCD2, CHA2DS2-VASc and ASPECTS tools are teaching aids, scored as published. Finish with flashcards, memory aids and a quiz.

For people who have had a stroke, families and carers

Look for the In plain words boxes. Recovery differs from person to person: outcome figures are population statistics, not predictions for one person. Bring questions to your own stroke team: the app reports what guidelines, trials and labels say, not what you should take, stop or change.
