Stenosis without symptoms, then and now

Early trials showed a modest gain from surgery. Modern trials compare a procedure with much better medical treatment, and medical-arm risks are far below those of ACST-1 [[Brott2026,Halliday2004]].

Outcomes and durations differ between trials, so compare within a row [[ACAS1995,Halliday2004,Halliday2010,Reiff2022,Brott2026]].

CREST-2 (NEJM 2026)

70% or more asymptomatic stenosis, intensive medical management with or without revascularisation. Stenting trial, 1245 people: 4-year events 6.0% medical vs 2.8% stenting (P=0.02). Endarterectomy trial, 1240 people: 5.3% vs 3.7% (P=0.24). Periprocedural events: 8 with stenting vs 0 medical; 9 with endarterectomy vs 3 medical [[Brott2026]].

ECST-2 interim

429 people with 50% or more stenosis and low-to-intermediate predicted risk. At 2 years the win ratio was 1.01 (0.60 to 1.70), with no benefit shown from adding revascularisation. The trial is ongoing [[Donners2025]].

Does severity matter?

In OxVasc, 5-year ipsilateral stroke was 14.6% at 70 to 99% and none in 154 people at 50 to 69%. Cohorts show severity dependence (OR 2.9) where trials did not (OR 0.8) [[Howard2021]].

ACST-1 and age

At 10 years, benefit was seen under age 75 but not in older people. ACAS's gain was conditional on surgery with under 3% perioperative risk [[Halliday2010,ACAS1995]].

A live disagreement. CREST-2's stenting arm showed benefit, whereas its endarterectomy arm, SPACE-2 and ECST-2 did not [[Brott2026,Reiff2022,Donners2025]]. Severity may be the key, since cohort data show high risk only at severe stenosis [[Howard2021]].

In plain wordsIf a narrowed neck artery has caused no symptoms, medicines work much better than they used to, and doctors are still working out who gains from a procedure [[Brott2026,Donners2025]].

Vertebral and intracranial stenosis

Intracranial large-artery disease has been described as likely the most common stroke subtype worldwide [[Gorelick2008]]. Here, stenting has not beaten medical treatment.

SAMMPRIS

451 people, 70 to 99% stenosis. 30-day stroke or death 14.7% stenting vs 5.8% medical; 1-year primary endpoint 20.0% vs 12.2% [[Chimowitz2011]]. Final, median 32.4 months: 23% vs 15% [[Derdeyn2014]].

WASID

Warfarin vs aspirin 1300 mg for 50 to 99% stenosis. Primary endpoint 21.8% vs 22.1%, death 9.7% vs 4.3%, major haemorrhage 8.3% vs 3.2% [[Chimowitz2005]].

CASSISS

358 people, refined selection, beyond 3 weeks from symptoms. Primary endpoint 8.0% vs 7.2% (HR 1.10, 0.52 to 2.35) [[Gao2022]].

Vertebral: VAST and pooled

VAST, 115 people: 30-day event 5% stenting vs 2% medical [[Compter2015]]. VIST, VAST and SAMMPRIS pooled, 354 people: any stroke HR 0.81 (0.45 to 1.44). Periprocedural stroke or death 16% intracranial vs 1% extracranial [[Markus2019]].

Exam hookSAMMPRIS: stenting worse than aggressive medical therapy at 30 days (14.7% vs 5.8%) [[Chimowitz2011]].

Patent foramen ovale (PFO)

A PFO occurs in about 1 in 4 adults [[Messe2020]], so finding one after a stroke does not show it caused the stroke. RoPE estimates how likely that is. PASCAL adds features of the PFO itself.

What statements say

AAN 2020 (level C): under 60 with an embolic-appearing infarct and no other mechanism, clinicians may recommend closure after discussing an absolute 5-year stroke reduction of 3.4%, a periprocedural complication rate of 3.9% and added non-periprocedural AF of 0.33% per year [[Messe2020]].

ANZ consensus 2026: PASCAL should be incorporated into decisions and high RoPE alone should not be the principal basis, with decisions by a multidisciplinary heart-brain team [[Chambers2026]].

Early evidence

Still open

Above age 60: not tested in the pooled trials [[Kent2021]]. A feasibility study estimated 5-year recurrence of 13.7% [[Wang2024]]. Observational data at 60 or over gave RR 0.49 for recurrent stroke or TIA [[deDeus2026]].

AF after closure: one meta-analysis suggests it is mostly early and transient (RR 2.27 overall; 0.60 after the first month, CI 0.02 to 19.88) [[Spyropoulou2026]], yet SCOPE reports excess AF beyond day 45 in the unlikely group [[Kent2021]].

Exam hookPASCAL: RoPE of 7 or more plus large shunt or atrial septal aneurysm = probable [[Chambers2026]].
In plain wordsAbout 1 in 4 adults has a small hole between the upper heart chambers. Often it did not cause the stroke, so doctors look at the whole picture before closing it [[Messe2020,Kent2021]].
