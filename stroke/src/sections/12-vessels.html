Part 1 · Understanding stroke

The brain's blood supply

Which artery is blocked decides what a stroke does. Pick an artery and a side of the brain, and see its territory wash in, then which half of the body and which abilities it maps to. Then block a vessel in the circle of Willis and watch the detours open.

The anterior circulation arises from the two internal carotid arteries and the posterior circulation from the two vertebral arteries. The circle of Willis joins them as an anastomosis [[Rosner2023]]. The brain receives 15-20% of cardiac output. One group calls that figure a "widely accepted dogma": in their 139 subjects the share fell 1.3% per decade and was higher in women [[Xing2017]].

70%

In plain wordsWhere the stroke is decides what it affects. A stroke in one artery can affect speech, or the leg more than the arm, for example [[Lo2025]]. The left side of the brain mostly works the right side of the body, and the other way round [[Lo2025]].

The circle lets one carotid or the vertebrobasilar system feed the other side, if the connecting vessels are there. Pick a block and an anatomy, then press Play. The clot forms, downstream vessels pale, and any working detour lights up and runs against its usual direction.

Lateral medulla: Wallenberg syndrome

Lateral medullary infarction comes from disease of the posterior inferior cerebellar artery (PICA) or the vertebral artery, and each small structure gives one sign [[Lo2025,EastonCarr2026]]. Move the slider to grow the wedge, or choose a structure.

Schematic: positions of structures are approximate. The drawing shows a lesion on one side of the medulla, viewed in cross-section with the back of the brainstem at the top.

Exam hookWallenberg gives ipsilateral face and contralateral body pain and temperature loss, with ipsilateral Horner syndrome [[Lo2025]].

130 people, pure lateral medullary infarction

Vertebral artery disease in 67%, PICA disease in 10%. Mechanisms: large vessel 50%, dissection 15%, small vessel 13%, cardiac embolism 5%. Rostral lesions favoured dysphagia and facial paresis [[Kim2003]].

The posterior circulation

New England registry, 407 people

59% had stroke with no TIA, 24% TIA then stroke, 16% TIA only. Embolism 40% (24% cardiac, 14% intra-arterial, 2% both), large-artery haemodynamic mechanisms 32%. 30-day mortality 3.6%, and the distal territory was most often affected [[Caplan2004]].

What people came in with

Symptoms: dizziness 47%, one-sided limb weakness 41%, dysarthria 31%, headache 28%, nausea or vomiting 27%. Signs: weakness 38%, gait ataxia 31%, limb ataxia 30%, dysarthria 28%, nystagmus 24% [[Searls2012]].

By territory

Proximal lesions featured dysphagia and Horner syndrome; middle territory, weakness and facial palsy; distal territory, sensory loss, lethargy and visual field loss [[Searls2012]].

PCA infarcts, 79 people

Causes: cardiac embolism 41%, proximal arterial disease 32%, cryptogenic 10%, intrinsic PCA disease 9%. Visual abnormality in 84%, motor, cognitive or ataxic signs in 25%, sensory signs in 15% [[Yamamoto1999]]. A second series reports common one-sided headache and embolism in up to half [[Brandt2000]].

Artery of Percheron

Infarcts fell into four imaging patterns at 43%, 38%, 14% and 5%. The midbrain "V sign" was seen in 67% of cases with midbrain involvement [[Lazzaro2010]].

Large MCA infarcts for comparison

Lausanne: 208 cases, 7.6% of infarcts; mortality 17%, severe disability 50%; oedema deaths came in younger people and sooner [[Heinsius1998]]. In 55 people with complete MCA infarction, 78% herniated over 2-5 days and 22% survived [[Hacke1996]].

Locked-in syndrome

What is lost and what is kept

Quadriplegia and bulbar palsy from an anterior pontine lesion, with cognition, vertical eye movement, blinking and hearing preserved. Classical, incomplete and total types are described [[Das2023]].

A 1986 review of 139 cases reported mortality of 60% [[Patterson1986]]. A 2023 review of 82 pontine infarcts cites a newer report of 75% acute mortality and a chronic survey with functional movement recovery in 72% [[Schnetzer2023]].

A person is still there

Diagnosis took on average over 2.5 months, and the family was first to realise in more than half of cases. Once stable, life expectancy can be several decades, and people living with it long term self-report meaningful quality of life [[Laureys2005]].

Exam hookLocked-in syndrome spares vertical eye movement and blinking [[Das2023]].
In plain wordsA person with locked-in syndrome can think and hear, but may move only their eyes and eyelids. Many live for decades and report meaningful quality of life [[Laureys2005]].

Small arteries: lacunar syndromes

Penetrating arteries measure 400-900 micrometres. Sites are lenticulostriate, anterior choroidal, thalamoperforating, basilar paramedian and the recurrent artery of Heubner [[Venkataraman2024]]. They are end arteries, and lacunes are classically under 15 mm [[Gore2026]]. Fisher described at least 20 syndromes, tying small lacunes to lipohyalinosis and larger ones to atheromatous or embolic mechanisms [[Fisher1982]].

Share and outlook

About one quarter of ischaemic strokes, with good short-term but worse mid and long-term outcome [[Arboix2009,Norrving2003]]. Another review says "nearly one-third" [[Regenhardt2018]]. Silent lesions occur in 20-50% of older adults. At 5 years, 25% had died, 20% had recurrence and 30% were impaired [[Gore2026]].

Exam hookFive classic lacunar syndromes: pure motor, pure sensory, sensorimotor, ataxic hemiparesis, dysarthria-clumsy hand [[Arboix2009]].

Why tissue depends on steady flow

Where the energy goes

In rodent grey matter, signalling energy was budgeted as action potentials 47%, postsynaptic glutamate effects 34%, resting potential 13% and glutamate recycling 3% [[Attwell2001]].

Ageing

In 34 volunteers aged 22-82 on PET, cerebral blood flow, oxygen use and blood volume fell about 0.5% per year in grey and white matter, while oxygen extraction stayed constant [[Leenders1990]].
