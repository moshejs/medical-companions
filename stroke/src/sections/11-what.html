Part 1 · Understanding stroke

What a stroke is

A stroke happens when part of the brain loses its blood supply, or when a blood vessel in or around the brain bleeds. Brain cells then get hurt or die [[Sacco2013]]. Follow the three kinds below, then explore how common stroke is and what it leaves behind.

The fall in incidence stagnated from 2015 onwards, with some increases in southeast Asia, east Asia and Oceania, in countries with lower sociodemographic index (SDI), and in people younger than 70 [[Feigin2024]]. Age-standardised deaths fell far more in high-SDI countries (-59.4%) than in low-SDI countries (-26.5%) [[Feigin2024]].

The WSO-Lancet Neurology Commission projected deaths rising from 6.6 million in 2020 to 9.7 million in 2050, and warned that the Sustainable Development Goal 3.4 target will not be met on these trends [[Feigin2023]]. In the United States the long decline has stalled: the age-adjusted death rate rose 7.0% from 2012 to 2022 (36.9 to 39.5 per 100,000) [[Martin2025HDSS]]. An AHA/ASA statement judged hypertension control, started in the 1970s, to have had the most substantial influence on the earlier fall [[Lackland2014]].

Three overlapping clusters

Metabolic 68.8%, environmental 36.7%, behavioural 35.2% of stroke burden. They overlap, so they sum to more than 100% [[Feigin2025]].

What grew most

Attributable DALYs, 1990 to 2021: high BMI +88.2%, high ambient temperature +72.4%, high fasting plasma glucose +32.1% [[Feigin2024]]. Module 4.1 covers prevention.

Lifetime risk: 100 people

This is the chance of a first stroke from age 25 onwards. The method accounts for the competing risk of dying of something else first [[Feigin2018]]. So a low figure in a poorer region is not low danger: competing deaths shorten the time at risk [[Feigin2018]].

The grid is rounded to whole people. These are population figures. They cannot say what will happen to one person [[Mohan2011]].

What counts as a stroke: the clock and the tissue

The World Health Organization (WHO) wording of 1980 used a clock. The 2013 AHA/ASA statement said the classic definition is mainly clinical and does not account for advances in science and technology, and it combined clinical and tissue criteria [[Sacco2013]]. Exam hookClassic WHO: a deficit lasting more than 24 hours or leading to death, with no apparent cause other than vascular [[Engelhardt2017]]. AHA/ASA 2013: imaging or pathology evidence of infarction counts, even if symptoms are brief [[Sacco2013]]. TIA is defined by absence of acute infarction, not by a 24-hour clock [[Easton2009]].

What comes after: death, disability, a second stroke

Move through time since the stroke. Each mark appears only where a study reports a figure; the lines between marks are straight joins, not data. Cohorts differ in era, age and case mix, so read the gap between them as a warning about comparing studies [[Hankey2000,Martin2025HDSS]].

In plain wordsMany people live for years after stroke: almost 94 million people are alive today after having one [[Feigin2025]]. A second stroke is a real risk, which is why follow-up care matters. About 1 person in 9 has another stroke within a year [[Mohan2011]]. Numbers describe groups. They cannot say what will happen to one person [[Mohan2011]].

By type, at 30 days and after

Sweden (22,929 people): 30-day mortality 11.1% for ischaemic stroke and 30.7% for ICH. Among 30-day survivors, 5-year mortality did not differ [[Sennfalt2019]].

ICH has not budged at one month

One-month case fatality: median 40.4% in studies to 2008 [[vanAsch2010]]; pooled 35.5% (32.3-38.9) in studies from 2008 to 2023, with no time trend. Good function after 3-12 months: 31.2% [[Wolsink2025]].

Falling, unevenly

Greater Cincinnati, adjusted 5-year mortality, 1993-94 to 2015: ischaemic stroke 53% to 48.3%; ICH 64.4% to 69.2%, no significant change [[Robinson2024]]. South London: 30-day case fatality after first ischaemic stroke fell 38% from 2000 to 2015 [[Wafa2020PLoSMed]].

SAH

Crude incidence fell from 10.2 per 100,000 person-years in 1980 to 6.1 in 2010 [[Etminan2019]]. Case fatality fell 17% between 1973 and 2002 while mean age rose from 52 to 62 years [[Nieuwkamp2009]].

Dementia

About 10% had dementia before a first stroke, 10% developed it soon after a first stroke, and more than a third had it after recurrent stroke [[Pendlebury2009]].

A second stroke is deadlier

Perth: 30-day case fatality was 41% after first recurrence and 22% after first-ever stroke [[Hardie2004]]. Recurrence has fallen over time: pooled 5-year risk from 32% to 16.2% across studies [[Mohan2011]], and in South London from 18% (1995-1999) to 12% (2000-2005), with no improvement since [[Flach2020]].

Exam hookPooled recurrence: 11.1% at 1 year, 26.4% at 5 years, 39.2% at 10 years [[Mohan2011]]. ICH survival: less than half at 1 year (46%), less than a third at 5 years (29%) [[Poon2014]].

Established

Younger and older diverge

Across 50 studies in 20 high-income countries, every study with data showed a less favourable trend under 55 than at 55 and over (pooled relative temporal rate ratio 1.57, 1.42-1.74). Absolute trends were heterogeneous: the firm finding is divergence, not a universal rise [[Scott2022]].

Established

Oxford, 2002-2010 against 2010-2018

2429 strokes. Incidence rose under 55 (IRR 1.67, 1.31-2.14) and fell at 55 and over (0.85, 0.78-0.92), independent of sex, severity, subtype and changes in investigation [[Li2022]].

Hypothesis

Why? Sources disagree

US inpatient data point to rising traditional risk factors [[George2017]]. In Oxford the rise persisted in people without diabetes, hypertension, smoking or obesity, so its cause is unexplained [[Li2022]].

Young does not mean spared

Dutch cohort, age 18-50: 20-year mortality among 30-day survivors was 26.8% after ischaemic stroke and 13.7% after ICH, 3.9 times expected [[RuttenJacobs2013]]. In high-income countries, 1-year recurrence up to age 65 was unchanged after 2000 (RR 1.04, 0.78-1.39) [[Nehme2026]].

GBD rounds also disagree here: for people under 70, GBD 2019 reported incidence up 15% for 1990-2019 [[Feigin2021]], while GBD 2021 reports 4.1% for 1990-2021 [[Feigin2024]].

Sex and gender

Rates are higher in men

Age-adjusted incidence per 100,000: men 160, women 125. Age-adjusted death rate: men 103, women 75 [[Feigin2025]].

SAH is the exception

About 51% of incident SAH occur in women [[Feigin2025]]. Pooled SAH risk ratio for women: 1.3 (0.98-1.7) [[Etminan2019]].

Crude against adjusted

INSTRUCT (16,957 people): crude mortality rate ratio for women 1.35 at 1 year; adjusted 0.81. Age, prestroke function, severity and atrial fibrillation explained the reversal [[Phan2017]]. In pooled trials women had higher survival (OR 0.82) and more disability (OR 1.20) [[Carcel2019]].

Who has more strokes?

Age-specific rates are higher in men, but women have more stroke events because they live longer [[Reeves2008]]. Globally, 47.4% of incident strokes are in females [[Feigin2024]]. In the US, females accounted for 56.6% of stroke deaths in 2022, against 47.9% globally [[Martin2025HDSS,Feigin2024]].

Recognition

Women are more likely to present with non-traditional symptoms and stroke mimics and can be misdiagnosed. There was no substantial difference in thrombolysis or thrombectomy outcome [[Bushnell2018]].

Unequal burden

Race in these studies is a social and recorded category, and socioeconomic adjustment changed several estimates [[Robinson2024,HowardG2011,Gardener2020]].

United States, Israel and the cost

United States

  • About 795,000 new or recurrent strokes a year: about 610,000 first and 185,000 recurrent. These, and the 87/10/3 split, trace back to 1999 Greater Cincinnati data [[Martin2025HDSS]].
  • 9.4 million adults self-report a stroke, a prevalence of 3.3% [[Martin2025HDSS]].
  • 165,393 deaths in 2022, about 1 in 20 US deaths; fifth among causes [[Martin2025HDSS]]. About 65% occurred outside an acute care hospital [[Martin2025HDSS]].
  • The 2026 AHA report exists, but its abstract states no stroke numbers and its full text could not be read, so these are from the 2025 edition [[Palaniappan2026,Martin2025HDSS]].

Israel

  • GBD 2021: 8000 incident strokes, 3000 deaths, 92,000 prevalent cases. Age-standardised death rate fell 67.2% from 1990 to 2021 [[Feigin2024]].
  • A registry-based paper states around 18,000 strokes a year [[Simaan2021]]. Registry reports count stroke or TIA in at least some cases [[Ram2019]]; the gap was not reconciled.
  • Age-adjusted rates were significantly higher in Arab than in Jewish people [[Ram2019]]. Overall acute treatment and outcomes were similar [[Simaan2021]].
  • By 2016-2017, t-PA use had stabilised at 9.2%, from 0.5% in a 2004 survey [[Ram2019,Tanne2006]].
Global cost, 2017US$891 billion

$315 billion in health care and $576 billion in lost income a year [[Feigin2023]]. WSO: over $890 billion, 0.66% of global GDP [[Feigin2025]].

Europe, 32 countries, 2017EUR 60 billion

Health care 45%, informal care EUR 16 billion. Care cost per citizen ranged from EUR 11 in Bulgaria to EUR 140 in Finland [[LuengoFernandez2020]].

Plans

The Commission built its plan on four pillars: surveillance, prevention, acute care and rehabilitation [[Feigin2023]]. The European plan targets 90% or more of patients in a stroke unit by 2030 [[Norrving2018]]; in 2023, 43.5% of 52 European nations had a national stroke plan [[Christensen2026]].
