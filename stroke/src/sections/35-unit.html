Part 3 · Acute treatment

The stroke unit

Clot-busting drugs and thrombectomy reach some people. Organised stroke unit care reaches everyone admitted, and its effect is large. See what it changes, what can go wrong in the first week, and which simple care and which big operation have trial evidence.

This page reports what trials and guidelines say. It is not advice about any one person's care. The 2026 AHA/ASA guideline replaces the 2018 guideline and its 2019 update, and covers care from before hospital through early complications and secondary prevention [[Prabhakaran2026,Powers2019]]. It names management of hyperglycaemia and dysphagia among its key updates [[Prabhakaran2026]]. Its recommendation wording was not read for this page, so no class of recommendation is quoted here.

Why stroke units work

Numbers from Cochrane 2020 [[Langhorne2020]]. Odds below 1 favour the stroke unit.

10%

lower odds of death with daily physician ward rounds, in a national audit of 143,578 people (95% CI 0.82 to 0.98) [[Paley2018]]

1332

deaths a year the audit's authors estimated could be saved in England and Wales, if the associations are causal, with at least 3 swallow-trained nurses per 10 beds and daily stroke physician rounds [[Paley2018]]

In that audit, 14.4% died within 30 days. Hospitals with more nurses trained in swallow screening had lower odds of death (P=0.004). The number of admissions and the overall weekend registered-nurse ratio were not independently associated with mortality [[Paley2018]].

The FeSS bundle: fever, sugar, swallow

QASC was a single-blind cluster randomised trial in 19 acute stroke units in New South Wales, with 1696 patients analysed. Intervention units received nurse-initiated protocols for fever, hyperglycaemia and swallowing dysfunction, plus multidisciplinary team-building workshops. Control units received an abridged version of existing guidelines [[Middleton2011]].

Pick one element, then scrub through 72 hours to see how the protocol runs at the bedside.

Thresholds and frequencies as listed by Fasugba 2023 [[Fasugba2023]]. Quoted as published, as teaching material, not a care plan.

QASC at 90 days: mRS 2 or more, p=0.002; number needed to treat 6.4; adjusted absolute difference 15.7%, 95% CI 5.8 to 25.4 [[Middleton2011]].

In plain wordsThree simple things matter in the first days: checking for fever, checking blood sugar, and checking swallowing. Units that did this well had fewer people left dead or dependent [[Middleton2011]].

Swelling in the back of the brain

The AHA statement says suboccipital craniectomy with dural expansion should be performed in people who deteriorate neurologically, and that ventriculostomy should be accompanied by it to avoid upward cerebellar displacement [[Wijdicks2014]].

The evidence is observational. Across 11 studies (283 people), moderate to severe disability was 28%, mortality 20% and adverse events 23% [[Ayling2018]]. One matched study suggested preventive surgery might beat medical treatment alone in selected people Early evidence [[Kim2016]].

Goals of care

The HAMLET authors wrote that the decision to operate should depend on the emphasis people and relatives place on survival and dependency [[Hofmeijer2009]]. Primary palliative care should be available to all people with serious or life-threatening stroke and their families, throughout the illness, says the 2014 AHA statement [[Holloway2014]]. The 2025 statement adds that needs are insufficiently addressed by current models [[Creutzfeldt2025]].

2026 neurocritical care guidance advises avoiding premature prognostication [[Mainali2026]]. Early care limitations can shape outcomes: an observational study in intracerebral haemorrhage tied an early do-not-resuscitate order to a doubled hazard of death (HR 2.17, 1.38 to 3.41) [[Zahuranec2007]]. That is not ischaemic stroke, and setting goals of care early remains the guidance [[Holloway2014]].

Early mobilisation: AVERT, negative and positive

AVERT is the trial where the main result and the dose analysis seem to pull in opposite directions. Both matter.

Figures from AVERT and its dose analysis [[Bernhardt2015,Bernhardt2016]], and Cochrane 2018 [[Langhorne2018]].

In the AVERT database, 4% of 2104 people had a serious fall in the first year. 60% of those falls caused a fracture, and 42% of fractures were hip fractures [[Wang2025]]. Module 5.3 covers falls care.

Also in the first days

Bleeding into the infarct

In ECASS II, parenchymal haematoma and symptomatic haemorrhage were tied to alteplase, but haemorrhagic infarction was not. Risk factors included alteplase, extent of hypoattenuation on CT, heart failure, older age and baseline systolic pressure [[Larrue2001]]. The AHA statement names prompt diagnosis and early correction of coagulopathy as the mainstay of treatment [[Yaghi2017]]. Classification after thrombectomy differs too: ECASS III assigned 101 of 156 bleeds unambiguously, and Heidelberg categorised 55 more [[Neuberger2017]].

Seizures

Risk after ischaemic stroke rose with cortical location (HR 2.09) and disability (HR 2.10) [[Bladin2000]]. The SeLECT score uses severity, large-artery cause, early seizures, cortical involvement and MCA territory. SeLECT 0 predicted 0.7% late seizures at 1 year and 1.3% at 5; SeLECT 9 predicted 63% and 83% [[Galovic2018]].

Delirium

25% (20 to 30) pooled across 32 papers in the first 6 weeks [[Shaw2019]]. A cohort of 527 found 11.8% in the first week [[Oldenbeuving2011]]. Delirium went with inpatient death (OR 4.71), a stay longer by 9.39 days, and discharge to an institution (OR 3.39) [[Shi2012]].

Finding atrial fibrillation

New AF across 50 studies: 7.7% on the admission ECG, 5.1% with in-hospital monitoring, 10.7% with Holter, 16.9% with later ambulatory methods, 23.7% after all phases [[Sposato2015]]. With implantable monitors: 12.2% at 3 months and 28.5% at 36 months [[Noubiap2021]]. Optimal duration is not established [[Haeusler2018]]. See module 4.2.
