Part 1 · Understanding stroke

Core, penumbra & time

When an artery blocks, the centre of its territory dies quickly, while the tissue around it, the penumbra, struggles on for a while and can still recover. Run the clock, change how fast the core grows, and choose when the artery reopens.

The term "ischaemic penumbra" (ischemic in US spelling) was named by Astrup and colleagues in 1981 [[Astrup1981]]. Human functional imaging supports the concept [[Heiss2000]]. This page reports what studies say about groups of people. It says nothing about any one person.

In plain wordsWhen an artery blocks, the centre dies fast. Around it, tissue is struggling but can still recover if blood comes back soon [[Baron2021]]. On average about 1.9 million brain cells can be lost each minute in a large stroke [[Saver2006]]. If you think someone is having a stroke, call your local emergency number.

The blood-flow ladder

Lower the flow and functions fail in order. Most numbers come from animals, so each rung names its species. Human thresholds are reported too, but they were too heterogeneous for a meta-analysis [[Bandera2006]].

Rodent values are published in mL/g/min [[Hossmann1994]] and shown here in mL/100 g/min (0.55 mL/g/min = 55). They are not human cutoffs. The neuron drawing is schematic: damage also depends on how long flow stays low [[Jones1981]].

Collaterals: fast and slow progressors

Collateral vessels over the brain's surface can keep tissue alive during an occlusion [[Liebeskind2003,Mangiardi2023]]. In 94 people with untreated occlusion, time showed no correlation with infarct growth (r=-0.05), and collateral status separated them instead [[Vagal2018]].

How long does the penumbra last? Early models gave it short hours and described it as short-lived [[Hakim1998,Hossmann1994]]. Later work shows core growth varies between people, with penumbra remaining salvageable up to and perhaps beyond 24 hours in selected people [[Baron2021]]. Collaterals largely account for the difference [[Vagal2018]]. Both statements can hold: "time is brain" is true on average, and time alone predicts poorly for one person.

In plain wordsBackup blood vessels buy time. This is why two people can have very different damage after the same delay [[Vagal2018]].

Why blocking the cascade has been so hard

Established

Animal work does not predict trials

Of 1,026 experimental treatments, the 114 that reached clinical testing were no better in animal models than the 912 that did not (31.3% vs 24.4%) [[OCollins2006]]. Targets are still under review [[Chamorro2016]].

Early evidence

Nerinetide

Disrupting the NMDA receptor to PSD-95 link reduced ischaemic damage in rats [[Aarts2002]]. In ESCAPE-NA1, mRS 0-2 was reached in 61.4% vs 59.2%, adjusted risk ratio 1.04 (0.96-1.14), with effect modification by alteplase [[Hill2020]].

Hypothesis

Splitting the NMDA signal

Extrasynaptic GluN2B has been proposed to promote death and GluN2A to promote survival, with PSD95 and nNOS coupling as a target [[Lai2014]].

When the artery reopens

Reperfusion is the aim, and it is not perfectly clean. With over 90% reperfusion, early diffusion volume correlated with final infarct at r=0.95. With under 10% reperfusion, Tmax over 6 seconds correlated at r=0.86 [[Wheeler2013]]. In one trial imaging analysis, median final infarct volume was 54.5 mL with intra-arterial therapy vs 81.8 mL in controls [[Boers2017]]. HERMES: common odds ratio 2.49 (1.76-3.53), number needed to treat 2.6 [[Goyal2016]].
