The NIH Stroke Scale

The NIHSS is a set of short bedside checks: 15 items, 0 to 42. Watch a score build item by item, try every item yourself with teaching notes, see what it misses, and meet the modified Rankin Scale that trials use for the end result.

Teaching toolThis calculator explains how the published scale adds up. It is never for an individual's diagnosis, prognosis or treatment, and it does not teach or certify examination. Results are shown as population figures from the cited studies, not predictions for a person.

Where the wording comes from. Item wording and score options here are taken from two secondary summaries: a 2022 narrative review [[Budincevic2022]] and a 2014 Continuum appendix [[ContinuumNIHSS2014]]. The official NINDS form and booklet could not be fetched for this guide (HTTP 403), so administration rules, such as which response to score, are not stated here and need checking against the official NIH Stroke Scale from NINDS.

Score every item

Pick one option for each item. Choosing an option moves the picture above to the full total. An untestable (UN) choice adds nothing to the total; one summary codes the same situations as 9 [[ContinuumNIHSS2014]]. The maximum of 42 is the sum of the item maxima and agrees with the published maximum [[Budincevic2022]].

In plain wordsA higher number means a more severe stroke now. It is not a promise about the future [[Adams1999,Kasner2006]]. A low score does not always mean a small stroke, especially with problems of balance or on the right side of the brain [[MartinSchild2011,Fink2002Stroke]].
Exam hookNIHSS: 15 elements, maximum 42. Left hemisphere bias and poor capture of posterior strokes are the two named weaknesses [[Budincevic2022,Woo1999,Sato2008]]. A score of 16 or more forecasts death or severe disability; 6 or less forecasts good recovery [[Adams1999]].

"Mild" has no single meaning

About a third of people with mild stroke were not functionally independent at 90 days, whatever the treatment or definition used, including NIHSS 0 to 5 [[Spokoyny2015]]. PRISMS (minor nondisabling deficits, median NIHSS 2): favourable outcome 78.2% with alteplase vs 81.5% with aspirin, symptomatic haemorrhage 3.2% vs 0. Stopped very early, so not definitive [[Khatri2018]].

How much it explains

NIHSS explained 11.3 to 25.0% of variation in outcome models, against 0.7 to 4.0% for a comorbidity index [[Rost2016]]. Lacunar subtype was the only one predicting outcome independently of NIHSS (OR 3.1) [[Adams1999]]. 24-hour NIHSS and 90-day mRS gave the same trial conclusion in 61 of 73 trials [[Rinkel2025]].

NIHSS and large vessel occlusion

At 10 or more, PPV for arterial occlusion was 97% in carotid and 96% in vertebrobasilar stroke, in selected people having arteriography [[Fischer2005]]. In 2152 people the best anterior cut-offs were 9 or more within 3 hours and 7 or more at 3 to 6 hours [[Heldner2013]]. Unselected data disagree: 11 or more misses over 25% of LVOs, and keeping misses at or below 10% needs a cut-off of 5 [[Turc2016]].

What limb weakness means later

Limb paresis explained most of the variance in quality of life at 90 days (17.5 to 24.1% of EQ-5D utility), far more than neglect (8.9%), aphasia (10.0%) or hemianopia (10.8%) [[vanderEnde2023]].

The left-hemisphere bias

Language adds points that right-sided signs do not, so left hemisphere strokes score higher for the same volume [[Woo1999,Lyden2004]]. Factor analysis finds left and right brain factors [[Lyden1999,Lyden2004]]. Choose a study to see the median volumes behind the same score.

The posterior blind spot

Medians from 1569 people [[Inoa2014]] and from people with occlusion [[Heldner2013]]. Cut-offs [[Sato2008]].

Cranial nerves are not tested in detail, and the scale underestimates brainstem or cerebellar severity [[Budincevic2022]]. In posterior stroke the total is lower, and only the ataxia and visual subscores are higher [[Sato2008]].

In 1569 people, 71% of those with posterior stroke scored 4 or less, and 15% of them had a poor outcome at 3 months. The authors argue thrombolysis should not be withheld on the NIHSS alone in posterior stroke [[Inoa2014]].

A score of zero does not exclude stroke. In one series 20 people (0.76% of stroke service admissions) scored 0 with an infarct on DWI, with headache, vertigo and nausea common, and truncal ataxia the commonest sign. Truncal ataxia is not on the scale, nor is isolated hand or finger palsy; 30% had a cardioembolic cause, which matters for prevention [[MartinSchild2011,Siniscalchi2018]].

The e-NIHSS adds posterior elements while keeping the same total and item count, raising posterior scores by an average of 2 points in early testing Early evidence [[Olivato2016]]. The original remains standard for comparability [[Lyden2001,Lees2012]].

Where the scale came from, and how far raters agree

1989

Designed as a 15-item examination for acute stroke trials. Interrater kappa averaged 0.69 in 24 people; test-retest kappa 0.66 to 0.77, with no difference among a neurologist, a resident, a neurology nurse and an emergency nurse. Correlation with infarct size r=0.68, with 3-month outcome r=0.79 [[Brott1989]].

Training helps

Video training of 162 investigators gave moderate to excellent agreement on most items, though facial paresis and ataxia stayed below 0.40 [[Lyden1994]]. Non-neurologists reached intraclass correlations of 0.93 and 0.95 [[Goldstein1997]]. A 2025 animated certification system: overall 0.979, while item kappas ran 0.25 to 0.90 [[Anderson2025]].

The modified NIHSS

Removes level of consciousness, face weakness, ataxia and dysarthria, and collapses sensory to 2 responses. Items with poor kappa fell from 8 to 3 [[Lyden2001]].

Scoring from the notes

Agreement with prospective scores r²=0.94 [[Williams2000]] and R²=0.991 in severe stroke [[Lindsell2005]], but interrater agreement fell to 0.48 at a hospital without neurologist assessment, with 62% of items missing [[Bushnell2001]].

The modified Rankin Scale

The NIHSS measures the deficit now. The mRS measures the result in daily life: how much help a person needs. It descends from Rankin's 1957 work [[Rankin1957]]. The 1988 interobserver study used six grades, 0 to 5 [[vanSwieten1988]]; modern usage is a 7-level scale that adds 6 for death [[Saver2021,Thomalla2018]]. Definitions below are verbatim as listed in a 2026 clinical study [[Luo2026]], because the 1988 abstract does not state them.

A structured interview

Agreement across multiple raters rose from 43% to 81%, kappa 0.25 to 0.74 [[Wilson2005]]. In an earlier study, 57% to 78%, weighted kappa 0.78 to 0.93 [[Wilson2002]].

Reliability disagrees

Original: the same grade in 65 of 100 people, weighted kappa 0.91 [[vanSwieten1988]]. A systematic review found weighted kappa from 0.25 to 0.95, overall kappa 0.46 [[Quinn2009]]. Another synthesis reports moderate inter-rater reliability, improved by structured interviews [[Banks2007]].

Quick versions

A simplified questionnaire took 1.67 minutes on average, with kappa 0.72 (95% CI 0.58 to 0.86) [[Bruno2010]].

Why trials use it

mRS at 3 months or later is the preferred primary outcome for acute trials [[Lees2012]]. Comorbidities and socioeconomic factors affect how it should be read [[Banks2007]]. A roundtable recommends uniform names, because trials had used "a bewildering variety" of labels [[Saver2021]].

In plain wordsThe mRS is a 0 to 6 scale of how much help a person needs in daily life [[Luo2026]].
Exam hookmRS 0 to 6, where 6 is death. Grade 3 walks unaided with some help; grade 4 cannot walk unaided [[Luo2026]].
