Part 1 · Understanding stroke

Causes of ischaemic stroke

Ischaemic (US: ischemic) stroke means an artery in the brain is blocked. The question that shapes prevention is where the blockage came from. Pick a source and follow it, then see how the shares change between populations, and why some causes stay unknown.

This page reports what studies and classifications say. It is not advice for any one person, and the figures describe groups of people. If you think someone is having a stroke, call your local emergency number.

TOAST and the other ways of sorting causes

TOAST defines five subtypes, each possible or probable. In its first test, two neurologists disagreed in one of 20 patients, with a specific diagnosis in 11 and undetermined in 9 [[Adams1993]]. The original paper's full text could not be read for this guide, so the cutoffs below come from a later review that restates them [[Kim2014]]. Other criteria are not listed here.

A real disagreement. Large-artery disease leads in pooled Asian populations at 33% (31 to 36) [[Ornello2018]], and one review calls intracranial large-artery disease the most common subtype worldwide [[Gorelick2008]]. A pooled Chinese vs white comparison found no clear evidence of different ischaemic subtypes, though intracerebral haemorrhage was 33% vs 12% [[Tsai2013]]. Annual trends differ too: cardioembolic in white populations +2.4%, large artery in Asian populations +5.7%, small artery in white populations -4.7% [[Ornello2018]].

Large arteries

  • Autopsy prevalence of intracranial atherosclerosis was 43%, 65% and 80% with increasing age [[Banerjee2017]].
  • In the Rotterdam Study, calcification contributed to 75% of strokes at the intracranial carotid, 45% at the aortic arch and 25% at the extracranial carotid [[Bos2014]].
  • SAMMPRIS, stenting vs aggressive medical management: stroke or death 14.7% vs 5.8% at 30 days and 20.0% vs 12.2% at 1 year [[Chimowitz2011]]. See

Small vessels

  • Small vessel disease involves endothelial dysfunction and barrier leak [[Wardlaw2019,Pantoni2010]].
  • Lacunar infarcts are put at about a quarter [[Arboix2009]] or "nearly one-third" [[Regenhardt2018]] of ischaemic strokes.
  • At 5 years, 25% had died, 20% had recurrence and 30% were impaired [[Gore2026]].

ESUS and cryptogenic stroke

ESUS stands for embolic stroke of undetermined source. It was proposed in 2014 for a non-lacunar infarct with no proximal stenosis and no cardioembolic source, a defined part of the cryptogenic group [[Hart2014]]. ESUS accounts for 9 to 25% of ischaemic strokes, on average 17%, with mean age 65, NIHSS 5 and recurrence of 4.5% per year [[Hart2017]]. ESUS is not a synonym for cryptogenic stroke [[Ntaios2020]].

Rates per year as reported. NAVIGATE ESUS bleeding: 1.8% vs 0.7%, HR 2.72 [[Hart2018]]. RE-SPECT ESUS HR 0.85 (0.69 to 1.03) [[Diener2019]]. ARCADIA HR 1.00 [[Kamel2024]].

Exam hookESUS is a non-lacunar infarct with no proximal stenosis or cardioembolic source [[Hart2014]]. The three anticoagulant ESUS trials were neutral [[Hart2018,Diener2019,Kamel2024]].

How big is "unknown"? It depends on definition and workup: about 25% [[Hart2014]], 30 to 40% [[Yaghi2017Cryptogenic]], 20 to 40% [[Sanna2014]]. Active cancer is present in 5 to 10% of ESUS [[Navi2021]].

Hunting for hidden atrial fibrillation

In the international survey, 15% had known AF, 10% new AF on the ECG and only 2% AF found with monitoring [[Perera2016]]. Longer monitoring finds more. Scrub through the 36 months of CRYSTAL AF, which compared an implantable monitor with control in 441 people [[Sanna2014,Brachmann2016]].

Stroke in younger people

Young stroke incidence is rising [[Ekker2018]]. In Helsinki (1,008 people aged 15 to 49), incidence was 10.8 per 100,000. Traditional factors were still common: dyslipidaemia 60%, smoking 44%, hypertension 39%. Silent infarcts appeared in 13% [[Putaala2009]]. Choose a cause to explore.

In plain wordsMany strokes come from clots made in the heart, blocked or narrowed arteries, or tiny vessel disease. In about one in four the cause stays unknown after tests [[Ornello2018]]. In younger people, a tear in a neck artery is one common cause [[Putaala2009]].
