Part 3 · Acute treatment

Clot-dissolving treatment

Thrombolysis means giving a medicine that dissolves a clot. Watch it work inside an artery, move the delay slider to see what time does to the benefit, then compare alteplase with tenecteplase. This page reports what trials, labels and guidelines say. It is not medical advice.

If you think someone is having a stroke, call your local emergency number. In the data below, earlier treatment brought more benefit [[Emberson2014,Saver2013]].

Disability-free survival rose by about 10% with alteplase, in a pool of 9 trials and 6756 people [[Emberson2014]].

The same analysis found about 5% for treatment from 3.1 to 4.5 hours [[Emberson2014]].

The cost

2 in 100

The early excess of fatal bleeding was about 2%, offset by the gains above [[Emberson2014]].

One minute saved

1.8 days

Modelling: about 1.8 days of extra healthy life on average for each minute of onset-to-treatment time saved (95% prediction interval 0.9 to 2.7) [[Meretoja2014]].

Time is benefit: scrub the delay

Pick a time from stroke onset to treatment. The chart shows the published odds ratio for that time band, and the people show what its absolute figures look like out of 100. These are population figures from trials. They do not predict what happens to any one person.

Bars span each published time band and shading is its 95% CI; figures are not interpolated between bands. Icons always use Emberson 2014, rounded to whole people. mRS 0 to 1 means no symptoms or no significant disability.

In plain wordsOut of 100 people treated within 3 hours, about 10 more recover with no real disability than if they had not had the medicine. The medicine can cause bleeding in the brain. Fatal bleeding goes up by about 2 in 100, yet more people overall come out with no real disability [[Emberson2014,Whiteley2016]].
Exam hookNINDS window 3 hours; ECASS III extended it to 4.5 hours [[NINDS1995,Hacke2008]]. The Emberson model puts zero effect at 6.3 hours (95% CI 5.0 to 13.8), with the lower CI first reaching 1.0 at 5.1 hours [[Emberson2014]].

Where does benefit end? The two pools differ. Lees 2010 saw no benefit after around 270 minutes. Emberson 2014 placed zero effect at 6.3 hours and loss of statistical confidence at 5.1 hours. Both support treatment up to 4.5 hours [[Lees2010,Emberson2014]].

Pooled data from the same patients give very different headline rates, because "symptomatic" can be defined in several ways. Switch views to see the definitions, the effect of severity, and the trade-off.

Alteplase versus tenecteplase: trial explorer

Tenecteplase is given as one bolus. Pick a trial. Compare the two arms within a trial rather than across trials: AcT had a median age of 74 and ORIGINAL allowed NIHSS from 1, which is why good outcomes range from 36.9% to 72.7% [[Menon2022,Meng2024]].

Rates as reported by each trial; outcomes and definitions differ between trials.

A live difference of opinion. ATTEST-2 authors conclude tenecteplase "should be preferred" [[Muir2024]]. The 2026 AHA/ASA guideline as summarised endorses either agent [[Musmar2026]], and a pharmacy review describes them as "equally preferred" [[HelixTalk2026]]. ESO 2023 says tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg "can be used as a safe and effective alternative" under 4.5 hours (moderate evidence, strong recommendation), recommends against 0.40 mg/kg (low evidence, strong), and prefers tenecteplase in large-vessel occlusion under 4.5 hours (moderate evidence, strong) [[Alamowitch2023]].

Other doses tested

Low-dose alteplase in ENCHANTED (3310 people, 63% Asian): 0.6 versus 0.9 mg/kg did not meet noninferiority for death or disability (53.2% versus 51.1%; OR 1.09, 0.95 to 1.25). Major symptomatic haemorrhage was 1.0% versus 2.1% [[Anderson2016]].

FDA approval of TNKase for stroke in adults was reported on 4 March 2025, resting on AcT [[Genentech2025,FDA-TNKase]].

Windows: what labels say, what trials tested

Licence and guideline differ. Both US labels say within 3 hours, whereas ESO guidance and the 2026 AHA/ASA guideline as summarised work to 4.5 hours [[FDA-Activase,FDA-TNKase,Berge2021,Musmar2026]]. The TNKase label analysed the 0 to 3 hour AcT subset because the comparator is approved for 0 to 3 hours [[FDA-TNKase]].

Hours from onset, or from last seen well. Secondary sources disagree on the outer limit of extended-window thrombolysis in the 2026 guideline; those read here give 9 hours, and the original should be checked [[Musmar2026,HelixTalk2026]].

Beyond 4.5 hours: extended-window explorer

When the clock is unknown or late, brain imaging is used to look for tissue that can still be saved. WAKE-UP uses DWI-FLAIR mismatch; EXTEND uses perfusion imaging [[Thomalla2018,Ma2019]].

Rates as reported by each trial. Note which good-outcome band each trial uses.

Early evidence Where thrombectomy is available and used (TIMELESS), adding a late lytic did not help; where it is not (TRACE-III), it did [[Albers2024,Xiong2024,Wang2026]]. Most positive data come from China, which limits generalisation [[Albers2024,Xiong2024,Ma2026,Zhou2025]].

In plain wordsIf you do not know when your stroke began, for example if you woke up with it, brain scans can sometimes show whether treatment could still help [[Thomalla2018,Thomalla2020]].

ESO 2021 recommends alteplase for people who wake with stroke, last seen well more than 4.5 hours earlier, with DWI-FLAIR mismatch and no planned thrombectomy (high quality evidence). ESO 2023 recommends against tenecteplase in wake-up or unknown-onset stroke selected by non-contrast CT alone (low evidence, strong) [[Berge2021,Alamowitch2023]].

Raised risk to weigh

Both labels list recent major surgery, recent gastrointestinal or genitourinary bleeding, recent trauma, and blood pressure above 175 systolic or 110 diastolic [[FDA-Activase,FDA-TNKase]].

Clotting tests

Without recent oral anticoagulant or heparin use, treatment can start before coagulation results. The Activase label says to discontinue if pretreatment INR is above 1.7 or aPTT is elevated [[FDA-Activase]].

Blood pressure, ESO 2025

Below 185/110 mmHg before the bolus. Below 180/105 mmHg during and for 24 hours after thrombolysis (low certainty). These thresholds come from early pilot studies and the NINDS protocol, not from trials testing the thresholds [[Sandset2026]].

Where it is given

Both labels limit stroke use to facilities with timely access to evaluation and management of intracranial haemorrhage [[FDA-Activase,FDA-TNKase]].

Lower blood pressure targets

ENCHANTED (2196 people): systolic 130 to 140 versus below 180 mmHg gave no difference in mRS (OR 1.01, 0.87 to 1.17). Any intracranial haemorrhage was 14.8% versus 18.7% (OR 0.75, 0.60 to 0.94) [[Anderson2019]].

Nursing points from the labels

  • Monitor and control blood pressure often during and after treatment
  • Avoid intramuscular injections and non-essential trauma
  • Arterial and venous punctures only as required; avoid internal jugular and subclavian punctures
  • Compress an arterial puncture for at least 30 minutes [[FDA-Activase,FDA-TNKase]]

The 2026 AHA/ASA guideline as summarised: thrombolysis should not be given for mild non-disabling symptoms, where dual antiplatelet therapy is preferred. Open questions

Early evidence

Recent DOAC: a controversy

The usual position: Meinel 2023 states that international guidelines recommend avoiding thrombolysis after recent DOAC intake [[Meinel2023]].

The challenge: in its retrospective cohort (64 centres, 33,207 people, 832 with a DOAC within 48 hours) symptomatic haemorrhage was 2.5% with a DOAC versus 4.1% without anticoagulation (adjusted OR 0.57, 0.36 to 0.92). 30.3% had reversal, 27.0% had levels measured and 42.7% had neither [[Meinel2023]]. A single-centre emulation (98 people) found 0 of 49 versus 2 of 49 [[Bucke2024]].

Both are observational, with selection bias acknowledged. Bücke and colleagues call for randomised trials [[Meinel2023,Bucke2024]].

Early evidence

Adding another drug: opposite results

MOST (514 people): utility-weighted mRS 5.2 with argatroban, 6.3 with eptifibatide, 6.8 with placebo. Mortality 24%, 12% and 8%. Neither reduced disability [[Adeoye2024]].

ASSET-IT (China, 832 people, non-cardioembolic, not thrombectomy candidates): tirofiban within 60 minutes after thrombolysis gave mRS 0 to 1 of 65.9% versus 54.9% (RR 1.20, 1.07 to 1.34). Symptomatic haemorrhage 1.7% versus 0 [[Tao2025]].

Different drugs, populations and countries. The 2026 guideline is summarised as not recommending argatroban or eptifibatide [[Prabhakaran2026,GuidelineCentral2026]].

Women and older people

In 18 studies women had 30% lower odds of receiving rt-PA (OR 0.70, 0.55 to 0.88). Among those arriving within 3 hours without contraindications, the difference was not significant (OR 0.81, 0.58 to 1.13) [[Reeves2009]].

By age: at 80 or under, mRS 0 to 1 was 39% versus 34% (OR 1.25, 1.10 to 1.42); above 80, 18% versus 13% (OR 1.56, 1.17 to 2.08). Age did not change the effect (p=0.53) [[Emberson2014]].

Swelling of the lips and tongue

Orolingual angioedema: 42 of 530 (7.9%) at one UK centre, 5 to 189 minutes after starting tPA. 12% of cases needed urgent advanced airway care. Prior ACE inhibitor use was the only independent predictor (OR 2.3, 1.1 to 4.7) [[Hurford2015]].

The rate is disputed: trial and label figures are near 1% [[FDA-TNKase,Muir2024]], against 7.9% in consecutive patients with retrospective grading [[Hurford2015]].

If a bleed happens

An AHA/ASA statement says treatment of symptomatic haemorrhage rests on expert opinion and small case series; prompt diagnosis and early correction of the coagulopathy remain the mainstay [[Yaghi2017]]. A separate guideline covers reversal of thrombolytics in intracranial haemorrhage [[Frontera2016]].

In plain wordsA few people get swelling of the lips or tongue after the clot-buster. Staff watch for this because it can affect breathing. It is more likely in people taking ACE inhibitor blood pressure tablets [[Hurford2015]].

What the 2026 guideline changed

The 2026 AHA/ASA guideline replaces the 2018 guideline and 2019 update [[Prabhakaran2026,Powers2019]]. Its stated key updates include thrombolytic choice and eligibility, thrombectomy eligibility and the approach to contraindications. Its literature search ran to December 2024, with high-impact additions to March 2025 [[Prabhakaran2026]]. So trials published later, such as OPTION, HOPE and ASSET-IT, are unlikely to be reflected. That is an inference from the search dates [[Prabhakaran2026,Ma2026,Zhou2025,Tao2025]].

The full text could not be read for this guide, so no class of recommendation is given. Secondary sources summarise it as: tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg up to 25 mg or alteplase within 4.5 hours; 0.4 mg/kg not recommended; no delay for CTA, MRA or perfusion imaging within 4.5 hours; imaging-selected thrombolysis for unknown onset or 4.5 to 9 hours [[Musmar2026,GuidelineCentral2026]]. Intensive systolic lowering below 140 mmHg is summarised as not improving function after thrombolysis [[Musmar2026,GuidelineCentral2026]]. Mobile stroke units are recommended where available [[GuidelineCentral2026,Chauhan2026]].

A correction has been issued, and a separate group of authors published proposed corrections in 2026. Their content was not available for this guide, so check the original before relying on any statement [[Prabhakaran2026Correction,Albers2026]]. Grotta's 2026 review frames the new directions: vascular targets for lysis versus thrombectomy, tenecteplase replacing alteplase, broader indications and ancillary drugs [[Grotta2026]].
