Part 1 · Understanding stroke

TIA: the warning stroke

A transient ischaemic attack (TIA, often called a mini stroke; US: ischemic) passes, but the risk does not pass with it. See how the definition moved from time to tissue, how the ABCD2 score adds up and where it fails, how the risk clusters in the first days, and what fast clinics changed.

This page reports what studies and guidelines say. It is not advice for any one person, and the figures describe groups of people. If you think someone is having a stroke, call your local emergency number.

A clot that clears: time or tissue?

The older definition drew the line at 24 hours. The 2009 AHA/ASA definition, following a 2002 proposal, draws it at tissue: a transient episode of focal brain, spinal cord or retinal ischaemia "without acute infarction" [[Easton2009,Albers2002]]. Compare three cases.

How the ABCD2 score adds up

ABCD2 sums five items to a score from 0 to 7 [[Johnston2007]]. Choose answers and watch the score and the published band change.

Teaching tool only. This shows how a published score adds up. It is never for an individual's diagnosis, prognosis or treatment. NICE says not to use scoring systems such as ABCD2 to assess risk or decide urgency after a suspected or confirmed TIA [[NICE-NG128]].

Misses people who need care

A score of 4 or more had sensitivity 86.7% and specificity 35.4% across 29 studies and 13,766 people. 20% of those under 4 had carotid stenosis over 50% or AF [[Wardlaw2015]].

Mimics score high too

35 to 41% of mimics and 66% of true TIAs scored 4 or more. 45% of those assessed were mimics [[Wardlaw2015]].

Moves risk only a little

Sensitivity 0.89, specificity 0.34, AUC 0.70. At a 5% baseline, a score over 3 adds 2.0% and a score of 3 or under lowers it by 2.9% [[Sanders2012]].

What NICE NG128 says

  • Aspirin 300 mg daily, unless contraindicated, started immediately after a suspected TIA (1.1.4, 2019) [[NICE-NG128]].
  • Immediate referral, seen within 24 hours of onset (1.1.5, 2019) [[NICE-NG128]].
  • Secondary prevention as soon as possible after diagnosis is confirmed (1.1.7) [[NICE-NG128]].
  • No CT unless an alternative diagnosis is suspected; consider MRI after specialist assessment, on the same day (1.2.1, 1.2.2, 2019) [[NICE-NG128]].

Quoted as guidance for clinicians, not instructions for any one person.

Why the first days of treatment matter

  • Aspirin, pooled 15,778 people from 12 trials: 6-week recurrent ischaemic stroke HR 0.42 (0.32 to 0.55). After TIA or minor stroke, weeks 0-2: 2 of 6,691 vs 23 of 5,726, HR 0.07 [[Rothwell2016]].
  • No benefit after 12 weeks, OR 0.97, in the same analysis [[Rothwell2016]].
  • CHANCE 8.2% vs 11.7%, HR 0.68 [[Wang2013]]. POINT 5.0% vs 6.5%, HR 0.75; major haemorrhage 0.9% vs 0.4% [[Johnston2018]]. THALES 5.5% vs 6.6%, HR 0.83 [[Johnston2020]].
  • Pooled, first 21 days: 5.2% vs 7.8%, HR 0.66 [[Pan2019]]. Bleeding rises with long courses, 2.42 [[Brown2021]]. See
Exam hookEXPRESS lowered 90-day stroke from 10.3% to 2.1% [[Rothwell2007]]. Aspirin's early effect is greatest in the first 2 weeks [[Rothwell2016]].
In plain wordsFast clinics and quick treatment cut the risk of stroke at 90 days from about 10 in 100 to about 2 in 100 in one study [[Rothwell2007]].

Mimics

Not every brief spell is a TIA. How long a spell lasts and how it behaves are two of the clues. Choose one to compare.

30-50%

Of suspected TIAs have an alternative diagnosis, the NICE committee notes [[NICE-NG128]].

22%

Mimics, with 25 different diagnoses, in 1,532 people; 75% TIA, 3% minor stroke. Up to 60% of referrals had no TIA [[Nadarajan2014]].

45.8 · 60.0%

Misdiagnosis in clinic and inpatient settings in one study [[Sadighi2019]].

OR 9.17

Memory loss pointed to a mimic, as did headache (OR 3.71); one-sided paresis pointed away (OR 0.35). 303 people; 18.2% were mimics [[Amort2011]].

Agreement is hard even for experts: GPs were confirmed in 19% and emergency departments in 13% in one comparison [[Ferro1996]], and stroke neurologists agree poorly [[Castle2010]]. Mimics also reach acute services: 24.2% of 2,305 hyperacute stroke unit admissions [[Dawson2016]], 31% in a brain attack series [[Hand2006]]. An overview is available in [[Lui2026]]. NICE lists excluding hypoglycaemia and using ROSIER among community and emergency steps [[NICE-NG128]].

Two open questions. The ESO keeps a pragmatic clinical definition of under 24 hours [[Fonseca2021]], while others say the 24-hour limit should be abandoned [[Nadarajan2014]]. Early risk also spreads widely: 5.2% pooled at 7 days [[Giles2007]], 2.1% in a modern registry of urgently treated people [[Amarenco2016]], 8.0% in an older population cohort [[Coull2004]]; setting and treatment explain much of it.
