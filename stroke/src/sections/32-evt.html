Part 3 · Acute treatment

Thrombectomy

Endovascular thrombectomy pulls the clot out through a thin tube. Watch a stent retriever do it, explore the trials by window and by who was treated, and see what delay costs. This page reports what trials and guidelines say. It is not medical advice.

Schematic, not to scale. Lesion size and reperfused share are illustrative, not measured.

HERMES common OR

2.49

Adjusted common odds ratio for reduced disability (95% CI 1.76 to 3.53), in 5 trials and 1287 people [[Goyal2016]].

HERMES NNT, as published

2.6

Treat 2.6 people to reduce disability by at least one mRS level for one person [[Goyal2016]].

One hour of delay

5.2 points

Functional independence fell by 5.2 percentage points per hour among those reperfused [[Saver2016]].

One minute saved

4.2 days

Modelling: 4.2 days of extra healthy life per minute (95% prediction interval 2.3 to 5.4). 20 minutes equalled about 3 months of disability-free life [[Meretoja2017]].

In plain wordsThrombectomy is a procedure where a doctor passes a thin tube up an artery, often from the groin, and pulls out the clot. It is for clots in larger arteries [[Goyal2016,Saver2015,Balami2018]]. For every 3 or so people who have thrombectomy, about one is left with less disability [[Goyal2016]].

Hypothesis A tension to teach. Early-window results are strongly time-dependent, yet DAWN and DEFUSE 3 show large effects late in people selected by imaging. Selection for slow progressors is the usual reconciliation, and these abstracts did not test it [[Saver2016,Nogueira2018,Albers2018]].

In plain wordsEvery minute counts. Saving one minute before thrombectomy is estimated to give about 4 days of extra healthy life on average [[Meretoja2017]].

Tenecteplase first: BRIDGE-TNK

China, 550 people. Tenecteplase then thrombectomy gave mRS 0 to 2 of 52.9% versus 44.1% with thrombectomy alone (RR 1.20, 1.01 to 1.43; P=0.04). Symptomatic haemorrhage 8.5% versus 6.7%; mortality 22.3% versus 19.9% [[Qiu2025]].

ESO 2023 expert consensus

For eligible people with large-vessel occlusion within 4.5 hours, tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg is preferable to skipping thrombolysis, even after direct admission to a thrombectomy-capable centre [[Alamowitch2023]].

Basilar, late: ATTENTION LATE

China, 330 people, NIHSS 10 or more, 4.5 to 24 hours: tenecteplase before thrombectomy gave no gain in functional independence (30% versus 30%; adjusted rate ratio 0.92, 0.67 to 1.25) [[Li2026JAMA]].

Open question

When delay or transfer differ

Individual participant data could not establish noninferiority of skipping the lytic, and BRIDGE-TNK favours tenecteplase first. Whether this holds when delay or transfer differ is open [[Majoie2023,Qiu2025]].

Adjuncts: a lytic into the artery after thrombectomy

After reperfusion, a small dose of lytic can be given into the artery. Two larger China trials were neutral, while CHOICE and ANGEL-TNK were positive. Doses, windows and thresholds differ, so the question remains open [[Renu2022,Huang2025,Liu2025,Miao2025]].

Anaesthesia: the data point opposite ways

Observational: in HERMES (797 procedures, non-randomised choice) outcomes were better without general anaesthesia (covariate-adjusted cOR 1.53, 1.14 to 2.04) [[Campbell2018GA]].

Randomised: 7 trials, 980 participants: general anaesthesia improved recanalisation (84.6% versus 75.6%) and functional recovery (44.6% versus 36.2%; OR 1.43, 1.04 to 1.98), with no difference in haemorrhage or mortality [[CampbellD2023]]. GOLIATH also shifted towards a lower mRS with it (OR 1.91, 1.03 to 3.56) [[Simonsen2018]].

Confounding by indication is the obvious explanation, since the HERMES choice was not randomised [[Campbell2018GA,CampbellD2023]].

Technique: aspiration or stent retriever

ASTER (France, 381 people): successful revascularisation 85.4% with first-line contact aspiration versus 83.1% with a stent retriever (OR 1.20, 0.68 to 2.10; P=.53) [[Lapergue2017]].

COMPASS (270 people, ASPECTS over 6, within 6 hours): mRS 0 to 2, 52% versus 50%, non-inferior. Mortality 22% each [[Turk2019]].

Complications

A narrative review puts complications with sequelae at about 15% across RCTs: access-site problems, vasospasm, arterial perforation and dissection, device detachment, symptomatic and subarachnoid haemorrhage, new-territory embolisation, anaesthetic and contrast effects, pseudoaneurysm [[Balami2018]].

Procedural complications were 14% in ATTENTION and 11% in BAOCHE [[Tao2022,Jovin2022]]. TESLA reported subarachnoid haemorrhage in 16.2% versus 6.2% [[Yoo2024]].

Who can reach it

Median 2.79% rounded to whole dots; IQR 0.70 to 11.74. MT-GLASS survey, 887 responses, 67 countries, 2020 to 2021 [[Asif2023]].

Access was under 1.0% in 18 countries (27%) and 0 in 7 (10%), with a 460-fold gap between the highest and lowest nonzero regions. Low-income countries were 88% lower than high-income ones [[Asif2023]].

Operators reached 16.5% and centres 20.8% of optimal availability. Access rose with a prehospital bypass protocol (OR 4.00, 1.70 to 9.42) [[Asif2023]]. Each 5% rise in rural population share had 13% lower odds of access after adjustment (OR 0.87, 0.78 to 0.96) [[Asif2026]].

In plain wordsMany people in the world cannot reach thrombectomy. In some countries it is not available at all [[Asif2023]].
