Life after stroke

The effects nobody can see, how they change with time, and the everyday questions: work, driving, sex, carers, being young, having a baby, and what people say they still need.

In plain wordsLow mood and worry are common, can last for years, and can be treated. They are part of the stroke, not a weakness [[Hackett2014,Rafsten2018,Richards2026]]. Feeling very tired is common too, and rest does not always fix it. It is worth telling your team [[Alghamdi2021,Richards2026]].

About the numbers. Everything here describes groups of people in studies. A percentage cannot tell you what will happen to you. This page reports research. It is not medical advice, and it does not replace your own stroke team.

Controversy

Can antidepressants prevent depression after stroke?

Robinson and Jorge reported that antidepressants significantly decrease incident depression and may improve recovery and survival [[Robinson2016]]. The 2026 guideline does not recommend routine prophylaxis, after the large trials [[Richards2026]].

Crying or laughing you cannot control

Pseudobulbar affect occurs in 11% to 37.8%, according to the 2026 guideline. It lists dextromethorphan/quinidine (COR 1, LOE A), noting that its pivotal trials did not include people with stroke [[Richards2026]].

The gap the mRS misses

Among 362 people aged 18 to 50 in Singapore, 87.8% reached mRS 0 to 2 at 90 days but 68.8% had returned to work. Pooled return in young ischaemic stroke was 63.2% (56.0 to 69.9) [[Kwok2025]].

Why reports differ

Earlier reports ranged from 0% to 100% because of methods [[Daniel2009]]. Predictors in the review of young stroke: ADL independence, fewer deficits, better cognition [[Edwards2018]].

2026 guideline

Early tailored vocational rehabilitation (COR 2a, LOE B-R), modified duties (2a, B-R), fatigue management and early employer engagement (2a, B-R) [[Richards2026]].

In plain wordsSex and intimacy can change after a stroke. You can raise this with your team, as many people have the same worries [[Richards2026]].
More than 50%

report sexual dysfunction, and it is under-addressed. The 2026 guideline says it "may be reasonable" for providers to discuss sexual function (COR 2b, LOE B-NR) [[Richards2026]].

From 0 to 80%

A clinician interview script raised discussion from 0 to 80% within 10 months. Eight intervention studies also included a 30 to 45 minute structured programme, sexual counselling and pelvic floor training [[Auger2021]].

More than the lesion

Psychological factors play a significant part, and the role of lesion location remains inconclusive [[Grenier2017]]. The cause is multifactorial, and it is frequently neglected by patients and clinicians [[Park2015]].

How long, and who was studied

Deterioration in sexual life was reported in 5% to 76% of working-age adults [[Daniel2009]]. Dysfunction can last months or as long as 2 years. Reviewed participants were predominantly men, with few women or younger people studied [[Dusenbury2017]].

Relationships are named in the Canadian best practice recommendations, alongside transitions, driving, vocational roles, leisure and long-term care, so people do not fall "through the cracks" between stages of care [[Mountain2020]]. "Relationship with caregiver" is one of the 11 areas of the Post-Stroke Checklist [[Philp2013]].

2026 guideline

Engage carers early and throughout (COR 2a, LOE B-R). Teach-back training (2a, B-R). Tailored multicomponent psychosocial intervention after discharge (2a, B-R) [[Richards2026]].

Controversy

Support is recommended, trials are mixed

The guideline supports carer engagement at 2a [[Richards2026]], but TRACS and ATTEND were neutral [[Forster2013,ATTEND2017]]. ATTEND's authors did not support new task-shifting services without new evidence, which matters in low-resource settings [[ATTEND2017]].

In plain wordsResearchers have followed young people for years after a stroke. In the FUTURE study about two in three did not have a poor outcome, and about one in three did. Getting back to work takes time, and a good score does not capture everything [[Synhaeve2014,Kwok2025]].

More young strokes

Average age of onset for ischaemic stroke is decreasing as incidence rises under age 50, with traditional risk factors increasingly important [[Maaijwee2014]].

FUTURE: 722 people aged 18 to 50

Mean follow-up 9.1 years. Poor outcome 32.0% overall. One of 8 survivors is dependent at 10 years, which also means 7 of 8 are not [[Synhaeve2014]].

Long-term survival

FUTURE (959 people): 20-year mortality among 30-day survivors 24.9% TIA, 26.8% ischaemic stroke, 13.7% intracerebral haemorrhage. Standardised mortality ratios 2.6, 3.9 and 3.9 versus the general population [[RuttenJacobs2013]].

Back to work

87.8% reached mRS 0 to 2 at 90 days and 68.8% had returned to work, among 362 people in Singapore. A good mRS does not capture everything [[Kwok2025]].

In plain wordsA second stroke is a real risk for years, so preventing one is lifelong work [[Mohan2011,Maaijwee2014]].
In plain wordsIn a French study, a past ischaemic stroke did not rule out a later pregnancy, and pregnancies went much as in the general population [[Lamy2000]]. Evidence is limited, so Canadian guidance rests on consensus. This is a topic for your own team.
"Not a contraindication"

441 French women aged 15 to 40, 187 later pregnancies. Recurrence 1% within 1 year, 2.3% within 5 years. Outcome of pregnancies resembled the general population. "A previous ischemic stroke is not a contraindication" [[Lamy2000]].

After the birth

In the same study, the risk ratio for recurrence was 9.7 (1.2 to 78.9) post partum, versus 2.2 (0.3 to 17.5) in pregnancy. The intervals are wide [[Lamy2000]].

Stroke during pregnancy

US pregnancy-related hospitalisations: 16,694 of 37,360,772 (0.045%, 1 in 2222) had acute stroke. In-hospital mortality was 42.1 versus 0.11 per 1000, falling among those with stroke from 5.5% in 2007 to 2.7% in 2015 [[Elgendy2020]].

Canadian consensus

Decisions rest on what would be done if the woman was not pregnant and if she had not had a stroke, since maternal health is vital for fetal wellbeing. Evidence is limited, hence consensus [[Swartz2018,Ladhani2018]].

In plain wordsIn one UK survey about half of people had no unmet needs. Across studies, information is one of the main concerns. If you still have questions, you are in good company [[McKevitt2011,Lin2021]].

Across 32 studies

15.08% to 97.59% had unmet needs, median 67.20%, 62.14% at 6 months and 81.37% at 2 years. Main concerns: information, physical function, mental health [[Lin2021]].

South London, 15 years on

Among people surviving 15 years, 87% lived at home. Disability mild 33.8%, moderate 14.3%, severe 15.0%. Cognitive impairment 30.0%, depression 39.1%, anxiety 34.9% [[Crichton2016]].

Self-management

14 trials, 1863 participants: quality of life SMD 0.34 (0.05 to 0.62), self-efficacy SMD 0.33 (0.04 to 0.61), no superior effect on ADL, participation or mood [[Fryer2016]].

2026 guideline

Community or home rehabilitation (COR 1, LOE A). Discharge planning (2a, B-R). Social health screening (1, B-NR). Community exercise (2a, C-LD) [[Richards2026]].

Moving for life

2026 guideline

150 to 300 minutes of moderate-intensity activity per week, 3 to 5 days of aerobic and 2 to 3 days of strength and balance training (COR 1, LOE A). A tailored programme (1, A). Behaviour change techniques (2a, B-R) [[Richards2026]].

Moving more, sitting less

An AHA statement describes low to moderate-intensity aerobic activity, muscle strengthening and less sedentary behaviour, customised for adherence [[Billinger2014]]. In BUST-stroke (19 people), 3-minute breaks every 30 minutes for 8 hours did not change glucose or insulin [[English2018]].

Rehabilitation around the world

2.41 billion

people globally had conditions that would benefit from rehabilitation in 2019, up 63% since 1990 [[Cieza2021]].

Where services are few

Most people with stroke in India have no access to organised rehabilitation [[ATTEND2017]]. A scoping review (25 studies, 11 countries, 16 RCTs) found mostly middle-income settings and one low-income study: 10 RCTs positive, 5 not, and no cost-effectiveness data [[Hardianto2026]].

Early evidence

Phones and apps

mHealth apps (11 trials, 264 people): 6 of 7 studies showed better physical function, most robustly for the arm [[Rintala2023]]. Pilots include WalkWithMe in Benin (9 adults) [[Noukpo2024]] and smartphone-guided electrical stimulation (31 adults) [[Purohit2026]]. Medication adherence with mHealth (10 RCTs, 2151 people): OR 2.81 (1.35 to 5.85) [[Zeng2022]].

Talking about what matters

Palliative care is part of stroke care

The 2025 AHA statement says palliative care needs "are insufficiently addressed by current models of care" and should be addressed throughout the illness, including communication about prognosis and goals [[Creutzfeldt2025]]. The 2014 statement listed nine expectations, including estimating prognosis, setting goals of care, and bereavement resources [[Holloway2014]].

Advance care planning

In a Corpus Christi cohort (148 people), 18% had no advance care planning, 44% informal conversations only, 38% formal documents. Both were linked with earlier transition to comfort measures (informal HR 3.55, 1.35 to 9.33; formal HR 2.85, 1.05 to 7.72). Formal documents added no extra association beyond informal talks (HR 0.80, 0.40 to 1.63) [[Lank2021]].

In plain wordsTalking with family about what matters to you, before you are unwell, can guide later choices [[Lank2021,Creutzfeldt2025]].

Survival and a second stroke: the population curves

You can stop here if you prefer. This is the last section, and nothing above depends on it. It shows survival and recurrence across hundreds of thousands of people. Some people want these figures, and some would rather not read them. Both are fine.

These curves describe whole populations, not you. They count everyone in large registers and studies, whatever their age or the size of their stroke. They cannot say what will happen to any one person. Dots are published, dashed lines between them are schematic.

Other long-term cohorts

Sweden (22,929 people): 5-year death or dependency 70.6% ischaemic, 79% haemorrhagic [[Sennfalt2019]]. Oxford: 70% dead or disabled at 5 years, 39% of survivors disabled, 19% care-home risk [[LuengoFernandez2013]]. South London: 20% to 30% have poor outcomes up to 10 years [[Wolfe2011]].

Measuring outcome

Trials use the modified Rankin Scale, 0 (no symptoms) to 6 (death), with 0 to 2 often a favourable outcome at 3 months [[ATTEND2017,Bernhardt2015]]. Inter-rater reliability is moderate and improves with structured interviews (kappa 0.56 versus 0.78) [[Banks2007]].

Exam hookFive-year recurrence 26.4% [[Mohan2011]]; 5-year survival 52.8% [[Peng2022]]. Depression about 31% [[Hackett2014]], anxiety about 29% [[Rafsten2018]], fatigue about 48% [[Alghamdi2021]], central pain about 11% [[Liampas2020]], hemiplegic shoulder pain about 29% [[AdeyWakeling2015]].
