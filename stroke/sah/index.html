Part 3 · Acute treatment

Treating subarachnoid haemorrhage

After an aneurysm bursts, each stretch of the next three weeks has its own danger. Scrub from day 0 to day 21, then compare coiling with clipping and see what the evidence says about nimodipine and life afterwards.

AHA/ASA 2023

Replaces the 2012 guideline 197,432. Recommends timely transfer to higher-volume centres (Class 1, B-NR), with no numeric threshold in the recommendation 432. Neurocritical Care Society guidance appeared the same year 983.

ESO/EANS/ESMINT 2026

15 evidence-based recommendations and 37 expert consensus statements. High-level evidence backs only oral nimodipine, and regular coiling when both options are equally suitable in good-grade patients. Consensus: treat in a centre with at least 70 aSAH patients a year (35 in remote areas), by a physician treating at least 30 aneurysms a year 1007.

Hours 0 to 12

Rebleeding: the first danger

Rebleeding occurs in about 40% if the aneurysm stays untreated 1007. Most occurs within 24 hours of onset 192, and the rate is highest in the first 6 hours 549. Rebleeding under 6 hours had OR 3.22 (1.46 to 7.12) in a meta-analysis of 14 studies 957. AHA/ASA 2023 asks for blood pressure control with short-acting agents to avoid severe hypotension, hypertension and variability (1, C-EO), and emergency anticoagulant reversal (1, C-EO) 432.

In plain wordsAfter an aneurysm bleeds, doctors try to seal it fast because it may bleed again, most often soon after the first bleed 192.

What raises rebleeding risk

Systolic pressure over 160 mm Hg had OR 3.1 753. High SBP 1.93, Hunt-Hess III to IV 3.43, intracerebral or intraventricular haematoma 1.65, posterior circulation 2.15, size over 10 mm 1.70 957. It is among the strongest predictors of poor outcome: in-hospital death aOR 7.69 942, unfavourable discharge OR 7.41 340.

Stopping the clot breaking down?

ULTRA (955 people, tranexamic acid up to 24 h): good outcome 60% versus 64%, rebleeding 10% versus 14% (OR 0.71, 0.48 to 1.04) 807. AHA text reads ULTRA as no significant reduction 432. Meta-analyses show less rebleeding with no gain in function 26,854,1007. Both guidelines agree: not routinely (AHA 3: No Benefit, A) 432,1007.

In plain wordsBlood around the brain can cause trouble days later, so people often stay in hospital for up to two weeks and take a medicine called nimodipine 432,796.

Open Both guidelines favour treatment within 24 hours 432,1007. ESO moved from the 2013 target of within 72 hours, yet stresses that the evidence for under 24 hours is low-quality observational, and very early treatment might worsen early brain injury and DCI. No randomised trial has settled it 1007.

ESO 2026 suggests reserving stent-assisted coiling for when other options are not feasible, and flow diversion as a last line. ISAT-2 stopped early for poor enrolment 1007. A non-randomised 24-centre comparison (n=9159) linked coiling to higher 14-day case fatality and non-superior 90-day outcome, as quoted in a review 192. AHA/ASA 2023 recommends follow-up cerebrovascular imaging after repair (1, B-NR) 432.

Exam hookISAT: 1-year death or dependence about 7% less with coiling, late rebleeding more common 685.

Nimodipine, and what else for DCI

The 6 doses a day and the total of 126 are worked out here from "every 4 hours for 21 days". Trial regimen, not an instruction.

The result

Fewer infarcts, fewer poor outcomes

Cerebral infarction 22% versus 33% (34% reduction, 13 to 50). Poor outcome 20% versus 33% (40% reduction, 20 to 55) 796. Cochrane (16 trials, 3361 people): oral nimodipine RR 0.67 (0.55 to 0.81), with results depending largely on one large trial 251.

Exam hookOral nimodipine 60 mg every 4 hours for 21 days in BRANT; never IV from capsules 295,796.

Boxed warning on the US label: nimodipine capsules must not be given intravenously or by other parenteral routes, as deaths have occurred. The label's indication covers Hunt and Hess I to V 295.

Fluid, sodium and the ventricles

One prospective series of 100, of whom 49 developed sodium under 135 mmol/L 395.

Flagged discrepancy

36 of 49 is 73.5%, the abstract prints 71.4%

Both figures are shown because they do not match in the source: SIAD in 36 of 49, printed as 71.4% 395. This series found no cerebral salt wasting. A review instead treats SIAD or cerebral salt wasting as causes that are hard to separate, says fluid restriction can be harmful, and focuses management on euvolaemia 192.

AHA/ASA 2023: goal-directed euvolaemia (2a, B-R); mineralocorticoids reasonable for natriuresis and hyponatraemia (2a, B-R) 432.

Exam hookHyponatraemia after SAH is mostly SIAD in the best prospective series 395.

For hydrocephalus, AHA/ASA 2023 recommends urgent CSF diversion when acute and symptomatic (1, B-NR), an EVD bundle (1, B-NR) and permanent diversion when chronic and symptomatic (1, B-NR). Routine lamina terminalis fenestration is not indicated (3: No Benefit, C-LD) 432. EVD or lumbar puncture before securing did not clearly raise rebleeding: HR 1.0 (0.4 to 2.7) and 0.7 (0.1 to 6.4) 421.

Outcomes over time

Each line joins two published points only. Nothing between them is measured here.

Greater Cincinnati, 1988 to 2010

Case fatality fell, with a caveat

Case fatality fell at 5 days 32 to 18%, at 30 days 46 to 25%, at 90 days 49 to 29%, while incidence stayed stable (8.8 to 7.7 per 100 000). For proven or highly likely aneurysmal SAH alone, the declines were no longer significant 618.

Other series agree on direction but differ on size. In 33 studies, case fatality ranged 8.3 to 66.7%, falling 0.8% a year, 17% over 1973 to 2002, despite mean age rising from 52 to 62 736. An earlier review gave 32 to 67% over 1960 to 1992 436. In the 1980s International Cooperative Study of 3521 people, 26% had died at 6 months and 58% had fully recovered 488. AHA text gives prehospital mortality of 22 to 26% and US inpatient mortality of 13.7% in 2006 and 13.1% in 2018 432. The ESO lay summary says about 40% die within 3 months and roughly one third regain independence 1007. A Swiss registry found in-hospital mortality of 20%, or 13.9% once those with active treatment discontinued were excluded 942. In 3078 one-year survivors, 20-year excess mortality was 17%, including young people and those with good recovery 448.

After hospital: long-term issues

Figures are rounded to whole numbers of 100. Each study has its own size and setting.

Long-term

Cognitive or emotional complaints

94.6% of 111 people reported at least one complaint that hampered daily life at 3 months. Most frequent: mental slowness, short-term memory, attention 774.

Hidden problems

People who look normal at discharge may have hidden cognitive deficits that interfere with daily function 749. Memory, executive function and language are often affected, compounded by depression, anxiety, fatigue and sleep disturbance 23.

Mood

AHA text: depression in about one-third of survivors, anxiety and post-traumatic stress disorder in about 15 to 20%, cognitive impairment in 40 to 70% 432.

Fatigue

Among 96 good-outcome people with fatigue, it ranked in their three most disabling symptoms, described as low motivation, mental fatigue and sensitivity to stress 1040.

What guidelines ask for

AHA/ASA 2023: validated screening for physical, cognitive, behavioural and quality-of-life deficits before discharge (1, B-NR), depression and anxiety screening (1, B-NR), early multidisciplinary rehabilitation (1, B-NR) 432. Long-term data remain limited 845.

In plain wordsMany people who survive still feel tired or find thinking and memory harder, even when they look well. This is real, common and worth raising with the care team 23,1040.
