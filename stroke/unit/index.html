Not medical adviceThe Stroke Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. If you think someone is having a stroke, call your local emergency number. It does not replace a doctor, nurse, physiotherapist, occupational therapist, speech and language therapist or the rest of a stroke team. Scores such as the NIHSS, ICH score, ABCD2, CHA2DS2-VASc and ASPECTS are teaching aids, never for an individual's diagnosis, prognosis or treatment. Doses, thresholds and guideline statements are quoted as published, never as instructions. Outcomes are population statistics; they are not predictions for any person.

Part 3 · Acute treatment

The stroke unit

Clot-busting drugs and thrombectomy reach some people. Organised stroke unit care reaches everyone admitted, and its effect is large. See what it changes, what can go wrong in the first week, and which simple care and which big operation have trial evidence.

This page reports what trials and guidelines say. It is not advice about any one person's care. The 2026 AHA/ASA guideline replaces the 2018 guideline and its 2019 update, and covers care from before hospital through early complications and secondary prevention 810,812. It names management of hyperglycaemia and dysphagia among its key updates 812. Its recommendation wording was not read for this page, so no class of recommendation is quoted here.

The FeSS bundle: fever, sugar, swallow

QASC was a single-blind cluster randomised trial in 19 acute stroke units in New South Wales, with 1696 patients analysed. Intervention units received nurse-initiated protocols for fever, hyperglycaemia and swallowing dysfunction, plus multidisciplinary team-building workshops. Control units received an abridged version of existing guidelines 676.

F is for fever

Fever

As listed for a later QASC translation trial: temperature monitored and recorded at least four times per day for the first 72 h, and "If temperature => 37.5°C treat with paracetamol or other anti-pyretic" 292. A meta-analysis of 14,431 people with stroke and other brain injury tied fever to worse outcome: relative risk 1.5 for mortality, 2.2 for modified Rankin 371.

Practice gap: 50% of people with fever of 37.5°C or more had paracetamol within an hour in a 2021 Australian audit 292.

Exam hookQASC tested nurse-initiated protocols for Fever, Sugar and Swallow, with number needed to treat 6.4 for death or dependency at 90 days 676.

QASC at 90 days: mRS 2 or more, p=0.002; number needed to treat 6.4; adjusted absolute difference 15.7%, 95% CI 5.8 to 25.4 676.

In plain wordsThree simple things matter in the first days: checking for fever, checking blood sugar, and checking swallowing. Units that did this well had fewer people left dead or dependent 676.

Mass effect

The midline moves

Warning signs in swollen hemispheric infarction: new or further impaired consciousness, cerebral ptosis and changes in pupil size 1049. In swollen cerebellar infarction: falling consciousness from brainstem compression, possibly with early loss of corneal reflexes and miosis 1049.

Swelling in the back of the brain

The AHA statement says suboccipital craniectomy with dural expansion should be performed in people who deteriorate neurologically, and that ventriculostomy should be accompanied by it to avoid upward cerebellar displacement 1049.

The evidence is observational. Across 11 studies (283 people), moderate to severe disability was 28%, mortality 20% and adverse events 23% 68. One matched study suggested preventive surgery might beat medical treatment alone in selected people Early evidence 503.

Goals of care

The HAMLET authors wrote that the decision to operate should depend on the emphasis people and relatives place on survival and dependency 432. Primary palliative care should be available to all people with serious or life-threatening stroke and their families, throughout the illness, says the 2014 AHA statement 434. The 2025 statement adds that needs are insufficiently addressed by current models 209.

2026 neurocritical care guidance advises avoiding premature prognostication 623. Early care limitations can shape outcomes: an observational study in intracerebral haemorrhage tied an early do-not-resuscitate order to a doubled hazard of death (HR 2.17, 1.38 to 3.41) 1091. That is not ischaemic stroke, and setting goals of care early remains the guidance 434.

Early mobilisation: AVERT, negative and positive

AVERT is the trial where the main result and the dose analysis seem to pull in opposite directions. Both matter.

Figures from AVERT and its dose analysis 98,99, and Cochrane 2018 544.

The negative result

More, earlier, was worse

Favourable outcome (mRS 0 to 2) was lower with very early mobilisation: 46% vs 50% (adjusted OR 0.73, 0.59 to 0.90; p=0.004). Deaths were 8% vs 7% (OR 1.34, 0.93 to 1.93), with no reduction in immobility-related complications 98.

Exam hookAVERT: higher dose earlier was worse (46% vs 50%), while shorter, more frequent sessions were associated with better outcome 98,99.
In the AVERT database, 4% of 2104 people had a serious fall in the first year. 60% of those falls caused a fracture, and 42% of fractures were hip fractures 1025. Module 5.3 covers falls care.

Also in the first days

Bleeding into the infarct

In ECASS II, parenchymal haematoma and symptomatic haemorrhage were tied to alteplase, but haemorrhagic infarction was not. Risk factors included alteplase, extent of hypoattenuation on CT, heart failure, older age and baseline systolic pressure 549. The AHA statement names prompt diagnosis and early correction of coagulopathy as the mainstay of treatment 1074. Classification after thrombectomy differs too: ECASS III assigned 101 of 156 bleeds unambiguously, and Heidelberg categorised 55 more 731.

Seizures

Risk after ischaemic stroke rose with cortical location (HR 2.09) and disability (HR 2.10) 108. The SeLECT score uses severity, large-artery cause, early seizures, cortical involvement and MCA territory. SeLECT 0 predicted 0.7% late seizures at 1 year and 1.3% at 5; SeLECT 9 predicted 63% and 83% 343.

Delirium

25% (20 to 30) pooled across 32 papers in the first 6 weeks 920. A cohort of 527 found 11.8% in the first week 755. Delirium went with inpatient death (OR 4.71), a stay longer by 9.39 days, and discharge to an institution (OR 3.39) 922.

Finding atrial fibrillation

New AF across 50 studies: 7.7% on the admission ECG, 5.1% with in-hospital monitoring, 10.7% with Holter, 16.9% with later ambulatory methods, 23.7% after all phases 935. With implantable monitors: 12.2% at 3 months and 28.5% at 36 months 745. Optimal duration is not established 384. See module 4.2.

Exam hookAspirin within 48 h: net 9 fewer further strokes or deaths per 1000 in hospital 178. Lying flat vs head up 30 degrees for 24 h: no difference in outcome or pneumonia (HeadPoST) 40.
In plain wordsIf a stroke is very severe, the team should talk with you and your family about what matters most 209,434.
