Part 4 · Preventing another stroke

Carotid disease and PFO

Watch plaque narrow a carotid artery and measure it two ways. See who gained from surgery and how much timing mattered, how modern trials changed the picture for people without symptoms, and how RoPE and PASCAL sort out when a hole in the heart mattered.

Schematic. Percentages are the geometry of this drawing, for teaching, and not a clinical measurement. Bars from 859.

Step 1 · the bifurcation

A healthy bifurcation

The common carotid artery (CCA) divides in the neck into the internal carotid (ICA), which widens into a bulb and supplies the brain, and the external carotid (ECA). Trials of surgery asked which narrowings of the ICA are worth treating 85,269.

In plain wordsTwo big arteries in your neck carry blood to the brain. Each one splits in two, and this split is where a narrowing is measured.

Try the sum: a NASCET measuring tool

A teaching tool, never for an individual's diagnosis, prognosis or treatment. Move the three widths and see that one narrowing gives two different percentages. Units are arbitrary.

NASCET band: 50 to 69%. In the pooled symptomatic trials, this band had a 5-year absolute reduction of 4.6 percentage points in ipsilateral ischaemic stroke with surgery 859. Population figure, not a result for anyone. Near-occlusion cannot be detected from this sum and was analysed apart.

Timing and who gains most

Number needed to treat over 5 years for stenosis of 50% or more: how many people have surgery to prevent one ipsilateral stroke. One figure in each block is marked 860. The authors state that surgery should ideally occur within 2 weeks.

In plain wordsIf a neck artery is badly narrowed and has caused symptoms, surgery works best when done soon, ideally within 2 weeks 860.

Stenosis without symptoms, then and now

Early trials showed a modest gain from surgery. Modern trials compare a procedure with much better medical treatment, and medical-arm risks are far below those of ACST-1 138,387.

Outcomes and durations differ between trials, so compare within a row 2,138,387,388,834.

CREST-2 (NEJM 2026)

70% or more asymptomatic stenosis, intensive medical management with or without revascularisation. Stenting trial, 1245 people: 4-year events 6.0% medical vs 2.8% stenting (P=0.02). Endarterectomy trial, 1240 people: 5.3% vs 3.7% (P=0.24). Periprocedural events: 8 with stenting vs 0 medical; 9 with endarterectomy vs 3 medical 138.

ECST-2 interim

429 people with 50% or more stenosis and low-to-intermediate predicted risk. At 2 years the win ratio was 1.01 (0.60 to 1.70), with no benefit shown from adding revascularisation. The trial is ongoing 250.

Does severity matter?

In OxVasc, 5-year ipsilateral stroke was 14.6% at 70 to 99% and none in 154 people at 50 to 69%. Cohorts show severity dependence (OR 2.9) where trials did not (OR 0.8) 442.

ACST-1 and age

At 10 years, benefit was seen under age 75 but not in older people. ACAS's gain was conditional on surgery with under 3% perioperative risk 2,388.

A live disagreement. CREST-2's stenting arm showed benefit, whereas its endarterectomy arm, SPACE-2 and ECST-2 did not 138,250,834. Severity may be the key, since cohort data show high risk only at severe stenosis 442.

In plain wordsIf a narrowed neck artery has caused no symptoms, medicines work much better than they used to, and doctors are still working out who gains from a procedure 138,250.

Vertebral and intracranial stenosis

Intracranial large-artery disease has been described as likely the most common stroke subtype worldwide 361. Here, stenting has not beaten medical treatment.

SAMMPRIS

451 people, 70 to 99% stenosis. 30-day stroke or death 14.7% stenting vs 5.8% medical; 1-year primary endpoint 20.0% vs 12.2% 184. Final, median 32.4 months: 23% vs 15% 239.

WASID

Warfarin vs aspirin 1300 mg for 50 to 99% stenosis. Primary endpoint 21.8% vs 22.1%, death 9.7% vs 4.3%, major haemorrhage 8.3% vs 3.2% 183.

CASSISS

358 people, refined selection, beyond 3 weeks from symptoms. Primary endpoint 8.0% vs 7.2% (HR 1.10, 0.52 to 2.35) 343.

Vertebral: VAST and pooled

VAST, 115 people: 30-day event 5% stenting vs 2% medical 194. VIST, VAST and SAMMPRIS pooled, 354 people: any stroke HR 0.81 (0.45 to 1.44). Periprocedural stroke or death 16% intracranial vs 1% extracranial 633.

Exam hookSAMMPRIS: stenting worse than aggressive medical therapy at 30 days (14.7% vs 5.8%) 184.

Patent foramen ovale (PFO)

A PFO occurs in about 1 in 4 adults 672, so finding one after a stroke does not show it caused the stroke. RoPE estimates how likely that is. PASCAL adds features of the PFO itself.

A teaching tool, never for an individual's diagnosis, prognosis or treatment. Points are quoted from secondary tables that cite the original RoPE paper 173,745.

RoPE 7 of 10

In the stratum 7, the PFO-attributable fraction was 72% and 2-year recurrence 6% 745. A higher score makes it more likely that the PFO was the cause, and also goes with a lower chance of another stroke.

What statements say

AAN 2020 (level C): under 60 with an embolic-appearing infarct and no other mechanism, clinicians may recommend closure after discussing an absolute 5-year stroke reduction of 3.4%, a periprocedural complication rate of 3.9% and added non-periprocedural AF of 0.33% per year 672.

ANZ consensus 2026: PASCAL should be incorporated into decisions and high RoPE alone should not be the principal basis, with decisions by a multidisciplinary heart-brain team 173.

Early evidence

Still open

Above age 60: not tested in the pooled trials 492. A feasibility study estimated 5-year recurrence of 13.7% 1022. Observational data at 60 or over gave RR 0.49 for recurrent stroke or TIA 224.

AF after closure: one meta-analysis suggests it is mostly early and transient (RR 2.27 overall; 0.60 after the first month, CI 0.02 to 19.88) 935, yet SCOPE reports excess AF beyond day 45 in the unlikely group 492.

Exam hookPASCAL: RoPE of 7 or more plus large shunt or atrial septal aneurysm = probable 173.
In plain wordsAbout 1 in 4 adults has a small hole between the upper heart chambers. Often it did not cause the stroke, so doctors look at the whole picture before closing it 492,672.
