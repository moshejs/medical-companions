Part 1 · Understanding CF

How CFTR works

CFTR is a gate for chloride and bicarbonate in the surface of cells that line the airways, gut, pancreas and sweat glands. Follow one channel through its cycle, then see what its loss does to the airway surface and why the same loss makes sweat salty.

When the gene was cloned in 1989, its predicted protein had two similar halves, each with a membrane-spanning domain and an ATP-binding domain, and the commonest CF variant, the loss of phenylalanine 508, sat in the middle of the first nucleotide-binding domain (NBD1) 231,373. CFTR belongs to the ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporter family, yet it works as an anion channel 80. Pick a step or press Play.

Step 1 · at rest

Closed: the R domain blocks the gate

CFTR has five domains: two membrane-spanning domains that form the pore, two nucleotide-binding domains (NBD1, NBD2) whose ATP binding and hydrolysis control gating, and a regulatory (R) domain 80,417. In structures without phosphorylation, the R domain sits between the NBDs (zebrafish, 3.7 Å) or inside the intracellular vestibule (human, 3.9 Å), and blocks opening 259,515.

In plain wordsCFTR is a tiny gate in the surface of cells lining the lungs, gut, pancreas and sweat glands. At rest it is shut 80,267.

The airway surface: with and without CFTR

The airway is lined by a thin airway surface liquid (ASL): a mucus layer on top of a periciliary liquid layer (PCL) in which the cilia beat. In cultured human airway epithelium, active ion transport kept the PCL at about 7 µm, and the mucus layer acted as a reservoir, giving or taking liquid to hold the PCL at the best height for mucus transport 461. Normal epithelium does this by blending chloride secretion with sodium absorption through the epithelial sodium channel (ENaC), steered by signals in the liquid (ATP, adenosine, proteases), not by the cilia 463.

With CFTR. Chloride and bicarbonate leave the cells while ENaC takes sodium back, and the balance holds the periciliary layer at about 7 µm, deep enough for cilia to beat and carry mucus 461,463.

Why mucus stops: brush, concentration and glands

Established

Liquid, not salt

Matsui 1998 found no evidence that ASL is hypotonic or that its salt content differs in CF. CF cultures instead absorbed ASL abnormally fast, depleting the PCL and stopping mucus transport, which supports the "isotonic volume" hypothesis over the "low-salt / defensin" one 274.

A gel on a brush

The periciliary layer is a brush of tethered mucins and mucopolysaccharides that keeps mucus out of the cilia. When the mucus layer gets more concentrated than the brush, it compresses it and clearance fails 52.

Mucus that is too concentrated

Measured physically, mucins in CF sputum were hyperconcentrated and the osmotic pressure of CF sputum was substantially higher than normal, enough to compress the periciliary layer. Antibody assays underestimate CF mucin because sputum proteases cut the antibody sites 206.

Early evidence

Mucus stuck to the glands

In newborn CF piglets, mucus strands from the submucosal glands stayed tethered to the gland ducts after cholinergic stimulation, so transport failed without PCL depletion. Blocking anion secretion in non-CF airways did the same 210.

Early evidence

Breathing helps, viruses hurt

Motion that mimics breathing increased ATP release, rebalanced CF ion transport and restored PCL volume in culture; respiratory syncytial virus abolished this rescue, one proposed reason viral infections tip CF airways into bacterial infection 462.

Rehydrate, and clearance returns

Inhaled 7% hypertonic saline in 24 people with CF cleared 14.0 ± 2.0% of mucus in an hour against 7.0 ± 1.5% after amiloride pretreatment, and improved FEV1 by a mean 6.62%, which fits the rehydration theory 106. After starting ivacaftor, people with G551D-CFTR had markedly faster mucociliary clearance at 1 month, sustained at 3 107.

Same channel, opposite job: why CF sweat is salty

In 1983, microperfusion of isolated sweat ducts showed abnormally low chloride permeability in CF: as the primary sweat passes along the duct, NaCl is poorly taken back, so the final sweat stays salty Established 346. In normal ducts ENaC activity rises with CFTR activity; in CF ducts the sodium conductance could not be switched on, so salt reabsorption fails through both chloride and sodium 367. In the airway, CFTR's job runs the other way: it sends chloride and bicarbonate out 267.

CFTR working. Primary sweat starts salty. Along the duct, chloride leaves through CFTR and sodium through ENaC, whose activity depends on CFTR, so the sweat reaching the skin has lost most of its salt 346,367.

Where CFTR is made in the airway

Submucosal glands

In the human bronchus, CFTR is expressed mainly in the submucosal glands, especially the serous cells of the secretory tubules, with a subpopulation of duct cells. ΔF508 homozygotes had no detectable CFTR protein in these cells 118.

Early evidence

The pulmonary ionocyte

Single-cell RNA sequencing found a rare FOXI1-positive cell that is the main source of CFTR transcripts in mouse and human airway epithelium; knocking out Foxi1 in mice removed Cftr expression and disturbed airway fluid and mucus 303. Ionocytes co-express FOXI1, V-ATPase subunits and CFTR, and account for a major share of CFTR activity in the conducting airway 340.

Lessons from the CF pig

CFTR-null newborn pigs had meconium ileus, exocrine pancreatic destruction and focal biliary cirrhosis, as newborn humans with CF do; CF mice do not show this pattern 376. At birth their lungs showed no inflammation but cleared instilled bacteria less well, and within months they developed inflammation, remodelling, mucus and infection 450.
