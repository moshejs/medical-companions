History & people

For centuries a salty-tasting child was thought to be bewitched. In under a hundred years CF went from a newly named disease of infants to a gene, a sweat test, a screening programme and medicines that act on the faulty protein itself. Scrub the timeline from 1606 to 2025, or filter it by theme.

References to conditions that probably reflect CF go back to the Middle Ages [[Quinton1999]]. Busch traced European folk sayings about licking a child's forehead, where a salty taste meant the child was bewitched and would soon die; he found them in 12 European countries [[Busch1990]]. Venture philanthropy: how the modulators were found

In 1999–2000 the CF Foundation funded Aurora Biosciences to run high-throughput screens for compounds that would correct or potentiate CFTR. A Lasker perspective calls it "the most successful example of Venture Philanthropy to date" [[Friedman2025]].

Vertex acquired Aurora in 2001. With continued Foundation funding, screening hits were found by 2003 and ivacaftor was identified by 2005 [[Friedman2025]]. In lab studies it raised chloride secretion in G551D/F508del bronchial cells about 10-fold, to about 50% of non-CF levels [[VanGoor2009VX770]].

Vertex won approval of 4 CF drugs in 7 years, and it was the first time the FDA accepted efficacy tests in primary human cells [[Friedman2025]]. By 2022, though, only 19,516 people, about 12% of the estimated number with CF worldwide, were receiving triple therapy [[Guo2022Access]].

The 2025 Lasker–DeBakey Clinical Medical Research Award went to Michael Welsh, Jesús González and Paul Negulescu for work that led to the triple combination [[Friedman2025]].

People to know

1938

Dorothy Andersen

A Columbia University pathologist who described "cystic fibrosis of the pancreas" and set it apart from coeliac disease [[Andersen1938,Davis2006]]. She found that infants who survived the newborn period often developed progressive lung disease [[Friedman2025]], and with di Sant'Agnese used sulfonamides and penicillin against lung infections from the 1940s [[Friedman2025,Trouve2024]].

1953

di Sant'Agnese and colleagues

Dehydrated children with CF during a New York heat wave in 1948–49 led di Sant'Agnese to study their sweat [[Friedman2025,McBennett2022]]. In 1953 the team reported the abnormal sweat electrolytes of CF [[DiSantAgnese1953]], with sweat chloride 3 to 5 times normal [[Friedman2025]].

1959

Gibson and Cooke

Standardised sweat collection by pilocarpine iontophoresis, still the basis of the sweat test [[Gibson1959,Trouve2024]]. The test let doctors find milder cases, so CF was no longer seen only as a disease of mucus [[Davis2006]].

1983

Quinton

Showed that the CF sweat duct is impermeable to chloride, which explains salty sweat [[Quinton1983]].

1989

The gene hunters

Three Science papers (Rommens, Riordan, Kerem and colleagues) cloned the gene on chromosome 7 and named its protein CFTR [[Rommens1989,Riordan1989,Kerem1989]]. About 70% of CF mutations were one three-base deletion, F508del [[Kerem1989]]. A 1985 DNA marker from Tsui and colleagues had already narrowed the search [[Tsui1985]].

2025

Welsh, González and Negulescu

Lasker award winners for the work that led to the triple combination [[Friedman2025]], approved by the FDA as Trikafta on 21 October 2019 [[FDA2026CFApprovals]].
