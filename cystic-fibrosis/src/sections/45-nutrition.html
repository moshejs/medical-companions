Nutrition & enzymes

Food is part of CF treatment. Follow the fat in a meal through the gut with and without an enzyme capsule, see the dose ranges the enzyme label publishes and why there is a ceiling, then the vitamins, the salt and the growth goals, which are changing in the modulator era.

Teaching, not dosingThe doses and targets on this page are quoted from labels and guidelines so that you can understand them. They are not instructions. Enzyme doses, vitamins and salt are set for each person by their CF team.

Enzymes on CFTR modulators. The 2020 Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics guideline found not enough evidence to reduce or stop enzymes when people start CFTR modulators; if a person stops, it advises monitoring faecal elastase, weight, gut symptoms and fat-soluble vitamins [[McDonald2021]]. In people aged 12 and over, faecal elastase did not meaningfully change after 6 months of elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor [[Schwarzenberg2023PROMISEGI]]; in young children it rose, some above the sufficiency threshold Early evidence [[Rosenfeld2018ARRIVAL,Goralski2026]]. The pancreas itself is covered in Pancreas, gut & liver.

Fat-soluble vitamins: A, D, E and K

Without enough fat absorption, the vitamins that dissolve in fat are absorbed poorly too. The CFF infant guideline recommends starting CF-specific multivitamins with at least the recommended vitamins A, D, E and K shortly after diagnosis, and checking blood levels about 2 months after starting, then yearly [[Borowitz2009]]. Many newly diagnosed infants have low levels of one or more; low vitamin E is associated with lower cognitive performance [[Borowitz2009]]. Vitamin D: the CFF vitamin D guideline advises yearly checks, preferably at the end of winter, and treats a 25-hydroxyvitamin D of at least 30 ng/mL (75 nmol/L) as sufficient [[Tangpricha2012]]. A liposomal preparation raised vitamin D more than standard preparations over 3 months in one trial Early evidence [[Nowak2022]].

Energy, fat and salt

energy

More calories than peers

ADA/CFF 2010: usually 120–150% of the recommended daily intake for age, because resting energy use is higher and calories are lost through malabsorption [[Moran2010CFRD]]. ISPAD 2022's table: fat 40% of energy, carbohydrate 45–50%, protein 200% of the non-CF reference intake, and unrestricted salt [[Ode2022ISPAD]].

modulator era

Needs are changing

The Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics notes that CFTR modulators may lower energy expenditure, so energy prescriptions may need reducing to avoid excess weight gain; it describes the older approach as a high-energy, high-fat "legacy diet" [[McDonald2021]].

salt

Extra salt for babies

The CFF infant guideline recommends 1/8 teaspoon of table salt a day from diagnosis, rising to 1/4 teaspoon at 6 months. One-eighth of a teaspoon holds about 12.5 mEq of sodium, and baby foods contain no added salt [[Borowitz2009]]. ESPEN-ESPGHAN-ECFS recommends sodium supplementation, guided by the urinary sodium:creatinine ratio [[Turck2016Nutrition]]. Why salt is lost: Sweat, diabetes, bones & fertility.

support

Supplements and tubes

ESPEN-ESPGHAN-ECFS: oral nutritional supplements, then polymeric tube feeding, when growth or nutrition is impaired; not enough evidence to recommend essential fatty acids, growth hormone, appetite stimulants or probiotics routinely [[Turck2016Nutrition]]. The CFF 2016 guideline covers when to consider tube feeding and care afterwards [[Schwarzenberg2016Tube]].

Growth goals, and weight on modulators

Good nutrition early in life goes with healthier lungs later: in 3,142 US children, those whose weight-for-age at 4 was above the 50th percentile had fewer pulmonary exacerbations and hospital days by 18, and a survival advantage through childhood [[Yen2013]]. Since modulators, the problem for some people has turned around. These are population figures, not a target for any one person.

Children and adolescents with a BMI-for-age below the 50th percentile, or worrying trends, should be referred for full nutrition assessment by a registered dietitian (Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics) [[McDonald2021]]. Overweight risk is higher in older, male and pancreatic-sufficient people with milder genotypes and better lung function [[McDonald2021]].
