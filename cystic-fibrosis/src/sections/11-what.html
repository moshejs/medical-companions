Part 1 · Understanding CF

What cystic fibrosis is

Cystic fibrosis (CF) is an inherited condition in which one faulty channel protein, CFTR, leaves mucus and other secretions too thick in the lungs, pancreas and several other organs. Scrub through seven decades of survival figures, see how common CF is around the world, and tour the organs it touches.

CF is a monogenic, autosomal recessive disease "considered to affect at least 100 000 people worldwide" [[Shteinberg2021]]. Changes (variants) in the CFTR gene damage an epithelial channel that normally carries chloride and bicarbonate, so mucus is poorly hydrated and poorly cleared [[Shteinberg2021]]. Classical CF means chronic lung infection and inflammation, pancreatic exocrine insufficiency and male infertility, often with CF-related diabetes or CF liver disease alongside Established [[Shteinberg2021]]. Lung disease causes "the vast majority" of illness and deaths [[Ratjen2015]].

Some variants leave part of the protein working. These can cause milder disease that is harder to diagnose and sometimes affects only one organ [[Shteinberg2021]]. CF was long described as the most common life-threatening inherited disorder of children of European descent; it is now also a disease of adults [[Scotet2020]].

How survival is measured

Not a survival measure

Median age at death

Describes only the people who died in a year. It ignores everyone still alive, so it is not a survival measure [[Scotet2020]]. In 2016–2018 registry reports it ranged from 29.0 years (European registry) to 35.6 years (Australia) [[Scotet2020]].

What registries report

Predicted median survival

Applies this year's death rates at each age to an imaginary group of babies born today: the age beyond which half would be expected to live if death rates never changed [[Scotet2020,Keogh2018Guide]]. Because death rates keep falling, it tends to understate the future.

For people already grown up

Conditional survival

The expected median survival for people who have already reached a given age, such as 30 [[Keogh2018Guide]]. In UK data for F508del homozygotes, median survival was 46 (males) and 41 (females) at birth, but 52 and 49 for those who had reached 30 [[Keogh2018]].

Handle with care

Why countries differ

Registries use different methods and terms, which has confused patients and clinicians [[Keogh2018Guide]]. Including people with mild or borderline disease raises apparent survival [[McBennett2022]], and direct comparison between national registries is hard [[Stephenson2017]].

Every figure in this table is a population statistic from a registry or a model. None of them can say how long one person will live [[Keogh2018Guide]].

Who does less well. In 1988–1992, US females aged 1–20 were 60% more likely to die than males (RR 1.6), even after adjustment for nutrition, lung function and other factors [[Rosenfeld1997]]. One Canadian analysis links this gap to a higher risk of death in women with CF-related diabetes [[Stephenson2015ERJ]], and US death-certificate data for 1999–2024 show equal age-adjusted rates by sex; whether the gap is closing is unresolved Early evidence [[Grover2026]]. Poverty matters too: US children on Medicaid had a 3.65-fold adjusted risk of death [[Schechter2001]], and UK children from the most deprived areas had FEV1 4.12 points lower, gaps that appeared early and did not widen [[TaylorRobinson2013]]. Hispanic patients in the US had a 1.27-fold adjusted death rate [[Rho2018]].

How common CF is, around the world

Incidence was traditionally quoted as 1 in 2,500 births in people of European descent; newborn screening now suggests 1 in 3,000 to 1 in 6,000, matching carrier rates of 1 in 28 to 1 in 40 [[Scotet2020]]. The bars turn each "1 in N births" figure into CF births per 100,000 on a log scale, so very rare and very common populations fit on one chart. Choose a region.

About 162,428 people (range 144,606–186,620) are estimated to live with CF across 94 countries; about 105,352 (65%) are diagnosed, and estimates of undiagnosed CF in low- and middle-income countries are highly uncertain [[Guo2022Access]]. Common screening panels miss 25% to over 70% of disease-causing variants in non-European groups Early evidence [[Bar2026]]. Incidence is falling in most countries studied, partly after prenatal diagnosis and carrier screening (Israel's fell about 60% over 22 years), but not everywhere: Colorado and Wisconsin saw no decline [[Scotet2020]].

Before newborn screening, diagnosis was often late. In the Wisconsin randomised trial of 650,341 newborns (1985–1994), the screened babies grew better throughout childhood, and severe malnutrition after a delayed diagnosis may not be reversible [[Farrell1997,Farrell2001]]. Compare the two groups.

Mean ages at diagnosis in the Wisconsin trial [[Farrell1997]]. US registry: median age at diagnosis 7 months in 1990 [[FitzSimmons1993]], now under 1 month [[McBennett2022]].

Newborn screening rests on a 1979 finding: immunoreactive trypsin(ogen) (IRT) is raised in babies with CF and can be measured on a dried blood spot [[Crossley1979]]. It was in every US state by 2011 and UK-wide from 2007 [[Rock2011,Schluter2020]], and about 10 million newborns are screened for CF each year worldwide [[Bell2020Commission]]. A positive screen is not a diagnosis: CF Foundation guidelines recommend that the diagnosis be established by a sweat chloride test [[Farrell2017Dx]]. In UK children born 2000–2015, screening was linked to higher lung function (+1.56 FEV1 points) and later chronic Pseudomonas, but it did not narrow early health inequalities [[Schluter2020]]. Screening and the sweat test are covered in Newborn screening & diagnosis.

Is CF contagious?

No.

CF is a genetic condition passed from parents to child; it is not an infection and cannot be caught [[Shteinberg2021,Ratjen2015]]. A child has CF only when both parents pass on a changed CFTR gene; when both parents are carriers, each pregnancy has a 1-in-4 chance of CF [[Shteinberg2021,Scotet2020]].

The germs that live in CF lungs, though, can pass between people with CF, which is why CF centres keep people with CF apart from each other [[McBennett2022]]. See The CFTR gene & inheritance and Infection, antibiotics & cross-infection.
