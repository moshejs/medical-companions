Infection, antibiotics & cross-infection

Germs settle in the thick mucus of CF airways, and one of them, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, shapes much of CF treatment. Follow it from the first culture to a chronic infection and an exacerbation, then meet the other organisms and the infection-control rules that keep people with CF apart.

Quoted as publishedDoses, label wording and infection-control rules below are quoted from the trials, US labels and the CF Foundation's 2013 infection prevention and control update. They are study material, not personal advice.

IV aminoglycosides: hearing and kidneys

Hearing

Ototoxicity is common when it is looked for

In 18 adults given once-daily IV tobramycin 10 mg/kg/day for ≥10 days, cochleotoxic audiometric change was found in 89% at 2 weeks and 93% at 4 weeks after treatment. Reported rates range from 0 to 56%, depending on methods [[Harruff2021Ototox]]. A prospective cohort found threshold shifts after a single course, and its authors argue for routine monitoring [[Garinis2021Ototox]].

Early evidence
Kidneys

Who is at risk of kidney injury

Risk factors for aminoglycoside-associated acute kidney injury in CF included an aminoglycoside in the prior 90 days, longer duration, low serum albumin and co-administered trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole [[Downes2015AKI]]. In TOPIC, once-daily tobramycin was as effective as three-times-daily (FEV1 +10.4% versus +10.0%) and less nephrotoxic in children [[Smyth2005TOPIC]].

Established

Exacerbations have become rarer. US registry rates fell from 70.6 to 14.7 (per the registry's rate unit) after the pandemic and ETI, and Canadian rates from 53.1 to 30.2; the authors estimated ETI's effect at 2.48 times the pandemic effect [[Stephenson2025PEx]].

Other organisms

Gram-negative rod

Pseudomonas aeruginosa

2.6-fold 8-year mortality risk in young children [[Emerson2002]]. Early eradication with 28 days of inhaled tobramycin (grade A) [[Mogayzel2014Pa]]; chronic suppression with cycled inhaled antibiotics [[FDA-TOBI,FDA-Cayston]].

Staphylococcus

MRSA

In US registry data (137,819 patient-years), mortality was 27.7 per 1,000 patient-years with methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus versus 18.3 without; adjusted HR 1.27 (95% CI 1.11 to 1.45) [[Dasenbrook2010]]. MRSA was also a risk factor for not recovering after an exacerbation [[Sanders2010]].

Burkholderia cepacia complex

B. cenocepacia and B. multivorans

At one UK centre, 31 of 33 people with initial B. cenocepacia became chronically infected, against 8 of 16 with B. multivorans; B. cenocepacia was linked to shorter survival than P. aeruginosa. Deaths from "cepacia syndrome" occurred with both [[Jones2004]]. Before transplant, Bcc infection meant higher 6-month mortality (33% versus 12%) [[Aris2001Bcc]].

Nontuberculous mycobacteria

NTM and M. abscessus

In 986 people aged ≥10 at 21 US centres, NTM prevalence was 13.0%: M. avium complex 72%, M. abscessus 16% [[Olivier2003]]. Whole-genome sequencing showed M. abscessus passing between people with CF despite cross-infection measures, and globally spreading clones linked to worse outcomes Established [[Bryant2013Lancet,Bryant2016]]. The exact route is still debated (fomites and aerosols) Hypothesis [[Bryant2016]].

Fungus

Aspergillus and ABPA

A meta-analysis of 64 studies put pooled allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis (ABPA) at 8.9% and Aspergillus sensitisation at 39.1%, with widely varying diagnostic criteria [[Maturu2015]]. The European registry found 7.8% [[Mastella2000]]; a CF Foundation consensus conference set out diagnostic criteria for CF [[Stevens2003]]. ABPA was a risk factor for not recovering after an exacerbation [[Sanders2010]].

On ETI

Stenotrophomonas, Achromobacter and others

On ETI, sputum densities of S. aureus, P. aeruginosa, Stenotrophomonas, Achromobacter and Burkholderia fell by 2–3 log10 CFU/mL after one month, but most people stayed culture-positive [[Nichols2023]].

Keeping people with CF apart: infection prevention and control

The CF Foundation's 2013 update starts from one principle: "all individuals with CF may harbor potentially transmissible pathogens", so the precautions apply to everyone with CF, whatever their culture results [[Saiman2014IPC]]. The guideline cites data that droplets can travel as far as 6 feet (2 metres) [[Saiman2014IPC]]. Move the second person, pick a setting, and press Cough.

    The 2013 rules predate CFTR modulators, and no updated CF Foundation infection-control guideline was validated for this module [[Saiman2014IPC,Nichols2023]]. People with CF follow their own centre's current policy.
