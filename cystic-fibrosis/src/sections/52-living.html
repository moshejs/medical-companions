Treatment burden and quality of life

The Cystic Fibrosis Questionnaire (teen and adult version, age 14 and older) has 44 items on 12 generic and CF-specific scales, and child and parent versions exist [[Quittner2005CFQ]]. Its revised respiratory scale (CFQ-R) is the usual quality-of-life outcome in trials. In the Sawicki study, the mean CFQ-R treatment-burden score was 52.3 out of 100 (higher is better); two or more nebulised medicines, and airway clearance of 30 minutes or more, were each linked to higher perceived burden [[Sawicki2009]].

Mental health

Screening, as published

Every year from age 12

"The ICMH recommends annual screening for depression and anxiety with the PHQ-9 and GAD-7 for adolescents and adults with CF (ages 12–adulthood)." The committee also recommends offering annual screening to at least one primary caregiver of children aged 0–17 (PHQ-9/GAD-7, PHQ-8/GAD-7 or PHQ-2/GAD-2, depending on resources). Its 15 recommendations each had 100% consensus [[Quittner2016ICMH]].

Stepped care, ages 12+

Matched to severity

  • Mild symptoms: education and rescreening
  • Moderate: CBT or interpersonal therapy (IPT)
  • Severe depression: psychological treatment plus antidepressants
  • Severe anxiety: exposure-based CBT

The ICMH names citalopram, escitalopram, sertraline and fluoxetine as appropriate first-line SSRIs for most people with CF aged 12 and older who need medication, with close monitoring for drug–drug interactions [[Quittner2016ICMH]]. Children aged 7–11 are evaluated when a caregiver's scores are raised or the child has significant symptoms, and psychological interventions come first [[Quittner2016ICMH]].

Why it matters

Mood, adherence and survival

The consensus lists worse adherence among the outcomes linked to psychological symptoms [[Quittner2016ICMH]]. Among 1005 US TIDES participants, 25% screened positive for depression; the adjusted 5-year hazard ratio for death was 1.6 in adults (95% CI 1.0–2.4) and 2.0 in the severe range. These are associations in a population, not predictions for any person [[Schechter2021Depression]].

Modulators and mood

What the label says

The Trikafta label (section 5.4) warns of "Serious neuropsychiatric events, including symptoms of anxiety, depression, suicidal ideation and behavior, and sleep disturbances", which "may occur within the first three months"; it advises assessing baseline symptoms and monitoring for new or worse ones [[FDA-Trikafta]]. The Alyftrek and Kalydeco labels carry the same class warning [[FDA-Alyftrek,FDA-Kalydeco]].

A case series described 6 people with "mental fogginess" in the first 3 months of ETI Early evidence [[Heo2022MentalStatus]], while a 48-week cohort found psychosocial health improved overall and anxiety unchanged, with women, people with lower lung function and those on psychotropic medicines possibly more vulnerable [[vanderHeijden2025]]. Whether modulators cause these symptoms is unresolved Early evidence.

SupportIf you feel foggy, sad or anxious, or have trouble sleeping after starting a CFTR modulator, tell your care team. Do not stop the medicine on your own [[FDA-Trikafta,Heo2022MentalStatus]]. If you ever feel unsafe or think about ending your life, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line now.

Everyday rules and choices

Cross-infection

Six feet apart

The 2013 CFF/SHEA guideline widened the recommended distance between people with CF from 3 to 6 feet [[Haggie2020,Saiman2014IPC]]. Haggie summarises its social advice: at least 2 m apart, no in-person meetings, and no shared facilities for children with CF at the same school [[Haggie2020,Saiman2014IPC]]. In hospital, people with CF should not share rooms, bathrooms or toilets [[Conway2014ECFS]].

Community

Meeting online

Of 138 US centres with indoor in-person events, 45% allowed one person with CF to attend and 51% allowed none; outdoors, 84% allowed more than one [[Saiman2018Survey]]. Because in-person contact is discouraged, online contact is a main route to peer community [[Haggie2020,Southern2024Standards]]. Only 53% of US programmes had adopted the inpatient 6-foot rule in writing [[Stoudemire2019IPC]].

Exercise

Fitness, not FEV1

A Cochrane review (24 trials, 875 people) found that activity programmes over 6 months probably improve exercise capacity a little (peak oxygen uptake MD 1.60), and may have no effect on FEV1 or the CFQ-R respiratory score Established [[Radtke2022Cochrane]]. The specialist physiotherapist leads airway clearance, exercise and inhalation therapy [[Conway2014ECFS]].

Travel

Flying with CF

In 79 adults, a pre-flight algorithm sorted people by resting and walking oxygen saturation; only 21 (27%) would have needed a hypoxia-altitude test. Like everything here it is a teaching example, not individual advice [[Akero2025AirTravel]].

Research priorities

What the community asked

The 2023 James Lind Alliance refresh drew on 1417 people with CF, family members and clinicians; in the second survey, 62% of respondents with CF were taking modulators [[Rowbotham2023]].

Palliative care

From diagnosis, not just at the end

The CFF consensus says CF teams should deliver primary palliative care "at the time of diagnosis, and throughout the disease course" [[Kavalieratos2021Palliative]]. Palliative care means extra support for comfort and quality of life, at any stage [[Kavalieratos2021Palliative]]. More in Advanced lung disease & transplant.
