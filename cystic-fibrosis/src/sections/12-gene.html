Part 1 · Understanding CF

The CFTR gene & inheritance

Cystic fibrosis comes from changes in one gene, CFTR, and a person needs two changed copies, one from each parent, to have it [[Deignan2020ACMG]]. Zoom from a cell down to the three missing DNA letters of F508del, then work out the odds for a family yourself.

From a cell to three missing letters

The gene was found in 1989, in three back-to-back papers in Science, by positional cloning: the teams worked along chromosome 7 without knowing in advance what protein they were looking for [[Rommens1989,Riordan1989,Kerem1989]]. Two copies, one from each parent

CF is autosomal recessive: disease needs disease-causing variants on both copies of CFTR [[Shteinberg2021,Deignan2020ACMG]]. The ACMG technical standard states that carriers of a single pathogenic or likely pathogenic variant are expected to be asymptomatic [[Deignan2020ACMG]]. When both parents are carriers, each pregnancy has a 1 in 4 (25%) chance of a child with two variants, a 2 in 4 (50%) chance of a carrier child and a 1 in 4 (25%) chance of a child with neither [[Deignan2020ACMG,Shteinberg2021]].

Chance has no memory

A 1 in 4 chance does not mean one child in every four. Each pregnancy starts fresh, with the same odds as the one before [[Deignan2020ACMG]]. Roll pregnancies for the parents chosen above and watch a real family's mix wander, while the long-run tally settles towards the square's odds.

In plain wordsBeing a carrier means having one changed copy of CFTR and one working copy. It is very common, it is nobody's fault, and carriers are usually healthy [[Deignan2020ACMG,Zeng2025Hetero]]. More than 10 million people in the US are estimated to carry one pathogenic CFTR variant [[Zeng2025Hetero,Miller2020PNAS]].

Carrier screening: what the guidance says

2001 · first of its kind

Universal CF screening

On joint ACMG and ACOG recommendation, CF became the first condition for which pan-ethnic universal carrier screening by molecular methods was recommended, with a 25-variant core panel [[Deignan2020ACMG]]. In 2004 two variants were dropped, leaving the "ACMG-23" panel [[Watson2004ACMG,Deignan2020ACMG]].

ACMG 2021

Ancestry-neutral, Tier 3

The ACMG recommends that all pregnant patients and those planning a pregnancy be offered Tier 3 carrier screening (conditions with a carrier frequency of at least 1 in 200), which includes CFTR. It does not recommend offering only Tier 1 or 2, "because these do not provide equitable evaluation of all racial/ethnic groups" [[Gregg2021ACMG]].

ACOG 2017

A choice, ideally before pregnancy

ACOG states that carrier screening information should be given to every pregnant woman, ideally before pregnancy, and that a patient may decline any or all screening. A carrier should be encouraged to tell relatives, the partner should be offered testing, and if both are carriers genetic counselling should be offered [[ACOG2017CO691]].

Testing a couple

One after the other, or together

The ACMG standard says couples can be tested one after the other (usually the female partner first, the male partner only if she carries a variant) or both at once, which may be preferred during pregnancy [[Deignan2020ACMG]].

Limits

A negative result is not zero

Screening looks for many variants but not all, so a negative result lowers the chance of being a carrier without making it zero [[Gregg2021ACMG,Palomaki2004]]. Carrier screening cannot detect a new (de novo) variant in a child and does not replace newborn screening [[Gregg2021ACMG]].

Gaps in panels

Who targeted panels miss

In the US registry, Hispanic, Black and Asian people with CF were less likely than white people to have two variants identified, and more likely to carry no variant on the 23-variant panel [[Schrijver2016]]. ACMG now also allows sequencing of all coding exons with reporting of every pathogenic and likely pathogenic variant [[Deignan2020ACMG]].

A family tree after a diagnosis

A child with CF has a variant on both copies of CFTR, one from each parent [[Deignan2020ACMG]], so both parents are usually carriers, and their relatives may be too. Toggle cascade testing to see who might be offered testing when a carrier tells their family, as ACOG encourages [[ACOG2017CO691]].

CFTR-related disorders

A CFTR-related disorder (CFTR-RD) is "a clinical entity associated with CFTR dysfunction that does not fulfil diagnostic criteria for CF". The recognised ones are congenital bilateral absence of the vas deferens (CBAVD), acute recurrent or chronic pancreatitis, and disseminated bronchiectasis [[Bombieri2011]].

Established

CBAVD (male infertility)

In 102 men with CBAVD, 19 had CFTR variants on both copies and 54 had one; 63% of those 54 had the 5T variant on the other copy [[Chillon1995]]. A meta-analysis found at least one CFTR variant in 78% of men with CBAVD (46% two, 28% one) [[Yu2012CBAVD]]. The commonest pattern is a CF-causing variant on one copy and 5T on the other [[Chillon1995]].

Established

Pancreatitis

In 134 people with chronic pancreatitis, 13.4% carried one CFTR mutation against 5.3% of 600 controls [[Sharer1998]]. In 27 people with idiopathic pancreatitis, 37% had at least one abnormal CFTR allele [[Cohn1998]].

Established

The 5T variant

About 10% of the general population carries 5T, a short run of T bases in intron 9 (legacy intron 8). Opposite a severe variant, 12 or 13 TG repeats beside 5T made an abnormal phenotype far more likely than 11 (OR 34.0) [[Groman2004,Deignan2020ACMG]].

Unresolved

Do carriers have health effects?

In a US claims database, 19,802 carriers had higher odds than matched controls for 57 of 59 CF-related conditions, though individual risk stayed low for most [[Miller2020PNAS]]. In the All of Us whole-genome cohort, no association reached phenome-wide significance; there were modest signals for ABPA, bronchiectasis and COPD [[Zeng2025Hetero]]. The designs differ, so the question is open.

Why is F508del so common? Founders and selection

Hypothesis

Very old: more than 52,000 years

Microsatellite haplotypes suggested in 1994 that F508del arose more than 52,000 years ago and spread through Europe in separate expansions [[Morral1994]].

Hypothesis

Bronze Age spread

A 2018 family study of 190 patients put the most recent common ancestor at 4,600 to 4,725 years ago in northwestern Europe and about 1,000 years in southeastern populations, proposing an early Bronze Age origin spread by Bell Beaker migrations [[Farrell2018Age]].

Hypothesis

A carrier advantage?

The high carrier frequency has prompted the idea that carriers once had a survival advantage. It remains unproven [[Farrell2018Age,Poolman2007]].

Hypothesis challenged

Cholera

Heterozygous CF mice secreted about half the normal fluid and chloride in response to cholera toxin [[Gabriel1994]], but in people, carriers secreted intestinal chloride at the same rate as non-carriers [[Hogenauer2000]].

Hypothesis

Typhoid and tuberculosis

Salmonella typhi uses CFTR to enter gut cells, and heterozygous F508del mice let 86% fewer bacteria through [[Pier1998]]. A population-genetic model found cholera and typhoid too weak to explain modern CF incidence, whereas tuberculosis from the 17th century could have been strong enough [[Poolman2007]].

Established

Founder effects

Some regional clusters trace to one ancestral chromosome: W1282X in Ashkenazi Jewish people [[Shoshani1992]] and the CFTRdele2,3(21kb) deletion in Slavic populations [[Dork2000]].

Exam hookCFTR: chromosome 7q31.2, 27 exons, a 1,480-amino-acid ABC-family protein that works as a chloride and bicarbonate channel [[Deignan2020ACMG,LopesPacheco2019]]. Two carrier parents: 25% CF, 50% carrier, 25% neither, in every pregnancy [[Deignan2020ACMG]].
