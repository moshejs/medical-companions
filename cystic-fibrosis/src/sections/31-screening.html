Part 3 · Screening, diagnosis & monitoring

Newborn screening & diagnosis

A few drops of blood from a baby's heel start a chain of tests: a protein called IRT, a search for CFTR gene variants, sometimes a read of the whole gene, and then a sweat test. Most babies who screen positive do not have CF. Follow one stream of newborns through each step and see where they leave it.

Schematic: the dots are not to scale and the IRT curve is a drawing, not data. Ranges and counts come from the 2025 CF Foundation guideline and the California programme. [[McGarry2025NBS,Kharrazi2015]]

Does finding CF early help?

The Wisconsin CF Neonatal Screening Project randomised 650,341 newborns between 1985 and 1994 to early diagnosis by screening or to standard diagnosis. Screening used IRT, with DNA testing for F508del added from 1991. [[Farrell2001,Farrell1997]] Over 13 years the screened group grew significantly better despite similar nutritional therapy, and the authors found that catch-up growth after a late diagnosis may not be possible. [[Farrell2001]]

Growth

Established benefit

A Cochrane review of two trials (1,124,483 neonates, 210 with CF): controls had odds ratios of 4.12 (95% CI 1.64–10.38) for weight below the 10th percentile and 4.62 (1.69–12.61) for height. Established [[Southern2009Cochrane]]

Lungs

Probable, confounded

The Wisconsin trial found no pulmonary benefit by school age; after age 10, screened children had worse chest radiograph scores linked to earlier P. aeruginosa. [[Farrell2003AJRCCM]] In UK registry data, screening went with a higher lung function trajectory (+1.56 FEV1 percentage points, 95% CI 0.1–3.02) and later chronic P. aeruginosa. Early evidence [[Schluter2020]]

Not a clean slate

Lung disease starts early

Of 57 Australian infants diagnosed by screening (median age 3.6 months), 84.2% had no respiratory symptoms, yet 21.1% had bacterial infection on lavage and 80.7% had an abnormal chest CT. [[Sly2009]]

The 2025 CF Foundation newborn screening guideline

Seven recommendations, each with 100% committee agreement, as the guideline lists them: [[McGarry2025NBS]]

#Recommendation
1A floating IRT cutoff rather than a fixed one.
2A very-high-IRT referral strategy when the variant panel does not cover all CFTR2 CF-causing variants or does not reach ≥95% sensitivity in all ancestral groups.
3No limiting variant detection to F508del or the ACMG-23 panel.
4Screening for all CF-causing variants in CFTR2.
5Variant screening at least twice weekly.
6A CFTR sequencing tier after IRT and the variant panel.
7Notifying both the primary care provider and the CF specialist of abnormal results.

Diagnostic criteria: the CF Foundation 2017 guideline

The 2017 guideline came from 32 experts in 9 countries, with ≥80% agreement required for each statement; 27 of 28 statements were approved. [[Farrell2017Dx,Farrell2017History]] Its full text was paywalled for our research pass, so what follows is taken from its abstract and companion papers and from an open-access review that quotes it; statement numbers are not given.

Testing CFTR function. It recommends that CF be diagnosed at all ages by testing CFTR function with a sweat chloride test, and that CFTR2 variant classifications be used to aid diagnosis. [[Farrell2017Dx]]

Sweat chloride bands, all ages including infants: ≥60 mmol/L diagnostic of CF; 30–59 mmol/L intermediate; <30 mmol/L negative (CF unlikely). [[Farrell2017Dx,DeCelieGermana2023]] The update lowered the lower edge of the intermediate band from 40 to 30 mmol/L. [[Sosnay2017Nonscreened]]

Screened babies. Even in babies with 2 CF-causing variants found by screening, the diagnosis must be confirmed by showing CFTR dysfunction; intestinal current measurement may be used when needed. [[Farrell2017Screened]]

Outside screening. Diagnosis rests on clinical evidence (symptoms, a family history or a positive screen) plus evidence of CFTR dysfunction: sweat chloride, CFTR genetics or CFTR physiological tests. Intermediate values on two occasions mean further CFTR testing. [[Sosnay2017Nonscreened,Farrell2008Dx]]

Terms. CRMS and CFSPID were merged and made equivalent: "CRMS/CFSPID". [[Farrell2017Dx,Ren2017CRMS]]

The sweat test itself, the chloride bands as an explainer and genetic testing:

CRMS/CFSPID: a positive screen, an inconclusive answer

Definitions

Two names, one group

CRMS (CF Foundation, 2009): high IRT on screening, sweat chloride <60 mmol/L, and up to 2 CFTR variants, at least 1 not clearly CF-causing. [[Borowitz2009CRMS]] CFSPID (ECFS): group A, normal sweat chloride and two variants, at least one of unclear consequence; group B, intermediate sweat chloride and one or no variants. [[Munck2015CFSPID]]

How common

3–5 CF cases for each

Where gene sequencing is not used there are about 3–5 cases of CF for every case of CRMS/CFSPID. [[Ren2017CRMS]] In US registry data for 2010–2012, 1540 infants met criteria for CF and 309 for CRMS (5.0 : 1.0); 40.8% of those with CRMS had been entered with a clinical diagnosis of CF. [[Ren2015Pediatrics]]

Outlook

Most stay healthy

9 of 82 infants with CFSPID (11%) were diagnosed with CF within 3 years in an 8-centre study. [[Ooi2015]] A 2025 meta-analysis cites conversion in about 5–11%. [[Wyatt2025]] The 2021 ECFS guidance recommends a detailed assessment in the sixth year of life and another as a young adult. [[Barben2021]]

Open question

Should screening aim to find it?

Some programmes favour fewer CRMS/CFSPID labels, given low conversion and family distress; others want to find every child at risk. Long-term outcomes beyond early childhood are unknown. [[Wyatt2025,Barben2021,Ren2017CRMS]]

When screening misses CF

FindingNumbers
US registry, 20203.8% of people with CF had a false-negative newborn screen and 11.8% a delayed diagnosis; Black, Hispanic and mixed-race people were over-represented. [[McGarry2023]]
Panels across ancestriesAt least one variant detected in 87.5–97.0% of non-Hispanic White people with CF and in 41.9–93.1% of Black, Asian and Hispanic people with CF, across seven panels. [[McGarry2023]]
California, 2007–202184 false negatives among 912 infants with CF: 38 from low IRT, 46 from incomplete variant detection. Asian (OR 6.3) and Black (OR 2.5) infants were more likely to be missed. [[McGarry2024]]
London and South East England4 years in, 10 screened children were missed and 6 of 34 infants with meconium ileus had a negative screen. [[Lim2014]] Low IRT has been reported in up to 18% of infants with meconium ileus in an Italian series. [[DeCelieGermana2023]]

Rock and colleagues listed 14 factors that can cause a missed diagnosis, across specimen collection, IRT and variant testing, communication and sweat testing, and advised suspecting CF whenever symptoms fit, whatever the screening result. [[Rock2011]]

Before birth: carrier screening

ACOG

Offer, do not impose

ACOG Committee Opinion 691 states that information about carrier screening should be offered to every pregnant woman, ideally before pregnancy; if both partners are carriers, genetic counselling should be offered; screening can be declined. [[ACOG2017CO691]]

ACMG

From 23 variants to 100

ACMG recommended a 23-variant core panel in 2001, revised in 2004; in 2023 it replaced it with a minimum of 100 variants for more equitable detection across US ancestral groups. [[Watson2004ACMG,Deignan2023,McGarry2025NBS]]

Ultrasound

Echogenic bowel

Over 16 years in Brittany, 289 fetuses with echogenic bowel were referred for CFTR analysis; CF was found in 7.6%, and ultrasound identified 10.7% of all CF cases in the region. [[Scotet2010]]
