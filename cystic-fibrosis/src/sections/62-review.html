Every card below is tagged memory aid: a short phrase written for this guide to hold a published fact in mind. Doses, thresholds and schedules are quoted as published, never as instructions.

Mnemonic in motion: the six variant classes

CFTR variants are grouped by what they do to the protein, from no protein at all (class I) to a channel that reaches the surface but does not stay there (class VI) [[LopesPacheco2019,Bell2020Commission]]. As a memory aid, walk the protein's life: make, fold, open, flow, amount, stay. Each word names the step that fails in that class. Play the animation to watch one channel fail in six different ways, with the chloride it lets out on a dial. More in .

Schematic, not to scale. The dial is a drawing proportion, not a measured value; many variants cause more than one defect. [[LopesPacheco2019,Bell2020Commission,Cutting2015]]

These cards are for learning, not for deciding anything about one person's care. Whether a medicine suits someone depends on their own variants, tests and history, and only their CF team can judge that. Survival and outcome figures on the cards are population statistics from registries and trials; they cannot predict what will happen to any one person.

Sources

Every reference behind the CF Companion: journal articles, randomised trials, registry studies, systematic reviews, CF Foundation, ECFS and other guidance, US drug labels and trial registrations. The small numbers throughout the app point here; filter by research area or search.

Research passes ran in parallel, one per area: the CFTR gene, inheritance and variants; CFTR physiology and the lungs; the digestive system, other organs and nutrition; newborn screening, diagnosis and monitoring; CFTR modulators and the research frontier; airway clearance, infection and transplant; and living with CF, fertility, pregnancy and nursing care. Before inclusion each source was checked, where possible, against PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, ClinicalTrials.gov or the regulator's label, and the green tag on an entry names the service that confirmed it. Works found by more than one pass appear once, with any extra keys listed beside them.

Entries marked %t:sources.check% are pages with no PMID or DOI, or works that could not be confirmed. They are not cited for any fact in this guide.
