Part 3 · Screening, diagnosis & monitoring

The sweat test & genetic testing

The sweat test has been the cornerstone of CF diagnosis since 1959: make a small patch of skin sweat, collect enough of it, and measure its chloride. Genetic testing then names the CFTR variants, and CFTR2 says which of them cause CF.

Step 1 · stimulate

Pilocarpine iontophoresis

In 1953 di Sant'Agnese and colleagues reported abnormal sweat electrolytes in CF. In 1959 Gibson and Cooke standardised the test: pilocarpine, a drug that makes sweat glands work, is carried into the skin of the forearm by iontophoresis, a small electric current between two electrodes. 103,167 For an asymptomatic screen-positive newborn the 2008 CF Foundation guideline recommends testing at ≥2 weeks of age, weight >2 kg and >36 weeks gestation at birth, ideally on both arms. 125

In plain wordsThe sweat test is painless. A small electric current carries a medicine into the skin of the arm to make it sweat. 125,167
Exam hookGibson–Cooke (1959): pilocarpine iontophoresis, then sweat electrolytes measured. 167

45 mmol/L falls in the 2017 band: 30–59, intermediate.
What the band means

An intermediate value (30–59 mmol/L) is not a diagnosis either way. People with symptoms, a family history or a positive screen whose values are intermediate on two separate occasions may have CF, and further CFTR testing is needed. 125,433

Exam hookThe 2017 update lowered the bottom of the intermediate band from 40 to 30 mmol/L, because registries follow many people with confirmed CF whose sweat chloride is 30–40. 433

Doing the test well

When, in a screened newbornAt ≥2 weeks of age, weight >2 kg and >36 weeks gestation at birth, ideally bilaterally; symptomatic newborns (e.g. meconium ileus) from 48 hours if enough sweat is collected. 125 A 2023 review summarises CF Foundation practice as testing after a positive screen by 4 weeks of age and not before 48 hours. 94
How much sweat"At least 75 mg of sweat obtained by the Gibson-Cooke method or at least 15 μL obtained by the Macroduct coil collection method". 125
QualityQNS rate below 5% for infants over 3 months; detect chloride down to 10 mmol/L; never pool samples from two sites. 125 Mean QNS in infants ≤3 months: 7.2% (±7.6), 0–40% across centres, whatever the collection method. 254
What is measuredSweat chloride is "the only analyte on which a diagnosis of CF should be based"; conductivity or osmolality "should not be used for diagnosing CF at the present time". 125 Conductivity is a screening test: false-positive rate 16.7% at a >50 mmol/L cutoff, best cutoff 69 mmol/L. 105

Genetic testing

CFTR2

Which variants cause CF

CFTR2 collected genotype and phenotype data on 39,696 people with CF. Of 159 variants with allele frequency ≥0.01%, 127 (80%) met clinical and functional criteria for causing disease. 431 As of September 2024 it had interpreted 1167 variants and deemed 1085 CF-causing: these are expected to cause CF when in trans with another CF-causing variant. 285

Testing

Panels, then the whole gene

ACMG updated its CFTR testing standard in 2020, noting that expanded panels and sequencing had become feasible. 95 A laboratory that sequences but reports only a pre-set list is still running a "variant panel". Sequencing misses structural and deep intronic variants unless they are targeted. 285

Interpretation

Genes are evidence, not the verdict

Two CF-causing variants in trans count as evidence of CFTR dysfunction; variants of variable or unknown consequence do not. 432 Even then, a screened baby's diagnosis is confirmed by showing CFTR dysfunction. 130

Try it: reading two CFTR results

A teaching tool, not genetic adviceChoose the category of each variant to see what the published guidance says about that combination. Real reports are interpreted by genetics and CF teams, with genetic counselling for families. 285,432

At least one variant of variable or unknown consequence
What the guidance says

Variants with variable or unknown consequence are not evidence of CFTR dysfunction, and other CFTR function tests may then help. 432 With a positive screen, a sweat chloride below 60 mmol/L and up to two variants, at least one not clearly CF-causing, this is the CRMS/CFSPID picture. 37,312 Variants not yet interpreted by CFTR2 are classified with the ACMG criteria; pathogenic and likely pathogenic ones prompt the same follow-up as CF-causing variants. 285

Sweat chloride on CFTR modulators

Modulators lower sweat chloride, a sign the CFTR channel is working better. 322 In PROMISE, 487 people aged 12 and over starting elexacaftor–tezacaftor–ivacaftor (ETI) by prescription saw sweat chloride fall by 41.7 mmol/L (95% CI −43.8 to −39.6) at 6 months. 322
