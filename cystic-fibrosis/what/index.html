Part 1 · Understanding CF

What cystic fibrosis is

Cystic fibrosis (CF) is an inherited condition in which one faulty channel protein, CFTR, leaves mucus and other secretions too thick in the lungs, pancreas and several other organs. Scrub through seven decades of survival figures, see how common CF is around the world, and tour the organs it touches.

CF is a monogenic, autosomal recessive disease "considered to affect at least 100 000 people worldwide" 420. Changes (variants) in the CFTR gene damage an epithelial channel that normally carries chloride and bicarbonate, so mucus is poorly hydrated and poorly cleared 420. Classical CF means chronic lung infection and inflammation, pancreatic exocrine insufficiency and male infertility, often with CF-related diabetes or CF liver disease alongside Established 420. Lung disease causes "the vast majority" of illness and deaths 364.

Some variants leave part of the protein working. These can cause milder disease that is harder to diagnose and sometimes affects only one organ 420. CF was long described as the most common life-threatening inherited disorder of children of European descent; it is now also a disease of adults 410.

1938 to 1954

A disease of infancy

When Dorothy Andersen described CF in 1938, life expectancy was about 6 months 89, and babies usually died in their first year 410. By 1954 the median survival age was estimated at 4–5 years 278. There was no registry yet, so there is no figure for the share of adults.

In plain wordsIn the 1950s, most children with CF died very young 278.
Exam hook1938: Andersen describes "cystic fibrosis of the pancreas" and separates it from coeliac disease 9,89.

How survival is measured

Not a survival measure

Median age at death

Describes only the people who died in a year. It ignores everyone still alive, so it is not a survival measure 410. In 2016–2018 registry reports it ranged from 29.0 years (European registry) to 35.6 years (Australia) 410.

What registries report

Predicted median survival

Applies this year's death rates at each age to an imaginary group of babies born today: the age beyond which half would be expected to live if death rates never changed 230,410. Because death rates keep falling, it tends to understate the future.

For people already grown up

Conditional survival

The expected median survival for people who have already reached a given age, such as 30 230. In UK data for F508del homozygotes, median survival was 46 (males) and 41 (females) at birth, but 52 and 49 for those who had reached 30 229.

Handle with care

Why countries differ

Registries use different methods and terms, which has confused patients and clinicians 230. Including people with mild or borderline disease raises apparent survival 278, and direct comparison between national registries is hard 445.

Who does less well. In 1988–1992, US females aged 1–20 were 60% more likely to die than males (RR 1.6), even after adjustment for nutrition, lung function and other factors 379. One Canadian analysis links this gap to a higher risk of death in women with CF-related diabetes 444, and US death-certificate data for 1999–2024 show equal age-adjusted rates by sex; whether the gap is closing is unresolved Early evidence 187. Poverty matters too: US children on Medicaid had a 3.65-fold adjusted risk of death 401, and UK children from the most deprived areas had FEV1 4.12 points lower, gaps that appeared early and did not widen 469. Hispanic patients in the US had a 1.27-fold adjusted death rate 371.

How common CF is, around the world

Incidence was traditionally quoted as 1 in 2,500 births in people of European descent; newborn screening now suggests 1 in 3,000 to 1 in 6,000, matching carrier rates of 1 in 28 to 1 in 40 410. The bars turn each "1 in N births" figure into CF births per 100,000 on a log scale, so very rare and very common populations fit on one chart. Choose a region.

Figures: Scotet 2020 compilation 410, US by ancestry 195, Brazil 23, Ireland's screening programme 286. Founder populations, descended from a small group of ancestors, have the highest rates.

About 162,428 people (range 144,606–186,620) are estimated to live with CF across 94 countries; about 105,352 (65%) are diagnosed, and estimates of undiagnosed CF in low- and middle-income countries are highly uncertain 188. Common screening panels miss 25% to over 70% of disease-causing variants in non-European groups Early evidence 18. Incidence is falling in most countries studied, partly after prenatal diagnosis and carrier screening (Israel's fell about 60% over 22 years), but not everywhere: Colorado and Wisconsin saw no decline 410.

The organs involved

CFTR works in the lining cells (epithelia) of many organs, so CF is a whole-body condition 115. Pick an organ.

Organ

Lungs: bronchiectasis and infection

The Lancet seminar lists bronchiectasis and small-airway obstruction 115. Lung disease causes "the vast majority" of illness and deaths 364, and the classic features include chronic infection, pneumothorax, haemoptysis (coughing blood) and respiratory failure 23.

In plain wordsThe changed gene makes mucus thick and sticky. It clogs the lungs, the pancreas and other organs 115.

Before newborn screening, diagnosis was often late. In the Wisconsin randomised trial of 650,341 newborns (1985–1994), the screened babies grew better throughout childhood, and severe malnutrition after a delayed diagnosis may not be reversible 121,122. Compare the two groups.

Mean ages at diagnosis in the Wisconsin trial 121. US registry: median age at diagnosis 7 months in 1990 146, now under 1 month 278.

Newborn screening rests on a 1979 finding: immunoreactive trypsin(ogen) (IRT) is raised in babies with CF and can be measured on a dried blood spot 78. It was in every US state by 2011 and UK-wide from 2007 375,403, and about 10 million newborns are screened for CF each year worldwide 23. A positive screen is not a diagnosis: CF Foundation guidelines recommend that the diagnosis be established by a sweat chloride test 128. In UK children born 2000–2015, screening was linked to higher lung function (+1.56 FEV1 points) and later chronic Pseudomonas, but it did not narrow early health inequalities 403. Screening and the sweat test are covered in Newborn screening & diagnosis.

Is CF contagious?

No.

CF is a genetic condition passed from parents to child; it is not an infection and cannot be caught 364,420. A child has CF only when both parents pass on a changed CFTR gene; when both parents are carriers, each pregnancy has a 1-in-4 chance of CF 410,420.

The germs that live in CF lungs, though, can pass between people with CF, which is why CF centres keep people with CF apart from each other 278. See The CFTR gene & inheritance and Infection, antibiotics & cross-infection.
