Part 4 · Treatment

Infection, antibiotics & cross-infection

Germs settle in the thick mucus of CF airways, and one of them, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, shapes much of CF treatment. Follow it from the first culture to a chronic infection and an exacerbation, then meet the other organisms and the infection-control rules that keep people with CF apart.

Quoted as publishedDoses, label wording and infection-control rules below are quoted from the trials, US labels and the CF Foundation's 2013 infection prevention and control update. They are study material, not personal advice.

Stage 1 · first growth

Pseudomonas arrives

In 3,323 US children aged 1–5, Pseudomonas aeruginosa in respiratory cultures in 1990 carried a 2.6-fold higher 8-year risk of death (95% CI 1.6 to 4.1) 117. The CFF 2014 guideline recommends against prophylactic antipseudomonal antibiotics to prevent acquisition (grade D), and recommends oropharyngeal cultures rather than routine bronchoscopy for people who cannot cough up sputum (grade B) 301.

In plain wordsPseudomonas is a germ that likes the thick mucus in CF airways. Finding it early matters, because the first infection can often still be cleared 363.
Exam hookP. aeruginosa in early childhood: 2.6× 8-year mortality risk 117. Prophylaxis to prevent it: grade D 301.

IV aminoglycosides: hearing and kidneys

Hearing

Ototoxicity is common when it is looked for

In 18 adults given once-daily IV tobramycin 10 mg/kg/day for ≥10 days, cochleotoxic audiometric change was found in 89% at 2 weeks and 93% at 4 weeks after treatment. Reported rates range from 0 to 56%, depending on methods 199. A prospective cohort found threshold shifts after a single course, and its authors argue for routine monitoring 161.

Early evidence
Kidneys

Who is at risk of kidney injury

Risk factors for aminoglycoside-associated acute kidney injury in CF included an aminoglycoside in the prior 90 days, longer duration, low serum albumin and co-administered trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 109. In TOPIC, once-daily tobramycin was as effective as three-times-daily (FEV1 +10.4% versus +10.0%) and less nephrotoxic in children 428.

Established

Exacerbations have become rarer. US registry rates fell from 70.6 to 14.7 (per the registry's rate unit) after the pandemic and ETI, and Canadian rates from 53.1 to 30.2; the authors estimated ETI's effect at 2.48 times the pandemic effect 447.

Other organisms

Gram-negative rod

Pseudomonas aeruginosa

2.6-fold 8-year mortality risk in young children 117. Early eradication with 28 days of inhaled tobramycin (grade A) 301; chronic suppression with cycled inhaled antibiotics 133,139.

Staphylococcus

MRSA

In US registry data (137,819 patient-years), mortality was 27.7 per 1,000 patient-years with methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus versus 18.3 without; adjusted HR 1.27 (95% CI 1.11 to 1.45) 85. MRSA was also a risk factor for not recovering after an exacerbation 398.

Burkholderia cepacia complex

B. cenocepacia and B. multivorans

At one UK centre, 31 of 33 people with initial B. cenocepacia became chronically infected, against 8 of 16 with B. multivorans; B. cenocepacia was linked to shorter survival than P. aeruginosa. Deaths from "cepacia syndrome" occurred with both 220. Before transplant, Bcc infection meant higher 6-month mortality (33% versus 12%) 12.

Nontuberculous mycobacteria

NTM and M. abscessus

In 986 people aged ≥10 at 21 US centres, NTM prevalence was 13.0%: M. avium complex 72%, M. abscessus 16% 327. Whole-genome sequencing showed M. abscessus passing between people with CF despite cross-infection measures, and globally spreading clones linked to worse outcomes Established 41,42. The exact route is still debated (fomites and aerosols) Hypothesis 42.

Fungus

Aspergillus and ABPA

A meta-analysis of 64 studies put pooled allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis (ABPA) at 8.9% and Aspergillus sensitisation at 39.1%, with widely varying diagnostic criteria 275. The European registry found 7.8% 273; a CF Foundation consensus conference set out diagnostic criteria for CF 448. ABPA was a risk factor for not recovering after an exacerbation 398.

On ETI

Stenotrophomonas, Achromobacter and others

On ETI, sputum densities of S. aureus, P. aeruginosa, Stenotrophomonas, Achromobacter and Burkholderia fell by 2–3 log10 CFU/mL after one month, but most people stayed culture-positive 323.

Keeping people with CF apart: infection prevention and control

The CF Foundation's 2013 update starts from one principle: "all individuals with CF may harbor potentially transmissible pathogens", so the precautions apply to everyone with CF, whatever their culture results 392. The guideline cites data that droplets can travel as far as 6 feet (2 metres) 392. Move the second person, pick a setting, and press Cough.

1.2 m (3.9 ft)
  • "Separate all people with CF from others with CF, regardless of their respiratory tract culture results, at least 6 feet (2 meters) in all settings" 392.
  • The guideline recommends against CF-specific camps and retreats, and encourages camps and sports with people who do not have CF 392.

The 2013 rules predate CFTR modulators, and no updated CF Foundation infection-control guideline was validated for this module 323,392. People with CF follow their own centre's current policy.
