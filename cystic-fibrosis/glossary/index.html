An animated, cited guide for students, people with cystic fibrosis (CF), and their families and caregivers. Educational only, not medical advice. Your place is saved on this device.

Not medical adviceThe CF Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. It does not replace a doctor, nurse, physiotherapist, dietitian, pharmacist, genetic counsellor or the rest of a CF care team. Sweat-chloride bands, FEV1 percent predicted, genotype and eligibility tables and the carrier calculator are teaching aids, never for diagnosis or treatment decisions. Doses, airway-clearance routines, enzyme dosing and infection-control guidance are quoted as published, never as instructions. Survival and life-expectancy figures are registry population statistics that are changing quickly with CFTR modulators; they are not predictions for any person.

Glossary

140 of 140 terms

ABC transporterThe ATP-binding cassette family of membrane proteins. CFTR belongs to it but works as an anion channel rather than a pump 80.
ABPA (allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis)An allergic reaction to Aspergillus in the airways. Pooled prevalence in CF 8.9%, with Aspergillus sensitisation in 39.1% 275; a CF Foundation conference set its diagnostic criteria 448.
AdherenceHow much of a prescribed treatment is actually taken. For nebulisers, device downloads showed 36% against 80% by self-report 84; mean adherence to pulmonary medicines was 48% in US claims data 351.
Advanced CF liver disease (aCFLD)Nodular liver, advanced fibrosis, multilobular cirrhosis or non-cirrhotic portal hypertension, as defined by the CF Foundation 2024 guideline 411.
Airway clearance techniques (ACTs)Methods for moving mucus out of the lungs, such as PEP, oscillating devices, the vest and postural drainage. Recommended regularly for everyone; no technique has been shown superior 152,501.
Airway surface liquid (ASL)The thin liquid lining the airways: a mucus layer on top of the periciliary layer, kept at about 7 µm by ion transport 461.
Alyftrek (vanzacaftor–tezacaftor–deutivacaftor)A once-daily triple modulator approved on 20 December 2024 for ages 6 and older, with a boxed liver warning 132. Non-inferior to ETI for ppFEV1 in SKYLINE 226.
AmilorideA blocker of the epithelial sodium channel (ENaC). It abolishes the benefit of hypertonic saline 106, and the nasal potential difference in CF shows a large amiloride response 240.
AminoglycosidesAntibiotics such as tobramycin and amikacin. Once-daily IV dosing works as well as thrice daily, with less kidney toxicity in children 28,428; repeated courses can harm hearing 7.
Annual reviewA yearly assessment including at minimum a full medical, dietetic, physiotherapy and psychosocial review and surveillance blood tests (ECFS) 74.
AREST CFAn Australian study following infants diagnosed by screening with CT and lavage; bronchiectasis rose from 29.3% at 3 months to 61.5% at 3 years 426.
ArthropathyJoint disease linked to CF: 4.9% in the German registry, 8.4% of adults and 0.7% of under-18s 184.
AtalurenA read-through drug meant to let ribosomes skip premature stop codons. It failed two phase 3 trials in nonsense-variant CF 235,245.
Autosomal recessiveInheritance that needs a disease-causing variant on both copies of a gene. Two carrier parents have a 25% chance of an affected child in each pregnancy 95.
AzithromycinA macrolide antibiotic used 3 days a week for its anti-inflammatory effect; HR for exacerbation 0.65 in Saiman 2003 391. NTM must be considered before starting 149.
Aztreonam lysine (Cayston)An inhaled antibiotic for P. aeruginosa, labelled three times daily for a 28-day course followed by 28 days off 133; it improved CFQ-R respiratory score by 9.7 points in AIR-CF1 370.
Base editingA gene-editing method that changes a single DNA letter without cutting both strands. About 20% of people in an organoid biobank had variants it could in theory correct (Early evidence) 165.
Bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻)An anion CFTR carries about one-quarter as well as chloride 342. Variants linked to pancreatic insufficiency did not support bicarbonate transport 65.
BiofilmA community of bacteria living in a slime matrix. Sputum signals in CF match P. aeruginosa grown as biofilm, which helps explain why infection persists 423.
BMI goalsCF Foundation targets: BMI at or above the 50th percentile for ages 2–20; at least 22 kg/m² for women and 23 for men 306,441.
BronchiectasisPermanent widening of the airways from infection and inflammation. In CF it can be seen in infants as young as 10 weeks 426.
Burkholderia cepacia complexA group of bacteria that can spread between people with CF. B. cenocepacia is linked to worse survival and higher post-transplant mortality 12,220.
CarrierSomeone with one disease-causing CFTR variant and one working copy, expected to be asymptomatic 95.
Carrier screeningTesting to find carriers before or during pregnancy. ACMG 2021 recommends Tier 3 screening, which includes CFTR, for all pregnant patients and those planning pregnancy 183; anyone may decline 2.
CataractsClouding of the lens. Labels of ivacaftor-containing drugs recommend eye examinations at baseline and follow-up in children 140.
CBAVDCongenital bilateral absence of the vas deferens: the cause of infertility in about 97–98% of men with CF 221. It is also a CFTR-related disorder in men without CF 35.
Cepacia syndromeA rapid, often fatal decline with Burkholderia infection; deaths occurred with both B. cenocepacia and B. multivorans 220.
CF liver disease (CFLD)Liver involvement in CF. About 5–10% develop multilobular cirrhosis, usually in the first decade 93; routine ursodeoxycholic acid is not recommended to prevent advanced disease 411.
CF nurse specialistA core member of the CF team who teaches, advocates and supports at key moments: diagnosis, first IV course, transition, pregnancy, transplant and end of life 74.
CF-related diabetes (CFRD)A distinct type of diabetes, different from type 1 and type 2 227. It affects about 2% of children, 19% of adolescents and 40–50% of adults 305; insulin is the recommended treatment 306.
CFQ-RThe Cystic Fibrosis Questionnaire–Revised, a quality-of-life measure 349. Its respiratory MCID is 4 points in stable disease 350.
CFTRCystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator: the gene on chromosome 7 and the anion channel it encodes 95,261.
CFTR2A project collecting genotype and phenotype data to classify CFTR variants as CF-causing, varying clinical consequence or non-CF-causing 95,431.
CFTR-related disorder (CFTR-RD)CFTR dysfunction that does not meet CF criteria: CBAVD, pancreatitis or disseminated bronchiectasis 35.
Chloride (Cl⁻)The main anion CFTR conducts. Changing pore residues changed its selectivity, which proved CFTR is itself the channel 11.
Chronic rhinosinusitisLong-lasting inflammation of the sinuses, highly prevalent in CF 194. Nasal polyps were seen in 37% of adults on endoscopy 191.
Colonoscopy screeningCF Foundation 2018: colonoscopy from age 40, or from 30 within 2 years of transplant 192.
ConstipationGradual faecal impaction of the whole colon 213; it affects 26–47% of children with CF over their lifetime 478.
Contact precautionsGown and gloves for staff with all people with CF, in clinic and hospital, as published in the 2013 guideline 392.
CorrectorA modulator that helps CFTR fold and traffic to the cell surface, such as lumacaftor, tezacaftor and elexacaftor 140,144.
CRMS/CFSPIDCFTR-related metabolic syndrome / CF screen positive, inconclusive diagnosis: a positive newborn screen with inconclusive sweat and genetic tests. Most children stay well 20,369.
Cross-infectionSpread of germs between people with CF. The guideline asks for at least 6 feet (2 m) between them in all settings 392.
CYP3ALiver enzymes that break down all three ETI components; strong inducers such as rifampin cut ivacaftor exposure by 89% 140.
DeutivacaftorA once-daily potentiator, the third component of Alyftrek 132.
DIOSDistal intestinal obstruction syndrome: an acute complete or incomplete faecal obstruction in the ileocaecum 213. Lifetime prevalence about 8% in children and 16% in adults 478.
Dornase alfa (rhDNase)An inhaled enzyme that cleaves extracellular DNA in sputum. Once-daily use cut exacerbations by 28% 158; the label dose is 2.5 mg once daily 137.
ElexacaftorA next-generation corrector with co-potentiator activity, the "E" in ETI 145,487.
ENaCThe epithelial sodium channel. βENaC-overexpressing mice develop CF-like lung disease 266, but newborn CF pigs showed no extra sodium absorption 60; the debate is open.
Enteral tube feedingFeeding through a tube into the gut, recommended after oral supplements when growth stays impaired 407,475.
ETI (elexacaftor–tezacaftor–ivacaftor, Trikafta)The triple modulator first approved on 21 October 2019 141. In F508del/minimal function, ppFEV1 rose 14.3 points against placebo 295.
Exacerbation (pulmonary)A flare of lung symptoms, often treated with antibiotics. About 1 in 4 IV-treated exacerbations ends without recovery to baseline FEV1 398.
F508delThe commonest CF variant: a 3-bp deletion that removes phenylalanine 508 in NBD1, on about 70% of CF alleles 231,307.
Faecal elastase-1A stool test of pancreatic function: below 200 µg/g supports insufficiency, below 100 µg/g is severe 36,182.
Fat-soluble vitaminsVitamins A, D, E and K, poorly absorbed with pancreatic insufficiency; CF-specific multivitamins start soon after diagnosis, with levels checked yearly 36.
FEV1 (ppFEV1)Forced expiratory volume in 1 second, usually as percent predicted (ppFEV1): the established marker of CF lung disease and the key trial endpoint 457.
Fibrosing colonopathyScarring and narrowing of the colon linked to very high enzyme doses (above 50,000 lipase units/kg/day, RR 199.5) 147.
Founder effectA variant common in one population because it traces back to a shared ancestor, such as W1282X in Ashkenazi Jewish people 419 or CFTRdele2,3 in Slavic populations 108.
FRT assayA test in Fischer rat thyroid cells. A rise in chloride transport of at least 10% of normal makes a variant "responsive" on the labels 135.
GatingThe opening and closing of the CFTR pore. Class III variants such as G551D reach the membrane but do not open properly 23.
Gene therapyDelivering a working CFTR gene to airway cells. A non-viral nebulised product gave a 3.7% relative ppFEV1 benefit (Early evidence) 8; AAV products are in early trials 316.
Genetic counsellingAdvice on inheritance and options. ACOG says it should be offered when both partners are carriers 2.
Gestational diabetesDiabetes first found in pregnancy: 10–36% of pregnancies in women with CF 302. OGTT at 12–16 and 24–28 weeks is recommended 306.
GLI equationsGlobal Lung Function Initiative reference equations for spirometry 344. The ATS now recommends the race-neutral GLI Global 27.
HaemoptysisCoughing up blood. Massive haemoptysis affects about 0.87% of people with CF each year 150.
Hand hygieneThe single most important infection-control practice, before and after all patient contact 193,392.
Heterozygote advantageThe idea that carriers once survived infections better (cholera, typhoid, tuberculosis). It remains unproven (Hypothesis) 131,341.
HGVS nomenclatureThe standard way to name variants, using cDNA numbering: F508del is c.1521_1523delCTT (p.Phe508del) 95,100.
Hypertonic salineInhaled 7% salt water that rehydrates the airway surface osmotically 106. It cut exacerbations by 56% from age 6 116.
Ibuprofen (high-dose)An anti-inflammatory that slowed FEV1 decline in a 4-year trial (−2.17% versus −3.60% a year) 242.
ICSIIntracytoplasmic sperm injection: with sperm retrieval it lets most men with CF become biological fathers 224.
Infection prevention and control (IP&C)The CF Foundation 2013 guideline: masks in healthcare settings, contact precautions, single rooms and the 6-foot rule, applied to everyone with CF 392.
Intermediate sweat chloride30–59 mmol/L since 2017; CFTR testing then continues 128,433.
Intestinal current measurement (ICM)A test of CFTR function on rectal biopsies, used when the diagnosis is unclear 101.
IPOSThe Integrated Palliative Care Outcome Scale, recommended yearly from age 12 to screen for unmet palliative-care needs 222.
IRT (immunoreactive trypsinogen)A pancreatic protein measured on the newborn blood spot. High in CF but not specific 285; first used for screening in 1979 78.
Ivacaftor (Kalydeco)The first potentiator; it raises CFTR open probability 135,481. Labelled from age 1 month for responsive variants 135.
Kidney stonesMore common in CF; one US adult cohort found a lifetime prevalence of 32% 389.
Leeds criteriaChronic P. aeruginosa means more than 50% of the previous 12 months' cultures positive 253.
LumacaftorA type I corrector, combined with ivacaftor as Orkambi for F508del homozygotes 136,482.
Lung clearance index (LCI)A multiple-breath washout measure of uneven ventilation that detects early disease when FEV1 is normal 15,374.
Lung transplantReplacement of both lungs for advanced disease. Median survival after transplant for adults with CF is 9.5 years (ISHLT) 357.
MacroductA coil device for collecting sweat; at least 15 µL is needed 125.
Mannitol (inhaled)A dry-powder osmotic agent that raised FEV1 by about 93 mL over control 30.
Meconium ileusBowel blockage at birth by thick meconium, in about 1 in 5 newborns with CF 399,478.
Minimal functionGenotype group with little or no CFTR function (classes I and II) 261.
Modifier genesGenes other than CFTR that shape disease; five lung-disease loci were found in 6,365 people 77.
MRSAMethicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus, rising from 0.1% to 17.2% of US people with CF between 1995 and 2005 366, with higher mortality (aHR 1.27) 85.
mRNA therapyDelivering CFTR instructions as mRNA. Inhaled MRT5005 left FEV1 stable but showed no benefit (Early evidence) 387.
MucinsThe gel-forming proteins of mucus. CF sputum mucins are hyperconcentrated, with high osmotic pressure 206.
Mucociliary clearanceCilia sweeping mucus out of the airways; it fails when the periciliary layer collapses 52,274.
Mucoid P. aeruginosaThe slimy, alginate-making form of Pseudomonas, acquired at a median age of 13 in one cohort 255.
Multidisciplinary team (MDT)Consultants, nurse specialist, microbiologist, physiotherapist, dietitian, pharmacist, psychologist, social worker and geneticist (ECFS) 74.
Mycobacterium abscessusA fast-growing NTM that can pass between people with CF, shown by whole-genome sequencing 41,42.
Nasal potential difference (NPD)A voltage measured on the nasal lining; in CF a high baseline, a large amiloride response and little chloride response 125,240.
Neuropsychiatric eventsA modulator label warning for anxiety, depression, suicidal thoughts and sleep problems, which may begin within the first 3 months 140.
Neutrophil elastaseAn enzyme released by neutrophils; in infant BAL it predicted bronchiectasis (OR 3.02) 426.
Newborn screeningTesting every baby's heel-prick blood spot; universal in the UK since 2007 and the US since 2010 285,403.
Nonsense variantA change creating a premature stop codon, so no full protein is made (class I). About 10% of people with CF carry one 235,261.
NTM (nontuberculous mycobacteria)Environmental mycobacteria found in about 13% of people with CF aged 10 or older 327; annual cultures are advised in people who expectorate 149.
Nucleotide-binding domains (NBD1, NBD2)The two parts of CFTR that bind ATP and drive gating; F508 sits in NBD1 80,373.
OGTTOral glucose tolerance test: a 2-hour 75-g test, yearly from age 10 to screen for CFRD 306.
OrganoidsMini-guts grown from rectal biopsies; forskolin-induced swelling measures CFTR function and drug response (Early evidence) 97,98.
OsteoporosisLow bone density; pooled prevalence 23.5% in adults with CF 333.
Palliative careCare for comfort and quality of life, recommended from diagnosis throughout the disease course 222.
Pancreatic insufficiency (PI)Too few digestive enzymes reach the gut; about 85% of people with CF 424.
Pancreatic sufficiency (PS)Enough pancreatic function to digest food without enzymes; linked to one or two mild alleles such as R117H 248.
PancreatitisInflammation of the pancreas, seen in pancreatic-sufficient people with CF (22% in one study) 328.
PEP (positive expiratory pressure)Breathing out against resistance to move mucus; PEP mask beat the vest on exacerbations in a 1-year trial 287.
PERTPancreatic enzyme replacement therapy, taken with meals; the Creon label sets a ceiling of 2,500 lipase units/kg/meal 134.
Periciliary layer (PCL)The liquid layer around the cilia, about 7 µm deep, that lets them beat 461.
PHQ-9 and GAD-7Questionnaires for depression and anxiety, recommended yearly from age 12 353.
Pilocarpine iontophoresisUsing a small current to drive pilocarpine into the skin to make it sweat for the test, standardised in 1959 167.
PneumothoraxCollapsed lung from air leaking into the chest, in about 0.64% of people with CF each year 151.
PolyT/TG tract (5T)Repeats in intron 9 that change CFTR splicing. 5T with 12 or 13 TG repeats carries more risk than with 11 95,185.
PotentiatorA modulator that raises the open probability of CFTR already at the cell surface, such as ivacaftor 135.
Prime editingA precise gene-editing method that corrected F508del in patient airway cells in the laboratory (Early evidence) 435.
PROMISEA US study of 487 people starting ETI: ppFEV1 +9.76 points and sweat chloride −41.7 mmol/L at 6 months 322.
Pseudo-Bartter syndromeHypochloraemic, hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis from sweat salt loss 269.
Pseudomonas aeruginosaThe classic CF airway bacterium; in young children a positive culture carried 2.6 times the 8-year risk of death 117.
Pulmonary ionocyteA rare FOXI1-positive airway cell that makes most of the airway's CFTR transcripts 303,340.
R domainThe regulatory domain of CFTR. PKA phosphorylation of it is needed before the channel can open 61,80.
Read-throughA drug approach to skip premature stop codons; ataluren failed and ELX-02 has no posted results (Hypothesis) 235,314.
Residual functionGenotype group keeping some CFTR function (classes IV, V, VI) 261.
Salt supplementationInfants with CF need extra salt: the CF Foundation infant guideline recommends 1/8 teaspoon a day from diagnosis, 1/4 from 6 months, as published 36.
SIMPLIFYTrials in which people on ETI with good lung function stopped hypertonic saline or dornase for 6 weeks without losing ppFEV1 277.
SKYLINETwo phase 3 trials showing once-daily VTD non-inferior to ETI for ppFEV1 226.
Staphylococcus aureusOften the first CF airway pathogen in childhood; MSSA prevalence rose from 37.0% to 52.4% (1995–2005) 366.
STRIVEThe pivotal ivacaftor trial in G551D: ppFEV1 +10.6 points, sweat chloride −48.1 mmol/L 360.
Sweat chloride testThe diagnostic test for CF: 60 mmol/L or more is diagnostic 128.
Sweat ductThe tube that carries sweat to the skin and normally takes salt back; in CF it cannot 346.
TezacaftorA type I corrector; with ivacaftor (Symdeko) it raised ppFEV1 by 4.0 points in EVOLVE 138,464.
TheratypeA grouping of variants by the molecular treatment they respond to 81.
TIDESAn international study of 6,088 people with CF and 4,102 parents: depression in 19% of adults, anxiety in 32% 352.
Tobramycin (inhaled)An antibiotic for P. aeruginosa: 28 days for first infection 301; TOBI labelled 28 days on, 28 days off 139.
Totally implantable venous access device (port)A device under the skin for repeated IV courses; median device survival 1,441 days in one CF series 388.
TransitionThe planned move from paediatric to adult care; gaps over 3 months predicted lower lung function 292.
Treatment burdenThe daily load of CF care: a mean of 108 minutes a day for adults before modulators 400.
Ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA)A bile acid once used for CF liver disease; evidence is insufficient for routine use 62, and CF Foundation 2024 recommends against it to prevent advanced disease 411.
Variant classesSix classes by molecular defect: I no protein, II misfolding, III gating, IV conductance, V reduced amount, VI instability 23,261.
Very high IRT (failsafe)Referral of infants with IRT above about the 99th percentile even when no variant is found, to catch variants a panel misses 285.

CF Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on cystic fibrosis (CF) and CFTR modulators, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people with CF, their families and caregivers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine, an airway-clearance routine, enzyme dosing or a care plan without your own CF care team. Sweat-chloride bands, the diagnostic flow, FEV1 percent predicted, genotype tables, the carrier calculator and the modulator eligibility explainer are for learning, never for diagnosis or treatment decisions. Doses, airway-clearance routines, enzyme dosing, infection-control guidance and guideline statements are quoted as published by the CF Foundation, ECFS, NICE and drug labels, never as instructions, and survival figures are registry population statistics, not predictions for any person.
