Part 2 · How CF affects the body

Sweat, diabetes, bones & fertility

Beyond the lungs and gut, CFTR matters in the islets of the pancreas, the sweat glands, the sinuses, the bones, the kidneys and the reproductive tract. Start with CF-related diabetes, the commonest of these as people with CF live longer, then explore the body map.

Childhood · the islets

Islets sit inside a scarring pancreas

The insulin-making islets (blue rings) sit inside the pancreas. Insulin secretion defects are present in essentially all people with CF, partly because damage to the islets spreads from the destruction of the exocrine pancreas (ISPAD 2022) 326. CF-related diabetes (CFRD) is a distinct type of diabetes, different from type 1 and type 2, and is linked with worse health outcomes in CF 227.

In plain wordsThe pancreas has small clusters of cells, the islets, that make insulin. In CF the scarring around them slowly damages them too.
Exam hookCFRD is its own type of diabetes, neither type 1 nor type 2 227.

A gap that closed. In Minnesota in 1987–2002, median survival was 49.5 years in males without diabetes and 47.4 with it, but 47.0 in females without diabetes and 30.7 with it 297. By 2003–2008 the sex difference in CFRD mortality had disappeared, and only 2 of 61 people diagnosed in that period died; the authors credit early diagnosis and aggressive treatment 305. Why women fared worse is unclear Hypothesis: the authors speculated about hormones and inflammation 297. These are cohort statistics from their era, not predictions.

Up to 80% of older people with severe genotypes have CFRD; incidence is about 4–9% a year in Denmark and 2.7 cases per 100 person-years in Minnesota 326. Whether highly effective modulators change this is open: ISPAD 2022 finds too little evidence yet to change screening for people taking them 326.

Sweat, salt and heat

  • In CF the sweat duct cannot take salt back as sweat flows up it, so the sweat that reaches the skin stays salty Established 346.
  • Sweat-gland dysfunction can cause "heat shock" (salt depletion) in people with CF 115.
  • Pseudo-Bartter syndrome is low chloride, low sodium (hyponatraemia), low potassium and a metabolic alkalosis from salt lost in sweat; it can be the first presentation of CF in early childhood 269.
  • The CFF infant guideline recommends 1/8 teaspoon of table salt a day from diagnosis, rising to 1/4 teaspoon at 6 months (about 12.5 mEq of sodium per 1/8 teaspoon), because baby foods contain no added salt 36. Quoted for teaching, not as advice.
