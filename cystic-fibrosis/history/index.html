Part 6 · Context & review

History & people

For centuries a salty-tasting child was thought to be bewitched. In under a hundred years CF went from a newly named disease of infants to a gene, a sweat test, a screening programme and medicines that act on the faulty protein itself. Scrub the timeline from 1606 to 2025, or filter it by theme.

References to conditions that probably reflect CF go back to the Middle Ages 347. Busch traced European folk sayings about licking a child's forehead, where a salty taste meant the child was bewitched and would soon die; he found them in 12 European countries 51. The timeline starts with the first dated text and ends with the 2025 Lasker award. The axis is compressed before 1930.

1606 to 1946 · 1606

A Spanish text notes that a "bewitched" child's forehead tastes salty

A 1606 Spanish text by Alonso y de los Ruyzes de Fonteca noted that the fingers taste salty after rubbing a "bewitched" child's forehead 472. Folk sayings of this kind were found in 12 European countries 51. People: Alonso y de los Ruyzes de Fonteca.

In plain wordsLong ago, people noticed that some babies tasted salty when kissed. Doctors later found this salty sweat is a sign of CF 51,103.
Exam hook1938: Andersen describes "cystic fibrosis of the pancreas"; 1946: autosomal recessive inheritance 9,10.

Venture philanthropy: how the modulators were found

In 1999–2000 the CF Foundation funded Aurora Biosciences to run high-throughput screens for compounds that would correct or potentiate CFTR. A Lasker perspective calls it "the most successful example of Venture Philanthropy to date" 156.

Vertex acquired Aurora in 2001. With continued Foundation funding, screening hits were found by 2003 and ivacaftor was identified by 2005 156. In lab studies it raised chloride secretion in G551D/F508del bronchial cells about 10-fold, to about 50% of non-CF levels 481.

Vertex won approval of 4 CF drugs in 7 years, and it was the first time the FDA accepted efficacy tests in primary human cells 156. By 2022, though, only 19,516 people, about 12% of the estimated number with CF worldwide, were receiving triple therapy 188.

The 2025 Lasker–DeBakey Clinical Medical Research Award went to Michael Welsh, Jesús González and Paul Negulescu for work that led to the triple combination 156.

People to know

1938

Dorothy Andersen

A Columbia University pathologist who described "cystic fibrosis of the pancreas" and set it apart from coeliac disease 9,89. She found that infants who survived the newborn period often developed progressive lung disease 156, and with di Sant'Agnese used sulfonamides and penicillin against lung infections from the 1940s 156,472.

1953

di Sant'Agnese and colleagues

Dehydrated children with CF during a New York heat wave in 1948–49 led di Sant'Agnese to study their sweat 156,278. In 1953 the team reported the abnormal sweat electrolytes of CF 103, with sweat chloride 3 to 5 times normal 156.

1959

Gibson and Cooke

Standardised sweat collection by pilocarpine iontophoresis, still the basis of the sweat test 167,472. The test let doctors find milder cases, so CF was no longer seen only as a disease of mucus 89.

1983

Quinton

Showed that the CF sweat duct is impermeable to chloride, which explains salty sweat 346.

1989

The gene hunters

Three Science papers (Rommens, Riordan, Kerem and colleagues) cloned the gene on chromosome 7 and named its protein CFTR 231,373,378. About 70% of CF mutations were one three-base deletion, F508del 231. A 1985 DNA marker from Tsui and colleagues had already narrowed the search 473.

2025

Welsh, González and Negulescu

Lasker award winners for the work that led to the triple combination 156, approved by the FDA as Trikafta on 21 October 2019 142.

CF Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on cystic fibrosis (CF) and CFTR modulators, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people with CF, their families and caregivers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine, an airway-clearance routine, enzyme dosing or a care plan without your own CF care team. Sweat-chloride bands, the diagnostic flow, FEV1 percent predicted, genotype tables, the carrier calculator and the modulator eligibility explainer are for learning, never for diagnosis or treatment decisions. Doses, airway-clearance routines, enzyme dosing, infection-control guidance and guideline statements are quoted as published by the CF Foundation, ECFS, NICE and drug labels, never as instructions, and survival figures are registry population statistics, not predictions for any person.
