Part 3 · Screening, diagnosis & monitoring

Lung function & monitoring

CF care is a rhythm of regular checks: blowing tests of the lungs, airway cultures, scans and a yearly review of other organs. Watch one spirometry blow build FEV1, FVC and percent predicted, then see why a gentler test, the lung clearance index, spots early disease that FEV1 misses.

Step 1 · get ready

Breathe all the way in

Spirometry asks for the biggest breath in that a person can take, then the hardest and longest blow out. ATS and ERS updated the technical standards for doing it in 2019, and their interpretation standards (reference equations, limits of normal, severity) in 2022. 177,443 It needs practice and cooperation: in the Wisconsin cohort, serial spirometry was not reliable with acceptable quality until after age 7, and chest radiograph scoring picked up disease earlier. 124

In plain wordsSpirometry measures how much air you can blow out, and how fast. It is a breathing test, not a scan or a needle. 40,344
Exam hookSpirometry is hard to do reliably in young children: in Wisconsin, not until after age 7. 124

Try it: FEV1 percent predicted bands

A teaching tool, not an assessmentSeverity bands quoted as published in a CF research paper: mild >70% FEV1, moderate 40–69%, severe <40%. 163 A single number says little alone: trends, symptoms, other tests and the care team's judgement matter, and bands differ between sources.

80% predicted falls in the published "mild" band.

The 30% marker: in a Toronto cohort of 673 patients (1977–1989), FEV1 below 30% predicted was linked to a 2-year mortality above 50%, and each 10% fall doubled the 2-year risk of death (RR 2.0, 95% CI 1.9–2.2). This is a historic population statistic from before modern treatment and cannot predict what happens to any one person. 234

Lung clearance index: seeing early disease

In multiple-breath washout a person breathes a tracer gas in and then washes it out with ordinary breaths; the lung clearance index (LCI) reflects how unevenly ventilation is spread. It can be done across a wider age range than spirometry and is more sensitive to early damage. A 2013 ERS/ATS consensus set its standards. 374

At preschool age, 35 of 48 children with CF (73%) had an abnormal LCI but only 5 had an abnormal FEV1. An abnormal preschool LCI predicted an abnormal school-age result with a positive predictive value of 94%. 15

Research tool

Valuable in trials

A CF Foundation workshop (2015) judged LCI a valuable trial outcome for preschool children and for older patients with normal FEV1, but found insufficient data to support it in routine clinical management. 454

Modulators

LCI moves when FEV1 may not

ETI improved median LCI in children aged 6–11 from 3 months on (−1.0 for F/MF, −0.8 for F/F), with better MRI scores. 440 In Danish children aged 6–17, mean LCI improved by 1.7 units at 12 months. 207 Lumacaftor–ivacaftor improved LCI (−1.6) without a significant FEV1 change. Early evidence 175

AREST CF

Bronchiectasis starts in infancy

In 127 infants diagnosed by screening and followed with CT and lavage, bronchiectasis rose from 29.3% at 3 months to 61.5% at 3 years. Risk factors: meconium ileus (OR 3.17), respiratory symptoms (2.27), free neutrophil elastase in lavage (3.02) and gas trapping (2.05). 426

CT technique

Lung volume matters

Expiratory CT scans had poor sensitivity (0.46) for bronchiectasis in young children; lung volume during CT should be standardised. 311

MRI

Without radiation

Chest MRI scores of morphology and perfusion respond to CFTR modulators and are used as radiation-free research outcomes. Early evidence 175,440
