## Findings

### lungs · Lung function still declines in some adults on ETI
- French prospective national observational study, 31 CF centres: 2321 adults (median age 29.2 years; pre-ETI ppFEV1 59.7%) with at least one F508del variant who started elexacaftor-tezacaftor-ivacaftor between 24 Dec 2019 and 23 Dec 2023, followed for 3 years (Early evidence) [[Burgel2026]].
- ppFEV1 rose by a median 14.5 percentage points (IQR 7.9–22.7) in the first 3 months. From that peak the mean decline was 0.70% (95% CI 0.57–0.83) a year [[Burgel2026]].
- Decline was uneven: 1583 (68.2%) had no or limited decline (1.5% a year or less); 738 (31.8%) lost more than 1.5% a year, and 342 of those lost more than 3.0% a year [[Burgel2026]].
- Decline was greater with a higher ppFEV1 at 3 months. Oral azithromycin at 1 year (relative risk 0.78, 95% CI 0.68–0.90) and inhaled antibiotics (0.81, 0.70–0.94) were associated with a lower risk of losing more than 1.5% a year. These are observational associations [[Burgel2026]].
- The authors state the background as a decline of about 1.5% a year without modulators, and note that extension phases of the pivotal ETI trials found no evidence of decline over 4 years [[Burgel2026]].

### Exam hooks
- On ETI, adults gained a median 14.5 ppFEV1 points in 3 months, then lost a mean 0.70% a year; about a third lost more than 1.5% a year [[Burgel2026]].

### In plain words
- The modulator medicine lifts lung function a lot, but for about one adult in three it still slowly falls afterwards. Regular breathing tests still matter [[Burgel2026]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Why do some adults keep declining on ETI, and do azithromycin and inhaled antibiotics slow it? The associations here are not from a randomised comparison [[Burgel2026]]. Status: open.
