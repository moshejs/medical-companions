## Findings

### living · Treatment burden and daily time
- In 204 adults with CF (Project on Adult Care in CF, US), the median number of daily therapies was 7 (IQR 5–9). Mean time spent on treatment was 108 minutes per day (SD 58) [[Sawicki2009]].
- In the same study, respondents reported a median of 3 inhaled and 3 oral therapies the day before the survey. Only 49% had done airway clearance that day [[Sawicki2009]].
- The mean CFQ-R treatment-burden score was 52.3 (SD 22.1; 0–100 scale, higher is better) and did not differ by age or FEV1. Two or more nebulised medicines, and airway clearance of 30 minutes or more, were each linked to higher perceived burden [[Sawicki2009]].
- The number of medicines and the time on therapies did not differ by gender, age or FEV1. In other words, the daily workload stays high whatever the disease severity [[Sawicki2009]].
- The 2023 James Lind Alliance refresh of the top 10 CF research priorities drew on 1417 people with CF, family members and clinicians, who chose from 971 new questions plus 15 from 2018. In the second survey, 62% of respondents with CF (or their family member with CF) were taking modulators [[Rowbotham2023]].
- SIMPLIFY randomised 594 people on ETI with good lung function (mean ppFEV1 96.9%). Stopping hypertonic saline or dornase alfa for 6 weeks was non-inferior to continuing:
  - hypertonic saline trial: between-group difference in ppFEV1 change −0.32% (95% CI −1.25 to 0.60)
  - dornase alfa trial: between-group difference +0.35% (95% CI −0.45 to 1.14) [[MayerHamblett2023SIMPLIFY]]
- Even so, adverse events were more frequent among those who stopped: 35% vs 24% in the hypertonic saline trial and 37% vs 23% in the dornase alfa trial. SIMPLIFY is short-term (6 weeks) evidence and does not give advice for any individual [[MayerHamblett2023SIMPLIFY]].
- ECFS 2024 standards stress a physically active lifestyle and the "strict avoidance of all recreational inhalations, including e-cigarettes". They also describe virtual consultations as a developing model of care [[Southern2024Standards]].

### living · Adherence
- In 95 people with CF aged 6 or older, a composite medication possession ratio (from pharmacy refills) predicted having at least one exacerbation treated with IV antibiotics over 12 months (IRR 2.34, p=0.05). It predicted baseline FEV1, but not the rate of FEV1 decline [[Eakin2011]].
- In a US commercial-claims cohort of 3287 people with CF aged 6 or older, mean composite adherence to pulmonary medicines was 48% ± 31%, and adherence fell with age [[Quittner2014Adherence]].
- In that cohort, low adherence (MPR <0.50) vs high (≥0.80) was linked to more CF-related hospitalisations (event rate ratio 1.35; 95% CI 1.15–1.57) and US$14,211 higher concurrent healthcare costs [[Quittner2014Adherence]].
- In 78 adults using data-logging I-Neb nebulisers, adherence measured three ways:
  - self-report: median 80% (IQR 60–95%)
  - device download: median 36% (IQR 5–84.5%)
  - clinician estimates: median 50–60% [[Daniels2011Adherence]]
- A systematic review of adherence to CFTR modulators (7 studies) found adherence of 0.62–0.99 by pharmacy data (PDC or MPR), 61% by electronic monitoring and 100% by self-report. Age under 18 and higher lung function were linked to better adherence [[Hansen2024Adherence]].
- Depressive symptoms and medication beliefs are linked to adherence. The CFF/ECFS mental-health consensus lists worse adherence among the outcomes associated with psychological symptoms [[Quittner2016ICMH]].

### living · Quality of life and the CFQ-R
- The Cystic Fibrosis Questionnaire (teen/adult version, age 14 and older) has 44 items on 12 generic and CF-specific scales. Child and parent versions also exist [[Quittner2005CFQ]].
- In 212 people aged 14–53 at 18 US centres, scale reliability (Cronbach alpha) was 0.67–0.94 and test–retest stability was rs 0.45–0.90 [[Quittner2005CFQ]].
- The minimal clinically important difference (MCID) for the CFQ-R respiratory scale was 4.0 points in people with stable disease and 8.5 points in people with an exacerbation (two tobramycin inhalation studies) [[Quittner2009MCID]].
- Compared with placebo at 24 weeks, ETI raised the CFQ-R respiratory domain score by 20.2 points in people aged 12 or older with F508del/minimal function. The trial took 4 points as the MCID [[Middleton2019ETI]].
- In people homozygous for F508del, ETI vs tezacaftor/ivacaftor at 4 weeks raised the CFQ-R respiratory domain by 17.4 points (95% CI 11.8–23.0) [[Heijerman2019ETI]].
- In a prospective Dutch cohort (RISE, n=174), the CFQ-R respiratory score rose by 19.55 points over the first 48 weeks on ETI [[vanderHeijden2025]].
- In 262 adults with CF in a palliative-care trial (78% on modulators), the commonest symptoms were lack of energy (74%) and cough (73%). The most distressing were difficulty sleeping and pain [[Smirnova2024Symptoms]].
- The symptoms with the largest negative association with quality of life were extrapulmonary: lack of energy, feeling sad and worrying [[Smirnova2024Symptoms]].
- The CFF pain guideline makes 13 recommendations on assessment, management and communication. It centres on a multimodal approach and on sensitivity to stigma and access [[Dellon2025Pain]].

### living · School, work and transition
- In a 2024 CFF survey of 146 people with CF and caregivers (ages 2–21), common school challenges were:
  - frequent absences for medical appointments
  - getting accommodations (medicines, bathroom passes)
  - managing infection control
  - the effect of CF-related health issues on schoolwork [[Heimbruch2026]]
- Families in that survey preferred the CF care team to start conversations about school [[Heimbruch2026]].
- ECFS standards describe CF nurse specialists visiting schools, with parental permission, to teach staff about nutrition, enzyme and other medicine administration, time off for clinic visits, and adherence [[Conway2014ECFS]].
- In 254 UK adults with CF, 65% were in employment or education. Among them:
  - 40% had stopped a job because of CF
  - 47% said CF affected their career choice
  - 23% reported workplace discrimination [[Targett2014Employment]]
- Employment in that UK study was independently associated with educational attainment, centre, and the CFQ-R role and health-perception domains, not with clinical severity [[Targett2014Employment]].
- In a Danish nationwide registry cohort (1990–2018), the adult CF population grew almost fourfold (88 to 331). Education was similar to matched comparators, but adults with CF were less often employed [[Jensen2024Employment]].
- In 3876 US adolescents and young adults who moved to adult care (2013–2019), the median gap between the last paediatric and first adult visit was 7.6 months. Gaps longer than 3 months predicted lower lung function [[Melton2024Transition]].
- Centres with more engagement in the CF RISE transition programme had better lung function and weight at the first adult visit [[Melton2024Transition]].
- ECFS standards: all children with CF should move from paediatric to adult care. Paediatric and adult CF nurse specialists coordinate involvement and communication during the transition [[Conway2014ECFS]].
- In the US, more than half of people with CF were expected to be adults by 2010. The CF Foundation required accredited programmes to set up adult CF programmes [[Tuchman2010Transition]].

### living · Exercise
- Cochrane 2022 (24 RCTs, 875 participants): physical activity programmes lasting over 6 months probably improve exercise capacity (peak oxygen uptake MD 1.60; 95% CI 0.16 to 3.05; 6 RCTs, 348 participants; moderate certainty). The effect was judged small [[Radtke2022Cochrane]].
- The same review found physical activity may have no effect on FEV1 % predicted (MD 2.41; 95% CI −0.49 to 5.31) or on the CFQ-R respiratory domain (MD −0.05; 95% CI −3.61 to 3.51) at 6 months or longer (low certainty) [[Radtke2022Cochrane]].
- The ECFS framework says the specialist CF physiotherapist leads airway clearance, physical exercise and inhalation therapy. Physiotherapy is "primarily preventive" from diagnosis onwards [[Conway2014ECFS]].

### living · Cross-infection rules in daily life (quoted as published)
- The 2013 CFF/SHEA infection prevention and control (IP&C) guideline updated the 2003 guideline. It added new evidence on droplet transmission and source containment of pathogens [[Saiman2014IPC]].
- New recommendations in the 2013 guideline included universal masks for people with CF in healthcare settings, contact precautions, and the "6-foot rule". Among US programmes' written policies:
  - patient masks: 85% (inpatient) and 87% (outpatient)
  - contact precautions: 90%
  - inpatient 6-foot rule: 53% [[Stoudemire2019IPC]]
- The 2013 guideline widened the recommended distance between people with CF from 3 feet to 6 feet [[Haggie2020,Saiman2014IPC]].
- Haggie summarises the guideline's social advice: people with CF should be at least 2 m apart, should not meet in person, and children with CF at the same school should avoid contact and shared facilities [[Haggie2020,Saiman2014IPC]].
- In a survey of 198 of 277 US CF centre directors, 74% held group education events. Of the 138 centres with in-person indoor meetings, 45% allowed 1 person with CF to attend and 51% allowed none. At outdoor events, 84% allowed more than one person with CF [[Saiman2018Survey]].
- Among healthcare workers at 25 centres, 43% advised more than 75% of people with CF or parents to avoid socialising with other people with CF. Among people with CF and parents, 53% reported discussing avoidance of socialisation with their care team [[Saiman2022Barriers]].
- ECFS standards: people with CF should not share rooms, bathrooms or toilets in hospital, and should not be in contact with each other in clinic, ward, pharmacy or radiology waiting areas [[Conway2014ECFS]].

### living · Travel
- In 79 adults with CF (mean FEV1 71% predicted), a COPD pre-flight algorithm was tested against a hypoxia-altitude simulation test (HAST, oxygen advised if SpO2 ≤85%). The study's rules were:
  - SpO2 >95% at sea level plus SpO2 ≥84% during a 6-minute walk: fly without further testing
  - SpO2 92–95% plus walk SpO2 <84%, or sea-level SpO2 <92%: in-flight oxygen recommended
  - otherwise: HAST [[Akero2025AirTravel]]
- Only 21 of the 79 (27%) would have needed referral for HAST. Like everything in this companion, the algorithm is a teaching example, not individual advice [[Akero2025AirTravel]].

### living · Social media and community
- In 50 adolescents and young adults with CF at one US centre, YouTube, Snapchat and Instagram were the most-used platforms. They did not routinely use social media for health information, support or adherence, but were interested in a CF-specific platform [[Perkins2021SocialMedia]].
- Because in-person contact between people with CF is discouraged, online contact is a main route to peer community. The ECFS 2024 standards also describe virtual consultations as an emerging care model [[Haggie2020,Southern2024Standards]].

### living · Ageing with CF and new comorbidities
- The 2024 ECFS standard "Planning for a longer life" frames people with CF "not as a 'problem' to be solved, but as a success, a potential and a voice to be heard". It covers life moments that CF teams should support, including having a family [[Gramegna2024Standards]].
- Reviews of the ageing CF population highlight higher risks of cancer and cardiovascular disease, obesity and body-composition change, menopause, and lifetime antimicrobial resistance [[Frost2025Ageing]].
- In a US registry analysis of 8262 adults (20 or older) who started ETI, weight change at 1 year was:
  - lost weight: 18.1%
  - gained <5 kg: 55.4%
  - gained 5 to <10 kg: 20.0%
  - gained ≥10 kg: 6.5% [[Ostrenga2026Obesity]]
- At one US paediatric centre (243 people aged 2–23), 19.3% were overweight or obese, and 85.1% of them were taking a CFTR modulator [[Bashir2026Obesity]].
- A case series described new systemic hypertension in 4 adults within the first weeks of starting ETI. The authors suggest monitoring cardiovascular parameters (Early evidence) [[Gramegna2022Hypertension]].
- In 41,188 people with CF in the US (1990–2009), standardised incidence ratios (SIRs) for cancer were:
  - overall, non-transplanted: 1.1 (95% CI 1.0–1.3)
  - digestive-tract cancer, non-transplanted: 3.5 (95% CI 2.6–4.7)
  - digestive-tract cancer after transplant: 17.3 [[Maisonneuve2013Cancer]]
- The CFF colorectal cancer task force put CRC risk at 5–10 times the general population, and 25–30 times after organ transplant. Its recommendations, quoted as published:
  - colonoscopy is the preferred test
  - start screening at age 40 (at 30, within 2 years of transplant, for transplant recipients)
  - re-screen every 5 years, with 3-year surveillance intervals [[Hadjiliadis2018CRC]]
- ECFS 2024: cancer is more common in people with CF than in the general population and needs regular screening. Planning for end of life should be discussed "openly, honestly, with sensitivity and compassion" [[Burgel2024Standards]].

### living · Mental health
- TIDES screened 6088 people with CF aged 12 and older and 4102 parents at 154 centres in 9 countries:
  - elevated depression: 10% of adolescents, 19% of adults, 37% of mothers, 31% of fathers
  - elevated anxiety: 22% of adolescents, 32% of adults, 48% of mothers, 36% of fathers [[Quittner2014TIDES]]
- These rates were 2–3 times those in community samples. People with elevated anxiety were far more likely to also report depression (OR 14.97 in adolescents, 13.64 in adults) [[Quittner2014TIDES]].
- In TIDES, adolescents whose parents reported depression were more likely to have elevated depression scores themselves (OR 2.32). The same held for anxiety (OR 2.22) [[Quittner2014TIDES]].
- The CFF/ECFS International Committee on Mental Health (22 members) finalised 15 recommendations, each with 100% consensus [[Quittner2016ICMH]].
- Its screening wording: "The ICMH recommends annual screening for depression and anxiety with the PHQ-9 and GAD-7 for adolescents and adults with CF (ages 12–adulthood)" [[Quittner2016ICMH]].
- It also recommends offering annual screening to at least one primary caregiver of children aged 0–17, using PHQ-9/GAD-7, PHQ-8/GAD-7 or PHQ-2/GAD-2 depending on resources [[Quittner2016ICMH]].
- Children aged 7–11 should be clinically evaluated when caregiver scores are elevated or when the child shows significant symptoms. Evidence-based psychological interventions are first-line in this age group [[Quittner2016ICMH]].
- For ages 12 and older, treatment follows a stepped-care model:
  - mild symptoms: education and rescreening
  - moderate symptoms: CBT or IPT
  - severe depression: combined psychological treatment and antidepressants
  - severe anxiety: exposure-based CBT [[Quittner2016ICMH]]
- On medicines, the ICMH names citalopram, escitalopram, sertraline and fluoxetine as appropriate first-line SSRIs for most people with CF aged 12 and older who need medication, with close monitoring for drug–drug interactions [[Quittner2016ICMH]].
- The consensus cites CFF Registry data in which 1.6% of deaths were categorised as explicit suicide [[Quittner2016ICMH]].
- Among 1005 US TIDES participants, 25% screened positive for depression. The 5-year hazard ratio for death was:
  - unadjusted: 2.0 (95% CI 1.3–3.0)
  - adjusted, whole population: 1.4 (95% CI 0.9–2.2)
  - adjusted, adults: 1.6 (95% CI 1.0–2.4)
  - adjusted, severe range: 2.0 (95% CI 1.2–3.4) [[Schechter2021Depression]]
- Anxiety was not associated with mortality in that cohort. These are associations, not predictions for any person [[Schechter2021Depression]].

### living · CFTR modulators and neuropsychiatric effects
- The Trikafta label (section 5.4) warns of "Serious neuropsychiatric events, including symptoms of anxiety, depression, suicidal ideation and behavior, and sleep disturbances", reported after marketing in adults and children with and without prior symptoms [[FDA-Trikafta]].
- The same label section says symptoms "may occur within the first three months". It advises assessing baseline neuropsychiatric symptoms, monitoring for new or worsening symptoms, and weighing whether to interrupt therapy [[FDA-Trikafta]].
- The Alyftrek and Kalydeco labels carry the same class warning, worded for drugs "containing the same or similar active ingredients" [[FDA-Alyftrek,FDA-Kalydeco]].
- A case series described 6 people at 4 US centres who reported "mental fogginess" or other mental status changes. All noticed the change within the first 3 months of ETI, and each was managed through shared decision-making (Early evidence) [[Heo2022MentalStatus]].
- In contrast, a prospective cohort (RISE, n=174, followed 48 weeks after starting ETI) found improved psychosocial health and a statistically significant but not clinically relevant fall in depressive symptoms (−1.25). Anxiety did not change [[vanderHeijden2025]].
- In RISE, women, people with lower lung function, and people already on psychotropic medicines seemed more likely to have lower mental-health scores after starting ETI [[vanderHeijden2025]].

### pregnancy · Male fertility
- More than 97% of males with CF are infertile, because of CFTR-related congenital bilateral absence of the vas deferens (CBAVD) and atresia or absence of the epididymis [[Kazmerski2022Family]].
- Most men with CF have obstructive azoospermia. Testosterone deficiency is also more common and affects muscle, bone density and quality of life [[Yoon2019Male]].
- Biological parenthood for males with CF needs sperm retrieval (for example TESA or TESE) followed by ICSI. With TESA, sperm are found in about one-third to one-half of males, and in about 10% more when TESE follows [[Kazmerski2022Family]].
- Clinical pregnancy rates with assisted reproduction are close to 50%, similar to the non-CF population, but miscarriage risk is higher [[Kazmerski2022Family]].
- Males who want to avoid any modulator exposure of sperm would need to stop modulators for about 80 days, the life of an average sperm [[Kazmerski2022Family]].
- Comparing 10 men with CF and 29 men with CBAVD only who had surgical sperm retrieval, men with CF had:
  - lower sperm concentration
  - more rescue testicular extraction (70.0% vs 27.6%)
  - lower ICSI fertilisation rate (32.5% vs 68.9%) [[McBride2021]]
- In an international survey of 42 males with CF whose partners became pregnant, 40 had used assisted reproduction, and 40 continued modulators during the partner's pregnancy. First-trimester miscarriage occurred in 11.9% and no congenital anomalies were reported [[TaylorCousar2024Males]].
- The CFF 2026 position paper says fertility may be improving with CFTR modulators for females, but not for males, who still need assisted reproductive technologies for biological parenthood [[Jain2026CFFReproductive]].
- Reproductive-health education should begin early in adolescence. Men first told about CF infertility after age 20 were more likely to report being "very upset" than those told before 16 [[West2022SRH]].

### pregnancy · Female fertility, contraception and the rise in pregnancies
- About 35% of females with CF who try to conceive are infertile, compared with 5–15% in the general population. Causes are multifactorial: nutrition, thick acidic cervical mucus, and lower ovarian reserve [[Kazmerski2022Family]].
- In the US CF Foundation Patient Registry, annual pregnancies numbered 200–310 from 2010 to 2019. In 2020 the count nearly doubled to 619 after CFTR modulators were approved for most people with CF in 2019 [[Montemayor2022Pregnancy]].
- An Italian centre reported more unplanned pregnancies after ETI began. It attributes them to reduced attention to the possibility of conception (a long-held sense of subfertility) rather than to contraceptive failure [[Dacco2023]].
- In a survey of 561 females with CF (aged 18–45) at 10 US centres, 89% used modulators and 99% used contraception. One-third (34%) had ever been pregnant, and 48% of reported pregnancies were unintended [[Godfrey2025Contraception]].
- In that survey, among people living with a partner, modulator use was linked to pregnancy (OR 1.8; 95% CI 1.3–2.6). Modulators did not appear to reduce the effectiveness of hormonal contraception [[Godfrey2025Contraception]].
- The CFF position paper: reproductive health education should begin at CF diagnosis and be revisited annually. Contraception is safe overall but underused in people with CF [[Jain2026CFFReproductive]].
- Other paths to parenthood include adoption, fostering, step-parenthood and surrogacy. People with CF have pursued and qualified for each of these [[Kazmerski2022Family]].

### pregnancy · Partner carrier testing and genetic counselling
- ACOG Committee Opinion 691 says information on carrier screening should be offered to every pregnant woman, ideally before pregnancy, and that a patient may decline any or all screening [[ACOG2017CarrierScreening]].
- If one partner is a carrier, the other partner should be offered testing. If both are carriers, genetic counselling should be offered [[ACOG2017CarrierScreening]].
- Because CF is inherited, genetic counsellors have an important role as people with CF consider their reproductive options [[Kazmerski2022Family]].
- The reproductive-health literature advises that the risk of having a child with CF must be considered when planning assisted reproduction [[Shteinberg2021Fertility]].

### pregnancy · Pregnancy outcomes and lung health
- Most women with CF have normal pregnancies, but premature birth is common, especially with reduced lung function and CF-related diabetes (CFRD) [[Shteinberg2021Fertility]].
- In 307 pregnancies at 11 US adult centres (2010–2021), 114 (37.1%) had modulator exposure. Adjusted change in ppFEV1 from before to during pregnancy was:
  - no modulator: −2.36 (95% CI −3.56 to −1.16)
  - highly effective modulators: +2.60 (95% CI 0.23 to 4.97) [[Jain2025MaternalOutcomes]]
- In the same study, the unexposed group declined overall from before to after pregnancy (−2.56; 95% CI −3.62 to −1.49), but the highly effective modulator group did not (+1.10; 95% CI −1.13 to 3.34) [[Jain2025MaternalOutcomes]].
- Italian Registry, 2016–2023 (172 pregnancies):
  - 86.0% carried to term
  - median age at conception 31.8 years
  - CFRD in 21.6%; 37.2% on modulators [[Salvatore2026Registry]]
- In that registry, preterm birth (<37 weeks) was 24.1% overall, 40.6% with CFRD and 22.7% on modulators. The caesarean rate was 54.9% overall and 81.2% with CFRD [[Salvatore2026Registry]].
- Higher-risk disease is defined as FEV1 ≤60% predicted and/or BMI ≤21 kg/m² before pregnancy. More severe disease is associated with preterm delivery, caesarean delivery and low birthweight [[Montemayor2022Pregnancy]].
- A preconception BMI of at least 22 kg/m² is described as the ideal goal [[Montemayor2022Pregnancy]].
- MAYFLOWERS is the first prospective multicentre study of pregnancy in CF. It plans to enrol about 285 pregnant people with CF, including those who continue or stop modulators and those not eligible [[Jain2022MAYFLOWERS,NCT04828382]].
- The registry record for MAYFLOWERS lists it as active, not recruiting (start 2021-09-30; estimated primary completion 2027-06-30) [[NCT04828382]].

### pregnancy · CFRD and gestational diabetes in pregnancy (quoted as published)
- CFF/ADA guideline: women with CF who are planning a pregnancy or are confirmed pregnant should be screened for pre-existing CFRD with a 2-h 75-g OGTT, if they have not had a normal CFRD screen in the last 6 months [[Moran2010CFRD]].
- The guideline recommends screening for gestational diabetes at both 12–16 weeks and 24–28 weeks of gestation in pregnant women with CF not known to have CFRD, using a 2-h 75-g OGTT with glucose at 0, 1 and 2 h [[Moran2010CFRD]].
- Women with gestational diabetes are not considered to have CFRD but should have an OGTT 6–12 weeks after the end of the pregnancy [[Moran2010CFRD]].
- Gestational diabetes occurs in 10–36% of pregnancies in women with CF, compared with under 10% in the general population [[Montemayor2022Pregnancy]].
- Up to 50% of people with CF of gestational capacity have CFRD. The published targets are HbA1c <6.5% at conception and <6.0% during pregnancy [[Montemayor2022Pregnancy]].
- The CFF 2026 position paper states that pregnancy "requires close monitoring and unique screening for diabetes" [[Jain2026CFFReproductive]].

### pregnancy · Modulator exposure in pregnancy and lactation (labels and data)
- Trikafta label 8.1: there are "limited and incomplete human data" in pregnancy. Animal studies of each component showed no adverse developmental effects at exposures up to about 2–14 times the maximum recommended human dose [[FDA-Trikafta]].
- The same label section gives the background US risk of major birth defects (2–4%) and miscarriage (15–20%) [[FDA-Trikafta]].
- Trikafta label 8.2: no information exists on the presence of elexacaftor, tezacaftor or ivacaftor in human milk, though all three pass into rat milk. Breastfeeding benefits should be weighed against the mother's clinical need and any potential infant effects [[FDA-Trikafta]].
- The Alyftrek label states there are no human pregnancy data for vanzacaftor/tezacaftor/deutivacaftor, and that "it is likely that the drug will be present in human milk" [[FDA-Alyftrek]].
- The ivacaftor-containing labels report cataracts in juvenile rats dosed from postnatal day 7 at ≥10 mg/kg/day (0.21 times the MRHD). The labels recommend baseline and follow-up eye examinations for children starting these medicines [[FDA-Trikafta,FDA-Kalydeco]].
- In a clinician survey of 45 ETI-exposed pregnancies, 5 of the 6 women who stopped ETI out of concern for unknown fetal risk restarted it because of clinical decline. Miscarriage rates were consistent with the general US population [[TaylorCousar2021ETI]].
- In a French prospective pharmacovigilance series of 58 modulator-exposed pregnancies (87.9% on ETI throughout), there were 53 live births. Major congenital anomaly prevalence was 3.4% (95% CI 0.4–11.9), and no cataracts were found in the 10 children examined [[Gautier2025]].
- A 2026 scoping review concluded that ETI may improve maternal lung function and nutrition, and that fetal exposure has not been clearly linked to serious adverse outcomes. It found insufficient evidence on lactation safety and long-term effects [[Wang2026Pregnancy]].

### pregnancy · The exposed infant: cataracts, liver tests, newborn screening
- Three infants exposed to ETI in utero and through breastfeeding had bilateral congenital cataracts found within 6 months of birth, without significant visual impairment. The authors advise counselling about infant cataract screening (Early evidence) [[Jain2022Cataracts]].
- A review recommends ophthalmological screening for infants exposed to ivacaftor-containing regimens in utero or through breastfeeding. It also suggests considering infant liver-function tests [[Montemayor2022Pregnancy]].
- Case reports describe raised liver-function tests in infants exposed to ETI in utero and while breastfeeding [[Kolaczkowski2025]].
- In Indiana, 19 infants exposed to ETI had lower newborn-screening IRT than infants with CF, CRMS/CFSPID or CF carriers (p<0.001). Their median IRT was 18.9 ng/mL, similar to infants with normal screens (22.5 ng/mL) [[Patel2023IRT]].
- The authors recommend that newborn-screening programmes consider CFTR variant analysis for all ETI-exposed infants [[Patel2023IRT]].
- A case report described a false-negative CF newborn screen and normal pancreatic function in a child with CF whose mother took a CFTR modulator during pregnancy [[Fortner2021]].
- The CFF position paper notes that in-utero modulator exposure "has implications for infant monitoring and cystic fibrosis screening results in newborns" [[Jain2026CFFReproductive]].

### pregnancy · Lactation and early parenting
- Clinicians report that more than half of people with CF choose to breastfeed. Calorie needs during lactation are higher than the roughly 500 kcal/day extra advised for lactating people without CF [[Montemayor2022Pregnancy]].
- Parenting adds burden: caring for a newborn, postpartum physiological change, and keeping up CF treatments [[Shteinberg2021Fertility]].

### nursing · Standards of care and the CF nurse specialist
- The ECFS best-practice guidelines (2018 revision) set standards for newborn screening, diagnosis, preventive lung treatment, nutrition, complications, transplant/end-of-life care and psychological support [[Castellani2018ECFS]].
- The ECFS framework lists the core MDT as consultants, clinical nurse specialist, microbiologist, physiotherapist, dietitian, pharmacist, clinical psychologist, social worker and clinical geneticist [[Conway2014ECFS]].
- Recommended specialist-nurse staffing, in whole-time equivalents (WTE):
  - paediatric centres: 2 for 50 patients, 3 for 150, 4 for ≥250
  - adult centres: 2 for 100 patients, 3 for 150, 5 for ≥250 [[Conway2014ECFS]]
- The CF Clinical Nurse Specialist role includes education, advocacy and psychosocial support at key moments:
  - screening result and diagnosis
  - first admission and first IV course
  - a second diagnosis such as CFRD
  - transition to adult care
  - reproductive and pre/postnatal care
  - transplant and end of life [[Conway2014ECFS]]
- It also includes support and education at home, for example for home IV antibiotics, nebulisers, enteral feeding and non-invasive ventilation, and linking the family with primary care and community services [[Conway2014ECFS]].
- In adolescence, the CF nurse specialist should be able to discuss honestly:
  - recreational drugs
  - sexuality and contraception
  - fertility and pregnancy
  - education and employment
  - body image
  - adherence and self-care [[Conway2014ECFS]]
- In the pre-school years, the nurse specialist works with the CF dietitian on adjusting pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy, including what to do when a child refuses enzymes, and with the physiotherapist on airway clearance and exercise [[Conway2014ECFS]].
- ECFS 2023–2024 standards were updated in four papers using Delphi agreement and CF-community input. They re-affirm the MDT and add guidance on CFTR modulator adverse events, dose reduction and interruption [[Southern2024Standards,Burgel2024Standards,Gramegna2024Standards]].

### nursing · Clinic schedule, assessment and annual review
- ECFS: routine appointments for people with stable disease should be every 2–3 months depending on severity. Newly diagnosed infants are seen more often, initially weekly [[Conway2014ECFS]].
- ECFS: everyone should have an annual assessment that includes, at minimum, a full medical, dietetic, physiotherapy and psychosocial review and all surveillance blood tests [[Conway2014ECFS]].
- Nutrition guideline items that feed into nursing assessment and annual review:
  - regular monitoring of pancreatic enzymes and fat-soluble vitamins
  - glucose tolerance testing from age 10
  - bone mineral density from age 8–10 [[Turck2016Nutrition]]
- Mental-health screening (PHQ-9/GAD-7 from age 12, plus caregiver screening) is an annual-review item under the CFF/ECFS consensus [[Quittner2016ICMH]].
- The CFF palliative-care guideline recommends screening for unmet palliative-care needs with the IPOS annually and at disease milestones from age 12. It also recommends offering at least one primary caregiver annual screening with the BASC [[Kavalieratos2021Palliative]].
- ECFS 2024: monitoring and treating airway infection, inflammation and exacerbations "remains important" despite modulators. Extrapulmonary issues (diabetes, liver, bone, kidney stones, intestinal obstruction) need multidisciplinary care [[Burgel2024Standards]].

### nursing · Infection prevention and control in clinic (quoted as published)
- Haggie summarises the CFF guideline's standard precautions, aimed at NTM, P. aeruginosa and non-multidrug-resistant S. aureus:
  - healthcare workers wear gown and gloves for aerosol-generating procedures such as chest physiotherapy, sputum sampling and spirometry
  - people with CF wear a surgical mask in healthcare settings
  - waiting times in common areas are kept short [[Haggie2020,Saiman2014IPC]]
- Contact precautions (for MRSA, B. cepacia complex, multidrug-resistant P. aeruginosa and S. maltophilia) mean hand hygiene, healthcare workers putting on gloves and gown on room entry, and appropriate environmental cleaning [[Haggie2020,Saiman2014IPC]].
- Hand hygiene is described as the single most important practice. It applies before and after all patient contact, regardless of glove use, with no artificial fingernails [[Haggie2020,Saiman2014IPC]].
- In a 2022 survey of 174 outpatient CF staff at 20 centres, 72.4% knew of written IP&C policies, but only 50.8% of those knew whether the policy set cleaning roles. Knowledge of disinfection frequency was lowest among physicians [[Williams2025IPC]].
- In a survey across 25 centres, inpatient nurses had generally lower knowledge scores and adherence to selected IP&C practices than other groups [[Saiman2022Barriers]].
- Only 49% of US CF centres audited exam-room cleaning [[Saiman2018Survey]].

### nursing · IV antibiotics, venous access and aminoglycoside monitoring
- Cochrane review (5 RCTs, 354 participants aged 5–50): once-daily and thrice-daily IV aminoglycosides were equally effective for exacerbations (FEV1 MD 0.33; 95% CI −2.81 to 3.48) [[Bhatt2019Cochrane]].
- In children, once-daily dosing probably reduced nephrotoxicity (percentage change in creatinine MD −8.20; 95% CI −15.32 to −1.08). There was no difference in adults [[Bhatt2019Cochrane]].
- In a randomised PK study, tobramycin levels were drawn at 1 h and at 3.5–5 h after infusion. Evening dosing raised the urinary kidney-injury marker KIM-1 more than morning dosing (mean difference 0.73 ng/mg; 95% CI 0.14–1.32) [[Prayle2016]].
- In 165 totally implantable venous access devices (ports) in 109 people with CF over 20 years, median device survival was 1441 days. Complications ran at 0.58 per 1000 catheter-days:
  - occlusion: 33 devices
  - infection: 23
  - leakage: 16 [[Royle2008]]
- At an Irish adult centre, port complications ran at 0.337 per 1000 catheter-days. 14 cases of candidaemia occurred, associated with lower FEV1 and more frequent exacerbations [[McCarthy2018TIVAD]].
- Cochrane review of home vs hospital IV antibiotics found only one small RCT (17 participants, 31 admissions). Home treatment needed fewer investigations, disrupted family and personal life less, and was cheaper. Fatigue and mastery were worse at home [[Balaguer2008Cochrane]].
- ECFS standards call for staffing and resources for home IV antibiotics supervised by trained outreach nurses. They also call for admission within 24 h for urgent cases and within 7 days for non-urgent ones [[Conway2014ECFS]].

### nursing · End-of-life and palliative care
- The CFF palliative-care consensus made 11 statements. CF teams should deliver primary palliative care as part of usual care "at the time of diagnosis, and throughout the disease course" [[Kavalieratos2021Palliative]].
- In a randomised feasibility trial of adults with advanced CF (mean FEV1 about 41% predicted), adding quarterly visits with a palliative-care nurse practitioner was:
  - feasible: 77% of those approached were randomised; 88% retention
  - acceptable: 94% said it was not burdensome [[Kavalieratos2023Embedded]]
- At a large UK adult centre, an improvement project (team debriefs, a CF-specific advance care plan, staff education) over 2 years raised:
  - people offered a discussion of end-of-life wishes: from 10% to 85%
  - deaths in the preferred place: from 7% to 85% [[Cathcart2020]]
- ECFS standards place transplant and end-of-life care, and psychological support, within core best practice [[Castellani2018ECFS,Burgel2024Standards]].

### Exam hooks
- Mean daily treatment time for adults with CF (pre-modulator) ≈ 108 min/day; median 7 therapies [[Sawicki2009]].
- Electronic monitoring shows far lower adherence than self-report: 36% vs 80% for nebulisers [[Daniels2011Adherence]].
- Mean composite adherence to pulmonary medicines ≈ 48% in a US claims cohort [[Quittner2014Adherence]].
- CFQ-R respiratory MCID = 4 points (stable) and 8.5 points (exacerbation) [[Quittner2009MCID]].
- TIDES: depression 10% (adolescents) and 19% (adults); anxiety 22% and 32%; mothers 37% depressed, 48% anxious [[Quittner2014TIDES]].
- Mental-health screening: annual PHQ-9 and GAD-7 from age 12, plus annual caregiver screening for children aged 0–17 [[Quittner2016ICMH]].
- First-line SSRIs named by the ICMH: citalopram, escitalopram, sertraline, fluoxetine [[Quittner2016ICMH]].
- Modulator labels carry a neuropsychiatric warning: assess at baseline and monitor; onset may be within the first 3 months [[FDA-Trikafta]].
- More than 97% of males with CF are infertile from CBAVD. Sperm production is usually present, so retrieval plus ICSI allows biological parenthood [[Kazmerski2022Family]].
- In-utero ETI exposure lowers newborn IRT and can give a false-negative CF newborn screen [[Patel2023IRT,Fortner2021]].
- Infants exposed to ivacaftor-containing regimens in utero or through breast milk: consider eye (cataract) screening [[Jain2022Cataracts,Montemayor2022Pregnancy]].
- Pregnancy in CF: OGTT at 12–16 and at 24–28 weeks if no known CFRD; OGTT 6–12 weeks postpartum after gestational diabetes [[Moran2010CFRD]].
- Infection control: hand hygiene first; masks for people with CF in healthcare settings; contact precautions (gown and gloves on room entry); "6-foot rule" between people with CF [[Haggie2020,Stoudemire2019IPC,Saiman2014IPC]].
- Once-daily IV aminoglycosides work as well as thrice-daily, with less nephrotoxicity in children [[Bhatt2019Cochrane]].
- ECFS: stable clinic visits every 2–3 months plus an annual review [[Conway2014ECFS]].
- CF colorectal cancer screening: colonoscopy from age 40 (age 30 after transplant) [[Hadjiliadis2018CRC]].
- Transition gaps longer than 3 months between paediatric and adult care predict lower lung function [[Melton2024Transition]].
- Palliative care in CF is recommended from diagnosis onwards, not only at the end of life [[Kavalieratos2021Palliative]].

### In plain words
- Daily CF care can take about two hours a day. Feeling tired of treatments is common and worth telling your team about [[Sawicki2009]].
- Most people find it hard to do every treatment every day. Teams want honest answers so they can help, not judge [[Daniels2011Adherence]].
- Feeling low or anxious is common for people with CF and for their parents. From age 12, clinics check in about mood once a year [[Quittner2014TIDES,Quittner2016ICMH]].
- If you feel foggy, sad, anxious or have trouble sleeping after starting a CFTR modulator, tell your care team. Do not stop the medicine on your own [[FDA-Trikafta,Heo2022MentalStatus]].
- People with CF are asked to keep at least 6 feet (about 2 metres) from each other, because germs can spread between them [[Haggie2020,Saiman2014IPC]].
- Many people with CF meet others with CF online instead of in person [[Perkins2021SocialMedia]].
- Most men with CF are born without the tubes that carry sperm, but they usually make sperm. Doctors can collect it to help them become fathers [[Kazmerski2022Family]].
- Many women with CF can get pregnant, sometimes without planning to, especially after starting modulators. Talking about birth control and planning with your team is important [[Godfrey2025Contraception,Dacco2023]].
- If you are thinking about pregnancy, your CF team, a diabetes check and a genetic counsellor can help you plan [[Moran2010CFRD,Kazmerski2022Family]].
- A baby whose mother took a modulator may need an eye check and a gene test for CF, because the usual newborn blood test can miss CF [[Patel2023IRT,Jain2022Cataracts]].
- Moving from children's to adult CF care goes better when there is no long gap between visits [[Melton2024Transition]].
- Regular exercise helps fitness and is part of good CF care [[Radtke2022Cochrane,Southern2024Standards]].
- Palliative care means extra support for comfort and quality of life. It can start at any stage, not only near the end of life [[Kavalieratos2021Palliative]].
- Survival and outcome numbers come from large groups of people and are changing fast with new medicines. They cannot predict what will happen to any one person [[Schechter2021Depression,Frost2025Ageing]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Do CFTR modulators cause neuropsychiatric symptoms?
  - The labels carry a postmarketing warning, and a case series describes "mental fogginess" within 3 months [[FDA-Trikafta,Heo2022MentalStatus]].
  - A prospective cohort found overall mental-health improvement after ETI, with possible vulnerable subgroups (women, lower lung function, psychotropic use) [[vanderHeijden2025]].
  - Status: causality unresolved (Early evidence).
- Modulators in pregnancy and lactation.
  - Labels state limited or no human data, and ivacaftor causes cataracts in juvenile rats [[FDA-Trikafta,FDA-Alyftrek]].
  - Cohorts and case series are reassuring for maternal lung health and anomaly rates [[Jain2025MaternalOutcomes,Gautier2025,Wang2026Pregnancy]]. Infant cataracts and liver-test rises have been reported [[Jain2022Cataracts,Kolaczkowski2025]].
  - Status: awaiting the prospective MAYFLOWERS results (estimated primary completion 2027) [[NCT04828382]].
- How strict should social separation be?
  - Pro: a uniform, pathogen-based approach, at least 2 m apart, no in-person meetings [[Haggie2020]].
  - Con: adoption is uneven and people report barriers. Only 53% of programmes adopted the inpatient 6-foot rule [[Stoudemire2019IPC,Saiman2022Barriers]].
  - Status: guidance unchanged since 2013. How modulator-era changes in infection rates affect the rules is not settled.
- Simplifying treatment on ETI.
  - Stopping hypertonic saline or dornase alfa was non-inferior for ppFEV1 over 6 weeks [[MayerHamblett2023SIMPLIFY]].
  - Long-term effects are unknown, and adverse events were more common in the discontinuation arms [[MayerHamblett2023SIMPLIFY]].
  - Status: open.
- Males on modulators during assisted reproduction.
  - Survey data show no excess miscarriage or anomalies [[TaylorCousar2024Males]].
  - Avoiding any sperm exposure would mean about 80 days off therapy, with the risk of clinical decline [[Kazmerski2022Family]].
  - Status: no prospective data.
- Weight gain and cardiometabolic risk on ETI.
  - Rising overweight and obesity in registry and centre data [[Ostrenga2026Obesity,Bashir2026Obesity]]; case reports of new hypertension [[Gramegna2022Hypertension]].
  - Status: long-term cardiovascular consequences unknown (Hypothesis).
