# B · CFTR gene, inheritance, variant classes and genotype

Pass date: 2026-11-04. Modules served: gene, variants.

Modulator trial results, newborn screening and sweat-test details belong to passes F and E; they appear here only where they explain a genetic idea.

## Findings

### gene · The CFTR gene and its discovery
- In 1989 three back-to-back Science papers identified the CF gene. Rommens and colleagues used chromosome walking and jumping to clone more than 500,000 base pairs from the long arm of chromosome 7 and found that the CF gene spans about 250,000 base pairs of genomic DNA [[Rommens1989]].
- Riordan and colleagues isolated complementary DNA (cDNA) clones. They found transcripts of about 6,500 nucleotides in affected tissues and a predicted protein with two repeated motifs, each with a membrane-spanning domain and an ATP-binding domain [[Riordan1989]].
- The same cDNA paper found a three-base-pair deletion that removes a phenylalanine at the centre of the first predicted nucleotide-binding domain [[Riordan1989]].
- Kerem and colleagues reported that about 70% of CF mutations are this three-base-pair deletion, which removes phenylalanine 508 (ΔF508, now F508del) [[Kerem1989]].
- In the same paper, extended haplotype data suggested that the rest of the CF gene pool is made up of many different mutations. About 8% of mutant alleles might allow residual pancreatic function [[Kerem1989]].
- The full genomic sequence (1991) placed the gene at 7q31–q32 and found 27 exons, each flanked by consensus splice signals. The authors designed 28 primer sets to amplify individual exons for mutation detection [[Zielenski1991]].
- Current references place CFTR at chromosome 7q31.2, with 27 coding exons. The reference mRNA is NM_000492 and the protein is NP_000483. The OMIM numbers are 602421 for the gene and 219700 for CF [[Deignan2020ACMG]].
- One review gives the gene as about 190 kb of genomic DNA, transcribed into a 6.2 kb mRNA that is translated into a 1,480-amino-acid protein [[LopesPacheco2019]]. The 1989 chromosome-walking paper estimated about 250 kb [[Rommens1989]].
- CFTR belongs to the ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporter family but works as an ion channel. A 3.9 Å cryo-EM structure of dephosphorylated human CFTR showed a regulatory (R) domain helix docked inside the intracellular vestibule, blocking channel opening [[Liu2017Cryo]].
- The gene product is a cyclic-AMP-regulated chloride channel found almost only in secretory epithelial cells [[Zielenski1995]].
- CFTR transports chloride and bicarbonate. The Lancet seminar describes impaired mucus hydration and clearance as the downstream result of CFTR mutations (Established) [[Shteinberg2021]].

### gene · Inheritance (autosomal recessive)
- CF is autosomal recessive: disease needs disease-causing variants on both copies of CFTR [[Shteinberg2021,Deignan2020ACMG]].
- The ACMG technical standard states that carriers of a single pathogenic or likely pathogenic variant are expected to be asymptomatic [[Deignan2020ACMG]].
- When both parents are carriers, each pregnancy has a 1 in 4 (25%) chance of a child with two variants, a 2 in 4 (50%) chance of a carrier child and a 1 in 4 (25%) chance of a child with neither variant. This follows from Mendelian segregation of an autosomal recessive trait as stated by the sources [[Deignan2020ACMG,Shteinberg2021]].
- Carrier screening cannot detect new (de novo) variants in a child and does not replace newborn screening [[Gregg2021ACMG]].
- ACOG advises that when a person is found to be a carrier, they should be encouraged to tell relatives, and their reproductive partner should be offered testing. If both partners are carriers, genetic counselling should be offered [[ACOG2017CO691]].
- ACOG states that carrier screening information should be given to every pregnant woman, ideally before pregnancy, and that a patient may decline any or all screening [[ACOG2017CO691]].
- The ACMG standard says couples can be tested one after the other (usually the female partner first, the male partner only if she carries a variant) or both at once. Testing at the same time may be preferred during pregnancy [[Deignan2020ACMG]].

### variants · Carrier frequency
- Abeliovich 1992 screened a random sample of 424 Ashkenazi Jewish people for three mutations. They found 13 heterozygotes, predicting a carrier frequency of 1 in 29, which they noted is similar to Northern European populations [[Abeliovich1992]].
- In about 3 million US carrier screens with the 23-variant ACMG/ACOG panel, the observed carrier frequency was 1 in 37.6 across all ancestries. Against an estimated US pan-ethnic carrier frequency of 1 in 29, this is a 77% detection rate [[Strom2011]].
- The same laboratory observed carrier frequencies of 1 in 29 in people who self-identified as white and 1 in 27 in Ashkenazi Jewish people [[Strom2011]].
- With a 98-mutation panel in 364,890 people with no family history of CF, 1 in 38 carried one of the panel mutations. African American, Hispanic and Asian people made up 33% of those tested [[Rohlfs2011]].
- With a 69-mutation panel (n = 109,830), carrier frequencies were 1 in 36 overall, 1 in 27 in Caucasian people, 1 in 79 in African American people and 1 in 48 in Hispanic American people. The 32-mutation panel found 1 in 69 in Hispanic American people [[Zvereff2014]].
- The ACMG 2021 practice resource, citing the 32-variant data, gives a US range of 1 in 28 ("Caucasian") to 1 in 105 ("African American") and 1 in 261 ("Asian"). It notes these panel-based figures depend on which variants are tested [[Gregg2021ACMG]].
- In the All of Us whole-genome cohort (317,964 adults; 363 pathogenic variants), 3.62% of people genetically most similar to a European reference carried one pathogenic CFTR variant. The figures were 1.35% for people most similar to an African reference and 1.86% for an Admixed American reference [[Zeng2025Hetero]].
- In the ACMG 2021 Tier-3 gene table, CFTR has a maximum carrier frequency of 0.040972 (about 1 in 24) in any US subpopulation (gnomAD-based) [[Gregg2021ACMG]].
- More than 10 million people in the US are estimated to carry one pathogenic CFTR variant [[Zeng2025Hetero,Miller2020PNAS]].
- Poolman and Galvani describe the carrier frequency in people of European descent as "upwards of 2%" [[Poolman2007]].
- Across more than 80 world regions, there is a significant positive correlation between a region's F508del frequency and its CF incidence [[Bobadilla2002]].
- Estimated US birth prevalence (2004) was 1 in 2,500 in non-Hispanic Caucasian, 1 in 2,270 in Ashkenazi Jewish, 1 in 13,500 in Hispanic Caucasian, 1 in 15,100 in African American and 1 in 35,100 in Asian American births. These are population estimates [[Palomaki2004]].
- The ACMG 2020 standard gives the prevalence of classic CF as about 1 in 2,500 to 3,300 live births in Caucasians [[Deignan2020ACMG]].

### variants · Carrier screening: guidelines and panels
- In 2001, on joint ACMG and ACOG recommendation, CF became the first condition for which pan-ethnic universal carrier screening by molecular methods was recommended. The core panel had 25 variants with an allele frequency of at least 0.1% in the US general population [[Deignan2020ACMG]].
- In 2004 the ACMG removed two variants from the panel. 1078delT was dropped (0.03% of CF chromosomes) and so was I148T, whose disease association was explained by a second variant, 3199del6. This left the "ACMG-23" panel [[Watson2004ACMG,Deignan2020ACMG]].
- The 2004 revision kept R117H but stressed testing for the intron 8 (now intron 9) 5T variant only as a reflex when R117H is found [[Watson2004ACMG]].
- In the CF patient data compiled for the 2004 revision (Table 1: CFF and Cystic Fibrosis Consortium data), the 25-variant panel covered 88.40% of CF alleles in non-Hispanic Caucasian people, 94.14% in Ashkenazi Jewish, 71.90% in Hispanic Caucasian, 64.51% in African American and 48.93% in Asian American people, and 84.00% across all ancestries [[Watson2004ACMG]].
- Estimated clinical sensitivity (the share of carrier couples detected) with the panel was 88.6% for Ashkenazi Jewish, 71.9% for non-Hispanic Caucasian, 51.7% for Hispanic Caucasian, 41.6% for African American and 23.4% for Asian American couples [[Palomaki2004]].
- The number of couples needed to screen to detect one affected fetus was about 2,600 (Ashkenazi Jewish), 3,200 (non-Hispanic Caucasian), 26,120 (Hispanic), 36,040 (African American) and 129,600 (Asian American) [[Palomaki2004]].
- In one laboratory, prenatal diagnosis identified 1 affected fetus per 25,000 carrier screens [[Strom2011]].
- The ACMG 2020 technical standard lets laboratories use either targeted testing (the ACMG-23 as a minimum) or "classification-based reporting". The second option sequences at least all coding exons and ±2 bp splice junctions and reports every pathogenic and likely pathogenic variant for classic CF [[Deignan2020ACMG]].
- The ACMG 2020 standard recommends always reporting two intronic variants from the original panel: c.2657+5G>A (legacy 2789+5G>A) and c.3718–2477C>T (legacy 3849+10kbC>T) [[Deignan2020ACMG]].
- Deletions and duplications make up an estimated <5% of detected CFTR variants. The ACMG says this may be an underestimate [[Deignan2020ACMG]].
- The ACMG 2021 practice resource defines four tiers. Tier 1 is CF and SMA screening regardless of ancestry. Tier 2 covers conditions with carrier frequency ≥1/100. Tier 3 covers ≥1/200. Tier 4 has no lower limit [[Gregg2021ACMG]].
- The ACMG 2021 recommendation is that all pregnant patients and those planning a pregnancy should be offered Tier 3 carrier screening, which includes CFTR. The ACMG does not recommend offering only Tier 1 or Tier 2, "because these do not provide equitable evaluation of all racial/ethnic groups" [[Gregg2021ACMG]].
- The ACMG 2021 resource recommends that carrier screening be "ethnic and population neutral" [[Gregg2021ACMG]].
- The ACMG published updated recommendations for CFTR carrier screening as a 2023 position statement. Its content was not accessible for this pass (unverified content) [[Deignan2023ACMG]].
- People of Hispanic, Black or Asian ancestry in the US CF registry were less likely to have two identified CFTR variants, and more likely to carry no variant on the 23-variant panel, than white people with CF (P < 0.0001 for Hispanic and Black; P = 0.003 for Asian) [[Schrijver2016]].
- In 140 nonwhite people with CF and uncharacterized genotypes, deletions and duplications accounted for 17% of unidentified alleles [[Schrijver2016]].
- In Hispanic people with CF, five variants not on the ACMG/ACOG panel (3876delA, W1089X, R1066C, S549N, 1949del84) accounted for 7.55% detection. In African American people with CF, A559T and 711+5G>A together accounted for 3.71% [[Sugarman2004]].

### variants · How many variants, and which cause CF
- Almost 2,000 variants had been reported to the Cystic Fibrosis Mutation Database by 2015 [[Cutting2015]]. A 2016 review states more than 2,000 [[Veit2016]].
- Cutting 2015 classified the database variants by type: 40% single amino acid substitutions, 36% RNA-processing changes (nonsense, frameshift, splicing), 3% large rearrangements, 1% promoter changes, 14% apparently neutral and 6% unclear [[Cutting2015]].
- A later count of 2,075 reported variants gives 38.9% missense, 16.1% frameshift, 11.1% splicing, 8.4% nonsense, 2.1% in-frame and 2.8% large deletions or insertions, 0.9% promoter and 13.0% possibly non-pathogenic [[LopesPacheco2019]].
- The CFTR2 project (Clinical and Functional Translation of CFTR) collected genotype and phenotype data on 39,696 people with CF in North America and Europe. In this group, 159 variants each had an allele frequency of at least 0.01% and together made up 96% of CF alleles [[Sosnay2013CFTR2]].
- Of those 159 variants, 127 (80%) met both clinical and functional criteria for causing CF. Penetrance analysis in 2,188 fathers of people with CF classed 12 of the remaining 32 as neutral, while 20 stayed indeterminate [[Sosnay2013CFTR2]].
- CFTR2 called a variant disease-causing on clinical criteria if mean sweat chloride in at least 3 carriers was ≥60 mmol/L. Below 10% of wild-type CFTR function was used as a conservative threshold for CF features [[Sosnay2013CFTR2]].
- By the 2020 ACMG standard, more than 1,600 of about 2,000 reported variants occur among more than 89,000 CFTR2 participants. About 5% cannot be assigned as disease-causing or not, and some of these are labelled "variants of varying clinical consequence" [[Deignan2020ACMG]].
- One 2019 review reports 432 variants annotated in CFTR2: 352 with confirmed disease liability and 46 with variable clinical consequences [[LopesPacheco2019]]. CFTR2 is updated over time, so current counts differ.
- The CFTR2 categories are CF-causing, varying clinical consequence and non-CF-causing. The ACMG adds that "non-CF-causing" does not rule out a CFTR-related disorder such as male infertility or pancreatitis [[Deignan2020ACMG]].
- The CF Foundation 2017 diagnosis guideline recommends using the latest CFTR2 variant classifications to help with diagnosis [[Farrell2017Dx]].
- The ACMG warns laboratories to be cautious with ClinVar assertions for CFTR variants made outside CFTR2, because many lack functional assessment [[Deignan2020ACMG]].

### variants · F508del and the other common variants
- F508del (HGVS c.1521_1523delCTT, p.Phe508del) is the most common CF-causing variant [[Farrell2018Age,Cutting2015]].
- About 70% of CF chromosomes worldwide carry F508del [[Morral1994,Kerem1989]].
- In 27,177 CF chromosomes from 29 European and 3 North African countries, F508del made up 66.8% of alleles. It ranged from 87.2% in Denmark to 26.3% in Algeria [[Estivill1997]].
- In the US (CFF data, 2004), F508del made up 72.42% of CF chromosomes in non-Hispanic Caucasian people, 54.38% in Hispanic Caucasian, 44.07% in African American, 38.95% in Asian American and 31.41% in Ashkenazi Jewish people, and 66.31% across all ancestries [[Watson2004ACMG]].
- "Nearly 90%" of people with CF have at least one F508del copy [[Middleton2019ETI]]. A 2019 review gives about 82% of the CF population [[LopesPacheco2019]]. The two figures come from different populations and denominators.
- In a 1990 Toronto cohort of 293 people with CF, F508del allele prevalence was 71%. Of the people, 52% were homozygous, 40% heterozygous and 8% had other genotypes [[Kerem1990NEJM]].
- In Asia, F508del accounts for 12–31% of CF alleles, and it was not seen on CF alleles of East Asian ancestry [[Bell2020Commission]].
- The other most common European variants were G542X (2.6%), N1303K (1.6%), G551D (1.5%) and W1282X (1.0%) [[Estivill1997]].
- G542X has a mean frequency of 6.1% in Mediterranean countries. N1303K peaks in Tunisia (17.2%). W1282X peaks in Israel (36.2%) [[Estivill1997]].
- W1282X was the most common CF mutation in Ashkenazi Jewish people with CF in Israel, found in 60% of Ashkenazi CF chromosomes, against 23% for F508del [[Shoshani1992]].
- In US Ashkenazi Jewish people with CF, W1282X made up 45.92% of CF chromosomes, G542X 7.55% and 3849+10kbC>T 4.77% [[Watson2004ACMG]].
- Five variants (F508del, G542X, W1282X, N1303K, 3849+10kbC→T) accounted for 97% of CF alleles in Ashkenazi Jewish people in Israel. In Jewish people of Sephardic or Oriental origin, F508del was the only one of the 11 tested variants found (43%) [[Abeliovich1992]].
- 3120+1G>T made up 9.57% of CF chromosomes in African American people with CF but ≤0.16% in other US groups [[Watson2004ACMG]].
- 3849+10kbC>T made up 5.31% of CF chromosomes in Asian American people in the 2004 CFF dataset [[Watson2004ACMG]].
- R334W made up 1.78% of CF chromosomes in Hispanic Caucasian people against 0.14% in non-Hispanic Caucasian people [[Watson2004ACMG]].
- G551D, the variant behind the first CFTR potentiator trial, made up 2.25% of non-Hispanic Caucasian CF chromosomes and 1.93% across all US ancestries [[Watson2004ACMG]].
- In a Toronto pancreatic cohort of 742 people, the most common alleles were F508del (71.3%), G551D (2.9%), G542X (2.3%), 621+1G→T (1.2%) and W1282X (1.2%) [[Ahmed2003Gut]].
- Some variants cluster regionally: 394delTT in northern Europe (1.1–28.8%), R117H in northwestern Europe (1.3–3.0%), R553X in central Europe and 1717-1G→A in Belgium and France [[Estivill1997]].
- CFTRdele2,3(21kb) deletes 21,080 bp, removing exons 2 and 3 and creating a premature stop in exon 4. It is a severe, Slavic-origin variant, found in 6.4% of Czech, 5.2% of Russian and 1.5% of German and Polish CF chromosomes [[Dork2000]].
- About 10% of people with CF worldwide carry a premature termination codon (PTC) variant. G542X (2.5%) and W1282X (1.2%) are the most common, as percentages of CF alleles [[LopesPacheco2019,Kerem2014Ataluren]].
- 3849+10kbC→T (HGVS c.3718–2477C>T) is a deep-intron change that creates an 84-base-pair pseudo-exon with an in-frame stop codon. About 8% of transcripts are normally spliced. It was first found in people with CF-like lung disease but normal sweat chloride [[Highsmith1994,Deignan2020ACMG]].

### variants · Nomenclature
- Variant names follow HGVS recommendations (version 15.11 in the 2016 update), maintained by the Human Genome Variation Society, the Human Variome Project and HUGO [[denDunnen2016]].
- CFTR has two naming systems. Legacy names (F508del, G542X, 3849+10kbC→T) come from older numbering. HGVS names use cDNA numbering: for example c.1521_1523delCTT (p.Phe508del), c.2657+5G>A (2789+5G>A), c.3718–2477C>T (3849+10kbC>T) and c.350G>A (p.Arg117His, R117H) [[Deignan2020ACMG,Farrell2018Age]].
- The intron that holds the polyT/TG tract is "intron 8" in legacy numbering and "intron 9" in current numbering: c.1210–12T[5/7/9] and c.1210–34TG[11/12/13] [[Deignan2020ACMG,Chillon1995]].

### variants · Variant classes I–VI (and VII)
- The functional class system began with Welsh and Smith (1993), who grouped CFTR mutations by how they disrupt the chloride channel [[Welsh1993]].
- By 1995, many of the more than 550 known mutations could be grouped into five general classes by their molecular consequences [[Zielenski1995]].
- Class I: no protein, or truncated protein. Causes include nonsense mutations (premature stop codons), frameshifts and canonical splice-site changes, and premature-stop transcripts also undergo nonsense-mediated mRNA decay. Examples: G542X, W1282X [[LopesPacheco2019]].
- Class II: the protein misfolds, is held by endoplasmic-reticulum quality control and is degraded by proteasomes. F508del is the main example. Others listed are G85E, I507del, R560T and N1303K (0.4%, 0.5%, 0.2% and 1.6% of CF alleles) [[LopesPacheco2019,Bell2020Commission]].
- Class III: gating defect. The protein reaches the membrane but does not open properly. Example: G551D (Gly551Asp) [[Bell2020Commission,LopesPacheco2019]].
- Class IV: conductance defect, with reduced ion flow through an open channel. Example: R117H (Arg117His) [[Bell2020Commission]].
- Class V: reduced amount of normal CFTR, from alternative splicing, promoter or missense changes [[LopesPacheco2019,Bell2020Commission]].
- Class VI: unstable protein at the plasma membrane, with faster endocytosis and less recycling. Corrector-rescued F508del behaves like a class VI protein [[Bell2020Commission,LopesPacheco2019]].
- A seventh class has been proposed for large deletions that abolish CFTR mRNA production, where no protein-targeted drug can act (Hypothesis / proposed classification) [[LopesPacheco2019,DeBoeck2016]].
- Many variants cause more than one defect. F508del causes misfolding and degradation, and the small amount that reaches the membrane is unstable and gates poorly. The authors note F508del "could be assigned to at least three classes" [[Cutting2015]].
- Veit and colleagues proposed an expanded "combinatorial" classification that records each defect a variant causes, to guide combination drug therapy [[Veit2016]].
- Bell et al. state that class I–III variants cause little or no CFTR function and a more severe phenotype, including exocrine pancreatic insufficiency. Class IV–V variants keep residual function, often with preserved pancreatic function early in life [[Bell2020Commission]].

### variants · Minimal function, residual function and theratypes
- "Minimal function" and "residual function" are genotype groups used in trials and labels. Lopes-Pacheco describes classes I and II as minimal function and classes IV, V and VI as residual function [[LopesPacheco2019]].
- The phase 3 elexacaftor–tezacaftor–ivacaftor trial enrolled people who had F508del on one allele and a minimal-function variant on the other (F508del–minimal function genotype), a group that earlier modulators did not help. The qualifying variants were listed in a supplementary table [[Middleton2019ETI]].
- Residual function was defined in one cohort as carrying at least one partial-function variant (class IV or V). Minimal-function individuals had a higher hazard of acquiring 9 of 13 respiratory organisms (HR 1.7 to 3.78) and acquired 12 of 13 organisms at younger ages [[Green2010Residual]].
- Theratypes are defined as "a recently invented term used to classify disease-associated DNA variants according to the molecular-based treatment to which they respond" [[Cutting2015]].
- Theratyping uses CFTR modulators in laboratory models to define a variant's defect and predict drug response. The models include Fischer rat thyroid (FRT) cell lines, human bronchial and nasal epithelial cells and rectal organoids [[Clancy2019]].
- In vitro, ivacaftor potentiated several of 54 missense CFTR forms in FRT cells, including variants with mild processing or conductance defects [[VanGoor2014]].
- In 2017 the FDA expanded the ivacaftor label to rare variants on the basis of in vitro FRT assay data instead of clinical trial data [[Durmowicz2018]].
- The Kalydeco label calls a variant responsive on clinical response and/or FRT data showing that ivacaftor raises chloride transport by at least 10% over baseline (as % of normal) [[FDA-Kalydeco]].
- The Trikafta label's indication covers people aged 2 years and older with at least one CFTR variant that is responsive on clinical and/or in vitro data, or that "results in production of CFTR protein". It gives a non-exhaustive list of responsive variants and notes that N1303K is predicted responsive by HBE assay [[FDA-Trikafta]].
- The Alyftrek (vanzacaftor–tezacaftor–deutivacaftor) label uses the same structure for people aged 6 and older. It lists N1303K as responsive "only by HBE assay" [[FDA-Alyftrek]].
- In FRT cells, 417 CFTR variants were tested against vanzacaftor–tezacaftor–ivacaftor. About 76% reached the ≥10%-of-wild-type responsiveness threshold, as did 50 variants previously non-responsive or borderline to elexacaftor–tezacaftor–ivacaftor (Early evidence: in vitro) [[Bihler2026]].
- G551D shows how a theratype works. In people aged 12 and older with at least one G551D allele, ivacaftor raised percent-predicted FEV1 by 10.6 points over placebo through week 24 and lowered sweat chloride by 48.1 mmol/L through week 48 [[Ramsey2011STRIVE]].
- The ataluren read-through trial for nonsense variants missed its primary endpoint: relative FEV1 change at 48 weeks was −2.5% versus −5.5%, a difference of 3.0% (95% CI −0.8 to 6.3; p = 0.12) [[Kerem2014Ataluren]].
- FRT data have predicted response for many missense variants. However, G970R response in vitro did not match clinical response to ivacaftor [[Clancy2019]].

### variants · Genotype–phenotype correlation
- Pancreatic status follows genotype closely. In a 1990 cohort, pancreatic insufficiency was present in 99% of F508del homozygotes, 72% of heterozygotes and 36% of people with other genotypes [[Kerem1990NEJM]].
- In 742 people with CF, more than 95% of those with class I, II or III variants were pancreatic insufficient or became so, whereas class IV and V variants were consistently pancreatic sufficient. In all but 4 cases, genotype correlated exclusively with pancreatic phenotype [[Ahmed2003Gut]].
- In a 14-country matched study, R117H/F508del compound heterozygotes were more often pancreatic sufficient than F508del homozygotes (87% vs 4%). They were diagnosed older (mean 10.2 vs 2.5 years) and had lower sweat chloride (80 vs 108 mmol/L) [[CFGPC1993]].
- The same consortium concluded that for none of the genotypes studied could predictions be made about the severity or course of pulmonary disease [[CFGPC1993]].
- In 15,651 people in the US CF registry (1993–2002), a "high-risk" genotype carried a relative risk of death of 2.25 (95% CI 1.77–2.84) against a "low-risk" genotype [[McKone2006]].
- In the same registry cohort, median age at death was 24.2 years for high-risk genotypes and 37.6 years for low-risk genotypes. These are registry figures from before modulators, not individual predictions [[McKone2006]].
- Among people with CF who are pancreatic sufficient, genotypes with mild pancreatic-insufficiency-prevalence (PIP) scores carried more pancreatitis risk: 50% versus 27% cumulative risk by age 50 (HR 2.4; 95% CI 1.3–4.5) [[Ooi2011Pancreatitis]].
- An ECFS-linked consensus states that genotype "does not accurately predict individual outcome". It does not recommend using CFTR genotype to predict prognosis at diagnosis [[Castellani2008Genotype]].
- Diagnosis rests on clinical presentation and CFTR function. The CF Foundation recommends confirming any CFTR-associated diagnosis with a sweat chloride test [[Farrell2017Dx,Castellani2008Genotype]].
- Residual-function variants can cause milder disease, sometimes affecting a single organ [[Shteinberg2021]].
- R117H's effect depends on the polyT tract on the same chromosome. R117H with 5T in cis plus a CF-causing variant generally gives nonclassic CF. R117H with 7T may range from no symptoms to CAVD, and very rarely nonclassic CF [[Deignan2020ACMG,Kiesewetter1993]].
- In France, the penetrance of classical CF for [R117H;T7]+[F508del] was estimated at 0.03%, and that of severe adult CF at 0.06%. Only about 3.1% of the estimated 3,650 people with this genotype were known to have CF-related symptoms [[ThauvinRobinet2009]].

### variants · Modifier genes and environment
- Lung disease severity correlates poorly with CFTR genotype. In twin and sibling studies, monozygotic twins had higher lung-function correlations (0.82–0.91) than dizygotic twins and siblings (0.50–0.64), and heritability estimates ranged from 0.54 to 1.0 [[Vanscoy2007]].
- Genetic factors explained 50% of lung-function variation, unique environmental or stochastic factors 36% and shared environment 14% (134 monozygotic twins; 272 dizygotic twins and siblings) [[Collaco2010Env]].
- Secondhand smoke exposure at home (23.2% of 812 participants) was linked to a 9.8 percentile-point lower cross-sectional lung function. The effect was larger in people with certain TGFB1 genotypes: 22.7 points in −509 TT homozygotes [[Collaco2008Smoke]].
- Meconium ileus affects about 15% of newborns with CF. Monozygotic twins were much more concordant for meconium ileus than dizygotic twins and siblings (P = 1×10⁻⁵), showing that modifier genes contribute [[Blackman2006MI]].
- Diabetes in CF was concordant in 0.73 of monozygotic twin pairs versus 0.18 of dizygotic twins and siblings, with heritability "near one" (95% CI 0.42–1.0) [[Blackman2009CFRD]].
- A genome-wide association study in 1,978 F508del homozygotes found a lung-severity modifier near EHF and APIP (chromosome 11p13; P = 3.34×10⁻⁸) and a linkage peak at 20q13.2 [[Wright2011GWAS]].
- A GWAS meta-analysis of 6,365 people with CF found five lung-disease modifier loci: MUC4/MUC20, SLC9A3, HLA class II, AGTR2/SLC6A14 and EHF/APIP [[Corvol2015]].
- Type 2 diabetes risk genes (TCF7L2, CDKAL1, CDKN2A/B, IGF2BP2) modify age at onset of CF-related diabetes [[Cutting2015]].

### variants · CFTR-related disorders and carriers
- A CFTR-related disorder (CFTR-RD) is "a clinical entity associated with CFTR dysfunction that does not fulfil diagnostic criteria for CF". Recognised CFTR-RDs are CBAVD, acute recurrent or chronic pancreatitis and disseminated bronchiectasis [[Bombieri2011]].
- In 102 men with CBAVD, 19 had CFTR variants on both copies. Fifty-four had one variant, and 63% of those had 5T on the other gene. The 5T allele was found in about 5% of the general population [[Chillon1995]].
- A meta-analysis found at least one CFTR variant in 78% of men with CBAVD (46% two, 28% one). The pooled odds ratio for CBAVD was 9.25 for 5T and 19.43 for 5T with (TG)12–13 [[Yu2012CBAVD]].
- About 10% of the general population carries 5T. When 5T sits in trans with a severe variant, 12 or 13 TG repeats next to 5T made an abnormal phenotype much more likely than 11 TG repeats (OR 34.0; 95% CI 11.1–103.7) [[Groman2004]].
- In 134 people with chronic pancreatitis, 13.4% carried one CFTR mutation versus 5.3% of 600 controls (P < 0.001), and 10.4% carried 5T [[Sharer1998]].
- In 27 people with idiopathic pancreatitis, 37% had at least one abnormal CFTR allele (prevalence ratio 11:1). Three had both alleles affected (prevalence ratio 80:1) [[Cohn1998]].
- In a US claims database, 19,802 CF carriers had higher odds than matched controls for 57 of 59 CF-related conditions. Examples include male infertility (OR 5.09), chronic pancreatitis (OR 6.76), bronchiectasis (OR 5.62) and nontuberculous mycobacterial infection (OR 2.75). Individual-level risk stayed low for most conditions [[Miller2020PNAS]].
- In the All of Us cohort, no phenome-wide association reached significance in CF heterozygotes. Among 52 CF-associated conditions, modest associations were seen for ABPA (OR 2.50), bronchiectasis (OR 1.21) and COPD (OR 1.14) [[Zeng2025Hetero]].
- CRMS/CFSPID is the label for infants with a positive newborn screen but inconclusive sweat and genetic testing. In 2015 the CF Foundation and ECFS definitions were merged [[Ren2017CRMS,Farrell2017Dx]].
- Where gene sequencing is not used in newborn screening, there are about 3 to 5 cases of CF for every case of CRMS/CFSPID. Most infants with CRMS/CFSPID do not develop CF, but 10–20% may develop features of concern such as Pseudomonas aeruginosa in airway cultures [[Ren2017CRMS]].
- Updated ECFS guidance recommends a detailed assessment in the sixth year of life, including lung function and imaging. Most children with CRMS/CFSPID stay healthy and do not convert to a CF diagnosis [[Barben2021]].

### variants · Founder effects and selection hypotheses
- Microsatellite haplotypes suggested in 1994 that F508del arose more than 52,000 years ago and spread through Europe in separate expansions (Hypothesis) [[Morral1994]].
- A 2018 family study of 190 patients estimated the age of the most recent common ancestor of F508del at 4,600–4,725 years in northwestern Europe and about 1,000 years in southeastern populations. The authors propose an early Bronze Age origin and spread by Bell Beaker migrations (Hypothesis) [[Farrell2018Age]].
- The high carrier frequency has prompted the idea that carriers had a survival advantage (heterozygote advantage). This remains unproven (Hypothesis) [[Farrell2018Age,Poolman2007]].
- Cholera hypothesis: in CF mice, heterozygotes had about 50% of normal intestinal CFTR and secreted about 50% of normal fluid and chloride in response to cholera toxin (Hypothesis; animal model) [[Gabriel1994]].
- Against the cholera hypothesis: by intestinal perfusion in humans, CF heterozygotes secreted chloride at the same rate as non-carriers (Hypothesis challenged) [[Hogenauer2000]].
- Typhoid hypothesis: Salmonella typhi uses CFTR to enter intestinal cells, and heterozygous F508del mice translocated 86% fewer S. typhi into the submucosa than wild-type mice (Hypothesis; animal and cell data) [[Pier1998]].
- A population-genetic model found that neither cholera nor typhoid gave enough historical selective pressure to explain modern CF incidence, whereas tuberculosis from the 17th century could have (Hypothesis) [[Poolman2007]].
- Founder effects explain regional clusters: for example W1282X in Ashkenazi Jewish people [[Shoshani1992]] and CFTRdele2,3(21kb) in Slavic populations, each traced to a common haplotype [[Dork2000]] (Established for these examples).

### Exam hooks
- CFTR sits on chromosome 7q31.2, has 27 exons and encodes a 1,480-amino-acid ABC-family protein that works as a chloride and bicarbonate channel [[Deignan2020ACMG,LopesPacheco2019]].
- The CF gene was identified in 1989 by positional cloning, in three Science papers by Rommens, Riordan and Kerem [[Rommens1989,Riordan1989,Kerem1989]].
- F508del is a 3-bp deletion (c.1521_1523delCTT) that removes phenylalanine 508 in NBD1. It accounts for about 70% of CF alleles worldwide [[Kerem1989,Morral1994,Farrell2018Age]].
- When two carriers have a child, the chances per pregnancy are 25% affected, 50% carrier and 25% non-carrier (autosomal recessive) [[Deignan2020ACMG]].
- Class I = no protein (nonsense, e.g., G542X). Class II = misfolding (F508del). Class III = gating (G551D). Class IV = conductance (R117H). Class V = reduced amount. Class VI = unstable at the membrane [[Bell2020Commission,LopesPacheco2019]].
- Pancreatic status correlates strongly with genotype: class I–III variants mean more than 95% pancreatic insufficient, while class IV–V variants are pancreatic sufficient [[Ahmed2003Gut]].
- Lung disease correlates poorly with genotype. About half of lung-function variation is genetic (modifier genes) and half environmental or stochastic [[Collaco2010Env,CFGPC1993]].
- W1282X is the most common CF variant in Ashkenazi Jewish people, and 3120+1G>T is common in African American people with CF [[Shoshani1992,Watson2004ACMG]].
- CF was the first condition for which pan-ethnic universal carrier screening was recommended (ACMG/ACOG 2001). The panel went from 25 variants to the ACMG-23 in 2004 [[Deignan2020ACMG,Watson2004ACMG]].
- ACMG 2021 recommends Tier 3 carrier screening (carrier frequency ≥1/200), which includes CFTR, for all pregnant patients and those planning pregnancy [[Gregg2021ACMG]].
- R117H's effect depends on the polyT tract in cis: 5T is pathogenic in a way that depends on the TG repeat, and TG12/13 is riskier than TG11 [[Deignan2020ACMG,Groman2004]].
- The commonest cause of CBAVD is a CF-causing variant on one CFTR copy plus 5T on the other [[Chillon1995]].
- A CFTR-related disorder is CFTR dysfunction that does not meet CF criteria: CBAVD, pancreatitis or disseminated bronchiectasis [[Bombieri2011]].
- 3849+10kbC→T can cause CF-like lung disease with normal sweat chloride [[Highsmith1994]].
- A theratype groups variants by the drug they respond to. FDA ivacaftor label expansions used an in vitro FRT threshold of ≥10% of normal chloride transport [[Cutting2015,FDA-Kalydeco,Durmowicz2018]].
- CRMS/CFSPID describes a positive newborn screen with inconclusive testing. Most of these children stay well [[Ren2017CRMS,Barben2021]].

### In plain words
- Cystic fibrosis comes from changes in one gene, called CFTR. You need two changed copies, one from each parent, to have CF [[Deignan2020ACMG]].
- Being a carrier means you have one changed copy and one working copy. It is very common, it is no one's fault, and carriers are usually healthy [[Deignan2020ACMG,Zeng2025Hetero]].
- When both parents are carriers, each pregnancy has a 1 in 4 chance of a child with CF, a 2 in 4 chance of a carrier child and a 1 in 4 chance of a child with neither change. Chance has no memory: each pregnancy starts fresh [[Deignan2020ACMG]].
- There are more than 2,000 known changes in the CFTR gene. Some cause CF, some cause milder problems and some are harmless [[Cutting2015,Deignan2020ACMG]].
- The most common change is called F508del. Most people with CF in Europe and North America have at least one copy [[Middleton2019ETI]].
- Different gene changes break the CFTR protein in different ways. Some make no protein, some make a misfolded protein and some make a protein that does not open well [[LopesPacheco2019]].
- Some medicines work only for certain gene changes. That is why knowing both CFTR changes matters [[Cutting2015,FDA-Trikafta]].
- Your genes do not decide everything. Other genes and your surroundings, like smoke exposure, also shape how CF affects the lungs [[Collaco2010Env,Collaco2008Smoke]].
- Carrier screening is a choice. It can help couples learn their chances before or during pregnancy, and anyone may decline it [[ACOG2017CO691]].
- Screening tests look for many changes but not all of them, so a negative result lowers the chance of being a carrier but does not make it zero [[Gregg2021ACMG,Palomaki2004]].
- Some babies have a positive newborn screen but unclear follow-up tests. Most of these children stay healthy and are checked over time [[Barben2021]].
- Gene results for CF are a learning tool here. They are not a diagnosis or a treatment plan for any one person [[Castellani2008Genotype]].

## Open questions and controversies
- How old is F508del? Morral 1994 dates it to more than 52,000 years [[Morral1994]], whereas Farrell 2018 estimates the most recent common ancestor at about 4,600–4,725 years in northwestern Europe [[Farrell2018Age]]. Status: the methods differ (haplotype diversity versus family-based tMRCA). Farrell's younger date is newer but not settled.
- Why are CF carriers so common? Cholera resistance has mouse support [[Gabriel1994]] but no human support [[Hogenauer2000]]. Typhoid has cell and mouse support [[Pier1998]]. A modelling study argues that only tuberculosis was strong enough [[Poolman2007]]. Status: all remain hypotheses.
- Do carriers have health effects? A large claims study reports raised odds for 57 of 59 CF-related conditions [[Miller2020PNAS]], whereas a whole-genome cohort found no phenome-wide significant associations and only modest respiratory signals [[Zeng2025Hetero]]. Status: unresolved. The designs differ (claims-identified carriers, likely tested because of symptoms, versus population sequencing).
- Carrier screening panel versus sequencing: targeted 23-variant panels detect far fewer carriers in non-European groups [[Watson2004ACMG,Palomaki2004,Schrijver2016]]. ACMG now allows classification-based reporting with full-exon sequencing [[Deignan2020ACMG]] and recommends ancestry-neutral Tier 3 screening [[Gregg2021ACMG]]. A 2011 analysis argued that expanded panels would add little [[Strom2011]]. Status: practice is moving to sequencing. ACMG's 2023 update [[Deignan2023ACMG]] was not accessed.
- Should R117H stay on screening panels? Thauvin-Robinet argue it should be withdrawn because of very low penetrance (0.03% for classical CF with T7) [[ThauvinRobinet2009]], whereas the ACMG kept it with reflex polyT testing [[Watson2004ACMG,Deignan2020ACMG]].
- Class system versus combinatorial or theratype classification: classes I–VI help teaching, but many variants have several defects [[Cutting2015,Veit2016]]. A class VII for mRNA-null large deletions was proposed [[DeBoeck2016,LopesPacheco2019]]. Theratyping based on drug response is increasingly used for eligibility [[Clancy2019,FDA-Trikafta]]. Status: the class labels remain in teaching, while labels use responsiveness lists.
- Can in vitro data alone predict clinical benefit? The FDA accepted FRT data for ivacaftor label expansion [[Durmowicz2018]], but discordant cases such as G970R exist [[Clancy2019]]. In vitro data now suggest broader responsiveness to vanzacaftor–tezacaftor–ivacaftor (Early evidence) [[Bihler2026]].
- Long-term outcome of CRMS/CFSPID: most children stay well, but 10–20% develop concerning features and some convert to CF. Lifetime CFTR-RD risk is unknown [[Ren2017CRMS,Barben2021]].
