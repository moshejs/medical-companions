Part 3 · Screening, diagnosis & monitoring

Newborn screening & diagnosis

A few drops of blood from a baby's heel start a chain of tests: a protein called IRT, a search for CFTR gene variants, sometimes a read of the whole gene, and then a sweat test. Most babies who screen positive do not have CF. Follow one stream of newborns through each step and see where they leave it.

Step 1 · the first days of life

A few drops of blood

Every newborn in the US and the UK has a few drops of blood dried on a card. CF screening became possible in 1979, when Crossley, Elliott and Smith in New Zealand set up a dried-blood-spot assay for immunoreactive trypsinogen (IRT), a pancreatic protein that is raised in babies with CF in the first months of life. Screening has been universal in the US since 2010 and across the UK since 2007; in Europe in 2016 there were 17 national programmes and 25 countries not screening. 19,78,285,403

In plain wordsA nurse pricks the baby's heel and puts a few drops of blood on a card. The card goes to a laboratory that checks it for several conditions, CF among them. 285,403
Exam hookThe first CF newborn screen was dried-blood-spot IRT (Crossley, 1979). 78

Does finding CF early help?

The Wisconsin CF Neonatal Screening Project randomised 650,341 newborns between 1985 and 1994 to early diagnosis by screening or to standard diagnosis. Screening used IRT, with DNA testing for F508del added from 1991. 121,122 Over 13 years the screened group grew significantly better despite similar nutritional therapy, and the authors found that catch-up growth after a late diagnosis may not be possible. 122

Mean age at diagnosis: 12 weeks with screening, 72 weeks in the control group (diagnosis confirmed by sweat test). 121

Growth

Established benefit

A Cochrane review of two trials (1,124,483 neonates, 210 with CF): controls had odds ratios of 4.12 (95% CI 1.64–10.38) for weight below the 10th percentile and 4.62 (1.69–12.61) for height. Established 436

Lungs

Probable, confounded

The Wisconsin trial found no pulmonary benefit by school age; after age 10, screened children had worse chest radiograph scores linked to earlier P. aeruginosa. 123 In UK registry data, screening went with a higher lung function trajectory (+1.56 FEV1 percentage points, 95% CI 0.1–3.02) and later chronic P. aeruginosa. Early evidence 403

Not a clean slate

Lung disease starts early

Of 57 Australian infants diagnosed by screening (median age 3.6 months), 84.2% had no respiratory symptoms, yet 21.1% had bacterial infection on lavage and 80.7% had an abnormal chest CT. 425

What the sketch shows

At California's prevalence of 1 in 6899, 100,000 births include about 14 babies with CF. A screen with 92% sensitivity finds about 13 and misses about 1, who still need a clinical diagnosis if symptoms appear. At a PPV of 34%, about 39 babies are referred for a sweat test; the 26 without CF are mostly carriers or babies with CRMS/CFSPID. This is arithmetic on published averages, not a forecast for any programme.

In plain wordsA newborn screen can miss CF. If a child has symptoms, CF should still be checked, even after a normal screen. 375

Diagnostic criteria: the CF Foundation 2017 guideline

The 2017 guideline came from 32 experts in 9 countries, with ≥80% agreement required for each statement; 27 of 28 statements were approved. 128,129 Its full text was paywalled for our research pass, so what follows is taken from its abstract and companion papers and from an open-access review that quotes it; statement numbers are not given.

Testing CFTR function. It recommends that CF be diagnosed at all ages by testing CFTR function with a sweat chloride test, and that CFTR2 variant classifications be used to aid diagnosis. 128

Sweat chloride bands, all ages including infants: ≥60 mmol/L diagnostic of CF; 30–59 mmol/L intermediate; <30 mmol/L negative (CF unlikely). 94,128 The update lowered the lower edge of the intermediate band from 40 to 30 mmol/L. 433

Screened babies. Even in babies with 2 CF-causing variants found by screening, the diagnosis must be confirmed by showing CFTR dysfunction; intestinal current measurement may be used when needed. 130

Outside screening. Diagnosis rests on clinical evidence (symptoms, a family history or a positive screen) plus evidence of CFTR dysfunction: sweat chloride, CFTR genetics or CFTR physiological tests. Intermediate values on two occasions mean further CFTR testing. 125,433

Terms. CRMS and CFSPID were merged and made equivalent: "CRMS/CFSPID". 128,369

CRMS/CFSPID: a positive screen, an inconclusive answer

Definitions

Two names, one group

CRMS (CF Foundation, 2009): high IRT on screening, sweat chloride <60 mmol/L, and up to 2 CFTR variants, at least 1 not clearly CF-causing. 37 CFSPID (ECFS): group A, normal sweat chloride and two variants, at least one of unclear consequence; group B, intermediate sweat chloride and one or no variants. 312

How common

3–5 CF cases for each

Where gene sequencing is not used there are about 3–5 cases of CF for every case of CRMS/CFSPID. 369 In US registry data for 2010–2012, 1540 infants met criteria for CF and 309 for CRMS (5.0 : 1.0); 40.8% of those with CRMS had been entered with a clinical diagnosis of CF. 368

Outlook

Most stay healthy

9 of 82 infants with CFSPID (11%) were diagnosed with CF within 3 years in an 8-centre study. 329 A 2025 meta-analysis cites conversion in about 5–11%. 505 The 2021 ECFS guidance recommends a detailed assessment in the sixth year of life and another as a young adult. 20

Open question

Should screening aim to find it?

Some programmes favour fewer CRMS/CFSPID labels, given low conversion and family distress; others want to find every child at risk. Long-term outcomes beyond early childhood are unknown. 20,369,505

Before birth: carrier screening

ACOG

Offer, do not impose

ACOG Committee Opinion 691 states that information about carrier screening should be offered to every pregnant woman, ideally before pregnancy; if both partners are carriers, genetic counselling should be offered; screening can be declined. 2

ACMG

From 23 variants to 100

ACMG recommended a 23-variant core panel in 2001, revised in 2004; in 2023 it replaced it with a minimum of 100 variants for more equitable detection across US ancestral groups. 96,285,497

Ultrasound

Echogenic bowel

Over 16 years in Brittany, 289 fetuses with echogenic bowel were referred for CFTR analysis; CF was found in 7.6%, and ultrasound identified 10.7% of all CF cases in the region. 409
