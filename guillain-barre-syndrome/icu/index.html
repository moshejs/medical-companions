Part 3 · Acute care

Intensive care & pain

Follow a schematic stay on the ventilator from intubation to weaning, against published medians for how long it lasts. Then explore the predictors of a long stay, the complications teams watch for, pain and its trials, and what people say helped.

Intensive care in GBS is mostly about buying time while the nerves recover: breathing for the person, guarding against infection and clots, managing autonomic swings and pain, and keeping contact. Even with complete paralysis, consciousness, vision and hearing are usually intact, so what is said at the bedside matters 266. Pick a step or press Play.

Schematic, not to scale. The stay and the rising vital capacity are illustrative. Published values: weaning lines 76,331; median ventilation 28 days (IQR 12 to 60) in a Dutch cohort 498 and 20 days (IQR 10 to 54) in IGOS 279.

Day 0

Intubation, planned if possible

Emergency intubation was uncommon at Mayo (7 of 43), but one person had brain injury from lack of oxygen after a respiratory arrest 508. Early pneumonia was more likely when intubation came more than 2 days after admission 340. Succinylcholine carries a risk of cardiac arrest from high potassium in denervated muscle 122.

Exam hookDelayed intubation (over 2 days) independently predicted early pneumonia 340.

Will ventilation be long? A week-one predictor

A long stay on the ventilator is the usual argument for an earlier tracheostomy. In a Dutch cohort of ventilated people, the picture one week after intubation predicted ventilation of 14 days or more 498. In Bangladesh, inability to flex the hip at day 7 of ventilation was the strongest predictor of a prolonged stay (predicted probability 82%) 350.

Status: no randomised trial. A review favours early tracheostomy 414 and the ten-steps guideline says it should be considered when there are risk factors for prolonged ventilation, without a fixed day 266. A 2025 review suggests considering it when someone is still ventilator-dependent after 2 weeks with failed weaning, or after failed extubation 504. National data show no mortality gain from going early 524, and a search of the 2023 EAN/PNS guideline text finds no mention of tracheostomy 473.

The ten-steps guideline also names pressure ulcers, choking, corneal ulceration when the eyelids cannot close, limb contractures, ossification, pressure palsies, delirium and depression 266. No GBS-specific pressure injury rate was found; guidance is standard preventive care 266. Rarely, IVIg causes acute lung injury: 17 cases were pooled, 2 with GBS 29.

Blood clots: an open question

A 2005 consensus group recommended heparin and graduated stockings for bed-bound adults in the acute phase, resting on observational data and expert opinion, not a trial 197. In 73 UK patients, 68% were anticoagulated (mean 72 days) and clinical DVT still occurred in 5 (7%), 3 of them on enoxaparin 146.

In a Russian intensive care cohort screened with ultrasound, DVT was found in 52% and pulmonary embolism in 15% despite low-molecular-weight heparin 394, while Mayo found pulmonary embolism very uncommon 184. Screening finds more than waiting for symptoms, which may explain part of the gap. The 2023 EAN/PNS guideline makes no statement on clot prevention 473.

Pain: common, real and under-treated

Before weakness36%

had pain in the 2 weeks before weakness began, in 156 Dutch people 392.

Acute phase66%

mostly moderate to severe, in every variant including Miller Fisher syndrome 392.

One year on38%

still had pain 392; it can last over 10 years 266.

Counts depend on the method: 89.1% of 55 Canadians had pain during the illness and 74.5% needed opioids 303, while a retrospective Chinese series found only 34.5% 519. Pain can be tingling and burning (paraesthesia, dysaesthesia), deep back and nerve-root pain, neck stiffness, muscle or joint pain, or discomfort in the gut 357. Deep back and leg pain usually settled by 8 weeks, but burning limb pain persisted longer in 5 to 10% 303. Pain can come before weakness and delay the diagnosis, especially in young children 266. Skin biopsies showed loss of small nerve fibres in about 60%, linked to how strong the pain was, even in pure motor forms 393.

Pain treatment: small trials

The Cochrane review found 3 short randomised trials with 277 people; none reported its primary outcome, and the evidence is insufficient to support any single drug 273. The largest effect came from a three-arm trial in 36 people in intensive care. Scrub or play through the week.

Median pain scores (0 to 10) in Pandey 2005, n=36 346. Day-1 fentanyl use was about 340, 348 and 590 µg in the three arms 346.

Mind, sleep and communication

Vivid dreams and hallucinations

Mental status changes in 31% of 139 people with GBS vs 16% of intensive care controls: vivid dreams, illusions, mainly visual hallucinations, paranoid ideas. Median onset day 9, lasting a median 8 days; linked to autonomic dysfunction, ventilation and high CSF protein 79.

Early evidence

A sleep mechanism?

The same authors link these experiences to a REM sleep disorder rather than ordinary intensive care delirium 79.

Distress

Among 49 very severely affected people in intensive care: anxiety 82%, depressive episodes 67%, brief reactive psychosis 25% 503. Top fears at admission were staying paralysed, uncertainty and intubation 417.

Locked in, but present

One person, fully conscious but unable to communicate for 31 days, described standard low-volume ventilation as profound breathlessness and physiotherapy as very painful with no change in heart rate or blood pressure. One account, but it shows monitors can miss pain 248.

What helpedPeople said losing the ability to communicate was the hardest part, and 90% found contact with relatives most helpful 503. The guideline advises asking directly about pain, hallucinations, anxiety and depression, explaining procedures, and giving accurate information about the relatively good chance of recovery and the low risk of recurrence (2 to 5%) 266. A small 2026 interview study named communication barriers among four themes of lived experience 435.

In ventilated people at Mayo, only age (OR 1.99) and transfer to a specialist centre delayed by more than 2 days (OR 19.8) independently predicted a poor outcome 129. Respiratory muscle strength is reported to recover in all patients eventually, though ventilation is tied to residual disability 339. Two years after onset, 7% of a Swedish cohort still could not walk 10 m independently 133.

Exam hookDelayed intubation (over 2 days) independently predicted early pneumonia 340. Cannot lift arms at 1 week: 87% chance of ventilation for 14 days or more 498. Early tracheostomy did not lower mortality in a national cohort 524. Pain: about two-thirds acutely, over a third at 1 year 392. Methylprednisolone does not prevent GBS pain 273. Fully paralysed people usually see and hear normally 266.
In plain wordsPain is common and real, even when weakness is the main problem; please say if you hurt 392. Vivid dreams or seeing things that are not there can happen in GBS. They usually pass, and they do not mean you are losing your mind 79. Someone who cannot move or speak can usually still hear and understand: talk to them, explain things and say who is there 266. Many people who needed a ventilator walk again, though it can take a long time. These are group figures, not a forecast for one person 129,466.
