An animated, cited guide for students, people who have or have had Guillain-Barré syndrome, and their families and carers. Educational only, not medical advice. Your place is saved on this device.

Not medical adviceThe GBS Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. Weakness that worsens quickly, or trouble breathing or swallowing, needs emergency care. It does not replace a doctor, nurse, physiotherapist, occupational therapist, speech and language therapist or the rest of a care team. Scores such as the Brighton criteria, EGRIS, EGOS and mEGOS are teaching aids, never for an individual's diagnosis, prognosis or treatment. Doses, thresholds and guideline statements are quoted as published, never as instructions. Outcomes are population statistics; they are not predictions for any person.

Part 6 · Context & review

Sources

Every reference behind the GBS Companion: randomised trials, Cochrane reviews, cohorts such as IGOS, the 2023 EAN/PNS guideline and the 2019 ten-steps guideline, US drug labels and trial registrations. The small numbers throughout the app point here; filter by research area or search.

Research passes ran in parallel, one per area: epidemiology and history; triggers, vaccines and molecular mimicry; subtypes and electrophysiology; the course, diagnostic criteria, CSF and look-alikes; immunotherapy; breathing, autonomic problems and intensive care; prognosis, rehabilitation and long-term outcomes; children, pregnancy, nursing and living with GBS; and the research frontier. Before inclusion each source was checked, where possible, against PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, ClinicalTrials.gov or the regulator's label, and the green tag on an entry names the service that confirmed it. Works found by more than one pass appear once, with any extra keys listed beside them.

Some guideline and criteria texts could not be read in full, including the original Brighton case definition and the 2014 classification criteria. Where that matters, modules say the statement is reported through a secondary source. Entries marked not matched in a research database are pages with no PMID or DOI, or works that could not be confirmed.
