Part 3 · Acute care

IVIg: intravenous immunoglobulin

IVIg is a drip of antibodies pooled from thousands of blood donors, and one of the two treatments shown to speed recovery from Guillain-Barré syndrome. Follow it from the donor pool to the nerve, see what happens to blood IgG, and how it compared with plasma exchange.

IVIg and plasma exchange are the two immunotherapies with trial support in GBS, and guidelines treat them as equally effective 266,473. This module reports what guidelines, trials and drug labels say. It is not medical advice, and the doses below are quoted as published, never as instructions. Pick a step or press Play.

Step 1 · the product

Antibodies pooled from many donors

IVIg is polyclonal serum IgG pooled from thousands of donors; the Bivigam label states not less than 1,000 donors per pool 118,280. Because it is a blood product, labels also warn of a theoretical risk of transmissible agents, such as CJD 118.

In plain wordsIVIg is a drip of antibodies collected from many blood donors 280.
Exam hookIVIg = pooled polyclonal IgG, at least 1,000 donors per pool on the Bivigam label 118.

The regimen as arithmetic

The guideline compared four ways of giving IVIg. Choose a course and move the weight slider to see what the published grams per kilogram add up to. A course quoted in g/kg scales with body weight, which is why the totals are large 473.

At 70 kg: 28 g a day for 5 days, 140 g in all (2.0 g/kg).

The 2023 EAN/PNS guideline weakly recommends this standard course over a low-dose, high-dose or 2-day course 473. The 1992 and 1997 trials used it 362,468.

No adequate placebo-controlled IVIg trial exists in adults. Plasma exchange was already proven against supportive care, so IVIg was tested against plasma exchange, and the evidence that IVIg works is therefore indirect 199,473. Neither treatment has been shown to reduce mortality 198.

How it might work

Established

Not settled

A 2015 review says IVIg's mechanisms "are poorly understood", and many proposed actions come from lab or animal work rather than human tissue 88,280.

Early evidence

Neutralising the antibody

IVIg reduced the nerve-blocking activity of seven GBS sera on mouse muscle, dose-dependently; Fab fragments worked as well as whole IVIg, Fc fragments did not 61. In vitro, IVIg's variable region bound anti-GM1 antibody 528. Dalakas states this anti-idiotype fraction "appears partially responsible" 88.

Hypothesis

Complement, Fc receptors, cytokines

Proposed actions include blocking complement binding and membrane attack complex formation, neutralising cytokines, lowering antibody production and modulating Fc-receptor-driven phagocytosis, inferred across diseases 87. A 2022 GBS review adds faster antibody breakdown and regulatory T-cell effects 369.

Early evidence

Sugar on the stem

In a mouse model of antibody-mediated nerve injury, sialylated IVIg protected at a tenfold lower dose than whole IVIg, while unsialylated fractions did not help. Animal data only 539.

Blood levels and outcome: association or cause?

In 174 Dutch people given a standard course, the rise in serum IgG at 2 weeks (ΔIgG) ranged widely, with a mean of 7.8 g/L (SD 5.6). A small rise went with slower recovery and fewer people walking unaided at 6 months, and stayed linked to poor outcome after adjustment 246. A later review summarised poor outcome at 6 months as 28% in the lowest quartile of rise and 7% in the highest 369. Quartile cut-offs differ between cohorts, partly because of differences in severity 477.

Serum albumin also fell after IVIg (median 4.2 to 3.7 g/dL at 2 weeks), and low albumin was associated with respiratory failure and inability to walk unaided 132. Published IgG half-lives, measured in people with immunodeficiency and not in GBS, are 30.0 days (Bivigam) and 28.5 days (Asceniv) 117,118.

Position A: exposure matters

A low IgG rise is independently linked to poor outcome, suggesting that IgG exposure matters and that higher dosing might help 246.

Position B: a marker of severity

More severely affected people may break IgG down faster, so a small rise may mark severity rather than cause poor outcome Hypothesis 369. A second course raised IgG but did not improve outcome 477,499.

Status: the association is established; dosing guided by measured IgG is untested 477.

Risks named on the labels

Pick a part of the body or a button to read what US IVIg labels warn about. Several label risk factors for clots, such as prolonged immobilisation and central venous catheters, are common in a person immobilised by GBS; that link is drawn by the research for this guide, not by the labels 118.

Label text from US prescribing information for immune-globulin products, not from GBS trials.

Thrombosis (boxed warning)

All current labels carry a boxed warning for thrombosis, renal dysfunction and acute renal failure 117,118. Label risk factors include advanced age, prolonged immobilisation, hypercoagulable states, past thrombosis, oestrogens, central venous catheters, hyperviscosity and cardiovascular risk factors; thrombosis can occur without any 118. In US admissions data, the odds of venous thromboembolism in the 30 days after IVIg for neurological disease were raised (OR 3.33, 95% CI 1.34 to 8.30), as they were after plasma exchange (OR 4.29) 214.

Is IVIg licensed for GBS in the US?

No IVIg label retrieved from the openFDA dataset lists GBS as an indication; the word "Guillain" appeared in none of the 26 immune-globulin labels checked. This is a search of a dataset, not an FDA statement, and three of the labels are 2010 to 2011 snapshots, so GBS use in the US appears to be off-label on this evidence (an inference) 117,118,119,120,121. Gamunex-C's retrieved label lists primary humoral immunodeficiency, ITP and CIDP 120.

In plain wordsIVIg is a drip of antibodies from many blood donors, usually given once a day for 5 days 280,473. It helps people recover faster; it does not switch the illness off straight away 199. It can cause headache, and rarely blood clots, kidney problems or a reaction of the lining around the brain; the care team watches for these 118.
Exam hookIVIg 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days (2 g/kg) within 2 weeks; equal to plasma exchange; boxed warning for thrombosis and renal failure; IgA deficiency with anti-IgA antibodies and a hypersensitivity history is a label contraindication 118,199,473.
