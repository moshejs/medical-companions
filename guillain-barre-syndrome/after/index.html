Part 4 · Recovery

Life after GBS

Walking again is not the whole story. Pain, tiredness, mood, work and family life can change for years after Guillain-Barré syndrome, even after a good physical recovery. Walk the timeline from before the weakness to twenty years on.

60 to 80%

report fatigue unrelated to weakness, often among their most disabling complaints 266. A 2025 meta-analysis found a much lower 32% 544.

38%

still had pain 1 year after onset in a Dutch cohort of 156 people 392.

63%

reported a change in work, leisure, home life or their partner's life 3 to 6 years on, including people who had physically recovered 39.

2 to 5%

have GBS again: rare, though higher than the 0.1% lifetime risk in the general population 266.

Each stop shows one study, scaled to 100 people. The studies followed different people in different ways, so the stops are not one cohort and should not be read as a single curve.

Before weakness · pain

Pain can come first

In a prospective Dutch cohort of 156 people, 36% reported pain in the 2 weeks before weakness began 392. In an IVIg trial sample, 55% of 223 had pain at randomisation, and for 70% of them it started before the weakness 390. Pain first can delay the diagnosis, especially in young children 266.

In plain wordsPain is common in GBS, and it can start before the weakness 392.
Exam hookPain can come before weakness: 36% reported it in the preceding 2 weeks 392.

Fatigue: common, real, and hard to count

Fatigue after GBS is not explained by remaining weakness. Severe fatigue was found in 80% of 113 people with past GBS, CIDP or a related neuropathy, including 81 to 86% of those with normal strength or sensation 293. In 100 people after GBS it was found in 60%, more often in women and people over 50, with no relation to disability at the worst point, the infection before GBS, or time since GBS 150. Yet a 2025 meta-analysis pooled a much lower figure. Select a study to see who was measured.

Garssen 2006: severe fatigue (mean Fatigue Severity Scale 5.0 or more) in 60% of 100 people after GBS, more often in women and people over 50, with no relation to disability at nadir, the preceding infection or time since GBS 150.

Pain that lasts

Pain can be tingling and burning (paraesthesia and dysaesthesia), deep back and root pain, meningism (a stiff, painful neck), muscle and joint pain, or discomfort in the gut 357. In 55 people, deep back and leg pain usually settled within 8 weeks, but dysaesthetic limb pain persisted longer in 5 to 10% 303. It happens in the pure motor form too, and lower skin nerve fibre density went with worse acute pain 393. Muscle aches and cramps persisted in 48% at 3 to 6 years, linked to remaining sensory loss rather than weakness 40. Pain in intensive care is covered in Intensive care & pain.

Straight lines join the published points of each cohort; the shape between them is not known. Netherlands: 156 people 392. Bangladesh: 644 people, median age 31 351.

TreatmentEvidence
Gabapentin, carbamazepineThe Cochrane review found three short trials in the acute phase (277 people), with very-low-quality evidence for gabapentin and carbamazepine, not enough to support any drug 273. In one three-arm trial (36 people), median day-7 pain scores were 2.0 with gabapentin, 3.0 with carbamazepine and 6.0 with placebo 346.
Guideline wordingThe EAN/PNS guideline weakly recommends gabapentinoids, tricyclic antidepressants or carbamazepine for pain 473; opioids may be used with care because of constipation, ileus, confusion, breathing suppression and addiction 473.
SteroidsMethylprednisolone did not reduce pain 273,390.
Pain months and years laterNo randomised trial was found for the recovery phase, and the Cochrane review calls for one 273.
In plain wordsPain is common in GBS, and it can start before the weakness. Tell your care team about it, because medicines may help 392,473.

Mood, fear and identity

Depression, mostly early

In a Danish population study of 853 people, 21.3% had depression within 2 years, against 3.3% of the general population. The risk was highest in the first 3 months (HR 20.5), and after 2 years it matched the general population (HR 0.8) 270.

Still present years later

In Melbourne, at a median of 6 years, 18% reported moderate to extreme depression, 22% anxiety and 17% stress 223. Psychosocial function was impaired even in people with no or mild remaining signs at 3 to 6 years 38, and it was still impaired at 12 months in a first-year study 43.

After a long ventilation

In 13 survivors of prolonged ventilation, quality of life matched population norms, yet 22% had post-traumatic stress symptoms 265.

What people describe

Interviews named two processes, "loss, determination, and adjustment" and "the unknown"; support, knowledge and hope helped 33. Two years on, some described a continuous wait for recovery 137.

SupportLow mood and worry are common, especially in the first months. Asking for help with them is part of recovery, and referral to a psychologist or psychiatrist may help some people 266,270.

SupportThe GBS|CIDP Foundation International and national patient organisations offer support and education, and can connect people with others who have had GBS. Support groups may also help relatives in the first year 42,266.

What the body keeps

Sensation

At 3 to 6 years, remaining sensory loss was found in the arms of 38% and the legs of 66%, and felt as moderate to severe in the arms of 27% and legs of 40% 40.

Strength and walking

At 10 years in 29 Swedish people, 52% had limits in walking, 38% tingling and 21% limits in the arms 136. At 3 to 6 years, 84 of 122 Dutch people had no or minor signs, 24 moderate and 14 severe 40.

Bladder

At a median of 6.1 years in 66 people, more than half reported getting up at night to pass urine and a third urgency and frequency; 49% said urinary symptoms interfered with daily life to some extent 16.

Even after mild GBS

Among 50 Norwegians, 34% reported pain and 13% remaining weakness, and fatigue was raised even after mild disease 377. At a median of 6 years, pain and disability scores were worse than in controls 387.

After childhood GBS

At a median of 11 years, 65% had some remaining complaint: tingling 38%, unsteady walking in the dark 37%, painful hands or feet 24%, severe fatigue 22%; 26% reported a negative effect on school 381. More in Children & pregnancy.

Will it come back?

Recurrent GBS affects 2 to 5% of people with GBS: rare, but higher than the 0.1% lifetime risk in the general population 266. Recurrences were defined as at least 2 months apart after full recovery, or 4 months after partial recovery 244. The lighter dots show the spread between 2% and 5%.

In a Dutch series, 32 people with recurrent GBS had 81 episodes in all. They were younger than people with a single episode (mean 34.2 against 46.9 years) and more often had Miller Fisher syndrome or milder symptoms 244. Later episodes resembled each person's first, though severity and the preceding infection varied 244. Worsening within 2 months of treatment is a treatment-related fluctuation, not a recurrence 244,266; see Steroids, repeats & relapses.

EmergencyIf weakness gets worse quickly, or breathing or swallowing becomes hard, this is an emergency and needs urgent care 266.

Vaccines after GBS. Prior GBS is not a strict contraindication to vaccination. Discussion with experts might help people diagnosed less than a year before a planned vaccination, or whose GBS followed the same vaccine, and the small, possibly only theoretical, risk should be weighed against the vaccine's benefit 197,266. In a Californian cohort, 279 people received 989 vaccines after GBS, including 405 flu doses in 107 people, with no recurrence after flu vaccination 30. None of 106 Dutch people reported recurrence after 775 flu vaccinations in all 245. More in Vaccines & GBS.

Exam hookRecurrence is 2 to 5%; it is commoner in younger people and after Miller Fisher syndrome 244,266.
