Part 2 · Diagnosis

Diagnostic criteria

How the published case definitions for Guillain-Barré syndrome work: the NINDS criteria, the 2023 EAN/PNS criteria and the Brighton levels of diagnostic certainty. Watch a case climb the Brighton ladder, then try the teaching calculator.

GBS has no single confirming test, so diagnosis is clinical, supported by spinal fluid and nerve conduction studies 266,473. Criteria give that judgement a shared shape. The two oldest sets, NINDS and Brighton, were both designed to study epidemiological links between GBS and vaccination; a consensus panel judged NINDS better suited to clinicians and Brighton useful for grading certainty 266.

Step 1 · why

Why case definitions exist

The Brighton Collaboration published case definitions for GBS and Fisher syndrome for collecting and analysing immunisation safety data 405. The NINDS criteria and Brighton were both designed to study epidemiological links between GBS and vaccination; a consensus panel judged NINDS better suited to clinicians and Brighton useful for grading certainty 266. The original Brighton paper could not be read for this guide, so the grid follows two published reproductions 158,277.

In plain wordsDoctors and researchers use checklists so that "GBS" means the same thing in different hospitals and studies.

NINDS and EAN/PNS side by side

The NINDS criteria were reaffirmed and their electrodiagnostic part expanded by Asbury and Cornblath in 1990 23. The original tables could not be read for this guide, so the NINDS column gives the labelled adaptation by Leonhard and colleagues 266. The 2023 European Academy of Neurology and Peripheral Nerve Society (EAN/PNS) guideline used that consensus for clinical diagnosis 473.

The guideline deliberately does not endorse specific electrodiagnostic criteria for AIDP, AMAN or AMSAN, because there is no gold standard and the subtype does not currently change management 473. Nerve conduction is covered in Nerve conduction studies.

Children

Cumulative sensitivity

In 46 Dutch children with complete data, Brighton reached level 1 in 72% (95% CI 57–84), level 2 or better in 96% (85–99) and level 3 or better in 98% (88–100) 382.

Specificity

A mimic can qualify

When the "no alternative diagnosis" item was left out, 8% of children with acute flaccid myelitis met the Brighton criteria for GBS 183. That item does real work.

Severity

Level is not severity

People at different Brighton levels did not differ in preceding events, first symptoms or outcome in the Dutch cohort, so the level reflects certainty of the data 131.

Variants

Who falls outside

Brighton is designed for classic symmetric, ascending GBS and Fisher syndrome and could miss rare regional variants such as pharyngeal-cervical-brachial weakness 238. Bilateral facial palsy with paraesthesias, pure sensory GBS and Bickerstaff brainstem encephalitis do not fulfil commonly used criteria 266.

The GBS Classification Group scheme

A 2014 classification splits GBS into classic and localised forms (such as pharyngeal-cervical-brachial weakness and bifacial weakness with paraesthesias), and Miller Fisher syndrome into incomplete forms (such as acute ophthalmoparesis and acute ataxic neuropathy) and a central nervous system subtype, Bickerstaff brainstem encephalitis, with criteria based on clinical features 491. In plain wordsNo single test proves GBS. Doctors put together the story, the examination and the test results. Checklists like Brighton help researchers say how sure they are, and a lower level does not mean a milder illness.
