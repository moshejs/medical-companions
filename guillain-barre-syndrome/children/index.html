Part 5 · Special situations & care

Children & pregnancy

GBS in children often starts with pain or a child who will not walk, and most children recover faster than adults. Follow one child's course day by day, see why young children are missed, compare cohorts, and look at what is known about pregnancy and the weeks after birth.

EmergencyIn a child or an adult, weakness that gets worse quickly, or trouble breathing or swallowing, is an emergency and needs urgent care 266. Not all people heading for breathing failure feel breathless, which is why breathing is measured routinely in hospital 266.

Step 1 · first signs

Pain, or not wanting to walk

In a prospective German-speaking study of 95 children (median age 6.2 years), the first symptom was usually a gait disturbance or neuropathic pain, and 79% had neuropathic pain at some point 237. Children under 6 can present with poorly localised pain, refusal to bear weight, irritability, meningism or an unsteady gait 266. In Rotterdam, 68% of preschool children were first misdiagnosed, against 21% of older children 380.

In plain wordsIn young children the first sign is often pain in the back or legs, or not wanting to walk, so GBS can be missed at first 330,380.

Why young children are missed

A preschool child may refuse to walk, limp, cry with leg pain or seem irritable. In Rotterdam, first diagnoses included myopathy, tonsillitis, meningitis, rheumatoid disorders, coxitis (hip inflammation) and discitis 380. Across low- and middle-income settings, GBS is correctly diagnosed at admission in only about one-third of affected preschool children 347. Choose an age group.

Under 6 years. 15 of 23 preschool children (68%) were first given another diagnosis. They reached a paediatrician as fast as older children (median 5 days), but the doctor took a median 3 days to suspect GBS, more than a week in a quarter, up to 22 days 380. Refusal to walk and leg pain were the most frequent first symptoms (65%) 380.

MRI of the nerve roots

Enhancement of the spinal nerve roots on MRI is not specific, but it is sensitive and can support the diagnosis, especially in young children, in whom examination and nerve conduction studies can be difficult 266.

Acute flaccid paralysis surveillance

AFP surveillance was built for polio eradication and covers children under 15; GBS is now its predominant cause in studies from China and Bangladesh 347. In Spain, 76.9% of 655 notified cases (1998 to 2015) were clinically GBS and no wild poliovirus was found 285; in a South African children's hospital, 99 of 119 children with AFP had GBS 469.

Who is likely to need breathing support?

Early evidence

EGRIS-Kids

Roodbol and colleagues found the adult EGRIS inaccurate in children and built EGRIS-Kids from age, cranial nerve involvement and GBS disability score at admission: a 9-point score with predicted risk of respiratory failure from 4 to 50% (AUC 0.71) 383.

Early evidence

The adult EGRIS in children

A Chinese cohort reported the adult EGRIS as helpful: median 6 in ventilated against 3 in non-ventilated children (p < 0.001), with 5 or more called high risk 364. Both studies are retrospective, and EGRIS-Kids has not yet been externally validated.

Signs linked to ventilation

  • Facial or bulbar weakness (OR 7.936) and axonal type (OR 4.582), in 103 Chinese children 281
  • Autonomic dysfunction: 39.1% of ventilated vs 8.8% of non-ventilated children (OR 70.415, 95% CI 9.265 to 535.158), unlike in adults 516
  • Peripheral facial paralysis and dysautonomia in French children 115
  • Bulbar, autonomic and upper limb weakness in South Africa 469

Autonomic problems

51% in the German study 237 and 46% of a US series, including blood pressure and pulse changes, bowel or bladder control and priapism; autonomic dysfunction, cranial nerve involvement and albuminocytological dissociation in the spinal fluid were each linked to a shorter time to the worst point 185. In critically ill Indian children, autonomic instability, especially high blood pressure, was more frequent with AIDP 28.

Plasma exchange in children: the risks

In 36 children (122 sessions), fast heart rate, low blood pressure and mild anaemia were most common, and three had serious complications 11. In a French mixed cohort of 23 children, one child died from venous air embolism, a rare but real procedural risk 130.

Two open questions. Two-day or five-day IVIg? The 2-day regimen brought more early deterioration in one underpowered trial 236, yet some paediatric centres still use it 266. IVIg or plasma exchange when a child is ventilated? Plasma exchange shortened ventilation by 2 days in one small trial 111, while guidelines favour IVIg for availability and comfort 239,266. Both are unresolved.

Recovery and the years after

Back to normal function

In 80 children with severe GBS treated in a paediatric ICU and seen a median 3.0 years later, 95% had a favourable neurological outcome, 97.5% attended school and 57.7% had satisfactory school performance; quadriparesis at admission, ventilation, tracheostomy and longer stays predicted a worse outcome 98.

Mild weakness can stay

Of 47 children followed at least 2 years, 23% had mild persisting weakness in at least one muscle group, all with perfect functional independence scores; young age and rapid progression predicted it 462. In 110 French children, 77% were cured and 9% had sequelae, linked to an axonal form and a short time to admission 115.

What children still notice

67% (95% CI 53 to 78) of 51 French children had sequelae at a median 6 years 4 months, mostly minor: tingling 43%, pain 35%, fatigue 31%, symptoms of depression 34% 116. In 37 people with childhood GBS (median 11 years), 65% had residual complaints, 26% a negative effect on their school career, and 2 a recurrence 381.

Scores that predict

In 142 Chinese children, mEGOS and EGOS were judged valid predictors of 6-month outcome for those admitted within 2 weeks Early evidence 364. How long problems last is debated: function is usually normal 98,462, yet two-thirds report sequelae years later 116,381. Both may be true; the measures differ.

For familiesMost children recover well, often faster than adults, but tiredness, tingling or low mood can linger and can affect school. Those are real effects of the illness, and they are worth raising with the care team and the school 116,381.

Step 1 · pregnancy

During pregnancy: risk not raised

In Swedish women aged 15 to 49 (1978 to 1993), the age-adjusted rate ratio for GBS during pregnancy was 0.89 (95% CI 0.52 to 1.53) 74; an earlier register (1973 to 1983) gave 0.86 (0.40 to 1.84) 212. When GBS does start in pregnancy, onset was spread across trimesters in 16 French women (31%, 31%, 38%) 242, while 52.5% of 101 published case reports began in the third trimester 518.

In plain wordsGBS in pregnancy is uncommon, and pregnancy itself does not seem to raise the risk 74.

How dangerous is GBS in pregnancy? Case-report pools give 45% ventilated 518, a French series 25% 242, and Indian hospital series 11.1% 355 or 2 of 47 415. Reporting bias probably inflates the first figure, and there is no prospective cohort. Compared with 18 non-pregnant women with GBS, the 16 French pregnant women more often had CMV infection (31% vs 11%), ICU admission (56% vs 33%) and treatment-related fluctuations (37% vs 0%) 242.

Treatment in pregnancy

Neither IVIg nor plasma exchange is contraindicated in pregnancy; as plasma exchange needs additional considerations and monitoring, IVIg might be preferred 266. An obstetric review says treatment should follow the same principles as outside pregnancy, with clot prevention, physiotherapy, pressure care, tube feeding where needed, respiratory support and close monitoring 344. The EAN/PNS guideline makes no pregnancy-specific treatment recommendation 473. Delivery and anaesthesia are covered in Nursing care.

GBS in a newborn

A baby born to a mother with ongoing GBS developed GBS 12 days after birth; serum from both blocked nerve-muscle transmission in animal preparations, and the blocking stopped after both recovered 60. The authors propose that the mother's antibodies cross the placenta and target the mature, not the fetal, nerve-muscle junction, which could explain the delay Hypothesis 60. A single case of congenital GBS in a preterm infant improved within 48 hours of IVIg 27.

In plain wordsChildren can get GBS too, but it is rare: roughly 1 child in 100,000 each year 289,395. Most children recover well, and usually faster than adults 237. GBS in pregnancy is uncommon; the risk seems highest in the first weeks after the baby is born. Treatment can be given during pregnancy. Most babies do well, but early birth is more common 74,266,518.
