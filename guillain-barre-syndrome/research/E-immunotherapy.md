## Findings

### ivig · Published regimen and guideline position
- The EAN/PNS guideline strongly recommends starting IVIg as soon as possible in people unable to walk unaided (GBS disability score grade 3 or more) if still within the first 2 weeks from onset of weakness [[vanDoorn2023]]
- The guideline weakly recommends the standard course of 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days over a low-dose course (0.4 g/kg/day for 3 days), a high-dose course (0.4 g/kg/day for 6 days) or a 2-day regimen (1 g/kg/day) [[vanDoorn2023]]
- The guideline has no preference between IVIg and plasma exchange [[vanDoorn2023]]
- The ten-steps guideline calls the two "equally effective" and says IVIg is usually the treatment of choice because it is easier to give and more widely available [[Leonhard2019]]
- A good practice point suggests treating people unable to walk unaided at 2–4 weeks from onset with either plasma exchange or IVIg. No RCT tested IVIg in that window, so the guideline extrapolates from plasma exchange [[vanDoorn2023]]
- The 2003 AAN practice parameter recommended IVIg for nonambulant adults "within 2 or possibly 4 weeks" of neuropathic symptom onset and rated the effects of plasma exchange and IVIg as equivalent [[Hughes2003]]
- The 2008 EFNS guideline rated the efficacy of IVIg in GBS as level A [[Elovaara2008]]
- The ten-steps guideline advises starting immunotherapy if a person cannot walk 10 m independently, and considering it in those who can if weakness is rapidly progressive or there is autonomic dysfunction, bulbar failure or respiratory insufficiency [[Leonhard2019]]
- The standard course totals 2 g/kg. At 70 kg that is 28 g a day and 140 g in total (arithmetic by this pass) [[vanDoorn2023]]

GBS use of IVIg in the US is therefore off-label on this evidence (inference by this pass) [[FDA-Bivigam,FDA-Asceniv,FDA-GamunexC,FDA-Privigen,FDA-GammagardLiquid]]

### ivig · Trial evidence
- Dutch trial (1992): people with GBS for under 2 weeks who could not walk independently received five plasma exchanges (each 200–250 mL/kg) or IVIg 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days. At 4 weeks, strength improved by one grade or more in 34% with plasma exchange and 53% with IVIg (difference 19%, 95% CI 3–34%, P=0.024; 150 patients treated) [[vanDerMeche1992]]
- In the same trial, median time to one-grade improvement was 41 days with plasma exchange and 27 days with IVIg (P=0.05). The IVIg group also had significantly fewer complications and less need for ventilation [[vanDerMeche1992]]
- Plasma Exchange/Sandoglobulin trial (1997): 383 adults randomised, 379 assessed, with severe GBS and onset within 14 days, assigned to plasma exchange (five 50 mL/kg exchanges over 8–13 days), IVIg (0.4 g/kg daily for 5 days) or plasma exchange immediately followed by IVIg [[PESGBS1997]]
- Mean disability grade improvement at 4 weeks in that trial was 0.9 (SD 1.3; n=121) with plasma exchange, 0.8 (SD 1.3; n=130) with IVIg and 1.1 (SD 1.4; n=128) with both. No difference was significant, and a 0.5-grade difference between plasma exchange and IVIg was excluded at the 95% level [[PESGBS1997]]
- Cochrane 2014 included 12 trials. Seven compared IVIg with plasma exchange in 623 severely affected participants [[Hughes2014]]
- In five of those trials (536 participants), the mean difference in 4-week disability change was 0.02 of a grade more improvement with IVIg (95% CI 0.25 to -0.20), which is not significant [[Hughes2014]]
- Cochrane found adverse events not significantly more frequent with either treatment, but IVIg was "significantly much more likely to be completed" than plasma exchange [[Hughes2014]]
- A 2023 meta-analysis gave a risk ratio for discontinuation of 0.22 with IVIg (95% CI 0.06–0.88; 3 studies). Complication risk was similar (RR 1.03, 95% CI 0.71–1.48) [[Zaki2023]]
- No adequate placebo-controlled IVIg trial exists in adults. As plasma exchange was already proven, IVIg was tested against it, and the evidence that IVIg works is indirect [[Hughes2014,vanDoorn2023]]
- An earlier systematic review found IVIg and plasma exchange very similar in five trials with 582 participants (weighted mean difference -0.02, 95% CI -0.25 to 0.20) and found that no treatment significantly reduced mortality [[Hughes2007]]

### ivig · Children and dose schedule
- Three trials with 75 children in total suggested IVIg hastens recovery compared with supportive care (low-quality evidence) [[Hughes2014]]
- In one trial of 21 mildly affected children, the 4-week disability improvement favoured IVIg (mean difference 1.42, 95% CI 2.57 to 0.27) [[Hughes2014]]
- German paediatric trial: 1 g/kg/day for 2 days versus 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days showed no significant recovery difference (median time to unaided walking 19 versus 13 days) [[Korinthenberg2005]]
- In that trial, early transient deterioration was more frequent with the 2-day regimen (5 of 23 versus 0 of 23) [[Korinthenberg2005]]
- The trial's early-treatment arm (21 children still able to walk 5 m) did not show a less severe course, though recovery was somewhat faster and the study was underpowered [[Korinthenberg2005]]
- The ten-steps guideline sees no need to depart from adult practice in children. IVIg is usually first line because plasma exchange is available only in experienced centres and seems to cause more discomfort and complications in children [[Leonhard2019]]
- The EAN/PNS panel considered the evidence too thin to support a 2-day regimen, or a lower or higher dose than standard [[vanDoorn2023]]
- A trial of 3 versus 6 days at 0.4 g/kg in 39 people with contraindications to plasma exchange showed non-significantly faster improvement with the higher dose and a significant difference in the ventilated subgroup (low certainty) [[vanDoorn2023]]

### ivig · Proposed mechanisms
- The mechanism of IVIg in GBS is not settled. A 2015 review says the mechanisms of action "are poorly understood" (Established) [[Lunemann2015]]
- IVIg is polyclonal serum IgG pooled from thousands of donors; Bivigam's label states not less than 1,000 donors per pool [[Lunemann2015,FDA-Bivigam]]
- Dalakas lists the most relevant neurological actions as inhibition of complement binding and prevention of membrane attack complex formation, neutralisation of pathogenic cytokines, downregulation of antibody production and modulation of Fc-receptor-mediated phagocytosis (Hypothesis in GBS: inferred across diseases) [[Dalakas1998]]
- A 2022 GBS review adds blockade of macrophage Fc receptors, neutralisation of pathogenic antibodies, increased antibody catabolism and regulatory T-cell effects (Hypothesis) [[Rajabally2022]]
- Anti-idiotype neutralisation: in seven GBS sera or IgG fractions tested on mouse hemidiaphragm, IVIg reduced neuromuscular blocking activity dose-dependently. Fab fragments worked as well as whole IVIg, but Fc fragments did not (Early evidence) [[Buchwald2002]]
- In vitro, the variable region of IVIg appeared to bind anti-GM1 antibody as well as cholera toxin, suggesting anti-idiotypic neutralisation (Early evidence) [[Yuki1996]]
- Dalakas states that an antigen-specific anti-idiotypic fraction "appears partially responsible" for the effect in GBS and myasthenia gravis (Early evidence) [[Dalakas2014]]
- Fc glycosylation: in a mouse passive-transfer model of antibody-mediated nerve injury, sialylated IVIg was protective at a tenfold lower dose than whole IVIg, while unsialylated fractions were not beneficial (Early evidence; animal data) [[Zhang2016ExpNeurol]]
- Many proposed actions come from in vitro or animal work rather than human tissue (Established) [[Dalakas2014]]

### ivig · Pharmacokinetics and the IgG rise
- In 174 Dutch people with GBS given a standard dose, the rise in serum IgG at 2 weeks (ΔIgG) varied widely: mean 7.8 g/L, SD 5.6 g/L [[Kuitwaard2009Pharm]]
- A low ΔIgG was linked to slower recovery and fewer people walking unaided at 6 months (log-rank P<0.001), and remained independently associated with poor outcome after adjustment (P=0.022) [[Kuitwaard2009Pharm]]
- Across ΔIgG quartiles in that study, poor outcome at 6 months (disability score above 2) ranged from 28% in the lowest quartile (<3.99 g/L rise) to 7% in the highest (>10.92 g/L), as summarised by a later review [[Rajabally2022]]
- SID-GBS pharmacokinetic analysis: baseline IgG was a median 12.5 g/L (placebo, randomised), 12.1 g/L (second dose) and 11.4 g/L (good prognosis, not randomised) [[vanTilburg2025]]
- At week 2 (days 13–15), median IgG was 21.2 g/L (IQR 18.1–23.2) with one course and placebo, 21.8 g/L (19.0–23.6) with one course and good prognosis, and 35.5 g/L (30.9–42.7) with a second course [[vanTilburg2025]]
- At week 4 (days 26–30), median IgG was 15.4 g/L (13.6–17.2), 15.5 g/L (13.8–17.2) and 19.4 g/L (15.9–21.9) in the same three groups [[vanTilburg2025]]
- ΔIgG quartile cut-offs differed between cohorts: <6.7, 6.7–9.4, 9.5–11.8, >11.8 g/L in the SID-GBS analysis versus <3.99, 3.99–7.30, 7.31–10.92, >10.92 g/L in Kuitwaard. The authors attribute this partly to differences in disease severity [[vanTilburg2025]]
- Severity may partly explain the association itself. More severely affected people may have higher IgG catabolism, so a low ΔIgG may be a marker of severity and not its cause (Hypothesis) [[Rajabally2022]]
- The second course raised IgG but did not improve outcomes, so raising IgG alone did not convert association into benefit [[vanTilburg2025,Walgaard2021]]
- Published IgG half-lives (not measured in GBS): 30.0 days (CV 37.5%) with Bivigam and 28.5 days (SD 4.4) with Asceniv in people over 16 with primary immunodeficiency, and 22 days quoted in an apheresis review [[FDA-Bivigam,FDA-Asceniv,Williams2014]]
- In 174 IVIg-treated people, median serum albumin fell from 4.2 g/dL before treatment to 3.7 g/dL at 2 weeks, and hypoalbuminaemia rose from 12.8% to 34.5%. It was associated with respiratory failure and inability to walk unaided [[Fokkink2017]]

### ivig · Safety: labels and observed harms
- Class boxed warning in current labels: "Thrombosis, renal dysfunction and acute renal failure" [[FDA-Bivigam,FDA-Asceniv]]
- Label risk factors for thrombosis: advanced age, prolonged immobilisation, hypercoagulable conditions, prior venous or arterial thrombosis, oestrogens, indwelling central vascular catheters, hyperviscosity and cardiovascular risk factors. Thrombosis can occur without known risk factors. Several of these are common in a person immobilised by GBS (link made by this pass) [[FDA-Bivigam]]
- Label risk factors for renal failure: any pre-existing renal insufficiency, diabetes mellitus, age over 65, volume depletion, sepsis, paraproteinaemia and nephrotoxic drugs. Risk is reported as higher with sucrose-containing products [[FDA-GamunexC,FDA-Bivigam]]
- Hypersensitivity: labels contraindicate use after anaphylactic or severe systemic reaction to human immune globulin, and in IgA-deficient people with anti-IgA antibodies and a history of hypersensitivity. Trace IgA is stated as ≤200 µg/mL (Bivigam, Asceniv) and an average of 46 µg/mL (Gamunex-C) [[FDA-Bivigam,FDA-Asceniv,FDA-GamunexC]]
- Aseptic meningitis syndrome usually begins within several hours to 2 days of treatment, may be more frequent with high doses (2 g/kg) or rapid infusion, and has remitted within several days after stopping. The standard GBS course reaches this 2 g/kg total, though the label wording does not address divided GBS dosing specifically [[FDA-Bivigam]]
- Haemolysis: blood group antibodies in the product can coat red cells. Named risk factors are high doses (for example ≥2 g/kg, single or divided over several days), non-O blood group and underlying inflammatory disease [[FDA-Asceniv]]
- Other label warnings: transfusion-related acute lung injury (typically 1–6 hours after treatment), hyperproteinaemia, increased serum viscosity, true hyponatraemia or pseudohyponatraemia, and transmissible infectious agents (theoretical CJD risk) [[FDA-Bivigam]]
- Passive antibody transfer can confound serological tests, so blood taken after IVIg can mislead [[FDA-GamunexC,Dalakas1998]]
- Common label reactions at ≥5% in Bivigam's immunodeficiency trial included headache, fatigue, infusion-site reaction, nausea, sinusitis, raised blood pressure, diarrhoea, dizziness and lethargy. This was not a GBS population at GBS doses [[FDA-Bivigam]]
- Case-crossover study of US admissions for thrombosis (2005–2014): odds of venous thromboembolism in the 30 days after IVIg for neurological disease were raised (OR 3.33, 95% CI 1.34–8.30), as they were after plasma exchange (OR 4.29, 95% CI 1.88–9.76). Myocardial infarction and ischaemic stroke were not associated [[Jin2020]]
- In SID-GBS, serious adverse events in the first 30 days were 35% with a second course versus 16% with placebo, including thromboembolic events [[Walgaard2021]]
- In the pharmacokinetic sub-analysis of SID-GBS, the three people with a serious thromboembolic event after a second course had lower IgG exposure than others in that group. The authors note this goes against a simple high-IgG explanation (Early evidence; n=3) [[vanTilburg2025]]

### ivig · Second course
- SID-GBS randomised people with mEGOS ≥6 to a second 2 g/kg course or placebo, 7–9 days after inclusion: 49 second course, 44 placebo (modified intention to treat). 327 people were included overall and 112 had a poor prognosis [[Walgaard2021]]
- The adjusted common odds ratio for improvement on the disability score at 4 weeks was 1.4 (95% CI 0.6–3.3; P=0.45) [[Walgaard2021]]
- Four people died in the second-course group, 13–24 weeks after randomisation [[Walgaard2021]]
- The authors concluded a second course "should not be considered" because of a poor prognosis. Before the trial, around 25% of severely affected people worldwide received one [[Walgaard2021]]
- The non-randomised ISID study (IGOS; 237 eligible, 199 one course, 20 early second course, 18 late second course) found adjusted ORs for a better 4-week score of 0.70 (95% CI 0.16–3.04) and 0.66 (0.18–2.50). Small numbers and baseline imbalance limit it [[Verboon2020]]
- The EAN/PNS guideline strongly recommends one standard course only for people with a poor prognosis [[vanDoorn2023]]
- This recommendation concerns prognosis-based re-dosing. It does not cover re-treatment of a treatment-related fluctuation, which the guideline handles separately (distinction drawn by the guideline's structure) [[vanDoorn2023]]
- Practice continues to vary: in a US healthcare database 2001–2018 (2,325 people), 39.7% received repeat IVIg [[Stino2024]]
- At one US centre, 10 of 68 people were re-treated between 2019 and 2025 [[Mayman2026]]
- In IGOS, 82 of 235 people (35%) not improving after first treatment received a second immunomodulatory treatment [[Verboon2019]]

### plex · What it does: removal kinetics
- Plasma exchange removes high-molecular-weight substances from plasma, including autoantibodies, immune complexes and immunoglobulin paraproteins [[Williams2014]]
- It may also alter lymphocyte numbers and function, T-suppressor function and the Th1/Th2 ratio, but "much remains unknown" about its mechanisms (Hypothesis) [[Reeves2014]]
- Each exchange removes a fixed proportion of what is present. The first single plasma volume removes 63% of a substance, the next 23% and the third 9% (first-order model, ignoring redistribution and synthesis) [[Williams2014]]
- Cumulatively that is 63%, 86% and 95% after 1, 2 and 3 plasma volumes (arithmetic by this pass) [[Williams2014]]
- IgG lies partly outside the bloodstream and returns from the extravascular space between sessions, so several exchanges are needed. Six exchanges "would be expected" to lower circulating IgG to one-fifth or one-sixth of baseline [[Williams2014]]
- This is why exchanges are spaced over days: re-equilibration from the extravascular space refills the plasma pool before the next session (link made by this pass) [[Williams2014]]
- No GBS-specific measurement of IgG or anti-ganglioside antibody removal per session was validated in this pass, so these are general apheresis figures (see Open questions) [[Williams2014]]

### plex · Regimen and guideline position
- The EAN/PNS guideline strongly recommends starting plasma exchange as soon as possible in people unable to walk unaided, within 4 weeks of onset [[vanDoorn2023]]
- It strongly recommends four to five exchanges over 1–2 weeks with a total of 12–15 L for people unable to walk unaided, bedridden or ventilated; that is about 3 L per exchange [[vanDoorn2023]]
- It weakly recommends two exchanges for people who can walk unaided but cannot run (grade 2) within the first 2 weeks [[vanDoorn2023]]
- The ten-steps guideline quotes 200–250 mL plasma/kg over five sessions [[Leonhard2019]]
- The effect is most evident when treatment starts in the first week, and was demonstrable only when started within 4 weeks [[vanDoorn2023]]
- The AAN parameter recommended plasma exchange for nonambulant adults within 4 weeks and said it should be considered for ambulant people within 2 weeks [[Hughes2003]]
- A review of the ASFA framework states strong evidence supports plasma exchange in GBS [[Memon2026]]

### plex · Trials
- Swedish trial (1984): 38 people with severe disease; benefit in time to improvement, weakness and disability grades over the first 2 months, and a cost-benefit analysis showed net savings [[Osterman1984]]
- North American trial (1985): 245 people. Improvement at 4 weeks, time to improve one grade, time to independent walking and 6-month outcome all favoured plasmapheresis, especially with treatment within 7 days of onset and in those needing ventilation after entry [[GBSStudyGroup1985]]
- The regimen in that trial removed 200–250 mL plasma/kg in five exchanges over 7–14 days [[vanDoorn2023]]
- French trial (1987): 220 people treated within 17 days, 111 controls and 109 plasma exchange (four exchanges of two plasma volumes each, on alternate days). Plasma exchange reduced the need for ventilation after randomisation and shortened time to weaning, motor recovery and walking [[FrenchCooperative1987]]
- In that trial median time to walk with aid was 30 days with plasma exchange and 44 days without [[Chevret2017]]
- One-year follow-up: full muscle strength in 71% with plasma exchange versus 52% of controls (P=0.007). Severe motor disability was 11% in both groups [[FrenchCooperative1992]]
- Cochrane 2017 pooled six trials and 649 participants against supportive care. Improvement by one or more grades at 4 weeks: RR 1.64 (95% CI 1.37–1.96; 5 trials, 623 people) [[Chevret2017]]
- Recovered walking with aid at 4 weeks: RR 1.60 (95% CI 1.19–2.15; 3 trials, 349 people) [[Chevret2017]]
- Recovered walking without aid: RR 1.72 (95% CI 1.06–2.79; 3 trials, 349 people) [[Chevret2017]]
- Need for ventilation: RR 0.53 (95% CI 0.39–0.74; 5 trials, 623 people) [[Chevret2017]]
- Full strength at 1 year: RR 1.24 (95% CI 1.07–1.45; 5 trials, 404 people). Severe motor sequelae: RR 0.65 (95% CI 0.44–0.96; 6 trials, 649 people) [[Chevret2017]]
- Death: RR 0.86 (95% CI 0.45–1.65), which neither confirms nor refutes a lower risk [[Chevret2017]]
- Relapse by the end of follow-up was more frequent with plasma exchange: RR 2.89 (95% CI 1.05–7.93) [[Chevret2017]]
- An earlier review gave a weighted mean difference at 4 weeks of -0.89 grades (95% CI -1.14 to -0.63) in four trials with 585 severely affected adults [[Hughes2007]]

### plex · How many exchanges
- French trial (1997) randomised 556 people by severity: 0 versus 2 exchanges (mild), 2 versus 4 (moderate) and 4 versus 6 (ventilated) [[FrenchCooperative1997]]
- The severity groups were 91 mild, 304 moderate and 161 ventilated [[vanDoorn2023]]
- Mild group: two exchanges beat none for time to onset of motor recovery (median 4 versus 8 days) [[FrenchCooperative1997]]
- Moderate group: four beat two for time to walk with aid (median 20 versus 24 days) and for full strength at 1 year (64% versus 46%) [[FrenchCooperative1997]]
- Severe group: six were no better than four [[FrenchCooperative1997]]
- Sources differ on the mild-group timing: the 1997 abstract reports median 4 versus 8 days to onset of motor recovery [[FrenchCooperative1997]]; Cochrane reports median 6 versus 10 days in 91 mildly affected participants, and the reason could not be checked from abstracts [[Chevret2017]]

### plex · Replacement fluid, complications and practicalities
- Albumin versus fresh frozen plasma in the French trial (57 versus 52 people): no significant efficacy difference, more incidents and complications with fresh frozen plasma, and the authors recommended abandoning it [[FrenchCooperative1987]]
- At 1 year, full recovery was 77% with fresh frozen plasma and 65% with albumin (P=0.22) [[FrenchCooperative1992]]
- Fibrinogen and prothrombin fell more with albumin replacement [[vanDoorn2023]]
- The guideline does not advise on a specific plasma exchange type or replacement fluid [[vanDoorn2023]]
- Severe cardiovascular autonomic instability is a relative contraindication with discontinuous machines because of large volume shifts; this is less of a problem with continuous-flow machines [[vanDoorn2023]]
- Cochrane (3 trials, 556 people) found no increase in serious infections compared with controls (RR 0.91, 95% CI 0.73–1.13) and no clear difference in blood pressure instability, arrhythmias or pulmonary emboli [[Chevret2017]]
- General apheresis series (1,895 sessions, 185 people, not GBS-specific): at least one adverse effect in 42.5% of sessions, mostly not serious [[Coirier2022]]
- In that series hypotension occurred in 15.2% of sessions (asymptomatic in 95.8%; 19.8% with 4% albumin versus 8.9% with fresh frozen plasma) and hypocalcaemia in 19.6% [[Coirier2022]]
- Allergic reactions occurred in 3% of sessions, exclusively with fresh frozen plasma; severe adverse effects in 0.3% of sessions and 5.4% of people [[Coirier2022]]
- Plasma exchange showed a venous thromboembolism signal similar to IVIg in administrative data (OR 4.29 within 30 days) [[Jin2020]]
- Central venous catheter risks include severe sepsis and catheter-related deep vein thrombosis, the pre-specified serious events in the Bangladeshi small-volume study [[Islam2018]]
- Paediatric series: tachycardia, hypotension and mild anaemia were most common in 36 children (122 sessions), and three had serious complications [[Ali2024]]
- In a French mixed cohort of 23 children, one child with one of the included neuroimmune disorders died from venous air embolism, a rare but real procedural risk [[Fleurance2025]]
- The US Nationwide Inpatient Sample (2000–2005, 1,657 people) showed higher mortality with plasma exchange than IVIg (3.3% versus 0.45%), but those treated were older and sicker. This is confounded and is not evidence that plasma exchange is more dangerous [[Alshekhlee2008]]

### plex · Combining or sequencing treatments
- Plasma exchange followed by IVIg: mean improvement 0.2 grade greater than plasma exchange alone (95% CI -0.14 to 0.54; 249 participants), not clinically significant but not excluding benefit [[Hughes2014]]
- The EAN/PNS guideline strongly recommends against plasma exchange followed immediately by IVIg, compared with either alone [[vanDoorn2023]]
- It also does not suggest switching to the other treatment in people who do not improve, for lack of trial evidence, and notes that exchanging plasma right after IVIg washes out the treatment just infused [[vanDoorn2023]]
- In US practice 6.1% of 2,325 people received combination therapy [[Stino2024]]
- Zipper method: alternating plasma exchange and 0.4 g/kg IVIg for 5 cycles in nine ventilated children, uncontrolled, with all nine walking unaided on day 28. Authors reported this as promising (Early evidence) [[Kesici2019]]
- The modified version (48-hour intervals, 7–10 cycles) was applied to four children with different neuroimmune disorders after standard therapy failed (Early evidence) [[Nikolaus2022]]
- The EAN/PNS guideline says the zipper schedule needs an appropriate-scale RCT before any objective effect can be claimed [[vanDoorn2023]]
- Immunoadsorption: the guideline does not recommend it; one RCT was stopped early (23 IVIg, 26 plasma exchange, 18 immunoadsorption) without significant benefit [[vanDoorn2023]]

### plex · Resource-limited settings and cost
- In Bangladesh, a full IVIg course for a 60 kg adult was estimated at US$12,000–16,000 and conventional plasma exchange at US$4,500–5,000, about 4,000 and 1,250 days of mean income [[Islam2018]]
- Only 10–12% of people with GBS in Bangladesh receive either treatment [[Papri2021]]
- In IGOS, 83% of 189 Bangladeshi participants were untreated, whereas 92% of 1,023 participants elsewhere received immunotherapy [[Verboon2019]]
- Among 159 Bangladeshi physicians surveyed, high cost of standard therapy was named a major challenge and 12% used specific treatment protocols [[Papri2023]]
- Small-volume plasma exchange (SVPE) separates plasma by sedimentation in a blood bag and returns the blood cells, repeated three to six times a day for eight consecutive days [[Islam2018]]
- It was designed as an affordable alternative: most people with GBS in Bangladesh cannot afford standard treatment [[Islam2017]]
- SVPE phase II (20 adults, Dhaka, single arm): median 140 mL/kg exchanged (range 110–175), and 8 L removed in 15 of 20 (75%). One serious adverse event (severe sepsis, possibly catheter-related) and no deaths [[Islam2018]]
- In that study, 14 of 20 (70%) improved by at least one grade at 4 weeks, which is not a controlled efficacy result. Expected cost was about US$500 [[Islam2018]]
- SVPE used fresh frozen plasma, which delivered IgG at a median of 1 g/kg in the abstract and 0.9 g/kg (range 0.6–1.3) in the full text, although much was probably removed again [[Islam2018]]
- The guidelines do not endorse SVPE for general use until efficacy is shown; the EAN/PNS panel rated it very low certainty [[Leonhard2019,vanDoorn2023]]
- Cost comparison, US hospital model: five IVIg infusions $10,329.85 versus five plasma exchanges $4,638.16 (direct costs) [[Winters2011]]
- Cost comparison, Indian randomised study (40 people): out-of-pocket INR 219,247 (US$4,298) with IVIg versus INR 104,070 (US$2,040.5) with plasma exchange; 53% higher overall with IVIg [[Maheshwari2018]]
- In one low-income setting, plasma exchange was reported as more cost-effective and accessible than IVIg [[Ali2024]]
- Costs date quickly and vary by setting, so these figures show relative differences only (note by this pass) [[Winters2011,Maheshwari2018]]

### whatnot · Corticosteroids do not work
- Cochrane 2016 (six trials, 587 participants): disability change at 4 weeks not significantly different, with 0.36 grade less improvement on corticosteroids (95% CI 0.16 more to 0.88 less) [[Hughes2016]]
- Oral corticosteroids (four trials, 120 participants; very low quality): 0.82 grade less improvement (95% CI 0.17–1.47 less). This is the signal for possible harm [[Hughes2016]]
- Intravenous corticosteroids (two trials, 467 participants): no significant difference (0.17 grade more improvement, 95% CI -0.06 to 0.39) [[Hughes2016]]
- Improvement by one or more grades: RR 1.08 (95% CI 0.93–1.24). Death or disability at 1 year: RR 1.51 (95% CI 0.91–2.5) [[Hughes2016]]
- Diabetes requiring insulin was more common (RR 2.21, 95% CI 1.19–4.12) and hypertension less common (RR 0.15, 95% CI 0.05–0.41) with corticosteroids (high quality) [[Hughes2016]]
- 1978 trial: prednisolone in 21 people versus 19 controls. Six prednisolone patients and one control had considerable disability, and there were three relapses with prednisolone and none in controls [[Hughes1978]]
- 1993 trial: IV methylprednisolone 500 mg daily for 5 days (n=124) versus placebo (n=118). The 4-week difference was only 0.06 grade (95% CI -0.23 to 0.36); median time to unaided walking 38 versus 50 days, not significant [[GBSSteroidTrial1993]]
- Dutch pilot (1994): methylprednisolone plus IVIg in 25 people, compared with historical IVIg controls: 76% versus 53% improved a grade at 4 weeks (P=0.04). This uncontrolled signal did not hold up in the later RCT [[DutchGBS1994,vanKoningsveld2004]]
- Dutch RCT (2004): IVIg plus IV methylprednisolone 500 mg/day for 5 days (n=112 analysed) versus IVIg plus placebo (n=113). Improvement of one or more grades was 68% versus 56% (OR 1.68, 95% CI 0.97–2.88; P=0.06) [[vanKoningsveld2004]]
- After adjustment for age and entry disability, the OR was 1.89 (95% CI 1.07–3.35; P=0.03). The authors reported no significant difference, called for further investigation and noted limited side-effects [[vanKoningsveld2004]]
- The EAN/PNS guideline strongly recommends against oral corticosteroids and weakly recommends against IV methylprednisolone alone or added to IVIg [[vanDoorn2023]]
- The AAN parameter stated corticosteroids are not recommended [[Hughes2003]]

### whatnot · Time windows and mild disease
- IVIg trials started within 2 weeks of onset, and plasma exchange trials within 4 weeks. Beyond these periods, evidence on efficacy is lacking [[Leonhard2019]]
- Treatment evidence comes mainly from adults with typical, severe GBS. Mild forms, variants and late presenters remain clinical dilemmas [[Verboon2017]]
- IGOS mild GBS (188 able to walk independently; 148 IVIg, 40 supportive care): IVIg was not associated with lower 4-week disability (adjusted OR 1.62, 95% CI 0.63–4.13). Confounding and wide limits restrict certainty [[Verboon2021]]
- In that study, time to full strength was shorter with IVIg (28 versus 56 days, P=0.03) and pain at 26 weeks lower (22% versus 40%, P=0.04). At 1 year, 40% of all participants had residual symptoms [[Verboon2021]]
- Plasma exchange has RCT support in mild disease: two exchanges beat none in time to motor recovery [[FrenchCooperative1997]]
- The guideline advises treatment for people still walking within 4 weeks if they have fast deterioration, ventilation risk, swallowing difficulty, autonomic disturbance or poor prognostic factors [[vanDoorn2023]]
- It suggests considering treatment for grade 2 within 2 weeks with stable or slowly deteriorating disease, especially with arm weakness or cranial nerve involvement [[vanDoorn2023]]
- It does not advise treatment in (a) grade 1 with stable disease in the first 2 weeks or (b) grade 1–2 with stable disease at 2–4 weeks, as benefit seems unlikely and treatment is expensive with side effects [[vanDoorn2023]]
- Treated despite thin evidence: in IGOS, 75% of mild GBS (126/168) and 76% of Miller Fisher syndrome (53/70) received immunotherapy, against 97% of severe GBS (724/743) [[Verboon2019]]
- Miller Fisher syndrome: in 92 people, neither IVIg (n=28) nor plasmapheresis (n=23) altered outcome compared with no immune treatment (n=41), presumably because natural recovery is good [[Mori2007]]
- The ten-steps guideline does not generally recommend treatment for pure Miller Fisher syndrome but advises close monitoring, as a subgroup develops limb, bulbar or respiratory involvement [[Leonhard2019]]

### whatnot · Fluctuations, acute-onset CIDP and recurrence
- A treatment-related fluctuation is disease progression within 2 months after initial treatment-induced improvement or stabilisation. It must be separated from progression with no initial response [[Leonhard2019]]
- The general view is that the treatment effect wears off while inflammation is still active (Hypothesis) [[Leonhard2019]]
- Fluctuation rate in guidelines: 6–10% [[Leonhard2019]]
- Fluctuation rate in the Dutch trial: 6/72 with plasma exchange and 8/74 with IVIg (8.3% and 10.8%, arithmetic by this pass) [[Kleyweg1991]]
- Fluctuation rate in Dutch cohorts: 16/172 (9.3%, arithmetic by this pass) [[Visser1998]] and 16/170 (9%) in the prospective study [[Ruts2010]]
- Fluctuation rate in IGOS: 53/1,023 (5%) [[Verboon2019]]
- Risk is unrelated to treatment type. None with preceding gastrointestinal illness, predominant distal weakness, acute motor neuropathy or anti-GM1 antibodies fluctuated, and those who did trended to a protracted course [[Visser1998]]
- Timing: first fluctuation always within 8 weeks of onset (median 18 days, range 10–54), and 5 of 16 (31%) had a second, with none having more [[Ruts2010]]
- In IGOS, 36 of 53 (68%) with a fluctuation were re-treated with IVIg or plasma exchange [[Verboon2019]]
- The EAN/PNS guideline suggests considering re-treatment with IVIg or plasma exchange after a fluctuation. There is no RCT, only observational data [[vanDoorn2023]]
- A-CIDP frequency: 8 of 170 (5%) in the prospective study and about 5% in guidelines [[Ruts2010,vanDoorn2023,Leonhard2019]]
- A-CIDP should be considered with deterioration after 8 weeks from onset or three or more episodes of worsening [[Ruts2010]]; the earlier retrospective series proposed 9 weeks [[Ruts2005]]
- Accuracy for A-CIDP: further worsening after 8 weeks has sensitivity 100% and specificity 92%; three or more fluctuations has sensitivity 52% and specificity 96% [[vanDoorn2023]]
- People with A-CIDP were less severely affected, did not need ventilation and rarely had cranial nerve involvement [[Ruts2010]]
- Sources differ widely on reclassification: 32.0% of people first coded GBS were later coded CIDP in a US claims database [[Stino2024]]; prospective clinical data and guidelines give about 5%, and claims coding is the less reliable of the two (judgement by this pass) [[Ruts2010,vanDoorn2023]]
- Recurrence: relapses of GBS occur in 2–5% [[Leonhard2019]]
- Survey data: nine confirmed recurrences in 245 people with GBS (3.7%, arithmetic by this pass) [[Kuitwaard2009Survey]]
- Recurrent GBS series (32 people, 81 episodes; defined by at least 2 months between episodes after full recovery, or 4 months if partial): mean age 34.2 versus 46.9 years in non-recurrent cases. Recurrence was more frequent under 30, with milder symptoms and in Miller Fisher syndrome [[Kuitwaard2009Recurrent]]
- Symptoms in later episodes were often similar, while severity and preceding infection varied [[Kuitwaard2009Recurrent]]

### whatnot · Other agents
- Cochrane found six trials of five other interventions with 151 participants in total, all very low certainty on the main outcome, and none showing a clinically important difference [[Doets2020]]
- Interferon beta-1a versus placebo (19 participants): mean difference -0.1 grade (95% CI -1.58 to 1.38) [[Doets2020]]
- Eculizumab versus placebo (2 trials, 41 participants): mean difference -0.23 (95% CI -1.79 to 1.34) [[Doets2020]]
- JET-GBS (phase 2, Japan): 61% (90% CI 42–78) with eculizumab plus IVIg versus 45% (20–73) with placebo plus IVIg walked independently at 4 weeks; the primary response threshold was not met (Early evidence) [[Misawa2018]]
- ICA-GBS: 8 randomised; two of five on eculizumab and two of two on placebo had improved a grade at 4 weeks (Early evidence) [[Davidson2017]]
- The EAN/PNS guideline weakly recommends against eculizumab. It strongly recommends against alemtuzumab, BDNF, CSF filtration, cyclophosphamide, interferon beta-1a, muromonab-CD3, mycophenolate mofetil and tripterygium polyglycoside [[vanDoorn2023]]
- ANX005 (anti-C1q) had reached placebo-controlled study in Bangladesh by 2022, with preliminary results described as promising (Early evidence; detail in the frontier pass) [[Rajabally2022]]

### whatnot · What to expect from treatment
- Treatment speeds recovery but does not cure everyone. About 20% of treated people cannot walk at 6 months and about 5% die as a consequence of GBS (population statistics) [[Leonhard2024]]
- About 40% of people on standard plasma exchange or IVIg do not improve in the first 4 weeks. This does not imply the treatment failed, as progression might have been worse without it [[Leonhard2019]]
- In IGOS, 235 people (32%) did not improve after their initial treatment. The abstract does not state which subgroup forms the denominator [[Verboon2019]]
- 60–80% can walk independently 6 months after onset, with or without treatment [[Leonhard2019]]
- About 25% need artificial ventilation and 20% are still unable to walk unaided after 6 months [[vanDoorn2010]]
- About 20% die or have persistent disability despite immunotherapy [[Hughes2007]]
- Neither treatment has been shown to reduce mortality [[Hughes2007,Chevret2017]]
- In IGOS, 92% of participants outside Bangladesh received immunotherapy, so treatment reached most but not all of them (observational cohort) [[Verboon2019]]

### whatnot · Pregnancy
- Neither IVIg nor plasma exchange is contraindicated in pregnancy, but as plasma exchange needs additional monitoring, IVIg might be preferred [[Leonhard2019]]
- The EAN/PNS guideline makes no pregnancy-specific treatment recommendation in the sections reviewed; pregnancy appears as an antecedent event (reading by this pass) [[vanDoorn2023]]
- IVIg labels state no human data on drug-associated risk, immune globulins cross the placenta, and (Bivigam) transfer rises after 30 weeks; the Bivigam label says give only if clearly needed [[FDA-Bivigam,FDA-Asceniv]]
- A 2026 systematic review of case reports (101 cases, so biased toward severe or unusual cases): ICU admission 64%, ventilation 45%, maternal mortality 5.2%, preterm birth 36%, caesarean delivery 54.8%, neonatal survival 85.6% [[Yakubu2026]]

### Exam hooks
- First-line immunotherapy for GBS: IVIg or plasma exchange. They are equally effective, so the choice is driven by access and patient factors [[vanDoorn2023,Hughes2014]]
- IVIg regimen as published: 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days (total 2 g/kg) [[vanDoorn2023]]
- Plasma exchange regimen as published: 200–250 mL/kg over five sessions, or four to five exchanges totalling 12–15 L over 1–2 weeks [[Leonhard2019,vanDoorn2023]]
- Windows: IVIg within 2 weeks; plasma exchange within 4 weeks [[Leonhard2019,vanDoorn2023]]
- Corticosteroids alone do not help, and oral corticosteroids may delay recovery [[Hughes2016]]
- Combining plasma exchange then IVIg adds no significant benefit [[PESGBS1997,Hughes2014]]
- A second IVIg course for poor prognosis showed no benefit and more serious adverse events (35% versus 16%) [[Walgaard2021]]
- Plasma exchange RR for needing ventilation: 0.53 [[Chevret2017]]
- Plasma exchange RR for improving one or more grades at 4 weeks: 1.64 [[Chevret2017]]
- Number of exchanges by severity (French 1997): mild, two better than none; moderate, four better than two; ventilated, six no better than four [[FrenchCooperative1997]]
- Fluctuation versus A-CIDP: fluctuation occurs within 8 weeks; suspect A-CIDP with worsening after 8 weeks or three or more episodes [[Ruts2010]]
- Treatment-related fluctuations affect 6–10%; A-CIDP about 5%; recurrence 2–5% [[Leonhard2019]]
- IVIg boxed warning: thrombosis, renal dysfunction and acute renal failure [[FDA-Bivigam]]
- IgA deficiency with anti-IgA antibodies and hypersensitivity history is a label contraindication to IVIg [[FDA-Bivigam]]
- A low IgG rise after IVIg correlates with poorer outcome, but giving more IVIg did not help in SID-GBS [[Kuitwaard2009Pharm,Walgaard2021]]
- Autonomic instability is a relative contraindication to discontinuous plasma exchange [[vanDoorn2023]]
- Fresh frozen plasma should not be the routine replacement fluid: more complications, no clear efficacy gain [[FrenchCooperative1987]]
- Pure Miller Fisher syndrome usually recovers without treatment [[Leonhard2019,Mori2007]]
- Worsening does not mean failure: about 40% do not improve in the first 4 weeks despite treatment [[Leonhard2019]]

### In plain words
- IVIg is a drip of antibodies collected from many blood donors. It is usually given once a day for 5 days [[vanDoorn2023,Lunemann2015]]
- Plasma exchange takes out the liquid part of your blood and replaces it, to remove harmful antibodies. It is usually done four or five times over 1 to 2 weeks [[vanDoorn2023,Chevret2017]]
- The two treatments work about equally well. Your team chooses based on what suits you and what the hospital can offer [[vanDoorn2023]]
- These treatments help you recover faster. They do not switch the illness off straight away [[Hughes2014,Chevret2017]]
- You may not improve in the first weeks, even with treatment. This is common, and it does not mean the treatment failed [[Leonhard2019]]
- Having both treatments one after the other has not been shown to work better than one alone [[vanDoorn2023]]
- Steroid medicines help some similar nerve conditions, but they have not helped GBS in trials [[GBSSteroidTrial1993,Hughes2016]]
- If you get better and then worse again within a couple of months, tell your team. A repeat treatment may be considered [[Leonhard2019,vanDoorn2023]]
- If you get worse after 8 weeks, doctors may check for a related long-term condition called CIDP [[vanDoorn2023]]
- IVIg can cause headache. Rarely it can cause blood clots, kidney problems or a reaction of the lining around the brain. Your team will watch for these [[FDA-Bivigam]]
- Plasma exchange often needs a large line in a vein. Blood pressure can drop during a session, and your team monitors this [[Coirier2022,Islam2018]]
- Getting GBS a second time is uncommon, at about 2 to 5 in 100 people [[Leonhard2019]]
- Both treatments are options in pregnancy. Doctors may prefer IVIg because plasma exchange needs extra monitoring [[Leonhard2019]]
- These treatments cost a lot and are out of reach for many people in poorer countries. Researchers are testing cheaper methods [[Islam2018,Papri2021]]

## Open questions and controversies
- Which comes first, IVIg or plasma exchange? Position A: equal, no preference [[vanDoorn2023]]. Position B: usually IVIg, for ease and availability [[Leonhard2019]]. Position C: plasma exchange on cost in some systems [[Winters2011,Maheshwari2018]]. Status: choice depends on setting, with no efficacy difference shown.
- Does a low IgG rise cause poor outcome? Position A: IVIg exposure matters and higher dosing might help [[Kuitwaard2009Pharm]]. Position B: low ΔIgG may reflect severity, and a second course gave no benefit [[Rajabally2022,Walgaard2021]]. Status: association established; model-informed dosing is untested [[vanTilburg2025]]
- Should people still walking be treated? Position A: trial of two exchanges supports treatment, and the guideline allows it with risk features [[FrenchCooperative1997,vanDoorn2023]]. Position B: observational IVIg data show no clear overall benefit, within confounded limits [[Verboon2021]]. Status: no adult RCT of IVIg; good practice points only.
- Is IV methylprednisolone with IVIg truly ineffective? Position A: unadjusted result not significant, and guideline weakly against [[vanKoningsveld2004,vanDoorn2023]]. Position B: adjusted OR 1.89 suggested possible benefit [[vanKoningsveld2004]]. Status: no confirmatory trial identified in this pass.
- Does the zipper method help? Position A: uncontrolled paediatric series are promising [[Kesici2019,Nikolaus2022]]. Position B: it washes out IVIg and needs an RCT [[vanDoorn2023]]. Status: unproven.
- Can low-cost plasma exchange replace standard care? Position A: SVPE is feasible and seems safe in 20 adults [[Islam2018]]. Position B: efficacy is unknown and it is not recommended for general use [[Leonhard2019,vanDoorn2023]]. Status: awaiting a controlled trial.
- Is switching after no response useful? Position A: common practice [[Verboon2019,Stino2024]]. Position B: no trial evidence and not suggested by the guideline [[vanDoorn2023]]. Status: open.
- Does plasma exchange increase relapse? Position A: Cochrane shows more relapses (RR 2.89) [[Chevret2017]]. Position B: long-term outcomes still favour plasma exchange [[Chevret2017]]. Status: small absolute signal, wide CI.
- How many steroid trials exist? The ten-steps guideline cites eight RCTs [[Leonhard2019]]; Cochrane reports six trials with outcome data [[Hughes2016]]. Status: likely a counting difference; use six for meta-analytic statements.
- How much antibody does one exchange remove in GBS? General kinetics are available [[Williams2014]], but no validated GBS-specific per-session measure was found here. Builders should label the removal animation as a general model. The extravascular IgG share quoted in that review (30–45%) should not be animated as a precise split.

## Provenance
- Validation: all 67 journal sources were checked in a batch PubMed esummary call on 2026-11-09 (authors, title, journal, year, PMID, DOI, PMCID). Group-authored records list no personal authors in PubMed, so the group name is used. Labels were retrieved from openFDA by set ID.
- Numbers came from PubMed abstracts, except as follows:
  - Open full text from Europe PMC: Leonhard2019, Islam2018, Papri2021, vanTilburg2025 and Rajabally2022.
  - vanDoorn2023: the open PDF of the J Peripher Nerv Syst co-publication, repository copy.
  - Williams2014: read through a web fetch of the PMC page because the XML route returned no body text. The phrases were quoted by an automated reader, and a direct re-check was blocked, so the 63/23/9% series, the 22-day half-life, the one-fifth to one-sixth figure and the 30–45% extravascular share should be re-read before any verbatim use. The extravascular figure is flagged as not suitable for animation.
- Not accessible: Wiley full text of the guideline, NEJM and Lancet full texts, French trial full texts, the Kuitwaard 2009 Ann Neurol full text and the Cochrane full analyses (PMC holds only abstract and plain-language summary). Figures present only in those documents are absent here, including ventilation percentages in the 1992 Dutch trial and exact Cochrane discontinuation rates.
- Dropped: American Society for Apheresis category statements for GBS and the 2023 and 2026 special issues, because the fact sheet could not be read; a claim of removal of anti-ganglioside antibody per session, because no validated measurement was found; complement and Fc-specific human GBS claims beyond those listed; and a commentary meta-analysis without an abstract (Hashmi 2026).
- openFDA records for Gamunex-C, Privigen and Gammagard Liquid are old snapshots. Brand-name searches in openFDA returned no current versions, so current indications for those brands were not confirmed. "Off-label in the US" is an inference from label text and is not a regulator statement.
