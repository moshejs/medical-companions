## Findings

### breathing · How often and how fast
- Mechanical ventilation (MV) is needed by about 20% of people with GBS (consensus guideline); up to 22% need it in the first week of admission [[Leonhard2019]].
- Older and trial-based cohorts quote higher figures: "up to 30%" [[Lawn2001]], 30% [[Sharshar2003]], 20–30% [[Durand2006]]. Unselected modern cohorts run lower: 16% (185/1133) in IGOS [[Luijten2023]] and 12% during follow-up (121/1023) in the EGRIS validation set [[Doets2022]]. The spread reflects case mix: trial cohorts enrolled severe disease, while IGOS includes mild disease and variants [[Luijten2023]].
- Among 722 French trial adults not ventilated at admission, 313 (43%) went on to endotracheal ventilation; this is a severe trial population, not all GBS [[Sharshar2003]].
- A Dutch pooled cohort: 150/552 (27%) required MV during 6 months [[Walgaard2017]]; a second Dutch cohort: 145/526 (28%) [[vanDenBerg2018]]. A Chinese single-centre retrospective series: 80/541 (14.8%) [[Wu2015]]. A US inpatient database: 9.1% had endotracheal intubation among 4,954 adults [[Alshekhlee2008]].
- In IGOS (full text), median time from onset of weakness to starting MV was 4 days (IQR 3–8, range 0–44); 149/182 (82%) who needed MV started within 1 week of admission [[Luijten2023]].
- In the IGOS prediction set, 126/1034 (12%) needed MV within 0 to 33 days of admission, 98/126 (78%) within the first week [[Luijten2023]].
- In the Dutch cohort (full text), people were intubated a median of 1 day after admission (IQR 0–4) [[Walgaard2017]].
- GBS can bring on respiratory insufficiency within hours to days, and failure to recognise it can cause death or hypoxic disability [[vanDoorn2023]].

### breathing · Why breathing fails
- The mechanism is progressive weakness of both inspiratory and expiratory muscles; aspiration pneumonia and atelectasis follow from bulbar weakness and poor cough [[Orlikowski2004]].
- Classical signs of respiratory distress appear too late to guide management; vital capacity and static pressures are the useful measures [[Orlikowski2004]].
- Respiratory failure can occur without the symptom of breathlessness, so function must be measured routinely [[Leonhard2019]].
- Unexplained deterioration can also have a non-muscular cause: one case of severe dysautonomia had intrapulmonary shunt up to six times normal (single case report; Early evidence) [[Sykora2008]].

### breathing · Bedside tests and thresholds
- 20/30/40 rule: risk of respiratory failure if vital capacity (VC) <20 mL/kg, maximal inspiratory pressure (MIP) <30 cmH2O, or maximal expiratory pressure (MEP) <40 cmH2O, or a fall of more than 30% in any of the three [[Lawn2001]]. Source population: 114 consecutive ICU patients with severe GBS at one tertiary centre, 1976–1996 (60 ventilated, 54 not) [[Lawn2001]].
- No clinical feature predicted the pattern of respiratory decline, but serial pulmonary function testing identified those at risk [[Lawn2001]].
- Clinical features in the same series linked to progression to MV: rapid disease progression, bulbar dysfunction, bilateral facial weakness, dysautonomia [[Lawn2001]].
- Serial VC, prospective, n=10: in the 5 who needed intubation, VC fell over the 48 h before respiratory failure, and intubation came at 15.2 ± 3.7 mL/kg; in the 5 who did not, VC stayed stable above 40 mL/kg [[Chevrolet1991]].
- In 81 intubated French patients, mean VC was 57 ± 22% of predicted at admission and 33 ± 11% at intubation [[Orlikowski2006]].
- Single breath count (SBC): the ten-steps guideline states a count of ≤19 predicts need for MV, and inability to count to 15 is one sign of imminent respiratory insufficiency [[Leonhard2019]]. EAN/PNS states SBC <20 (cannot count aloud from 1 to 20 in one breath) is a useful bedside tool for ICU transfer [[vanDoorn2023]].
- In a prospective Indian neuro-ICU cohort, neck weakness, bulbar weakness, SBC and forced vital capacity were independent predictors; the NSB (neck, SBC, bulbar) score at a cutoff of 60 had sensitivity 95% and specificity 93%, and SBC correlated with spirometry and phrenic latency [[KannanKanikannan2014]].
- Imminent respiratory insufficiency in the ten-steps guideline: breathlessness at rest or while talking, cannot count to 15, accessory muscle use, raised respiratory or heart rate, VC <15–20 mL/kg or <1 L, or abnormal blood gas or pulse oximetry [[Leonhard2019]].
- EAN/PNS good practice point (full text): check FVC three to six times a day depending on severity; about 4-hourly while FVC is falling [[vanDoorn2023]].
- EAN/PNS FVC triggers (full text): a fall >30% below predicted baseline is a concern; a >30% fall in 24 h likely means immediate ICU transfer; a 50% fall in under 24 h likely means ventilation; consider elective ventilation at FVC ≤20 mL/kg; at ≤10 mL/kg ventilation is almost inevitable [[vanDoorn2023]].
- Laboratory hints of higher MV risk per EAN/PNS: raised liver function tests, HSV or CMV infection, or demyelinating electrodiagnostic features [[vanDoorn2023]].
- In one blood-gas protocol, single breath count below 12 with respiratory rate >30/min defined respiratory compromise, and intubation followed for PaO2 <60 mmHg on venturi mask, PaCO2 >50 mmHg or pH <7.3; this was a deliberately conservative policy, not the guideline standard [[Kalita2016]].
- Abnormal blood gases are listed among signs of imminent failure, not as an early screen, and VC and static pressures are the measures advised for timing [[Leonhard2019,Orlikowski2004]].
- Non-invasive ventilation: an ICU review considers it inappropriate as a treatment in GBS because of bulbar dysfunction and lack of rapid improvement, reserving it for pre-oxygenation [[Weiss2025]]. A 2003 review of neurological patients in general saw an important role and possible expansion [[Rabinstein2003]].

### breathing · Early predictors
- Six independent predictors in 722 French adults (odds ratios): cannot cough 9.09, cannot lift head 4.34, cannot lift elbows 2.99, cannot stand 2.53, onset-to-admission <7 days 2.51, raised liver enzymes 2.09 [[Sharshar2003]].
- With VC measured (n=196): onset-to-admission <7 days OR 5.00, cannot lift head OR 5.00, VC <60% OR 2.86 [[Sharshar2003]].
- MV was required in >85% of those with at least four predictors from the first model, and in 85% of those with all three predictors of the second model [[Sharshar2003]].
- Prospective cohort (n=154, 34 ventilated, 22%): probability of ventilation was below 2.5% when the peroneal proximal/distal CMAP ratio exceeded 55.6% and VC exceeded 81% of predicted [[Durand2006]].
- Demyelinating GBS was commoner in those later ventilated (85% vs 51%, p=0.0003) [[Durand2006]].
- Meta-analysis of 34 studies: bulbar weakness OR 9.0 (95% CI 3.94–20.6), neck weakness OR 6.36 (2.32–17.5), autonomic instability OR 6.40 (2.83–14.5), facial weakness OR 3.74 (2.05–6.81) [[Green2018]]. Autonomic instability and facial weakness were not significant in individual multivariable analyses, and models (AUC 0.79–0.96) looked more reliable than single variables [[Green2018]].
- Bulbar involvement, short onset-to-admission time and low MRC sum score are rated high-certainty predictors by EAN/PNS; neck weakness, higher disability, low VC and hypoalbuminaemia are moderate-certainty [[vanDoorn2023]].
- Baseline plasma cortisol was the only independent predictor of respiratory failure beyond 24 h after adjusting for validated clinical predictors (OR 2.45 per 10 ng/mL), in 93 patients; research marker, not routine (Early evidence) [[Strauss2009]].
- Anxiety: in 110 adults, uncertainty about the disease course was independently associated with later MV (23 ventilated, 22%); subjective distress may signal deterioration (Early evidence) [[Sharshar2012]].

### breathing · EGRIS and mEGRIS
- EGRIS items: days from onset of weakness to admission (>7 = 0; 4–7 = 1; ≤3 = 2), facial and/or bulbar weakness at admission (absent = 0; present = 1), MRC sum score at admission (51–60 = 0; 41–50 = 1; 31–40 = 2; 21–30 = 3; ≤20 = 4); total 0–7 [[Doets2022,Leonhard2019]].
- Risk bands of needing MV within 1 week: EGRIS 0–2 low (4%), 3–4 intermediate (24%), ≥5 high (65%) [[Leonhard2019]].
- The range is stated as 1 to 91% in the derivation abstract [[Walgaard2010]] and as 1 to 90% in later papers [[Leonhard2019,Doets2022]].
- Derivation: 397 prospective Dutch patients, 22% ventilated in the first week, AUC 0.84; validation: 191 patients, 14% ventilated, AUC 0.82 [[Walgaard2010]].
- International validation (IGOS, n=1,023, aged ≥6, includes mild disease and variants): 104 (10%) ventilated in the first week; AUC ≥0.80 in all subgroups; EGRIS over-predicted in full cohort (10% observed vs 21% predicted) and Europe/North America (9% vs 21%), less so in Asia (17% vs 23%) [[Doets2022]]. A recalibrated EGRIS-Europe/North America model corrected this [[Doets2022]].
- In Bangladesh (n=541), EGRIS discriminated well (AUC 0.81) but over-predicted (41% vs 20% observed) until the intercept was updated [[Papri2024]].
- mEGRIS items (full text, Table 4): bulbar weakness yes = 5, no = 0; days from weakness to admission 0 = 7, 1 = 6, 2 = 5, 3 = 4, 4 = 3, 5 = 2, 6 = 1, ≥7 = 0; neck flexion MRC 0 = 10, 1 = 8, 2 = 6, 3 = 4, 4 = 2, 5 = 0; bilateral hip flexion MRC sum (0–10) scores 10 minus the sum; total 0–32 [[Luijten2023]].
- mEGRIS AUC 0.84 (0.80–0.88), predicts at several time points, and tests only three muscle groups; range of predicted risk 0 to 100% [[Luijten2023]].
- Worked example in the paper: bulbar weakness (5), admitted 1 day after onset (6), neck flexion MRC 4 (2), hip flexion 3/5 each side (4) gives 17, with predicted risk 17% <1 day, 26% <3 days, and 35% (text) or 32% (Figure 3 caption) <1 week; the paper disagrees with itself on the 1-week value [[Luijten2023]].
- EAN/PNS advises using a prognostic model at admission and names mEGRIS; it also advises re-assessing risk factors regularly during admission [[vanDoorn2023]].

### breathing · Bulbar weakness and swallowing
- In 30 consecutive ICU adults, piecemeal swallowing and impaired breathing–swallowing interaction each occurred in 19 (65.5%); only about half of these had clinically apparent swallowing impairment; 10 were intubated and 6 developed pneumonia [[Ogna2017]].
- The same study reports swallowing impairment in over 80% early after ICU admission, and advises systematic swallow evaluation, possibly with tongue protrusion strength [[Ogna2017]].
- FVC was lower with facial or bulbar weakness (median 2.4 L vs 3 L) in IGOS, and low FVC remained associated with MV after adjustment [[Luijten2023]].
- Reasons for ICU admission in the ten-steps guideline: evolving respiratory distress, severe autonomic cardiovascular dysfunction, severe swallowing dysfunction or diminished cough, rapid progression of weakness [[Leonhard2019]].

### autonomic · How common
- "Up to two-thirds" of people with GBS have autonomic dysfunction in the literature, and a 2019 review argues mortality from presumed autonomic causes now exceeds that of respiratory failure [[Zaeem2019]].
- Estimates vary widely with the definition: 38% (71/187) at Mayo Clinic, 2000–2017 [[Chakraborty2020]]; 10–50% depending on criteria, with 10–20% significant in the acute phase [[Weiss2025]]. There is no consensus definition or screening standard [[Zaeem2019,Weiss2025]].
- Manifestations involve cardiovascular, sudomotor and gastrointestinal systems, with both sympathetic and parasympathetic fibres [[Zochodne1994]].
- Dysautonomia marks severity: associated with quadriparesis, bulbar and neck flexor weakness, and MV [[Chakraborty2020]].
- Commonest features at Mayo Clinic: ileus 42%, hypertension 39%, hypotension 37%, fever 29%, tachycardia or bradycardia 27%, urinary retention 24% (abstract does not state the denominator; apparently those with dysautonomia) [[Chakraborty2020]].
- US inpatient sample (n=2,587, administrative codes, likely under-counting): diarrhoea/constipation 15.5%, hyponatraemia 14.9%, SIADH 4.8%, bradycardia 4.7%, urinary retention 3.9% [[Anandan2017]].
- Mortality was 6% with dysautonomia versus 2% in the whole cohort (P=0.02) [[Chakraborty2020]].
- About 23% have a heart rate above 100/min, rising to 75% among those on MV (review figure) [[Weiss2025]].
- Autonomic abnormality appears even in people who do not need ICU: in 10 non-ventilated patients vs 10 matched controls, heart rate variability and baroreflex sensitivity were reduced (pilot; Early evidence) [[Tan2019Autonomic]].

### autonomic · Blood pressure swings and arrhythmia
- In 36 ventilated people with GBS compared with 11 ventilated people with myasthenia, tolerance limits were set from controls: the daily systolic range exceeded 85 mmHg in 27 patients [[Pfeiffer1999]].
- All 16 with mean systolic BP above 165 mmHg also had persistent tachycardia (mean >125 bpm) or were on beta-blockers; 6 also had bradycardia below 48 bpm; 3 had hypotension (minimum systolic <85 mmHg) with unprovoked bradycardia [[Pfeiffer1999]].
- Except in one patient, every bradycardia episode was preceded by a raised daily systolic variation above 85 mmHg, making it a warning signal [[Pfeiffer1999]].
- Case physiology (76-year-old man): hypotension behaved like a vasodepressor response with a "vagotomised" heart, and hypertension like sympathetic overactivity; failed baroreflex buffering may explain the rapid swings [[Ropper1990]].
- Among 100 patients, 11 of 33 ventilated people had serious arrhythmias and 7 of those died; serious arrhythmias occurred only in those ventilated [[Winer1988]].
- Serious arrhythmias were often preceded by wide pulse or BP fluctuation and transient asystole after tracheal suction; about 25% had sinus tachycardia or raised systolic BP, and half had reduced R-R variation on deep breathing [[Winer1988]].
- Minor stimulation (suction, intubation, mouth care, bathing) can provoke severe bradycardia; some teams keep 1 mg IV atropine ready, and a pacemaker is rarely indicated (expert review; quoted as published, not as an instruction) [[Weiss2025]].
- Eyeball pressure testing in 13 patients: vagal overreactivity in 4 (about 30%), not limited to severe weakness, and one abnormal test preceded repeated pacing; small, older study, and not a guideline test [[Flachenecker1996]].
- Monitoring in the ten-steps guideline: ECG, heart rate, blood pressure, bowel and bladder function [[Leonhard2019]].
- Other causes of hypotension or arrhythmia (sepsis, bleeding, pulmonary embolism) must be excluded before attributing them to dysautonomia [[Weiss2025]].
- Autonomic failure also complicates ventilator care: failed extubation was associated with autonomic dysfunction (73% vs 27%, p=0.008) [[Nguyen2006]].

### autonomic · Sudden death
- Cardiovascular and respiratory dysfunction account for most deaths, and up to two-thirds of deaths occur in the recovery phase; the guideline stresses monitoring for those who have recently left ICU [[Leonhard2019]].
- In 527 Dutch patients, 10 of 15 deaths (67%) occurred in the recovery phase after neurological improvement [[vanDenBerg2013]].
- EAN/PNS advises frequent careful monitoring for late respiratory or cardiovascular complications after ICU discharge [[vanDoorn2023]].

### autonomic · Gut and bladder
- Adynamic ileus occurred in 17/114 (15%) with severe GBS; cardiovascular dysautonomia coincided in only 5, while ventilation and immobilisation (4) or prior abdominal surgery or rising opioid doses (8) were implicated in the others; medical management succeeded in all [[Burns2001]].
- So ileus is not automatically autonomic: one series lists it as the leading autonomic feature (42%) while another finds it infrequently autonomic [[Chakraborty2020,Burns2001]].
- Advice in ileus: keep potassium normal and avoid sedatives [[Weiss2025]].
- Urinary dysfunction in 27.7% of 65 consecutive patients, retention in 9.2%; more common in AIDP (39%) than AMAN (19%) [[Sakakibara2009]].
- Earlier series: 7 of 28 (25%) with micturitional disturbance, appearing after the weakness and improving with it [[Sakakibara1997]].
- Bladder or bowel dysfunction at onset, or persisting through the disease course, is a red flag prompting alternative diagnoses [[Leonhard2019]].

### autonomic · Sodium
- Serum sodium <133 mmol/L in 26/84 (31%), 12 of whom developed it during or after IVIg, implicating pseudohyponatraemia; 6 significantly hyponatraemic patients died versus none of the others; age and ventilation confound this [[Colls2003]].
- SIADH in 24/50 (48%) prospectively, median 8.8 days after onset; tied to bulbar weakness, ventilation and longer stay [[Saifudheen2011]].
- US inpatients (n=54,778): hyponatraemia 11.8% (vs 4.0% without GBS), rising from 6.9% in 2002 to 13.5% in 2011; mortality 20.5% vs 11.6%; stay 16.07 vs 10.41 days; IVIg an independent risk factor [[Rumalla2017]]. The 11.6% comparison figure is well above the 2.58% in-hospital mortality in other US data, so treat it with caution [[Alshekhlee2008]].
- Northern China (n=455): 21.5% overall and 36.2% in severe cases; MV OR 12.871 for hyponatraemia; hyponatraemia OR 12.770 for death [[Wang2015Hyponatremia]].
- Italy (n=51): 25.5% hyponatraemic; each 1-unit rise in baseline sodium lowered the odds of severe disability at discharge by 27%; IVIg raised the odds of hyponatraemia [[Gagliardi2021]].
- Finland (n=69): lower admission sodium was associated with worse 1-year function (OR 1.37, 95% CI 1.07–1.76) but not ventilation [[Sipila2017]].
- Mechanism is debated: SIADH [[Saifudheen2011]], salt wasting [[Weiss2025]], or IVIg pseudohyponatraemia [[Colls2003,Gagliardi2021]]; a 2025 review states incidence and mechanism are still not well known [[Netto2025]].

### autonomic · PRES and takotsubo
- PRES is reported in 7% of dysautonomic patients (review figure); extreme BP swings are the proposed driver and IVIg may add to risk [[Weiss2025]]. PRES was more common with dysautonomia at Mayo Clinic [[Chakraborty2020]].
- Altered consciousness in GBS should prompt consideration of hyponatraemia or PRES [[Weiss2025]].
- Children: 3 of 75 with GBS (31 with dysautonomia) developed PRES with hypertension, seizures and hyponatraemia, all ventilated; all were discharged walking after a mean of 104 days of care [[Surve2024]].
- Takotsubo: 8 Japanese cases versus 62 controls; onset mean 6.5 days after GBS onset (range 3–42), older age (76.5 vs 52 years), lower cranial nerve deficits 100% vs 41.9%, MV 62.5% vs 11.3% [[Terayama2024]].
- A 2021 review counted 12 prior reports plus 2 new cases, with reversible echocardiographic changes [[Puentes2021]].
- One case was mistaken for acute coronary syndrome: no coronary stenosis, ejection fraction 45%, recovered within two weeks; authors advise echocardiography when repolarisation changes appear [[Martins2010]].

### autonomic · Drug sensitivity warnings
- Succinylcholine: titled warnings since 1981 in BMJ [[Fergusson1981]] and 1991 in JNNP [[Reilly1991]]; a fatal electromechanical dissociation after suxamethonium for re-intubation in a tetraplegic person [[Dalman1994]].
- Risk may outlast apparent recovery: cardiac arrest from succinylcholine-induced hyperkalaemia occurred within 1 month of recovery, and no safe interval could be recommended (single case with an unconfirmed diagnosis) [[Feldman1990]].
- US label: succinylcholine is contraindicated after the acute phase of injury following extensive denervation of skeletal muscle, because of severe hyperkalaemia and cardiac arrest [[FDA-Quelicin]].
- An anaesthesia review (full text) advises special caution with succinylcholine in GBS because of hyperkalaemia and arrhythmia [[Radkowski2024]].
- Significant dysautonomia should favour non-hypotensive induction agents, and antihypertensives or antiarrhythmics are risky unless truly necessary; conservative management is preferred outside severe bradycardia [[Weiss2025]].
- Tricyclics need care in autonomic failure, per EAN/PNS [[vanDoorn2023]].

### icu · Tracheostomy and ventilation duration
- Median MV 28 days (IQR 12–60) in 150 Dutch patients; 106 (71%) needed ≥14 days [[Walgaard2017]]. IGOS median 20 days (IQR 10–54, range 1–525) [[Luijten2023]]. Indian median 21 days (range 1–88) [[Kalita2016]].
- At 1 week after intubation, the chance of ≥14 days of MV is 87% if unable to lift arms from the bed and 96% with axonal or unexcitable nerves as well (full text, Table 2) [[Walgaard2017]].
- Bangladesh: inability to flex the hip at day 7 of MV was the strongest predictor of prolonged MV, predicted probability 82% [[Papri2024]].
- Mayo series: 87% of 60 had tracheostomy, at a median of 9 days after intubation; only 13 (22%) weaned within 3 weeks; older age and lung disease predicted it [[Lawn1999]].
- Early tracheostomy (≤7 days) in Japan (n=919): 71% had tracheostomy, median 12 days (IQR 8–15); early tracheostomy did not change in-hospital mortality (risk difference 0.4%, 95% CI −5.6 to 6.7) or pneumonia [[Yonezawa2020]].
- French multicentre cohort (n=494): direct tracheostomy without any weaning attempt was associated with fewer people alive and free of MV at day 28 (43% vs 83%); adjusted difference −39.5% (95% CI −47.3 to −31.5); the design is observational, so sicker people may be over-represented [[Thille2026]].
- Guidance: early tracheostomy should be considered with risk factors for prolonged MV [[Leonhard2019]], and an ICU review suggests considering it when ventilator-dependent after 2 weeks with failed weaning, or after failed extubation [[Weiss2025]].
- The ten-steps guideline gives no fixed day [[Leonhard2019]], and a text search of EAN/PNS finds no mention of tracheostomy [[vanDoorn2023]].

### icu · Weaning
- VC guided weaning in a small series: no trial succeeded below 7 mL/kg, and extubation came above 15 mL/kg [[Chevrolet1991]].
- Successful extubation versus not (n=44): NIF −50.3 vs −28.6 cmH2O and VC 21.9 vs 13.0 mL/kg; a VC gain above 4 mL/kg had 82% sensitivity and 90% positive predictive value [[Nguyen2006]].
- French nationwide cohort (513 with GBS): prolonged weaning 64%, extubation attempted in only 46%, reintubation 26%, tracheostomy 57%, mortality 9.4% [[Thille2025]].
- Weaning usually starts once general clinical improvement has begun [[Weiss2025]].
- Respiratory muscle function is reported to recover normally in all patients eventually, though ventilation is tied to residual functional impairment [[Orlikowski2004]].

### icu · Timing of intubation
- Emergency intubation was uncommon: 7 of 43 at Mayo, one with anoxic encephalopathy after respiratory arrest; prolonged weaning about twice as common, but mortality and pulmonary morbidity similar [[Wijdicks2003]].
- In 81 intubated patients, pneumonia occurred in 63 (78%), early-onset in 48; admission-to-intubation over 2 days was the only independent predictor of early-onset pneumonia [[Orlikowski2006]].

### icu · Complications
- Major morbidity in 60% of 114; respiratory complications in about half, systemic infection in one-fifth; complications uncommon if ICU stay was under 3 weeks; GI bleeding and pulmonary embolism very uncommon [[Henderson2003]].
- 76 ICU adults: median ICU stay 21 days; 78% ventilated, median 28 days; two-thirds had at least one major complication, pneumonia 54%; mortality 6.5% [[Dhar2008]].
- 173 ventilated people in India averaged 6.8 ± 1.8 complications: tracheobronchitis 128, hyponatraemia 115, hypoalbuminaemia 76, anaemia 75, hypokalaemia 67, urinary infection 47, sepsis 40, dysautonomia 27, DVT 7, ileus 5 (counts; derived percentages in the animation table) [[Netto2017]].
- Pneumonia in those ventilated: 18% septic shock, 10% ARDS, 14% death among the 63 with pneumonia [[Orlikowski2006]].
- Complications named by the ten-steps guideline: pressure ulcers, hospital-acquired infection, DVT, choking, corneal ulceration with facial palsy, limb contractures, ossification, pressure palsies, delirium, depression, hyponatraemia [[Leonhard2019]].
- Pressure injury: no GBS-specific incidence was found; guidance is standard preventive care [[Leonhard2019]].
- Readmission: 14.1% within 90 days among 2,587 US adults; 46.7% of those within 15 days; septicaemia 9.6% (2020 data) [[Voth2026]].
- IVIg-associated acute lung injury is rare (17 cases pooled; 2 with GBS); consider it for respiratory distress during or soon after infusion [[Baudel2020]].

### icu · Venous thromboembolism
- A 2005 consensus group recommended heparin and graduated pressure stockings for bed-bound adults in the acute phase; no RCT supports this, and it rests on observational data and expert opinion [[Hughes2005]].
- 73 UK patients: 50 (68%) anticoagulated for a mean of 72 days; clinical DVT in 5 (7%), 3 with pulmonary embolism, 4 within 2 months of onset; 3 occurred on enoxaparin, and one death [[Gaber2002]].
- Russian ICU cohort (n=65, duplex-screened), despite LMWH: DVT 52%, pulmonary embolism 15%; risks include ventilation, more than 3 days of bed rest, infection, central line, age over 40 [[Ryabinkina2016]].
- These contrast with very uncommon pulmonary embolism at Mayo; part of the gap may be that duplex screening finds more than clinical diagnosis (interpretation of this pass) [[Henderson2003,Ryabinkina2016]].
- EAN/PNS 2023 makes no VTE statement: a text search of the guideline finds no mention of heparin or DVT; its only thrombotic concern is events with a second IVIg course [[vanDoorn2023]].

### icu · Outcome and mortality
- Population statistics, not individual predictions: of ventilated Mayo patients, mortality was 20%, and 79% of survivors eventually walked independently; 19% improved at least one grade beyond 1 year [[Fletcher2000]].
- Independent predictors of poor outcome: age (OR 1.99) and transfer delay over 2 days (OR 19.8) [[Fletcher2000]].
- 6-month unaided walking: 57% ventilated vs 87% not (OR 5.0); MV under 14 days had recovery comparable to no MV [[Walgaard2017]].
- MV beyond 2 months affected 33/526 (6%); 18% walked unaided at 6 months vs 76% with shorter MV [[vanDenBerg2018]].
- In the cross-sectional part (median follow-up 11 years, n=26 with prolonged MV, median 93 days): 15 (58%) walked unaided at last follow-up, and 8 (31%) reached this after more than 1 year [[vanDenBerg2018]].
- ICU cohort: 75% recovered independent walking over an average of 3 years, median 198 days, sometimes as late as 10 years [[Dhar2008]].
- German cohort (n=110): hospital mortality 5.5%, long-term 13.6%; 53.8% good outcome; 30.4% severe fatigue; quality of life limited mainly by immobility and chronic pain [[Witsch2013]].
- Overall mortality: 2.8% at 6 months and 3.9% at 12 months in Dutch cohorts, with a median of 76 days from onset to death; only 47% of those who died did so in ICU [[vanDenBerg2013]].
- US in-hospital mortality 2.58% (128/4,954); intubation adjusted OR 5.09 (95% CI 3.21–8.05) [[Alshekhlee2008]].
- Ventilated mortality elsewhere: 12.1% of 273 [[Netto2011Mortality]], 10.4% of 173 [[Netto2011Prognosis]], 5% at 6 months [[Walgaard2017]].
- Regional variation at IGOS: 17% in Bangladesh vs 5% in Europe/Americas and 2% in Asia [[Doets2018]]. Guidelines estimate 3–10% overall [[Leonhard2019]], about 4% in the first year [[Rajabally2012]].
- Risk of death in ventilated people in India: pneumonia 4.69-fold, dysautonomia 3.14-fold, hypokalaemia 2.44-fold [[Netto2011Mortality]].

### icu · Pain: how common and what kind
- Dutch cohort (n=156): pain in the 2 weeks before weakness 36%, acute phase 66%, 1 year 38%; mostly moderate to severe, across all variants including Miller Fisher [[Ruts2010]].
- Canada (n=55): 89.1% had pain during the illness; 47.3% distressing or worse on admission; 74.5% needed opioids, 29.0% parenteral morphine [[Moulin1997]].
- China (retrospective, n=252): only 34.5% acute-phase pain, showing how methods change the number [[Yao2018]]. A 1994 review gave up to 72% [[Pentland1994]].
- Types: paraesthesiae, dysaesthesia, axial and radicular pain, meningism, myalgia, joint pain and visceral discomfort [[Pentland1994]].
- Time course: deep back and leg pain usually settled by 8 weeks; dysaesthetic limb pain persisted longer in 5 to 10% [[Moulin1997]].
- Pain intensity on admission did not predict prognosis [[Moulin1997]].
- Skin biopsy (n=32): intraepidermal nerve fibre density reduced in about 60%; lower density correlated with acute pain intensity, even in pure motor forms [[Ruts2012]].
- Severe pain at 1 year in at least a third, and it can last over 10 years [[Leonhard2019]]. Median 6 years on, pain VAS 2.9 vs 1.5 in controls [[Rudolph2008]].
- Pain can precede weakness, which delays diagnosis, especially in young children [[Leonhard2019]].

### icu · Pain: treatment evidence
- Cochrane: 3 short RCTs, 277 people; no trial reported the primary outcome, and evidence is insufficient to support any single drug [[Liu2015]].
- Gabapentin vs placebo crossover (n=18, ventilated): pain score 7.22 to 2.33 on day 2; fentanyl fell from 211 to 66 µg vs about 317 µg on placebo [[Pandey2002]]. Cochrane day-7 mean difference −3.61 on a 0–10 scale (95% CI −4.12 to −3.10; very low quality) [[Liu2015]].
- Three-arm RCT (n=36): median day-7 score 2.0 gabapentin, 3.0 carbamazepine, 6.0 placebo; day-1 fentanyl about 340, 348 and 590 µg [[Pandey2005]].
- Carbamazepine crossover (n=12, ICU): pain 1.7 vs 3.1 on a 1–5 scale; pethidine 1.7 vs 3.7 mg/kg/day [[Tripathi2000]].
- Corticosteroid: methylprednisolone 5 days (n=223) gave no difference in developing pain (RR 0.89, 95% CI 0.68–1.16) [[Liu2015]]. EAN/PNS weakly recommends against high-dose corticosteroids for pain [[vanDoorn2023]].
- EAN/PNS weakly recommends gabapentin or carbamazepine, and advises gabapentinoids or tricyclics before carbamazepine; opioids may be used with care for constipation, ileus, confusion, respiratory suppression and addiction [[vanDoorn2023]].
- A systematic review agrees that no robust evidence favours one option [[Pena2015]]. Supportive-care consensus urges cautious narcotic use [[Hughes2005]].
- An ICU review also notes possible drug interactions with carbamazepine, and lidocaine 5% plaster as a low-risk option [[Weiss2025]].
- No RCT evidence exists for convalescent-phase pain, and Cochrane calls for it [[Liu2015]].

### icu · Mind, sleep and communication
- Mental status changes in 31% of 139 people with GBS vs 16% of ICU controls (OR 2.3); included vivid dreams, illusions, hallucinations (mainly visual) and paranoid delusions; median onset day 9, lasting a median 8 days [[Cochen2005]].
- Risk factors were autonomic dysfunction, assisted ventilation and high CSF protein; authors link them to REM sleep disorder rather than ICU delirium (Early evidence for the mechanism) [[Cochen2005]].
- Among 49 very severe ICU patients: anxiety 82%, depressive episodes 67%, brief reactive psychosis 25% (oneiroid psychosis 14%) [[Weiss2002]].
- People said losing communication was hardest to cope with; 90% found contact with relatives most helpful [[Weiss2002]].
- Top fears at admission: staying paralysed, uncertainty, intubation [[Sharshar2012]].
- Case report: fully conscious but unable to communicate for 31 days; standard low tidal volume ventilation caused profound breathlessness, and physical therapy was very painful without any heart rate or BP change; this is one person's account, but it shows monitors can miss pain [[Kumta2022]].
- Even with complete paralysis, consciousness, vision and hearing are usually intact, so bedside talk matters and procedures should be explained [[Leonhard2019]].
- Ask directly about pain, hallucinations, anxiety and depression, especially with limited communication [[Leonhard2019]].
- A small 2026 qualitative study named communication barriers among four lived-experience themes [[Susanti2026]].
- Accurate information on the relatively good chance of recovery and low recurrence risk (2–5%) can reduce fear [[Leonhard2019]].
- Two-year impairment: 7% still could not walk 10 m independently; over 50% had detectable motor and sensory deficit [[Forsberg2004]].

### Exam hooks
- 20/30/40: VC <20 mL/kg, MIP <30 cmH2O, MEP <40 cmH2O, or a >30% fall [[Lawn2001]].
- Signs of respiratory distress come late and dyspnoea can be absent; serial VC comes first [[Orlikowski2004,Leonhard2019]].
- EGRIS uses three items: onset-to-admission days, facial/bulbar weakness, MRC sum score; range 0–7 [[Doets2022]].
- EGRIS ≥5 means about 65% risk of MV within a week [[Leonhard2019]].
- mEGRIS replaces the MRC sum score with neck flexion and bilateral hip flexion; range 0–32 [[Luijten2023]].
- Inability to cough has the highest odds ratio in the French model (9.09) [[Sharshar2003]].
- SBC <20 is a useful ICU-transfer tool per EAN/PNS [[vanDoorn2023]].
- Succinylcholine risks hyperkalaemic cardiac arrest in denervation [[FDA-Quelicin,Feldman1990]].
- Suctioning can trigger asystole in severe dysautonomia [[Winer1988,Weiss2025]].
- A daily systolic range above 85 mmHg preceded bradycardia [[Pfeiffer1999]].
- Up to two-thirds of deaths come in the recovery phase [[Leonhard2019,vanDenBerg2013]].
- Delayed intubation (>2 days) independently predicts early pneumonia [[Orlikowski2006]].
- Unable to lift arms at 1 week: 87% chance of MV ≥14 days [[Walgaard2017]].
- Early tracheostomy did not lower mortality in a national cohort [[Yonezawa2020]].
- IVIg can cause pseudohyponatraemia; low sodium has several causes [[Colls2003,Gagliardi2021]].
- Pain is present in about two-thirds acutely and over a third at 1 year [[Ruts2010]].
- Methylprednisolone does not treat GBS pain [[Liu2015]].
- Gabapentinoids or tricyclics come first per EAN/PNS; opioids risk ileus [[vanDoorn2023,Burns2001]].
- Hallucinations in GBS are tied to autonomic dysfunction and REM abnormalities [[Cochen2005]].
- Fully paralysed people usually hear and see normally [[Leonhard2019]].

### In plain words
- GBS can weaken the muscles you use to breathe, so about 1 in 5 people need a breathing machine for a while [[Leonhard2019]].
- Most who need it start within the first week of being in hospital [[Luijten2023]].
- Your team may ask you to breathe into a device or count aloud on one breath, often many times a day. This finds a problem before you feel short of breath [[vanDoorn2023,Leonhard2019]].
- A planned move to intensive care is a safety step, not a sign that all hope is gone [[vanDoorn2023]].
- GBS can also affect nerves that control heart rate, blood pressure, bowels and bladder, so these are watched closely [[Leonhard2019]].
- The dangerous time does not always end with the worst weakness, so checks continue during recovery [[Leonhard2019]].
- Pain is common and real, even when weakness is the main problem. Please say if you hurt [[Ruts2010]].
- Some medicines can help nerve pain, though the research is small, and steroids do not seem to help [[vanDoorn2023,Liu2015]].
- Vivid dreams or seeing things that are not there can happen in GBS. They usually pass, and they do not mean you are losing your mind [[Cochen2005]].
- Someone who cannot move or speak can usually still hear and understand. Talk to them, explain things, and tell them who is there [[Leonhard2019]].
- Being unable to communicate is often the hardest part, and family contact helps most [[Weiss2002]].
- Many people who needed a ventilator walk again, though it can take a long time, sometimes more than a year. These are group figures, not a forecast for one person [[Fletcher2000,vanDenBerg2018]].

## Open questions and controversies
- MIP and MEP numbers: Lawn's rule is MIP <30 and MEP <40 cmH2O, and the ten-steps guideline matches it [[Lawn2001,Leonhard2019]]. The EAN/PNS text reads MEP <30 or MIP <40 [[vanDoorn2023]]. This looks like a transposition; builders should present 20/30/40 from Lawn and footnote the difference. Status: unresolved in the published text.
- When to intubate: early pneumonia rose with delayed intubation [[Orlikowski2006]], while a conservative blood-gas policy did not raise mortality or disability in one Indian cohort [[Kalita2016]]. Both are observational.
- Tracheostomy timing: a review favours early tracheostomy [[Shang2020]] and guidelines suggest considering it with risk factors [[Leonhard2019,Walgaard2017]]; national data show no mortality gain [[Yonezawa2020]], and direct tracheostomy was linked to slower liberation [[Thille2026]]. No RCT was found.
- Is autonomic death now commoner than respiratory death? One review says yes [[Zaeem2019]]; large cohorts attribute many deaths to pneumonia and sepsis, with respiratory and cardiovascular causes jointly in recovery [[vanDenBerg2013,Netto2011Mortality]]. Status: unproven.
- Frequency of dysautonomia: up to two-thirds [[Zaeem2019]], 38% [[Chakraborty2020]], or 10–50% [[Weiss2025]]. No agreed definition.
- Is ileus autonomic? Listed as leading [[Chakraborty2020]], but mostly other causes in another series [[Burns2001]].
- Hyponatraemia as cause or marker: linked with death and disability [[Wang2015Hyponatremia,Rumalla2017]], but confounded by age and ventilation [[Colls2003]], and IVIg pseudohyponatraemia muddies the numbers [[Gagliardi2021]].
- VTE prophylaxis: consensus supports heparin and stockings [[Hughes2005]], yet DVT occurred on prophylaxis [[Gaber2002,Ryabinkina2016]] and the 2023 guideline is silent [[vanDoorn2023]]. Optimal agent and duration are unknown.
- Non-invasive ventilation: viewed as unsuitable in GBS [[Weiss2025]] and as promising in neuromuscular failure generally [[Rabinstein2003]].
- Corticosteroid add-on in ventilated people: associated with worse short-term prognosis in one retrospective series at nadir MRC 0–12 (OR 10.2, 95% CI 1.068–97.407) [[Wu2015]]. The wide interval and retrospective design limit this (Hypothesis).
