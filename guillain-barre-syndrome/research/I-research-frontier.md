# I · Research frontier

Pass date: 2026-11-10. Modules served: frontier, whatnot.

Access notes:
- This pass covers what is new, unproven or argued about. Standard IVIg and plasma exchange evidence sits in pass E; prognosis scores in pass G. Findings here repeat those passes only where the frontier story needs them.
- The tanruprubart (ANX005) phase 3 trial (NCT04701164) has no peer-reviewed full report as of this pass date, and ClinicalTrials.gov holds no posted results. Its numbers come from three peer-reviewed secondary sources that report a 2025 congress abstract and company topline data: an Annexon-funded review (Gorson2025), an independent review (Wiegers2025) and an industry pipeline survey (Crescioli2026). They are labelled Early evidence and attributed each time. The congress abstract itself (Kroon et al., Neurology 2025;104(7 Suppl 1):4954, doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000212033) was confirmed in Crossref, but its text was blocked, so it is not cited directly.
- The imlifidase phase 2 trial (NCT03943589) has no journal publication found in PubMed or Europe PMC. Its numbers come from results posted on ClinicalTrials.gov, read directly from the registry API.
- Doses appear only as trial design facts. They are not instructions.
- Outcome figures are group statistics from trials or cohorts and do not predict what happens to one person.

## Findings

### frontier · Why new treatments are being sought
- IVIg and plasma exchange have been the only proven immune treatments for GBS for 30 years, and a substantial proportion of people recover incompletely (Established) [[Wiegers2025]]
- New treatment trials target two steps: lowering IgG antibodies to nerves, and blocking complement activation (Established, as a research direction) [[Wiegers2025]]
- An Annexon-funded review argues that IVIg and plasma exchange only partly lower antibody levels and complement activity and take days to deliver (5 days for IVIg, 7–14 days for plasma exchange), which limits their effect during the active phase (Hypothesis) [[Gorson2025]]
- RCTs in GBS are hard: the disease is acute and rare, enrolment must be fast, pathogenesis and severity vary, and it is ethically difficult to test a new drug without standard care [[Wiegers2025]]

### frontier · Complement: the rationale
- In a mouse model of Miller Fisher syndrome, anti-GQ1b antibodies with human serum caused respiratory paralysis through complement activation at the diaphragm nerve terminals; intravenous eculizumab prevented this paralysis (Early evidence; animal data) [[Halstead2008]]
- In a mouse model in which anti-GM1 antibody targets the Schwann cell nodal membrane, the anti-C2 antibody ARGX-117 was used to test whether blocking early complement protects demyelinating-type injury (Early evidence; animal data) [[Campbell2022]]
- Complement deposits are also found on myelin in AIDP even though no specific antibodies are known there, which is cited as a reason complement might be a target across subtypes (Hypothesis) [[Freiha2026]]

### frontier · Eculizumab (anti-C5): two negative trials
- ICA-GBS (UK, phase 2): 28 people screened and 8 (29%) randomised; 5 received eculizumab for 4 weeks with IVIg. At 4 weeks, two of two on placebo and two of five on eculizumab had improved by one or more grades. The sample was too small for statistical analysis (Early evidence) [[Davidson2017]]
- JET-GBS (Japan, phase 2): 34 people unable to walk independently were randomised 2:1 to IVIg plus eculizumab (23) or IVIg plus placebo (11) [[Misawa2018]]
- At week 4, 61% (90% CI 42–78) on eculizumab and 45% (20–73) on placebo could walk independently. The predefined response threshold (lower 90% CI bound above 50%) was not met, and the design made no statistical comparison with placebo (Early evidence) [[Misawa2018]]
- Cochrane 2020 pooled the two eculizumab trials (41 participants): it is uncertain whether eculizumab improves disability at 4 weeks (mean difference -0.23, 95% CI -1.79 to 1.34; very low certainty) [[Doets2020]]
- Phase 3 (Japan, NCT04752566): 57 adults with severe GBS within 2 weeks of onset were randomised 2:1 to eculizumab (37) or placebo (20), weekly for 4 weeks, with IVIg [[Kuwabara2024]]
- The primary endpoint, time to first reach functional grade 1 or less (able to run), was not met: hazard ratio 0.9 (95% CI 0.45–1.97; p=0.89). Key secondary endpoints were also not significant (Established: no benefit shown) [[Kuwabara2024]]
- Registry results: median time to functional grade 1 or less was 112 days with eculizumab and 168 days with placebo. At week 8, 12 of 37 and 7 of 20 had reached it; at week 24, 20 of 37 and 13 of 20 [[NCT04752566]]
- Serum C5 fell by 99.99% at 1 hour after the dose and stayed suppressed to week 5, so the drug did block its target, yet function did not improve. No new safety signals were identified; the registry lists one death in the eculizumab group [[Kuwabara2024,NCT04752566]]
- The 2023 EAN/PNS guideline does not recommend eculizumab for GBS, as summarised in a 2025 review [[Wiegers2025]]

### frontier · Tanruprubart (ANX005, anti-C1q)
- Tanruprubart (ANX005) is a humanised IgG4 monoclonal antibody that blocks C1q, the first protein of the classical complement pathway, upstream of C5 [[Freiha2026,Crescioli2026]]
- Phase 1 (Bangladesh, published 2025): 50 people with recent-onset GBS who had no access to IVIg or plasma exchange received escalating single doses of ANX005 (38) or placebo (12) [[Mohammad2025]]
- In that trial, no dose-limiting toxicity was seen up to 100 mg/kg, and no one died. Infusion-related rash occurred in 76% (29/38) on ANX005 versus 8% (1/12) on placebo; all were grade 1 or 2 and resolved. Serious adverse events were 5% on ANX005 versus 25% on placebo, none treatment-related [[Mohammad2025]]
- Doses of 18 or 36 mg/kg blocked complement for 1–2 weeks; 75 and 100 mg/kg for more than 2 weeks. C1q in cerebrospinal fluid was fully occupied in everyone given 36 mg/kg or more [[Mohammad2025]]
- In the exploratory efficacy group, 28% (5/18) on ANX005 versus 0% (0/8) on placebo could run at week 8. Mean MRC sum score gain to day 8 was 11.8 versus 3.9 points (p=0.09). Groups were too small for significance (Early evidence) [[Mohammad2025]]
- Phase 1 participants were young (mean age 31), all Asian, 75% classified as AMAN, and had mean baseline MRC sum score 15.5, which differs from typical European or North American cohorts [[Mohammad2025]]
- Phase 3 design (NCT04701164): randomised, quadruple-masked, two single-dose arms versus placebo, 242 enrolled in Bangladesh and the Philippines, age 16 and over, weakness for 10 days or less, GBS-DS 3–5; primary outcome GBS-DS at week 8. Completed April 2024; no results posted on the registry [[NCT04701164]]
- Participants had not received IVIg or plasma exchange; in both countries many people receive supportive care only because standard treatment is unavailable or unaffordable [[Wiegers2025]]
- Reported phase 3 result: the 30 mg/kg dose met the primary endpoint at week 8 with 2.4-fold higher odds of functional improvement versus placebo; the 75 mg/kg dose did not (Early evidence; company-reported data, not yet in a peer-reviewed trial paper) [[Crescioli2026]]
- Reported secondary results for 30 mg/kg versus placebo: independent walking a median 31 days earlier, 28 fewer days on mechanical ventilation, and a GBS-DS score of 0 at 26 weeks in 22% versus 9% (OR 4.1, 95% CI 1.42–12.04; p=0.0092) (Early evidence; source funded by the manufacturer and based on a congress poster) [[Gorson2025]]
- The same review reports MRC sum score separation from placebo from week 1 (p<0.0001) and response maintained in AMAN and AIDP subgroups. Infusion-related reactions were the most common adverse events (Early evidence) [[Gorson2025]]
- An industry survey reports an 11.2% reduction in neurofilament light chain relative to placebo between weeks 2 and 4 (Early evidence; company-reported) [[Crescioli2025]]
- Key limitation: the control arm received no standard care, so the trial cannot show how tanruprubart compares with IVIg or plasma exchange; external validity is limited (Established, as a design limitation) [[Freiha2026]]
- After feedback from the FDA, the company began a real-world evidence protocol comparing trial participants with matched IGOS patients treated with IVIg or plasma exchange [[Crescioli2025,Wiegers2025]]
- Regulatory status as reported in early 2026: FDA Fast Track and Orphan Drug designations and an EMA orphan designation for GBS, with an EU marketing application planned for January 2026. Current approval status was not confirmed by this pass [[Crescioli2026]]
- FORWARD (NCT07020819): an open-label phase 3 study giving a single 30 mg/kg dose to about 30 people aged 8–85 in the US, UK, Denmark, France and Spain, excluding anyone given IVIg or plasma exchange in the prior 90 days; recruiting, primary outcomes are pharmacokinetic [[NCT07020819]]

### frontier · Other complement agents
- Empasiprubart (ARGX-117) is an anti-C2 antibody that blocks the classical and lectin pathways; in 78 healthy volunteers one intravenous dose lowered circulating C2 by up to 99%, with an elimination half-life of 70–88 days [[VandeWalle2025]]
- A phase 2 trial (NCT07821762) plans to randomise about 63 adults within 7 days of weakness and GBS-DS 3–5 to empasiprubart or placebo, both with IVIg; primary outcome GBS-DS 2 or less at week 4. Not yet recruiting; participants must take antibiotic prophylaxis or be vaccinated against meningococcus and pneumococcus [[NCT07821762]]
- Crovalimab (anti-C5) had a phase 3 GBS trial (NCT05494619) withdrawn by the sponsor before enrolment, for an internal decision not related to safety [[Freiha2026,NCT05494619]]

### frontier · Imlifidase (IgG-cleaving enzyme)
- Imlifidase is an enzyme from Streptococcus pyogenes that cleaves all four subclasses of human IgG; it is already approved for highly sensitised kidney transplant candidates [[Freiha2026,Wiegers2025]]
- Phase 2 (NCT03943589): single-arm, open-label; 30 adults within 10 days of onset and unable to walk 10 m unaided received one dose of imlifidase (0.25 mg/kg) followed by a standard IVIg course starting at least 48 hours later. 26 were enrolled in France, 3 in the UK and 1 in the Netherlands [[NCT03943589]]
- Posted results (27 in efficacy analysis): median time to improve one GBS-DS grade 6 days (range 3–16) and two grades 16 days (range 8–92) (Early evidence; no control arm) [[NCT03943589]]
- Ability to walk unaided: 0 of 27 at baseline, 10 at day 7, 18 at day 57, 23 at day 180 and 24 at day 360. A review gives these as 67% at 8 weeks and 85% at 6 months [[NCT03943589,Wiegers2025]]
- Mean MRC sum score rose from 39.1 at baseline to 50.0 at day 8 and 54.1 at day 180 [[NCT03943589]]
- Of 30 treated, 7 had serious adverse events, including pulmonary embolism, pneumonia, acute kidney injury and respiratory failure; 1 died and 1 was withdrawn after a diagnosis of encephalomyelitis [[NCT03943589]]
- Comparison with matched IVIg-treated IGOS patients as an external control is ongoing and full results are unpublished; a 2026 review names the small sample and the lack of an IVIg-alone comparison as limits [[Wiegers2025,Freiha2026]]

### frontier · FcRn blockade (efgartigimod)
- Efgartigimod blocks the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), which normally recycles IgG; blocking it speeds IgG breakdown. It has shown benefit in CIDP, but extrapolation to monophasic GBS may not be appropriate (Hypothesis in GBS) [[Freiha2026]]
- No RCT of efgartigimod in GBS has reported. A 2026 review states this absence precludes conclusions on efficacy, dosing, timing or safety [[Freiha2026]]
- A US randomised phase 2 trial of efgartigimod versus IVIg (NCT05701189) was withdrawn with 0 enrolled, because of continued difficulty enrolling at a single site [[NCT05701189]]
- GREAT (NCT07806916, Beijing): a planned randomised phase 2 of efgartigimod versus IVIg in about 110 adults within 14 days of onset with GBS-DS 3–5; primary outcome GBS-DS at week 4. Not yet recruiting [[NCT07806916]]
- Observational study, China (22 people, 11 per group): median time to GBS-DS improvement 4 days with efgartigimod and 8 days with IVIg; at week 2, response was 90.9% versus 81.8%. The authors state comparative efficacy cannot be established (Early evidence) [[Cai2025]]
- Retrospective two-centre study (21 people; 9 efgartigimod, 12 IVIg): the primary outcome (GBS-DS 2 or less at 4 weeks) was not different (OR 0.67, 95% CI 0.10–4.48); MRC sum score improved more with efgartigimod (p=0.007) (Early evidence) [[Zhang2025]]
- Retrospective single-centre series (36 people): median time to GBS-DS 2 or less was 6 days when efgartigimod was first-line (17) versus 25 days when used after IVIg or plasma exchange (19); two mild adverse events were reported (Early evidence; no untreated control) [[Bi2026]]

### frontier · Other ways to remove antibodies
- A Chinese randomised trial (GBS-PRAISING, NCT05114941) plans to compare protein A immunoadsorption with IVIg in 204 people; primary outcome change in Hughes score at 4 weeks. Registered as not yet recruiting, last updated March 2025 [[NCT05114941]]
- Small-volume plasma exchange (Bangladesh, 20 adults, single arm) separates plasma by sedimentation in a blood bag; 8 L was removed in 15 of 20 (75%), one serious adverse event (severe sepsis, possibly catheter-related) occurred, no one died, and 14 of 20 (70%) improved by a grade at 4 weeks (Early evidence; no control arm) [[Islam2018]]
- A review of GBS in low- and middle-income countries calls for novel, targeted, cost-effective treatment strategies, and notes that lack of affordable effective treatment contributes to worse outcomes and higher mortality [[Papri2021]]

### whatnot · Second IVIg course: settled for poor prognosis, open elsewhere
- SID-GBS (Netherlands, 59 hospitals): 93 people with mEGOS 6 or more were analysed; 49 received a second 2 g/kg course and 44 placebo, 7–9 days after inclusion [[Walgaard2021]]
- The adjusted common OR for improvement on the GBS-DS at 4 weeks was 1.4 (95% CI 0.6–3.3; p=0.45). Serious adverse events in the first 30 days were 35% versus 16%, including thromboembolic events, and 4 people died in the second-course group (Established) [[Walgaard2021]]
- The earlier non-randomised IGOS analysis (ISID; 199 one course, 20 early and 18 late second course) gave adjusted ORs of 0.70 (95% CI 0.16–3.04) and 0.66 (0.18–2.50), not favouring a second course [[Verboon2020]]
- A 2025 review notes that ISID's propensity matching could not remove unobserved confounding, since lack of improvement itself prompted clinicians to give the second course [[Wiegers2025]]
- Pharmacodynamics in SID-GBS: the second course raised serum IgG as the model predicted but did not improve outcome; serious adverse events, including thromboembolism, were associated with lower IVIg exposure [[vanTilburg2025]]
- A systematic review of IVIg-unresponsive GBS (37 articles, 422 patients; 1 RCT, 3 observational, 33 case reports or series) found no superiority of repeat IVIg or plasma exchange; evidence on plasma exchange after IVIg was insufficient [[Roe2025]]
- A contrasting synthesis of 10 small studies (94 patients; two trials, the rest case studies and series) reported motor improvement after a second dose in 70% of studies (low-quality evidence) [[Alnaeem2025]]
- The guideline position differs for treatment-related fluctuations: a second course of the initial treatment is recommended only for fluctuations within 8 weeks of onset, which affect about 10% of people [[Wiegers2025]]
- In IGOS practice, 82 of 235 people (35%) who did not improve received a second immune treatment, and 36 of 53 with a fluctuation (68%) were re-treated [[Verboon2019]]
- Whether people not responding to IVIg benefit from switching to plasma exchange is undefined [[Wiegers2025,Roe2025]]

### whatnot · Mild GBS, Miller Fisher syndrome and timing
- In IGOS, 188 people still able to walk at entry were compared: 148 (79%) received IVIg and 40 (21%) supportive care. IVIg was not associated with a lower GBS-DS at 4 weeks (adjusted OR 1.62, 95% CI 0.63–4.13) [[Verboon2021]]
- In that study IVIg was linked to faster return of full strength (28 versus 56 days) and less pain at 26 weeks (22% versus 40%), and 40% of all patients had residual symptoms at 1 year. Confounding and wide confidence limits limit certainty (Early evidence) [[Verboon2021]]
- A 2025 review suggests a placebo-controlled trial of new treatments at the earliest stage in mild GBS, to test prevention of progression and residual symptoms (Hypothesis) [[Wiegers2025]]
- Miller Fisher syndrome: a Cochrane review found no randomised or non-randomised prospective controlled trials of immunotherapy [[Overell2007]]
- In 92 Japanese people with Miller Fisher syndrome, IVIg slightly hastened improvement of ophthalmoplegia and ataxia, but the time to their disappearance was similar with IVIg, plasmapheresis or no treatment, presumably because natural recovery is good [[Mori2007]]
- In IGOS practice, 53 of 70 people with Miller Fisher syndrome (76%) and 126 of 168 with mild GBS (75%) were treated, despite the lack of trial support [[Verboon2019]]
- Timing: in 136 IVIg-treated Korean patients, a shorter time to IVIg was independently associated with better disability and faster walking at all time points to 12 months, and the effect was notably smaller after the first 2 weeks (Early evidence; retrospective) [[Min2024]]

### frontier · Biomarkers: neurofilament light, albumin, IgG
- Neurofilament light chain (NfL) is a marker of axonal damage. In a Spanish IGOS cohort, median serum NfL was 55.49 pg/mL in GBS versus 9.83 pg/mL in healthy controls, higher in AMAN (190.55 versus 46.79 pg/mL), and returned to normal by 1 year [[MartinAguilar2020]]
- High baseline serum NfL predicted inability to run at 1 year (OR 1.65, 95% CI 1.14–2.40). Proposed cut-offs with high specificity: inability to walk above 319 pg/mL, inability to run above 248 pg/mL, ability to run below 34 pg/mL (Early evidence; not validated for individual use) [[MartinAguilar2020]]
- In 281 SID-GBS patients, high serum NfL at 2 weeks was associated with inability to walk at 4 weeks (OR 1.74, 95% CI 1.27–2.45), and high NfL at 4 weeks with inability to walk at 26 weeks (OR 2.79, 1.72–4.90). Baseline NfL had prognostic value independent of mEGOS [[vanTilburg2024]]
- The authors propose serum NfL as an intermediate endpoint for treatment trials (Hypothesis) [[vanTilburg2024]]
- In 293 SID-GBS patients, adding NfL improved EGOS and mEGOS (change in C-statistic 0.01–0.11). A model of serum NfL at entry, GBS-DS at entry and MRC sum score at week 2 predicted inability to walk at 26 weeks with C-statistic 0.88 (95% CI 0.83–0.94). NfL did not predict respiratory failure [[Thomma2025]]
- Serum albumin: in 174 IVIg-treated people, median albumin fell from 4.2 to 3.7 g/dL by 2 weeks, and hypoalbuminaemia rose from 12.8% to 34.5%. Hypoalbuminaemia was associated with respiratory failure, inability to walk and weaker muscles at 6 months [[Fokkink2017]]
- IgG increment: a lower rise in serum IgG after IVIg is associated with poorer outcome, but SID-GBS showed that raising IgG with a second course does not convert this association into benefit (Established) [[vanTilburg2025,Walgaard2021]]

### frontier · Prognostic modelling and IGOS
- IGOS is a prospective observational cohort that enrols people within 2 weeks of weakness regardless of age, severity, variant or treatment, collecting clinical data and serial biosamples; by January 2017 it had more than 1,400 participants from 143 centres in 19 countries, and the registry lists 2,000 participants with primary completion in September 2024 [[Jacobs2017,NCT01582763]]
- IGOS first 1,000: walking unaided at 1 year was 91% in Asia (31/34), 83% in Europe/Americas (334/404) and 69% in Bangladesh (67/97); mortality was 17% in Bangladesh, 5% in Europe/Americas and 2% in Asia [[Doets2018]]
- mEGOS validation in IGOS: AUC above 0.7 in all regions; outcomes were worse than predicted in Europe/North America and better than predicted in Asia, leading to a recalibrated Europe/North America version [[Doets2022mEGOS]]
- In Bangladesh (541 patients), EGRIS discriminated well (AUC 0.81) but overestimated ventilation risk (41% predicted versus 20% observed), corrected by updating the intercept; inability to flex the hip at day 7 of ventilation predicted prolonged ventilation [[Papri2024]]
- New trial methods proposed: IGOS as an external control arm, Bayesian adaptive platform trials and ordinal or longitudinal analysis of the full GBS-DS instead of a walk/no-walk split, which loses statistical power [[Wiegers2025]]
- The GBS-DS mainly measures walking and breathing; it does not capture arm function, pain, mood or quality of life, which drives interest in patient-reported outcomes [[Wiegers2025]]

### frontier · Nerve ultrasound
- At days 2–3 after onset, 27 people with GBS had enlarged nerves on ultrasound in all nerves measured except the sural, compared with 31 controls; the vagus nerve was enlarged (median 3.0 versus 2.0 mm²), most in people with autonomic dysfunction (4.0 mm²) [[Grimm2016]]
- Cervical spinal nerve enlargement had resolved at 6 months, while the vagus stayed enlarged in those with persistent autonomic dysfunction. The authors propose vagus enlargement as a possible risk marker for dysautonomia (Hypothesis) [[Grimm2016]]
- A 2026 review states MRI and nerve ultrasound are not routine diagnostic tests for GBS [[Freiha2026]]

### frontier · Lessons from Zika and SARS-CoV-2
- French Polynesia 2013–2014: 41 of 42 people with GBS (98%) had anti-Zika IgM or IgG and all had neutralising antibodies, versus 56% of 98 hospital controls (p<0.0001). This case-control study was the first evidence that Zika causes GBS (Established) [[CaoLormeau2016]]
- Colombia 2015–2016: 66 of 68 people (97%) had Zika-compatible symptoms before GBS, median 7 days earlier; Zika RNA was found in 17 of 42 tested (40%), mostly in urine. 20 of 42 (48%) had a parainfectious onset [[Parra2016]]
- The two outbreaks differed in subtype: the Polynesian cases had AMAN-type electrophysiology, while 78% of Colombian cases tested were AIDP-type [[CaoLormeau2016,Parra2016]]
- SARS-CoV-2, UK: GBS incidence fell from March to May 2020 compared with 2016–2019, and regional GBS and COVID-19 incidences did not correlate (r=0.06). The authors found no epidemiological or phenotypic clue of causation [[Keddie2021]]
- SARS-CoV-2, IGOS: 11 of 49 people (22%) with GBS early in the pandemic had confirmed or probable infection, with a demyelinating subtype in all 8 tested and median 16 days from infection to weakness. The authors note recruitment bias and that GBS may rarely follow SARS-CoV-2 [[Luijten2021]]

### Exam hooks
- Eculizumab (anti-C5) failed its primary endpoint in both JET-GBS and the 57-patient phase 3 trial despite near-complete C5 blockade [[Misawa2018,Kuwabara2024]]
- Tanruprubart blocks C1q, the first step of the classical pathway; eculizumab blocks C5, the last step before the membrane attack complex [[Freiha2026]]
- In the tanruprubart phase 3 trial the control arm had no IVIg or plasma exchange, so it compared drug with placebo, not with standard care [[Freiha2026,Wiegers2025]]
- Imlifidase cleaves IgG; efgartigimod blocks FcRn so IgG is broken down faster. Neither has a randomised GBS result [[Freiha2026]]
- SID-GBS: a second IVIg course for poor prognosis gave no benefit (adjusted OR 1.4) and more serious adverse events (35% versus 16%) [[Walgaard2021]]
- A repeat course is still considered for a treatment-related fluctuation, which differs from non-response [[Wiegers2025]]
- High serum NfL early in GBS predicts poorer walking outcome and adds to mEGOS, but not to respiratory prediction [[vanTilburg2024,Thomma2025]]
- Zika was linked to GBS by a case-control study in French Polynesia; SARS-CoV-2 showed no clear causal signal in UK epidemiology [[CaoLormeau2016,Keddie2021]]

### In plain words
- Doctors have used the same two treatments for GBS for about 30 years. They help, but some people still recover slowly or not fully [[Wiegers2025]]
- Researchers are testing new medicines. Some try to clear the harmful antibodies faster. Others try to switch off "complement", a part of the immune system that damages nerves [[Wiegers2025]]
- One complement medicine, eculizumab, did not help in two trials in Japan [[Kuwabara2024,Misawa2018]]
- Another, tanruprubart, looked helpful in a large trial in Bangladesh and the Philippines. The full results are not yet published in a medical journal, and it has not been compared with usual treatment [[Freiha2026,Crescioli2026]]
- Giving a second full course of IVIg to people expected to do badly did not help in a trial, and it caused more side effects [[Walgaard2021]]
- A blood test called neurofilament light shows how much nerve damage there is. It may one day help predict recovery, but it is not yet a routine test [[vanTilburg2024,Freiha2026]]

## Open questions and controversies
- Is tanruprubart effective? Position A: a single 30 mg/kg dose improved week-8 disability and later outcomes versus placebo (company-reported, funded review) [[Gorson2025,Crescioli2026]]. Position B: the control arm had no standard care, the full paper is unpublished, and results may not transfer to IVIg-treated populations [[Freiha2026,Wiegers2025]]. Status: peer-reviewed full report, IGOS comparison and regulatory review awaited.
- Why did C1q blockade appear to work when C5 blockade did not? Position A: tanruprubart acts at the start of the classical pathway (C1q), upstream of the C5 step that eculizumab blocks (Hypothesis) [[Freiha2026,Gorson2025]]. Position B: trial populations differed (Bangladesh and Philippines without IVIg, versus Japan with IVIg), so the comparison may reflect design not mechanism [[Kuwabara2024,Wiegers2025]]. Status: unresolved; the empasiprubart (anti-C2) add-on trial may add evidence [[NCT07821762]].
- Should anyone get a second IVIg course? Position A: no, for poor prognosis alone [[Walgaard2021,Roe2025]]. Position B: small low-quality series report improvement after a second dose [[Alnaeem2025]]. Status: settled against routine use for poor prognosis; re-treatment for treatment-related fluctuations remains accepted [[Wiegers2025]].
- Should IVIg non-responders switch to plasma exchange? Position A: no evidence of benefit from either repeat IVIg or plasma exchange [[Roe2025]]. Position B: question undefined and needs an RCT [[Wiegers2025]]. Status: open.
- Should mild GBS be treated? Position A: one IVIg course did not change 4-week disability [[Verboon2021]]. Position B: 40% had residual symptoms at 1 year and IVIg shortened time to full strength, so better or earlier treatment may help [[Verboon2021,Wiegers2025]]. Status: open; an early-stage placebo-controlled trial has been proposed.
- Should Miller Fisher syndrome be treated? Position A: natural recovery is good and IVIg did not change time to recovery [[Mori2007]]. Position B: about three quarters are treated in practice [[Verboon2019]]. Status: no controlled trials exist [[Overell2007]].
- Can FcRn blockers replace IVIg? Position A: fast responses in retrospective Chinese series [[Bi2026,Cai2025]]. Position B: no RCT exists, and a two-centre comparison did not meet its primary outcome [[Zhang2025,Freiha2026]]. Status: one US RCT withdrawn; a Chinese RCT not yet recruiting [[NCT05701189,NCT07806916]].
- Can neurofilament light serve as a trial endpoint? Position A: tracks damage and adds to prognosis [[vanTilburg2024,Thomma2025]]. Position B: cost and standardisation not solved [[Freiha2026]]. Status: used as an exploratory marker in complement trials [[Mohammad2025]].
- Can observational data replace control arms? Position A: IGOS external controls make trials feasible in a rare disease [[Wiegers2025]]. Position B: unobserved confounding likely influenced the ISID results despite propensity matching [[Wiegers2025,Verboon2020]]. Status: being tested with the imlifidase and tanruprubart programmes.
