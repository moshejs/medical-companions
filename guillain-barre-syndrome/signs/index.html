Part 2 · Diagnosis

Signs & red flags

What Guillain-Barré syndrome usually looks like as it unfolds, how often each feature appears, and the findings that should make anyone look for a different cause. Scrub the timeline or press Play.

No single sign or test proves GBS. The diagnosis rests on the pattern: weakness of both sides that worsens over days, reflexes that fade, and often tingling and pain, in a person with no better explanation 266,473. About two-thirds report an infection in the 6 weeks before, and about one-third report none of the usual events, so a missing infection does not rule GBS out 266,473.

EmergencyWeakness that is getting worse quickly, or trouble breathing or swallowing, needs emergency care. In GBS, breathing can fail without the person feeling breathless 266,473.

Step 1 · before the weakness

Tingling and pain often come first

Classic GBS begins with tingling (paraesthesias) or numbness in the feet and hands, with weakness at the same time or soon after 266. In 156 people followed prospectively, 36% had pain in the 2 weeks before any weakness 392. Severe diffuse pain, or an isolated cranial nerve problem, can come before the weakness 266.

In plain wordsIt often starts with pins and needles in the toes and fingers. Many people also ache in the back or legs, sometimes before they feel weak.
Exam hookPain precedes weakness in about one-third (36%) 392.

How often each feature appears, and where

IGOS enrolled people with GBS across Europe and the Americas, Asia and Bangladesh. It is a hospital cohort, not a population sample, and the same feature was much commoner in some regions than in others 102. Pick a region and a time point.

Both sides

Weakness is bilateral

All 494 Dutch adults had weakness of limbs on both sides. It stayed in the legs in 6% and in the arms in 1% 131. Among 67 Dutch children, 70% presented with weakness of all four limbs and 23% with weakness of the legs only 382.

Reflexes

Reduced in 91% at first

Reflexes in weak limbs were reduced at the first assessment in 91% of the Dutch adults and in all during follow-up, though 10 people (2%) kept normal reflexes in weak arms 131.

Variant

Pure motor in Bangladesh

The commonest clinical variant was sensorimotor in Europe and the Americas (69%) and in Asia (43%), but pure motor in Bangladesh (69%) 102.

Antecedents

Infection is common, not required

About two-thirds report an infection in the 6 weeks before onset; about one-third report none of the events asked about 266,473.

Pain is part of GBS

Pain is easy to overlook in a disease named for weakness. It can be muscular, radicular (from the nerve roots) or neuropathic (from damaged nerve) 266, and it occurs across the whole spectrum, including Miller Fisher syndrome, mild disease and pure motor forms 392.

Reflexes: the people who keep them

Reduced or absent reflexes are a required feature in the criteria 473, yet a minority of people with GBS keep normal or even brisk reflexes. In 213 people from three hospital cohorts, 23 (about 10%) kept normal or exaggerated reflexes throughout 534.

In 54 Japanese patients, 7 (13%) developed brisk reflexes in early recovery, 6 of them with AMAN and all 7 with anti-GM1 antibodies 253. The guideline states that initial hyperreflexia does not exclude GBS, though it still makes it less likely 473.

Exam hookRequired in the NINDS criteria as adapted: progressive weakness of both arms and legs plus reduced or absent reflexes in the affected limbs 266. Remember the exception: about 10% keep reflexes, mostly pure motor AMAN 534.

Autonomic signs

How often dysautonomia is reported depends on how it is defined and where people are studied: 25% at IGOS entry 102, 71 of 187 (38%) in a retrospective Mayo Clinic series 71, and up to two-thirds in a literature review 535. One Mexican abstract reports "51 (31%)" of 214, but 51 of 214 is 24%, so that percentage is internally inconsistent 32.

Children can look different

Under 6 years

Young children can present with poorly localised pain, refusal to bear weight, irritability, meningism or an unsteady gait, which can delay the diagnosis 266.

Faster to the worst point

In 67 Dutch children (median age 5.0 years), the progressive phase lasted a median 6 days (IQR 3–8) and under 4 weeks in all 382. Bilateral limb weakness was present at admission in 93% and at the worst point in all 382.

Red-flag sorter

Pick a finding. The panel says whether published consensus counts it as typical of GBS, possible but less typical, or a red flag that casts doubt on the diagnosis, and the body map shows where it is found. Red flags are prompts to look further, not proof that something else is going on 10,266.

Finding 7 of 20

A sharp sensory level on the trunk

Red flag: look for another cause

A sharp sensory level casts doubt on GBS 266. With extensor plantars, hyperreflexia and a cord lesion on MRI it points to transverse myelitis or another cord cause 452,473.
