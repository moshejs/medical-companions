The 20/30/40 rule, EGRIS and mEGOS are published names, and their cards are tagged so. Every other card is a memory aid written for this guide: a short phrase to hold a published fact in mind. None of those is an official grouping from any guideline, drug label or patient organisation, and none replaces the source it points to. Doses, thresholds and schedules are quoted as published, never as instructions.

Mnemonic in motion: the 20/30/40 rule

The 20/30/40 rule names three bedside breathing measurements that flag a risk of respiratory failure in GBS [[Lawn2001]]. Play the animation to see each number in turn, the 30% fall that also counts, the single breath count, and the vital capacity triggers in the 2023 EAN/PNS guideline [[vanDoorn2023]]. For the published risk scores, EGRIS and mEGRIS, see .

A teaching tool that explains how a published bedside rule is built. It is never for an individual's diagnosis, prognosis or treatment. The readings drawn are schematic, not measurements; the thresholds are quoted as published. [[Lawn2001,vanDoorn2023]]

These cards are for learning, not for deciding anything about one person's care. Outcome figures are population statistics from cohorts and trials, and they cannot predict what will happen to any one person. Weakness that is getting worse quickly, or trouble breathing or swallowing, needs emergency care [[Leonhard2019,vanDoorn2023]].

Sources

Every reference behind the GBS Companion: randomised trials, Cochrane reviews, cohorts such as IGOS, the 2023 EAN/PNS guideline and the 2019 ten-steps guideline, US drug labels and trial registrations. The small numbers throughout the app point here; filter by research area or search.

Research passes ran in parallel, one per area: epidemiology and history; triggers, vaccines and molecular mimicry; subtypes and electrophysiology; the course, diagnostic criteria, CSF and look-alikes; immunotherapy; breathing, autonomic problems and intensive care; prognosis, rehabilitation and long-term outcomes; children, pregnancy, nursing and living with GBS; and the research frontier. Before inclusion each source was checked, where possible, against PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, ClinicalTrials.gov or the regulator's label, and the green tag on an entry names the service that confirmed it. Works found by more than one pass appear once, with any extra keys listed beside them.

Some guideline and criteria texts could not be read in full, including the original Brighton case definition and the 2014 classification criteria. Where that matters, modules say the statement is reported through a secondary source. Entries marked %t:sources.check% are pages with no PMID or DOI, or works that could not be confirmed.
