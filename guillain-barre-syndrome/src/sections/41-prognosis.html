Part 4 · Recovery

Prognosis: who walks again, and when

Most people with Guillain-Barré syndrome walk again, though it can take months or years. Watch two large cohorts regain walking over six months, then try the published EGOS and mEGOS scores as teaching calculators.

8 in 10

walk on their own again by 6 months after onset [[Leonhard2019]]. About 2 in 10 cannot yet walk unaided at 6 months despite treatment [[vanDoorn2008]].

3 to 10%

of people with GBS die, most often from heart and breathing complications, in the acute or the recovery phase [[Leonhard2019,vanDoorn2008]].

67%

of deaths in a Dutch cohort came in the recovery phase, and only 47% happened in intensive care [[vanDenBerg2013]].

79%

of survivors who had needed a ventilator regained independent walking in a 20-year intensive care series [[Fletcher2000]].

Every figure on this page describes a group of people in a named study. None of them predicts what will happen to one person, and recovery after GBS varies widely [[Busl2023,Leonhard2019]].

Why the two cohorts differ

The Dutch cohort was used to build mEGOS, and everyone in it was treated with IVIg or plasma exchange. In the Bangladeshi cohort, 86% received supportive care only. The people were also different: median age 28 against 52 years, and preceding diarrhoea in 51% against 23% [[Papri2022mEGOS]]. Diarrhoea matters because preceding Campylobacter jejuni infection was linked to axonal damage, slower recovery and more disability at 1 year [[Rees1995]].

In the international IGOS cohort, the share walking unaided at 1 year was 91% in Asia outside Bangladesh, 83% in Europe and the Americas, and 69% in Bangladesh [[Doets2018]]. Money and access shape recovery too: in Colombia, lower socioeconomic status went with worse disability, and 22% had no rehabilitation during the acute episode [[AceroGarces2024]].

EGOS and mEGOS: how the scores add up

Two Dutch scores estimate the chance of being unable to walk unaided, meaning a GBS disability score above 2 [[Papri2022mEGOS]]. EGOS is scored 2 weeks after hospital entry from age, diarrhoea and the GBS disability score, and predicts walking at 6 months [[vanKoningsveld2007]]. mEGOS swaps the disability score for the MRC sum score, so it can be used at admission or day 7, and predicts walking at 4 weeks, 3 months and 6 months [[Walgaard2011]]. The GBS disability score runs from 0 (no disability) to 6 (death); the MRC sum score from 0 (quadriplegic) to 60 (normal strength) [[vanTilburg2024]].

In plain wordsDoctors can use a score based on age, recent diarrhoea and muscle strength to estimate walking chances for groups of people. It is a guide, not a verdict [[Walgaard2011,Busl2023]].

The EAN/PNS guideline advises mEGOS for assessing outcome, and mEGRIS for the risk of needing ventilation [[vanDoorn2023]]. The Neurocritical Care Society rates EGOS and mEGOS moderately reliable for independent walking at 3 months and beyond, judges the disability grade at nadir alone not reliable (weak recommendation, low-quality evidence), and advises that prognosis should consider the whole clinical picture and never rest on a single variable (strong recommendation) [[Busl2023]]. In the SID-GBS trial, mEGOS of 6 or more defined a poor prognosis (112 of 327 people), and a second IVIg course did not help them; see Steroids, repeats & relapses [[Walgaard2021]].

Axonal does not always mean slow

Early evidence

A trend, not a rule

Across all IGOS regions, people with the axonal subtype were younger, had fewer sensory problems and showed a trend towards poorer recovery than those with the demyelinating subtype [[Doets2018]].

Slow, then walking

In 97 Japanese people, 14% with AMAN and 6% with AIDP could not walk alone at 6 months. Of the 6 slow AMAN recoveries, 4 walked by 1 year and the other 2 at 28 and 57 months [[Hiraga2005]].

Two patterns with one antibody

People with IgG anti-GM1 antibodies had more poor recoveries (5 of 19 against 0 of 22 unable to walk alone at 6 months) and also more very fast ones (9 of 19 against 4 of 22 improving at least two grades within a month) [[Kuwabara1998]].

Subtype is not part of EGOS or mEGOS. The nerve mechanisms are in Subtypes: AIDP & AMAN.

After a ventilator

Most walk again

79% of ventilated survivors regained independent walking in a 20-year series, and 19% improved by at least one grade after more than a year [[Fletcher2000]]. In 76 intensive care patients, 75% walked independently at an average of 3 years, with a median of 198 days to get there [[Dhar2008]].

Long ventilation, slower road

Of 526 trial patients, 6% needed ventilation beyond 2 months. At 6 months 18% of them walked unaided, against 76% after shorter ventilation. Yet 58% of a long-ventilated group walked unaided at last follow-up, 31% only after more than a year [[vanDenBerg2018]].

German centres

In 110 ventilated people followed for a mean of 52.6 months, 53.8% reached a GBS disability score of 0 or 1, and 73.7% of survivors had no or mild disability on the Barthel Index [[Witsch2013]].

In plain wordsNeeding a breathing machine is serious, but many people who needed one walk again. Recovery can keep going for years, and some people first walk again more than a year after becoming ill [[Fletcher2000,vanDenBerg2018,Hiraga2005]].

Deaths: when, where and why

Death is uncommon but real. In 527 Dutch trial and cohort patients, 2.8% died within 6 months and 3.9% of those followed for a year, at a median of 76 days after weakness began. Risk rose with age, weakness at entry, ventilation, delay before hospital entry and time to peak disability [[vanDenBerg2013]]. Because up to two-thirds of deaths come in recovery, the ten-step guideline advises watching for arrhythmias, blood pressure swings and mucus plugs after leaving intensive care [[Leonhard2019]].

Exam hookMortality 3 to 10%; up to two-thirds of deaths in the recovery phase; in Dhaka the lack of a ventilator when needed was the strongest risk factor (HR 11.9) [[Leonhard2019,vanDenBerg2013,Ishaque2017]].

Special groups

Miller Fisher

Usually a full recovery

Pure Miller Fisher syndrome tends to be mild, and most people recover completely within 6 months [[Leonhard2019]]. In 50 consecutive people, recovery was usually good with no residual deficits [[Mori2001MFS]], and in 92 people IVIg and plasma exchange did not seem to change the outcome [[Mori2007]].

Children

In 421 children, 19% were ventilated and one died [[Roodbol2023]]. Yet in 37 people followed a median of 11 years after childhood GBS, 65% had some residual complaint [[Roodbol2014Kids]]. More in Children & pregnancy.

Hearing a prognosis

At admission, the most stressful things for 110 French adults were fear of staying paralysed, uncertainty about the course, and fear of intubation [[Sharshar2012]]. Accurate information about the fairly good chance of recovery and the low risk of recurrence can reduce fear [[Leonhard2019]]. But in interviews two years on, some people described disappointment after an overly positive early prognosis, and a long wait for recovery [[Forsberg2015Balance]].

SupportWaiting is hard. Talking with others who have had GBS can help, and the GBS|CIDP Foundation International and national groups can connect people [[Leonhard2019]].
