IVIg: intravenous immunoglobulin

IVIg is a drip of antibodies pooled from thousands of blood donors, and one of the two treatments shown to speed recovery from Guillain-Barré syndrome. Follow it from the donor pool to the nerve, see what happens to blood IgG, and how it compared with plasma exchange.

IVIg and plasma exchange are the two immunotherapies with trial support in GBS, and guidelines treat them as equally effective [[vanDoorn2023,Leonhard2019]]. This module reports what guidelines, trials and drug labels say. It is not medical advice, and the doses below are quoted as published, never as instructions. Pick a step or press Play.

The standard course in total: 0.4 g/kg a day for 5 days, as published in the 2023 EAN/PNS guideline [[vanDoorn2023]].

2 weeks

IVIg trials started within 2 weeks of onset of weakness. Beyond that, evidence on efficacy is lacking [[Leonhard2019,vanDoorn2023]].

No adequate placebo-controlled IVIg trial exists in adults. Plasma exchange was already proven against supportive care, so IVIg was tested against plasma exchange, and the evidence that IVIg works is therefore indirect [[Hughes2014,vanDoorn2023]]. Neither treatment has been shown to reduce mortality [[Hughes2007]].

How it might work

Established

Not settled

A 2015 review says IVIg's mechanisms "are poorly understood", and many proposed actions come from lab or animal work rather than human tissue [[Lunemann2015,Dalakas2014]].

Early evidence

Neutralising the antibody

IVIg reduced the nerve-blocking activity of seven GBS sera on mouse muscle, dose-dependently; Fab fragments worked as well as whole IVIg, Fc fragments did not [[Buchwald2002]]. In vitro, IVIg's variable region bound anti-GM1 antibody [[Yuki1996]]. Dalakas states this anti-idiotype fraction "appears partially responsible" [[Dalakas2014]].

Hypothesis

Complement, Fc receptors, cytokines

Proposed actions include blocking complement binding and membrane attack complex formation, neutralising cytokines, lowering antibody production and modulating Fc-receptor-driven phagocytosis, inferred across diseases [[Dalakas1998]]. A 2022 GBS review adds faster antibody breakdown and regulatory T-cell effects [[Rajabally2022]].

Early evidence

Sugar on the stem

In a mouse model of antibody-mediated nerve injury, sialylated IVIg protected at a tenfold lower dose than whole IVIg, while unsialylated fractions did not help. Animal data only [[Zhang2016ExpNeurol]].

Blood levels and outcome: association or cause?

In 174 Dutch people given a standard course, the rise in serum IgG at 2 weeks (ΔIgG) ranged widely, with a mean of 7.8 g/L (SD 5.6). A small rise went with slower recovery and fewer people walking unaided at 6 months, and stayed linked to poor outcome after adjustment [[Kuitwaard2009Pharm]]. A later review summarised poor outcome at 6 months as 28% in the lowest quartile of rise and 7% in the highest [[Rajabally2022]]. Quartile cut-offs differ between cohorts, partly because of differences in severity [[vanTilburg2025]].

Serum albumin also fell after IVIg (median 4.2 to 3.7 g/dL at 2 weeks), and low albumin was associated with respiratory failure and inability to walk unaided [[Fokkink2017]]. Published IgG half-lives, measured in people with immunodeficiency and not in GBS, are 30.0 days (Bivigam) and 28.5 days (Asceniv) [[FDA-Bivigam,FDA-Asceniv]].

Position A: exposure matters

A low IgG rise is independently linked to poor outcome, suggesting that IgG exposure matters and that higher dosing might help [[Kuitwaard2009Pharm]].

Position B: a marker of severity

More severely affected people may break IgG down faster, so a small rise may mark severity rather than cause poor outcome Hypothesis [[Rajabally2022]]. A second course raised IgG but did not improve outcome [[vanTilburg2025,Walgaard2021]].

Status: the association is established; dosing guided by measured IgG is untested [[vanTilburg2025]].

Is IVIg licensed for GBS in the US?

No IVIg label retrieved from the openFDA dataset lists GBS as an indication; the word "Guillain" appeared in none of the 26 immune-globulin labels checked. This is a search of a dataset, not an FDA statement, and three of the labels are 2010 to 2011 snapshots, so GBS use in the US appears to be off-label on this evidence (an inference) [[FDA-Bivigam,FDA-Asceniv,FDA-GamunexC,FDA-Privigen,FDA-GammagardLiquid]]. Gamunex-C's retrieved label lists primary humoral immunodeficiency, ITP and CIDP [[FDA-GamunexC]].

In plain wordsIVIg is a drip of antibodies from many blood donors, usually given once a day for 5 days [[vanDoorn2023,Lunemann2015]]. It helps people recover faster; it does not switch the illness off straight away [[Hughes2014]]. It can cause headache, and rarely blood clots, kidney problems or a reaction of the lining around the brain; the care team watches for these [[FDA-Bivigam]].
Exam hookIVIg 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days (2 g/kg) within 2 weeks; equal to plasma exchange; boxed warning for thrombosis and renal failure; IgA deficiency with anti-IgA antibodies and a hypersensitivity history is a label contraindication [[vanDoorn2023,Hughes2014,FDA-Bivigam]].
