Part 2 · Diagnosis

Signs & red flags

What Guillain-Barré syndrome usually looks like as it unfolds, how often each feature appears, and the findings that should make anyone look for a different cause. Scrub the timeline or press Play.

No single sign or test proves GBS. The diagnosis rests on the pattern: weakness of both sides that worsens over days, reflexes that fade, and often tingling and pain, in a person with no better explanation [[Leonhard2019,vanDoorn2023]]. About two-thirds report an infection in the 6 weeks before, and about one-third report none of the usual events, so a missing infection does not rule GBS out [[Leonhard2019,vanDoorn2023]].

EmergencyWeakness that is getting worse quickly, or trouble breathing or swallowing, needs emergency care. In GBS, breathing can fail without the person feeling breathless [[Leonhard2019,vanDoorn2023]].

How often each feature appears, and where

IGOS enrolled people with GBS across Europe and the Americas, Asia and Bangladesh. It is a hospital cohort, not a population sample, and the same feature was much commoner in some regions than in others [[Doets2018]]. Pick a region and a time point.

Region
When

Both sides

Weakness is bilateral

All 494 Dutch adults had weakness of limbs on both sides. It stayed in the legs in 6% and in the arms in 1% [[Fokke2014]]. Among 67 Dutch children, 70% presented with weakness of all four limbs and 23% with weakness of the legs only [[Roodbol2017]].

Reflexes

Reduced in 91% at first

Reflexes in weak limbs were reduced at the first assessment in 91% of the Dutch adults and in all during follow-up, though 10 people (2%) kept normal reflexes in weak arms [[Fokke2014]].

Variant

Pure motor in Bangladesh

The commonest clinical variant was sensorimotor in Europe and the Americas (69%) and in Asia (43%), but pure motor in Bangladesh (69%) [[Doets2018]].

Antecedents

Infection is common, not required

About two-thirds report an infection in the 6 weeks before onset; about one-third report none of the events asked about [[Leonhard2019,vanDoorn2023]].

Pain is part of GBS

Pain is easy to overlook in a disease named for weakness. It can be muscular, radicular (from the nerve roots) or neuropathic (from damaged nerve) [[Leonhard2019]], and it occurs across the whole spectrum, including Miller Fisher syndrome, mild disease and pure motor forms [[Ruts2010Pain]].

In plain wordsMany people with GBS feel pain, sometimes before any weakness. It is a real part of the illness, and it is worth telling the care team about it.

Reflexes: the people who keep them

Reduced or absent reflexes are a required feature in the criteria [[vanDoorn2023]], yet a minority of people with GBS keep normal or even brisk reflexes. In 213 people from three hospital cohorts, 23 (about 10%) kept normal or exaggerated reflexes throughout [[Yuki2012JNeurol]].

In 54 Japanese patients, 7 (13%) developed brisk reflexes in early recovery, 6 of them with AMAN and all 7 with anti-GM1 antibodies [[Kuwabara1999]]. The guideline states that initial hyperreflexia does not exclude GBS, though it still makes it less likely [[vanDoorn2023]].

Exam hookRequired in the NINDS criteria as adapted: progressive weakness of both arms and legs plus reduced or absent reflexes in the affected limbs [[Leonhard2019]]. Remember the exception: about 10% keep reflexes, mostly pure motor AMAN [[Yuki2012JNeurol]].

Autonomic signs

How often dysautonomia is reported depends on how it is defined and where people are studied: 25% at IGOS entry [[Doets2018]], 71 of 187 (38%) in a retrospective Mayo Clinic series [[Chakraborty2020]], and up to two-thirds in a literature review [[Zaeem2019]]. One Mexican abstract reports "51 (31%)" of 214, but 51 of 214 is 24%, so that percentage is internally inconsistent [[BazanRodriguez2023]].

Children can look different

Under 6 years

Young children can present with poorly localised pain, refusal to bear weight, irritability, meningism or an unsteady gait, which can delay the diagnosis [[Leonhard2019]].

Faster to the worst point

In 67 Dutch children (median age 5.0 years), the progressive phase lasted a median 6 days (IQR 3–8) and under 4 weeks in all [[Roodbol2017]]. Bilateral limb weakness was present at admission in 93% and at the worst point in all [[Roodbol2017]].
