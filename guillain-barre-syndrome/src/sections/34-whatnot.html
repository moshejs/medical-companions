Part 3 · Acute care

Steroids, repeats & relapses

What did not work in Guillain-Barré syndrome, what to make of no early improvement, and what it means when someone gets better and then worse again: a treatment-related fluctuation, acute-onset CIDP or, rarely, GBS a second time.

Steroid medicines help some similar nerve conditions, but they have not helped GBS in trials [[GBSSteroidTrial1993,Hughes2016]]. Treatment also does not always bring a smooth recovery. How often is GBS relabelled CIDP?

In a US claims database, 32.0% of people first coded GBS were later coded CIDP [[Stino2024]]. Prospective clinical data and guidelines give about 5% [[Ruts2010,vanDoorn2023]]. Claims coding is the less reliable of the two; that judgement is the research team's for this guide.

Who fluctuates?

Fluctuation rates were 6 of 72 after plasma exchange and 8 of 74 after IVIg in the Dutch trial [[Kleyweg1991]], 16 of 172 in a Dutch cohort [[Visser1998]] and 53 of 1,023 in IGOS [[Verboon2019]]. Risk did not depend on the treatment given, and those who fluctuated tended to have a protracted course [[Visser1998]].

Position A: ineffective

The unadjusted result was not significant, and the guideline weakly recommends against IV methylprednisolone added to IVIg [[vanKoningsveld2004,vanDoorn2023]].

Position B: possible benefit

The adjusted odds ratio of 1.89 suggested a possible benefit, and the authors called for further investigation [[vanKoningsveld2004]].

Status: no confirmatory trial was identified. The ten-steps guideline cites eight steroid trials and Cochrane six with outcome data, probably a counting difference [[Leonhard2019,Hughes2016]].

A second IVIg course

A small rise in blood IgG after IVIg is linked to poor outcome (module 3.2), so a second dose seemed logical [[Kuitwaard2009Pharm]]. Before the SID-GBS trial, around 25% of severely affected people worldwide received one [[Walgaard2021]]. The trial included 327 people, of whom 112 had a poor prognosis; it found no benefit and more serious adverse events, and the authors concluded that a second course "should not be considered" for a poor prognosis [[Walgaard2021]].

That conclusion is about re-dosing because of a poor prognosis. Re-treating a fluctuation is a separate question, which the guideline handles on its own [[vanDoorn2023]]. Practice still varies: 39.7% of 2,325 people in a US database received repeat IVIg [[Stino2024]], one US centre re-treated 10 of 68 people between 2019 and 2025 [[Mayman2026]], and in IGOS 35% of those not improving got a second immunomodulatory treatment [[Verboon2019]].

Mild GBS, late arrivals and Miller Fisher syndrome

Treatment evidence comes mainly from adults with typical, severe GBS. Mild forms, variants and people who arrive late remain clinical dilemmas [[Verboon2017]]. IVIg trials started within 2 weeks and plasma exchange trials within 4 weeks; beyond that, evidence on efficacy is lacking [[Leonhard2019]].

Mild GBS in IGOS

Of 188 people able to walk, IVIg (148) was not associated with lower 4-week disability than supportive care (40): adjusted OR 1.62, 95% CI 0.63 to 4.13. Time to full strength was shorter (28 against 56 days) and pain at 26 weeks less common (22% against 40%). At 1 year, 40% still had symptoms [[Verboon2021]].

Established

Two exchanges for mild GBS

Plasma exchange has randomised support in mild disease: two exchanges beat none for time to motor recovery [[FrenchCooperative1997]].

Treated anyway

In IGOS, 75% of people with mild GBS and 76% with Miller Fisher syndrome received immunotherapy, against 97% with severe GBS [[Verboon2019]].

Miller Fisher syndrome

In 92 people, neither IVIg nor plasmapheresis changed outcome compared with no immune treatment, presumably because natural recovery is good [[Mori2007]]. The ten-steps guideline does not generally recommend treatment for pure Miller Fisher syndrome but advises close monitoring, as some develop limb, bulbar or breathing involvement [[Leonhard2019]].

Pregnancy

Neither IVIg nor plasma exchange is contraindicated in pregnancy; IVIg might be preferred because plasma exchange needs additional monitoring [[Leonhard2019]]. IVIg labels state that there are no human data on drug-associated risk and that immune globulins cross the placenta [[FDA-Bivigam,FDA-Asceniv]]. A 2026 review of 101 published cases, biased toward severe or unusual ones, reported ventilation in 45%, maternal mortality of 5.2% and neonatal survival of 85.6% [[Yakubu2026]].

In plain wordsSteroid medicines help some similar nerve conditions, but they have not helped GBS in trials [[GBSSteroidTrial1993,Hughes2016]]. If you get better and then worse again within a couple of months, tell your team; a repeat treatment may be considered [[Leonhard2019,vanDoorn2023]]. If you get worse after 8 weeks, doctors may check for a related long-term condition called CIDP [[vanDoorn2023]]. Getting GBS a second time is uncommon, at about 2 to 5 in 100 people [[Leonhard2019]].
Exam hookSteroids do not help, and oral steroids may delay recovery [[Hughes2016]]. A second IVIg course for poor prognosis: no benefit, serious adverse events 35% against 16% [[Walgaard2021]]. Fluctuation within 8 weeks (6 to 10%); A-CIDP with worsening after 8 weeks or 3 or more episodes (about 5%); recurrence 2 to 5% [[Leonhard2019,Ruts2010]].
