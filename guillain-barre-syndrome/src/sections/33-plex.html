Plasma exchange

Plasma exchange (plasmapheresis) filters the liquid part of the blood out of the body, taking circulating antibodies with it. It was the first treatment proven to speed recovery from Guillain-Barré syndrome. Watch the circuit, see why it takes several sessions, and explore what the trials found.

Plasma exchange beat supportive care in randomised trials in the 1980s, and IVIg was later tested against it [[Chevret2017,Hughes2014]]. Today guidelines rank the two as equally effective [[vanDoorn2023]]. What follows reports guidelines and trials; it is not medical advice, and volumes are quoted as published, never as instructions. Combining and sequencing

Established

Both, one after the other

Plasma exchange followed by IVIg gave 0.2 grade more improvement than plasma exchange alone (95% CI -0.14 to 0.54; 249 people): not clinically significant [[Hughes2014]]. The guideline strongly recommends against it [[vanDoorn2023]]. In US practice, 6.1% of 2,325 people still received combination therapy [[Stino2024]].

Switching after no response

The guideline does not suggest switching to the other treatment, for lack of trial evidence, and notes that exchanging plasma right after IVIg washes out the treatment just given [[vanDoorn2023]].

Early evidence

The zipper method

Alternating plasma exchange and IVIg for five cycles in nine ventilated children, uncontrolled: all nine walked unaided by day 28 [[Kesici2019]]. A modified version was used in four children after standard therapy failed [[Nikolaus2022]]. The guideline says it needs a proper randomised trial before any effect can be claimed [[vanDoorn2023]].

Immunoadsorption

Not recommended: one randomised trial was stopped early without significant benefit [[vanDoorn2023]].

When treatment is out of reach

In Bangladesh, a full IVIg course for a 60 kg adult was estimated at US$12,000 to 16,000 and conventional plasma exchange at US$4,500 to 5,000, about 4,000 and 1,250 days of mean income [[Islam2018]]. Only 10 to 12% of people with GBS there receive either treatment [[Papri2021]]; in IGOS, 83% of 189 Bangladeshi participants were untreated, whereas 92% of 1,023 participants elsewhere received immunotherapy [[Verboon2019]].

Small-volume plasma exchange (SVPE) separates plasma by letting blood settle in a bag, then returns the cells, three to six times a day for eight days [[Islam2018]]. It was designed as an affordable alternative [[Islam2017]]. In a single-arm study of 20 adults in Dhaka, the median exchange was 140 mL/kg, there was one serious adverse event (severe sepsis) and no deaths, and the expected cost was about US$500. 14 of 20 improved a grade by 4 weeks, but without a control group this is not an efficacy result [[Islam2018]].

Position A: feasible and cheap

SVPE was feasible and seemed safe in 20 adults Early evidence [[Islam2018]].

Position B: not yet proven

Efficacy is unknown; the guidelines do not endorse it for general use, and the EAN/PNS panel rated the evidence very low certainty [[Leonhard2019,vanDoorn2023]].

In plain wordsPlasma exchange takes out the liquid part of the blood and replaces it, to remove harmful antibodies. It is usually done four or five times over 1 to 2 weeks [[vanDoorn2023,Chevret2017]]. It works about as well as IVIg, and having both one after the other has not been shown to work better [[vanDoorn2023]]. Blood pressure can drop during a session, and the team monitors this [[Coirier2022]].
