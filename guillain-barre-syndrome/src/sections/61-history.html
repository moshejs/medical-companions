Who first described it?

Landry is credited with the first report and Guillain, Barré and Strohl with the spinal fluid finding. [[Afifi1994,Waclawik2018]] The 1916 paper did not mention Landry, and others, including Duménil, Osler and Grainger Stewart, had reported similar cases without lumbar puncture. [[Afifi1994,Freitas2017]] Haymaker and Kernohan brought Landry's paralysis, acute febrile polyneuritis and GBS under one heading in 1949. [[Haymaker1949,Berciano2017]]

Strohl's name then dropped out of use, and the reasons given vary. [[Afifi1994]] His recordings of the tendon reflexes were an early step towards the nerve tests used at the bedside today. [[Waclawik2018]]

Exam hookAlbuminocytological dissociation, high CSF protein with a normal cell count, dates from the 1916 report in two soldiers. [[Goodfellow2016,Waclawik2018]] Lumbar puncture was then new: Quincke had described it in 1891. [[Afifi1994]]
In plain wordsGBS is named after French doctors who described it in 1916, though Landry wrote about a similar paralysis in 1859. The key clue was the spinal fluid: lots of protein, but few cells.

How the cause was pieced together

1955

An animal model

Waksman and Adams produced experimental allergic neuritis in rabbits with peripheral nerve tissue and adjuvants, with raised CSF protein and usually normal cells. Their model pointed to an immune cause. [[Waksman1955,Afifi1994]]

1969

Inflammation in the nerve

Asbury, Arnason and Adams showed inflammatory cells around small veins with segmental demyelination, from the roots to the peripheral nerves. [[Asbury1969,Afifi1994]]

1986 to 1995

Axons, not just myelin

Feasby proposed an axonal form; summer epidemics in northern China led McKhann's group to name AMAN, and autopsies gave it a structural basis. [[Feasby1986,McKhann1993,Griffin1995]]

1990 to 2004

The germ and the nerve

Anti-GM1 antibodies after C. jejuni (1990), a GM1-like structure on the bacterium (1993) and the disease reproduced in rabbits (2004). [[Yuki1990,Yuki1993,Yuki2004]] A case-control study found recent C. jejuni in 26% of 103 people, against 2% of household controls. [[Rees1995]]

Not everything is settled. Why the 1976 swine influenza vaccine carried a measurable risk is still unresolved, and mouse studies found that inducing anti-GM1 antibodies was not unique to the 1976 vaccines. Early evidence [[Nachamkin2008,Sejvar2011Flu]]

Exam hookSummer epidemics in northern China are the classic AMAN setting. [[McKhann1993,Ho1995]] The 1976 swine influenza vaccine carried about 1 extra case per 100,000 vaccinations; later influenza vaccines about 1 per million. [[Schonberger1979,Leonhard2019]]
In plain wordsOver a hundred years, doctors moved from describing the paralysis to finding its cause: the immune system, often set off by a gut germ whose coat looks like part of the nerve. Treatments that work were found in the 1980s and 1990s.

The mechanism in detail is in Molecular mimicry; today's research, with its evidence labels, is in Research frontier.
