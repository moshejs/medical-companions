Rehabilitation and the long recovery

Recovery from Guillain-Barré syndrome is measured in months and years, and rehabilitation runs through all of it. Follow one Swedish cohort for two years, see the two ways a nerve gets back to work, and compare exercise programmes from the trials.

About 40%

of people hospitalised with GBS need inpatient rehabilitation; earlier ventilator support most strongly predicted a longer stay [[Meythaler1997]].

82 days

was the mean hospital stay in an unselected Swedish cohort of 41 people [[Forsberg2006Burden]].

68% vs 32%

improved in function with high- against low-intensity outpatient rehabilitation in the largest randomised trial [[Khan2011RCT]].

5+ years

Recovery from remaining problems can still happen more than 5 years after onset [[Leonhard2019]].

The ten-step guideline calls arranging rehabilitation with a rehabilitation specialist, physiotherapist and occupational therapist a crucial step: first to reduce disability, and later to restore movement, sensation and fitness [[Leonhard2019]]. A consensus group recommends that disabled people be treated by a multidisciplinary rehabilitation team with an assistive exercise programme [[Hughes2005]].

Straight lines join the three published time points (2 weeks, 1 year, 2 years) of one cohort of 42 adults [[Forsberg2004Impairment,Forsberg2005Disability]]. The body wash and the time spacing are schematic.

Two ways a nerve gets back to work

How fast strength returns depends partly on what the immune attack did. Where antibodies only stopped the signal at the nodes of Ranvier, conduction can come back within weeks [[Kokubun2010,Kuwabara1998GM1]]. Where the axon itself broke down, the nerve has to regrow, and that takes months or longer [[Kuwabara2001,Capasso2011]]. Choose a route and press Play. The nerve damage itself is drawn in Subtypes: AIDP & AMAN.

Exam hookAMAN can recover as fast as AIDP (median 31 against 32 days to walk 5 m with help in northern China): too fast for regrowth of the whole nerve. Reversal of changes at the node and regrowth of nerve terminals in the muscle are the proposed reasons [[Ho1997Recovery,Griffin1996Nodal]].
In plain wordsIf the signal was only blocked, strength can come back fairly quickly. If the nerve fibre itself was damaged, it has to regrow, which can take many months, and some people keep improving for years [[Kuwabara2001,Hiraga2005]].

From the ward to home

In an Israeli rehabilitation cohort of 70 people, the average stay was 20 days on acute wards and 61 days in rehabilitation. The total Functional Independence Measure (FIM) rose by 30 points and the GBS disability score improved by a mean of 1.23, with similar gains after IVIg or plasma exchange [[Bondi2021]]. In 47 inpatients, the Spinal Cord Independence Measure rose from 50 at admission to 80 at discharge and 95 at a mean of 7.5 years [[Shniper2022]].

Not every skill comes back at the same speed. In US data from 2019, the share independent at the end of inpatient rehabilitation was [[Andrews2023]]:

Communication87.5%
Social cognition74.8%
Self-care35.9%
Transfers34.2%
Walking and moving about24.7%

Of 1,304 people in US inpatient rehabilitation, 81.8% went home or to the community, and gains in bed, chair and wheelchair transfers were the strongest predictor of going home [[Kushner2023]]. After discharge the gaps show: in a UK survey of 216 people, over a quarter did not receive community physiotherapy, and age and body pain predicted falls [[Davidson2022Falls]]. In Colombia, 22% had no rehabilitation in the acute episode and 16% none after discharge [[AceroGarces2024]].

Exercise programmes compared

Teaching toolThis compares a made-up schedule with the programmes described in published studies. It is not an exercise prescription. Exercise intensity must be monitored, because overwork can cause fatigue [[Leonhard2019]]; therapists set and adjust any real programme.

In plain wordsPushing too hard can make tiredness worse, so therapists adjust the effort as you go. Programmes that were supervised and built up over about three months did best in the studies [[Leonhard2019,DeLeonMunoz2026,Shah2022]].

How the first two years go

Fastest early

In 6 treated people, strength measured on machines rose fastest in the first 6 months and more slowly from 6 to 18 months, still improving at 18 months; the authors suggest at least 24 months of follow-up. A longer plateau went with less recovery of knee and elbow strength [[ElMhandi2007]].

Quality of life

In a systematic review of 14 studies, the three that followed people over time found large gains in quality of life in the first year, but not clearly after it; most studies were cross-sectional and of low quality [[Darweesh2014]].

Work at two years

Everyone working before GBS was unable to work at 2 weeks; 17% were still unable to work at 2 years [[Forsberg2005Disability]]. More in Life after GBS.
