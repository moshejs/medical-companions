Part 3 · Acute care

Intensive care & pain

Follow a schematic stay on the ventilator from intubation to weaning, against published medians for how long it lasts. Then explore the predictors of a long stay, the complications teams watch for, pain and its trials, and what people say helped.

Intensive care in GBS is mostly about buying time while the nerves recover: breathing for the person, guarding against infection and clots, managing autonomic swings and pain, and keeping contact. Even with complete paralysis, consciousness, vision and hearing are usually intact, so what is said at the bedside matters [[Leonhard2019]]. Pick a step or press Play.

Schematic, not to scale. The stay and the rising vital capacity are illustrative. Published values: weaning lines [[Chevrolet1991,Nguyen2006]]; median ventilation 28 days (IQR 12 to 60) in a Dutch cohort [[Walgaard2017]] and 20 days (IQR 10 to 54) in IGOS [[Luijten2023]].

Will ventilation be long? A week-one predictor

A long stay on the ventilator is the usual argument for an earlier tracheostomy. In a Dutch cohort of ventilated people, the picture one week after intubation predicted ventilation of 14 days or more [[Walgaard2017]]. In Bangladesh, inability to flex the hip at day 7 of ventilation was the strongest predictor of a prolonged stay (predicted probability 82%) [[Papri2024]].

Status: no randomised trial. A review favours early tracheostomy [[Shang2020]] and the ten-steps guideline says it should be considered when there are risk factors for prolonged ventilation, without a fixed day [[Leonhard2019]]. A 2025 review suggests considering it when someone is still ventilator-dependent after 2 weeks with failed weaning, or after failed extubation [[Weiss2025]]. National data show no mortality gain from going early [[Yonezawa2020]], and a search of the 2023 EAN/PNS guideline text finds no mention of tracheostomy [[vanDoorn2023]].

The ten-steps guideline also names pressure ulcers, choking, corneal ulceration when the eyelids cannot close, limb contractures, ossification, pressure palsies, delirium and depression [[Leonhard2019]]. No GBS-specific pressure injury rate was found; guidance is standard preventive care [[Leonhard2019]]. Rarely, IVIg causes acute lung injury: 17 cases were pooled, 2 with GBS [[Baudel2020]].

Blood clots: an open question

A 2005 consensus group recommended heparin and graduated stockings for bed-bound adults in the acute phase, resting on observational data and expert opinion, not a trial [[Hughes2005]]. In 73 UK patients, 68% were anticoagulated (mean 72 days) and clinical DVT still occurred in 5 (7%), 3 of them on enoxaparin [[Gaber2002]].

In a Russian intensive care cohort screened with ultrasound, DVT was found in 52% and pulmonary embolism in 15% despite low-molecular-weight heparin [[Ryabinkina2016]], while Mayo found pulmonary embolism very uncommon [[Henderson2003]]. Screening finds more than waiting for symptoms, which may explain part of the gap. The 2023 EAN/PNS guideline makes no statement on clot prevention [[vanDoorn2023]].

Pain: common, real and under-treated

Before weakness36%

had pain in the 2 weeks before weakness began, in 156 Dutch people [[Ruts2010Pain]].

Acute phase66%

mostly moderate to severe, in every variant including Miller Fisher syndrome [[Ruts2010Pain]].

One year on38%

still had pain [[Ruts2010Pain]]; it can last over 10 years [[Leonhard2019]].

Counts depend on the method: 89.1% of 55 Canadians had pain during the illness and 74.5% needed opioids [[Moulin1997]], while a retrospective Chinese series found only 34.5% [[Yao2018]]. Pain can be tingling and burning (paraesthesia, dysaesthesia), deep back and nerve-root pain, neck stiffness, muscle or joint pain, or discomfort in the gut [[Pentland1994]]. Deep back and leg pain usually settled by 8 weeks, but burning limb pain persisted longer in 5 to 10% [[Moulin1997]]. Pain can come before weakness and delay the diagnosis, especially in young children [[Leonhard2019]]. Skin biopsies showed loss of small nerve fibres in about 60%, linked to how strong the pain was, even in pure motor forms [[Ruts2012]].

Pain treatment: small trials

The Cochrane review found 3 short randomised trials with 277 people; none reported its primary outcome, and the evidence is insufficient to support any single drug [[Liu2015]]. The largest effect came from a three-arm trial in 36 people in intensive care. Scrub or play through the week.

Median pain scores (0 to 10) in Pandey 2005, n=36 [[Pandey2005]]. Day-1 fentanyl use was about 340, 348 and 590 µg in the three arms [[Pandey2005]].

Doses and recommendations are quoted as published, never as instructions. Pain at admission did not predict outcome [[Moulin1997]], and there are no randomised trials for pain in the recovery phase [[Liu2015]].

Mind, sleep and communication

Vivid dreams and hallucinations

Mental status changes in 31% of 139 people with GBS vs 16% of intensive care controls: vivid dreams, illusions, mainly visual hallucinations, paranoid ideas. Median onset day 9, lasting a median 8 days; linked to autonomic dysfunction, ventilation and high CSF protein [[Cochen2005]].

Early evidence

A sleep mechanism?

The same authors link these experiences to a REM sleep disorder rather than ordinary intensive care delirium [[Cochen2005]].

Distress

Among 49 very severely affected people in intensive care: anxiety 82%, depressive episodes 67%, brief reactive psychosis 25% [[Weiss2002]]. Top fears at admission were staying paralysed, uncertainty and intubation [[Sharshar2012]].

Locked in, but present

One person, fully conscious but unable to communicate for 31 days, described standard low-volume ventilation as profound breathlessness and physiotherapy as very painful with no change in heart rate or blood pressure. One account, but it shows monitors can miss pain [[Kumta2022]].

What helpedPeople said losing the ability to communicate was the hardest part, and 90% found contact with relatives most helpful [[Weiss2002]]. The guideline advises asking directly about pain, hallucinations, anxiety and depression, explaining procedures, and giving accurate information about the relatively good chance of recovery and the low risk of recurrence (2 to 5%) [[Leonhard2019]]. A small 2026 interview study named communication barriers among four themes of lived experience [[Susanti2026]].

In ventilated people at Mayo, only age (OR 1.99) and transfer to a specialist centre delayed by more than 2 days (OR 19.8) independently predicted a poor outcome [[Fletcher2000]]. Respiratory muscle strength is reported to recover in all patients eventually, though ventilation is tied to residual disability [[Orlikowski2004]]. Two years after onset, 7% of a Swedish cohort still could not walk 10 m independently [[Forsberg2004Impairment]].

Exam hookDelayed intubation (over 2 days) independently predicted early pneumonia [[Orlikowski2006]]. Cannot lift arms at 1 week: 87% chance of ventilation for 14 days or more [[Walgaard2017]]. Early tracheostomy did not lower mortality in a national cohort [[Yonezawa2020]]. Pain: about two-thirds acutely, over a third at 1 year [[Ruts2010Pain]]. Methylprednisolone does not prevent GBS pain [[Liu2015]]. Fully paralysed people usually see and hear normally [[Leonhard2019]].
In plain wordsPain is common and real, even when weakness is the main problem; please say if you hurt [[Ruts2010Pain]]. Vivid dreams or seeing things that are not there can happen in GBS. They usually pass, and they do not mean you are losing your mind [[Cochen2005]]. Someone who cannot move or speak can usually still hear and understand: talk to them, explain things and say who is there [[Leonhard2019]]. Many people who needed a ventilator walk again, though it can take a long time. These are group figures, not a forecast for one person [[Fletcher2000,vanDenBerg2018]].
