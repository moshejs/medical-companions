Part 2 · Diagnosis

Lumbar puncture & CSF

What the spinal fluid shows in Guillain-Barré syndrome: protein that rises over days while white cells stay low. See why an early sample can be normal, read a sample against fixed and age-adjusted limits, and see what antibody tests add.

A lumbar puncture (spinal tap) takes a small sample of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) from the lower back. The classic finding in GBS is a raised protein with a normal cell count, called albuminocytological (or albuminocytologic) dissociation [[Leonhard2019]]. It is the only consistent CSF biomarker that supports the diagnosis among many studied Established [[Illes2017]]. The two main guidance papers differ on when to do it: the 2023 EAN/PNS guideline advises CSF examination particularly when the diagnosis is uncertain or another cause must be excluded, while the 2019 consensus advises it in the initial evaluation, mainly to rule out other causes [[vanDoorn2023,Leonhard2019]].

The guideline's wording is "usually <5 cells/μL, rarely 5–50 cells/μL and very rarely >50 cells/μL" [[vanDoorn2023]]. Marked pleocytosis above 50/µl suggests leptomeningeal malignancy or infectious or inflammatory disease of the cord or roots; mild pleocytosis of 10–50/µl is compatible with GBS but should still prompt consideration of infectious polyradiculitis [[Leonhard2019]]. In one series of GBS mimics, a person with 79 white cells/µl had lymphoma cells in the CSF, which is why cytology matters when the count is raised [[Tham2018]].

Exam hookCSF over 50 cells/µl should send you looking for another diagnosis [[vanDoorn2023,Leonhard2019]]. A normal protein in week 1 does not rule GBS out: about 50% are raised within 3 days and 84% after 7 days [[Doets2018]].

Read a sample: fixed and age-adjusted limits

Most laboratories flag protein above a fixed 0.45 g/l; over 85% of surveyed clinical centres use that limit [[Bourque2019]]. A systematic review found a weighted upper reference limit of 0.55 g/l in high-quality studies, above 0.60 g/l after age 50 [[Breiner2019JNeurol]]. The trade-off: early sensitivity against specificity

A higher limit flags fewer normal people but also misses more GBS early on. In Ottawa (138 people at Brighton level 1 or 2), the share of GBS caught by each limit depended on timing [[Bourque2019]]. Pick a limit and a time window; each dot is one person with GBS in 100.

Fewer positives with age adjustment

In 110 people with GBS, the yield fell from 66% at the fixed 0.45 g/l limit to 49% with age-adjusted limits. The CSF/serum albumin quotient tracked total protein closely (r = 0.95) with similar yield, so it added little [[Rath2021]].

Raised protein has many causes

Among 2,627 samples with raised protein and white cells of 5×10⁹/l or fewer, a diagnosis explaining it was found in 57%: 75% when protein was above age-adjusted norms against 51% below them [[Brooks2019]].

After IVIg, both CSF protein and cell count may be artefactually raised, which matters when the puncture follows treatment [[vanDoorn2023]].

Antibody blood tests

Anti-ganglioside antibodies are measured in serum. Their value is limited and depends on the assay: a positive result can help when the diagnosis is in doubt, a negative one does not exclude GBS, and published guidance is that treatment should not wait for the results [[Leonhard2019]].

As published, the guideline does not advise routine anti-ganglioside testing in most motor-sensory GBS or testing to subtype AIDP, AMAN or AMSAN; it suggests testing may help in some suspected motor GBS, variants or other uncertainty, and advises anti-GQ1b testing in suspected Miller Fisher syndrome [[vanDoorn2023]]. How these antibodies arise is in Molecular mimicry.

Neurofilament light chain: a research marker

Neurofilament light chain (NfL) can be measured in serum and CSF. In GBS it is a research marker, studied mainly for what it says about outcome [[vanTilburg2024,Thomma2025]].

Early evidence

Higher in GBS

Median serum NfL 85.5 pg/ml in 27 people with GBS at admission against 9.1 pg/ml in 22 controls [[Altmann2020]]. In Spanish IGOS patients, 55.49 against 9.83 pg/ml, and 190.55 pg/ml in AMAN against 46.79 pg/ml in other variants [[MartinAguilar2020]].

Early evidence

Linked to walking later

In 281 trial patients, high levels at week 2 went with inability to walk unaided at week 4 (OR 1.74), and high levels at week 4 with inability at week 26 (OR 2.79) [[vanTilburg2024]]. In 293, NfL improved prediction models for walking (C-statistic 0.88 for one model at 26 weeks) but was not associated with respiratory failure [[Thomma2025]].

Early evidence

Unvalidated cut-offs

One study proposed baseline cut-offs: over 319 pg/ml for inability to walk independently and over 248 pg/ml for inability to run at 1 year, and under 34 pg/ml for ability to run. These are research values [[MartinAguilar2020]].

Hypothesis

A diagnostic index?

An NfL index separated 96 Swedish patients from healthy controls, multiple sclerosis and ALS with an AUC of 0.86; it has not been tested against true mimics [[Hafsteinsdottir2024]]. Low CSF/serum ratios in axonal or mixed cases (mean 4.4 against 26.7 in controls) suggest a peripheral origin [[Kortvelyessy2020]].

Imaging, when the picture is atypical

As published, routine MRI or nerve ultrasound is not advised for typical GBS; they may be considered in atypical cases, and whole-spine MRI with contrast can help exclude compression, transverse myelitis or tumour [[vanDoorn2023]]. In small uncontrolled studies, nerve-root MRI had a sensitivity of 82–95% with unknown specificity, and ultrasound a sensitivity of 47–95% and specificity of 36–91%, with no objective cut-offs [[vanDoorn2023]]. Ultrasound showed nerve enlargement on days 1–3 in 18 patients against 21 controls Early evidence [[Grimm2014]].

In plain wordsThe spinal fluid test looks for high protein with few cells. In the first days it can be normal, and the person can still have GBS. Antibody blood tests can help in some cases, but treatment does not wait for them.
