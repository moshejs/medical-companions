Part 1 · Understanding GBS

What GBS is

Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS) is an attack by the immune system on the nerves outside the brain and spinal cord, usually after an infection. Weakness climbs over days, reaches its worst point within four weeks, and then slowly improves. [[vanDoorn2023,Leonhard2019]] Press play to follow one person's nerves through it, then explore how common it is and how outcomes differ around the world.

EmergencyWeakness that is getting worse quickly, or any trouble breathing or swallowing, needs emergency care. About 1 person in 5 with GBS develops breathing failure and needs a ventilator for a time. [[Leonhard2019,Doets2018]]

An attack on the peripheral nerves

The 2023 guideline of the European Academy of Neurology and the Peripheral Nerve Society (EAN/PNS) calls GBS "an acute polyradiculoneuropathy": many nerves and nerve roots go wrong at once, and quickly. [[vanDoorn2023]] It is rare, but it is the most common cause of sudden floppy (flaccid) paralysis worldwide. [[Shahrizaila2021]] Surveillance for floppy paralysis was built to find polio, and in children in China and Bangladesh it now finds GBS most often. [[Papri2021]] Step through the six stages below.

Exam hookGBS is an acute, usually monophasic, immune-mediated polyradiculoneuropathy, and the commonest cause of acute flaccid paralysis worldwide. [[vanDoorn2023,Shahrizaila2021]] Maximum weakness within 24 hours, or progression beyond 4 weeks, should prompt a search for another diagnosis. [[Leonhard2019]]
In plain wordsGBS happens when the immune system attacks the nerves outside the brain and spinal cord, usually after an infection. It often starts as weakness or tingling in the legs that moves upward over days. Most people get much better, though it can take months.

How common is GBS?

In Western countries, a pooled analysis of 16 studies found a median of 1.11 new cases per 100,000 person-years (range 0.81 to 1.89). Incidence rose about 20% with every 10 years of age, and men were affected 1.78 times as often as women. [[Sejvar2011]] A 2024 global meta-analysis of 122 articles gave 1.12 per 100,000 person-years. [[Xu2024]] Over a whole life, the risk is about 1 in 1,000 (0.1%). [[Leonhard2019]] Move the age slider to read the published model, or switch to compare countries.

Seasons follow the infections that come first. The winter excess was stronger after a respiratory illness (rate ratio 3.06) than after diarrhoea (1.10). [[Webb2015]] Northern China and Bangladesh see summer rises, thought to reflect more diarrhoeal illness when heat and humidity favour bacterial growth. [[Papri2021]] When UK lockdown cut the spread of infections in spring 2020, GBS cases treated with immunoglobulin fell: 56 in May 2020 against a mean of 113 in the same month of 2016 to 2019. [[Keddie2021]] In New Zealand, three years after rules cut Campylobacter on fresh chicken, notified campylobacteriosis fell 52% and GBS hospital admissions 13%. [[Baker2012NZ]]

Outcomes differ around the world

The International GBS Outcome Study (IGOS) follows people of any age and severity from 143 centres in 19 countries. [[Jacobs2017]] Its first 925 analysed participants were split into three regions. [[Doets2018]] Choose a region to bring it forward; the other two stay visible for comparison.

Percentages of each region's participants in the first IGOS analysis, rounded as published. IGOS is not population-based, so these are not national rates. Group figures; they do not predict any one person's course. [[Doets2018]]

Why is the outlook worse in Bangladesh? Younger age, axonal disease, severe weakness at the worst point, 86% without immunotherapy and scarce ventilators all travel together, and observational data cannot separate their effects. [[Doets2018,Ishaque2017]] In a Dhaka hospital cohort, 12% died; having no ventilator when one was needed was the strongest risk factor (hazard ratio 11.9). [[Ishaque2017]] Immunoglobulin there costs about US$12,000 to 16,000 and plasma exchange about US$4,500 to 5,000, so only 10 to 12% receive either. [[Papri2021]]

A note on these numbersFigures like these describe large groups. They cannot tell you what will happen to you or your family member. Most people, even those who were fully paralysed or on a ventilator for a long time, recover a great deal, especially in the first year. [[Leonhard2019]]

Outbreaks

GBS usually appears one person at a time, but clusters follow epidemics of the infections that trigger it. [[Papri2021]]

Key numbers

New cases1.11

per 100,000 person-years, the Western median (range 0.81 to 1.89). About 100,000 people develop GBS each year worldwide. [[Sejvar2011,Willison2016]]

Lifetime risk0.1%

About 1 in 1,000 people over a lifetime. Getting GBS a second time happens to 2 to 5 in 100. [[Leonhard2019]]

Men to women1.78

The male to female rate ratio in the Western model; about 1.5 to 1 across most reports, including lower-income countries. [[Sejvar2011,Papri2021]]

Breathing support~20%

About 1 in 5 develop breathing failure and need a ventilator; 19% in IGOS. [[Leonhard2019,Doets2018]]

Walking at 6 months~80%

About 8 in 10 regain independent walking by 6 months; about 20% worldwide cannot walk unaided then. [[Leonhard2019,Papri2021]]

Deaths3–10%

Even with the best medical care; up to two-thirds of deaths happen in the recovery phase, so monitoring continues after improvement starts. [[Leonhard2019,vanDenBerg2013]]

Lasting effects60–80%

report fatigue not explained by remaining weakness, and severe pain is reported by at least one-third at 1 year. [[Leonhard2019]]

The burden$1.7 bn

Estimated yearly cost of GBS in the United States in 2004, 86% of it indirect, mostly from early deaths. [[Frenzen2008]]

Is GBS becoming more common?

This is unsettled. One systematic review found a fall between the 1980s and the 1990s, [[McGrogan2009]] another review calls Europe and North America stable across 1980 to 2000, [[Papri2021]] while the 2024 meta-analysis found a significant rise in Europe, Australia and America from 1960 to 2020. [[Xu2024]] The Global Burden of Disease study models a 6.4% rise in prevalence since 1990, from hospital and claims data. [[Bragazzi2021]] Case definitions and data sources differ, so the studies are not measuring quite the same thing.

Exam hookAbout 20% need ventilation, about 20% cannot walk unaided at 6 months, and 3 to 10% die. [[Leonhard2019,vanDoorn2008]] In IGOS, 36% in Bangladesh had the axonal subtype, against 6% in Europe/Americas and other Asian countries. [[Doets2018]]
In plain wordsGBS is rare: about 1 or 2 people in every 100,000 each year. Men get it somewhat more often than women, and the risk rises with age, but children can get it too. Where a person lives changes the outlook, mostly because treatment and intensive care cost money that many hospitals and families do not have.
