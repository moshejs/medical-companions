Part 5 · Special situations & care

Children & pregnancy

GBS in children often starts with pain or a child who will not walk, and most children recover faster than adults. Follow one child's course day by day, see why young children are missed, compare cohorts, and look at what is known about pregnancy and the weeks after birth.

EmergencyIn a child or an adult, weakness that gets worse quickly, or trouble breathing or swallowing, is an emergency and needs urgent care [[Leonhard2019]]. Not all people heading for breathing failure feel breathless, which is why breathing is measured routinely in hospital [[Leonhard2019]].

Why young children are missed

A preschool child may refuse to walk, limp, cry with leg pain or seem irritable. In Rotterdam, first diagnoses included myopathy, tonsillitis, meningitis, rheumatoid disorders, coxitis (hip inflammation) and discitis [[Roodbol2011]]. Across low- and middle-income settings, GBS is correctly diagnosed at admission in only about one-third of affected preschool children [[Papri2021]]. Choose an age group.

MRI of the nerve roots

Enhancement of the spinal nerve roots on MRI is not specific, but it is sensitive and can support the diagnosis, especially in young children, in whom examination and nerve conduction studies can be difficult [[Leonhard2019]].

Acute flaccid paralysis surveillance

AFP surveillance was built for polio eradication and covers children under 15; GBS is now its predominant cause in studies from China and Bangladesh [[Papri2021]]. In Spain, 76.9% of 655 notified cases (1998 to 2015) were clinically GBS and no wild poliovirus was found [[MasaCalles2018]]; in a South African children's hospital, 99 of 119 children with AFP had GBS [[vanderPijl2018]].

Who is likely to need breathing support?

Early evidence

EGRIS-Kids

Roodbol and colleagues found the adult EGRIS inaccurate in children and built EGRIS-Kids from age, cranial nerve involvement and GBS disability score at admission: a 9-point score with predicted risk of respiratory failure from 4 to 50% (AUC 0.71) [[Roodbol2023]].

Early evidence

The adult EGRIS in children

A Chinese cohort reported the adult EGRIS as helpful: median 6 in ventilated against 3 in non-ventilated children (p < 0.001), with 5 or more called high risk [[Qinrong2023]]. Both studies are retrospective, and EGRIS-Kids has not yet been externally validated.

Signs linked to ventilation

  • Facial or bulbar weakness (OR 7.936) and axonal type (OR 4.582), in 103 Chinese children [[Luo2020]]
  • Autonomic dysfunction: 39.1% of ventilated vs 8.8% of non-ventilated children (OR 70.415, 95% CI 9.265 to 535.158), unlike in adults [[Wu2016]]
  • Peripheral facial paralysis and dysautonomia in French children [[Estrade2019]]
  • Bulbar, autonomic and upper limb weakness in South Africa [[vanderPijl2018]]

Autonomic problems

51% in the German study [[Korinthenberg2007]] and 46% of a US series, including blood pressure and pulse changes, bowel or bladder control and priapism; autonomic dysfunction, cranial nerve involvement and albuminocytological dissociation in the spinal fluid were each linked to a shorter time to the worst point [[Hicks2010]]. In critically ill Indian children, autonomic instability, especially high blood pressure, was more frequent with AIDP [[Bansal2026]].

Plasma exchange in children: the risks

In 36 children (122 sessions), fast heart rate, low blood pressure and mild anaemia were most common, and three had serious complications [[Ali2024]]. In a French mixed cohort of 23 children, one child died from venous air embolism, a rare but real procedural risk [[Fleurance2025]].

Two open questions. Two-day or five-day IVIg? The 2-day regimen brought more early deterioration in one underpowered trial [[Korinthenberg2005]], yet some paediatric centres still use it [[Leonhard2019]]. IVIg or plasma exchange when a child is ventilated? Plasma exchange shortened ventilation by 2 days in one small trial [[ElBayoumi2011]], while guidelines favour IVIg for availability and comfort [[Leonhard2019,Korinthenberg2020Guideline]]. Both are unresolved.

Recovery and the years after

Back to normal function

In 80 children with severe GBS treated in a paediatric ICU and seen a median 3.0 years later, 95% had a favourable neurological outcome, 97.5% attended school and 57.7% had satisfactory school performance; quadriparesis at admission, ventilation, tracheostomy and longer stays predicted a worse outcome [[Devi2023]].

Mild weakness can stay

Of 47 children followed at least 2 years, 23% had mild persisting weakness in at least one muscle group, all with perfect functional independence scores; young age and rapid progression predicted it [[Vajsar2003]]. In 110 French children, 77% were cured and 9% had sequelae, linked to an axonal form and a short time to admission [[Estrade2019]].

What children still notice

67% (95% CI 53 to 78) of 51 French children had sequelae at a median 6 years 4 months, mostly minor: tingling 43%, pain 35%, fatigue 31%, symptoms of depression 34% [[Estublier2024]]. In 37 people with childhood GBS (median 11 years), 65% had residual complaints, 26% a negative effect on their school career, and 2 a recurrence [[Roodbol2014Kids]].

Scores that predict

In 142 Chinese children, mEGOS and EGOS were judged valid predictors of 6-month outcome for those admitted within 2 weeks Early evidence [[Qinrong2023]]. How long problems last is debated: function is usually normal [[Vajsar2003,Devi2023]], yet two-thirds report sequelae years later [[Estublier2024,Roodbol2014Kids]]. Both may be true; the measures differ.

For familiesMost children recover well, often faster than adults, but tiredness, tingling or low mood can linger and can affect school. Those are real effects of the illness, and they are worth raising with the care team and the school [[Estublier2024,Roodbol2014Kids]].

How dangerous is GBS in pregnancy? Case-report pools give 45% ventilated [[Yakubu2026]], a French series 25% [[Krief2023]], and Indian hospital series 11.1% [[Patra2022]] or 2 of 47 [[Sharma2015]]. Reporting bias probably inflates the first figure, and there is no prospective cohort. Compared with 18 non-pregnant women with GBS, the 16 French pregnant women more often had CMV infection (31% vs 11%), ICU admission (56% vs 33%) and treatment-related fluctuations (37% vs 0%) [[Krief2023]].

Treatment in pregnancy

Neither IVIg nor plasma exchange is contraindicated in pregnancy; as plasma exchange needs additional considerations and monitoring, IVIg might be preferred [[Leonhard2019]]. An obstetric review says treatment should follow the same principles as outside pregnancy, with clot prevention, physiotherapy, pressure care, tube feeding where needed, respiratory support and close monitoring [[Pacheco2016]]. The EAN/PNS guideline makes no pregnancy-specific treatment recommendation [[vanDoorn2023]]. Delivery and anaesthesia are covered in Nursing care.

GBS in a newborn

A baby born to a mother with ongoing GBS developed GBS 12 days after birth; serum from both blocked nerve-muscle transmission in animal preparations, and the blocking stopped after both recovered [[Buchwald1999]]. The authors propose that the mother's antibodies cross the placenta and target the mature, not the fetal, nerve-muscle junction, which could explain the delay Hypothesis [[Buchwald1999]]. A single case of congenital GBS in a preterm infant improved within 48 hours of IVIg [[Bamford2002]].

Exam hookUnder-6s present with pain, refusal to walk or irritability; 68% of preschool children were first misdiagnosed [[Leonhard2019,Roodbol2011]]. Autonomic dysfunction strongly predicts ventilation in children (OR 70.4 in one Chinese cohort) [[Wu2016]]. GBS risk is not raised in pregnancy but rises after delivery (rate ratio 2.93 in the first 30 days) [[Cheng1998]]; in pregnancy neither IVIg nor plasma exchange is contraindicated, and IVIg might be preferred [[Leonhard2019]].
In plain wordsChildren can get GBS too, but it is rare: roughly 1 child in 100,000 each year [[McGrogan2009,Ryan2005]]. Most children recover well, and usually faster than adults [[Korinthenberg2007]]. GBS in pregnancy is uncommon; the risk seems highest in the first weeks after the baby is born. Treatment can be given during pregnancy. Most babies do well, but early birth is more common [[Cheng1998,Leonhard2019,Yakubu2026]].
