Part 1 · Understanding GBS

Miller Fisher syndrome and other variants

Not every Guillain-Barré syndrome starts in the legs. Miller Fisher syndrome takes the eyes, balance and reflexes; other variants stay in the throat and arms, the face or the legs. Follow one course of Miller Fisher syndrome, then see how often it spreads in the first week.

Miller Fisher syndrome (MFS) is defined by ophthalmoplegia (weak eye movements), ataxia (unsteadiness) and areflexia (absent reflexes) [[Mori2001MFS]]. It is estimated to make up 5–25% of the GBS spectrum, and overlaps with classic sensorimotor GBS in an estimated 15% [[Leonhard2019]]. How common it is depends on where you look: MFS and MFS-GBS overlap together were 22% of IGOS inclusions in Asia, 11% in Europe and the Americas and 1% in Bangladesh [[Doets2018]].

EmergencyWeakness that spreads to the arms or legs, or new trouble swallowing or breathing, needs emergency care. In Miller Fisher syndrome, every overlap with wider weakness began within the first 7 days [[Sekiguchi2016,Verboon2017MFS]], and overlap needed a ventilator in 24% of one series [[Funakoshi2009]].

The first week: how often MFS spreads

In 60 consecutive people who began with the triad alone, half stayed pure and half went on to an overlap syndrome [[Sekiguchi2016]]. Pick a group to see what spread, and when.

43%

of 23 people with MFS in a Dutch cohort developed limb weakness, all in the first week; no early predictor was found, so the authors advise careful monitoring of everyone with MFS for at least a week [[Verboon2017MFS]].

24%

of people with Fisher/GBS overlap needed ventilation, against 10% with GBS and none with Fisher syndrome alone. Titubation (head or trunk tremor) as a first symptom and descending tetraparesis marked those ventilated [[Funakoshi2009]].

Timing changes the result: titres peak at presentation and can be undetectable as early as about one month after onset [[Overell2007]]. The 2023 EAN/PNS guideline advises anti-GQ1b testing when MFS is suspected (good practice point) [[vanDoorn2023]]. On nerve tests, 25 of 27 people with MFS in IGOS had a normal or equivocal first study [[Arends2026]]; the soleus H-reflex is typically absent [[Kuwabara2017]].

Fisher syndrome and Bickerstaff: one spectrum?

Bickerstaff brainstem encephalitis was defined in 62 people by progressive, fairly symmetrical ophthalmoplegia and ataxia within 4 weeks, plus disturbed consciousness or brisk reflexes [[Odaka2003]]. An autopsy showed inflammation with oedema and glial nodules in the brainstem [[Odaka2003]]. A Japanese series of 581 people compared the two directly [[Ito2008]]:

One spectrum

The Japanese authors conclude Fisher syndrome and Bickerstaff form one continuous spectrum [[Ito2008]]. A review groups them with acute ophthalmoparesis and acute ataxic neuropathy at the limited end, and pharyngeal-cervical-brachial weakness and GBS overlap at the extensive end, as "anti-GQ1b antibody syndrome" [[Shahrizaila2013JNNP]].

Or outside GBS criteria

Bickerstaff makes up an estimated under 5% of the spectrum and does not meet commonly used GBS criteria [[Leonhard2019]]. In the 62-person series, 92% had a preceding illness, 74% disturbed consciousness, 60% flaccid tetraparesis and 66% anti-GQ1b IgG [[Odaka2003]].

Treatment and recovery

No controlled trials

A Cochrane review found no randomised or non-randomised prospective controlled trials of immunotherapy in Fisher syndrome or related disorders [[Overell2007]].

Treated or not, a similar course

In a Mori cohort of 92, median days until ophthalmoplegia and ataxia disappeared were 82 and 39 with IVIg, 92 and 21 with plasma exchange and 76 and 31 with supportive care, with no statistically significant difference. Symptom-free at 6 months: 89%, 96% and 95% [[Overell2007]].

What reviews say

Because most people with pure MFS recover completely without treatment within 6 months, a 2019 consensus review says treatment is generally not recommended, with close monitoring advised [[Leonhard2019]]. For Bickerstaff, the severity of the course is described as justifying IVIg or plasma exchange, although evidence of benefit is limited [[Leonhard2019]].

Other variants

A 2014 classification splits GBS into classic and localised forms, and MFS into incomplete forms (acute ophthalmoparesis, acute ataxic neuropathy) and a central nervous system form (Bickerstaff) [[Wakerley2014]]. Debated

Inside or outside the spectrum?

Bilateral facial palsy with paraesthesias, pure sensory GBS and Bickerstaff do not meet commonly used diagnostic criteria but are often included in the spectrum, and this inclusion is debated [[Leonhard2019]]. Acute facial diplegia with brisk reflexes after C. jejuni shows that hyperreflexia does not rule out a variant [[Susuki2004]].

Counted with the 2014 criteria

In 103 Japanese people: classic GBS 71%, classic MFS 17%, Bickerstaff 3%, pharyngeal-cervical-brachial 2% and 1–2% each for the other variants [[Wakerley2016]]. In 61 Malaysian people: classic GBS 67%, classic MFS 10%, paraparetic 7%, pharyngeal-cervical-brachial 5%, bifacial 5%, overlap 5% [[Hiew2017Fruste]].

Look-alikes run both ways: MFS and the pharyngeal-cervical-brachial variant are often mistaken for brainstem stroke, botulism or myasthenia gravis, and Bickerstaff for Wernicke's encephalopathy [[Wakerley2015]]. See Look-alikes.

In plain wordsMiller Fisher syndrome affects the eyes, balance and reflexes. Most people recover fully, usually within 6 months. It can spread to the arms, legs, swallowing or breathing in the first week, which is why doctors watch closely at first. Blood tests for antibodies help in this form. The numbers here describe groups of people. They cannot tell you exactly what will happen to you.
