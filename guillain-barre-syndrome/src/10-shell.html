Guillain-Barré syndrome, explained

In Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS) the immune system attacks the peripheral nerves, usually a few weeks after an infection, and weakness climbs from the legs over days [[Leonhard2019,Shahrizaila2021]]. It is rare, about 1.1 new cases per 100,000 people a year [[Xu2024]]. About 1 in 5 people need help to breathe for a time, yet most recover substantially: in the international IGOS cohort about 8 in 10 in Europe and the Americas walked unaided at 6 months [[Leonhard2019,Doets2018]].

For students

Every module has an Exam hook box and a citation trail. The Brighton criteria, EGRIS, EGOS and mEGOS tools are teaching aids, scored as published. Finish with flashcards, memory aids and a quiz.

For people with GBS, families and carers

Look for the In plain words boxes. Recovery differs from person to person and can take months or years: outcome figures are population statistics, not predictions for one person. Bring questions to your own care team: the guide reports what guidelines, trials and labels say, not what you should take, stop or change.
