Nursing care

A 24-hour ward shift in myasthenia gravis: pyridostigmine timed to meals, breath counts and swallow checks, and when to escalate. Press play, or switch the scenario.

ScopeThis page reports what published studies, drug labels, NHS safety documents and patient charities say. It is not a care plan, and it does not replace local protocols, your scope of practice or the prescriber. Doses and schedules are set by the treating clinician. Where the literature has no answer, the page says so.
EmergencyNew or worsening difficulty breathing, swallowing or speaking in a person with MG is an emergency that needs urgent medical help. The pyridostigmine teaching literature tells patients to contact the prescriber urgently for exactly these signs, and the MGFA emergency alert card warns first responders that breathing or swallowing difficulty may signal a crisis. 353,410

07:00 · Admission

Baseline strength and breathing

Myaware's guidance for nurses asks for a baseline respiratory measurement on admission, forced vital capacity (FVC) at least four times a day when the breathing muscles are involved, and a recheck after any change. The single breath count is the bedside companion: a deep breath, then counting aloud at about 2 counts a second, seated, best of 2 attempts. In 31 people with AChR-antibody MG it correlated with FVC (r = 0.554), inspiratory force (r = 0.530) and neck flexor strength (r = 0.519). 113,376

In plain wordsAt the start of the shift the nurse writes down how strong the breathing and swallowing are, so that any change later is easy to see.
Exam hookThe MG Core Exam scores the breath count 0 (30 or more), 1 (25 to 29), 2 (20 to 24) or 3 (under 20). Ptosis is scored after 60 seconds of upgaze. 214

High-dose steroids went with less crisis (RR 0.41). The baseline is what every later check is compared against. 192,376

2 · Giving the medicines

Most of what goes wrong with pyridostigmine in hospital is about the clock. Drag how late the 07:30 dose is and watch which rule applies and where the next dose falls. The rules come from NHS Scotland's pyridostigmine Q&A and a King's Health Partners safety bulletin; doses themselves are set by the treating clinician. 292,453

Rules as published by NHS Scotland and King's Health Partners. The effect curve is schematic. No study has measured outcomes of late doses in hospital. 292,453

45 minutes late

Up to 1 hour late: give it, keep the schedule

This is outside the 30-minute standard. NHS Scotland's Q&A says a dose missed by up to 1 hour is taken and the usual schedule kept. Breakfast may now come before the dose has started to work, which is what the 30 to 60 minute lead time is meant to avoid. 292,453

Where the evidence stopsNo published study has measured the clinical outcomes of late or missed pyridostigmine doses in hospital. The 30-minute standard rests on NHS safety documents and charity guidance, not on outcome data. 292,376
Exam hookUp to 1 hour late: give it and keep the schedule. Over 1 hour: give it now and push the next dose back 3 to 4 hours. Never double. 453
In plain wordsIf a tablet is late, take it when you remember. If it is very late, the next one moves later too. Never take two at once.
Timing

Timed to meals

  • Usually starts working in 30 to 60 minutes, so doses are taken about 30 to 60 minutes before meals, the first on rising.
  • Usual duration 3 to 4 hours by day and about 6 hours for a bedtime dose.
  • Sources differ: Myaware's nurse page says about 20 minutes before meals for a modified diet; the nursing pharmacology text describes timing doses so the peak falls at meals.

376,410,453

Critical medicine

On time means within 30 minutes

  • King's Health Partners lists pyridostigmine as a critical medicine to be given within 30 minutes of the due time, even if that does not match ward rounds.
  • It warns that a missed or late dose can precipitate myasthenic crisis, which can be fatal.
  • NHS Scotland reports a known risk of adverse incidents from repeated missed doses.

292,453

Route

Nil by mouth is not "no tablets"

  • Nasogastric administration is the preferred option when a patient is nil by mouth.
  • The oral solution is licensed for NG and PEG tubes, except latex tubes.
  • Immediate-release tablets can be crushed to a fine powder and mixed with at least 10 mL of water.
  • Myaware: medication should never be withheld because the patient is nil by mouth.

376,453

Neurology advice

No enteral route at all

  • Parenteral neostigmine is a short-term option on neurology advice.
  • Oral pyridostigmine 60 mg is about equivalent to 1 to 1.5 mg of neostigmine IM or SC.
  • Neostigmine's half-life is about 1 to 2 hours, against 3 to 4 hours for pyridostigmine, so doses come closer together.
  • Current US injectable neostigmine labels are indicated only for reversing neuromuscular blockade, not for MG.

152,453

Extended release

Timespan, the extended-release tablet

  • Timespan 180 mg acts at first about like 60 mg of the ordinary tablet and lasts on average 2.5 times as long; doses are at least 6 hours apart, and ordinary tablets or syrup may be needed alongside.
  • The nursing pharmacology text teaches that extended-release forms must not be chewed, broken or crushed.
  • The US label itself does not address crushing; it says only that Timespan tablets are single-scored. A patient on Timespan who becomes nil by mouth needs the prescriber to review the regimen.

150,410

Look at the clock

Too much or too little?

  • Worsening 15 to 60 minutes after a dose suggests cholinergic toxicity; worsening 3 to 4 hours after a dose suggests underdosing.
  • The label recommends atropine for cholinergic crisis and warns it can mask the signs of overdose.
  • Cramps, diarrhoea, salivation and sweating may be countered with propantheline 15 mg taken 15 to 30 minutes before the dose, or loperamide.
  • IV cholinesterase inhibitors may provoke uterine contractions and are not used in pregnancy.

150,453,476

Published ranges

Dose ranges, set by the clinician

  • NHS Scotland: usual start 15 to 30 mg three to four times a day; most people need about 180 mg a day; usual maximum 360 mg a day.
  • The US label gives an average of 600 mg a day (ten 60 mg tablets), up to 25 tablets in severe cases.
  • The two sources disagree on the upper range. The dose is set by the treating clinician; the nursing text warns that raising it because weakness increases risks cholinergic crisis.
  • Caution in the elderly, the frail and in heart disease (an ECG is suggested); lower doses may be needed in renal impairment.

IVIG: what the label asks

  • Boxed warning: thrombosis, renal dysfunction and acute renal failure.
  • At-risk patients: lowest practicable dose and rate, adequate hydration, watch for thrombosis and hyperviscosity.
  • Haemolysis risk higher at 2 g/kg or more and in non-O blood groups.
  • Serious rare effects also include arrhythmia, haemolytic anaemia and TRALI; switching to subcutaneous immunoglobulin is one way reviews describe reducing reactions.

142,215

Plasma exchange

  • Peripheral venous access succeeded in 75% of 230 courses.
  • Central catheters: total complications 68% vs 35%, serious 41% vs 4%, including 2 deaths.
  • In the prospective cohort 55% had no complication at all.
  • Choice between exchange and IVIG depends on the patient: exchange is out in sepsis, IVIG in renal failure.

111,217,476

Corticosteroids

  • May cause transient worsening of weakness at the start; published rates vary widely.
  • The prednisone label warns that anticholinesterases together with corticosteroids can cause severe weakness in MG.
  • Myaware asks nurses to monitor blood glucose; the Nordic guidance lists blood pressure, weight, bone density, glucose, sleep and mood.
  • The 2022 ACR bone guideline (not MG-specific) applies to 2.5 mg a day or more for over 3 months. No MG-specific recommendation on stomach protection was found.

157,200,260,376,476

Children on steroids

  • Blood pressure, growth velocity and weight are monitored.
  • Vitamin D for all children; gastric protection considered.
  • Bone density assessment with long-term use.
  • Families are given a list of medicines that may worsen MG.

400

Immunosuppressants

  • Myaware asks for bloods and a watch for signs of infection in people on immunosuppression.
  • The azathioprine label warns of severe marrow toxicity with low or absent TPMT activity, and says TPMT testing cannot replace blood-count monitoring.
  • Serious infections were more common in an Ontario MG cohort than in comparators (adjusted HR 1.39), most often bacterial pneumonia.

145,283,376

3 · Teaching, and what gets misunderstood

The nursing pharmacology text sets out what to teach about pyridostigmine, and the charities add what people most often get wrong on the ward. Pick a belief to see what the sources say, and a question that brings it out. 376,410

What the sources say

Not automatically

Increasing weakness is not automatically underdosing. Raising the dose risks cholinergic crisis, and both under- and overdosing can stop the drug helping. The clock helps: worsening 15 to 60 minutes after a dose points toward too much, 3 to 4 hours after toward too little. The dose belongs to the prescriber. 150,410,453

Ask: "When did you last take a tablet, and when did the weakness start?"

Teaching points in the nursing text

  • MG treatment is lifelong.
  • The signs of underdosing (ptosis, trouble swallowing) and of cholinergic toxicity.
  • Keep a record of dose times, tired times, and strength before and after doses.
  • Wear a medical alert bracelet.
  • Do not stop pyridostigmine abruptly.
  • Rest between activities.
  • Contact the prescriber urgently for trouble swallowing, speaking or breathing.

410

The emergency alert card

  • The MGFA Emergency Alert Card is a fillable card in English and Spanish, kept in a wallet, a glove box or on the fridge.
  • It warns first responders that MG weakness, drooping eyelids, double vision and breathing or swallowing trouble can be mistaken for intoxication, and may signal a crisis.
  • People on a C5 inhibitor also carry the REMS safety card for months after the last dose.

165,353

Medicines that may worsen MG

  • Myaware notes more than 30 drugs that could in theory impair neuromuscular transmission.
  • It names telithromycin as the one drug that must be avoided (deaths reported), penicillamine as best avoided, and IV magnesium as to be avoided if possible.
  • For many neurology and psychiatry drugs it advises caution rather than a ban: a listed drug may still be used when needed, with the clinician watching closely.

352,375

Speech, swallowing and communication

  • Consider swallow safety and NG tubes; arrange regular speech and language therapy reviews.
  • When speech is affected, avoid long conversations and offer a pad and pen.
  • The patient questionnaire was one of the measures that correlated with aspiration on barium swallow, so asking the person is part of the screen.

300,376

Fatigue, measured

  • Severe fatigue was present in 62% of 420 Dutch registry patients, linked to disease severity, female sex, BMI and depressive symptoms, and inversely to strenuous activity.
  • The MG-ADL is an 8-question patient-reported survey; in 254 encounters it correlated with the QMG (r = 0.583).

468,551

Children and families

  • Families of children with MG should be given a list of medicines that may worsen MG.
  • Ophthalmology input helps prevent amblyopia in young children with ptosis.
  • Steroid monitoring in children covers blood pressure, growth velocity and weight.

400

Where the evidence stops. These gaps were found while writing this page and are left open rather than filled in:

  • No study measures the outcomes of late or missed in-hospital pyridostigmine doses; the 30-minute rule is a safety standard, not a trial result. 292,453
  • The "20/30/40" rule is from Guillain-Barré syndrome; MG thresholds here come from one series of 42 patients, and another small series found serial vital capacity unhelpful. 308,452,511
  • Hypocalcaemia and hypotension during plasma exchange are widely taught, but MG-specific rates were not verified. 111
  • The US Mestinon label does not address crushing Timespan; the "do not crush" teaching comes from the nursing text. 150,410
  • "Saturation is a late sign" has no MG-specific number behind it; the 54% figure is from chronic neuromuscular disease. 208
  • Sources disagree on the dose lead time before meals (20 vs 30 to 60 minutes) and on the upper daily dose (360 vs 600 mg or more). 150,376,453
