# D. Symptomatic & conventional immunotherapy, and the guidance that orders them
Scope: this file covers acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (pyridostigmine, with neostigmine and edrophonium in brief, and amifampridine as a LEMS drug), corticosteroids, the classical steroid-sparing immunosuppressants, rituximab, IVIG and plasma exchange (PLEX), and the guidelines that set their order: the international consensus guidance of 2016 and its 2020 update, the German/DGN guideline (English translation 2023), the Nordic guidance (2024), the ABN 2025 update, Japanese guidance (2014 rationale and 2022 overview) and a 2026 German-Austrian expert paper. It also covers ocular MG. Every number comes from an abstract, a full text on PMC, or a drug label that I opened. Where I could read only part of a source, the fact says so. Not accessible: the full text of the ABN 2025 and ABN 2015 guidelines (BMJ returned 403, so only the 2025 abstract is used); the German AWMF 2025 guideline PDF (blocked by bot protection, so the changes since the 2023 translation are unknown); the Japanese 2022 guideline itself (only an overview abstract, which is a Japanese-language paper with an English abstract); the full text of Pascuzzi 1984 and Seybold & Drachman 1974. Missing or partly missing: (1) I found no citable primary source for the clinical onset time of oral pyridostigmine in minutes. (2) I found no citable MG-specific source for glycopyrrolate or propantheline as treatment for pyridostigmine side effects. The sources I read mention only atropine or "anticholinergic drugs" in general. (3) I found no MG-specific source for GI (PPI) prophylaxis during steroid treatment. (4) The day-5-to-10 timing of steroid-induced worsening could not be confirmed in any source I read. The sources I read give "within 2 weeks" or "within 4 weeks" windows instead. Edrophonium (Tensilon) has no current label in the openFDA/DailyMed search I ran.

## Facts

### Pyridostigmine: mechanism and chemistry
- The US Mestinon label calls pyridostigmine bromide "an orally active cholinesterase inhibitor" and gives its chemical name as 3-hydroxy-1-methylpyridinium bromide dimethylcarbamate. That is a carbamate ester on a quaternary (permanently charged) pyridinium ring. [FDAMestinon2020]
- The label states its action: Mestinon inhibits the destruction of acetylcholine by cholinesterase, which allows freer transmission of nerve impulses across the neuromuscular junction. Compared with neostigmine, it has a longer duration of action and fewer gastrointestinal side effects. [FDAMestinon2020]
- Pyridostigmine, neostigmine and edrophonium are reversible quaternary cholinesterase inhibitors. They are hydrophilic, ionised compounds with low oral bioavailability. [Aquilonius1986]
- The UK SmPC states that pyridostigmine "does not cross the blood-brain barrier at non-toxic doses". [UKSmPCMestinon2025]
- Pyridostigmine is excreted mainly unchanged by the kidney, so the US label says lower doses may be needed in renal disease. The UK SmPC says 5-15% of an oral dose is excreted in urine as the parent compound. [FDAMestinon2020; UKSmPCMestinon2025]

### Pyridostigmine: pharmacokinetics (anchor points for a 0-8 h curve)
- **Oral bioavailability.** The figures differ between sources:
  - 7.6 ± 2.4% after 120 mg orally in healthy volunteers [Aquilonius1980]
  - 11.5-18.9% (mean 14.3%) after 60 mg orally in 10 healthy subjects [BreyerPfaff1985]
  - "approximately 10%" in a 1986 review [Aquilonius1986]
  - absolute oral bioavailability "may decrease to 3.3%" in people with MG, per the UK SmPC [UKSmPCMestinon2025]
- **Time to peak plasma level: 1.7 h fasting, 3.2 h with food.** Food did not change the amount absorbed (bioavailability). [Aquilonius1980]
- **Peak level after a 60 mg dose: 40-60 µg/L, reached 1-2 h after the dose.** [Aquilonius1986]
- **Half-life.** The figures differ, which matters for an animation:
  - elimination half-life 1.52 h after IV and 1.78 ± 0.24 h after oral dosing [Aquilonius1980]
  - mean plasma decline half-life after oral dosing 200 min, twice the 97 min seen after IV infusion. Absorption may therefore be slower than elimination, which flattens the curve. [BreyerPfaff1985]
  - 3-4 h after oral use, per the UK SmPC [UKSmPCMestinon2025]
- **Clinical duration of action: 3-4 h per daytime dose.** A dose taken at bedtime often lasts longer, about 6 h. [UKSmPCMestinon2025]
- In MG patients on maintenance therapy, plasma levels change little within one dosing interval. Pre-dose levels differed up to seven-fold between patients on the same daily dose. [Aquilonius1983; Aquilonius1980]
- **A bell-shaped dose-response curve.** In 6 patients, the deltoid decrement improved most at plasma pyridostigmine levels of 30-60 ng/mL; the optimal neostigmine range was 5-15 ng/mL. A separate study of 18 patients found that levels above 100 ng/mL may impair neuromuscular function. These are anchors for showing too little, optimal and too much drug. [Aquilonius1983; BreyerPfaff1990]
- **Timespan 180 mg extended-release tablet.** Its immediate effect is about equal to a 60 mg conventional tablet. Its duration averages 2.5 times that of a 60 mg dose, though it varies between patients. The label's minimum interval between Timespan doses is 6 h. [FDAMestinon2020]
- Methylcellulose has been reported to block pyridostigmine absorption completely. Severe renal impairment prolongs elimination. [Aquilonius1986]

### Pyridostigmine: label doses and guideline doses
- The US label lists three forms: syrup (oral solution) at 60 mg per 5 mL with 5% alcohol, 60 mg conventional tablets, and 180 mg Timespan tablets. [FDAMestinon2020]
- **US label doses (syrup and 60 mg tablets).** The average is ten 60 mg tablets or ten 5 mL teaspoons a day (600 mg/day), "spaced to provide maximum relief when maximum strength is needed". Severe cases may need up to 25 a day (1,500 mg/day); mild cases may need 1-6 a day. [FDAMestinon2020]
- **US label doses (Timespan).** One to three 180 mg tablets, once or twice daily, at least 6 h apart. Conventional tablets or syrup may be added for optimum control. [FDAMestinon2020]
- The US label says the syrup allows fractional doses for children and "brittle" patients, and is easier to swallow for patients with bulbar involvement. [FDAMestinon2020]
- **UK SmPC doses.** Adults take 30-120 mg per dose, usually 5-20 tablets a day (300-1,200 mg/day). Children under 6 start at 30 mg; total pediatric doses are usually 30-360 mg/day. [UKSmPCMestinon2025]
- **Nordic guidance (2024).** The usual adult dose is 30-60 mg every 4-6 h. Doses of 450-600 mg/day or more may cause more weakness and cholinergic crisis. Children start at 1 mg/kg/day, with a typical dose of 7 mg/kg/day. [Gilhus2024]
- **German guideline.** Pyridostigmine is used up to 720 mg/day; higher oral doses "are tolerated only in exceptional cases". Ambenonium, neostigmine or distigmine are options when pyridostigmine is not tolerated or does not work. [Wiendl2023]
- **Disagreement on the upper dose.** The US label allows up to 1,500 mg/day in severe cases. The German guideline caps routine use at 720 mg/day. The Nordic guidance warns of increased weakness from 450-600 mg/day. [FDAMestinon2020; Wiendl2023; Gilhus2024]
- The international consensus says pyridostigmine should be part of initial treatment in most patients, with the dose adjusted to symptoms. Patients who do not meet treatment goals after an adequate trial should receive corticosteroids or immunosuppressants. [Sanders2016]
- In the Dutch-Belgian registry, 93% of patients (78/84) started treatment with pyridostigmine. At 10 years, 65% (54/83) were still using it. [RemijnNelissen2026c]

### Pyridostigmine: evidence of efficacy
- A 2014 Cochrane review found no RCTs of oral acetylcholinesterase inhibitors in generalized MG; the only trial was a small intranasal neostigmine crossover. It concluded that the observational response is so clear that a placebo-controlled RCT "would be difficult to justify". [Mehndiratta2014]
- IMPACT-MG part 1 (2026) was the first such RCT. In this double-blind crossover, 19 AChR-MG patients already taking pyridostigmine had two 5-day periods separated by a 2-day washout. Pyridostigmine beat placebo on every measure, as mean differences: MG Impairment Index 5.3 points (95% CI 1.9-8.7; p = 0.004), QMG 1.4, MG-ADL 1.2 and MG-QOL15r 2.0. [RemijnNelissen2026]
- **Disagreement.** The 2026 trial ran the RCT that the 2014 Cochrane review called hard to justify. Its benefit (MG-ADL about 1.2 points over 5 days) is modest. [Mehndiratta2014; RemijnNelissen2026]
- In a survey of 410 Dutch-Belgian registry patients, median self-rated effectiveness was 60 (IQR 28-78) and net benefit 65 (IQR 45-84). These are self-ratings, on what appears to be a 0-100 scale; the abstract does not state the scale. [RemijnNelissen2022]

### Pyridostigmine: side effects, cholinergic weakness, MuSK MG
- The US label groups side effects into two kinds:
  - muscarinic: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal cramps, increased peristalsis, increased salivation, increased bronchial secretions, miosis and sweating
  - nicotinic: muscle cramps, fasciculations and weakness
  The label adds that bromide can cause a rash. [FDAMestinon2020]
- **Side-effect rates (survey).** Of current users, 91% reported side effects, compared with 55% of a control group. The most frequent were flatulence, urinary urgency, muscle cramps, blurred vision and excessive sweating. Of those who had stopped pyridostigmine, 26% stopped because of side effects, most often diarrhea, abdominal cramps and muscle twitching. [RemijnNelissen2022]
- **Side-effect rates (clinic cohort).** In 71 Korean MG patients, 9 (13%) could not tolerate oral pyridostigmine and 17 (24%) had side effects. Patients with repetitive CMAPs after a neostigmine test had a lower top daily dose (240 vs 480 mg/day). They were also more often intolerant (37.5% vs 0%) and had more side effects (45.8% vs 12.8%). [Lee2016]
- **Atropine.** The US label says muscarinic effects "can usually be counteracted by atropine". It warns that this can mask signs of overdose and lead to an unnoticed cholinergic crisis. The UK SmPC says large doses may need "atropine or other anticholinergic drugs". The Nordic guidance mentions low-dose atropine for temporary use. [FDAMestinon2020; UKSmPCMestinon2025; Gilhus2024]
- I found no MG-specific citable source for glycopyrrolate or propantheline as treatment for pyridostigmine side effects. None of the guideline texts I could open (Nordic, German, international) name them. Their common use is unverified. [Gilhus2024; Wiendl2023]
- **Cholinergic crisis (US label).** Overdose can cause cholinergic crisis, with increasing weakness that can reach the respiratory muscles and cause death. It may be hard to tell from myasthenic crisis by symptoms alone. The label says cholinergic crisis calls for prompt withdrawal of all such drugs and immediate atropine. [FDAMestinon2020]
- **Cholinergic weakness (other sources).** Cochrane: "Higher than recommended doses of AChEIs desensitise AChRs and increase weakness". The international consensus adds that excess cholinesterase inhibitor "cannot be completely excluded" as a cause of worsening in crisis, and that these drugs increase airway secretions. [Mehndiratta2014; Sanders2016]
- **Contraindications.** The US label contraindicates pyridostigmine in mechanical intestinal or urinary obstruction and urges caution in asthma. The UK SmPC also cautions in obstructive airway disease (asthma, COPD). [FDAMestinon2020; UKSmPCMestinon2025]
- **Meals.** Food delays the peak (tmax 1.7 to 3.2 h) without changing the amount absorbed [Aquilonius1980]. I found no citable guideline statement on timing doses before meals for chewing or swallowing. The US label says only that doses should be spaced for maximum strength when it is needed. [FDAMestinon2020]
- **MuSK MG.** The international consensus says patients with MuSK MG respond poorly to cholinesterase inhibitors and often get side effects at conventional doses. The German guideline agrees: they may respond poorly and more often tolerate it poorly. [Sanders2016; Wiendl2023]
- In 37 Italian MuSK-positive patients, the edrophonium or neostigmine test was equivocal or negative in 11 (29.7%). The response to oral pyridostigmine ranged "from mild benefit to overt intolerance". [Evoli2003]
- A single case report in MuSK MG showed extra repetitive discharges after the CMAP (signs of cholinergic hyperexcitability), possibly triggered by the drug. The authors read this as abnormal sensitivity to acetylcholine. [Punga2006]

### Neostigmine, edrophonium, amifampridine (brief)
- Oral neostigmine has even lower bioavailability than pyridostigmine. A 30 mg oral dose gives plasma levels of only 1-5 µg/L. [Aquilonius1986]
- Current US injectable neostigmine and injectable pyridostigmine (Regonol) labels are indicated only to reverse non-depolarizing neuromuscular blockade after surgery, not to treat MG. [FDANeostigmine2024; FDARegonol2021]
- Edrophonium is a short-acting cholinesterase inhibitor used for the diagnostic "Tensilon test". [Mehndiratta2014]
- In a 1991-2024 Vienna series (182 MG patients, 324 controls), the edrophonium test had 83.5% sensitivity and 87.7% specificity. MuSK-MG patients were less likely to respond (OR 0.08). Adverse events occurred in 11.5%, and 91.4% of these were mild. [Keritam2026]
- In ocular MG, a positive test needed a mean edrophonium dose of 3.3 mg (SD 1.6) for ptosis and 2.6 mg (SD 1.1) for eye-movement weakness. [Kupersmith2003]
- Amifampridine (Firdapse) is a potassium-channel blocker labelled in the US only for Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome (LEMS), in adults and children aged 6 years and older, not for MG. [FDAFirdapse2026]
- **Amifampridine in MG.** IMPACT-MG part 2 was a randomized crossover in 20 AChR-MG patients not well controlled on pyridostigmine. Adding amifampridine 30 or 60 mg was not better than placebo (MGII difference -1.0 and -1.7; p = 0.68 and 0.38). Adverse events occurred in 60% and 75% on the two doses vs 30% on placebo. [RemijnNelissen2026b]

### Corticosteroids: dosing strategies and response
- **Pascuzzi 1984 series (high-dose start).** 116 patients received prednisone 60-80 mg daily until they improved, followed by alternate-day therapy for years. Of these, 80.2% reached remission (27.6%) or marked improvement (52.6%), 14.7% improved moderately and 5.2% did not improve. [Pascuzzi1984]
- The 2005 Cochrane review found only limited RCT evidence of short-term benefit over placebo. Retrospective series report remission or marked improvement in 63.4-81.7% of patients. [SchneiderGold2005]
- **Time to benefit (Cochrane).** Improvement began within a few days and was "usually obvious after two weeks", occasionally only after 4-6 weeks (Sghirlanzoni 1984). After IV methylprednisolone 2 g on two consecutive days, improvement was significant at 2 weeks and lasted a mean of 8 weeks, range 4-14 weeks (Lindberg 1998). [SchneiderGold2005]
- **Time to benefit (guidelines).** The Nordic guidance says about 75% of patients respond within 2-4 weeks. For ocular MG, the German guideline says 0.5-1 mg/kg is often effective after 2-4 weeks. [Gilhus2024; Wiendl2023]
- **Nordic dosing.** Moderate to severe MG: early high-dose prednisolone, 0.75-1.00 mg/kg. Mild to moderate MG: start at 0.25 mg/kg and increase gradually; to avoid early worsening, the dose can rise from 5 to 10 mg daily over a few weeks. When rapid response is needed, 30-50 mg daily is combined with IVIG or PLEX. [Gilhus2024]
- **Low-dose Japanese strategy (2018).** In a cross-sectional study of 590 Japanese patients, a low-dose prednisolone regimen was more likely than a high-dose one to reach the target (minimal manifestations or better on ≤5 mg/day for >6 months). The odds ratios for low vs high dose were 10.4, 2.75 and 1.86 at years 1, 2 and 3. [Imai2018; Imai2020]
- **Japanese strategy (2020 review).** Early fast-acting therapy (OR 2.19 at 2 years) and calcineurin inhibitors (OR 2.09 at 2 years) were also linked to reaching the target. Even so, about 35% of patients had not reached it after 3 years. [Imai2020]
- **Alternate-day vs daily dosing.** The ABN 2025 update now treats daily steroids as standard, replacing the alternate-day regimen of its 2015 guideline. The Nordic guidance says many centers prefer alternate-day dosing but evidence is lacking. This is a real disagreement. [Jacob2025; Gilhus2024]
- **Tapering.** The international consensus says the dose should be tapered gradually once treatment goals are reached. Relapse is more likely in patients who still have symptoms, or after a rapid taper. [Sanders2016]
- **Tapering (Nordic).** Taper over months to the lowest effective dose. [Gilhus2024]
- **Long-term use.** The German guideline advises against long-term steroid monotherapy, notes a Cushing's threshold of about 7.5 mg/day prednisolone, and recommends early steroid-sparing therapy. [Wiendl2023]
- **Japanese target.** The Japanese guidelines set a target of minimal manifestations or better on ≤5 mg/day prednisolone ("MM-or-better-5mg"). [Murai2018; Imai2020]

### Corticosteroids: early transient worsening
- **Frequency across studies (anchor range: about 21% to 48%):**
  - Cochrane review of retrospective series: 21% (Evoli 1992) to 48% (Pascuzzi 1984) [SchneiderGold2005]
  - 55 Korean patients on 40-80 mg prednisone: 42% (23 patients), defined as worsening within 4 weeks [Bae2006]
  - 62 Japanese AChR-MG patients: 26%, defined as a QMG rise of ≥3 points within 2 weeks [Kanai2019]
- **Risk factors.** Bae 2006 found older age, severe bulbar symptoms and more severe disease; the dose per kg did not predict worsening [Bae2006]. Kanai 2019 found thymoma-associated or early-onset MG, a starting prednisolone dose ≥40 mg/day, and upper-limb weakness. A 0-3 point score predicted worsening with an AUC of 0.770. [Kanai2019]
- **Timing.** None of the sources I could read gives a specific day window (such as days 5-10). The Cochrane review says no included trial reported early deterioration and that older reports gave no timing. The anchor window available is "within 2 weeks" or "within 4 weeks" of starting. (day 5-10: unverified) [SchneiderGold2005; Kanai2019; Bae2006]
- Seybold and Drachman (1974) developed the gradually increasing dose schedule to avoid the early worsening seen with oral doses of 60-100 mg. The NEJM paper has no abstract, so this is as the Cochrane review describes it. [Seybold1974; SchneiderGold2005]
- **Consensus advice.** Because steroids may cause transient worsening, "it may be appropriate to wait several days for PLEX or IVIg to have a beneficial effect before starting corticosteroids". [Sanders2016]
- **Nordic advice.** Combine 30-50 mg/day with IVIG or PLEX to avoid the "steroid dip". [Gilhus2024]
- The EPITOME trial in ocular MG started prednisone at 10 mg every other day and titrated it over 16 weeks to a maximum of 40 mg/day. [Benatar2016]

### Corticosteroids: adverse effects and prophylaxis
- **Bone.** The 2022 ACR guideline applies to anyone taking ≥2.5 mg/day glucocorticoid for >3 months. It strongly recommends early fracture-risk assessment (bone density, vertebral imaging, FRAX if ≥40 years). It strongly recommends drug treatment for those at medium, high or very high fracture risk. It is not MG-specific. [Humphrey2023]
- **Monitoring.** The Nordic guidance advises monitoring blood pressure, weight, bone density, glucose, sleep and mood during steroid treatment. It does not name specific prophylactic drugs. [Gilhus2024]
- **Pneumocystis (PJP).** The German guideline says PJP prophylaxis, for example co-trimoxazole three times a week, "should be considered" during longer-term (>6 months) combination immunosuppression. [Wiendl2023]
- I found no MG-specific citable recommendation on GI (proton-pump inhibitor) prophylaxis during steroid treatment.
- **Pregnancy.** The international consensus calls prednisone "the IS agent of choice during pregnancy". The Nordic guidance advises keeping prednisolone below 20 mg/day in pregnancy. [Sanders2016; Gilhus2024]

### Steroid-sparing agents: azathioprine
- **Palace 1998 trial.** This double-blind trial randomized 34 antibody-positive generalized MG patients for 3 years. Both groups received alternate-day prednisolone, starting at 1.5 mg/kg; one group also got azathioprine 2.5 mg/kg and the other placebo. [Palace1998]
  - At 1 year, the prednisolone dose did not differ (median 37.5 vs 45 mg alternate days).
  - At 3 years it did: median 0 mg with azathioprine vs 40 mg alternate days with placebo (p = 0.02).
  - The azathioprine group had fewer treatment failures and longer remissions.
  This trial is the anchor showing azathioprine's slow onset.
- **Witte 1984 series.** 18 of 24 patients tolerated azathioprine; 15 of those 18 (83%) improved. First response came after 4-10 months (mean 6.4). Improvement continued up to 24 months, with mean peak improvement at 14 months. All 6 patients who stopped relapsed within a year. [Witte1984]
- The international consensus: "Expert consensus and some RCT evidence support the use of azathioprine as a first-line IS agent in MG." [Sanders2016]
- **German dosing.** Start at 25-50 mg/day and increase over 3-4 weeks with laboratory checks, toward 2-3 mg/kg/day. The lymphocyte target is 600-1,000/µL; the effect takes several months. Use beyond 10 years should generally be avoided because of malignancy risk. [Wiendl2023]
- **Nordic dosing.** 2-3 mg/kg. Test TPMT activity and avoid azathioprine if TPMT is absent or low (<20 mU/L). The Nordic guidance considers it safe in pregnancy and breastfeeding. [Gilhus2024]
- **US label.** Imuran carries a boxed malignancy warning. It says patients with low or absent TPMT activity risk severe, life-threatening bone-marrow toxicity at conventional doses, and that TPMT testing "cannot substitute for" complete blood count monitoring. Its US indications are kidney transplant and rheumatoid arthritis, so MG use is off-label. [FDAImuran2025]

### Steroid-sparing agents: mycophenolate mofetil
- **Muscle Study Group trial (2008).** 80 people with mild to moderate AChR+ generalized MG were randomized to 2.5 g/day mycophenolate plus prednisone 20 mg/day, or placebo plus prednisone. After 12 weeks, QMG change did not differ: -4.4 vs -3.6 (p = 0.71). [MuscleStudyGroup2008]
- **International phase III trial (2008).** 176 patients on steroids were randomized to 2 g/day mycophenolate or placebo for 36 weeks while prednisone was tapered on a set schedule. The primary endpoint was met by 44% vs 39% (p = 0.541). [Sanders2008]
- **Why both trials were negative.** The trialists and a 2010 retrospective series give two reasons: prednisone worked better than predicted, and the trials were too short. In the retrospective series of 102 AChR+ patients, the share with a good outcome began to rise only after 6 months, and 80% of those followed >24 months did well. After 25 months, 54.5% took no prednisone. [MuscleStudyGroup2008; Hehir2010]
- **Guideline status.** The international consensus says RCTs do not support mycophenolate or tacrolimus, yet both are widely used and appear in several national guidelines. The German guideline lists mycophenolate as second-line and off-label. [Sanders2016; Wiendl2023]
- **PROMISE-MG (2024).** This prospective cohort at 19 North American centers compared mycophenolate (n = 47) with azathioprine (n = 31) in patients never treated with immunosuppressants. MG-QOL15r fell by -10.4 vs -6.8 points (p = 0.15). The composite of minimal manifestations or better with grade ≤1 adverse events was reached by 47.7% vs 28.1% (p = 0.12). Adverse events occurred in 19% on mycophenolate vs 32% on azathioprine; hepatotoxicity occurred in 15% on azathioprine. [Narayanaswami2024]
- **Teratogenicity.** The US CellCept label carries a boxed warning of embryofetal toxicity: increased first-trimester pregnancy loss and congenital malformations. Females of reproductive potential must be counseled on pregnancy prevention and planning. [FDACellCept2026]
- **Guidance on pregnancy.** The international consensus says mycophenolate and methotrexate are contraindicated in pregnancy. The Nordic guidance says neither should be used in women of childbearing age. [Sanders2016; Gilhus2024]

### Steroid-sparing agents: methotrexate, tacrolimus, ciclosporin, cyclophosphamide
- **Methotrexate trial (Pasnoor 2016).** 50 AChR+ patients were randomized to oral methotrexate 20 mg weekly or placebo for 12 months. Methotrexate did not reduce the prednisone area under the dose-time curve for months 4-12 (difference -488 mg; 95% CI -2,443 to 1,467; p = 0.26). It did not improve secondary measures either. [Pasnoor2016]
- **Methotrexate vs azathioprine (Heckmann 2011).** In a single-blinded trial in 24 South African patients, methotrexate 17.5 mg weekly matched azathioprine 2.5 mg/kg. Methotrexate patients needed less prednisone at months 10 and 12 (0.15 vs 0.31 mg/kg at month 12). [Heckmann2011]
- **Methotrexate in the 2020 update.** The 2020 consensus update added that oral methotrexate "may be considered as a steroid-sparing agent" when better-supported agents have failed or are not tolerated. [Narayanaswami2021]
- **Tacrolimus trial (Yoshikawa 2011).** 80 patients stable on prednisolone 10-20 mg/day were randomized for 28 weeks. Tacrolimus missed its primary steroid-sparing endpoint (p = 0.078), though some secondary analyses suggested benefit. [Yoshikawa2011]
- **Tacrolimus onset and status.** In 20 patients, an excitation-contraction coupling measure improved within 1-2 weeks of starting tacrolimus 3 mg/day. The authors suggest a possible early, non-immune effect through the ryanodine receptor. Tacrolimus is approved for MG in Japan. [Imai2012; Wiendl2023]
- **Ciclosporin trial (Tindall 1987).** 20 patients with recent-onset generalized MG were randomized to ciclosporin 6 mg/kg/day or placebo. At 6 months, strength improved significantly more with ciclosporin. Nephrotoxicity occurred in 3 patients and reversed when the dose was reduced or the drug stopped. [Tindall1987]
- **Ciclosporin status.** The international consensus says RCT evidence supports ciclosporin, but serious side effects and drug interactions limit its use. [Sanders2016]
- **Cyclophosphamide trial (De Feo 2002).** 23 patients with poorly controlled or steroid-toxic MG were randomized to monthly IV cyclophosphamide (starting at 500 mg/m²) or placebo for 12 months. Steroid doses fell more with cyclophosphamide at 6 and 12 months, and 5 patients on it came off steroids. Strength improved at 3 and 6 months and differed significantly from placebo at 12 months. [DeFeo2002]
- **Cyclophosphamide status.** The German guideline reserves it, alongside bortezomib and stem-cell therapy, for individual cases of highly active or refractory disease. [Wiendl2023]
- **Time-to-onset anchors (months):**
  - Nordic guidance, all non-steroid immunosuppressants: 1-6 months, sometimes >1 year for full effect [Gilhus2024]
  - azathioprine: 4-10 months, peak about 14 months [Witte1984]
  - mycophenolate: after about 6 months [Hehir2010]
  - methotrexate: about 10 months [Heckmann2011]
  - tacrolimus: 1-2 weeks, on an electrophysiological measure [Imai2012]
  - cyclophosphamide: 3-12 months [DeFeo2002]

### Rituximab
- **MuSK MG (Hehir 2017).** In a blinded multicenter review of 55 MuSK patients, 58% (14/24) of rituximab-treated patients reached the favorable primary outcome vs 16% (5/31) of controls (p = 0.002; NNT 2.4). At the last visit, 29% vs 74% were taking prednisone (mean 4.5 vs 13 mg/day). This is Class IV evidence. [Hehir2017]
- **MuSK MG (Díaz-Manera 2012).** After a mean of 31 months, all 6 MuSK patients were in remission (4) or minimal manifestations (2) without reinfusion, and antibody titers fell. By contrast, 6 of the 10 AChR+ patients who improved needed reinfusions. [DiazManera2012]
- **MuSK MG (meta-analysis 2025).** Across 12 studies (111 patients), 82% reached minimal manifestations or better and 56% reached complete stable or pharmacologic remission. [Chayanopparat2025]
- **MuSK dosing protocol.** In 25 MuSK patients, a 4+2 protocol (375 mg/m² weekly for 4 weeks, then monthly for 2 months) relapsed in 18.2%. Two 1 g doses ("1+1") relapsed in 80%. [CortesVicente2018]
- **BeatMG (AChR+).** This phase 2 futility trial randomized 52 patients with mild to moderate AChR+ generalized MG on prednisone ≥15 mg/day. The steroid-sparing endpoint was met by 60% on rituximab vs 56% on placebo, and the trial met its futility criterion (p = 0.03). Rituximab was safe. [Nowak2022]
- **RINOMAX (new-onset).** 47 patients with generalized MG for ≤12 months were randomized to a single 500 mg dose or placebo. At 16 weeks, 71% (17/24) vs 29% (6/21) reached minimal manifestations (QMG ≤4 on prednisolone ≤10 mg with no rescue), p = 0.007. Rescue treatment was needed by 4% vs 36%. [Piehl2022]
- **RINOMAX long-term follow-up.** Early rituximab gave lower QMG at 12 and 24 months and a lower hospitalization risk (HR 0.24). Severe infection occurred in 12.5% of the early-rituximab group and 18.8% of the delayed group. [Wu2025]
- **Cochrane 2025 (2 RCTs, 99 participants).** Rituximab made little or no difference to steroid sparing (RR 1.00). It probably greatly reduces relapse needing rescue therapy: 220 vs 490 per 1,000 (RR 0.45; moderate certainty). Effects on symptom scores are very uncertain. [Dodd2025]
- **Current place (international consensus).** Consider rituximab early in MuSK MG after an unsatisfactory response to initial immunotherapy. Its efficacy in refractory AChR+ MG is uncertain; it is an option when other agents fail. [Narayanaswami2021]
- **Current place (newer guidance).** ABN 2025 says RCT evidence now supports early rituximab, within 1 year of generalized onset. The Nordic guidance lists it as an alternative first-line option to steroids plus azathioprine. It sees no clear benefit above 500 mg per dose and advises considering re-dosing after ≥6 months. [Jacob2025; Gilhus2024]
- **Rituximab safety (Nordic).** Adverse events occurred in about 20% of patients in a meta-analysis, including one PML case. [Gilhus2024]
- **German guideline on rituximab.** Rituximab "must be used" in highly active or refractory MuSK MG. It may be a first-choice option in highly active AChR-negative, LRP4-positive or seronegative disease (the excerpt I read was ambiguous on the exact antibody groups). [Wiendl2023]

### IVIG and plasma exchange
- **Indications (international consensus).** Short-term IVIG or PLEX is used for life-threatening signs such as respiratory insufficiency or dysphagia, before surgery in patients with significant bulbar weakness, when a rapid response is needed, or when other treatments are not enough. The two are "probably equally effective" in severe generalized MG. [Sanders2016]
- **Other consensus statements.** IVIG's effect is less certain in milder or ocular MG. PLEX may work better than IVIG in MuSK MG. In crisis, "expert consensus suggests that PLEX is more effective and works more quickly". [Sanders2016]
- **Contraindications.** IVIG is contraindicated in hypercoagulable states, renal failure and hypersensitivity to immunoglobulin. PLEX cannot be used in sepsis. [Sanders2016]
- **Barth 2011.** 84 patients with QMG >10.5 and worsening weakness were randomized to IVIG 1 g/kg/day for 2 days or 5 single-volume PLEX sessions every other day. [Barth2011]
  - QMG at baseline: 14.30 vs 14.35.
  - QMG change at day 14: -3.2 vs -4.7 (p = 0.13).
  - Improved by day 14: 69% vs 65%.
  - Improvement lasted to day 28 in both arms, and the duration of improvement was similar.
- **Gajdos 1997.** 87 patients in exacerbation were randomized to 3 PLEX sessions or IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day for 3 or 5 days. The median change in muscle score by day 15 was +18 vs +15.5 (p = 0.65). Side effects occurred in 8 PLEX patients vs 1 IVIG patient (p = 0.01). [Gajdos1997]
- **Gajdos 2005 (dose).** In 173 patients in exacerbation, 2 g/kg IVIG was not significantly better than 1 g/kg. Mean improvement at 2 weeks was 19.33 vs 15.49 points (p = 0.12). [Gajdos2005]
- **Zinman 2007.** In 51 patients with worsening weakness, IVIG 2 g/kg gave a clinically meaningful QMG improvement at day 14 that persisted at day 28. The benefit was largest in patients with QMG >10.5. [Zinman2007]
- **Cochrane IVIG review (2012).** In exacerbations, there is some RCT evidence that IVIG works and no difference from PLEX. In chronic MG, the evidence is insufficient. [Gajdos2012]
- **Cochrane PLEX review (2002).** Only one small RCT existed and it was inconclusive. Many case series report short-term benefit, especially in crisis. [Gajdos2002]
- **Cochrane IVIG review (2025; 12 RCTs, 515 participants).** IVIG may slightly improve QMG vs placebo in the medium term (-1.86 points, low certainty, in a sensitivity analysis), below clinical-significance thresholds. Headache was more common (risk difference 0.32; NNTH 3). IVIG may help less than PLEX in the short term (QMG difference 2.92; very low certainty) and may lengthen hospital stay by 2.9 days. No trial tested subcutaneous immunoglobulin. [Manolopoulos2025]
- **German dosing.** IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days or 1 g/kg/day for 2 days. PLEX: 5-10 exchanges of about 1-1.5 plasma volumes every other day. Without other immunotherapy, PLEX's effect lasts only a few weeks. Immunoadsorption is considered equally effective. [Wiendl2023]
- **Nordic dosing.** IVIG 2 g/kg total over 3-5 days. PLEX may act slightly faster. In a prolonged severe crisis, PLEX first, then IVIG. [Gilhus2024]
- **Maintenance IVIG (guidance).** The international consensus says maintenance IVIG "can be considered" for refractory MG or when immunosuppressants are relatively contraindicated. The German guideline calls it off-label and expert-opinion based (typically 1 g/kg every 4-8 weeks after a pulse). [Sanders2016; Wiendl2023]
- **Maintenance IVIG (Bril 2025 trial).** This phase 2 RCT in 62 AChR+ patients gave IGIV-C 2 g/kg, then 1 g/kg every 3 weeks to week 21, or placebo. [Bril2025]
  - Primary endpoint missed: QMG change at week 24 was -5.1 vs -3.1 (p = 0.187).
  - MG-ADL improvement of ≥2 points: 70% vs 40.6% (p = 0.025).
  - Three of the 4 exacerbations needing hospital admission occurred in the IVIG arm, including one death.
- **Disagreement.** The Bril 2025 trial missed its primary endpoint, and the 2025 Cochrane review rates the evidence for IVIG outside exacerbations as low certainty. Guidelines nonetheless still permit maintenance IVIG in refractory disease. [Bril2025; Manolopoulos2025; Sanders2016]
- **PLEX safety.** Of 42 patients randomized to PLEX, 90% were treated as outpatients and 83% via peripheral veins. 55% had no complications and 45% had mild to moderate reactions, mostly citrate reactions and vein problems. Two had serious adverse events (one related). [Ebadi2013]
- **IVIG adverse events in Barth 2011 (counts, n = 41-43 per arm).** Headache 8, nausea or vomiting 7, fever 3, chills 2, allergic reaction 2, hemolytic anemia 1. [Barth2011]
- **MuSK MG.** In 110 MuSK patients, PLEX improved 93% and IVIG only 61%. [Guptill2011]

### Guidance: international consensus 2016 and 2020 update
- **Method (2016).** A panel of 15 international experts used the RAND/UCLA appropriateness method, with three rounds of anonymous e-mail voting. It defined treatment goals, minimal manifestations, remission, ocular MG, impending crisis, crisis and refractory MG. [Sanders2016]
- **Treatment goal.** MGFA post-intervention status of minimal manifestations or better, with no more than CTCAE grade 1 medication side effects. [Sanders2016]
- **Minimal manifestation status.** No symptoms or functional limitations from MG, but some weakness on examination. [Sanders2016]
- **Impending crisis.** Rapid worsening that the physician judges could lead to crisis within days to weeks. [Sanders2016]
- **Manifest crisis.** Worsening that requires intubation, or noninvasive ventilation to avoid intubation. [Sanders2016]
- **Refractory MG.** Post-intervention status unchanged or worse after corticosteroids and at least two other immunosuppressants at adequate dose and duration, with persistent symptoms or side effects that limit function. [Sanders2016]
- **Tapering.** Once goals have been held for 6 months to 2 years, taper the immunosuppressant slowly to the minimal effective dose, adjusting no more often than every 3-6 months. [Sanders2016]
- **When to use non-steroidal immunosuppressants:**
  - alone, if steroids are contraindicated or refused
  - from the start with steroids, when steroid risk is high
  - added later, for significant steroid side effects, an inadequate response, or a dose that cannot be reduced without relapse
  [Sanders2016]
- **2020 update method.** The 2019 panel added one South American member and kept the RAND/UCLA method. [Narayanaswami2021]
- **What the 2020 update added.** It updated the thymectomy statements and added new statements on rituximab, eculizumab, methotrexate, early immunosuppression in ocular MG, and MG associated with immune checkpoint inhibitors. [Narayanaswami2021]
- **Thymectomy (2020).** Consider early thymectomy in non-thymomatous AChR+ generalized MG at ages 18-50. Strongly consider it after failed or intolerable immunotherapy. There is no indication in MuSK, LRP4 or agrin antibody MG. [Narayanaswami2021]
- **Eculizumab (2020).** Consider it in severe, refractory AChR+ generalized MG, generally after other immunotherapies fail, until cost-effectiveness data exist. Give meningococcal vaccination first. [Narayanaswami2021]

### Guidance: newer national and regional guidelines (biologics earlier) and disagreements
- **German/DGN guideline (English adaptation 2023).** It sets "rapid achievement of complete disease control" as a central goal. It staggers therapy by antibody status and disease activity, using MG-ADL, QMG and MGFA class to separate mild/moderate from highly active (including refractory) disease. [Wiendl2023]
- **German definition of highly active disease.** Any one of the following, despite adequate therapy:
  - persistent symptoms relevant to daily life (≥MGFA IIb) and/or at least 2 severe exacerbations needing IVIG, PLEX or immunoadsorption within 1 year of diagnosis
  - persistent symptoms (≥MGFA IIa) plus a severe exacerbation or crisis in the past year
  - persistent symptoms (≥MGFA IIa) for more than 2 years
  [Wiendl2023]
- **German ladder.** Steroids plus classical immunosuppressants (mainly azathioprine) are basic therapy from mild/moderate to highly active disease. In highly active AChR+ disease, complement inhibitors (eculizumab, ravulizumab) or the FcRn blocker efgartigimod are recommended. Rituximab is recommended in MuSK+ disease. [Wiendl2023]
- **German contrast with the international consensus.** The German ladder brings biologics in on activity criteria. The international consensus places eculizumab only after other immunotherapies fail and defines refractory MG by failure of steroids plus 2 other agents. [Wiendl2023; Narayanaswami2021; Sanders2016]
- **German 2025 update.** The AWMF register lists a 2025 update of the German guideline; I could not open it, so its changes are unverified.
- **German-Austrian expert opinion (2026).** A panel of 18 neurologists published practical statements for highly active generalized MG. They cover when to start C5 inhibitors (eculizumab, ravulizumab, zilucoplan) and FcRn inhibitors (efgartigimod, rozanolixizumab), how to sequence and switch them, and when to move to rituximab, apheresis or immunoglobulin. Abstract only. [Meisel2026]
- **Nordic guidance (2024).** First-line: pyridostigmine, early thymectomy (thymoma, or non-thymoma up to age 50-65), and the lowest possible steroid dose combined with azathioprine. Rituximab is an alternative first-line option. Second-line: mycophenolate, methotrexate and tacrolimus. [Gilhus2024]
- **Nordic position on biologics.** Complement inhibitors and FcRn blockers are second-line options for severe, difficult-to-treat MG, depending on local approval and cost. The guidance notes that 10-20% of patients stay moderately to severely weak after at least two high-dose immunosuppressants. [Gilhus2024]
- **ABN 2025 update.** Changes from 2015: daily rather than alternate-day steroids, emphasis on early thymectomy, early rituximab within 1 year of generalized onset, and a role for complement and FcRn inhibitors that is "being slowly established", especially in patients unresponsive to conventional therapy. [Jacob2025]
- **Japanese guidance (2014, rationale paper 2018).** The top priority is health-related quality of life. The initial goal is minimal manifestations or better on prednisolone ≤5 mg/day, reached as fast as possible by:
  - minimizing oral prednisolone
  - starting calcineurin inhibitors early
  - using early fast-acting treatment (IV methylprednisolone, PLEX, IVIG)
  [Murai2018]
- **Japanese guidance (2022).** It does not recommend a high-dose oral steroid regimen with escalation and de-escalation. It defines refractory MG, includes molecular-targeted drugs, and divides MG into six clinical subtypes. [Murai2024]
- **Evidence behind early fast-acting treatment.** In 688 Japanese generalized MG patients, the target (minimal manifestations or better on ≤5 mg for ≥6 months) was reached more often and earlier with early fast-acting treatment (log-rank p = 0.0001). [Utsugisawa2017]
- **Disagreement on starting steroid dose.** The Japanese guidance and Nordic guidance (mild disease) favor a low start, and the Nordic guidance allows high-dose 0.75-1 mg/kg in moderate to severe disease. Older North American practice (Pascuzzi 1984) started at 60-80 mg/day. Palace 1998 started at 1.5 mg/kg on alternate days. [Murai2024; Gilhus2024; Pascuzzi1984; Palace1998]
- **Disagreement on the treatment goal.**
  - International: minimal manifestations or better with grade ≤1 side effects [Sanders2016]
  - Japanese: adds a prednisolone ceiling of ≤5 mg/day [Murai2018]
  - German: aims for complete disease control [Wiendl2023]
  - Nordic: sets no numeric target and asks patients and doctors to agree on "ambitious" goals together [Gilhus2024]
  - Gilhus 2019 review: aim for remission or only mild symptoms with near-normal function [Gilhus2019]
- **Disagreement on azathioprine vs mycophenolate.** Azathioprine is first-line in the German and Nordic guidance. In North America, mycophenolate is used about as often. PROMISE-MG found no significant outcome difference, with fewer adverse events on mycophenolate. [Wiendl2023; Gilhus2024; Narayanaswami2024]
- **Gilhus 2019 review.** IVIG and PLEX are fast-acting treatments for exacerbations. [Gilhus2019]

### Ocular MG
- **2020 consensus update on ocular MG:**
  - Treat with immunosuppressants when ptosis or eye-movement weakness that has not responded to cholinesterase inhibitors is functionally limiting or troublesome.
  - Use corticosteroids as the first immunosuppressant, with steroid-sparing agents when steroids fail or are not tolerated.
  - Low-dose steroids may suffice.
  - AChR+ ocular MG unresponsive to cholinesterase inhibitors may be offered thymectomy when immunosuppression is declined, contraindicated or ineffective.
  [Narayanaswami2021]
- **EPITOME (2016).** This double-blind RCT planned 88 participants but randomized only 11. Treatment failed in 100% on placebo (n = 5) vs 17% on prednisone (n = 6), p = 0.02. Median time to sustained minimal manifestation status was 14 weeks, at an average of 15 mg/day prednisone. [Benatar2016]
- **Kupersmith 2003 (steroids and generalization).** In 94 ocular MG patients followed 2 years, generalized MG developed in 4 of 58 (7%) prednisone-treated patients vs 13 of 36 (36%) untreated patients (OR 0.13). Abnormal AChR antibody levels raised the risk (OR 6.33). Thymoma occurred in 0.7%. This was an observational study. [Kupersmith2003]
- **Cochrane 2012.** RCTs do not allow meaningful conclusions on any treatment for ocular MG. Observational data suggest that corticosteroids and azathioprine may lower the risk of progression to generalized MG. About 50% of people with MG present with purely ocular symptoms, and 50-60% of these generalize, mostly within 2 years. [Benatar2012]
- **German guideline (ocular MG).** Pyridostigmine first, then steroids with or without azathioprine. Alternatives are mycophenolate, methotrexate, tacrolimus or ciclosporin. Thymectomy may be considered without generalization when other MGTX criteria are met. [Wiendl2023]
- **Other guidance on ocular MG.** The international consensus says IVIG's efficacy is less certain in ocular MG. In juvenile ocular MG, pyridostigmine alone may be the first treatment because children more often remit spontaneously. [Sanders2016]
- **Disagreement.** A 2025 expert minireview states that ocular MG "has no agreed-upon diagnostic and management criteria". The Nordic guidance excludes ocular MG entirely. [Kupersmith2025; Gilhus2024]
