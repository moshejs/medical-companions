# E. The thymus, thymectomy & thymoma
This pass covers the randomized MGTX thymectomy trial and its 5-year extension, with time-course anchor points for a "Thymectomy over the years" curve widget. It also covers the post hoc and economic analyses, surgical approaches (transsternal, VATS, robotic; extended resection), peri-operative timing and crisis risk, who guidance says thymectomy is for, thymus pathology and thymoma (WHO types, Masaoka-Koga staging, titin/RyR antibodies, post-thymectomy MG), and the history from Sauerbruch and Blalock to MGTX. Full text was read for MGTX 2016 (PMC author manuscript, including Tables 1-3 and Figure 1), the MGTX extension 2019 (PMC author manuscript, all tables), the 2016 and 2020 consensus guidance (PMC), the Lord 2026 economic evaluation (PMC), Romi 2011 and Okuno 2025 (PMC), a 1949 Keynes lecture (read, not cited) and the Barohn 2021 commentary (PDF). Everything else comes from abstracts. I could not access: the full text of Lee 2020 (the publisher blocks text-mining), the Gilhus 2019 Nat Rev Dis Primers full text (paywall), the 2000 AAN practice parameter itself (it has no abstract in PubMed and the publisher page returned 403), the MGTX supplementary appendix (thymus histology Table S2 and MMS/MG-ADL by timepoint), the extension's Figure 2 (the PMC page served a CAPTCHA, which I did not attempt), and the Blalock 1939/1941 and Schumacher & Roth 1912 originals (scans or not indexed). ## Facts

### MGTX design (Wolfe 2016, NEJM)
- MGTX was a multicentre, international, rater-blinded randomized trial comparing extended transsternal thymectomy plus a standardized alternate-day prednisone protocol with the same prednisone protocol alone. 126 patients were randomized (60 prednisone alone, 66 thymectomy plus prednisone) at 36 recruiting sites between September 2006 and November 2012 [Wolfe2016].
- Final eligibility: age 18-65 years, generalized non-thymomatous MG of <5 years' duration, MGFA class II-IV, and AChR antibody >1.00 nmol/L. Levels of 0.50-0.99 nmol/L were accepted if the diagnosis was confirmed by edrophonium, repetitive nerve stimulation or single-fibre EMG [Wolfe2016].
- The original criteria were stricter (age 18-60, disease <3 years). In October 2008 they were widened to <5 years and up to 65 years to improve recruitment. In October 2009 the planned sample size was cut from 200 to 150 [Wolfe2016].
- Screening funnel: 6958 people screened at 67 centres in 18 countries. 6727 were ineligible, mainly for disease ≥5 years (47%), age outside range (42%), non-glucocorticoid immunosuppressants (29%), or previous thymectomy or chest surgery (28%). Of 231 eligible, 105 declined and 126 were randomized [Wolfe2016].
- Prednisone protocol: start at 10 mg alternate days, rise in 10-mg steps to 100 mg alternate days (or 1.5 mg/kg, whichever was lower). The dose was held until minimal manifestation status (MMS) was reached with QMG <14 and at least 1 point below baseline. It was then tapered by 10 mg every 2 weeks to 40 mg, then by 5 mg per month. Azathioprine was allowed if MMS was not reached by month 12 or side effects were unacceptable [Wolfe2016].
- Surgery was performed within 30 days of randomization (randomization = month 0). To preserve blinding, participants wore black high-collared pullover shirts at visits to hide sternotomy scars. Only an unblinded neurologist saw patients until month 4 [Wolfe2016].
- Baseline (prednisone alone vs thymectomy): median age 33 vs 32 years. Female 65% vs 76%. Median disease duration 1.14 vs 1.08 years. Mean QMG 12.35±4.90 vs 11.40±5.12. Prednisone at baseline in 78% vs 74%, at 42.49 vs 43.43 mg [Wolfe2016].
- Protocol deviations: 8 patients assigned to thymectomy declined surgery, and 8 assigned to prednisone alone had thymectomy outside the protocol. A thymoma was found at surgery in 1 patient. All were analysed by intention to treat [Wolfe2016].
- Extended transsternal thymectomy was chosen because it gives reproducible maximal resection with low morbidity. It removes about 85-95% of thymic tissue [Wolfe2016].

### MGTX 3-year primary and secondary results
- Time-weighted average QMG over 3 years: **8.99±4.93 prednisone alone vs 6.15±4.09 thymectomy**, a difference of 2.85 points (99.5% CI 0.47-5.22; P<0.001) [Wolfe2016].
- Time-weighted average alternate-day prednisone dose over 3 years: **60±27 mg vs 44±21 mg**, a difference of 16 mg (95% CI 7-25; P<0.001). Using prescribed rather than pill-counted doses gave 59 vs 43 mg (P=0.001) [Wolfe2016].
- Azathioprine use: 48% prednisone alone vs 17% thymectomy (P<0.001). Hospitalization for MG exacerbation: 37% (22/60) vs 9% (6/66) (P<0.001). Mean cumulative hospital days 19.2±24.5 vs 8.4±8.6 (P=0.09) [Wolfe2016].
- MMS at month 36: 47% prednisone alone vs 67% thymectomy (P=0.03). Time-weighted MG-ADL: 3.41 vs 2.24 (P=0.008) [Wolfe2016].
- Treatment-associated complications did not differ (P=0.73). The thymectomy group had fewer patients with immunosuppression-related symptoms (P<0.001), fewer symptoms in total (P<0.001) and lower symptom-related distress (P=0.003). SF-36 physical and mental components did not differ [Wolfe2016].
- Adverse events: 93 vs 48 events (P<0.001). Life-threatening events 12% vs 2% (P=0.03). One death in the prednisone-alone group. One thymectomy complication (2%), with post-thymectomy diaphragmatic hemiparesis listed among the disability events [Wolfe2016].
- Penalty analysis (prednisone dose frozen at the level when azathioprine was added): 68 vs 46 mg alternate days, a 33% lower exposure with thymectomy (difference 22 mg, 95% CI 11-34) [Wolfe2016].
- Subgroups: interaction tests were not significant for prior steroid use, sex or onset age (<40 vs ≥40). Differences were not significant in men for QMG or in the 26 steroid-naïve patients for either outcome. The trial reports these as underpowered subgroups [Wolfe2016].
- In a post hoc analysis, patients aged ≥50 at enrolment showed no significant between-group difference in QMG or prednisone dose [Narayanaswami2021]. A later cohort paper notes that only 17 MGTX participants were aged ≥50 [Heber2026].

### MGTX time-course anchor points for the curve widget (months 0-36; approximate, digitized from Figure 1)
- Mean QMG (prednisone alone / thymectomy). Month 0: 12.35 / 11.40 (Table 1, exact). Month 3: ~9.5 / ~7.4. Month 6: ~8.1 / ~6.7. Month 12: ~8.9 / ~6.0. Month 24: ~8.2 / ~5.3. Month 36: ~8.2 / ~5.2 [Wolfe2016].
- The QMG curves separate by month 3 and stay apart. The prednisone-alone curve plateaus at about 8-9 from month 6 onward. The thymectomy curve keeps falling slowly to about 4.7-4.8 at months 30-33 [Wolfe2016].
- Mean alternate-day prednisone dose (prednisone alone / thymectomy). No month-0 dose is plotted; the baseline dose among users was 42.5 / 43.4 mg (Table 1). Month 1: ~78 / ~67 mg. Peak at months 2-3: ~84 / ~80 mg (month 2) and ~84 / ~72 mg (month 3). Month 6: ~71 / ~61 mg. Month 12: ~60 / ~39 mg. Month 24: ~53 / ~33 mg. Month 36: ~44 / ~30 mg [Wolfe2016].
- Shape of the prednisone curves: both arms ramp up during dose escalation (peak at months 2-3), then taper. The gap is widest between months 12 and 30, at about 18-21 mg alternate days [Wolfe2016].
- Benefits appeared within the first year and were sustained through year 3 [Narayanaswami2021].

### MGTX extension to 5 years (Wolfe 2019, Lancet Neurol)
- Of 111 patients who completed 36 months, 68 (61%) entered the rater-blinded extension (33 prednisone alone, 35 thymectomy). 50 (74% of entrants) completed month 60: 24 prednisone alone and 26 thymectomy [Wolfe2019].
- Time-weighted average QMG from month 0 to 60: **9.34±5.08 prednisone alone vs 5.47±3.87 thymectomy**, a difference of 3.87 points (99.5% CI 0.71-7.04; P=0.0007). This is larger than the 2.85-point difference at 3 years [Wolfe2019].
- Time-weighted average alternate-day prednisone from month 0 to 60: **48±29 mg vs 24±21 mg**, a difference of 24 mg (95% CI 12-36; P=0.0002) [Wolfe2019].
- Month-60 anchor: the thymectomy arm's mean alternate-day prednisone fell to **11.9 mg at month 60** (about 5 mg/day or less). The month-60 value for the prednisone-alone arm is shown only in Figure 2B, which I could not access (unverified) [Wolfe2019].
- MMS proportions over time (prednisone alone vs thymectomy). Month 36: 47% (24/51) vs 67% (39/58) (all MGTX completers). Month 48: 52% (15/29) vs 74% (23/31) (P=0.07). **Month 60: 58% (14/24) vs 88% (23/26)** (P≈0.02) [Wolfe2019].
- From month 0 to 60, azathioprine use was 58% (19/33) vs 20% (7/35) (P=0.0014). IVIG use was 33% (11/33) vs 9% (3/35) (P=0.0162). Plasma exchange did not differ (12% vs 14%) [Wolfe2019].
- Hospitalization for MG exacerbation over 0-60 months: 30% (10/33) prednisone alone vs 6% (2/35) thymectomy. All-cause hospitalization: 48% vs 14% [Wolfe2019].
- MG-QOL15 favoured thymectomy at month 39 (13.1 vs 4.8; P=0.0029) but not at month 48 or 60. Time-weighted MG-ADL from 0 to 60 months was 3.26 vs 1.61 (P=0.0044), with no difference at month 60 itself (2.04 vs 1.23; P=0.21) [Wolfe2019].
- Penalized prednisone analyses also favoured thymectomy. Method 1 (maximum dose before azathioprine): 66.2 vs 31.0 mg. Method 2 (dose at azathioprine start): 60.6 vs 28.3 mg (both P<0.0001) [Wolfe2019].
- Between months 36 and 60, only 4 patients (2 per arm) had a QMG rise of ≥2 points. There were no deaths during the extension [Wolfe2019].
- Limitation acknowledged by the authors: only 45% of 3-year completers reached month 60, and extension entrants had better MG-ADL scores and fewer complications at month 36, so selection bias cannot be excluded [Wolfe2019].

### Post hoc and economic analyses of MGTX
- Sustained MMS (no MG symptoms for 6 months) with complete prednisone withdrawal over 5 years: **64% thymectomy vs 38% prednisone alone**. Median time to reach it was 30 months with thymectomy and was not reached with prednisone alone (p<0.001) (Class II evidence) [Lee2020].
- In the post hoc analysis, prednisone-associated adverse symptoms were more frequent in the prednisone-alone group, and distress rose with higher prednisone doses [Lee2020].
- The 2020 AAN practice advisory used MGTX to estimate a 36-month risk difference for MMS of 20% (95% CI 1.6%-37%) favouring thymectomy plus prednisone (moderate confidence) [Gronseth2020].
- UK NHS lifetime Markov model built on MGTX patient-level data: thymectomy plus prednisolone dominated prednisolone alone, at 14.63 vs 14.11 QALYs and £181,716 vs £194,730. That is +0.52 QALYs and £13,014 saved per patient, with >99% probability of cost-effectiveness at £25,000/QALY [Lord2026].
- The model assumed a VATS procedure cost of £9,730. Thymectomy stayed dominant even if benefit stopped after the 5 observed trial years. Assuming a lifetime durable effect gave +1.25 QALYs and £49,629 saved [Lord2026].
- MGTX authors noted that thymectomy can cost up to $80,000 in the US [Wolfe2016].

### Surgical approaches, extent and peri-operative care
- MGTX tested only extended transsternal thymectomy. Its authors note that less invasive approaches report similar effectiveness with shorter recovery and better cosmesis. The concern is that they may leave ectopic thymic tissue in perithymic and pericardial fat [Wolfe2016].
- The 2016 and 2020 consensus says endoscopic and robotic approaches have a good safety record in experienced centres. No randomized comparisons exist, but across studies less invasive approaches appear to give similar results [Sanders2016; Narayanaswami2021].
- **Disagreement:** the 2020 AAN advisory recommends (Level B) that people considering minimally invasive thymectomy be told it is uncertain whether the benefit seen with extended transsternal thymectomy carries over to minimally invasive approaches [Gronseth2020]. The consensus panel is more permissive [Narayanaswami2021].
- A meta-analysis of 26 studies (3,588 patients) found no difference in complete stable remission (CSR) at 1, 3 or 5 years between open and minimally invasive thymectomy. CSR at 1 year was better after minimally invasive surgery in non-thymomatous MG (P=0.03). Minimally invasive surgery had shorter hospital stay and less blood loss, but longer operating time [LeeY2023].
- A 4-institution cohort of non-thymomatous MG (29 minimally invasive, 25 transsternal) found no difference in prednisone dose or QMG over 2 years. Hospital stay was 2.5 vs 5 days (P<0.01) [Beller2025].
- Robotic vs open, propensity-matched single centre (79 robotic, 155 open): robotic had fewer in-hospital clinical complications (6.8% vs 21.1%) and surgical complications (1.7% vs 11.4%), and fewer Clavien-Dindo ≥2 complications (15.3% vs 28.1%), with similar 90-day mortality [Soder2023].
- By 2012, robotic thymectomy was performed in more than 100 centres, at about 1,000 procedures a year. The largest single centre had about 500 cases, with a peri-operative complication rate below 2% [Rueckert2015].
- Extent of resection: in early-onset MG, more ectopic germinal centres in the resected thymus predicted better 2-year and long-term post-thymectomy outcomes (AUC 0.79 in EOMG) [Sarkkinen2022].
- Timing: thymectomy is elective. Guidance says it should be done when the patient is stable and judged safe for a procedure in which postoperative pain and mechanical factors can limit breathing [Sanders2016; Narayanaswami2021].
- The 2016 guidance gives "the long delay in onset of effect" as the reason thymectomy is elective, and recommends postponing it until after pregnancy [Sanders2016].
- Guidance lists plasma exchange (PLEX) and IVIG as appropriate short-term treatment "in preparation for surgery in patients with significant bulbar dysfunction" [Sanders2016].
- A double-blind RCT in 47 patients with well-controlled MG (minimal manifestations) before surgery under general anaesthesia (16 thymectomies) gave IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days or saline. One crisis occurred, in the placebo arm (non-invasive ventilation, no reintubation). The authors concluded routine preoperative IVIG is not needed in well-controlled patients (Class I) [Gamez2019].
- In a small RCT (n=24) of preparation before thymectomy, IVIG 1 g/kg/day for 2 days was compared with five 1-L plasma exchanges. The plasma exchange group had longer intubation (p=0.01) and longer surgery (p=0.05) [AlipourFaz2017].
- A 2026 evidence review reports that postoperative myasthenic crisis is now below 10%. Established risk factors are bulbar involvement, reduced lung function and prior crises. Routine IVIG or PLEX for all patients before surgery is not supported; risk-based use guided by scores such as Leuzzi and Kanai is suggested [Alhammadi2026].
- A meta-analysis of 25 studies identified these independent risk factors for crisis after thymectomy: prior crisis, bulbar symptoms, Osserman stage, preoperative pyridostigmine dose, AChR antibody >100 nmol/L, lung function, disease duration, blood loss >1,000 mL, thoracotomy, postoperative lung infection, thymoma and WHO type [Liu2020].
- In 127 thymoma-MG patients, 10% had postoperative crisis needing intubation (weaned after 2-28 days, median 9). WHO B2-B3 histology (OR 10.0) and Osserman IIA-IV (OR 6.0) were independent predictors [Xue2017].
- US NSQIP 2005-2023, 352 non-thymomatous MG thymectomies: broad composite complication rate 7.1%, narrow composite 3.1%, no 30-day deaths [Carrera2026].
- Japanese national registry (1996-2023): postoperative hospital mortality after thymectomy for non-thymomatous MG was 0.26%, and minimally invasive VATS/RATS use rose steadily [Nakajima2026].

### Uptake after MGTX
- In US NSQIP, the annual rate of thymectomy for MG rose until about 2019-2020 (APC +2.52%), then fell through 2023 (APC −10.79%, P=0.02). The authors describe this as limited adoption [Carrera2026].
- In Japan, thymectomy for non-thymomatous MG fell from a peak of 375 cases in 2001 to 97 in 2023, while thymoma resection rose. The author links this partly to thymectomy's "optional" status in Japanese guidelines [Nakajima2026].

### Who thymectomy is recommended for (guidance and evidence by subgroup)
- AChR+ generalized non-thymomatous MG aged 18-50: thymectomy "should be considered early in the disease" to improve outcomes and reduce immunotherapy needs and hospitalizations (2020 update) [Narayanaswami2021].
- AChR+ generalized MG of any age: thymectomy "should be strongly considered" if an initial adequate trial of immunotherapy fails or causes intolerable side effects [Narayanaswami2021].
- The 2020 AAN advisory recommends that clinicians discuss thymectomy with patients with AChR+ generalized MG (Level B). It is based on one Class I study of patients under 65 [Gronseth2020].
- **Disagreement on age:** MGTX enrolled people up to 65 [Wolfe2016]. The consensus recommendation is framed for ages 18-50 because the post hoc ≥50 analysis was negative [Narayanaswami2021]. A pathology review describes MGTX as showing benefit "up to 60 years" [Weis2018]. The UK economic paper describes thymectomy as an adjunct for patients under 45 [Lord2026].
- Thymoma: with rare exceptions, everyone with MG and thymoma should have surgery to remove the tumour and all thymic tissue. Removing it may not improve the MG. In elderly or multimorbid patients, palliative radiotherapy can be considered, and small thymomas may be observed [Sanders2016].
- Seronegative generalized MG: thymectomy "may be considered" if immunosuppression fails or causes intolerable effects. In children with seronegative MG, congenital myasthenic syndromes should be excluded first [Sanders2016; Narayanaswami2021].
- MuSK, LRP4 or agrin antibodies: "current evidence does not support an indication for thymectomy" [Sanders2016; Narayanaswami2021]. In a multicentre MuSK-MG cohort, 35% of thymectomized patients vs 55% of non-thymectomized reached MM or better (adjusted OR 0.43, 95% CI 0.12-1.53) [Clifford2019]. The thymus is thought not to be involved in MuSK MG [Marx2013].
- Ocular MG: AChR+ patients who respond poorly to anticholinesterases and who prefer not to take, cannot take, or do not respond to immunosuppression "may be offered thymectomy" (median panel score 8). The supporting evidence is retrospective [Narayanaswami2021].
- Ocular MG evidence: one case-control study (47 operated vs 67 not) found no difference in stable remission at about 100-116 months. Case series report complete remission of 26% (110 patients after extended transsternal thymectomy) to 51%. A 26-study meta-analysis in non-thymomatous MG gave a pooled CSR of 0.51 with high heterogeneity [Narayanaswami2021].
- In a robotic cohort, thymectomy before generalization of ocular-onset MG gave a higher 5-year CSR (49.5%) than thymectomy after generalization (33.4%; P=0.0053) [LiF2020].
- Late-onset MG (≥50), meta-analysis of 20 studies: response with surgery vs medical therapy OR 1.42 (0.86-2.35, not significant). In the subgroup with onset ≥45 or surgery at ≥50, OR 1.92 (1.06-3.48). Overall 34% improved, with higher response when operated <3 years from diagnosis [Chen2025].
- Late-onset MG, conflicting cohorts: in Pisa (127 patients; 87 operated), thymectomy was associated with remission (adjusted HR 3.25, 95% CI 1.31-8.1) [Latini2025]. In another cohort (45 <50 vs 34 ≥50), outcomes and complications were similar by age [Heber2026]. No RCT exists [Chen2025].
- Children: the value of thymectomy before puberty is unclear. It should be considered in children with generalized AChR+ MG who respond poorly to pyridostigmine and immunosuppression, or to avoid immunosuppression complications [Sanders2016].
- Juvenile MG, review of 17 studies (588 operated): 77% improved or needed less medication, and complete remission was 40% (172/430). Earlier surgery, surgery after puberty onset, AChR positivity, more severe disease and hyperplastic thymus were associated with better outcomes. All studies were retrospective [Ng2021].
- Juvenile MG meta-analysis (1,109 patients): mean presentation age 7.38 years, 60.6% ocular onset, 78.7% AChR+. Of 641 with thymus examined, 64.9% had hyperplasia and 2.2% thymoma. 45.6% had thymectomy and CSR was 23.7% [Lin2023].
- In a robotic-thymectomy juvenile cohort (47 operated vs 20 medical), thymectomy was associated with CSR (HR 3.84, 95% CI 1.12-13.23) and with a steroid-sparing effect [LiZ2022].
- Reviews summarize that benefit is established for adult early-onset AChR+ MG. Evidence remains limited for patients over 50, juvenile onset and purely ocular MG, and many real-world patients do not reach CSR [Rath2023].

### Thymus pathology and MG subgroups
- MG subgroups are defined by antibody, symptoms, age at onset and thymus pathology, and treatment (including thymectomy) is chosen by subgroup [Gilhus2019].
- Thymic changes have different roles by subtype: inflammatory changes in early-onset MG, neoplastic changes in thymoma-associated MG, and (probably) age-related changes in late-onset MG [Marx2013].
- MGTX authors state that thymoma is present in about 10% of MG patients, and that up to 70% of the remaining patients have hyperplastic thymic changes not seen in healthy people [Wolfe2016].
- Early-onset MG (onset <50) is associated with thymic hyperplasia and HLA-DR3/8.1 ancestral haplotype. Patients with thymic atrophy (mainly late-onset) had a worse prognosis than those with hyperplasia in one study [Romi2011].
- On digital pathology, the ratio of thymic tissue to fat/stroma was higher in early-onset than late-onset MG (p=8.7e-07). The number of ectopic germinal centres correlated with AChR seropositivity [Sarkkinen2022].
- MGTX set standard operating procedures for thymus pathology, including grading of lymphofollicular hyperplasia and morphometric assessment of involution [Marx2012].
- Histology in MGTX thymectomy specimens was heterogeneous, and few patients were corticosteroid-naïve. The authors argue that a single intrathymic pathogenesis model may not fit all early-onset MG [Weis2018].
- Thymoma share of MG: about 15% [Romi2011]. About 10-20% [Okuno2025]. A pooled estimate of 21% (95% CI 20-22%) across 49 studies, higher in surgical series and more frequent in men and in onset after 40 [Mao2012].
- MG share of thymoma: about half of patients with cortical (WHO type B) thymomas develop MG [Romi2011]. MG is the most common autoimmune disease with thymoma, at 30-44% [Okuno2025].
- MG frequency by WHO type: rare in type A. Reported ranges are 24-71% for B2, 7-70% for B1 and 25-65% for B3 [Okuno2025]. MG-associated thymomas are usually cortical (type B) [Romi2011].
- Thymoma MG affects men and women equally, peaks at about age 50, and has a non-limb onset profile (bulbar, ocular, neck, respiratory) [Romi2011].
- Paraneoplastic antibodies: titin antibodies occur in about 95% and RyR antibodies in about 70% of thymoma-MG patients, vs 58% and 14% in late-onset MG and 10% / 0% in early-onset MG. They are linked to more severe disease [Romi2011].
- Titin and RyR antibodies in an MG patient younger than 60 strongly suggest thymoma, and their absence at any age strongly argues against it [Romi2011].
- Masaoka staging (1981). Stage I: encapsulated, no capsular invasion. Stage II: macroscopic invasion into surrounding fat or mediastinal pleura, or microscopic capsular invasion. Stage III: invasion of neighbouring organs. Stage IVa: pleural or pericardial dissemination. Stage IVb: lymphogenous or haematogenous metastasis. Five-year survival was 92.6%, 85.7%, 69.6% and 50% for stages I-IV [Masaoka1981].
- Koga et al. (1994) modified the Masaoka system to rebalance stages I and II (the Masaoka-Koga system). Masaoka-Koga is now used alongside TNM staging, and histology follows WHO types A, AB, B1, B2 and B3 [Koga1994; Okuno2025].
- After resection of thymoma in patients without MG, pooled incidence of new postoperative MG was 8% (13 cohorts, 2,448 patients). Risk factors were preoperative AChR antibodies (RR 5.53), open thymectomy (RR 1.84), non-R0 resection (RR 1.87), WHO type B (RR 1.80) and postoperative inflammation (RR 1.63) [Tang2023].
- In one series, 10 of 33 AChR-antibody-positive thymoma patients without MG developed post-thymectomy MG (3 to 2,859 days after surgery), vs 0 of 90 seronegative patients [Yamada2015].

### History
- Ernst Ferdinand Sauerbruch performed a transcervical thymectomy in 1911 on a 19-year-old woman with MG, operating for thyrotoxicosis [Singh2018]. German reviews credit Sauerbruch's thymectomy in an MG patient as preceding Blalock [Overhaus2007]. The 1912/1913 case report (often cited as Schumacher & Roth, Mitt Grenzgeb Med Chir) was not opened and is not indexed (unverified). Sources disagree on whether the resection was partial or total.
- Alfred Blalock removed a thymic tumour by partial sternotomy in a patient with MG in 1936 [Singh2018]. He reported it in Annals of Surgery in 1939 [Blalock1939].
- In 1941, Blalock and colleagues published a planned-thymectomy series in non-thymomatous MG [Blalock1941; Singh2018]. MGTX describes this first report as 6 operated patients, 3 of whom had a favourable response [Wolfe2016].
- Kirschner and Osserman (1969) promoted transcervical total thymectomy as their procedure of choice (21 patients), and Cooper et al. (1988) described an improved transcervical technique [Singh2018].
- Before MGTX, retrospective studies showed widely varying improvement rates. Some suggested benefit was confined to the first few years and that surgical and medical patients converged after about 5 years [Wolfe2016].
- The 2000 AAN practice parameter reviewed 21 cohorts, found numerous methodological flaws, and could recommend thymectomy for non-thymomatous MG only as a treatment option, calling for an RCT [Wolfe2016; Wolfe2019; Gronseth2000].
- Barohn recalls that about 25 comparative articles were found among hundreds of thymectomy reports, all retrospective. Each used its own definitions of remission and improvement [Barohn2021].
- The 2013 Cochrane review found no RCTs of thymectomy in MG and called for one [Cea2013].
- MGTX authors cite a US database showing that hospital admissions for thymectomy in MG fell dramatically after 2000 [Wolfe2016]. MGTX (2016) was the first RCT to show benefit, about 75 years after Blalock [Wolfe2016; Wolfe2019].
