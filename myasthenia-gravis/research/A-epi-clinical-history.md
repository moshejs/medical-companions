# A. Epidemiology, clinical picture, subtypes & history
This pass covers how common myasthenia gravis (MG) is (pooled, regional and Israeli figures, time trends), age and sex patterns, the antibody and clinical subgroups, onset symptoms and fluctuation, ocular-to-generalized conversion, course and prognosis (maximal severity, mortality, crisis overview, remission, fatigue and quality of life), associated autoimmunity, family risk and HLA, checkpoint-inhibitor MG, dossier codes, and a history timeline from Willis (1672) to the newest FDA approvals (2026). Every journal source below was validated through PubMed, Europe PMC or Crossref with the helper. Numbers come from abstracts. Where full text was open (Dresser 2021, Bubuioc 2021, Juel 2007, Estephan 2022, Yoshikawa 2022, the 2025 Israeli national guideline, Deymeer's history review), numbers were also taken from the text. Paywalled and not read in full: Gilhus 2019 (Nat Rev Dis Primers), Gilhus & Verschuuren 2015, Grob 2008, Hughes 2005, Keesey 2004, Punga 2022. For these only abstract-level statements are used. Not readable: the scanned full text of Oosterhuis 1989, the Blalock 1939 PDF, and Jolly's 1895 paper (not indexed in PubMed or Crossref), so Jolly's contributions are cited through Deymeer's review. No population-based Israeli prevalence study was found; the Israeli guideline itself notes "limited availability of published Israeli epidemiological data". The SNOMED CT code was confirmed through NCBI MedGen, because the SNOMED browser blocks automated access. The crisis figures here are an overview only; pass G covers crisis in depth.

## Facts
### Prevalence and incidence: pooled and global figures
- A 2010 systematic review of 55 population-based studies (1950-2007; 1.7 billion person-years) gave a pooled incidence of 5.3 per million person-years (95% CI 4.4-6.1; range 1.7-21.3) and a pooled prevalence of 77.7 per million (95% CI 64.0-94.3; range 15-179). Mortality ranged from 0.1 to 0.9 per million person-years. [Carr2010]
- The same review gave a pooled incidence for AChR-antibody MG of 7.3 per million person-years (range 4.3-18.0) and a MuSK-MG incidence range of 0.1-0.32 per million person-years. Frequency rose with year of study and with study quality, which the authors read mainly as better case ascertainment. [Carr2010]
- A 2025 systematic review of 94 studies (1952-2022) found prevalence ranging from 20 to 475 per million: mean 173.3 (95% CI 129.7-215.5), median 129.6. Incidence ranged from 2.3 to 61.3 per million person-years: mean 15.7 (95% CI 11.5-19.9), median 13.3. Mean mortality was 1.4 per million person-years (95% CI 0.8-2.1). [Sciancalepore2025]
- A 2021 meta-analysis of 63 studies estimated a global prevalence of 12.4 per 100,000 (95% CI 10.6-14.5). [Salari2021]
- MedlinePlus Genetics states that MG "affects about 20 per 100,000 people worldwide" and says prevalence has risen in recent decades. A 2007 review also put recent prevalence rates at about 20/100,000. [MedlinePlusMG2026; Juel2007]
- In the past 5-10 years, studies worldwide suggest an incidence of AChR-antibody MG of up to 29 per million per year. MuSK-MG and Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome are each about 20 times less common. [Punga2022]
- A 2021 review puts incidence at 4.1-30 per million person-years and prevalence at 150-200 per million. [Dresser2021]
- Disagreement on scale: pooled prevalence estimates differ by more than twofold, from 77.7 per million [Carr2010] through 124 per million (12.4/100,000) [Salari2021] and about 200 per million (20/100,000) [MedlinePlusMG2026] to a mean of 173.3 per million [Sciancalepore2025]. Incidence summaries also vary: 5.3 per million person-years [Carr2010] vs a mean of 15.7 [Sciancalepore2025]. The Israeli guideline quotes an incidence of 0.3-2.8 per 100,000 (3-28 per million) and a global total of about 700,000 people. Most of the spread reflects case definition (claims, laboratory or clinical) and study period. [Shelly2025; Carr2010; Sciancalepore2025]
- Animation anchor, prevalence over time: before 1934, about 1 in 200,000; after anticholinesterases arrived in 1934, about 1 in 20,000; after AChR antibodies were discovered, about 1 in 17,000. Dresser dates the antibody discovery to 1969. [Dresser2021; Bubuioc2021]
- The number of MG patients "has more than doubled in the last 20 years", mainly because incidence has risen in the elderly. Better diagnosis, better treatment and longer life are the likely reasons. [Bubuioc2021]

### Prevalence and incidence by region
- United States, 2021 (claims and EHR data): diagnosed prevalence 37.0 per 100,000 and incidence 3.1 per 100,000 per year. Both are higher than reports from the 1980s and early 2000s. [Rodrigues2024]
- United States, Olmsted County, Minnesota (Rochester Epidemiology Project, 1990-2017): adult MG incidence 2.20 per 100,000 per year. Mean age at diagnosis was 59 years and 62% of patients were male. [Hendricks2019]
- An older US estimate was about 60,000 patients. Europe was estimated at 56,000-123,000 patients. [Bubuioc2021]
- Europe, selected national figures (tabulated in a 2021 review): Sweden 2006-2016, incidence 29 per million person-years and prevalence 36.1 per 100,000; Norway 13.8 and the Netherlands 16.7 per 100,000 (2010-2012); Spain (Osona) prevalence 32.89 per 100,000; Ireland 15.12 per 100,000; Hungary 17.42 per 100,000. [Bubuioc2021]
- Asia: Japan's estimated prevalence was 11.8 per 100,000 in 2006 (about 15,100 patients) [Murai2011] and 23.1 per 100,000 in 2017 (95% CI 20.5-25.6; about 29,210 patients) [Yoshikawa2022]. Korea reported 9.67-10.42 per 100,000 in 2010 and 12.99 in 2014. [Dresser2021]
- Animation anchor, Japan prevalence per 100,000 from nationwide surveys: 1973, 1.35; 1987, 5.1; 2006, 11.8; 2018, 23.1. [Yoshikawa2022]
- Israel (all four national AChR-antibody laboratories, 1994-2013): incidence of AChR-positive MG was 13.1 per million per year overall. It rose from 7.7 per million (1994-2003) to 18.5 per million (2004-2013) (P < .0001). The rise was mostly among older men: the male:female incidence ratio moved from 2:3.2 to 3:1.8, and mean age at diagnosis rose from 56.7 to 59.9 years. [Lotan2020]
- Israel has no published population-based prevalence figure. The national guideline lists the scarcity of Israeli epidemiological data as a challenge. The Lotan study counts only AChR-positive cases, so it misses seronegative MG. [Shelly2025; Lotan2020]
- An Israeli Clalit Health Services case-control study (machine-learning case definition) found 253 new adult MG cases in 2020-2022. COVID-19 infection was not significantly associated with new-onset MG (OR 1.67 at 180 days, 95% CI 0.98-2.84). The Pfizer-BioNTech vaccine was associated (OR 2.45 within 180 days, 95% CI 1.51-3.95). This is a single observational finding. [Kab2026]

### Age and sex
- Women and men show different onset patterns. In women, incidence is bimodal, with peaks around age 30 and 50. In men, incidence rises steadily with age and is highest between 60 and 89. [Dresser2021]
- The sex ratio changes with age. Before age 40, women outnumber men 3:1 [Dresser2021] or 7:3 [Juel2007]. In the fifth decade (ages 40-49) the sexes are equally affected. After 50, men slightly predominate (3:2). [Dresser2021; Juel2007]
- MedlinePlus says MG is most often diagnosed in women younger than 40 and men older than 60. A US inpatient study called MG "still a disease of young women and old men". [MedlinePlusMG2026; Alshekhlee2009]
- Age cut-off: early-onset MG means onset before 50 years and late-onset MG means onset at 50 or later. A 2026 meta-analysis proposes splitting the older group into intermediate-onset (50-64 years) and very-late-onset (65 years and older). [Dresser2021; Liampas2026]
- US data, 2021: prevalence and incidence were higher in women than men under 50, and higher in men than women aged 65 and over. [Rodrigues2024]
- Late-onset incidence is rising. In an Italian AChR-MG cohort (1,023 patients, onset 1970-2020, non-thymoma), median age at onset rose from 35 years in the 1970s to 65 years in 2010-2020. [Falso2026]
- Animation anchor, Japan, share of patients with onset at 50 or later: 28.8% (1987), 41.7% (2006), 66.1% (2018). Over the same surveys the male:female ratio moved from 1:2.1 (1973) to 1:1.15 (2018). [Yoshikawa2022]
- Elderly-onset MG (65 years and older) rose from 7.3% of Japanese patients in 1987 to 16.8% in 2006. [Murai2011]
- Compared with onset before 65, very-late-onset MG has more AChR-antibody positivity (OR 3.08), less double-seronegativity (OR 0.34) and less thymoma (OR 0.25). Female share does not differ significantly. [Liampas2026]
- Earliest reported onset is 1 year and oldest is 98 years. In MG overall, the most common onset age is 20-39 in women and 50-70 in men. [Bubuioc2021]

### Juvenile MG and ethnic differences
- Juvenile MG (onset before 18) is about 10% of cases overall and 10-15% in one US study. Pooled incidence is 1-5 per million person-years. [Dresser2021]
- US claims data, 2020: juvenile MG incidence was 5.9 per million person-years (commercial insurance) and 8.7 (Medicaid). Prevalence was 25.3 and 37.6 per million. Rates were higher in girls than boys and higher in Black than White children. [Zhou2025]
- East Asia: juvenile MG can be up to 50% of all MG cases in Asian series, mostly ocular. Taiwanese and Japanese studies report juvenile incidence of 3.7-8.9 per million person-years. [Dresser2021]
- Japan: infantile-onset MG (age 0-4) was 10.1% of cases in 1987, 7.0% in 2006 and 2.3% in 2018. In 2006, 80.6% of infantile-onset patients had the ocular form, against 26.4% in early-onset adults. Only about 50% of infantile-onset patients were AChR-positive, compared with nearly 90% of elderly-onset patients. [Murai2011; Yoshikawa2022]
- Ocular disease follows a U-shaped curve by onset age in Japan, with the low point in the 20s. Thymoma peaks with onset in the 50s and thymic hyperplasia with onset in the 20s-40s. [Murai2011]
- Juvenile-onset and especially infantile-onset ocular MG is more common in Asian populations, reaching up to 30% of patients, and has been linked to HLA-Bw46 and DR9. In a Japanese survey, 80.6% of infantile-onset children had the ocular form, compared with 14-30% of children in Europe and North America. Only about 50% of juvenile-onset patients in Asian series are AChR-positive. [Bubuioc2021]
- Generalization from ocular to generalized MG occurs in 20-25% of juvenile patients, compared with up to 80% in adults. [Dresser2021]
- African ancestry: MG is slightly more prevalent in people of African descent. In a South African study, Black patients more often had treatment-resistant ophthalmoplegia and ptosis, while White patients more often had refractory generalized disease. [Dresser2021; Bubuioc2021]
- Among US hospital admissions, annual MG incidence was higher in Black women (0.01 per 1,000) than in White women (0.009), White men (0.008) or Black men (0.007). [Alshekhlee2009]
- Israel: Ashkenazi Jewish patients had a higher frequency of ocular MG than non-Ashkenazi patients. [Bubuioc2021]

### Subgroups and their proportions
- MG is grouped by antibody, clinical distribution (ocular or generalized), age at onset and thymus pathology. The subgroups are early-onset, late-onset, thymoma-associated, MuSK, LRP4, antibody-negative and ocular. [Gilhus2019; Gilhus2015]
- AChR antibodies: found in about 80% of all MG patients [Dresser2021; Pevzner2012] and about 85% of generalized MG [MSDManual2026]. They were found in 87% of 71 patients in the first radioimmunoassay series (1976). [Lindstrom1976]
- About 20% of patients with generalized MG are AChR-seronegative. In 2001, Hoch found MuSK antibodies in 70% of them. [Hoch2001]
- MuSK-MG is 7-10% of all MG [Dresser2021] or 5-10% [Pevzner2012], and up to 85% of MuSK patients are women [Dresser2021]. Estimates of the MuSK share among AChR-seronegative patients disagree: 70% [Hoch2001], over 40% [Juel2007], and 30.8% (143/465) in a 2026 worldwide 46-centre series [Vincent2026].
- MuSK frequency follows latitude. Among seronegative patients in Europe and North America it ranges from 0% to 47% across centres, peaking near 40 degrees North. MuSK antibodies were not found in adult ocular MG (0/63). [Vincent2026]
- LRP4 antibodies are found in 2-50% of double-seronegative patients, depending on the series. LRP4-MG skews female, tends to start before 50 and is milder; about 20% remain ocular-only after 2 years. [Dresser2021]
- In the original LRP4 series, about 50% of 13 double-seronegative sera bound LRP4-transfected cells. [Pevzner2012; Higuchi2011]
- Thymoma: present in 10-20% of MG patients [Dresser2021] or about 10% [Juel2007; MSDManual2026]. A systematic review of 49 studies gave a pooled 21% (95% CI 20-22%), higher in surgical series, in men and with onset after 40. [Mao2012]
- Conversely, about 30% of people with thymoma develop MG, and about 50% have AChR antibodies without symptoms. [Dresser2021]
- Thymic hyperplasia is seen in about 65% of MG patients. [Juel2007; MSDManual2026]
- Japanese 2015-2017 cohort (n = 1,195): 26.4% of AChR-positive patients had thymoma (265/1003). Of the 1,189 patients in the four analysis groups, 738 were AChR-positive without thymoma, 265 AChR-positive with thymoma, 34 MuSK-positive and 152 double-negative. Median onset age was 64, 55, 49 and 47 years respectively. [Yoshikawa2022]
- Ocular vs generalized: in the US population-based Olmsted study, 51% (33/65) presented with purely ocular MG [Hendricks2019]. In Japan 36.8% were MGFA class I (ocular) in 2018 [Yoshikawa2022]. Ocular muscles are the only muscles ever affected in about 15% [MSDManual2026]. The definitions differ: share at onset, share at a survey date, or share that stays ocular.
- Ocular MG is usually defined as isolated eye-muscle involvement for 2 years or more. Over half of these patients have AChR antibodies. [Dresser2021]

### First symptoms and distribution of weakness
- Ocular weakness, with asymmetric ptosis and binocular diplopia, is the most common first presentation. Oropharyngeal (bulbar) or limb onset is less common. [Juel2007]
- About two-thirds of patients start with ocular symptoms [Estephan2022]. Another source says ocular muscles are affected first in 40% and eventually in 85% [MSDManual2026]. In nearly all patients the eye muscles or eyelid levator are involved at some point [Estephan2022].
- Animation anchor, initial symptoms in Japan 2015-2017 (AChR-positive without thymoma, n = 738): ptosis 74.9%, ophthalmoplegia (double vision) 47.4%, limb or trunk weakness 13.6%, dysarthria 13.3%, dysphagia 12.1%, neck weakness 5.8%, facial weakness 3.4%, chewing weakness 3.4%, dyspnea 2.6%. In MuSK-positive patients (n = 34): ptosis 44.1%, dysphagia 35.3%, limb or trunk 29.4%, neck 17.6%, dyspnea 8.8%. [Yoshikawa2022]
- Bulbar weakness (dysarthria or dysphonia, dysphagia, chewing weakness) is the main first symptom in up to 15% of cases, often without much eye involvement, and more often in the elderly. [Estephan2022]
- MuSK-MG: over 40% present with bulbar weakness, often with neck and respiratory involvement. About 30% present with ptosis or diplopia. Diurnal variation is less common. [Dresser2021]
- About 10% of patients develop head drop during the disease, linked to age over 60 and male sex in one centre. [Dresser2021]
- Limb weakness is usually symmetric and proximal, for example climbing stairs, rising from chairs or lifting the arms. [Dresser2021]
- The weakness is painless. Pupils are normal [Juel2007]. Sensation and deep tendon reflexes are normal [MSDManual2026]. Sensory nerves are not involved [Estephan2022].
- Signs on examination include ptosis that worsens with sustained upgaze, Cogan's lid twitch, frontalis overactivity raising the eyebrow, a "myasthenic snarl", nasal speech and tongue weakness. Chronic tongue weakness can cause atrophy with a triple furrow. [Juel2007; Estephan2022]

### Fatigability and fluctuation
- Weakness fluctuates from day to day and hour to hour and is generally worse later in the day. Sustained exercise and higher body temperature can increase it. [Juel2007]
- Weakness eases with rest and recurs with use [MSDManual2026]. It lessens in cooler temperatures [MSDManual2026]. An ice pack that improves ptosis by about 2 mm or more strongly suggests myasthenic eyelid weakness [Estephan2022].
- Symptoms can worsen with intercurrent illness (especially viral respiratory infections), thyroid disease, pregnancy, fever and drugs that impair neuromuscular transmission. [Juel2007]
- Willis (1672) described patients who were well in the morning and weak by noon, the first recorded account of diurnal fatigability. [Deymeer2021]

### Ocular-to-generalized conversion
- Most patients with ocular onset who go on to generalize do so within 2 years. Of those still ocular after 2 years, 90% stay ocular. [Dresser2021]
- Up to 80% of adults with ocular onset develop generalized symptoms, usually within 2 years [Dresser2021]. Another review puts it at nearly 85% within 3 years [Juel2007]. Both figures come from older cohorts treated largely without immunotherapy.
- In a retrospective study of 108 ocular-onset patients, only 15% of generalizations happened after more than 2 years of purely ocular disease. [Bever1983]
- Animation anchor, US population-based data: 55% (18/33) of ocular-onset patients generalized, at a median of 13 months (range 2-180). By 5 years, 67% of AChR-positive ocular patients had generalized, against 11% of seronegative patients (HR 8.2). A positive single-fibre EMG predicted 77% conversion at 5 years, against 18% when it was negative. [Hendricks2019]
- Animation anchor, steroid effect: at 2 years, 36% (13/36) of untreated ocular patients had generalized, against 7% (4/58) of prednisone-treated patients (OR 0.13). Abnormal AChR antibody levels raised the risk (OR 6.33). [Kupersmith2003]
- Disagreement: modern conversion estimates (about 50-55%) [Hendricks2019; MSDManual2026; Kupersmith2003] are well below older estimates (80-85%) [Dresser2021; Juel2007]. Possible reasons are early treatment and differences in referral and case definition.

### Course, maximal severity and remission
- The most severe weakness and the highest mortality occurred in the first 1-2 years of disease in 1,976 patients followed from 1940 to 2000. Many improved afterwards. [Grob2008]
- Maximum severity is reached within the first year in almost two-thirds of patients. [Juel2007]
- In an Amsterdam natural-history cohort (73 patients, 1926-1965, mostly treated only with anticholinesterases), 87% reached maximum severity within 7 years of onset. [Oosterhuis1989]
- Animation anchor, natural course (Oosterhuis, outcome in 1985): 22% in complete clinical remission, 18% considerably improved, 16% moderately improved, 16% unchanged, 2 patients worse and 29% dead. Patients with thymoma did worse. [Oosterhuis1989]
- Before steroids, about a quarter of patients had a complete or near-complete spontaneous remission, lasting on average 4.6 years and up to 17 years. Half of these remissions occurred in the first year. [Dresser2021]
- One proposed course for generalized MG has three phases: an active phase of about 7 years with relapses and remissions, an inactive phase of about 10 years, and a "burned-out" phase with fixed weakness and atrophy. [Juel2007]
- Animation anchor, modern remission by onset age (Italy, AChR-MG, onset 2000-2020): complete stable remission in 24% (25/104) of early-onset patients and 4% (8/192) of very-late-onset patients. "Minimal manifestations or better on 5 mg/day prednisone or less" was reached by 60% and 48% respectively. [Falso2026]
- Guidelines set the treatment goal as remission or minimal manifestation status with near-normal function and quality of life. [Gilhus2019; Shelly2025]

### Mortality: historical vs modern
- Before immunomodulators were widely used, MG mortality was about 30% [Juel2007]. In 1934, 70% of the few diagnosed patients died of respiratory failure or pneumonia within two years [Bubuioc2021].
- Animation anchor: mortality fell from about 40% in the early 1960s to about 5% in the 1970s, largely because of positive-pressure ventilation and intensive care units. [Deymeer2021]
- Contemporary disease-specific mortality is under 5% and life expectancy is nearly normal [Juel2007]. Other reviews give 5-9%, slightly higher in men (14%) than women (11%) [Dresser2021; Bubuioc2021]. Pooled population mortality is 0.1-0.9 [Carr2010] or a mean of 1.4 [Sciancalepore2025] per million person-years.
- US inpatients 2000-2005 (5,502 admissions): in-hospital mortality was 2.2% overall and 4.47% in MG crisis. Older age (adjusted OR 9.28) and respiratory failure (OR 3.58) predicted death. [Alshekhlee2009]
- Israel (Rambam, 2000-2023, 436 patients): mortality was 14% at 5 years and 21% at 10 years, higher than matched controls. Mean age at death was below national life expectancy: 78.3 vs 81.6 years in men and 76.5 vs 85.2 in women. Infections linked to chronic steroid use were the main cause of death. This is a single tertiary-centre result. [Khateb2025]

### Myasthenic crisis (overview; see pass G)
- Lifetime risk of crisis is estimated at 15-20% of AChR-MG patients [Dresser2021; MSDManual2026] or about 20%, usually within the first year [Juel2007]. MedlinePlus gives about 10% [MedlinePlusMG2026]. In Japanese surveys the share with a history of crisis fell from 16.0% (1973) to 6.9% (2018) [Yoshikawa2022].
- In a 1983-1994 series of 73 crisis episodes, median time from symptom onset to first crisis was 8 months. Infection was the most common trigger (38%). Mortality was 4% per episode. [Thomas1997]

### Quality of life, fatigue and mood
- Fatigue, as distinct from muscle fatigability, affects about 60% of patients in large registry studies. It is linked to disease severity, depression and physical inactivity. [Campman2026]
- Pooled prevalence across 38 studies was 36% for depression (95% CI 28-45%) and 33% for anxiety (95% CI 25-42%). [Nadali2023]
- Across 38 studies from Europe, the Middle East and Africa, quality of life in generalized MG was worse than in healthy controls and in ocular MG, and it fell further as severity increased. [McCallion2024]
- Comorbidity is frequent, especially in elderly patients, and active physical training is encouraged. [Gilhus2019]

### Associated autoimmune disease, family risk and HLA
- In a 2011 systematic review of 25 studies, 13% of MG patients had another autoimmune disease (95% CI 12-14%). Autoimmune thyroid disease was the most common, and coexisting autoimmunity was more frequent in women and AChR-positive patients. [Mao2011]
- Autoimmune thyroiditis occurs in about 10% of MG patients. [Bubuioc2021]
- The most common accompanying autoimmune diseases are autoimmune thyroid disease, systemic lupus erythematosus and rheumatoid arthritis. [Nacu2015]
- Natural-history cohort: associated autoimmune diseases were found in 25% of patients. [Oosterhuis1989]
- North America (1,032 AChR-positive patients): 5.6% reported a family history of MG. 26.6% of patients and 28.4% of their relatives had an autoimmune disease. [Green2020]
- Israel (Rabin, 281 patients): 5.7% had familial MG. Familial cases started younger (median 44.5 vs 58 years) and were more often severe at onset (MGFA IV-V, 43.8% vs 15%). [Hellmann2025]
- Reported familial rates vary: 3-5% (MedlinePlus), 3.8-7.1% (Bubuioc review), 5.6% (Green) and 5.7% (Hellmann). Concordance is about 35.5% in identical twins and 4-5% in non-identical twins. [MedlinePlusMG2026; Bubuioc2021; Green2020; Hellmann2025; Ramanujam2011]
- HLA: early-onset MG in people of European descent is associated with the A1-B8-DR3 haplotype (AH8.1, HLA-B*08:01). Late-onset MG is associated with other markers, such as HLA-DRB1*15:01 in Norwegian patients over 60. MuSK-MG is associated with HLA-DQB1*05 and DRB1*14/*16. Thymoma-associated MG shows no consistent HLA association. [Dresser2021; Bubuioc2021]
- Thymic hyperplasia mainly affects women of childbearing age (female:male ratio 9:1). [Bubuioc2021]
- In an Israeli Clalit cohort (1,558 MG patients vs 4,674 controls), MG patients had more cancers outside the thymus, both before diagnosis (OR 1.95) and after it (HR 1.56). Immunosuppressive treatment did not raise the risk. [Pardo2026]

### Immune-checkpoint-inhibitor-associated MG
- Japanese post-marketing data (2014-2016): MG developed in 0.12% of nivolumab-treated patients (12/9,869) and in none of 408 ipilimumab patients. Of the 12 MG cases, 4 had myositis and 3 had myocarditis. Creatine kinase averaged 4,799 IU/L and 2 patients died. [Suzuki2017]
- A review of 65 cases found MG onset a median 4 weeks (range 1-16) after starting the checkpoint inhibitor. Median age was 73 and 82% had received anti-PD-1 therapy. Concurrent myositis occurred in 37%, respiratory failure in 45%, and death in 38% (23% from MG). 20% had MG before the drug was started. [Safa2019]
- A meta-analysis of 657 trials (91,340 participants) found any-grade neurological adverse events in 0.24%. Mortality reached 38% in the MG-myositis-myocarditis overlap ("triple M"). [Nielsen2025]

### History timeline (animation anchors)
- 1672: Thomas Willis gives the first recognisable account, in Latin, of patients well in the morning and fatigued by noon, including a woman whose speech failed after long talking. The account went largely unnoticed until 1903. [Hughes2005; Deymeer2021]
- 1877: Samuel Wilks gives what is usually called the first modern description. Deymeer notes an earlier detailed case by Hérard (1868). [Hughes2005; Deymeer2021]
- 1879: Wilhelm Erb describes three patients with oculobulbar weakness, remissions and exacerbations. [Erb1879; Deymeer2021]
- 1893: Samuel Goldflam reviews the literature, adds three cases and sets out diagnostic and prognostic features. The disease was briefly called "Erb-Goldflam syndrome". [Goldflam1893; Deymeer2021; Hughes2005]
- 1895: Friedrich Jolly proposes the name "myasthenia gravis pseudoparalytica", later shortened to "myasthenia gravis". He shows that repeated faradic (electrical) stimulation causes a progressive decline in muscle response, the forerunner of repetitive nerve stimulation. He suggests physostigmine but does not pursue it because of toxicity. A dating of the name to a December 1894 lecture appears in web sources (unverified). [Deymeer2021]
- 1900: Campbell and Bramwell publish a major review, "Myasthenia gravis", in Brain (60 pages). They held that rest was the best remedy. [Campbell1900; Deymeer2021]
- 1934: Mary Walker reports in the Lancet that physostigmine produced dramatic improvement. She reported prostigmine (neostigmine) in 1935. [Walker1934; Deymeer2021; Keesey2004]
- 1939: Blalock and colleagues report removing a thymic tumour from a patient with MG (Ann Surg). Deymeer dates the operation to 1936; Blalock operated on about 20 patients without thymoma in 1941-1944. Timelines differ between operation date (1936) and publication date (1939). [Blalock1939; Deymeer2021]
- 1960: John Simpson (Edinburgh) proposes that MG is autoimmune, caused by antibodies to the endplate receptor, and suggests a role for the thymus. Nastuk and colleagues report changes in serum complement in the same period. [Simpson1960; Deymeer2021]
- 1973: Patrick and Lindstrom immunise rabbits with purified eel AChR and produce flaccid paralysis that reverses with anticholinesterases. This is the experimental autoimmune MG (EAMG) model. [Patrick1973]
- 1973: Fambrough, Drachman and Satyamurti show, using labelled alpha-bungarotoxin, that AChR numbers at MG neuromuscular junctions are reduced compared with controls. [Fambrough1973]
- 1976: Lindstrom and colleagues detect anti-AChR antibodies in 87% of 71 MG patients and in none of 175 controls, which established the diagnostic assay. [Lindstrom1976]
- Early 1960s to 1970s: mortality falls from about 40% to about 5% with ventilation and intensive care. [Deymeer2021]
- 2001: Hoch and colleagues discover MuSK antibodies in 70% of AChR-seronegative generalized MG. [Hoch2001]
- 2011: Higuchi and colleagues report LRP4 antibodies, mainly IgG1, in AChR-negative MG [Higuchi2011]. Pevzner and colleagues report LRP4 antibodies in about 50% of 13 double-seronegative sera (published 2012) [Pevzner2012].
- 23 Oct 2017: FDA approves eculizumab (Soliris) for adults with AChR-antibody-positive generalized MG, supplement BLA 125166/S-422. It was the first complement inhibitor approved for MG. [FDASoliris2017]
- 17 Dec 2021: FDA approves efgartigimod (Vyvgart) for AChR-antibody-positive generalized MG in adults, the first FcRn blocker. [FDAVyvgart2021]
- 28 Apr 2022: ravulizumab (Ultomiris) approved for AChR-positive generalized MG in adults, the first long-acting C5 inhibitor (trade-press report). [NeurologyLive2022]
- 26 Jun 2023: rozanolixizumab (Rystiggo) approved for adults with AChR- or MuSK-antibody-positive generalized MG. 17 Oct 2023: zilucoplan (Zilbrysq) approved for AChR-positive generalized MG. [FDANovel2023]
- 29 Apr 2025: nipocalimab (Imaavy) approved "to treat generalized myasthenia gravis". [FDANovel2025]
- 8 May 2026: FDA expands efgartigimod (Vyvgart and Vyvgart Hytrulo) to adults with AChR-antibody-seronegative generalized MG, based on ADAPT SERON (n = 119; NCT06298552) (trade-press report). [NeurologyLive2026]
- Israel: efgartigimod (2023) and nipocalimab (2025) were added as fifth-line options for AChR-positive MG. [Shelly2025]
