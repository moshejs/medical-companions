## Findings

### biologics · FDA approval of gefurulimab (Klygefa)
- The FDA approved Klygefa (gefurulimab-gaks; BLA 761488, Alexion) on 13 November 2026 for the treatment of generalised myasthenia gravis in adults who are anti-AChR antibody positive (Established: regulatory record) [[FDAKlygefa2026]].
- Label dose, subcutaneous, by body weight: below 80 kg, 600 mg on day 1, then 300 mg once weekly from day 8; 80 kg or more, 900 mg on day 1, then 600 mg once weekly. Intended for self-administration or injection by a caregiver (prefilled syringe or autoinjector, 300 mg/2 mL). A supplemental 300 mg dose follows plasma exchange or plasmapheresis [[FDAKlygefa2026]].
- Boxed warning: serious meningococcal infections. Vaccination against serogroups A, C, W, Y and B at least 2 weeks before the first dose; available only through the KLYGEFA REMS; the patient safety card is carried during treatment and for 3 months after it [[FDAKlygefa2026]].
- Label Clinical Studies (Study 1, NCT05556096; 131 vs 129): MG-ADL −4.2 vs −2.6, difference −1.6 (95% CI −2.4 to −0.8); QMG −4.5 vs −2.4, difference −2.1 (−3.1 to −1.1); the same as the published PREVAIL values [[FDAKlygefa2026,Gwathmey2026]]. Label responder figures differ slightly from the paper (MG-ADL ≥3 points 65.5% vs 52.2%; QMG ≥5 points 46.3% vs 25.2%); the site keeps the paper's figures.
- Label adverse reactions (at least 5% and more than placebo): injection-site reactions 12% vs 3%, back pain 8% vs 2%, vomiting 5% vs 1% [[FDAKlygefa2026]].
- Label drug interaction: FcRn inhibitors that lower serum albumin may lower gefurulimab exposure and effectiveness (gefurulimab binds albumin) [[FDAKlygefa2026]]. Not added to the site in this pass.
- This replaces "no FDA decision was found" and "no label in our sources" for gefurulimab.

### Exam hooks
- Gefurulimab (Klygefa): a once-weekly, self-injected C5 inhibitor for AChR-antibody-positive adults; like every C5 inhibitor it carries a boxed warning for meningococcal infection and a REMS [[FDAKlygefa2026]].

### In plain words
- A fourth complement-blocking medicine for myasthenia gravis is now approved in the US. It is injected under the skin once a week at home, and it needs the same meningococcal vaccines first as the other medicines of its kind [[FDAKlygefa2026]].
