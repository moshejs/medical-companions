# I. Research toward a cure

Scope: this file reports what the published literature, trial registries and regulatory/company documents say (as of 2026-10-20) about interventions aimed at durable, treatment-free remission in myasthenia gravis (MG), organised along the immune chain the module animates: thymus -> T cell -> B cell -> plasma cell -> antibody -> complement -> neuromuscular junction. Each route carries an evidence label (Established / Early evidence / Hypothesis) with the reason for the label stated in the bullets. Several of the most recent results exist only as conference presentations or company press releases, not peer-reviewed papers: the telitacicept China phase 3, the KYSA-6 (KYV-101/miv-cel) phase 2 interim and 52-week data, the RESET-MG (rese-cel/CABA-201) phase 1/2 data, the MuSK-CAART phase 1 outcome, and the batoclimab phase 3. Those are flagged as press/conference sources and their numbers are marked where sources disagree. Publisher full texts for Haghikia 2023 (Lancet Neurol correspondence), Schneider-Gold 2017 (bortezomib in MuSK-MG) and the Jaretzki 2000 MGFA task-force paper were not retrievable through automated access, so facts from them are limited to what the registry record, press release or a quoting open-access source states, and are marked. Nothing here is phrased as guidance for a reader.

## Facts

### What would count as a cure: definitions and the baseline
- The MGFA clinical research standards define Post-Intervention Status categories including Complete Stable Remission, Pharmacologic Remission and Minimal Manifestations; these categories are the common currency for "remission" claims in MG trials [Jaretzki2000].
- The 2020 international consensus guidance states the treatment goal as Minimal Manifestation Status or better, with no more than grade 1 CTCAE medication side effects, and defines refractory MG as Post-Intervention Status unchanged or worse after corticosteroids plus at least two other immunosuppressive agents at adequate dose and duration [Narayanaswami2021].
- In that guidance, Minimal Manifestation Status is "no symptoms or functional limitations from MG but some weakness on examination of some muscles", and remission is "no symptoms or signs of MG", excluding patients taking daily cholinesterase inhibitors with symptomatic benefit [Narayanaswami2021].
- A 2026 review proposes an explicit cure definition for MG: complete remission without ongoing treatment, plus a high probability of success, low relapse risk, repeatability if relapse occurs, low and proportionate toxicity, and cost-effectiveness [ElWahsh2026].
- The same review states that thymectomy is the only currently available, widely applied, sometimes curative treatment for AChR-antibody-positive MG, citing an MGTX post hoc steroid-free remission rate of 52% after thymectomy, which it puts at roughly one third of the whole population above the background remission rate [ElWahsh2026].
- The same review argues that recently approved therapies are highly effective but are "largely chronic continuous suppressive strategies at significant cost", and that antigen-specific approaches (antigen-bait, anti-idiotype, tolerogenic) have not yet been demonstrated in humans with spontaneously acquired MG [ElWahsh2026].
- Drug-free remission is rare under conventional care: in a 421-patient single-centre cohort with median disease duration about 10 years, maintainable complete stable remission (all MG treatment stopped, symptom-free for at least 1 year to last follow-up) was reached by 12.2% (20/164) after thymectomy versus 4.3% (11/257) with conservative treatment (p=0.002) [Zhang2026].
- In that cohort thymectomy was an independent predictor of maintainable complete stable remission overall (HR 3.40, 95% CI 1.61-7.19, p=0.001) and in non-thymomatous MG (HR 5.56, 95% CI 2.35-13.2), with the strongest effect in early-onset MG (HR 4.61, 95% CI 1.83-11.63) [Zhang2026].
- Background on the thymus route: in MGTX (n=126 randomised 2006-2012, 36 sites), extended transsternal thymectomy plus prednisone gave a lower 3-year time-weighted average QMG than prednisone alone (6.15 vs 8.99, p<0.001) and a lower average alternate-day prednisone dose (44 mg vs 60 mg, p<0.001), with fewer patients needing azathioprine (17% vs 48%) and fewer hospitalised for exacerbation (9% vs 37%) [Wolfe2016].
- MG is described in the standard reference review as an antibody-mediated disorder of the neuromuscular junction in which thymic pathology (hyperplasia with germinal centres in early-onset disease, thymoma in about 10-15%) is part of the disease process, which is why the thymus is the first cut point in the chain [Gilhus2019].
- Evidence label, thymus/thymectomy: **Established** for improving disease course and steroid requirement (one randomised controlled trial with blinded QMG rating plus consistent cohort data) [Wolfe2016; Zhang2026], but only partially curative: even with thymectomy, maintained drug-free remission is reached by a minority [Zhang2026; ElWahsh2026].

### CAR-T cell therapy: anti-CD19
- Mechanism: autologous T cells are engineered with a chimeric antigen receptor against CD19 and, after lymphodepletion, expand in vivo and deplete CD19+ B-lineage cells more deeply and into tissue than antibody-mediated depletion, with the aim of an "immune reset" when naive B cells repopulate [Haghikia2024].
- First MG case: a single patient with severe treatment-refractory AChR-antibody-positive generalised MG was treated with KYV-101 (fully human anti-CD19 CAR-T) on a named-patient basis and reported in Lancet Neurology correspondence in 2023 [Haghikia2023].
- Per the sponsor's press release on that case, the patient was 33 years old, had about a 70% reduction in anti-AChR autoantibodies, had B cells eliminated, showed improved muscle strength and reduced fatigue, and had no CAR-T-related adverse events including no cytokine release syndrome (CRS) and no immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) over 60 days of follow-up; MG-ADL and QMG values were not given [KyvernaPR2023]. The treating centre's press release gives the patient's age as 34 and the infusion as May 2023 (so the age differs between the two non-journal sources) [UMMagdeburg2023].
- Long-term German case series (Hegelmaier 2026, Cell Rep Med): three patients with severe treatment-resistant MG, one with concomitant rheumatoid arthritis, received autologous fully human anti-CD19 CAR-T cells; all three achieved rapid sustained clinical remission for at least 19 months and discontinued MG-specific immunotherapy [Hegelmaier2026].
- In that series B-cell depletion was profound and treatment-related adverse events were transient and manageable, but anti-AChR autoantibody titres remained detectable despite clinical remission, which separates clinical remission from serological cure [Hegelmaier2026].
- Registrational trial: KYSA-6 (NCT06193889) is a phase 2/3 open-label randomised controlled multicentre study of KYV-101 (anti-CD19 CAR-T, given after a standard lymphodepletion regimen) versus ongoing standard-of-care immunosuppression in generalised MG; planned enrolment 66, start 2024-08-28, primary completion estimated 2027-09, co-primary endpoints MG-ADL and QMG change from baseline at 24 weeks plus adverse events to 18 months [CTKYSA6].
- Phase 2 interim results for KYSA-6 were presented at AANEM 2025 and extended at AAN 2026 (data cut 2026-02-25, seven patients treated). Trade coverage of the 52-week data reports mean reductions at 24 weeks of 8.3 points MG-ADL and 11.7 points QMG, minimal symptom expression (MG-ADL 0-1) in 57% at last follow-up, all 7 free of standard immunotherapies at 24 weeks and 6/7 (86%) still off immunosuppressants at last follow-up, with improvements maintained in the 5 patients reaching 1 year [NeurologyLiveKYV2026].
- The two trade reports of the same KYSA-6 dataset disagree on the 24-week effect size (one gives MG-ADL -8.0 and QMG -7.7, the other MG-ADL -8.3 and QMG -11.7), so these numbers are only as reliable as conference reporting and are not from a peer-reviewed paper (unverified against a primary publication) [NeurologyLiveKYV2026].
- Reported KYSA-6 safety: no high-grade CRS, no ICANS, no haemophagocytic-lymphohistiocytosis-like syndrome; one serious adverse event of grade 4 neutropenia that improved with supportive care and was grade 1 at data cutoff [NeurologyLiveKYV2026].
- A second CD19 CAR-T product, rese-cel (CABA-201), is in RESET-MG (NCT06359041, phase 1/2, start 2024-12-17, estimated completion 2029-09) [CTRESETMG]. Conference coverage from AANEM 2026 reports 13 patients treated (7 AChR-positive, 6 AChR-negative) at 1x10^6 cells/kg as a single infusion after standard preconditioning; 10/13 had meaningful MG-ADL improvement while off immunomodulators; mean MG-ADL improvement in patients without rescue therapy was 7.4 points at week 12 (n=11) and 9.8 points at week 24 (n=4); B-cell depletion in all 13 by day 15 with reconstitution at a median 85 days (IQR 56-111); low-grade CRS in 2/13; follow-up 8-44 weeks [NeurologyLiveRESET2026].
- Cross-disease anchor for the CD19 CAR-T "reset" concept: in 15 patients with SLE, idiopathic inflammatory myositis or systemic sclerosis given a single CD19 CAR-T infusion after fludarabine/cyclophosphamide, median follow-up 15 months, mean B-cell aplasia lasted 112 (SD 47) days, all patients stopped immunosuppressive therapy, grade 1 CRS occurred in 10 patients, and there was one each of grade 2 CRS, grade 1 ICANS and pneumonia requiring hospitalisation [Mueller2024].
- Paediatric anchor: in eight children with refractory autoimmune disease given 1x10^6/kg point-of-care anti-CD19 CAR-T after lymphodepletion, grade 1 CRS occurred in six and grade 1 ICANS in one, late haematotoxicity was grade 1 in two, no severe infections occurred, and all improved over a median 16.5 months (range 9-24) [Becilli2026].
- Evidence label, anti-CD19 CAR-T in MG: **Early evidence**. Consistent, large effect sizes with drug-free remission in small open series and an open-label randomised phase 2/3 still enrolling; no completed blinded randomised MG trial, denominators in single digits, and the key efficacy numbers published only in conference form [Hegelmaier2026; NeurologyLiveKYV2026; CTKYSA6].

### CAR-T cell therapy: mRNA anti-BCMA (Descartes-08)
- Mechanism: autologous T cells are transiently engineered with mRNA encoding an anti-BCMA CAR (rCAR-T), targeting BCMA-expressing plasmablasts and plasma cells. Because the receptor is not genomically integrated, the therapy is given as repeated outpatient infusions without lymphodepletion chemotherapy [Granit2023].
- Phase 1b/2a (MG-001, NCT04146051, 8 US sites, 14 participants enrolled: 3 in phase 1b, 11 in phase 2a): no dose-limiting toxicity, no CRS and no neurotoxicity; common adverse events were headache (6/14), nausea (5/14), fever (4/14) and vomiting (3/14), all resolving within 24 h; fevers were not accompanied by rises in IL-6, IL-2 or TNF [Granit2023].
- MG-001 efficacy, baseline to week 12: MG-ADL -6 (95% CI -9 to -3), QMG -7 (-11 to -3), MG Composite -14 (-19 to -9), MG-QOL15r -9 (-15 to -3); median follow-up in part 2 was 5 months (range 3-9) [Granit2023].
- Durability follow-up of the seven MG-001 participants who received six weekly infusions: all seven had clinically meaningful improvement at month 3 without significant toxicity and still at month 9; 5/7 maintained the response at month 12; three patients who lost clinical effect were retreated, and two of those had rapid improvement to minimal symptom expression by week 8 maintained through 12 months of retreatment follow-up [Chahin2025].
- Phase 2b (same NCT04146051, randomised double-blind placebo-controlled, n=26: 15 Descartes-08, 11 placebo, six once-weekly intravenous infusions over 6 weeks): the primary endpoint, a >=5-point MG Composite improvement at month 3, was met in 66.7% (10/15) versus 27.3% (3/11) placebo (p=0.0472), and in AChR-antibody-positive patients 63.6% (7/11) versus 12.5% (1/8) (p=0.0258) [Vu2026].
- Phase 2b secondary outcomes: in the Descartes-08 arm, mean changes from baseline at month 4 were MG Composite -7.1, MG-ADL -5.5 and QMG -4.8; 83.0% had a sustained clinically meaningful response at month 12; 33.0% reached minimum symptom expression (MG-ADL <=1) by month 6 sustained through month 12, and among biologic-naive patients 55.6% reached minimum symptom expression by month 6 and maintained it through month 12 without additional treatment [Vu2026].
- Phase 2b safety: infusion-related reactions were the most common adverse event (Descartes-08 80.0%, 16/20; placebo 56.3%, 9/16); the therapy was given in an outpatient setting without lymphodepletion [Vu2026].
- Prespecified biomarker analysis of the phase 2b trial reports decreased BCMA+ plasma cell and plasmacytoid dendritic cell activity, falls in disease-associated cytokines including IL-6, altered circulating self-reactive antibody and T-cell receptor repertoires, and no decline in vaccine-specific antibodies and no hypogammaglobulinaemia, which the authors describe as a "precision retuning of self-reactivity" without general immunosuppression [Fedak2026].
- A phase 3 trial, AURORA (NCT06799247), is a randomised double-blind placebo-controlled study of Descartes-08 in generalised MG with planned enrolment 128, start 2025-05-06, primary outcome MG-ADL at 4 months, primary completion estimated 2026-12-30 [CTAURORA]; the registry lists it as active, not recruiting as of the date read.
- Evidence label, mRNA anti-BCMA CAR-T: **Early evidence**, the strongest in the cell-therapy group, because it is the only CAR-type therapy in MG with a completed randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, but n=26 with a phase 3 not yet reported [Vu2026; CTAURORA].

### CAR-T cell therapy: dual BCMA/CD19 (Chinese series)
- Mechanism: a single product targets both CD19 (B cells) and BCMA (plasmablasts/plasma cells), intended to remove the autoantibody-producing compartment that CD19- plasma cells escape [Ruan2026].
- Phase 1 single-centre dose-finding study (NCT06371040) in refractory generalised MG, given without prior lymphodepletion: CAR-T cells expanded in vivo and depleted B cells and plasma cells; all six patients had a favourable safety profile; minimal symptom expression (MG-ADL 0) was reached in five patients by day 90 with responses sustained through day 120-150, and all patients discontinued glucocorticoids while reducing other immunosuppressants [Ruan2026].
- Immune profiling in that study showed a transient decline in memory B cells and plasma cells, repopulated B cells with attenuated B-cell-receptor signalling and increased inhibitory signals from bone-marrow niche cells, and significantly reduced clonal expansion of T and B cells [Ruan2026].
- A separate six-patient BCMA/CD19 CAR-T series reports grade 1 CRS, profound B-cell depletion, sustained reduction in AChR antibody titres, and drug-free remission with minimal manifestations in five patients by month 6 persisting through 12 months despite B-cell reconstitution; repopulated B cells were naive-predominant with diminished AChR specificity [Huang2026I].
- In that series, the relapsing patient showed expansion of age-associated B cells with elevated FCRL5, a pattern also seen in rituximab-relapsed patients, which the authors propose as a relapse mechanism and a candidate next target [Huang2026I].
- An earlier two-patient BCMA CAR-T report (one AChR-IgG, one MuSK-IgG) describes favourable safety and persistent clinical improvement over 18 months, with B-cell lineage reconstitution and sustained reduction of pathogenic autoantibodies as the proposed mechanism; the same work found compromised cytotoxic and proliferative signatures and mitochondrial dysfunction in CD8+ effector T cells before infusion, suggesting product quality limits efficacy in some patients [Tian2024].
- Evidence label, dual BCMA/CD19 CAR-T: **Early evidence** (two independent six-patient phase 1 series plus a two-patient report, consistent drug-free remission signals, no control arm, follow-up 6-18 months) [Ruan2026; Huang2026I; Tian2024].

### CAR-T durability, relapse and risk (cross-route)
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of CAR-based cellular therapy in autoimmune disease (56 studies, 15 pooled; search to 2025-10-20) found pooled medication-free remission of 0.76 in SLE with DORIS remission 0.75, and reported promising efficacy and seroconversion in other autoimmune diseases including MG; longer disease duration was associated with lower remission rates [Wong2026].
- Pooled safety across autoimmune diseases in that meta-analysis: any-grade CRS 0.63 but grade >=3 CRS 0.00 and any-grade ICANS 0.00; any-grade hypogammaglobulinaemia 0.47 with rare grade >=3; neutropenia and anaemia were the most frequent cytopenias with severe events in <=0.3; infection within 3 months 0.29 [Wong2026].
- An MG-specific review of five independent case series (seven patients receiving CD19-, BCMA- or bispecific-targeted CAR-T) reports peak CAR-T expansion 8-22 days post-infusion with persistence from 28 to about 180 days, mainly grade 1-2 CRS, rare ICANS and no severe or life-threatening events; it counts seven registered MG CAR-T trials enrolling more than 260 patients with results expected 2026-2029 [Hegelmaier2026b].
- A 2026 scoping review of CAR-T in autoimmune neurological disease (19 studies, January 2023 to January 2026) finds the most consistent efficacy signals in refractory AChR-antibody-positive generalised MG and AQP4-antibody-positive NMOSD, notes that relapse was reported across several conditions with follow-up generally under two years, and flags cytopenias, infection, prolonged hypogammaglobulinaemia and emerging local immune effector cell-associated toxicity as clinically significant [Bradhurst2026].
- The same review states that current evidence is limited to small, largely uncontrolled studies with heterogeneous outcome reporting, and that adequately powered longer-term controlled trials are needed before CAR-T can be recommended more broadly [Bradhurst2026].
- Open questions explicitly raised in the CAR-T MG literature: whether autoantibody titres need to fall for clinical remission (they did not in the German CD19 series) [Hegelmaier2026]; what drives relapse after B-cell reconstitution (candidate: FCRL5-high age-associated B cells) [Huang2026I]; whether lymphodepletion can be omitted (two MG series and the mRNA platform did omit it) [Ruan2026; Vu2026]; and long-term malignancy and infection risk, cost, manufacturing and equitable access, which the reviews list as unresolved [Bradhurst2026; Hegelmaier2026b].

### Antigen-specific approaches
- MuSK-CAART mechanism: instead of a CAR against a B-cell surface marker, T cells express the MuSK autoantigen itself as a chimeric autoantibody receptor with CD137-CD3zeta signalling, so only B cells bearing anti-MuSK B-cell receptors are killed [Oh2023].
- Preclinical data: MuSK-CAART depleted anti-MuSK B cells with efficacy similar to anti-CD19 CAR-T, retained cytolytic activity in the presence of soluble anti-MuSK antibody, and in experimental autoimmune MG reduced anti-MuSK IgG without reducing total B cells or total IgG; no specific off-target interactions were identified in vivo, in primary human cell screens or on a high-throughput human membrane proteome array [Oh2023].
- Clinical status: the phase 1 open-label dose-finding study of autologous MuSK-CAART in anti-MuSK-antibody-positive MG (NCT05451212) evaluated MuSK-CAART alone, with cyclophosphamide, and with cyclophosphamide plus fludarabine; it is listed as completed with 7 participants actually enrolled, start 2022-11-23, primary completion 2025-07-24, primary outcome adverse events at 3 months, and no results posted to the registry [CTMuSKCAART]. No peer-reviewed phase 1 efficacy or safety report was found in PubMed as of 2026-10-20.
- Antigen-specific tolerance (preclinical): intravenous administration of a soluble mutated extracellular domain of the AChR alpha1 subunit (alpha1-ECDm), which carries most MG auto-epitopes, abrogated established experimental autoimmune MG in rats in a dose- and time-dependent way by clinical score, body weight and compound muscle action potential decrement, with a more pronounced effect than drugs representing current standard of care and no observed toxicity; the protein had a short plasma half-life and accumulated in liver, kidney and spleen [Lazaridis2022].
- AChR-specific/polyclonal regulatory T cells (preclinical): a single intravenous dose of 1x10^6 ex vivo GM-CSF-conditioned autologous Treg cells modulated experimental autoimmune MG in rats when given 3 days or 3 weeks after disease induction, with reduced total AChR-specific serum antibody; the same cells also suppressed adjuvant arthritis [Aricha2016].
- Antigen-specific immunoadsorption: a 2025 engineering study produced sterilisable columns with immobilised recombinant MuSK (and Caspr2) extracellular domains that captured the corresponding antibodies from antisera and retained capacity after standard sterilisation; this is a device-platform proof of concept in rabbit antisera, not a patient study, and it excludes autoantibodies with conformational epitope specificity [Futami2025].
- Evidence label, MuSK-CAART: **Early evidence** for safety/feasibility only (phase 1 completed, n=7, no published outcomes), with **Hypothesis** status for the claim of antigen-specific cure in humans [Oh2023; CTMuSKCAART].
- Evidence label, tolerogenic vaccines / antigen-specific Treg / decoy receptors / antigen-specific immunoadsorption: **Hypothesis**. Effects exist only in rodent models of experimental autoimmune MG or in in-vitro device work, and a 2026 review states that antigen-bait, anti-idiotype and tolerogenic approaches have not been demonstrated in humans with spontaneously acquired MG [Lazaridis2022; Aricha2016; Futami2025; ElWahsh2026].

### Autologous haematopoietic stem cell transplantation
- Mechanism: high-dose cytoreductive conditioning plus anti-thymocyte globulin ablates the autoreactive lymphocyte repertoire, and a CD34-selected autologous graft reconstitutes a new immune system.
- Largest series (21 patients with severe refractory MG, retrospective, CD34-selected grafts, cyclophosphamide/G-CSF mobilisation, high-dose conditioning plus anti-thymocyte globulin, median 4.0 years from diagnosis to transplant): the primary outcome of clinically stable remission or minimal manifestations off all treatment, maintained to last follow-up, was reached in 16/18 evaluable patients (89%) at a median of 1.7 years and maintained over a median follow-up of 6.7 years (range 1.0-21.9) [Beland2025].
- Harms in that series: 100-day transplant-related mortality was 9.5% (two patients died within 12 months of transplant, one further patient not evaluable due to confounding illness) and two late deaths occurred with uncertain relation to the transplant; the authors state that transplant-related mortality in medically complex MG patients may be high and that prospective studies are warranted [Beland2025].
- MuSK-MG subgroup (three patients, MGFA IVb-V, all refractory including to rituximab): all became symptom-free or near-symptom-free and remained so for 2, 3.5 and 5.5 years; mean worst MG-ADL improved from 14.7 before to 0.3 after transplant and mean worst MG-QOL15 from 26.7 to 0; adverse events ranged from treatable infections and transient dyspnoea to persistent fatigue and premature menopause [Beland2023].
- A separate single-patient report of high-dose immunosuppressive therapy (carmustine, etoposide, cytarabine, melphalan, rabbit anti-thymocyte globulin) with autologous transplantation in AChR-MG of 14 years' duration, MGFA class IVb, failing thymectomy, plasma exchange and multiple agents, describes symptom-free status at 1 and 2 years while AChR-binding autoantibodies persisted and immune-cell subset frequencies shifted [Schlatter2023].
- Evidence label, autologous HSCT: **Early evidence** for durable drug-free remission in highly refractory MG (one 21-patient retrospective series with the longest follow-up of any curative-intent MG intervention, plus small series), but with no randomised comparison and a non-trivial early mortality that bounds its use [Beland2025; Beland2023].

### B-cell targeting
- Anti-CD19 antibody, MINT (NCT04524273, phase 3, 81 sites, 18 countries, n=238 randomised 1:1; inebilizumab 300 mg on days 1 and 15, plus day 183 for AChR-antibody-positive patients; protocol-specified glucocorticoid taper from week 4 to a target of <=5 mg/day by week 24): MG-ADL change at week 26 was -4.2 with inebilizumab versus -2.2 with placebo (adjusted difference -1.9, 95% CI -2.9 to -1.0, p<0.001) and QMG -4.8 versus -2.3 (difference -2.5, 95% CI -3.8 to -1.2, p<0.001) [Nowak2025].
- MINT enrolled 190 AChR-antibody-positive and 48 MuSK-antibody-positive participants; the sponsor reports week-26 MG-ADL differences of 1.8 points in the AChR-positive subgroup (-4.2 vs -2.4, p=0.0015) and 2.2 points in the MuSK-positive subgroup (-3.9 vs -1.7, p=0.0297), a non-significant MuSK QMG difference of 2.3 points (p=0.1326), and an exploratory week-52 AChR-positive MG-ADL difference of 2.8 points (-4.7 vs -1.9) [FDAUplizna2025].
- MINT prespecified exacerbation analysis: inebilizumab reduced the risk of exacerbation (myasthenic crisis, worsening of individual MG-ADL items, or rescue IVIg/plasma exchange) with hazard ratio 0.41 (95% CI 0.24-0.70) by week 26 in the combined population, 0.39 (0.23-0.68) by week 52 in AChR-positive patients, and 0.21 (0.06-0.79) by week 26 in MuSK-positive patients [Nowak2026].
- Regulatory status: the FDA approved inebilizumab-cdon (Uplizna) on 2025-12-11 for adults with generalised MG who are AChR- or MuSK-antibody positive, with two loading doses then one dose every 6 months; labelled risks include infusion reactions including anaphylaxis (10.1% of gMG trial patients), serious and fatal infections, hepatitis B reactivation (one case in the gMG trial), tuberculosis screening, hypogammaglobulinaemia monitoring and progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (no confirmed trial case, one trial death with PML in the differential) [FDAUplizna2025].
- Anti-CD20 rituximab, BeatMG (NCT02110706, phase 2 futility design, n=52, AChR-antibody-positive generalised MG on prednisone >=15 mg/day, two cycles): the steroid-sparing primary outcome (>=75% reduction in mean daily prednisone with clinical improvement or no significant worsening at week 52) was met by 60% on rituximab versus 56% on placebo, and the futility endpoint was reached (p=0.03), meaning a clinically meaningful 30% absolute steroid-sparing advantage was unlikely in a larger trial; no safety issues were identified [Nowak2022].
- Anti-CD20 rituximab in new-onset disease, RINOMAX (NCT02950155, n=47, single 500 mg infusion, Sweden): minimal disease manifestations at 16 weeks (QMG <=4 on prednisolone <=10 mg/day without rescue) were reached by 71% (17/24) versus 29% (6/21) placebo (Fisher exact p=0.007; probability ratio 2.48, 95% CI 1.20-5.11); rescue treatment was needed by 1 (4%) versus 8 (36%) [Piehl2022].
- RINOMAX long-term follow-up (registry-linked, to 5 years): time-weighted mean QMG was lower with rituximab at 12 months (mean difference 2.9, 95% CI 0.9-4.9, p=0.005) and 24 months (2.6, 0.3-4.9, p=0.027); rescue incidence from 48 weeks to 5 years was 0.09 versus 0.16 per person-year (p=0.121); early versus delayed rituximab was associated with lower hospitalisation risk (HR 0.24, 95% CI 0.07-0.83), but the six patients who never received rituximab also had low hospitalisation risk (HR 0.08, 95% CI 0.01-0.96); severe infection occurred in 12.5% of early and 18.8% of delayed rituximab patients [Wu2025].
- The BeatMG/RINOMAX contrast is a genuine controversy in the field: the same drug was futile for steroid-sparing in established mild-to-moderate AChR-positive disease [Nowak2022] yet superior to placebo for minimal manifestations in new-onset generalised disease [Piehl2022], which the long-term follow-up interprets as a disease-trajectory effect that has to be weighed against B-cell-depletion infection risk [Wu2025].
- Evidence label, anti-CD19 antibody (inebilizumab): **Established** as a treatment (positive phase 3 with blinded primary endpoint, regulatory approval) but not as a cure (fixed repeat dosing every 6 months, effect sizes about 2 MG-ADL points over placebo) [Nowak2025; FDAUplizna2025].
- Evidence label, anti-CD20 (rituximab): **Established** for new-onset generalised MG on one randomised trial with long-term registry follow-up [Piehl2022; Wu2025], while **not** established for steroid-sparing in established mild-to-moderate AChR-positive disease, where a phase 2 futility trial was negative [Nowak2022].

### B-cell survival factors and plasma-cell targeting
- Anti-BAFF belimumab (NCT01480596, phase 2, randomised, double-blind, n=40: 18 belimumab 10 mg/kg IV, 22 placebo, 24-week treatment plus 12-week follow-up): the primary endpoint, mean change in QMG at week 24, was not significantly different from placebo (p=0.256), and no secondary endpoint (MG Composite, MG-ADL) differed; AChR antibody levels fell over time in both arms; adverse events occurred in 78% (belimumab) versus 91% (placebo), and the one death (severe sepsis) was in the placebo arm [Hewett2018].
- Dual BAFF/APRIL inhibition with telitacicept, phase 2 (NCT04302103, multicentre, randomised, open-label, n=29 of 41 screened, subcutaneous weekly for 24 weeks on top of standard of care): mean QMG reduction from baseline to week 24 was 7.7 (SD 5.34) with 160 mg and 9.6 (SD 4.29) with 240 mg; at week 12 the reductions were 5.8 (SD 5.85) and 9.5 (SD 5.03); serum IgA, IgG and IgM all fell consistently; no adverse event led to discontinuation and there were no deaths [Yin2024].
- Telitacicept phase 3 in China (NCT05737160, randomised, placebo-controlled, n=114 AChR- or MuSK-antibody-positive adults, 240 mg subcutaneously weekly for 24 weeks then a 24-week open-label phase; registry lists the study as completed 2024-12-12): conference reports of the week-24 primary endpoint give MG-ADL -5.74 versus -0.91 for placebo in one account and -6.4 versus -1.6 (p<0.001) in another; QMG is reported as -8.66 versus -2.27 in the first and -7.8 versus -1.9 in the second; both report >=3-point MG-ADL response in 98.1% versus 12.0% and >=5-point QMG response in 87.0% versus 16.0% [MGNewsTelitacicept2025; EMJTelitacicept2025; CTTelitacicept3].
- The two conference reports of the telitacicept phase 3 disagree on the exact MG-ADL and QMG means, and no peer-reviewed publication of this trial was found in PubMed as of 2026-10-20, so these numbers are marked unverified; infection-related adverse events were reported as 45.6% with telitacicept versus 59.6% with placebo, and decreased IgM was the most frequently reported adverse event [MGNewsTelitacicept2025; EMJTelitacicept2025].
- A global phase 3 of telitacicept in generalised MG (UPSTREAM MG, NCT06456580) has 191 participants enrolled, started 2024-07-17 and has estimated completion 2029-01, so the international replication of the China result is pending [CTUpstream].
- Proteasome inhibition (bortezomib) mechanism and preclinical basis: in experimental autoimmune MG in Lewis rats, subcutaneous bortezomib 0.2 mg/kg twice weekly reduced bone-marrow plasma cells by up to 81%, limited the rise in anti-AChR titres, prevented ultrastructural damage to the postsynaptic membrane, improved neuromuscular transmission and reduced myasthenic symptoms [Gomez2011].
- In primary thymic cell cultures from nine early-onset MG patients, bortezomib at >=10 nM killed long-lived plasma cells and halted spontaneous production of both anti-AChR antibody and total IgG, with endoplasmic-reticulum-stress ultrastructure at 8 h and no detectable plasma cells at 24 h, while lenalidomide and dexamethasone had little effect [Gomez2014].
- Clinical bortezomib evidence remains thin: the TAVAB trial was designed as a single-centre, non-randomised, non-placebo-controlled phase IIa study of only 18 treatment-refractory patients across MG, SLE and rheumatoid arthritis, with change in autoantibody level at 6 months as the primary endpoint [Kohler2019]; a 29-patient refractory autoimmune neurology cohort that included 2 MG patients reported median mRS-9Q improving from 4 at initiation to 3 at 60 days (p<0.001), with severe but not immediately life-threatening adverse events in 29% and 2 deaths from SARS-CoV-2 infection that could not be attributed to bortezomib alone [Popova2026]. First clinical experience in severe MuSK-antibody-positive MG was reported in 2017 but no abstract is indexed, so its numbers are not cited here [SchneiderGold2017].
- Anti-CD38 daratumumab: in a retrospective single-centre series of seven patients with refractory antibody-mediated neurological disease (one with seronegative MG) given 4-20 cycles of 16 mg/kg, the MG patient's QMG fell from 16 to 8; across the series total IgG fell 66% in serum and 58% in CSF and vaccine-induced titres fell for rubella (50%) and tetanus toxoid (74%); grade 3 or higher treatment-related toxicity occurred in five of seven patients, including one death [Scheibe2022].
- Anti-CD38 mezagitamab (TAK-079) phase 2 in generalised MG (NCT04159805, randomised, placebo-controlled, n=36 actual, completed 2022-07-12): registry-posted results show MG-ADL change from baseline over weeks 1-7 that does not separate the active arms from placebo (for example, at the first post-baseline visit -2.8 placebo, -2.5 for 300 mg, -1.8 for 600 mg; at a later visit -4.2 placebo, -4.2 for 300 mg, -2.5 for 600 mg), with posted p-values of 0.772 and 0.299 for MG-ADL and 0.406 and 0.418 for QMG; TEAEs occurred in 66.7% (placebo), 75.0% (300 mg) and 91.7% (600 mg) [CTMezagitamab].
- Evidence label, anti-BAFF alone (belimumab): **Hypothesis / refuted as monotherapy add-on** for MG. The phase 2 primary endpoint was negative with Class I evidence, and no later MG programme followed [Hewett2018].
- Evidence label, dual BAFF/APRIL (telitacicept): **Early evidence**. A positive randomised China phase 3 exists but only in conference form with conflicting numbers, the phase 2 was open-label, and the global phase 3 does not complete before 2029 [Yin2024; MGNewsTelitacicept2025; CTUpstream].
- Evidence label, proteasome inhibition (bortezomib): **Hypothesis**. Strong, quantitative preclinical plasma-cell depletion data; clinical data limited to a small non-randomised trial design and case cohorts mixing diagnoses [Gomez2011; Gomez2014; Kohler2019; Popova2026].
- Evidence label, anti-CD38: **Early evidence for salvage use, negative for mezagitamab in phase 2**. Daratumumab data are a seven-patient retrospective series with substantial grade >=3 toxicity, and the randomised mezagitamab phase 2 did not separate from placebo on posted MG-ADL/QMG results [Scheibe2022; CTMezagitamab].

### Cytokine and signalling targets upstream of the antibody
- Anti-IL-6 receptor satralizumab, LUMINESCE (NCT04963270, phase 3, 105 sites, n=188 randomised: 96 satralizumab, 92 placebo; 166 AChR-antibody-positive in the modified intention-to-treat set): week-24 MG-ADL change was -3.59 (95% CI -4.15 to -3.02) with satralizumab versus -2.57 (-3.25 to -1.88) with placebo, a difference of -1.02 (-1.88 to -0.16, p=0.0120), which the authors describe as statistically significant yet small [Habib2025F].
- LUMINESCE safety and programme fate: at least one adverse event occurred in 90% (86/96) of satralizumab versus 73% (67/92) of placebo patients; three serious adverse events in three satralizumab patients versus nine in six placebo patients; no deaths; the open-label extension was terminated early because the sponsor halted development of satralizumab for generalised MG, and the registry lists the study as terminated [Habib2025F; CTLuminesce].
- Anti-IL-6 receptor tocilizumab, prospective single-centre cohort in refractory AChR-antibody-positive generalised MG (n=34: 20 tocilizumab, 14 conventional immunotherapy): the adjusted mean MG-ADL difference favoured tocilizumab at week 4 (-3.4, 95% CI -4.7 to -2.0, p<0.001) and was sustained at week 24 (-4.5, 95% CI -6.4 to -2.6, p<0.001), with prednisone dose reduction and no severe or unexpected safety issues [Ruan2024].
- JAK inhibition: a single-arm 24-week study of the pan-JAK inhibitor tofacitinib in 19 patients with refractory MG (NCT04431895) reported significant glucocorticoid dose reduction and improved MG scores and quality of life, with mechanism via downregulation of p-STAT3, IL-6 and IL-23 and suppression of pathogenic Th17.1 cells [Zhao2026].
- Supporting preclinical JAK data: tofacitinib ameliorated experimental autoimmune MG in rats, reducing Th1, Th17 and memory B-cell proportions and anti-AChR antibody levels while increasing Treg proportions, via decreased phosphorylated STAT1 and increased phosphorylated STAT5 [Bi2025].
- Evidence label, IL-6 receptor blockade: **Early evidence with a negative commercial verdict**. The phase 3 met its endpoint but with a 1.02-point MG-ADL difference and development was halted [Habib2025F], while open-label tocilizumab data in refractory disease show larger differences without randomisation [Ruan2024].
- Evidence label, JAK inhibition: **Hypothesis**. The only MG clinical data are a 19-patient single-arm study plus rodent models; no randomised MG trial was found [Zhao2026; Bi2025].

### Downstream control: complement and FcRn ("control, not cure")
- Next-generation complement, cemdisiran and pozelimab, NIMBLE (NCT05070858, phase 3, 86 centres, 13 countries, 284 randomised as of the 2025-07-08 data cut; subcutaneous cemdisiran 600 mg every 12 weeks, pozelimab 200 mg every 4 weeks, the combination, or placebo over 24 weeks): week-24 MG-ADL least-squares mean change was -4.5 (SE 0.4) for cemdisiran (n=64), -4.0 (0.4) for the combination (n=67) and -2.2 (0.5) for placebo (n=59), giving placebo-adjusted differences of -2.3 (95% CI -3.6 to -1.0, p=0.0005) and -1.7 (-3.0 to -0.4, p=0.0086) [Vu2026b].
- NIMBLE safety: at least one adverse event in 69% (54/78) cemdisiran, 81% (65/80) combination, 82% (40/49) pozelimab and 77% (54/70) placebo; the most common cemdisiran adverse event was upper respiratory tract infection (12%, similar to placebo at 11%); no serious or meningococcal infections in the cemdisiran group; no deaths during the double-blind period, with two deaths afterwards, one judged treatment-related by the investigator but not by the sponsor [Vu2026b].
- Gefurulimab (a dual-binding anti-C5 nanobody), PREVAIL (NCT05556096, phase 3, 113 sites, 20 countries, n=260 randomised: 131 gefurulimab, 129 placebo; once-weekly subcutaneous self-injection; 249 completed 26 weeks): week-26 MG-ADL change was -4.2 versus -2.6 (treatment difference -1.6, 95% CI -2.4 to -0.8, p<0.001) and QMG -4.5 versus -2.4 (difference -2.1, -3.1 to -1.1, p<0.001), with MG-ADL improvement within 1 week and QMG within 4 weeks, sustained to week 26; no meningococcal infections were reported [Gwathmey2026].
- Oral alternative-pathway inhibition: iptacopan (LNP023), a factor B inhibitor given as a capsule, is in a phase 3 randomised double-blind placebo-controlled study in AChR-antibody-positive generalised MG (NCT06517758, planned enrolment 146, start 2024-07-31, primary endpoint MG-ADL change at month 6, primary completion estimated 2027-04-30, listed as active not recruiting); no efficacy results are available [CTIptacopan].
- Anti-C1s riliprubart: this C1s inhibitor has reported phase 1b/2 data in cold agglutinin disease [DSa2024] and phase 3 programmes in CIDP, but no myasthenia gravis trial for riliprubart was found in PubMed or in a ClinicalTrials.gov term search on 2026-10-20, so the C1s route in MG is at present an extrapolation from other complement-mediated diseases rather than an MG programme (this corrects an assumption that a riliprubart MG trial exists).
- FcRn blockade, batoclimab in China (NCT05039190, randomised, placebo-controlled, n=132 enrolled / 131 antibody-positive; six weekly subcutaneous 680 mg injections then 4 weeks observation per cycle): sustained MG-ADL improvement (>=3 points for >=4 consecutive weeks in cycle 1) occurred in 58.2% (39/67) versus 31.3% (20/64) placebo (odds ratio 3.45, 95% CI 1.62-7.35, p=0.001); MG-ADL diverged by week 2 and the maximum difference came 1 week after the last dose (day 43: 3.6 vs 1.7 points, difference -1.9, 95% CI -2.8 to -1.0) [Yan2024].
- Batoclimab phase 3 (NCT05403541, induction with 680 mg or 340 mg weekly versus placebo for 12 weeks, then re-randomisation to maintenance; 233 actually enrolled): the sponsor's topline release reports week-12 MG-ADL improvement of 5.6 points (680 mg, 74% mean IgG reduction), 4.7 points (340 mg, 64% IgG reduction) and 3.6 points (placebo), and durable minimal symptom expression (maintained >6 weeks) in 75% (680 mg), 39% (340 mg) and 0% (placebo); the company stated it does not currently intend to seek approval for batoclimab in MG and will instead pursue registrational studies with IMVT-1402 [ImmunovantPR2025]. The registry now lists this phase 3 as terminated by sponsor decision [CTBatoclimab].
- FcRn blockade, nipocalimab, Vivacity-MG3 (NCT04951622, phase 3, 81 centres, 17 countries, 196 dosed / 153 antibody-positive; 30 mg/kg loading then 15 mg/kg every 2 weeks for 24 weeks): the primary endpoint, MG-ADL change averaged over weeks 22-24, was -4.70 (SE 0.329) versus -3.25 (0.335) for placebo (difference -1.45, 95% CI -2.38 to -0.52, p=0.0024); adverse events occurred in 84% of both arms, serious adverse events in 9% versus 14%, with three fatal outcomes (nipocalimab: myasthenic crisis; placebo: cardiac arrest and myocardial infarction) [Antozzi2025].
- Nipocalimab sustained-control post hoc analysis: significantly more nipocalimab-treated than placebo-treated participants achieved MG-ADL meaningful (>=2-point), substantial (>=3-point) and considerable (>=4-point) improvement sustained for >=12 consecutive weeks, with onset as early as week 1; odds ratio 6.3 for composite response (MG-ADL >=2 plus QMG >=3) sustained >=8 weeks, and a 4-fold higher likelihood of minimal symptom expression (MG-ADL 0-1) sustained >=8 weeks (p=0.022) [Vu2026c].
- Nipocalimab was approved by the FDA on 2025-04-30 (Imaavy) for adults and paediatric patients aged 12 and older with generalised MG who are AChR- or MuSK-antibody positive [AJMCNipocalimab2025].
- Long-term FcRn data showing control rather than cure: in ADAPT-SC+ (NCT04818671, open-label extension, 180 participants dosed, mean 2.6 (SD 0.9) years of treatment plus follow-up, 459.4 participant-years, up to 33 cycles of 4 weekly subcutaneous efgartigimod PH20 1000 mg injections), 93.9% had at least one treatment-emergent adverse event (injection-site reactions 46.1%, COVID-19 29.4%, headache 26.7%); 95.1% of AChR-antibody-positive and 94.7% of AChR-antibody-negative participants achieved a clinically meaningful >=2-point MG-ADL improvement at some point, and 59.2% versus 34.2% achieved minimal symptom expression, but treatment continued in cycles throughout [Meisel2026F].
- Evidence label, next-generation complement inhibition (cemdisiran, gefurulimab): **Established as control**. Both have positive randomised phase 3 results with placebo-adjusted MG-ADL differences of 1.6-2.3 points, achieved only while dosing continues, so neither is a candidate cure [Vu2026b; Gwathmey2026].
- Evidence label, oral factor B inhibition (iptacopan) and C1s inhibition (riliprubart) in MG: **Hypothesis**. Iptacopan has a running phase 3 with no results before 2027 [CTIptacopan]; no MG trial of riliprubart was identified [DSa2024].
- Evidence label, FcRn blockade: **Established as control, not cure**. Repeated cycles are needed, effects track the depth and duration of IgG reduction, and the mechanism removes circulating IgG without touching the plasma cells that make it [Yan2024; Antozzi2025; Meisel2026F; ImmunovantPR2025].

### Biomarkers for personalised therapy
- A 2026 review states that predictive biomarkers for personalised MG treatment remain underdeveloped: AChR and MuSK autoantibodies are established diagnostic biomarkers, miR-150-5p and miR-30e-5p are promising prognostic candidates, and CD20+ B-cell counts can act as a pharmacodynamic biomarker for rituximab, while metabolites, T- and B-cell markers, complement factors and proteomics remain exploratory [Bhandage2026].
- MicroRNA, prospective longitudinal data in newly diagnosed drug-naive AChR-antibody-positive patients sampled at baseline, 6 and 12 months: three of eight microRNAs changed with treatment (miR-150-5p, miR-27a-3p, miR-21-5p); miR-150-5p correlated inversely with MG-ADL (p=0.007, rho -0.433), QMG (p=0.002, rho -0.355) and MG Composite (p=7.8e-5, rho -0.457), and pre-treatment miR-150-5p discriminated responders from non-responders at 12 months with AUC 0.85 (p=0.0076) by minimal clinical expression and AUC 0.86 (p=0.016) by MG-ADL [Garai2026].
- Antibody titre as a response biomarker is contested. In 62 AChR-antibody-positive generalised MG patients followed at baseline, 3 and 6 months, falling titres paralleled falling QMG and MG-ADL, with a correlation between titre change and MG-ADL change at 6 months and between titre rate-of-change and MG-ADL rate-of-change at 3 months, but no correlation at baseline between titre and age, disease duration, sex, MGFA class or thymic abnormality [Luo2024].
- Cutting against titre as a cure endpoint: clinical remission after anti-CD19 CAR-T occurred despite persistently detectable anti-AChR antibody [Hegelmaier2026], and symptom-free status after autologous transplantation occurred while AChR-binding antibodies persisted [Schlatter2023].
- Antibody fine specificity may stratify patients: in 513 AChR-antibody-positive patients, titres rose monotonically with disease severity (rho 0.31, 95% CI 0.17-0.44, p<0.0001) and were higher in women (median 14.6 vs 7.7 nM, p<0.0001); gamma-subunit immunodominance was more common in women (30% vs 24.5%, p=0.0027) and correlated with higher titre (rho 0.25, p<0.0001) and with severe MGFA IV-V disease (44% vs 27%, p=0.0141), defining two endotypes (early-onset, gamma-immunodominant, high-titre, thymic hyperplasia versus late-onset, alpha-immunodominant, low-titre) [Oved2025].
- Complement-related measures are not yet usable as response biomarkers: in 75 MG patients with AChR-binding antibodies, AChR-blocking antibodies were present in 49.3% and higher blocking-antibody titres were associated with milder disease and higher C4 (indicating less complement consumption), the opposite direction to a simple severity marker [Aguirre2025].
- Candidate non-antibody response markers: in 22 AChR-antibody-positive patients starting C5 inhibitors or FcRn inhibitors, rising serum calprotectin and serum neurofilament light chain were associated with poor MG-ADL response, suggesting systemic-inflammation and junction-destruction markers as treatment-decision aids, in a pilot of 22 patients needing validation [Stascheit2025].
- Clinical predictors of non-response are also being mapped: in 35 patients treated with efgartigimod, non-responders (10/35, defined as failing to improve >2 MG-ADL or >3 QMG points in one cycle and then responding to eculizumab) more often had thymoma, non-thymoma tumours, thyroid disease and other autoimmune disease, with combinations giving a predicted poor-response probability up to 97.9% in multivariate analysis [Niu2025].
- Evidence label, biomarkers for personalised therapy: **Early evidence** for miR-150-5p (one prospective longitudinal cohort with AUC 0.85-0.86) [Garai2026] and antibody-subunit endotyping (one 513-patient cross-sectional study) [Oved2025]; **Hypothesis** for complement-activity and calprotectin/neurofilament-guided treatment choice (pilot studies, n=22-75, with at least one counter-intuitive direction of effect) [Stascheit2025; Aguirre2025].

### Open questions the module should leave open
- Thymus role: whether removing the thymus adds anything after a successful B-cell or plasma-cell reset, and whether thymectomy's remission benefit extends to late-onset and seronegative disease, is unresolved; the available remission data are retrospective outside MGTX's 18-65-year, AChR-positive, non-thymomatous, under-5-years-duration population [Wolfe2016; Zhang2026].
- Durability: the longest follow-up with drug-free remission is autologous HSCT (median 6.7 years, range 1.0-21.9) [Beland2025]; anti-CD19 CAR-T MG follow-up is at least 19 months in three patients [Hegelmaier2026], and reviews state that CAR-T follow-up in autoimmune neurological disease is generally under two years with relapses already reported [Bradhurst2026].
- Long-term safety: hypogammaglobulinaemia (any-grade pooled 0.47), infection within 3 months (0.29) and cytopenias are the recurring CAR-T harms [Wong2026]; transplant-related mortality at 100 days was 9.5% in the largest MG HSCT series [Beland2025]; grade >=3 toxicity occurred in 5/7 patients including one death with daratumumab salvage [Scheibe2022].
- Cost and access: a 2026 review names cost-effectiveness as part of its cure definition and states that approved therapies deliver chronic suppression "at significant cost" [ElWahsh2026]; equity of access to cellular therapy is being raised as a distinct problem in the rheumatology literature (unverified for MG specifically) [ElWahsh2026].
- Trial landscape: a 2026 Trial Watch counts six ongoing MG studies targeting B-cell survival modulation, lymphocyte-directed immune reconstitution, BCMA- and CD19-directed CAR-T, T-cell engagement and response-guided combination therapy [Hoffmann2026I], and an MG CAR-T review counts seven registered trials with more than 260 patients concluding 2026-2029 [Hegelmaier2026b], so most of the cure-route evidence in this file is expected to change within three years.
