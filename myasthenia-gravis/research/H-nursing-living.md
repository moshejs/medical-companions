# H. Nursing care, pregnancy, surgery & living with MG
Scope: bedside nursing assessment (respiratory, bulbar, ocular, scales), medication administration (pyridostigmine timing and enteral routes, IVIG, plasma exchange, corticosteroids, complement and FcRn inhibitors), teaching points, alert cards and documentation; pregnancy, delivery, breastfeeding, transient neonatal MG and fetal AChR antibody disorders; perioperative care and anaesthesia; and living with MG (exercise, temperature, sleep, mental health, employment, driving, vaccination, infection), juvenile MG and late-onset MG. Numbers come from abstracts, open full texts (PMC/Europe PMC: Sanders2016, Narayanaswami2021, Elsheikh2016, Gilhus2020, Banner2022, Miegel2026, O'Connell2020, Bhat2024, Guidon2021 table), current DailyMed labels, and UK NHS/charity documents, all opened 2026-10-20. Could not access: Norwood2014 full text (abstract only); the Eisenkraft 1988 succinylcholine dose-response paper (no abstract, so the 2.6-fold figure is cited through Bhat2024's secondary statement); Daum 2021 BJA Education (server error); Gilhus2019 full text (abstract only). Not found despite searching: any study measuring outcomes of late or missed in-hospital pyridostigmine doses (the "time-critical" status rests on NHS safety documents, not outcome data); a primary source for the commonly quoted Leventhal point weights (12/10/8/4, cut-off 10), so only the four risk factors and their thresholds are given; any controlled evidence on diet or weight management specific to MG; any US national driving rule specific to MG (only UK DVLA rules were found).

## Facts

### Nursing assessment: respiratory
- In 31 AChR-antibody-positive adults (mostly MGFA class 1-2), single breath count (SBC) correlated with FVC (r = 0.554, P < 0.01), negative inspiratory force (r = 0.530, P < 0.01) and neck flexor strength (r = 0.519, P < 0.01). Neck flexion strength correlated with FVC (r = 0.505, P = 0.02); shoulder abduction did not correlate with SBC (r = 0.438, P = 0.052) [Elsheikh2016].
- Method in that study: the patient takes a deep breath and counts aloud in a normal voice at about 2 counts per second. The better of 2 attempts is recorded, seated [Elsheikh2016].
- Anchor for animation: each counted number corresponded on average to 116 ± 24 mL of FVC (range 76-199). This is close to the bedside rule of thumb "SBC x 100 = estimated FVC in mL" [Elsheikh2016].
- SBC thresholds: a count of 20 had earlier been proposed as indicating respiratory compromise in MG. The authors proposed ≥25 as suggesting normal respiratory muscle function. No MGFA class 1 patient counted <25, and all MGFA class 3 patients failed to reach 25. SBC range was 16-40 (mean 28 ± 7) [Elsheikh2016].
- Caveat: some patients had a low SBC (<20) with near-normal FVC, possibly from vocal cord or bulbar weakness. Testing was seated only, and supine testing might reveal more deficit [Elsheikh2016].
- The MG Core Exam (MG-CE) scores SBC as 0 (≥30), 1 (25-29), 2 (20-24) or 3 (<20) [Guidon2021].
- In 42 patients with severe MG (55 ICU admissions), mechanical ventilation was unlikely when vital capacity was >20 mL/kg, maximal expiratory pressure >40 cmH2O, or maximal inspiratory pressure more negative than -40 cmH2O. A decline of ≥30% in maximal inspiratory pressure identified higher risk of needing ventilation, and hypercapnia was frequent [Thieben2005].
- Controversy: a small ICU series (5 patients, 10 episodes, VC measured at least every 4 h) found that repeated vital capacity, including <20 or <13 mL/kg thresholds, did not distinguish episodes that needed ventilation. The authors attributed this to the erratic course of MG [Rieder1995].
- The "20/30/40" rule (VC 20 mL/kg, MIP -30, MEP 40) comes from Guillain-Barré literature. No MG-specific validation of that exact rule was found; the MG thresholds above are from Thieben2005 (unverified for the 20/30/40 form in MG).
- Myaware's nursing guidance: record a baseline respiratory measurement on admission, check FVC at least four times daily if respiratory muscles are involved, and recheck after any change in condition [MyawareNurses].
- In a German cohort of 815 patients, worse outcomes of myasthenic crisis were associated with lower vital capacity before and at admission, older age, more comorbidities and infection as the trigger [Nelke2022].

### Nursing assessment: bulbar, swallowing and aspiration
- Of 20 MG patients who had a modified barium swallow, 7 aspirated, and 4 of those did so silently. A self-administered questionnaire, the neurologist's clinical prediction and the QMG bulbar subscore all correlated with aspiration. The bedside speech-pathology prediction was highly sensitive but less specific [Koopman2004].
- Myaware's nursing guidance on swallowing: consider swallow safety and NG tubes. It says medication should never be withheld because the patient is nil by mouth, regular speech and language therapy reviews should be arranged, and when speech is affected, long conversations should be avoided and a pad and pen offered [MyawareNurses].
- OpenStax's nursing implications for pyridostigmine are to assess respiratory rate, depth and effort, airway patency, chewing and swallowing, and ease of eyelid raising (an important sign of improvement). Heart rate, rhythm and blood pressure are also monitored [OpenStaxPharmNurses].

### Nursing assessment: ocular, limb and neck, and scales
- MG-CE (8 items, each 0-3, maximum 24, about 10 minutes, designed for telemedicine). Ptosis is scored after 60 s of sustained upgaze: 0 none; 1 lid above pupil; 2 at pupil; 3 below pupil. Diplopia is scored on sustained gaze: 0 none at 60 s; 1 at 11-60 s; 2 at 1-10 s; 3 immediate. Arm abduction: 0 no drift at 120 s; 1 drift at 90-120 s; 2 at 10-89 s; 3 at 0-9 s. Sit-to-stand is scored from 0 (arms crossed, no difficulty) to 3 (unable unassisted) [Guidon2021].
- MG-ADL is an 8-question patient-reported survey. In 254 encounters the mean MG-ADL was 4.89 ± 3.63, and it correlated with the QMG (r = 0.583, P < 0.001) [Wolfe1999].
- Fatigue scales: in 420 Dutch registry patients, severe fatigue (Checklist Individual Strength fatigue subscale) was present in 62%, with a mean score of 37.1 ± 13.2. It was associated with disease severity, female sex, BMI and depressive symptoms, and inversely with strenuous physical activity [Ruiter2021].
- Myaware suggests checking ptosis or diplopia about 20 minutes after a pyridostigmine dose and documenting the response [MyawareNurses].

### Medication administration: pyridostigmine and other cholinesterase inhibitors
- Pyridostigmine usually starts working in 30-60 minutes, so doses are taken about 30-60 minutes before meals (the first on rising). Usual duration is 3-4 hours by day and about 6 hours for a bedtime dose [RightDecisionsPyrido2025].
- Discrepancy: Myaware's nurse page says about 20 minutes before meals for patients on an altered diet, and not needed with NG feeding [MyawareNurses]. OpenStax states the aim as timing doses so that peak effect falls at meals [OpenStaxPharmNurses].
- The King's Health Partners (London) medicines safety bulletin calls pyridostigmine a critical medicine to be given within 30 minutes of the due time, even when this does not match ward rounds. It says a late dose can cause excessive weakness or inability to swallow, and a missed or late dose can precipitate myasthenic crisis, which can be fatal [KHP2025].
- The NHS Scotland (Right Decisions) Q&A has these missed-dose rules. A dose missed by up to 1 hour is taken and the schedule kept. A dose more than 1 hour late is taken immediately and the next is delayed 3-4 hours. Doses are never doubled. It also reports a known risk of adverse incidents from repeated missed doses [RightDecisionsPyrido2025].
- Allowing patient self-administration in hospital is suggested because pyridostigmine timing often does not fit drug rounds [MyawareNurses].
- No published study measuring clinical outcomes of late or missed in-hospital pyridostigmine doses was found. The time-critical status rests on NHS safety documents and charity guidance.
- Enteral route (NHS Scotland Q&A):
  - NG administration is the preferred option when a patient is nil by mouth.
  - The oral solution is licensed for NG and PEG tubes, except latex tubes.
  - Immediate-release tablets can be crushed to a fine powder and mixed with at least 10 mL of water.
  - When no enteral route is possible, parenteral neostigmine is a short-term option on neurology advice. Oral pyridostigmine 60 mg is about equivalent to 1-1.5 mg IM or SC neostigmine.
  - Neostigmine's half-life is about 1-2 h, against about 3-4 h for pyridostigmine.
  [RightDecisionsPyrido2025]
- Extended-release Mestinon Timespan 180 mg: the immediate effect is about that of 60 mg conventional tablets, and duration averages 2.5 times a 60 mg dose. The dose interval should be at least 6 hours, and regular tablets or syrup may be needed alongside it [FDAMestinon2020].
- OpenStax's client teaching says ER forms must not be chewed, broken or crushed [OpenStaxPharmNurses]. The US label itself does not address crushing; it says only that Timespan tablets are single-scored [FDAMestinon2020].
- Dosing anchors:
  - Usual starting dose 15-30 mg three to four times daily. Most patients need about 180 mg/day, and the usual maximum is 360 mg/day [RightDecisionsPyrido2025].
  - The US label gives an average of 600 mg/day (ten 60 mg tablets), up to 25 tablets in severe cases [FDAMestinon2020].
  - These sources disagree on upper dosing.
- Telling cholinergic toxicity from underdosing: worsening 15-60 minutes after a dose suggests cholinergic toxicity, while worsening 3-4 hours after a dose suggests underdosing [RightDecisionsPyrido2025].
- The label recommends atropine for cholinergic crisis and warns that atropine can mask overdose signs [FDAMestinon2020].
- Common cholinergic GI effects (cramps, diarrhoea, salivation, sweating) may be countered with propantheline 15 mg taken 15-30 minutes before the dose, or with loperamide [RightDecisionsPyrido2025].
- IV cholinesterase inhibitors may provoke uterine contractions and are not used in pregnancy [Sanders2016; Gilhus2020].

### Medication administration: IVIG and plasma exchange
- IVIG class boxed warning (Gamunex-C label): thrombosis, renal dysfunction and acute renal failure. Thrombosis risk factors include advanced age, immobilisation, hypercoagulable states, prior thrombosis and oestrogen use. For at-risk patients the label calls for the minimum dose and infusion rate practicable, adequate hydration, and monitoring for thrombosis and hyperviscosity [FDAGamunexC2026].
- Infusion-rate anchor from the same label (PI indication): start at 1 mg/kg/min (0.01 mL/kg/min) and, if tolerated, increase gradually to a maximum of 8 mg/kg/min [FDAGamunexC2026]. That label lists no MG indication; IVIG for MG follows consensus guidance [Sanders2016].
- Aseptic meningitis syndrome usually begins within several hours to 2 days after IVIG. It is more frequent with high doses (2 g/kg) and/or rapid infusion. Haemolysis risk rises with doses ≥2 g/kg and non-O blood group, and the label suggests haemoglobin checks before infusion, at about 36 h and at 7-10 days in higher-risk patients. TRALI and volume overload are also listed [FDAGamunexC2026].
- Most IVIG adverse effects (flushing, headache, malaise, fever, chills, fatigue) are transient and mild. Serious rare effects include renal impairment, thrombosis, arrhythmia, aseptic meningitis, haemolytic anaemia and TRALI. Slow infusion, premedication and switching to subcutaneous immunoglobulin can reduce them [Guo2018].
- Choosing between IVIG and plasma exchange (PLEX) depends on patient factors: PLEX cannot be used in sepsis and IVIG cannot be used in renal failure. The two are probably equally effective in severe generalised MG, and PLEX may be more effective in MuSK-MG [Sanders2016].
- PLEX safety, prospective trial cohort (n = 42): 55% had no complications. 45% had mild-moderate reactions, mostly citrate reactions and peripheral vascular issues. 83% completed PLEX by peripheral venous access, and 90% were treated as outpatients [Ebadi2013].
- PLEX venous access, retrospective (134 patients, 230 courses): peripheral venous access succeeded in 75% of courses. Central venous catheters carried more total complications (68% vs 35%) and more serious complications (41% vs 4%), including 2 deaths [Guptill2013].
- Hypocalcaemia and hypotension during PLEX are widely taught. The abstracts read give only "citrate reactions" (Ebadi2013), and no MG-specific rates for hypocalcaemia or hypotension were verified (unverified).

### Medication administration: corticosteroids
- Corticosteroids may cause transient worsening of myasthenic weakness, so it may be appropriate to wait several days for PLEX or IVIG to work before starting them in impending crisis [Sanders2016].
- Myaware asks nurses to monitor blood glucose in patients on steroids, and bloods and infection signs in patients on immunosuppression [MyawareNurses].
- In children on steroids, O'Connell2020 recommends monitoring blood pressure, growth velocity and weight, starting vitamin D for all children, considering gastric protection, and bone density assessment for long-term use [OConnell2020].

### Medication administration: complement and FcRn inhibitors
- Eculizumab (Soliris), ravulizumab (Ultomiris) and zilucoplan (Zilbrysq) carry boxed warnings for serious meningococcal infection. Vaccination against serogroups A, C, W, Y and B should be completed or updated at least 2 weeks before the first dose, or antibacterial prophylaxis given if treatment is urgent. Each drug is available only through a REMS [FDASoliris2026; FDAUltomiris2025; FDAZilbrysq2026; Narayanaswami2021].
- How long patients carry the REMS Patient Safety Card differs by drug: 3 months after the last eculizumab dose [FDASoliris2026], 8 months after ravulizumab [FDAUltomiris2025], and 2 months after zilucoplan [FDAZilbrysq2026]. The card lists symptoms (fever, headache with stiff neck, rash, photophobia, confusion) that should prompt immediate medical evaluation [FDASoliris2026].
- Meningococcal vaccination does not give absolute protection. One case of meningococcal meningitis occurred in a vaccinated patient in the REGAIN open-label extension. For prophylaxis in penicillin-allergic patients, the guidance notes that macrolides and fluoroquinolones can worsen MG [Narayanaswami2021].
- Eculizumab is infused IV over 35 minutes in adults (1-4 h in children). If the infusion is slowed, total time should not exceed 2 h in adults. Patients are monitored for at least 1 hour after the infusion [FDASoliris2026].
- Zilucoplan is a once-daily subcutaneous injection dosed by weight, and baseline lipase and amylase are required before starting [FDAZilbrysq2026].
- Efgartigimod IV (Vyvgart) is given at 10 mg/kg over 1 hour once weekly for 4 weeks (1,200 mg if ≥120 kg). Further cycles are based on clinical evaluation. Live vaccines are not recommended during treatment because IgG falls [FDAVyvgart2026].
- Efgartigimod SC with hyaluronidase (Vyvgart Hytrulo) comes as a 1,000 mg prefilled syringe given over about 20-30 s, which patients or caregivers can self-administer. The 1,008 mg vial is given over 30-90 s by a healthcare professional with a winged set. Both are weekly for 4-week cycles [FDAVyvgartHytrulo2026].
- Rozanolixizumab (Rystiggo) is given by healthcare-provider subcutaneous infusion pump at up to 20 mL/h, weekly for 6 weeks: 420 mg if <50 kg, 560 mg if 50 to <100 kg, 840 mg if ≥100 kg. The label warns of aseptic meningitis, and live vaccines are not recommended [FDARystiggo2026].

### Teaching points, common misunderstandings, cards and resources
- OpenStax's client-teaching points:
  - MG treatment is lifelong.
  - Learn signs of underdosing (ptosis, dysphagia) and of cholinergic toxicity.
  - Keep a record of dose times, fatigue times and strength before and after doses.
  - Wear a medical alert bracelet.
  - Do not stop pyridostigmine abruptly.
  - Rest between activities.
  - Contact the prescriber urgently for trouble swallowing, speaking or breathing.
  [OpenStaxPharmNurses]
- Common misunderstanding: increasing muscle weakness is not automatically underdosing. Raising the dose risks cholinergic crisis, and both under- and overdosing can cause lack of improvement [OpenStaxPharmNurses; FDAMestinon2020].
- Common misunderstanding: fatigability means that a task possible earlier the same day may not be possible now [MyawareNurses].
- The MGFA Emergency Alert Card is a fillable card in English and Spanish, kept in a wallet, glove box or on the fridge. It warns first responders that MG weakness, ptosis, diplopia and breathing or swallowing difficulty can be mistaken for intoxication and may signal a crisis [MGFACard].
- Myaware's "drugs to avoid" page notes that more than 30 drugs could theoretically impair neuromuscular transmission. It names telithromycin as the one drug that must be avoided (deaths reported), penicillamine as best avoided, and IV magnesium as to be avoided if possible. It advises caution rather than bans for neurology and psychiatry drugs [MyawareDrugs].
- Families of children with MG should be given a list of medicines that may worsen MG [OConnell2020].

### Documentation items
- Items the sources ask to be recorded:
  - baseline respiratory measure on admission, and serial FVC [MyawareNurses]
  - response of ptosis or diplopia after each dose [MyawareNurses]
  - dose times against fatigue and strength [OpenStaxPharmNurses]
  - SBC best of 2 attempts and test position [Elsheikh2016]
  - MG-CE item scores, including pre-visit patient-reported scales [Guidon2021]
  - for complement inhibitors, vaccination dates, any prophylactic antibiotics and that the REMS card was issued [FDASoliris2026]
  - for at-risk IVIG patients, pre-infusion haemoglobin and follow-up haemoglobin [FDAGamunexC2026]

### Pregnancy: course of MG
- Batocchi1999 (47 women, 64 pregnancies):
  - Pregnancies on therapy: MG improved in 39%, was unchanged in 42% and deteriorated in 19%.
  - Asymptomatic women off therapy: 17% relapsed.
  - Worsening after delivery: 28% of pregnancies.
  - Pregnancy did not worsen long-term outcome.
  [Batocchi1999]
- Djelmis2002 (69 pregnancies): MG deteriorated in 15% during pregnancy and in a further 16% in the puerperium. All women with puerperal infections had exacerbations [Djelmis2002].
- Banner2022 (systematic review, 824 pregnancies): exacerbation 33.8%; myasthenic crisis 6.4% in pregnancy and 8.2% postpartum; spontaneous vaginal delivery 56.3%; transient neonatal MG 13.0% [Banner2022].
- Miegel2026 (meta-analysis, 34 studies, 3,720 pregnancies): worsening in 30%, most often in the first trimester; postpartum exacerbation 27%; preterm birth 9%; vaginal delivery 68% and caesarean 32%; neonatal MG 9% of 1,530 infants. Definitions and scores varied widely between studies [Miegel2026].
- Guidance: if MG is well controlled before pregnancy, most women remain stable, and any worsening is more likely in the first few months after delivery [Sanders2016; Norwood2014].
- New-onset risk: the relative risk of MG onset was not increased during pregnancy but rose about fivefold in the first 6 months postpartum, highest after a first birth. Exacerbations are also more common in the first 6 months postpartum than during pregnancy [Gilhus2020].
- Previous pregnancies did not predict the course in the next one. Prior thymectomy, antibody level and disease duration did not determine exacerbation [Gilhus2020; Batocchi1999].
- Animation anchors (pregnancy course): about 30% worsen (peak in first trimester), 27-28% worsen postpartum (mostly in the first 6 months), and crisis occurs in 6.4% antenatally and 8.2% postpartum [Miegel2026; Batocchi1999; Banner2022; Gilhus2020].

### Pregnancy: drugs before and during pregnancy
- Planning should start well in advance, and multidisciplinary communication should continue through pregnancy, delivery and postpartum [Sanders2016; Norwood2014].
- Oral pyridostigmine is first-line in pregnancy and does not cross the placenta in significant amounts. Some exacerbations in pregnancy probably reflect dose reduction or withdrawal for fear of harming the baby [Sanders2016; Gilhus2020].
- Prednisone is the immunosuppressant of choice in pregnancy. Azathioprine and cyclosporine are considered relatively safe. Mycophenolate mofetil and methotrexate are contraindicated as teratogenic [Sanders2016].
- Controversy: there was a strong minority opinion against azathioprine in pregnancy. It is the nonsteroidal immunosuppressant of choice in Europe but has been considered high risk in the US, a difference based on few animal studies and case reports [Sanders2016].
- Gilhus2018 and Gilhus2020 regard pyridostigmine, prednisolone and azathioprine as safe in pregnancy and breastfeeding. They list mycophenolate, methotrexate and cyclophosphamide as teratogenic, to be avoided by any woman who may become pregnant [Gilhus2018; Gilhus2020].
- Rituximab crosses the placenta and causes transient neonatal B-cell depletion, which normalises by about 6 months. Gilhus2020 says it should be avoided in the last months before conception and during pregnancy [Gilhus2020].
- IVIG and PLEX are useful when a prompt response is needed in pregnancy. IVIG is often preferred for convenience [Sanders2016; Gilhus2020].
- Thymectomy is postponed until after pregnancy [Sanders2016].
- Biologics, pregnancy data from labels:
  - Eculizumab: limited human data have not identified a specific developmental concern. A murine anti-C5 analogue caused developmental abnormalities at 2-8 times the human dose [FDASoliris2026].
  - Zilucoplan: no human data; embryofetal death in monkeys at exposures similar to human therapeutic levels [FDAZilbrysq2026].
  - Rozanolixizumab: limited human data; embryonic death and impaired immune function in monkeys at above-clinical doses [FDARystiggo2026].
  - Efgartigimod: no adverse embryofetal findings in rats or rabbits at 3-10 times the human dose. Because it lowers maternal IgG, reduced passive immunity in the newborn is anticipated, and live vaccines for exposed infants need risk-benefit consideration [FDAVyvgart2026].
  - Pregnancy registries exist for efgartigimod, zilucoplan and rozanolixizumab.

### Pregnancy: labour, delivery and anaesthesia
- The uterus is smooth muscle and is usually not affected by MG, so early labour should not be hindered. The second stage requires striated-muscle effort when pushing, and fatigability may then worsen [Miegel2026].
- An assisted second stage has been proposed for women symptomatic at delivery, but it is not standard. Guidelines aim for spontaneous vaginal delivery [Banner2022; Sanders2016; Norwood2014].
- Magnesium sulfate is not recommended for eclampsia in MG because of its neuromuscular-blocking effects; barbiturates or phenytoin usually suffice [Sanders2016].
- Myaware advises avoiding IV magnesium if possible [MyawareDrugs].
- Norwegian birth registry (127 births vs 1.9 million): MG was associated with more delivery complications (40.9% vs 32.9%), preterm rupture of membranes (5.5% vs 1.7%), interventions (33.9% vs 20.0%) and caesarean section (17.3% vs 8.6%). 5 children (3.9%) had severe anomalies [Hoff2003].
- Swedish register (443 MG vs 4,249 matched women, 1987-2019):
  - no increase in pregnancy complications
  - less spontaneous onset of labour (69.8% vs 79.5%; aOR 0.59)
  - more induction (16.0% vs 12.3%) and elective caesarean (12.0% vs 8.1%)
  - babies born on average 2 days earlier
  - no increase in low Apgar, small-for-gestational-age or congenital malformation
  These malformation findings differ from the Norwegian 3.9% [OConnor2024].
- Caesarean section in MG is for obstetric indications, which include an exhausted mother in prolonged labour. Vacuum or forceps were used in 9% of MG births vs 6% in the Norwegian population [Gilhus2020].
- Epidural analgesia is preferred to general anaesthesia when possible, and standard MG drugs are continued through labour [Gilhus2020; Norwood2014].
- In a Portuguese series of 17 MG deliveries, epidural block was used in 75% of both stable and exacerbating patients without anaesthetic complications. One woman with undiagnosed MG given general anaesthesia had delayed emergence and respiratory failure [Almeida2010].
- Birth should be at a hospital with neonatal intensive care and rapid access to neonatal critical care [Sanders2016; Gilhus2020; Norwood2014].

### Breastfeeding
- Breastfeeding is encouraged for mothers with AChR or MuSK MG, including when the baby has neonatal MG. Maternal IgG in milk is about 2% of serum levels [Gilhus2020].
- Pyridostigmine, prednisolone/prednisone and azathioprine are not contraindications to breastfeeding. Rituximab, cyclosporine and tacrolimus are probably safe, with rituximab in milk about 200 times lower than in serum. Breastfeeding is advised against with cyclophosphamide, mycophenolate or methotrexate [Gilhus2020].
- Label exception: the ravulizumab label advises stopping breastfeeding during treatment and for 8 months after the final dose [FDAUltomiris2025]. There is no human milk information for efgartigimod [FDAVyvgart2026].

### Transient neonatal MG (TNMG)
- Mechanism: maternal IgG antibodies against AChR, MuSK or LRP4 cross the placenta [Gilhus2018; Gilhus2020].
- Placental IgG transfer timing: IgG transfer does not begin until about week 13, after organogenesis. At weeks 17-22 fetal IgG is still only 5-10% of maternal, and maternal and neonatal levels are similar at delivery [Gilhus2020].
- Incidence varies by series: 9% (5/55) [Batocchi1999], 9% pooled [Miegel2026], about 10% [Gilhus2018; Gilhus2020], 13.0% [Banner2022] and 30% (single centre) [Djelmis2002]. Variation probably reflects differing diagnostic sensitivity [Gilhus2020].
- Onset and duration anchors: weakness nearly always appears in the first 24 h after birth. It usually lasts up to 4 weeks, is most pronounced in the first week, and resolves as maternal IgG is cleared [Gilhus2020]. The commonly quoted onset window of "up to 3 days" was not found in the sources read (unverified).
- Signs: generalised hypotonia, poor sucking, dysphagia and weak cry. Respiratory insufficiency and aspiration are rare but make neonatal-ward observation necessary [Gilhus2020].
- In the Norwegian cohort, 5/125 babies had definite TNMG and another 10 were transferred to a neonatal ward [Gilhus2020].
- Most cases need no treatment. Very low-dose pyridostigmine or neostigmine and feeding support help, and IVIG or PLEX are very rarely needed [Gilhus2020].
- All babies of mothers with MG should be examined for transient weakness, even when maternal MG is well controlled [Sanders2016; Norwood2014].
- Risk factors:
  - A previously affected child raises the risk [Gilhus2020].
  - Maternal thymectomy roughly halved the risk (P = 0.03) [Hoff2007], with a lower rate also reported in [Djelmis2002].
  - In one series, incidence was inversely associated with maternal disease duration [Djelmis2002].
  - Neonatal MG was associated with signs of fetal distress during delivery [Hoff2007].
- Controversy over antibody level: Batocchi1999 and Gilhus2020 found no correlation of TNMG with maternal severity or antibody titre. Vernet-der Garabedian1994 found TNMG correlated with high overall anti-AChR level and especially with a high ratio of anti-fetal to anti-adult AChR antibodies (P < 0.0002) [Batocchi1999; Gilhus2020; VernetderGarabedian1994].

### Arthrogryposis and fetal AChR antibody-related disorders (FARAD/FARIS)
- Maternal antibodies against the fetal AChR isoform (gamma subunit) restrict fetal movement and can cause arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, or a milder persistent myopathy (fetal AChR inactivation syndrome). The proposed umbrella term is FARAD [Allen2023; Gilhus2020].
- Multicentre FARAD cohort (n = 46):
  - 50% of mothers had no prior MG diagnosis.
  - Offspring death occurred in 23.9%, mainly termination for severe arthrogryposis (15.2%) or early respiratory failure (8.7%).
  - Long-term survivors had facial weakness (73.5%), velopharyngeal insufficiency (75%), feeding difficulties (44.4%) and hearing loss (37.5%).
  - Oral salbutamol improved symptoms in 13/16 (81.3%).
  - Maternal IVIG or PLEX early in pregnancy was associated with fewer offspring deaths.
  [Allen2023]
- Hacohen2015 described 8 affected children from 4 families. Mothers had clinically manifest MG or were asymptomatic with raised AChR antibodies, and recurrence risk in later pregnancies is high [Hacohen2015].
- After a child with arthrogryposis, later pregnancies are at definite risk, and Gilhus2020 states such women should receive IVIG or PLEX in later pregnancies [Gilhus2020].
- In the Norwegian registry, 3.9% (5/127) of children of MG mothers had severe anomalies. Other small series reported none, and maternal MG is not considered a major cause of arthrogryposis overall [Hoff2003; Gilhus2020].
- Children of mothers with MG have at least a 10-fold higher risk of MG and other autoimmune disease [Gilhus2018].

### Surgery and anaesthesia
- Preoperative PLEX or IVIG are appropriate in patients with significant bulbar dysfunction [Sanders2016].
- With thorough preoperative evaluation, continued daily pyridostigmine and careful monitoring, patients with MG can usually be anaesthetised (general or peripheral nerve block) without postoperative ventilation. Volatile agents or epidurals can avoid neuromuscular blockers, which, when used, should be given in smaller doses with monitoring [BlichfeldtLauridsen2012].
- Sensitivity to non-depolarising blockers: vecuronium ED50, ED90 and ED95 in MG were 50%, 55% and 56% of normal (ED95 20 vs 36 µg/kg). Sensitivity did not differ with preoperative pyridostigmine or chronic steroids [Eisenkraft1990].
- Resistance to succinylcholine: AChR loss produces resistance to succinylcholine, with dose requirements up to 2.6 times normal. Azathioprine may prolong succinylcholine and inhibit non-depolarising agents [Bhat2024]. The original dose-response paper (Eisenkraft 1988) was not accessible.
- Sugammadex, systematic review of 24 studies (2010-2024): train-of-four recovery ranged from 79.7 s to 10 min, with short extubation times, low postoperative crisis rates and no deaths. Evidence is limited [Kaye2025]. Reversal failure after sugammadex has been reported [Bhat2024].
- Leventhal score (24 thymectomy patients): four preoperative factors predicted postoperative ventilation and correctly classified 91%. The factors are MG duration ≥6 years, chronic respiratory disease, pyridostigmine ≥750 mg/day and vital capacity ≤2.9 L [Leventhal1980; Grant1982].
- Limitations of the Leventhal score: it had some value for thymectomy but none for 41 non-thymectomy procedures [Grant1982]. In 36 thymectomies it showed 22.2% sensitivity and 25% positive predictive value. Postoperative ventilation was linked to balanced anaesthesia (OR 4.2) and especially myorelaxants (OR 13.9) [Chevalley2001].
- Post-thymectomy crisis meta-analysis (15 studies, 2,626 patients): risk factors were prior crisis (RR 3.36), bulbar symptoms (RR 3.59), thymoma (RR 2.10), postoperative morbidity (RR 2.59) and higher pyridostigmine dose. High-dose steroids were associated with less crisis (RR 0.41) [Geng2020].
- Regional anaesthesia: epidural-based techniques are preferred in obstetrics [Gilhus2020; Almeida2010]. Propofol plus epidural for thymectomy was associated with fewer ICU admissions [Chevalley2001].

### Exercise, temperature, weight and sleep
- In 15 patients with generalised MG (MGFA II-IV) randomised to 8 weeks of progressive resistance or aerobic training, adherence was 95%. QMG did not change, and the resistance group gained strength and function. Some adverse events (bulbar symptoms in 2, increased fatigue in 3) occurred [Rahbek2017].
- In 10 patients with mild MG doing supervised aerobic plus resistance training twice weekly for 12 weeks, the MG Composite was unchanged, performance improved and muscle enzymes stayed normal [Westerberg2017].
- Inspiratory muscle training plus breathing retraining (RCT, n = 27, 8 weeks, load increased from 20% to 60% of maximal inspiratory pressure) improved PImax (p = 0.001) and PEmax (p = 0.01) but not lung function [Fregonezi2005].
- Guideline disagreement: Gilhus2019 says active physical training should be encouraged and O'Connor2020 says patients with mild MG can follow general exercise recommendations. The 2020 international consensus update excluded exercise recommendations because the evidence was low quality [Gilhus2019; OConnor2020; Narayanaswami2021].
- Temperature: in 28 patients, slight local cooling reduced the electrophysiological decrement, but force decrement also increased, so cooling was not practical as therapy. Severe cooling (18-22 °C) caused transmission failure [Ricker1977].
- The ice-pack test (ice over the eyelid for up to 5 minutes) is a bedside sign used in children [OConnell2020]. No controlled study quantifying heat-induced worsening was found (unverified).
- Weight and diet: steroid-related weight gain is managed with healthy eating and exercise in juvenile MG [OConnell2020]. Fatigue was associated with higher BMI [Ruiter2021]. No MG-specific diet trial was found.
- Sleep: obstructive sleep apnoea prevalence was 36% in MG vs an expected 15-20% in the general population (OSA syndrome 11% vs 3%) [Nicolle2006].

### Mental health, quality of life and employment
- Meta-analysis (38 studies): pooled depression prevalence was 36% (95% CI 28-45%) and anxiety prevalence 33% (95% CI 25-42%). Depression was mild in 27%, moderate in 14% and severe in 9% [Nadali2023].
- German MG Association survey (1,660 patients vs 2,556 controls): SF-36 physical functioning was 56.0 vs 81.8 and mental health 67.3 vs 74.1. Lower quality of life was associated with depression, anxiety, fatigue, low income and low social support [Lehnerer2022].
- Low mood and depression-related fatigue can be mistaken for myasthenic weakness, leading to overtreatment [OConnell2020].
- Employment meta-analysis (19 studies, 3,600 participants, mean age 47.5): the pooled proportion employed was 50% (95% CI 41-60%), ranging from 28% to 82%. Lower employment was seen with generalised, bulbar and respiratory symptoms [Guastafierro2020].

### Driving
- UK, car or motorcycle: people with MG must tell the DVLA (form CN1); failure to disclose can bring a fine of up to £1,000. Bus and lorry drivers use form CN1V [DVLAMG].
- UK, diplopia, Group 1 (car/motorcycle): driving must stop and the DVLA be notified. Driving may resume once diplopia is controlled, for example by glasses, or with a patch the driver undertakes to wear while driving (monocular rules then apply). Stable uncorrected diplopia of ≥6 months may be licensable with a specialist report of satisfactory functional adaptation [DVLAVision].
- UK, diplopia, Group 2 (bus/lorry): driving must stop, patching is not acceptable, and insuperable diplopia means licence refusal or revocation [DVLAVision].

### Vaccination and infection
- Influenza RCT (47 AChR-MG patients): seroprotection was similar to healthy controls (H3N2 89.4% vs 93.6%; H1N1 95.7% vs 97.9%). AChR titres did not change, MG did not exacerbate, and immunosuppression did not significantly blunt response [Strijbos2019].
- Ontario self-controlled case series: the relative incidence of MG admission in the 6 weeks after influenza vaccination was 0.84 (95% CI 0.65-1.09), so withholding vaccination was not supported [Zinman2009].
- Korean survey (258 patients): MG worsened in 40% after influenza-like illness, 15.6% after a common cold and 1.5% after influenza vaccination [Seok2017].
- Ontario COVID-19 cohort (4,411 MG vs matched controls): people with MG who contracted COVID-19 had more hospital admission (30.5% vs 15.1%) and 30-day mortality (14.6% vs 8.5%) than general population controls. 80.3% received 2 vaccine doses, fewer than 6 of 3,461 first doses were followed by MG admission within 30 days, and vaccination lowered infection risk (HR 0.43) [Alcantara2023].
- Czech COVID-19 series (93 MG patients): 38% had severe pneumonia and 11% died. Higher pre-infection FVC and good MG control were protective, while chronic corticosteroids (dose-related) and recent rituximab raised risk. MG exacerbated during infection in 15% [Jakubikova2021].
- Vaccine review: inactivated and subunit vaccines are considered safe and effective in MG. In a cohort of 80, 5% exacerbated within 6 weeks of SARS-CoV-2 vaccination [Sansone2022].
- Live vaccines: vaccinations are generally recommended, but live organisms should be avoided if possible in immunosuppressed patients [Gilhus2018b].
- The 2020 COVID-19 guidance said only "dead" vaccines should be used in this group [InternationalMGCOVID2020].
- Labels advise against live vaccines during efgartigimod or rozanolixizumab treatment [FDAVyvgart2026; FDARystiggo2026].
- In children, delayed live vaccination contributes to infection susceptibility on steroids [Sanders2016].
- Serious infections, Ontario cohort (3,823 MG patients): 33.4% had a serious infection vs 19.4% of comparators (72.5 vs 35.0 per 1,000 person-years; adjusted HR 1.39), most often bacterial pneumonia [Kassardjian2020].
- Infections can trigger and worsen MG, and a few antibiotics are avoided because of effects on neuromuscular transmission [Gilhus2018b].
- In pregnancy, all puerperal infections were followed by exacerbation [Djelmis2002].

### Children and adolescents (juvenile MG)
- Juvenile MG is MG before age 18. UK incidence of antibody-positive JMG is about 1.5 per million person-years (95% AChR, 5% MuSK), with only 20% diagnosed under 10 years. Norway reports 1.6 per million (0.9 pre-pubertal) [OConnell2020].
- Ocular JMG is more frequent in pre-pubertal children, and spontaneous remission is more frequent than in adults: 14% pre-pubertal vs 5% post-pubertal in a Norwegian series [OConnell2020].
- Young children with only ocular MG can start with pyridostigmine alone [Sanders2016].
- Children face particular steroid risks (growth failure, poor bone mineralisation, infection), so long-term steroids use the lowest effective dose. Maintenance IVIG or PLEX are alternatives [Sanders2016].
- Ophthalmology input is needed to prevent amblyopia. In crisis, PLEX is preferred over IVIG for speed, balanced against feasibility in very young children [OConnell2020].

### Older adults (late-onset MG)
- Late-onset MG is defined as onset at ≥65 years. Its incidence is rising, it is more common in men, and it often presents with ocular or bulbar weakness. Death is often from comorbidity [Alkhawajah2013].
- UK incidence rose steeply from age 45-74, reaching 9.9 per 100,000 in men, then fell after 80. 0.7% of sera from people ≥75 had AChR antibodies, mostly without a diagnosis, which suggests underdiagnosis [Vincent2003].
- Comorbidity is frequent, particularly in elderly patients [Gilhus2019].
- Osteoporosis: in a Taiwanese cohort, MG carried an HR of 1.96 for osteoporosis (2.37 with corticosteroids, 1.52 without) [Yeh2014].
- Controversy on fractures: in Ontario, patients with MG had a lower fracture risk than comparators (HR 0.74). The authors suggest this may reflect osteoporosis prevention [Kassardjian2021].
- Pyridostigmine needs caution in the elderly, the frail and those with cardiac disease (an ECG is suggested), and lower doses may be needed in renal impairment [RightDecisionsPyrido2025].
