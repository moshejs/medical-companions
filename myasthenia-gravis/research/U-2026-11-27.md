## Findings

### biologics · FDA approval of cemdisiran (Livsayis)
- The FDA approved Livsayis (cemdisiran; NDA 221251, Regeneron) on 18 November 2026 for the treatment of generalised myasthenia gravis in adults who are anti-AChR antibody positive (Established: regulatory record) [[FDALivsayis2026]].
- Label dose: 567 mg (three 189 mg/mL vials) by subcutaneous injection once every 3 months; it must be given by a healthcare provider [[FDALivsayis2026]]. NIMBLE reported the dose as 600 mg every 12 weeks [[Vu2026b]]; the label does not explain the difference and the site states each as its source gives it.
- Boxed warning: serious meningococcal infections. Vaccination against serogroups A, C, W, Y and B at least 2 weeks before the first dose; available only through the LIVSAYIS REMS; the patient safety card is carried during treatment and for 12 months after the last dose [[FDALivsayis2026]].
- Label Clinical Studies (Study 1, NCT05070858; 284 randomised, 64 vs 59 in the primary analysis): MG-ADL −4.5 vs −2.2, difference −2.3 (95% CI −3.6 to −1.0; p=0.0005), the same as the published NIMBLE values; QMG −4.2 vs −1.5, difference −2.8 (−4.5 to −1.1; p=0.0015) [[FDALivsayis2026,Vu2026b]].
- Label adverse reactions (at least 5% and more than placebo; 79 vs 72): injection-site reactions 8% vs 3%, viral infections 6% vs 4%, urinary tract infections 5% vs 4% [[FDALivsayis2026]].
- Label mechanism: an siRNA-GalNAc conjugate that degrades C5 mRNA, lowering liver synthesis and circulating levels of C5 [[FDALivsayis2026]].
- The approval covers cemdisiran alone, not the combination with pozelimab.
- This replaces "An FDA decision was targeted for November 2026 (press)" and "under FDA review" for cemdisiran.

### Exam hooks
- Cemdisiran (Livsayis): an siRNA that lowers C5 production, injected by a healthcare provider every 3 months, for AChR-antibody-positive adults; it carries the same boxed warning for meningococcal infection and a REMS as the C5-binding medicines [[FDALivsayis2026]].

### In plain words
- A fifth complement-blocking medicine for myasthenia gravis is now approved in the US. It is injected under the skin at a clinic four times a year, and it needs the same meningococcal vaccines first as the other medicines of its kind [[FDALivsayis2026]].
