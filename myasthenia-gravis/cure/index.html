Toward a cure

No therapy has yet been shown to cure myasthenia gravis reliably. This module follows the immune chain that makes the harmful antibodies and shows where each research route cuts it, with an evidence label. Press play to move the cut along the chain, or pick a route.

Research reported, not recommended. Many of these results come from small, early or unpublished studies. Whether any person could join a trial or receive one of these treatments is a question for their specialist team.

The chain

Where the antibodies come from

In AChR MG the thymus is part of the disease, with hyperplasia and germinal centres in early-onset disease and a thymoma in about 10 to 15%. 198 T helper cells drive B cells to become plasma cells that make IgG against the receptor. FcRn recycles IgG so it lasts in the blood, and bound IgG switches on complement, whose membrane attack complex is found at essentially every MG endplate examined. 380,460 Each treatment cuts this chain at a different point.

In plain wordsHarmful antibodies come off a production line of immune cells. Every treatment, old or new, acts somewhere along that line.
Exam hookNo therapy has yet been shown to cure MG reliably. A 2026 review calls thymectomy only sometimes curative and describes the approved biologics as continuous suppression at significant cost. 114

Negative and halted results

Results that did not work, or worked too little to continue, belong on the map too. They show which cuts did not hold.

Negative

Belimumab: blocking BAFF alone

In a randomised double-blind phase 2 of 40 people, the change in QMG at week 24 did not differ from placebo (P=0.256), nor did any secondary endpoint. AChR antibody levels fell in both arms. No later MG programme followed. 236

Futile

Rituximab in BeatMG

In 52 people with established AChR-positive MG on at least 15 mg prednisone a day, 60% on rituximab and 56% on placebo met the steroid-sparing goal at week 52, so a meaningful advantage in a larger trial was judged unlikely. In new-onset disease, RINOMAX found the opposite. 396,425

Negative

Mezagitamab: anti-CD38

The randomised placebo-controlled phase 2 (36 people) posted MG-ADL and QMG results that did not separate from placebo (posted P values 0.299 to 0.772). Adverse events rose with dose: 66.7% on placebo, 75.0% on 300 mg and 91.7% on 600 mg. 93

Halted

Satralizumab: anti-IL-6 receptor

LUMINESCE (188 randomised) met its primary endpoint, but the MG-ADL difference was 1.02 points, which its authors called small. The sponsor halted development in generalized MG and the extension was terminated. Open-label tocilizumab in refractory disease showed larger differences without randomisation; JAK inhibition has only a 19-patient single-arm study. 92,221,463,572

Corrections, and reports we could not confirm.

  • No riliprubart MG trial. The C1s inhibitor riliprubart has data in cold agglutinin disease and phase 3 programmes in CIDP, but no myasthenia gravis trial was found in PubMed or ClinicalTrials.gov on 20 October 2026. The C1s route in MG is an extrapolation, not an MG programme. 108
  • MuSK-CAART has no posted results. Its phase 1 (7 enrolled) is listed as completed, with no results on the registry and no peer-reviewed report. 94
  • Conflicting trade reports, not confirmed. Two reports of the KYSA-6 anti-CD19 CAR-T phase 2 give different 24-week effects, and two conference reports of the telitacicept China phase 3 give different means. The press releases on the first CAR-T-treated MG patient give different ages (33 and 34). 122,306,360,389,525

What would count as a cure

Definitions

Remission has a precise meaning

The MGFA research standards define post-intervention categories including complete stable remission, pharmacologic remission and minimal manifestations. 271 The 2020 consensus sets the treatment goal as minimal manifestation status or better, with no more than mild medication side effects. Minimal manifestation status means no symptoms or functional limits but some weakness on examination; remission means no symptoms or signs, and excludes people taking daily cholinesterase inhibitors that help. 381

A 2026 review proposes that a cure would be complete remission without ongoing treatment, with a high chance of success, low relapse risk, repeatability after relapse, proportionate toxicity and cost-effectiveness. 114

Under conventional care, lasting drug-free remission is rare: in 421 people followed about 10 years, 12.2% after thymectomy and 4.3% without reached complete stable remission with all treatment stopped for at least a year. 571

Exam hookClinical remission is not the same as serological cure: after anti-CD19 CAR-T and after autologous transplant, people were symptom-free while AChR antibodies stayed detectable. 230,481

Open questions

These are gaps the research literature itself names. Most of the evidence on this page is expected to change within about three years, as seven registered CAR-T trials with more than 260 patients report between 2026 and 2029. 231,242

Does removing the thymus add anything after a B-cell or plasma-cell reset?

Unresolved. The randomised thymectomy evidence covers only MGTX's population (18 to 65, AChR-positive, no thymoma, under 5 years); remission data elsewhere are retrospective. 552,571

Do antibody levels need to fall for remission?

Apparently not always: remission after anti-CD19 CAR-T and after autologous transplant occurred with AChR antibodies still detectable. In 62 people on standard treatment, falling titres did parallel falling scores over 6 months. 230,329,481

What drives relapse when B cells come back?

One candidate is a population of FCRL5-high age-associated B cells, seen in a relapse after BCMA/CD19 CAR-T and after rituximab. 256

Can the chemotherapy before CAR-T be left out?

Two MG series and the mRNA anti-BCMA platform omitted lymphodepletion. Whether results hold without it is open. 464,542

How long does remission last?

The longest drug-free follow-up is after autologous transplant, a median 6.7 years (range 1.0 to 21.9). CAR-T follow-up in MG is at least 19 months in three patients, and generally under two years across autoimmune neurology. 39,54,230

What are the long-term harms?

Across autoimmune CAR-T studies: hypogammaglobulinaemia 47% (any grade), infection within 3 months 29%, and cytopenias. Transplant-related mortality was 9.5% at 100 days in the largest MG series. Malignancy risk over decades is unknown. 39,54,554

Who will pay, and who will get access?

A 2026 review makes cost-effectiveness part of its definition of cure and notes that current biologics deliver chronic suppression at significant cost. 114

Can we predict who will respond?

Predictive biomarkers are underdeveloped. Pre-treatment miR-150-5p separated responders from non-responders at 12 months (AUC 0.85 to 0.86) in one prospective cohort, and antibody subunit patterns define two endotypes in 513 patients. 46,189,412
