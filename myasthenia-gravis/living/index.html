EmergencyAt any stage of life, new or worsening difficulty breathing or swallowing in a person with MG is an emergency: nursing teaching material tells patients to contact the prescriber urgently for these signs. In a newborn of a mother with MG, the signs described are floppiness, poor sucking, swallowing trouble and a weak cry, nearly always within 24 hours of birth, and the guidance asks that every such baby be examined. 199,353,410,476

Months before

Planning, and the drugs that stop

The 2016 international consensus asks for planning well in advance, with multidisciplinary communication through pregnancy, delivery and after birth. It lists mycophenolate mofetil and methotrexate as contraindicated because they are teratogenic; Gilhus adds cyclophosphamide, and says all three should be avoided by any woman who may become pregnant. Rituximab crosses the placenta and Gilhus advises avoiding it in the last months before conception. Thymectomy is postponed until after pregnancy. 199,395,476

In plain wordsSome MG medicines can harm a developing baby, so they are changed months before trying to get pregnant. Others are safe to keep taking.
Exam hookMycophenolate and methotrexate are stopped before conception (teratogenic). Pyridostigmine and prednisone continue. 476

Who is more likely to have an affected baby

  • A previously affected child raises the risk.
  • Maternal thymectomy roughly halved it in one registry (P = 0.03), and a lower rate was also reported elsewhere.
  • In one series incidence fell with longer maternal disease duration; neonatal MG also went with signs of fetal distress in delivery.
  • Antibody level is disputed: two series found no link with maternal severity or titre, another found neonatal MG tracked a high level and a high ratio of anti-fetal to anti-adult receptor antibodies.

35,104,199,240,533

Delivery, in two registries

  • Norway, 127 births: more delivery complications (40.9% vs 32.9%), interventions (33.9% vs 20.0%) and caesarean section (17.3% vs 8.6%); 3.9% of children had severe anomalies.
  • Sweden, 443 women: no increase in pregnancy complications, less spontaneous labour (69.8% vs 79.5%), more induction and elective caesarean, babies born about 2 days earlier, and no increase in malformation.
  • The two disagree on malformations.

239,402

Anaesthesia for birth

  • Epidural analgesia is preferred to general anaesthesia when possible, and standard MG drugs continue through labour.
  • In a Portuguese series of 17 deliveries, epidurals were used in 75% without anaesthetic complications; one woman with undiagnosed MG given a general anaesthetic had delayed emergence and respiratory failure.
  • Vacuum or forceps were used in 9% of MG births vs 6% in the Norwegian population.

14,199,395

The longer view for the child

  • Children of mothers with MG have at least a 10-fold higher risk of MG and other autoimmune disease.
  • Rituximab exposure in pregnancy depletes the newborn's B cells, which normalise by about 6 months.
  • Oral salbutamol improved symptoms in 13 of 16 children with fetal AChR antibody disorders.

13,196,199

Established Surgery & anaesthesia

Anaesthesia can usually be managed

Before the operation
The consensus calls preoperative plasma exchange or IVIG appropriate for people with significant bulbar dysfunction. A review says that with thorough evaluation, continued daily pyridostigmine and careful monitoring, people with MG can usually be anaesthetised, general or nerve block, without postoperative ventilation. 51,476
Muscle relaxants
People with MG needed about half the usual vecuronium: ED50, ED90 and ED95 were 50%, 55% and 56% of normal, unaffected by preoperative pyridostigmine or steroids. Fewer receptors make them resistant to succinylcholine instead, up to 2.6 times the usual dose. Volatile agents or epidurals can avoid relaxants altogether; when used, smaller doses with monitoring. 47,51,112
Sugammadex
A systematic review of 24 studies found train-of-four recovery from 79.7 seconds to 10 minutes, short extubation times, low postoperative crisis rates and no deaths, from limited evidence. Reversal failure has been reported. 47,286
Leventhal factors
In 24 thymectomy patients, four preoperative factors classified 91% correctly for postoperative ventilation: MG for 6 years or more, chronic respiratory disease, pyridostigmine 750 mg a day or more, and vital capacity 2.9 L or less. It had no value in 41 other operations, and in 36 later thymectomies sensitivity was 22.2%; there, ventilation went with balanced anaesthesia (OR 4.2) and especially muscle relaxants (OR 13.9). 79,207,317
Exam hookMG is sensitive to non-depolarising relaxants (about half the vecuronium dose) and resistant to succinylcholine (up to 2.6 times). 47,112
In plain wordsAnaesthetists plan around MG. With preparation, most people with MG have operations without needing a ventilator afterwards; the muscle-relaxing drugs are the part that needs care.

Questions people bring to a clinic visit

Plain-words prompts built from what the guidance says should be discussed. They are starting points for a conversation, not advice.

  • If I plan a pregnancy, which of my medicines would need to change, and how far ahead?
  • Where should I give birth, and will the baby be checked for weakness after birth?
  • Before an operation, does the anaesthetist know I have MG, and should my pyridostigmine continue?
  • Which vaccines are right for me on my current treatment, and are any live vaccines a problem?
  • Who do I tell about double vision and driving where I live?
  • Is my tiredness the MG, or could it be sleep or mood?
In plain wordsMost people with MG work, exercise and have children. The planning is mostly about medicines, operations and infections.

Where the evidence stops

  • The commonly quoted Leventhal point weights could not be traced to a primary source, so only the four risk factors are given. 207,317
  • No controlled study measuring heat-induced worsening of MG was found. 451
  • No MG-specific trial of diet or weight management was found; steroid weight gain is managed with healthy eating and exercise in juvenile MG. 400
  • No US national driving rule specific to MG was found; only the UK DVLA rules are given. 109
  • The onset window for neonatal MG often quoted as "up to 3 days" was not found; the source read says weakness nearly always appears within 24 hours. 199
  • The succinylcholine 2.6-fold figure is cited through a review, because the original 1988 paper could not be accessed. 47
