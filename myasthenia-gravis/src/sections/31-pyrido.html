Doses as published

These are the figures printed in labels and guidance, quoted for study. They are not a dose for anyone: the dose, the form and the timing for any person are set by the treating clinician, who adjusts them to symptoms. The international consensus says pyridostigmine should be part of initial treatment in most patients, with the dose adjusted to symptoms. [[Sanders2016]]

Side effects

Common, and often from the gut

The US label sorts them into muscarinic effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal cramps, more gut movement, saliva and bronchial secretions, small pupils, sweating) and nicotinic effects (muscle cramps, fasciculations, weakness). Bromide can cause a rash. [[FDAMestinon2020]]

  • Dutch-Belgian survey: 91% of current users reported side effects, against 55% of a control group. The most frequent were flatulence, urinary urgency, muscle cramps, blurred vision and sweating. 26% of those who stopped did so because of side effects. [[RemijnNelissen2022]]
  • Korean clinic, 71 patients: 13% could not tolerate it and 24% had side effects. [[Lee2016]]
Safety

Cholinergic crisis, and why atropine can hide it

An overdose can cause cholinergic crisis: weakness that grows until it reaches the breathing muscles, and that may be hard to tell from myasthenic crisis by symptoms alone. The label says it calls for prompt withdrawal of all such drugs and immediate atropine. Atropine can usually counteract the muscarinic effects, but the label warns that this can mask signs of overdose. The consensus adds that excess drug cannot be completely excluded as a cause of worsening in a crisis, and that these drugs increase airway secretions. [[FDAMestinon2020,Sanders2016]]

Difficulty breathing or swallowing is an emergency.

Exam hookToo much pyridostigmine causes weakness, not strength: receptors desensitise. The label's muscarinic signs (secretions, cramps, sweating, small pupils) can come with excess. [[Mehndiratta2014,FDAMestinon2020]]
Evidence check

IMPACT-MG 2026 against the Cochrane view

A 2014 Cochrane review found no randomised trial of oral cholinesterase inhibitors in generalized MG and judged that the observed response was so clear that a placebo-controlled trial "would be difficult to justify". [[Mehndiratta2014]]

IMPACT-MG part 1 (2026) ran one anyway: a double-blind crossover in 19 people with AChR MG already on pyridostigmine, two 5-day periods with a 2-day washout. Pyridostigmine beat placebo on every measure: MG Impairment Index by 5.3 points (95% CI 1.9–8.7), QMG by 1.4, MG-ADL by 1.2 and MG-QOL15r by 2.0. [[RemijnNelissen2026]]

The two views differ in emphasis: the benefit is real but modest, about 1.2 MG-ADL points over 5 days. [[Mehndiratta2014,RemijnNelissen2026]]

MuSK MG

A weaker response, and more side effects

The international consensus and the German guideline both say people with MuSK MG respond poorly to cholinesterase inhibitors and often get side effects at conventional doses. In 37 Italian MuSK-positive patients the edrophonium or neostigmine test was equivocal or negative in 11 (29.7%), and the oral response ranged from mild benefit to overt intolerance. [[Sanders2016,Wiendl2023,Evoli2003]]

One MuSK patient showed extra repetitive discharges after the muscle response, possibly triggered by the drug, which the authors read as abnormal sensitivity to acetylcholine (a single case). In MuSK passive-transfer mice, pyridostigmine at therapeutically relevant levels made receptor loss worse. Early evidence [[Punga2006,Morsch2013]]

Neostigmine and edrophonium

All three are reversible quaternary cholinesterase inhibitors with low oral bioavailability. Oral neostigmine is absorbed even less: 30 mg gives plasma levels of only 1–5 µg/L. Current US injectable neostigmine and injectable pyridostigmine (Regonol) labels are only for reversing muscle-relaxant drugs after surgery, not for MG. [[Aquilonius1986,FDANeostigmine2024,FDARegonol2021]]

Edrophonium is short-acting and was used for the diagnostic "Tensilon test". In a Vienna series of 182 MG patients and 324 controls it had 83.5% sensitivity and 87.7% specificity; MuSK MG patients were less likely to respond (OR 0.08), and 11.5% had adverse events, 91.4% of them mild. [[Mehndiratta2014,Keritam2026]]

Not for MG

Amifampridine is a Lambert-Eaton drug

Amifampridine (Firdapse) blocks potassium channels on the nerve terminal. In the US it is labelled only for Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome (LEMS), in adults and children aged 6 and over. [[FDAFirdapse2026]]

IMPACT-MG part 2 tested it in 20 people with AChR MG not well controlled on pyridostigmine. Adding 30 or 60 mg was no better than placebo, and adverse events were more common (60% and 75% against 30%). [[RemijnNelissen2026b]]

Cautions printed on the label

  • Contraindicated in mechanical intestinal or urinary obstruction; caution in asthma (US label) and in obstructive airway disease such as asthma and COPD (UK SmPC). [[FDAMestinon2020,UKSmPCMestinon2025]]
  • The US label says it is excreted mainly unchanged by the kidney, so lower doses may be needed in kidney disease; severe renal impairment prolongs elimination. The UK SmPC says 5–15% of an oral dose appears unchanged in urine. [[FDAMestinon2020,Aquilonius1986,UKSmPCMestinon2025]]
  • Methylcellulose has been reported to block absorption completely. [[Aquilonius1986]]

Meals and timing

Food delays the peak from 1.7 to 3.2 hours without changing the amount absorbed. [[Aquilonius1980]] The US label says only that doses are spaced to give the most strength when it is most needed. [[FDAMestinon2020]] An NHS Scotland Q&A says the drug usually starts working in 30 to 60 minutes, so doses are taken about 30 to 60 minutes before meals; a Myaware nurse page gives about 20 minutes for people on an altered diet. No neurology guideline read for this guide sets meal timing. [[RightDecisionsPyrido2025,MyawareNurses]]

In plain wordsA meal slows the tablet down. It does not make it weaker overall, but the best moment comes later.

How long people stay on it

In the Dutch-Belgian registry, 93% of patients (78 of 84) started with pyridostigmine and 65% (54 of 83) were still taking it at 10 years. In a survey of 410 registry patients, median self-rated effectiveness was 60 (IQR 28–78) and net benefit 65 (IQR 45–84), on what appears to be a 0–100 scale. [[RemijnNelissen2026c,RemijnNelissen2022]]
