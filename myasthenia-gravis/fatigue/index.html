Part 1 · The disease

Weakness over a day

In MG, weakness grows with use and through the day, and eases with rest and cold. Press play to run a waking day beside the junction's electrical rundown, then switch between a healthy and an MG junction and try rest breaks, an ice pack and heat.

Fatigability is the hallmark of MG. Weakness fluctuates from day to day and hour to hour, is generally worse later in the day, and can be increased by sustained exercise and a higher body temperature. 276 It eases with rest and recurs with use, and it lessens in the cold. 367 The cause sits at the junction: each nerve impulse releases a little less acetylcholine than the one before, and in MG the margin for that loss has almost gone. 301,467

07:00 · Morning

Strongest after sleep

Weakness fluctuates from hour to hour and is generally worse later in the day 276. Willis described it in 1672: patients well in the morning and weak by noon 100. At a healthy junction each impulse normally releases 2-4 times more transmitter than is needed to fire the muscle fibre: the safety factor 497. Ruff and Lennon measured it in human intercostal muscle as 2.98 in controls and 1.09 in MG 467.

In plain wordsAfter a night's sleep the muscles work best. In MG, though, the message from nerve to muscle has almost no spare strength even in the morning.

The safety factor, in numbers

Healthy margin2-5×

more transmitter than needed: 2-4 times at low firing rates 497, generally 3-5 in adult mammals 555, four to five times in a clinical review 301. Humans rely on elaborate postsynaptic folds rather than on releasing a lot of transmitter 555.

Measured in people2.98 → 1.09

safety factor in healthy versus MG intercostal muscle, 37% of control. MG miniature potentials were 53% and EPPs 58% of control, and a fibre needed 21.6 mV of depolarisation to fire instead of 13.5 mV. Loss of sodium current accounted for 40% of the fall 467.

Two losses, not one

Endplate sodium current fell to about 60% of control in MG, while miniature potentials fell to under 30%: receptor loss is larger than sodium-channel loss 466. The sodium channels are lost with the endplate membrane that complement destroys, not by a direct antibody effect 467.

A small reserve to start with20-30

quanta (packets) released per impulse at human junctions, against 50-100 in mice, the smallest known among vertebrates studied 497. During 10-100 Hz firing, quantal content and EPP size fall but normally stay adequate 497.

The nerve tries to compensate

In muscle from people with AChR MG and from rats with experimental MG, nerve endings released more quanta than normal, and the increase at each endplate tracked the loss in miniature-potential size 427. MuSK MG may lack this compensation: in mice given patient MuSK IgG4, release did not rise and depression during fast firing was exaggerated 294. Early evidence (mouse study)

Proximal muscles first

On RNS the decrement completes earlier in the deltoid (shoulder) than in a hand muscle, consistent with a lower safety factor in proximal muscles 522. Proximal RNS is abnormal in 60-70% of generalized MG, distal in 30-35% 461. More in Diagnosis and The neuromuscular junction.

Bedside tests

Three quick tests use fatigability itself. Sustained upgaze provokes ptosis, an ice pack relieves it, and a rest lets it recover. Pick a test and press play; the accuracy figures are the published ones.

In 155 consecutive ptosis referrals (102 with ocular MG), the ice pack test had 86% sensitivity and 79% specificity; combined with single-fibre EMG, sensitivity rose to 98% if either was positive, and specificity to 92% if both were 194. Across 20 studies (1,264 people), sensitivity ranged from 38.5% to 100%; specificity was above 95% in six studies, and no adverse effects were reported 431.

Ice is held over the symptomatic eye for 2-5 minutes, and 2 mm or more of improvement counts as positive, a cut-off Rousseff calls somewhat arbitrary because heat produced similar changes in some patients; some report specificity as low as about 25% 461. Mean improvement was 2.3 mm with ice, 1.3 mm with rest and 0.33 mm with heat, and repeating the ice test raised sensitivity by 35% 564.

Exam hookIce improves myasthenic ptosis; a lift of about 2 mm or more strongly suggests it. 128
Cogan's lid twitch

After looking down, then back to the front, the lid overshoots and twitches. Sensitivity 50-99% and specificity 75-100% across 7 studies 426; "relatively low" in a 35-patient prospective series 529.

Forced eyelid closure

An excess upward lid overshoot after squeezing the eyes shut for 5-10 seconds: sensitivity 94% and specificity 91% in 48 patients 22.

Enhanced ptosis (curtain sign)

Lifting the more drooping lid makes the other one fall. It is described clinically, but our sources found no study of its accuracy, so no figure can be given.

Edrophonium, now historical

The Tensilon test acts within 30 s and lasts about 5 min, but serious adverse events include bradyarrhythmia to asystole and bronchospasm 461. It has been discontinued in the US as a test for MG since 2018 564.

What makes it worse

Heat and fever

A higher body temperature can increase weakness, and fever is a recognised trigger for worsening 276. Cooler temperatures lessen it 367. Our sources found no controlled study measuring how much heat worsens MG.

Cold, but only a little

In 28 patients slight local cooling reduced the electrical decrement, but the force decrement also increased, so cooling was not practical as a treatment; severe cooling (18-22 °C) caused transmission failure 451.

Sustained effort

Sustained exercise can increase weakness 276. Supervised training is a different thing: in two small trials (15 and 10 people), 8-12 weeks of aerobic or resistance training did not worsen MG scores and improved strength or performance, though a few people in the larger trial had bulbar symptoms or more fatigue 440,548. See Living with MG.

Infection

Intercurrent illness, especially viral respiratory infection, worsens symptoms 276. Infection was the commonest trigger (38%) of myasthenic crisis in one series 512.

Thyroid, pregnancy, medicines

Thyroid disease, pregnancy and drugs that impair neuromuscular transmission can all worsen MG 276. With antibiotics the real-world risk is small and often confounded by the infection itself: worsening followed 2.4% of ciprofloxacin and 1.3% of amoxicillin courses (p = 0.68) 527. A 2021 review says infections must still be treated, and lists antibiotic classes to avoid if possible and others it considers safe 490.

Tiredness is not only weakness

Fatigue, as distinct from muscle fatigability, affects about 60% of patients and is linked to severity, depression and inactivity 69. Severe fatigue was present in 62% of 420 Dutch registry patients and was associated with BMI and depressive symptoms 468. Obstructive sleep apnoea was found in 36%, against an expected 15-20% in the general population 392. Low mood can be mistaken for myasthenic weakness and lead to overtreatment 400.

Sudden difficulty breathing or swallowing is an emergency, not ordinary fatigue. See Myasthenic crisis, and for medicines, Drugs that can worsen MG.

Exam hookFatigability is the hallmark. The decrement on repetitive nerve stimulation is the electrical picture of the same rundown: a reproducible fall of 10% or more from the 1st to the 4th or 5th response at 2-5 Hz. 1,301
In plain wordsEach time a nerve fires in quick succession, it releases a little less messenger. With fewer catchers on the muscle, a few fibres stop responding, and the muscle tires. Rest lets the nerve ending restock.
