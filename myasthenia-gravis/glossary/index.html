Glossary

Every specialist term used in the guide. Type to filter; the arrow on each entry jumps to the module where it matters most.

129 of 129 terms

Acetylcholine (ACh)The transmitter the motor nerve releases at the junction. Each vesicle holds a quantum of about 10,000 molecules; two ACh molecules must bind to open one receptor.
Acetylcholine receptor (AChR)The ion channel on the muscle side of the junction that opens when ACh binds. A five-subunit protein (adult form α2βδε), packed on the crests of the junctional folds. The main target of MG antibodies.
Acetylcholinesterase (AChE)The enzyme in the synaptic cleft that breaks down ACh, ending each signal within about a millisecond. Pyridostigmine inhibits it.
AgrinA protein released by the motor nerve that binds LRP4 and switches on MuSK, starting the signal that clusters AChRs under the nerve.
Amifampridine (3,4-DAP)A potassium-channel blocker, US-labelled for Lambert-Eaton syndrome, not MG; in IMPACT-MG it did not beat placebo as an add-on in AChR-MG.
AminoglycosidesAntibiotics such as gentamicin and tobramycin. Labels warn of curare-like effects at the junction and advise caution in MG.
Antigenic modulationLoss of receptors because antibodies cross-link neighbouring AChRs, which are then internalised and degraded about three times faster than normal.
Arthrogryposis (FARAD)Joint contractures present at birth. Maternal antibodies against the fetal form of the AChR can restrict movement in the womb; the umbrella term is fetal AChR antibody-related disorders (FARAD).
AtropineAn antimuscarinic drug. The pyridostigmine label says it can counter muscarinic side effects but may mask signs of overdose and an unnoticed cholinergic crisis.
AutoantibodyAn antibody that targets the body's own tissue. In MG the main targets are AChR, MuSK and LRP4.
Autologous HSCTAutologous haematopoietic stem cell transplantation: high-dose conditioning wipes out the immune repertoire and the person's own stem cells rebuild it. Early evidence in severe refractory MG, with 9.5% transplant-related mortality at 100 days in the largest series.
AzathioprineAn oral steroid-sparing immunosuppressant. First response after 4-10 months. The Nordic guidance advises testing TPMT activity before starting, because low activity risks severe marrow toxicity.
BCMAB-cell maturation antigen, a surface marker of plasmablasts and plasma cells, the cells that make antibodies. The target of Descartes-08 and dual BCMA/CD19 CAR-T.
BiPAP / NIVNon-invasive ventilation through a mask. In one series it avoided intubation in about 58% of crises where it was the first support; a PaCO2 above 45 mmHg at the start predicted failure.
Botulinum toxinA neurotoxin injected for blepharospasm, dystonia and cosmetic use. Labels carry a boxed warning for distant spread and advise monitoring in MG; it raises jitter for 6 months or more.
BotulismPoisoning by botulinum toxin: acute, symmetric paralysis descending from the cranial nerves in an alert, afebrile person, sometimes with dilated pupils. An MG mimic.
Bulbar weaknessWeakness of the muscles for speaking, chewing and swallowing (controlled from the brainstem, or bulb). The main first symptom in up to 15% of people with MG.
C5Complement component 5, the last common step before the membrane attack complex forms. The target of eculizumab, ravulizumab, zilucoplan and newer agents.
CAR-T cell therapyT cells engineered with a chimeric antigen receptor that makes them kill cells bearing a chosen marker, such as CD19 or BCMA. Early evidence in MG from small series and one small randomized trial.
Cell-based assay (CBA)An antibody test using cells that display the target protein in its natural form, for example clustered AChR with rapsyn. It finds antibodies that standard assays miss.
Checkpoint-inhibitor MGMG triggered or worsened by cancer immunotherapy that blocks PD-1, PD-L1 or CTLA-4. Median onset about 4 weeks; in a 65-patient series 37% also had myositis and 8% myocarditis.
Cholinergic crisisWeakness from too much cholinesterase inhibitor, with muscarinic signs (sweating, salivation, diarrhoea, slow heart) and fasciculations. Now rare, and hard to tell from myasthenic crisis by symptoms alone.
CMAPCompound muscle action potential: the summed electrical response of a muscle to a nerve stimulus, measured in nerve conduction and RNS studies.
Cogan's lid twitchA brief upward twitch of the eyelid seen on eye examination, a bedside sign of MG. Across 7 studies its sensitivity was 50-99% and specificity 75-100%.
ColQThe collagen tail that anchors acetylcholinesterase to the basal lamina of the cleft. Without it, mice have no AChE at the junction.
ComplementA cascade of blood proteins that can punch holes in membranes. In AChR-MG, antibody-bound receptors trigger the classical pathway and the membrane attack complex destroys the junctional folds.
Complete stable remission (CSR)MGFA post-intervention status: no symptoms or signs of MG for at least 1 year, with no MG therapy during that time.
Congenital myasthenic syndromes (CMS)Inherited, non-autoimmune disorders of the junction caused by variants in 32 known genes. Onset is usually from birth to childhood, antibodies are negative, and immunotherapy does not help.
CorticosteroidsPrednisone or prednisolone: the usual first immunosuppressant in MG. They can cause early transient worsening, and long-term use brings bone, glucose and infection risks.
CPEOChronic progressive external ophthalmoplegia, usually mitochondrial: slowly progressive, symmetric eye-movement weakness with little double vision and no fluctuation.
Cytokine release syndrome (CRS)A systemic inflammatory reaction after CAR-T infusion, with fever and sometimes low blood pressure. In MG series it has been mainly grade 1-2.
D-penicillamineA drug that can induce true AChR-antibody MG in up to 7% of treated patients, usually mild and ocular, often remitting within a year of stopping.
DecrementThe fall in CMAP amplitude across a train of nerve stimuli. A reproducible decrement of 10% or more at 2-5 Hz supports MG.
DiplopiaDouble vision from weak, misaligned eye muscles. With ptosis, the most common first symptom of MG.
Dok-7A cytoplasmic adaptor protein that carries the MuSK signal; mice without it form no AChR clusters or neuromuscular synapses.
Double-seronegative MGMG with neither AChR nor MuSK antibodies, about 6-10% of MG. Some patients have LRP4 or clustered-AChR antibodies.
Early-onset MGMG beginning before age 50. Linked to thymic hyperplasia and, in people of European descent, the HLA A1-B8-DR3 haplotype.
EculizumabAn IV monoclonal antibody against C5, approved by the FDA in 2017 for AChR-antibody-positive gMG; the first complement inhibitor for MG.
Edrophonium (Tensilon) testAn IV test with a very short-acting cholinesterase inhibitor. Rarely used now because mimics test positive and it carries cardiac risks.
EfgartigimodAn FcRn blocker given IV or SC in 4-week cycles. Approved in the US in 2021 (AChR+) and widened in May 2026 to adults with gMG regardless of antibody status.
Endplate potential (EPP)The local depolarisation of the endplate after one nerve impulse. If it reaches threshold it fires a muscle action potential; in MG many EPPs fall short.
Experimental autoimmune MG (EAMG)The animal model in which immunising an animal with purified AChR causes myasthenic weakness; first shown in rabbits in 1973.
Extended transsternal thymectomyRemoval of the thymus and surrounding fat through a split breastbone, taking about 85-95% of thymic tissue. The operation tested in MGTX.
Fab-arm exchangeThe swapping of half-molecules between IgG4 antibodies, which makes most MuSK IgG4 bispecific and functionally monovalent, unable to cross-link its target.
FatigabilityWeakness that builds with repeated or sustained use and eases with rest: the hallmark of MG, as distinct from general tiredness (fatigue).
FatigueA feeling of tiredness or lack of energy, separate from muscle fatigability. Reported by about 60% of people with MG.
FcRnThe neonatal Fc receptor, which rescues IgG from breakdown inside cells and recycles it, giving IgG a half-life of about 19-21 days.
FcRn blockerA drug that occupies FcRn so IgG, including pathogenic antibodies, is degraded. Total IgG falls by about 55-80% and recovers after dosing stops: efgartigimod, rozanolixizumab, nipocalimab.
FluoroquinolonesAntibiotics such as ciprofloxacin and levofloxacin. They carry an FDA boxed warning for exacerbation of MG.
Generalized MG (gMG)MG affecting muscles beyond the eyes: bulbar, neck, limb or breathing muscles (MGFA class II-V).
IgG1 and IgG3IgG subclasses that activate complement. Most AChR antibodies belong to them, which is why complement drives damage in AChR-MG.
IgG4An IgG subclass that does not activate complement and undergoes Fab-arm exchange. Most MuSK antibodies are IgG4.
Impending crisisRapid worsening of MG that, in the treating physician's opinion, could lead to crisis within days to weeks (2016 consensus).
InebilizumabAn anti-CD19 B-cell-depleting antibody given IV every 6 months after two loading doses. FDA-approved in December 2025 for AChR+ or MuSK+ gMG.
Inspiratory muscle trainingBreathing exercises against a resistance. In a 27-person randomized trial in MG it improved inspiratory and expiratory pressures but not lung volumes.
IVIGIntravenous immunoglobulin: pooled donor IgG used as a fast-acting treatment in exacerbations and before surgery. Boxed warning for thrombosis and kidney injury.
JitterVariation in the timing of the muscle response between two fibres of one motor unit, measured by single-fibre or concentric-needle EMG. The most sensitive test for MG, but not specific.
Juvenile MGMG beginning before age 18, about 10% of cases. Ocular forms and spontaneous remission are more common in pre-pubertal children.
Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome (LEMS)An autoimmune disease of the nerve terminal with antibodies to P/Q calcium channels. Proximal leg weakness, dry mouth, low reflexes, and a CMAP that increments after exercise; about half have small-cell lung cancer.
Late-onset MGMG beginning at age 50 or later. Its incidence is rising, mainly in men, and titin antibodies are more common (58% vs 10% in early-onset MG).
Live vaccineA vaccine containing a weakened living organism. Guidance says to avoid it if possible under immunosuppression, and FcRn blocker labels advise against it during treatment; inactivated and subunit vaccines are considered safe in MG.
LRP4Low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein 4, the agrin receptor that activates MuSK. LRP4 antibodies (mainly IgG1) are found in some double-seronegative MG.
Magnesium sulfateAn IV drug that blocks neuromuscular transmission. The consensus does not recommend it for eclampsia in MG.
Main immunogenic region (MIR)A site at the top of the AChR α1 subunit that half or more of MG antibodies bind.
Membrane attack complex (MAC)C5b-9, the pore-forming end product of complement. It is found at endplates in MG biopsies, where it destroys the junctional folds.
Meningococcal vaccinationVaccination against Neisseria meningitidis serogroups A, C, W, Y and B, required by labels at least 2 weeks before a C5 inhibitor because C5 blockade raises meningococcal infection risk.
MG CompositeA 10-item scale with weighted scoring, maximum 50. A 3-point improvement signified clinical improvement in validation.
MG-ADLMyasthenia Gravis Activities of Daily Living: 8 patient-reported items, each 0-3, total 0-24. A 2-point improvement indicates clinical improvement.
MG-QOL15rThe revised 15-item MG quality-of-life questionnaire with a 3-response format, the preferred MG quality-of-life instrument.
MGFA clinical classificationThe Myasthenia Gravis Foundation of America classes I (ocular only) to V (intubated), with a and b subtypes for limb or bulbar predominance.
MGFA post-intervention status (MGFA-PIS)Categories for the state of MG after treatment: complete stable remission, pharmacologic remission, minimal manifestations, improved, unchanged, worse, exacerbation, died of MG.
MGTXThe randomized trial (126 people, AChR+ non-thymomatous generalized MG, aged 18-65) showing that extended transsternal thymectomy plus prednisone beat prednisone alone over 3 and 5 years.
Miller Fisher syndromeAcute eye-muscle paralysis with ataxia and lost reflexes in a fully conscious person; anti-GQ1b antibodies in 83% and raised CSF protein without cells in 76%.
Miniature endplate potential (mEPP)The small depolarisation (about 1 mV) from the spontaneous release of a single quantum. mEPPs are smaller in MG because receptors are lost.
Minimal manifestations (MM)No symptoms of functional limitation from MG, though some weakness can be found on examination (MGFA wording). MM or better is the 2016 consensus treatment goal.
Minimal symptom expression (MSE)An MG-ADL score of 0 or 1, used as an endpoint in recent trials.
Muscarinic effectsCholinesterase-inhibitor side effects on glands and smooth muscle: nausea, diarrhoea, cramps, salivation, bronchial secretions, sweating, small pupils.
MuSKMuscle-specific kinase, the receptor tyrosine kinase that, once activated by agrin and LRP4, clusters AChRs. MuSK antibodies cause about 7-10% of MG.
MuSK-CAARTT cells engineered to display the MuSK protein, so they kill only B cells that make anti-MuSK antibodies. A completed phase 1 (7 participants) has no published outcomes yet.
Myasthenia gravisAn autoimmune disease in which antibodies against AChR, MuSK or related proteins at the neuromuscular junction cause fluctuating, fatigable muscle weakness.
Myasthenic crisisWorsening of MG that needs intubation, or non-invasive ventilation to avoid intubation (MGFA class V). Lifetime risk about 15-20%.
Mycophenolate mofetilAn oral steroid-sparing immunosuppressant. Two 2008 trials were negative, but it is widely used; contraindicated in pregnancy (boxed warning for embryofetal toxicity).
NeostigmineA short-acting cholinesterase inhibitor, used by injection when oral pyridostigmine cannot be given (60 mg oral pyridostigmine is about 1-1.5 mg IM or SC neostigmine).
Neuromuscular junction (NMJ)The synapse where a motor nerve terminal meets a muscle fibre: nerve terminal, a 50-100 nm cleft, and the folded endplate.
Nicotinic effectsCholinesterase-inhibitor side effects at the junction itself: muscle cramps, fasciculations and, with excess, weakness.
NipocalimabAn IV anti-FcRn antibody given every 2 weeks, FDA-approved in April 2025 for AChR+ or MuSK+ gMG from age 12.
Non-depolarizing neuromuscular blockersMuscle relaxants such as rocuronium and vecuronium. People with MG are more sensitive to them (vecuronium ED95 about 56% of normal); an anaesthesia review says they should be given in smaller doses with monitoring.
Obstructive sleep apnoeaRepeated collapse of the upper airway during sleep. Found in 36% of people with MG in one study, against an expected 15-20% in the general population.
Ocular MGMG limited to the eyelid and eye muscles (MGFA class I). Of people still purely ocular after 2 years, about 90% stay ocular.
OPMDOculopharyngeal muscular dystrophy: an inherited (autosomal dominant) disease usually starting in the sixth decade with ptosis and difficulty swallowing; a PABPN1 repeat expansion.
Passive transferInjecting a patient's antibodies into an animal to see whether they reproduce the disease. Done for MG in 1975, showing the antibody alone is enough.
Pharmacologic remissionMGFA-PIS: the same criteria as complete stable remission, but the person still takes some MG therapy (not cholinesterase inhibitors).
Plasma exchange (PLEX)Removal of the person's plasma, and with it circulating antibodies: a fast-acting treatment for crisis and severe worsening. The 2016 consensus says it cannot be used in sepsis.
Postsynaptic foldsDeep infoldings of the endplate membrane, up to about 1 µm deep in humans. AChRs line the crests; sodium channels line the troughs. Complement destroys them in MG.
PtosisA drooping upper eyelid. It worsens with sustained upgaze in MG and is the most common first symptom.
PyridostigmineThe standard oral cholinesterase inhibitor for MG symptoms (Mestinon). Each dose acts for about 3-4 hours.
QMGQuantitative Myasthenia Gravis score: 13 clinician-scored items, each 0-3, total 0-39.
Quantal contentThe number of quanta released by one nerve impulse: about 20-30 at a human junction.
QuantumThe packet of transmitter in one synaptic vesicle: about 10,000 ACh molecules.
Radioimmunoassay (RIPA)The standard blood test that measures AChR or MuSK antibodies binding a radio-labelled antigen in solution.
RapsynA protein under the membrane that tethers AChRs into dense clusters; without it, no AChR clusters form.
RavulizumabA long-acting IV C5 inhibitor given every 8 weeks, FDA-approved in 2022 for AChR+ gMG.
Readily releasable poolThe vesicles docked and ready to release; it empties during a train of impulses, causing rundown until reserve vesicles refill it after 1-2 seconds.
Refractory MGMGFA-PIS unchanged or worse after corticosteroids and at least two other immunosuppressants at adequate dose and duration, with persistent symptoms or limiting side effects.
REMSRisk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy: the US controlled-access programme for C5 inhibitors, with a patient safety card listing meningococcal warning signs.
Repetitive nerve stimulation (RNS)Stimulating a nerve several times at 2-5 Hz while recording the muscle response; a decrement of 10% or more supports MG.
RituximabAn anti-CD20 antibody that depletes B cells. Strongest evidence in MuSK-MG; in new-onset AChR+ gMG, RINOMAX favoured it.
RozanolixizumabA subcutaneous anti-FcRn antibody given weekly for 6 weeks per cycle, approved for AChR+ or MuSK+ gMG. Headache is common.
Ryanodine receptor antibodiesAntibodies to the calcium-release channel inside muscle, found mainly in thymoma and late-onset MG, linked to more severe disease.
Safety factorThe ratio of the endplate potential to the depolarisation needed to fire the muscle: normally 2-4 times what is needed. MG lowers it towards 1.
Seronegative MGMG without detectable AChR antibodies on the standard assay. MuSK, LRP4 or clustered-AChR antibodies explain some cases.
Single breath countA bedside test: a deep breath, then counting aloud at about 2 per second. A count of 25 or more fits normal breathing-muscle function.
Single-fibre EMG (SFEMG)A needle EMG technique that measures jitter and blocking between muscle fibres of one motor unit.
Steroid dipTransient worsening of MG soon after corticosteroids are started, reported in about 21-48% of patients. No source gives a typical timing; studies used 2-week and 4-week windows.
SuccinylcholineA depolarizing muscle relaxant. People with MG are resistant to it, needing up to about 2.6 times the usual dose.
SugammadexA drug that reverses neuromuscular block after surgery. A 24-study review in MG found fast reversal and low postoperative crisis rates.
Synaptic cleftThe 50-100 nm gap between nerve terminal and muscle, filled with basal lamina that holds acetylcholinesterase.
TelithromycinA ketolide antibiotic contraindicated in MG after fatal respiratory failure, sometimes within hours of the first dose; no longer marketed in the US.
ThymectomySurgical removal of the thymus. In MGTX it improved strength and reduced steroid needs in AChR+ generalized MG; current evidence does not support it in MuSK-MG.
Thymic hyperplasiaAn enlarged thymus with lymphoid follicles and germinal centres, seen in about 65% of people with MG, mainly early-onset.
ThymomaA tumour of thymic epithelial cells, present in about 10-20% of people with MG; about 30-44% of people with thymoma have MG. The 2016 consensus says that, with rare exceptions, people with MG and thymoma should have surgery.
ThymusAn organ behind the breastbone where T cells learn tolerance to the body's own proteins. Changes in it are thought to start the AChR immune response in early-onset MG.
Time-critical medicineA drug whose late or missed dose can cause harm. UK safety bulletins class pyridostigmine this way, to be given within 30 minutes of the due time.
Titin antibodiesAntibodies to the giant muscle protein titin, found in about 95% of thymoma-MG. Their presence in a person under 60 strongly suggests thymoma.
TPMTThiopurine methyltransferase, an enzyme that handles azathioprine. Low or absent activity risks severe bone-marrow toxicity, so guidance advises testing it before treatment.
Transient neonatal MGTemporary weakness in a newborn caused by maternal antibodies crossing the placenta: about 10% of babies, usually appearing in the first 24 hours and resolving within 4 weeks.
U-shaped decrementThe RNS pattern in MG: the biggest fall between the 1st and 2nd responses, a low point at the 4th-5th, then partial recovery.
Vital capacity (VC)The largest volume of air a person can breathe out after a full breath in. In MG, ventilation was unlikely when VC stayed above 20 mL/kg.
ZilucoplanA small subcutaneous peptide C5 inhibitor self-injected once daily. Approved in 2023 for AChR+ gMG; MG-ADL responders 73.1% vs 46.1% at week 12.
