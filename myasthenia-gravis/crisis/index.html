Part 4 · Crisis, care & the future

Myasthenic crisis

Myasthenic crisis is MG weakness severe enough to need help with breathing. Press play to follow a schematic 21-day course: the trigger, falling breathing numbers, BiPAP or intubation, plasma exchange or IVIG, and weaning. Then compare myasthenic and cholinergic crisis.

Trouble breathing or swallowing is an emergency. In a person with MG, new or worsening trouble breathing, swallowing or speaking can be the start of a crisis. Nursing teaching material tells patients to contact their prescriber urgently for trouble swallowing, speaking or breathing, and the MGFA emergency alert card warns first responders that breathing or swallowing difficulty may signal a crisis and can be mistaken for intoxication. 353,410 This module describes published studies and guidance. It is not medical advice and cannot tell anyone what to do in an emergency.

What counts as crisis. The international consensus defines manifest crisis as worsening severe enough to need intubation, or noninvasive ventilation to avoid intubation (MGFA class V, unless the intubation is routine after surgery). Impending crisis is rapid worsening that, in the treating physician's opinion, could lead to crisis within days to weeks. Crisis can come from weak breathing muscles or from bulbar weakness with collapse of the upper airway. 82,271,476

Who and when. About 15-20% of people with MG have at least one crisis; other reviews give 10-20%, or one-fifth to one-third of generalized MG. It usually happens in the first 2-3 years: median 8 months from first symptoms in one series. In about one-fifth, crisis is the first sign of MG. 75,82,254,512,547

Days 2-4 · The numbers fall

Vital capacity and pressures cross the thresholds

In 42 people with severe MG, ventilation was unlikely while vital capacity stayed above 20 mL/kg, expiratory pressure above 40 cmH2O and inspiratory pressure more negative than −40 cmH2O; a fall of 30% or more in inspiratory pressure marked higher risk. A review gives VC under 1 L (or 20-25 mL/kg) and NIF weaker than −20 cmH2O as signs of significant weakness. Not every study agrees: in a small ICU series, repeated VC did not separate the episodes that needed ventilation. The familiar "20/30/40" figures come from a Guillain-Barré study: switch on the overlay to compare. 308,452,511,547

In plain wordsTwo things are measured: how much air the person can blow out, and how hard they can breathe in and out. How fast the numbers fall matters as much as any one reading.

3 · Plasma exchange or IVIG: where the sources disagree

Consensus Expert opinion

The 2016 international consensus: trials suggest the two work equally well in impending or manifest crisis, but "expert consensus suggests that PLEX is more effective and works more quickly". A 2023 review says plasmapheresis "is preferred", and a crisis-centre guideline favours PLEX. 82,270,476

Randomized trials RCTs

  • 87 people in exacerbation: muscle score change at day 15 +18 with 3 PLEX vs +15.5 with IVIG; side effects in 8 vs 1. 184
  • 84 with moderate-to-severe worsening: IVIG 1 g/kg/day × 2 as effective as 5 PLEX at day 14 (69% vs 65% improved). 34
  • Meta-analysis: QMG difference −2.8 favouring PLEX, not significant, with a trend to faster effect in the first 2 weeks. 419

Crisis cohorts Observational

  • 54 crises: PLEX gave better breathing at 2 weeks and function at 1 month, with more complications (13 vs 5). 436
  • US inpatient data: similar outcomes, possible cost advantage for IVIG. 333
  • Germany: PLEX or immunoadsorption first went with less invasive ventilation, and IVIG first with more weaning failure (OR 2.53); another German cohort found no difference. 383,384,385

Who gets which

The consensus ties the choice to the person: PLEX is avoided in sepsis, IVIG in hypercoagulable states, renal failure or immunoglobulin hypersensitivity. Regimens in one review: IVIG 400 mg/kg/day for 5 days, or 5 plasma exchanges over 10 days. Any regimen for a person is set by the treating clinician. 476,547

4 · Myasthenic or cholinergic crisis?

Cholinergic crisis is rare now. The consensus says cholinergic crises are now rare, though too much cholinesterase inhibitor cannot always be ruled out as a cause of worsening; one crisis series found no cholinergic cases at all. The pyridostigmine label says the two crises may be difficult to distinguish on symptoms, and that cholinergic crisis calls for prompt withdrawal of these drugs. 151,476,547

The old edrophonium test. The edrophonium (Enlon) label lists use for evaluating emergency treatment in myasthenic crises, with atropine 1 mg ready and a warning to watch a patient in crisis for bradycardia or cardiac standstill. The consensus documents read do not recommend the test in crisis; no guideline sentence saying "avoid" was found. 138,476

Time on the ventilator

12 daysmedian in 250 ventilated German crises; 20.5% of survivors still needed ventilation at discharge. Mean 10.4 days at Mayo; median hospital stay 17 days for intubated patients in a review. 384,486,547

Tracheostomy

49.8%of ventilated crises had one. Ventilation lasted 34.4 days with tracheostomy vs 7.9 without, and early tracheostomy (by day 10) was linked to about 2 weeks less ventilation and ICU stay. 18

43 vs 17 daysventilation in MuSK-only vs AChR crises (43.0 vs 17.4); ICU stay 45.3 vs 21.2 days. Severity at diagnosis, thymoma and MuSK antibodies independently predicted crisis or exacerbation in 815 patients; thymoma-associated MG reached crisis sooner. 253,299,383

Newer drugs in crisis Early evidence

  • C5 inhibitors in 17 crises not responding to standard therapy: 82% left ICU/IMC within 6 weeks; 2 died of sepsis; no meningococcal infection. 193
  • Emergency eculizumab, 5 episodes: QMG 28.2 to 15.0 within 48 h. 255
  • Efgartigimod, 16 with impending crisis, no control arm: 75% met the MG-ADL endpoint at week 8; in 10 crises all were weaned. 492,501

Case series and single-arm studies only; no randomized trial in crisis was found. 193,492

6 · Deaths per crisis, then and now

All bars are published values. The cohorts differ: the German one was ventilated only and older (median age 72), which its authors called comparable to earlier reports given age and disease burden; in the Chinese cohort, all-cause mortality over about 29 months was 15.16%, mostly septic shock and multiorgan failure. A review gives 5-12%. At Columbia all deaths came from overwhelming medical illness. 16,84,254,384,512,534

In plain wordsA crisis means the muscles for breathing or swallowing have become too weak to do the job safely, so a machine has to help for a while. It is an emergency. An infection is the most common spark, but often no cause is found. With intensive care, most people today survive and come off the ventilator, though some need weeks.
