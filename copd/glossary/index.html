An animated, cited guide for students, people with COPD, and their families and caregivers. Educational only, not medical advice. Your place is saved on this device.

Not medical adviceThe COPD Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. It does not replace a doctor, nurse, physiotherapist, pharmacist or the rest of a respiratory team. Spirometry bands, GOLD grades and groups, mMRC and CAT scores and the blood-gas explainer are teaching aids, never for diagnosis or treatment decisions. Doses, oxygen targets and inhaler routines are quoted as published, never as instructions. Survival and risk figures are population statistics, not predictions for any person.

Glossary

6-minute walk test (6MWD)A field walking test of how far someone walks in six minutes; the ERS/ATS standard advises two tests 194. A fall of more than 30 m carried a hazard ratio of 1.93 for death in ECLIPSE 371.
ABE toolThe GOLD 2023 assessment that replaced ABCD: groups A and B by symptoms, and group E (for exacerbations) merging the old C and D 12.
Acidaemia (respiratory acidosis)A blood pH below 7.35 from a raised PaCO2; normal pH is 7.35–7.45 393. With hypercapnia it defines a Rome "severe" exacerbation 67.
Action plan (written)A written plan for what to do when symptoms worsen. Within self-management it improved SGRQ by 2.69 points and cut respiratory admissions (OR 0.69) 249.
Advance care planningStructured talks about future care. One nurse-led session improved end-of-life communication without raising distress 197.
Air trappingAir left in the lungs at the end of breathing out because small airways close and empty slowly; it underlies hyperinflation 32,191.
Alpha-1 antitrypsin (AAT)A protein made mainly in the liver whose main job is to protect the lung against neutrophil elastase 430.
Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency (AATD)An inherited, autosomal co-dominant shortage of AAT that causes early emphysema and liver disease 140,430; about 1 in 2,000–5,000 people 430.
AlveoliThe air sacs where gas exchange happens; their walls are destroyed in emphysema 12,191.
Ambulatory oxygenOxygen used while moving about; ATS 2020 suggests it for severe exertional hypoxaemia, defined as SpO2 ≤88% on exertion 202.
Anthonisen criteriaIncreased dyspnoea, sputum volume and sputum purulence; antibiotic success was 68% vs 55% with placebo in the 1987 trial 21.
Antimuscarinic (anticholinergic)A drug that blocks muscarinic receptors; tiotropium bronchodilates by inhibiting M3 receptors on airway smooth muscle (label) 142.
Arterial blood gas (ABG)A sample of arterial blood measuring pH, PaO2, PaCO2 and bicarbonate. Rome adds it in hospital to decide on ventilatory support 12,67.
Asthma–COPD overlapFeatures of both conditions; pooled prevalence 27% among people with COPD in population studies 19. GOLD says COPD with asthma should be treated as asthma 12.
Augmentation therapyWeekly intravenous plasma-derived AAT, 60 mg/kg by label 140. In RAPID it slowed CT lung density loss at TLC (p=0.03) 70.
Azithromycin (long-term)A macrolide antibiotic given at 250 mg daily in trials; time to first exacerbation 266 vs 174 days, with more hearing decrements 13.
Base excessA blood-gas measure of the metabolic component; −2 to +2 mmol/L is normal 393.
BenralizumabAn IL-5 receptor-α antibody that did not significantly lower exacerbations in GALATHEA or TERRANOVA 92.
Beta-blocker (cardioselective)A heart drug blocking β1 receptors. GOLD 2023 supports it when there is a clear cardiovascular indication 12; without one, metoprolol increased hospitalised exacerbations 117.
Bicarbonate (HCO3⁻)The blood buffer that rises to compensate for a high PaCO2: about 1 mEq/L per 10 mmHg acutely and 3.5 chronically 420.
Biomass smokeSmoke from burning wood, crop waste, dung or coal indoors; about 3 billion people are exposed 392. Solid fuel use: OR 2.80 for COPD 238.
Blood eosinophil countA blood test used to judge the likely benefit of an inhaled steroid: <100 cells/µL little or no effect, ≥300 strong likelihood 12.
BODE indexBody-mass index, airflow Obstruction, Dyspnoea and Exercise capacity, a 0–10 score; each point HR 1.34 for death 66.
BOLD studyBurden of Obstructive Lung Disease, a population spirometry study; GOLD stage II+ COPD in 10.1% of 9,425 adults aged 40+ 55.
BOREAS and NOTUSPhase 3 trials of dupilumab in COPD with eosinophils ≥300/µL on triple therapy; exacerbation rate ratios 0.70 and 0.66 40,41.
BronchiectasisPermanently widened bronchi. 57.2% of 201 people with moderate-to-severe COPD had it on HRCT, with higher mortality (HR 2.54) 280.
Bronchiolitis (obstructive)Inflammation and narrowing of the small airways, one of the two lesions of COPD with emphysema 12,32.
BronchodilatorA medicine that relaxes airway smooth muscle; SABA with or without SAMA are the initial bronchodilators in a flare-up 12.
Bronchodilator responsivenessThe rise in FEV1 or FVC after a bronchodilator; since 2022, positive means >10% of the predicted value 423,497. It varies between tests 14.
BullectomyRemoval of a large bulla; GOLD 2023 lists it as an option for a large bulla 12.
CAT (COPD Assessment Test)Eight items scored 0–5, total 0–40, higher is worse 212,288; MCID 2 points 230.
CD8+ T cellA cytotoxic lymphocyte raised in the peripheral airways of smokers with COPD compared with smokers without it 389.
Centrilobular emphysemaEmphysema starting around the respiratory bronchioles, most closely linked to smoking 191,192.
Chronic bronchitisMucus overproduction and hypersecretion by goblet cells 224; clinically, chronic cough for at least three months in at least two years (ATS 1962) 366.
Chronic hypercapniaA long-standing raised PaCO2. ATS 2020 uses resting PaCO2 >45 mm Hg outside an exacerbation for chronic hypercapnic COPD 266.
Collateral ventilationAirflow between lobes that bypasses the bronchi; it "prevents lobar atelectasis" and blocks valve benefit 95.
Comorbidome / COTE indexThe pattern of conditions that predict death in COPD. 12 of 79 comorbidities formed COTE; a score ≥4 roughly doubled death risk 109.
Compliance (lung)How easily the lung stretches; emphysema makes it more compliant, so it empties slowly 191.
Cor pulmonalePulmonary hypertension caused by lung disease that enlarges the right ventricle and may cause right heart failure; COPD is its leading cause 481.
CRP (C-reactive protein)A blood marker of inflammation; ≥10 mg/L is one Rome sign 67,203. Point-of-care CRP cut antibiotic use from 77.4% to 57.0% in PACE 57.
CytisineA smoking-cessation medicine that helped more people quit than placebo (RR 1.30) 259.
DECAF scoreDyspnoea (eMRCD), Eosinopenia, Consolidation, Acidaemia, atrial Fibrillation: predicts in-hospital death, AUROC 0.86 425; scored 0–6 119.
Diffusing capacity (DLCO)A test of how well gas crosses from air to blood; low in panlobular and centrilobular emphysema in MESA 418.
Discharge bundleA set of checks before leaving hospital; pooled readmission RR 0.80 352. GOLD advises follow-up within one month 12.
Dry powder inhaler (DPI)An inhaler with internal resistance that relies on the person's own breath to break up the powder 270.
Dual bronchodilation (LABA + LAMA)One long-acting β2-agonist plus one long-acting antimuscarinic; GOLD 2023 recommends it for group B 12.
DupilumabAn IL-4Rα antibody blocking IL-4 and IL-13; labelled 300 mg every 2 weeks as add-on for eosinophilic COPD 137.
Dynamic hyperinflationA temporary rise in operating lung volumes during exertion, seen as a fall in inspiratory capacity (mean 0.37 L) 345,346.
DyspnoeaThe sensation of breathlessness; the most prominent and distressing symptom of COPD 346, with several distinct qualities 357.
Early COPDGOLD 2023 term only for the biological first steps of disease studied experimentally 12.
E-cigaretteA device delivering nicotine vapour; it raised quit rates versus NRT (RR 1.59) in the Cochrane living review 255. Considered unsafe with home oxygen 83.
EmphysemaEnlarged airspaces with loss of alveolar walls (ATS 1962, anatomical definition) 366.
Endobronchial valveA one-way valve placed by bronchoscope to collapse a hyperinflated lobe; Zephyr was FDA-approved on 29 June 2018 95,147.
EnsifentrineA nebulised dual PDE3/PDE4 inhibitor (Ohtuvayre), FDA-approved 26 June 2024 139.
Eosinophilic exacerbationA flare-up with sputum eosinophilia, 28% of exacerbations in one cohort; blood eosinophil percentage identified it (AUROC 0.85) 28.
Exacerbation (ECOPD, flare-up)Dyspnoea and/or cough and sputum that worsen over ≤14 days (Rome definition, GOLD 2023) 12,67.
Expiratory flow limitationFlow out that cannot rise however hard the person pushes; called the pathophysiological hallmark of COPD 346.
FazirsiranAn RNAi drug that silences Z-AAT in the liver; liver Z-AAT fell a median 83% (Early evidence) 435.
FEV1Forced expiratory volume in one second; it sets GOLD grades 1–4 once the ratio is <0.7 12.
FEV1/FVC ratioThe share of the forced vital capacity blown out in the first second; post-bronchodilator <0.7 confirms obstruction for GOLD 12.
Fixed ratioThe 0.70 cut-off for FEV1/FVC that GOLD prefers for simplicity; it may overdiagnose older adults 12,438.
FrailtyA state of reduced reserve; 19% of people with COPD are frail and 56% pre-frail 273.
Frequent exacerbatorTwo or more exacerbations a year; 22% of GOLD 2 and 47% of GOLD 4 in ECLIPSE 199.
FVCForced vital capacity: the total volume blown out after a full breath in. An FVC manoeuvre has four phases 169.
GLI equationsGlobal Lung Function Initiative reference equations from 97,759 records 375; GLI Global (2022) is race-neutral 49.
GOLDThe Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease 12, launched by the WHO and NHLBI in 2001 360,366.
GOLD gradeSeverity of airflow obstruction, 1 to 4, from post-bronchodilator FEV1: ≥80, 50–79, 30–49, <30% predicted 12,252.
Goblet cellA mucus-secreting airway cell; more of them in smokers with chronic bronchitis and airflow limitation 390.
Handheld fanA fan directed at the face for 5 minutes reduced breathlessness compared with the legs (P = 0.003) 157.
HDAC2Histone deacetylase 2; its activity falls with GOLD stage and is linked to steroid resistance 33,201.
Heart failureA frequent look-alike and companion of COPD; 20.5% of older people labelled COPD had it unrecognised 388.
Home non-invasive ventilationA mask ventilator used at home, usually at night. In HOT-HMV, with oxygen, it lengthened time to readmission or death (HR 0.49) 300.
Hospital at homeTreatment of an exacerbation at home by a hospital team; readmissions RR 0.76 versus inpatient care 208.
HypercapniaA raised arterial CO2: PaCO2 >6 kPa (45 mmHg) in Rome and BTS; BTS/ICS uses >6.5 kPa to start NIV 97,203.
Hyperinflation (static)Lungs larger than normal at rest; a low IC/TLC independently predicted death 65.
HypoxaemiaLow arterial oxygen; Rome uses PaO2 ≤8 kPa 203. In COPD it comes from V/Q mismatch 465.
Hypoxic challenge test (HCT)Breathing 15% oxygen to imitate a 2,438 m cabin; SpO2 <85% or PaO2 <6.6 kPa means in-flight oxygen is recommended 78.
ICS (inhaled corticosteroid)An inhaled steroid such as fluticasone or budesonide; it raised serious pneumonia (fluticasone OR 1.78) 222.
ICS withdrawal (de-escalation)Stepping an inhaled steroid down; ERS 2020 conditionally recommends it without frequent exacerbations, and not at eosinophils ≥300/µL 68.
IL-33An epithelial alarmin; IL33 gene variants are linked to COPD risk 377. Antibodies include itepekimab and tozorakimab 377,403.
IMPACT trial10,355 patients: single-inhaler triple therapy versus FF/VI or UMEC/VI; exacerbation rate ratios 0.85 and 0.75 256.
Inspiratory capacity (IC)The largest breath in from the end of a normal breath out; it falls during exercise in dynamic hyperinflation 106,345.
Isoelectric focusing (phenotyping)A lab method separating AAT variants, kept for confirmation after level and genotyping 289.
LABALong-acting β2-agonist such as salmeterol, formoterol or vilanterol; versus placebo, fewer hospitalised exacerbations (OR 0.73) 221.
LAMALong-acting muscarinic antagonist such as tiotropium; versus placebo, exacerbations OR 0.78 216.
Lower limit of normal (LLN)The bottom 5% of a healthy reference population; it depends on the reference equation chosen 12,38.
Long-term oxygen therapy (LTOT)Oxygen for at least 15 h/day in severe chronic resting hypoxaemia (ATS 2020, strong recommendation) 202.
LOTTThe Long-Term Oxygen Treatment Trial: no benefit for moderate desaturation (HR 0.94) 15.
Lung cancer screeningAnnual low-dose CT; NLST cut lung-cancer deaths by 20.0% 3. USPSTF 2021: ages 50–80, 20 pack-years 235.
Lung Health StudyA trial in 5,887 smokers with early COPD; quitting halved FEV1 decline and lowered mortality over 14.5 years 22,24,399.
Lung transplantationReplacing one or both lungs; median survival 5.0 years in COPD, longer after bilateral (6.41 vs 4.59) 447.
Lung volume reduction surgery (LVRS)Removing the most damaged lung; in NETT it lowered mortality in upper-lobe emphysema with low exercise capacity (RR 0.47) 149.
Macrophage elastaseAn enzyme that smoke-induced emphysema needs in mice; knockout mice did not develop emphysema 185.
MepolizumabAn IL-5 antibody, labelled 100 mg every 4 weeks for eosinophilic COPD 138; MATINEE rate ratio 0.79 402.
Metered-dose inhaler (pMDI)A pressurised canister that needs priming; commonest errors are coordination (45%) and no breath-hold (46%) 134,394.
mMRC dyspnoea scaleA 0–4 self-rating of breathlessness during activity; GOLD uses ≥2 as "more symptomatic" 176,491.
Mucus plugA mucus cast blocking an airway on CT; plugs in ≥3 segments carried an adjusted HR for death of 1.24 107.
NETTThe National Emphysema Treatment Trial of LVRS in 1,218 people; no overall survival benefit at first 149, a 5-year risk ratio of 0.86 later 301.
Neutrophil elastaseA protease from neutrophils that digests lung elastin; alpha-1 antitrypsin is its main inhibitor in the lower airways 140,430.
NEWS2 scale 2The early-warning oxygen scale targeting 88–92% for hypercapnic respiratory failure 120.
Nicotine replacement therapy (NRT)Nicotine by patch, gum, lozenge, inhalator or spray; raises quitting by 50% to 60% 184.
Non-invasive ventilation (NIV)Breathing support by mask; in acute hypercapnic failure NNT 12 for death and 5 for intubation 351.
NOTTThe 1980 Nocturnal Oxygen Therapy Trial: mortality 1.94 times higher with 12-hour nocturnal than continuous oxygen 342.
Occult hypoxaemiaSaO2 <88% while the oximeter reads 92–96%; more common in Black, Asian and Hispanic patients 132.
Opioids for breathlessnessLow-quality evidence of benefit from oral or parenteral opioids 34; BEAMS found no benefit at 8 or 16 mg/day morphine 125.
OsteoporosisThin, fragile bone; 9–69% of people with COPD 167. Recurrent systemic steroids raise the risk 12.
Overlap syndromeCOPD plus obstructive sleep apnoea; without CPAP, mortality RR 1.79 274.
Oxygen alert cardA card for people with previous hypercapnic failure; initial oxygen follows it until blood gases are known 348.
Pack-yearA measure of lifetime tobacco exposure. GOLD 2023 names more than 20 pack-years among the risk factors for case-finding spirometry 12.
Palliative careCare focused on symptoms and choices; GOLD 2023 says all with advanced COPD should be considered for it 12.
Panlobular (panacinar) emphysemaEmphysema involving the whole acinus evenly, associated with AATD 191.
PaCO2Arterial carbon dioxide pressure; normal 35–45 mmHg 393.
PDE4 inhibitorA drug blocking phosphodiesterase 4, which breaks down cyclic AMP; roflumilast is oral 136.
Peak inspiratory flow (PIF)The fastest breath in; at least 60 L/min is cited as optimal for DPIs 270.
pHBlood acidity; normal 7.35–7.45 393. [H⁺] ≈ 80 minus the two digits after the decimal point 420.
Pneumococcal vaccineA vaccine against Streptococcus pneumoniae; in COPD, NNTB 21 for pneumonia and 8 for an exacerbation 468.
Pre-COPDSymptoms and/or structural or functional changes with FEV1/FVC ≥0.7; no evidence yet on the best treatment 12.
PRISmPreserved ratio impaired spirometry: FEV1/FVC ≥0.7 with FEV1 <80% predicted 12; mortality 29.6 vs 18.0 per 1,000 person-years 471.
Protease–antiprotease imbalanceThe model in which elastase outweighs its inhibitors and digests the lung 140, founded on papain experiments and AATD 366.
Pulmonary artery-to-aorta ratioA CT ratio above 1 was associated with severe exacerbations (OR 3.44) 482.
Pulmonary embolismA blood clot in the lung arteries; found in 16.1% of unexplained exacerbations 16.
Pulmonary hypertensionRaised pressure in the lung arteries, usually mild in COPD (mean 20–35 mmHg) 481.
Pulmonary rehabilitationExercise training, education and behaviour change tailored after assessment (ATS/ERS 2013) 383,422.
Pulse oximetry (SpO2)A finger probe estimate of saturation; it may overestimate oxygen in darker skin tones 12,132.
Pursed-lip breathingBreathing out through narrowed lips; it lengthens expiration and lowers end-expiratory volume 42.
Reid indexThe thickness of the bronchial mucous gland layer, proposed by Reid in 1960 as a yardstick for chronic bronchitis 366,380.
RoflumilastAn oral PDE4 inhibitor labelled for severe COPD with chronic bronchitis and prior exacerbations; not a bronchodilator 136.
Rome proposalThe 2022 exacerbation definition and point-of-care severity scale adopted by GOLD 2023 12,67.
RSV vaccineA vaccine against respiratory syncytial virus; ACIP 2024 recommended one dose for all adults ≥75 and at-risk adults 60–74 50.
SABAShort-acting β2-agonist such as salbutamol (albuterol), labelled 2 inhalations every 4 to 6 hours 146.
Self-managementProgrammes that teach people to manage their own condition; "unlikely to cause harm" in the 2022 Cochrane review 401.
SERPINA1The gene for alpha-1 antitrypsin, on chromosome 14q32.1 484.
SGRQSt George's Respiratory Questionnaire of health status; a 4-point change is the minimal important difference 283.
Small airwaysAirways under 2 mm across, the main site of obstruction in COPD 284.
Soft mist inhalerAn inhaler such as Respimat that makes a slow aerosol cloud; needs priming before first use (label) 143.
SpacerA tube that holds the aerosol from a pMDI; GOLD names MDI "with a spacer device if necessary" for flare-ups 12.
SpirometryThe forced blow test measuring FEV1 and FVC, needed to diagnose COPD 12,169.
Systemic corticosteroidOral or IV steroid for a flare-up; reduced treatment failure (OR 0.48) 467.
TelerehabilitationPulmonary rehabilitation delivered remotely; little or no difference from centre-based in 6MWD 84.
Terminal bronchioleOne of the smallest conducting airways. GOLD 4 lungs had 72–89% fewer, and their loss came before emphysematous destruction 284.
TezepelumabAn anti-TSLP antibody; COURSE missed its primary endpoint (rate ratio 0.83) 413.
TiotropiumA once-daily LAMA; protection lasted longer than 24 hours in preclinical studies (label) 142.
Treatable traitsAn approach treating each identified feature or mechanism rather than a label 9.
Triple therapyLABA + LAMA + ICS, often in one inhaler; lowered exacerbations versus dual therapy in IMPACT and ETHOS 256,376.
TSLPThymic stromal lymphopoietin, an epithelial cytokine at the top of the inflammatory cascade, higher in COPD 413.
VareniclineA nicotine receptor partial agonist; beat placebo (RR 2.32), bupropion and single NRT 259.
Venturi maskA mask that gives more accurate, controlled oxygen than nasal prongs 12; BTS 2017 lists 24% at 2–3 L/min or 28% at 4 L/min in COPD 348.
V/Q mismatchAn uneven match of ventilation and blood flow in lung units, the cause of hypoxaemia in COPD 385,465.
Vital capacityThe volume exhaled from fully inflated lungs, named by Hutchinson as "the capacity to live" 200,365.
Young COPDGOLD 2023 term for COPD in people aged 20–50 12.
Z allele (PI*Z)The commonest severe AAT variant, Glu342Lys; most severe deficiency is PI type ZZ 175,484.

COPD Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), emphysema and chronic bronchitis, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people with COPD, their families and caregivers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change an inhaler, a medicine, oxygen or a care plan without your own care team. The spirometry explainer, GOLD grade and group tools, mMRC and CAT scores, the blood-gas explainer and the other calculators are for learning, never for diagnosis, self-triage or treatment decisions. Doses, oxygen targets, inhaler routines and guideline statements are quoted as published (GOLD, NICE, ATS/ERS, BTS and drug labels), never as instructions, and survival and risk figures are population statistics. If breathing becomes much worse than usual, or there is confusion, drowsiness, chest pain or blue lips, follow your action plan or seek urgent care.
