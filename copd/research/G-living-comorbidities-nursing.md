# G · Living, comorbidities & nursing

Pass date: 2026-11-07. Modules served: living, comorbid, nursing.

This pass covers daily life with COPD: self-management and written exacerbation action plans, breathing techniques and the handheld fan, physical activity, flying and hypoxic challenge testing, sex, work, carers, and advance care planning and palliative support. It then covers the conditions that travel with COPD: heart disease and cardiovascular events after exacerbations, beta-blockers and statins, lung cancer risk and CT screening, osteoporosis, frailty, muscle wasting and nutrition, anxiety, depression and cognition, sleep apnoea (overlap syndrome), reflux, and pulmonary embolism in unexplained exacerbations. Finally it covers nursing care: respiratory rate, early warning scores (NEWS, NEWS2 and SpO2 scale 2), controlled oxygen and target ranges, pulse-oximeter bias, inhaler technique teaching, sputum colour, and home-oxygen fire safety. Drug trials for stable COPD (pass D), exacerbation treatment and blood gases (pass E), and oxygen, rehabilitation and palliative drug trials (pass F) are left to those passes; they appear here only where they bear on living, comorbidity or nursing.

## Findings

### living · Self-management and written action plans
- A 2017 Cochrane review of 22 studies (3,854 people) found that self-management interventions that include a written action plan for exacerbations improved quality of life over 12 months (SGRQ total score mean difference −2.69 points, 95% CI −4.49 to −0.90; high-quality evidence) compared with usual care [[Lenferink2017]].
- In the same review, the odds of at least one respiratory-related hospital admission were lower with self-management plus an action plan (OR 0.69, 95% CI 0.51 to 0.94; 14 studies). The number needed to treat to prevent one admission over a year was 12 for people at high baseline risk and 17 for those at low baseline risk [[Lenferink2017]].
- All-cause hospital admission was not significantly reduced (OR 0.74, 95% CI 0.54 to 1.03), and an exploratory analysis found a very small but statistically significant excess in respiratory-related mortality (risk difference 0.028, 95% CI 0.0049 to 0.0511; very low-quality evidence) [[Lenferink2017]].
- Interventions that included a smoking-cessation programme improved quality of life more (SGRQ −4.98) than those without one (−1.33) [[Lenferink2017]].
- The 2022 Cochrane update of all COPD self-management interventions (27 studies, 6,008 people) found an SGRQ improvement of −2.86 points (95% CI −4.87 to −0.85), which is below the 4-point minimal clinically important difference, and fewer respiratory-related admissions (OR 0.75, 95% CI 0.57 to 0.98; very low-quality evidence) [[Schrijver2022]].
- The 2022 review found no excess in respiratory-related mortality (risk difference 0.01, 95% CI −0.02 to 0.04) or all-cause mortality (risk difference −0.01, 95% CI −0.03 to 0.01; 24 studies, 5,719 people), and the authors concluded self-management is "unlikely to cause harm" [[Schrijver2022]].
- The earlier 2014 Cochrane version (23 RCTs, 3,189 people) reported an SGRQ difference of −3.51 points, fewer respiratory admissions (OR 0.57) and less breathlessness on the mMRC scale (mean difference −0.83) [[Zwerink2014]].
- Not every trial was positive. In the Glasgow supported self-management trial (464 people after an admission), COPD readmission or death was no different (48% vs 47%; HR 1.05, 95% CI 0.80 to 1.38). Only 42% of the intervention group became "successful self managers", and they were younger and more often lived with others [[Bucknall2012]].
- A US Veterans Affairs comprehensive care management programme (education, action plan and case-management calls) was stopped early for safety: 28 deaths in the intervention group versus 10 with usual care (HR 3.00, 95% CI 1.46 to 6.17), with no fall in COPD hospitalisation (27% vs 24%) [[Fan2012]].
- A nurse-delivered, hospital-initiated 3-month programme for patients and their family caregivers in Baltimore (240 people) led to more COPD-related acute care events at 6 months (1.40 vs 0.72 per participant; difference 0.68, 95% CI 0.22 to 1.15) and no significant improvement in quality of life [[Aboumatar2019]].

### living · Breathing techniques and the handheld fan
- Pursed-lip breathing is a strategy many people with COPD adopt on their own when distressed. In 22 patients, it lowered end-expiratory chest-wall volume by 0.33 ± 0.24 L and raised tidal volume by 0.65 ± 0.48 L compared with spontaneous breathing, by lengthening expiratory time. Borg breathlessness scores fell [[Bianchi2004]].
- In that study, the fall in end-expiratory volume explained 27% of the variation in the Borg breathlessness score [[Bianchi2004]].
- A Cochrane review of breathing exercises (16 studies, 1,233 people, mean FEV1 30–51% predicted) found improved 6-minute walk distance after yoga with pranayama breathing (45 m, 95% CI 29 to 61 m), with similar gains in single trials of pursed-lip breathing (50 m) and diaphragmatic breathing (35 m) [[Holland2012]].
- The same review found no consistent effect of breathing exercises on breathlessness or quality of life. The authors suggest they may help people who cannot do exercise training, but the data do not support a widespread role (Established) [[Holland2012]].
- In a randomised crossover trial in 50 people with advanced disease, a handheld fan blowing air across the nose and mouth for 5 minutes reduced breathlessness on a visual analogue scale compared with the fan directed at the leg (P = 0.003) [[Galbraith2010]].

### living · Staying active
- In a Copenhagen population cohort of 2,386 people with COPD, any regular physical activity above "very low" was linked to fewer COPD admissions (incidence rate ratio 0.72, 95% CI 0.53 to 0.97) and lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.65 to 0.90) [[GarciaAymerich2006]].
- In 170 outpatients followed for a median of 48 months, objectively measured physical activity (multisensor armband) was the strongest predictor of death. Each 0.14 increase in physical activity level was associated with a hazard ratio of 0.46 (95% CI 0.33 to 0.64), and activity level discriminated 4-year survival with a C statistic of 0.81 [[Waschki2011]].
- These are observational associations: people who are sicker move less, so they do not prove that adding activity extends life (Early evidence for a causal effect) [[GarciaAymerich2006,Waschki2011]].

### living · Flying and the hypoxic challenge test
- European and North American regulators cap normal cabin altitude at 2,438 m (8,000 ft). That height was chosen because the average healthy person's arterial saturation stays above 90% up to it [[Coker2022]].
- If cabin altitude rises above 3,048 m (10,000 ft), saturation in healthy people falls to about 89% [[Coker2022]].
- A hypoxic challenge test (HCT) has the person breathe 15% oxygen, which gives roughly the same inspired oxygen pressure as air at a cabin altitude of 2,438 m [[Coker2022]].
- The BTS 2022 clinical statement reads the HCT as follows: PaO2 ≥ 6.6 kPa (≥ 50 mm Hg) or SpO2 ≥ 85% means in-flight oxygen is not required; PaO2 < 6.6 kPa or SpO2 < 85% means in-flight oxygen is recommended [[Coker2022]].
- The statement considers HCT for people with COPD who have resting SpO2 ≤ 95%, MRC score 3 or more, or desaturation below 84% on a walking test, and in whom there are concerns about hypercapnia [[Coker2022]].
- For people already on long-term oxygen without hypercapnia, the statement says it "seems reasonable" to raise the flow by 2 L/min in flight, if the equipment can provide it [[Coker2022]].
- The statement advises that people with severe COPD carry a copy of their management plan, that a history of pneumothorax or bullous disease needs specialist assessment before flying, and it notes that COPD raises the risk of venous thromboembolism, especially after an exacerbation [[Coker2022]].
- A Norwegian pre-flight algorithm (100 patients, validated in 50 more) used sea-level SpO2 and 6-minute-walk desaturation. People with SpO2 > 95% and walk SpO2 ≥ 84% could fly without further testing; SpO2 92–95% with walk SpO2 < 84%, or SpO2 < 92%, pointed to in-flight oxygen; everyone else needed HCT. Sensitivity was 100% and specificity 80% [[Edvardsen2012]].
- In 82 patients with moderate to very severe COPD, 76% had PaO2 below 6.6 kPa during simulated altitude (mean 6.3 kPa), and 46% had had respiratory symptoms on earlier flights. PaO2 during HCT did not differ between those with and without in-flight symptoms (6.3 vs 6.3 kPa) [[Edvardsen2013]].
- In that study, people who flew with supplemental oxygen reported symptoms less often than those without it (17% vs 48%; P = 0.039) [[Edvardsen2013]].

### living · Sex, work and carers
- In 55 outpatients, men with COPD reported lower sexual quality of life than age- and sex-matched norms on every dimension, and women with COPD reported less frequent sexual intimacy. Patients said they did not discuss sex with their doctor [[Kaptein2008]].
- In an international survey of 2,426 people aged 45–67 with COPD (Brazil, China, Germany, Turkey, US, UK), about 40% (447) of the working-age group had retired early because of COPD, at an estimated average age of about 54 [[Fletcher2011]].
- In that survey, health-related quality of life (EQ-5D) fell from 0.84 in mild to 0.58 in moderate and 0.41 in severe COPD, and 75% reported at least one other chronic condition [[Fletcher2011]].
- A systematic review of research on COPD caregivers (2000–2014) found 27 studies. Twenty-five were descriptive, there was only one intervention study, and only two measured carer burden over time, so the evidence base for supporting carers is thin [[Mansfield2016]].
- In a nurse-led advance care planning trial, anxiety symptoms in loved ones were lower at follow-up in the intervention group (p = 0.02) [[Houben2019]].

### living · Advance care planning and palliative support
- In a comparison of 50 people with end-stage COPD and 50 with inoperable lung cancer, the COPD group had worse daily living and physical, social and emotional function. Ninety percent had clinically relevant anxiety or depression on HADS (vs 52% with lung cancer), and none had access to specialist palliative care (vs 30% with lung cancer) [[Gore2000]].
- A cluster-randomised trial of one 1.5-hour, structured, nurse-led advance care planning session in 165 people with advanced COPD improved the quality of patient–physician end-of-life communication (p < 0.001) and made an advance care planning discussion with a physician within 6 months more likely (p = 0.003), without raising distress [[Houben2019]].
- GOLD 2023 states that all patients with advanced COPD should be considered for end-of-life and palliative care support, to optimise symptom control and allow patients and families to make informed choices [[Agusti2023]].
- A South London breathlessness support service (palliative care, respiratory medicine, physiotherapy and occupational therapy) improved breathlessness mastery at 6 weeks (mean difference 0.58, 95% CI 0.01 to 1.15) in 105 people with refractory breathlessness. Six-month survival was 94% vs 75%, a safety finding the authors say needs further study [[Higginson2014]].

### comorbid · The comorbidome: how many conditions, and which ones matter
- GOLD 2023 states that COPD "almost invariably" coexists with other diseases, that these can mimic COPD symptoms, and that comorbidities should generally be treated to usual standards regardless of COPD, with attention to simple regimens and avoiding polypharmacy [[Agusti2023]].
- In the BODE cohort (1,664 patients followed for a median of 51 months), 79 comorbidities were recorded and 12 predicted mortality. These formed the COTE index; a COTE score ≥ 4 roughly doubled the risk of death (HR 2.26–2.68) in every BODE quartile [[Divo2012]].
- Increasing COTE score raised the risk of both COPD-related death (HR 1.13 per point) and non-COPD death (HR 1.18 per point) [[Divo2012]].

### comorbid · Heart disease
- A meta-analysis of 29 datasets found that people with COPD were more likely to have cardiovascular disease (OR 2.46, 95% CI 2.02 to 3.00), including a two- to five-fold higher risk of ischaemic heart disease, arrhythmia, heart failure, pulmonary circulation disease and arterial disease. Hypertension (OR 1.33) and diabetes (OR 1.36) were also more common [[Chen2015]].
- GOLD 2023 puts the prevalence of cardiovascular disease in COPD at 20–70% [[Agusti2023]].
- Among 405 people aged 65 or over with a GP diagnosis of COPD, 20.5% (95% CI 16.7 to 24.8) had previously unrecognised heart failure on full work-up, split almost evenly between systolic (42) and isolated diastolic (41) dysfunction [[Rutten2005]].

### comorbid · Heart attacks and strokes after exacerbations
- In a UK primary-care database of 25,857 people with COPD (self-controlled case series), the risk of myocardial infarction was 2.27-fold higher (95% CI 1.1 to 4.7) in the 1–5 days after an exacerbation treated with both steroids and antibiotics. One in 2,513 exacerbations was followed by an MI within 1–5 days [[Donaldson2010]].
- Stroke risk was 1.26-fold higher (95% CI 1.0 to 1.6) in the 1–49 days after an exacerbation [[Donaldson2010]].
- In the SUMMIT trial (16,485 people with moderate COPD and cardiovascular disease or risk), the hazard of a cardiovascular event was highest in the first 30 days after an exacerbation (HR 3.8, 95% CI 2.7 to 5.5) and stayed raised up to a year. After a hospitalised exacerbation, the 30-day HR was 9.9 (95% CI 6.6 to 14.9) [[Kunisaki2018]].
- In a post hoc analysis of the IMPACT trial (10,355 people), cardiovascular adverse event risk rose during a moderate exacerbation (HR 2.63) and much more during a severe one (HR 21.84), stayed raised for 30 days after a moderate exacerbation (HR 1.63), and returned to baseline by 90 days, even in people at low cardiovascular risk [[Dransfield2022]].

### comorbid · Beta-blockers and statins
- GOLD 2023 states that cardiovascular disease in COPD should be treated per established guidelines, including selective β1-blockers when there is a clear cardiovascular indication [[Agusti2023]].
- In BLOCK COPD (532 people without a beta-blocker indication), extended-release metoprolol did not delay the first exacerbation (median 202 vs 222 days; HR 1.05, 95% CI 0.84 to 1.32) and was linked to more exacerbations needing hospital admission (HR 1.91, 95% CI 1.29 to 2.83). The trial stopped early for futility and safety concerns [[Dransfield2019]].
- In BICS (515 people with ≥ 2 treated exacerbations in the prior year), bisoprolol titrated up to 5 mg/day did not change the exacerbation rate (2.03 vs 2.01 per year; adjusted IRR 0.97, 95% CI 0.84 to 1.13), and serious adverse events were similar (14.5% vs 14.3%) [[Devereux2024]].
- In STATCOPE (885 people without a statin indication), simvastatin 40 mg daily did not change exacerbation rates (1.36 vs 1.39 per person-year) or time to first exacerbation (223 vs 231 days) [[Criner2014]].

### comorbid · Lung cancer
- In a study of 602 people matched for age, sex and pack-years, COPD (GOLD 2+) was present in 50% of those with lung cancer compared with 8% of community controls (OR 11.6), which the authors read as COPD being an independent risk factor for lung cancer [[Young2009]].
- In 2,507 outpatients with COPD followed for a median of 60 months, 215 developed lung cancer, an incidence density of 16.7 per 1,000 person-years. Squamous cell carcinoma was the most common type (44%) [[deTorres2011]].
- In that cohort, lung cancer was more frequent with milder airflow obstruction (GOLD I–II), older age, lower BMI and DLCO below 80% [[deTorres2011]].
- In the US National Lung Screening Trial (53,454 high-risk people), three annual low-dose CT scans reduced lung-cancer deaths by 20.0% (95% CI 6.8 to 26.7) compared with chest radiography: 247 vs 309 deaths per 100,000 person-years [[Aberle2011]].
- In NLST, 24.2% of low-dose CT screens were positive, and 96.4% of those positives were false positives [[Aberle2011]].
- In the Dutch–Belgian NELSON trial (13,195 men aged 50–74), volume CT screening lowered lung-cancer mortality at 10 years (2.50 vs 3.30 deaths per 1,000 person-years; rate ratio 0.76, 95% CI 0.61 to 0.94) [[deKoning2020]].
- The USPSTF 2021 recommends annual low-dose CT screening for adults aged 50–80 with a 20 pack-year history who smoke or quit within the past 15 years (B recommendation), widening the 2013 criteria of age 55 and 30 pack-years [[Krist2021]].
- GOLD 2023 states that annual low-dose CT is recommended for lung cancer screening in COPD due to smoking, as in the general population, and that there are insufficient data for COPD not due to smoking [[Agusti2023]].

### comorbid · Bones, frailty, muscle and weight
- A systematic review found osteoporosis in 9–69% and osteopenia in 27–67% of people with COPD, generally more than in healthy people. Correlates were body composition, disease severity and corticosteroid use, and no treatment trials had been done in COPD-only samples [[GraatVerboom2009]].
- In 255 Dutch outpatients, half had radiological evidence of osteoporosis once bone density scans and spinal X-rays (for vertebral fractures) were combined. Prevalence did not differ by GOLD stage, and most people with osteoporosis were untreated [[GraatVerboom2011]].
- GOLD 2023 states that osteoporosis in COPD is often underdiagnosed and that recurrent systemic corticosteroids raise its risk and should be avoided if possible [[Agusti2023]].
- A meta-analysis of 27 studies found frailty in 19% (95% CI 14 to 24) and pre-frailty in 56% of people with COPD. COPD roughly doubled the odds of frailty (OR 1.97, 95% CI 1.53 to 2.53) [[Marengoni2018]].
- Among 816 people referred for pulmonary rehabilitation, 25.6% were frail (Fried criteria). Frail people had twice the odds of not completing the programme (OR 2.20), but those who completed it did well, and 61.3% no longer met frailty criteria afterwards [[Maddocks2016]].
- The ATS/ERS 2014 statement calls limb muscle dysfunction a key systemic consequence of COPD, linked to poorer exercise tolerance, quality of life and survival. It names exercise training as the most potent treatment [[Maltais2014]].
- In 400 patients, low BMI, age and low PaO2 independently predicted death, and mortality rose clearly below a BMI of 25 kg/m². In a separate trial cohort, weight gain of more than 2 kg in 8 weeks predicted survival, so the risk from low weight is partly reversible [[Schols1998]].
- A Cochrane review (17 studies, 632 people) found that nutritional supplements produced a weight gain of 1.65 kg (95% CI 0.14 to 3.16) in undernourished patients but no significant difference in a mixed population [[Ferreira2012]].
- A meta-analysis of 13 RCTs found nutritional support (mostly oral supplements) raised daily intake by 14.8 g protein and 236 kcal, body weight by 1.94 kg and grip strength by 5.3% [[Collins2012]].

### comorbid · Anxiety, depression and thinking
- A critical review reported prevalence ranges of 8–80% for depression and 6–74% for anxiety in COPD, too wide to settle on a figure, because of differing tools and methods [[Yohannes2010]].
- A meta-analysis of 8 studies (39,587 people with COPD vs 39,431 controls) found depressive symptoms in 24.6% of people with COPD vs 11.7% of controls (pooled OR 2.81, 95% CI 1.69 to 4.66) [[Zhang2011]].
- In the FLOW cohort (1,202 with COPD, 302 controls), COPD was linked to anxiety (OR 1.85). Anxiety predicted a shorter 6-minute walk (−66.3 feet), more functional limitation (OR 2.41) and more exacerbations over time (HR 1.39) [[Eisner2010]].
- An ACCP workshop concluded that anxiety and depression in COPD are common, worsen disability and health-care use, are underdiagnosed, and are treated effectively in only a small proportion of patients [[Maurer2008]].
- A Cochrane review of psychological therapy for anxiety in COPD found 3 trials of cognitive behavioural therapy (319 people) with a modest improvement in Beck Anxiety Inventory score (mean difference −4.41 points, 95% CI −8.28 to −0.53; low-quality evidence) [[Usmani2017]].
- GOLD 2023 calls anxiety and depression important and underdiagnosed comorbidities that are associated with poor prognosis [[Agusti2023]].
- A review of cognition in COPD concluded that cognitive function is only mildly impaired without hypoxaemia, that cognitive dysfunction is more common with hypoxaemia, and that it may be linked to higher mortality and disability [[Dodd2010]].

### comorbid · Sleep apnoea, reflux and blood clots
- GOLD 2023 puts sleep apnoea at about 14% of people with COPD and notes that the combination brings more nocturnal desaturation and longer time with hypoxaemia and hypercapnia [[Agusti2023]].
- In a Spanish cohort followed for a median of 9.4 years, people with COPD plus obstructive sleep apnoea (overlap syndrome) who were not treated with CPAP had higher mortality (RR 1.79, 95% CI 1.16 to 2.77) and more severe exacerbations (RR 1.70) than people with COPD alone. Those treated with CPAP had no excess risk (observational) [[Marin2010]].
- In ECLIPSE (2,138 people), the frequent-exacerbator phenotype was independently associated with a history of gastro-oesophageal reflux or heartburn [[Hurst2010]].
- A meta-analysis of 10 observational studies (13,245 people) found reflux disease associated with higher odds of exacerbation (OR 5.37, 95% CI 2.71 to 10.64) [[Huang2020]].
- A Cochrane review of proton pump inhibitors in COPD found a single small Japanese RCT (100 people analysed), in which 12 of 50 on lansoprazole and 26 of 50 on usual care had an exacerbation over a year (RR 0.46). The evidence was low to very low certainty [[Kikuchi2020]].
- A meta-analysis of 7 CT-angiography studies (880 people) found pulmonary embolism in 16.1% (95% CI 8.3 to 25.8) of exacerbations with no clear cause. Two-thirds of the emboli were in main, lobar or interlobar arteries, and pleuritic chest pain and signs of heart failure were more common in those with PE [[Aleva2017]].

### comorbid · Prognosis after admission
- In 171 people admitted with an exacerbation, in-hospital mortality was 8%, rising to 23% at one year (35% for those admitted to ICU). Maintenance oral corticosteroids (RR 5.07), higher PaCO2 and older age independently predicted death [[Groenewegen2003]].

### nursing · Respiratory rate and early warning scores
- Respiratory rate is often not recorded in hospital, even though an abnormal respiratory rate is a recognised predictor of serious clinical events [[Cretikos2008]].
- In 35,585 acute medical admissions, the National Early Warning Score (NEWS) discriminated death within 24 hours with an AUROC of 0.894 and the combined outcome (cardiac arrest, unplanned ICU admission or death) with 0.873, better than 33 other scores [[Smith2013NEWS]].
- NEWS2 has two oxygen saturation scales. The second scale sets the target at 88–92% for people with hypercapnic respiratory failure [[Echevarria2019]].
- In 2,645 people admitted with an exacerbation of COPD, using scale 2 for everyone with COPD cut alerts by 12.6% (16.1% if scoring for oxygen use was exempted) without missing same-day deaths. Its AUROC for inpatient mortality was 0.72, vs 0.65 for NEWS [[Echevarria2019]].
- In that study, the DECAF score predicted inpatient death better than any NEWS version (AUROC 0.82), but the authors note it does not replace repeated NEWS2 to detect deterioration [[Echevarria2019]].
- A multicentre study of 251,266 admissions reached a different conclusion: in 48,898 people coded as at risk of type 2 respiratory failure, NEWS2 discriminated 24-hour mortality slightly less well than NEWS (c-statistic 0.860 vs 0.881), and for documented type 2 failure the two were similar (0.841 vs 0.862) [[Pimentel2019]].

### nursing · Controlled oxygen at the bedside
- The BTS 2017 guideline recommends prescribing oxygen to a target saturation of 94–98% for most acutely ill patients, or 88–92% (or a patient-specific range) for those at risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure, and says best practice is to prescribe a target range for every patient on admission [[ODriscoll2017]].
- Before blood gases are available in COPD, the guideline lists a 24% Venturi mask at 2–3 L/min, a 28% Venturi mask at 4 L/min, or nasal cannulae at 1–2 L/min, aiming for 88–92%. The target is adjusted to 94–98% if the PCO2 is normal [[ODriscoll2017]].
- The guideline says the Venturi mask flow can be increased by up to 50% if respiratory rate is above 30 breaths per minute [[ODriscoll2017]].
- If a patient has an oxygen alert card, the BTS guideline says initial oxygen should follow the card until blood gas results are available [[ODriscoll2017]].
- The guideline recommends clinical assessment if saturation falls by 3% or more or below the target range. It notes that oximetry can be normal despite abnormal pH or PCO2, so blood gases are still needed [[ODriscoll2017]].
- In 1,216 patients with COVID-19 with paired measurements, occult hypoxaemia (SaO2 < 88% while SpO2 read 92–96%) occurred in 28.5% of Black, 30.2% of Asian and 29.8% of non-Black Hispanic patients, compared with 17.2% of White patients. SpO2 overestimated SaO2 by 1.1–1.7 percentage points in these groups [[Fawzy2022]].

### nursing · Inhaler technique teaching
- A systematic review of 144 studies (54,354 people, 1975–2014) found correct inhaler technique in only 31%. The commonest metered-dose inhaler errors were poor coordination (45%), wrong speed or depth of inhalation (44%) and no breath-hold (46%), and technique had not improved over 40 years [[Sanchis2016]].
- In 1,664 experienced chest-clinic patients, critical errors ranged from 12% (metered-dose inhalers) to 35% (Diskus, HandiHaler) and 44% (Turbuhaler). Misuse was linked to older age, less schooling and never having been taught by a health professional, and to more admissions, ER visits and steroid and antibiotic courses [[Melani2011]].
- The authors of that study concluded that instruction by health caregivers was the only modifiable factor linked to less mishandling [[Melani2011]].
- In a randomised trial in hospitalised adults, most said they were confident with their inhaler, yet 62% misused their metered-dose inhaler before teaching. After "teach-to-goal" (repeated demonstration plus teach-back), misuse was 12.5% vs 46% after brief verbal and written instruction (p = 0.01) [[Press2012]].

### nursing · Sputum and exacerbation monitoring
- In 121 people presenting to primary care with an exacerbation, purulent (green) sputum grew bacteria in 84% compared with 38% for mucoid sputum. Green sputum was 94.4% sensitive and 77.0% specific for a high bacterial load [[Stockley2000]].
- In that study, everyone with white (mucoid) sputum during the exacerbation improved without antibiotics [[Stockley2000]].
- In ECLIPSE, exacerbation rates rose with severity (0.85, 1.34 and 2.00 per person in year 1 for GOLD 2, 3 and 4), and the best single predictor of future exacerbations was a history of exacerbations [[Hurst2010]].

### nursing · Home oxygen fire safety
- The BTS home oxygen guideline includes risk assessment of home-oxygen users who smoke as one of its key areas [[Hardinge2015]].
- A review of the risks says tobacco smoking is the greatest fire risk for people on home oxygen, that any naked flame is a hazard, and that the European Industrial Gases Association considers e-cigarettes unsafe to use with home oxygen [[Cooper2015]].
- The same review cites estimates that 14–51% of home-oxygen users continue to smoke. US data from four states recorded 38 deaths from smoking-related home oxygen fires over 2000–2007 [[Cooper2015]].
- In a Canadian burn-centre series (1999–2008), 17 patients, all with COPD, were burned while smoking on home oxygen (mean burn size 2.8% of body surface). Two needed intubation for inhalation injury, mean hospital stay was 42.8 days, two died, and fewer could return home afterwards (a 35.3% reduction) [[Murabit2012]].
- A US modelling study found that a national mandate for thermal fuses (firebreaks that stop flames travelling along the oxygen tubing) would save money from a societal perspective and be cost-effective for Medicare (Early evidence: modelling) [[Sheckter2024]].

### Exam hooks
- Self-management with a written action plan: SGRQ −2.69 and fewer respiratory admissions (OR 0.69; NNT 12 at high risk) [[Lenferink2017]].
- The 2022 Cochrane SGRQ gain (−2.86) is below the 4-point MCID [[Schrijver2022]].
- A VA self-management trial was stopped for excess deaths (28 vs 10) [[Fan2012]].
- Pursed-lip breathing lengthens expiration and reduces end-expiratory lung volume [[Bianchi2004]].
- Cabin altitude is capped at 8,000 ft (2,438 m); the hypoxic challenge test uses 15% oxygen [[Coker2022]].
- HCT: PaO2 < 6.6 kPa or SpO2 < 85% means in-flight oxygen is recommended [[Coker2022]].
- Cardiovascular events peak in the first 30 days after an exacerbation (SUMMIT HR 3.8; 9.9 after a hospitalised exacerbation) [[Kunisaki2018]].
- MI risk is 2.27-fold in days 1–5 after an exacerbation [[Donaldson2010]].
- One in five older people labelled COPD have unrecognised heart failure (20.5%) [[Rutten2005]].
- Cardioselective β-blockers are used when there is a cardiac indication (GOLD) [[Agusti2023]]; metoprolol given without one increased hospitalised exacerbations [[Dransfield2019]].
- Lung cancer incidence in COPD is about 16.7 per 1,000 person-years; squamous is the commonest type [[deTorres2011]].
- NLST: low-dose CT cut lung-cancer mortality by 20% [[Aberle2011]]. USPSTF 2021: age 50–80, 20 pack-years, quit within 15 years [[Krist2021]].
- COTE index ≥ 4 doubles mortality risk at any BODE quartile [[Divo2012]].
- Frailty affects about 1 in 4–5 people with COPD and is reversible with rehabilitation in many [[Marengoni2018,Maddocks2016]].
- Mortality rises below BMI 25 kg/m² in COPD [[Schols1998]].
- Overlap syndrome untreated with CPAP: mortality RR 1.79 [[Marin2010]].
- PE is found in about 16% of unexplained exacerbations [[Aleva2017]].
- Oxygen target in hypercapnic risk: 88–92% (24% Venturi 2–3 L/min or 28% at 4 L/min) [[ODriscoll2017]]; NEWS2 scale 2 uses this range [[Echevarria2019]].
- Only 31% of patients use inhalers correctly; teach-back cuts MDI misuse to 12.5% [[Sanchis2016,Press2012]].
- Green sputum: 84% culture-positive vs 38% for mucoid [[Stockley2000]].
- Smoking is the main cause of home-oxygen fires [[Cooper2015]].

### In plain words
- A written action plan tells you what your usual day looks like and what to do when your breathing, cough or phlegm get worse. Studies show plans like this help people stay out of hospital a bit more often [[Lenferink2017]].
- Breathing out slowly through pursed lips, as if blowing out a candle, gives the lungs more time to empty. This can ease breathlessness [[Bianchi2004]].
- A small handheld fan blowing on your face can make breathlessness feel less intense [[Galbraith2010]].
- People with COPD who stay active, even a little, tend to do better, although studies cannot fully prove that the activity itself is the cause [[GarciaAymerich2006]].
- Aeroplane cabins have less oxygen than air at sea level. If your oxygen is low, a test before you fly can show whether you need oxygen on the plane [[Coker2022]].
- Many people with COPD also have heart problems. The weeks right after a flare-up are a time of higher risk for heart attacks and strokes [[Kunisaki2018]].
- If you smoked, you may be able to have a yearly low-dose CT scan to look for lung cancer early [[Krist2021]].
- COPD can thin your bones, so fractures are more likely, especially after many courses of steroid tablets [[GraatVerboom2009,Agusti2023]].
- Losing weight without trying is a warning sign in COPD. Extra food and supplements can help people who are too thin [[Schols1998,Ferreira2012]].
- Feeling anxious or low is common with COPD. It is part of the illness, and help is available [[Zhang2011,Usmani2017]].
- Oxygen makes fire burn much faster. Smoking, e-cigarettes and open flames near home oxygen have caused serious burns and deaths [[Cooper2015,Murabit2012]].
- Most people make at least one inhaler mistake without knowing it. Being watched and shown again by a nurse cut mistakes a lot in one study [[Sanchis2016,Press2012]].
- It is normal to talk with your care team about sex, work, and what care you would want if you became very ill. These talks can help both you and the people close to you [[Kaptein2008,Houben2019]].

### living · Step sequence
1. Kicker "Know your baseline" — Title: Your usual day. A written action plan starts from your stable symptoms and is part of a structured self-management programme [[Lenferink2017]].
2. Kicker "Spot the change" — Title: Symptoms worsen. The plan names the warning signs of an exacerbation, such as more breathlessness or more or greener sputum (green sputum grew bacteria in 84% of cases) [[Stockley2000,Lenferink2017]].
3. Kicker "Act early" — Title: Start the plan. People given plans that include prompt treatment had fewer respiratory admissions (OR 0.69; NNT 12 at high risk) [[Lenferink2017]].
4. Kicker "Breathe and pace" — Title: Pursed lips, fan, rest. Pursed-lip breathing lowered end-expiratory volume by 0.33 L, and a fan to the face reduced breathlessness [[Bianchi2004,Galbraith2010]].
5. Kicker "Call for help" — Title: Contact the team. Self-management only helped some people: in one trial just 42% became successful self-managers, so support and follow-up matter [[Bucknall2012]].
6. Kicker "Plan ahead" — Title: Future wishes. A single nurse-led advance care planning session improved end-of-life conversations without raising distress [[Houben2019]].

### comorbid · Step sequence
1. Kicker "Stable" — Title: Baseline risk. People with COPD have 2.46-fold higher odds of cardiovascular disease [[Chen2015]].
2. Kicker "Flare-up" — Title: Exacerbation begins. During a severe exacerbation, cardiovascular event risk rises steeply (HR 21.84 in IMPACT) [[Dransfield2022]].
3. Kicker "Days 1–5" — Title: Heart attack window. MI risk is 2.27-fold higher in the first 5 days after a treated exacerbation [[Donaldson2010]].
4. Kicker "First 30 days" — Title: Peak cardiovascular risk. In SUMMIT, the 30-day HR was 3.8 overall and 9.9 after a hospitalised exacerbation [[Kunisaki2018]].
5. Kicker "Up to 49 days" — Title: Stroke risk lingers. Stroke risk was 1.26-fold higher for 1–49 days after an exacerbation [[Donaldson2010]].
6. Kicker "By 90 days" — Title: Back toward baseline. In IMPACT, risk had returned to baseline by 90 days, which is why exacerbation prevention also protects the heart [[Dransfield2022]].

### nursing · Step sequence
1. Kicker "Count" — Title: Respiratory rate. Respiratory rate is often left unrecorded in hospital, even though an abnormal rate predicts serious events [[Cretikos2008]].
2. Kicker "Score" — Title: NEWS2 and scale 2. For confirmed hypercapnic failure, scale 2 targets 88–92%, which in one study reduced alerts by 12.6% without missing early deaths [[Echevarria2019]].
3. Kicker "Prescribe a target" — Title: Controlled oxygen. BTS lists a 24% Venturi mask at 2–3 L/min or 28% at 4 L/min aiming for 88–92% until blood gases are back [[ODriscoll2017]].
4. Kicker "Check the gas" — Title: Oximetry has limits. Oximetry can be normal despite abnormal pH or PCO2, and it can overestimate saturation in people with darker skin [[ODriscoll2017,Fawzy2022]].
5. Kicker "Teach back" — Title: Inhaler technique. Teach-to-goal with teach-back lowered MDI misuse from 46% to 12.5% [[Press2012]].
6. Kicker "Go home safely" — Title: Oxygen fire safety. Smoking while on home oxygen causes burns with long hospital stays and deaths [[Murabit2012,Cooper2015]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Is self-management safe? Cochrane 2017 found a small exploratory excess in respiratory deaths [[Lenferink2017]], and two RCTs found harm (excess deaths in the VA programme; more acute care events in the Baltimore programme) [[Fan2012,Aboumatar2019]]. Cochrane 2022, with stricter inclusion, found no excess mortality [[Schrijver2022]]. Status: the average benefit is modest and below the SGRQ MCID, and programme design and who delivers it appear to matter.
- Beta-blockers without a cardiac indication: observational data suggested benefit, but BLOCK COPD (metoprolol) increased hospitalised exacerbations [[Dransfield2019]] and BICS (bisoprolol) showed no effect on exacerbations with similar safety [[Devereux2024]]. GOLD supports cardioselective β-blockers when a cardiac indication exists [[Agusti2023]]. Status: settled for "no routine use to prevent exacerbations"; the safety of bisoprolol when indicated is reassuring.
- NEWS2 scale 2: Echevarria et al. found scale 2 for all COPD admissions safely cut alerts and improved prognostic performance over NEWS [[Echevarria2019]]; Pimentel et al. found NEWS2 slightly worse than NEWS in people at risk of type 2 respiratory failure [[Pimentel2019]]. Status: unresolved; it depends on who is assigned to scale 2.
- Does the hypoxic challenge test predict trouble in flight? It is the BTS reference test [[Coker2022]], but PaO2 during simulation did not separate people with and without in-flight symptoms [[Edvardsen2013]]. Status: it predicts hypoxaemia, not symptoms.
- Do proton pump inhibitors prevent exacerbations? The association with reflux is strong but observational [[Huang2020,Hurst2010]]; the only RCT was small and unblinded [[Kikuchi2020]]; GOLD calls their value controversial [[Agusti2023]]. Status: open.
- Does CPAP improve survival in overlap syndrome? The supporting cohort is observational and CPAP users may differ from non-users [[Marin2010]]. Status: no RCT cited here (Early evidence).
- Lung cancer screening in COPD not caused by smoking: GOLD says there are insufficient data on benefit versus harm [[Agusti2023]]. Status: open.

## Provenance
- Search: Europe PMC REST search (title phrases and author terms) and PubMed esearch. All 69 PMIDs were then confirmed in two batched PubMed esummary calls (65 + 4). Authors (first six), title, journal, year, volume, issue, pages, PMCID and DOI in the Sources lines come from those esummary records; article numbers for online-only journals (Cochrane CD numbers, BMJ e1060, BMC Public Health 612, JAHA e024350, BMJ Open Respir Res e000170) come from the record's pages field.
- Abstracts were read for all sources through PubMed efetch (two batched calls). All numbers come from those abstracts, except for four sources with no or minimal abstract (Coker 2022, O'Driscoll 2017, Agustí 2023, Cooper 2015). Their numbers and statements were read from the open-access full text through Europe PMC fullTextXML (PMC8938676, PMC5531304, PMC10111975, PMC4487390). Hardinge 2015, Maurer 2008, Mansfield 2016, Dodd 2010, Maltais 2014 and Yohannes 2010 are cited only for statements their abstracts make.
- Reuse: Smith2013NEWS (PMID 23295778) already exists in sepsis-and-shock/src/sources.json and keeps that key and text. No other source in this pass was found in any companion's sources.json (all PMIDs grepped). Agusti2023 is the shared canonical key and was checked (PMID 36856433, the AJRCCM version). Hurst2010 may also be cited by pass E; the compiler merges by PMID.
- Not used: the Royal College of Physicians NEWS2 report (2017), which is not indexed as a journal record in any validation service; Sjoding 2020 NEJM pulse-oximetry letter (no abstract); the Wendling 2008 US home-oxygen fire deaths analysis (quoted only second-hand through Cooper 2015, whose full text was read); NICE NG115 (not indexed).
- Not stated because no validated abstract or open full text gave it: specific NEWS2 point bands for respiratory rate and SpO2, nationwide home-oxygen fire incidence, specific prevalence figures for each item in the BODE "comorbidome" (only the COTE index results from the abstract are quoted), and sexual-dysfunction prevalence figures.
