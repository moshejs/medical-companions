# D · Pharmacological treatment of stable COPD and inhalers

Pass date: 2026-11-07. Modules served: inhalers, escalation, technique.

This pass covers the medicines used for stable COPD: short-acting relievers (SABA, SAMA), long-acting bronchodilators (LAMA, LABA) alone and combined, inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) and the blood eosinophil count, triple therapy and mortality, ICS withdrawal, roflumilast, long-term azithromycin, the biologics dupilumab and mepolizumab, and the inhaled PDE3/4 inhibitor ensifentrine. It also covers inhaler devices (pressurised metered-dose inhalers, dry powder inhalers, soft mist inhalers, nebulisers), peak inspiratory flow, error rates, and the carbon footprint of propellant inhalers. Trial numbers are copied from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstracts. Guideline statements and label text are quoted as published. They report what guidelines, trials and labels say. They are not instructions for treating anyone.

## Findings

### inhalers · How the bronchodilators work (label pharmacology)
- The Spiriva label describes tiotropium as "a long-acting, antimuscarinic agent" with similar affinity for muscarinic receptor subtypes M1 to M5. In the airways it acts through "inhibition of M3-receptors at the smooth muscle leading to bronchodilation" [[FDA-SpirivaHandiHaler]].
- In preclinical studies, tiotropium's protection against methacholine-induced bronchoconstriction "lasted longer than 24 hours" (label). This is why it is dosed once daily [[FDA-SpirivaHandiHaler]].
- The Ventolin HFA label says albuterol (salbutamol) acts preferentially on β2-adrenergic receptors. β2-receptors predominate in bronchial smooth muscle. They also make up 10% to 50% of the β-receptors in the human heart, which "raises the possibility that even selective beta2-agonists may have cardiac effects" [[FDA-VentolinHFA]].
- Ventolin HFA is labelled for "treatment or prevention of bronchospasm" in people aged 4 and older with reversible obstructive airway disease. The labelled dose is "2 inhalations by oral inhalation every 4 to 6 hours" [[FDA-VentolinHFA]].
- The Stiolto Respimat label describes the LAMA-LABA pairing as combining "2 different classes of medication (an anticholinergic and a beta-agonist) that have different effects on clinical and physiological indices" [[FDA-Stiolto]].

### inhalers · Short-acting relievers (SABA, SAMA)
- A Cochrane review of 13 crossover trials found that regular SABA use for at least 7 days in stable COPD gave a small rise in post-bronchodilator FEV1: weighted mean difference 0.14 L (95% CI 0.04 to 0.25) [[Sestini2002]].
- In the same review, daily breathlessness scores improved with β2-agonists (SMD 1.33; 95% CI 1.0 to 1.65). Patients preferred β2-agonists to placebo almost 10 times more often (OR 9.04; 95% CI 4.64 to 17.61) [[Sestini2002]].
- The SABA trials were too short and too small to tell anything reliable about long-term adverse effects or mortality (review authors' conclusion) [[Sestini2002]].
- For flare-ups, GOLD 2023 says SABA "with or without short-acting anticholinergics (SAMA), are the initial bronchodilators for acute treatment" of an exacerbation, given by MDI "with a spacer device if necessary, or nebulization" [[Agusti2023]].
- Every maintenance product in this pass is labelled as not for acute symptoms. Examples: Trelegy "NOT indicated for the relief of acute bronchospasm"; Stiolto "NOT indicated to treat acute deterioration of COPD"; Daliresp "is not a bronchodilator" [[FDA-Trelegy,FDA-Stiolto,FDA-Daliresp]].

### inhalers · LAMA: tiotropium and the UPLIFT trial
- UPLIFT randomised 5993 patients (mean post-bronchodilator FEV1 1.32 L, 48% of predicted) to tiotropium or placebo for 4 years. Other respiratory medicines were allowed, except inhaled anticholinergics [[Tashkin2008]].
- UPLIFT's co-primary end points were the rate of decline in FEV1 before and after bronchodilator. The two arms did not differ significantly in rate of decline after day 30 [[Tashkin2008]].
- Tiotropium kept FEV1 higher throughout UPLIFT: by 87–103 mL before bronchodilator and 47–65 mL after bronchodilator versus placebo (P<0.001). SGRQ scores were 2.3–3.3 units better at every time point [[Tashkin2008]].
- At 4 years and 30 days, tiotropium was associated with lower risks of exacerbations, related hospitalisations and respiratory failure [[Tashkin2008]].
- Cochrane (22 studies, 23,309 participants): tiotropium versus placebo reduced the number of people with exacerbations (OR 0.78; 95% CI 0.70 to 0.87). That is a number needed to treat of 16 (95% CI 10 to 36) for 1 year, at a placebo event rate of 44% [[Karner2014]].
- In that review there was no overall mortality difference (Peto OR 0.98; 95% CI 0.86 to 1.11). Subgroups split by device: the soft mist inhaler showed more deaths with tiotropium (Peto OR 1.47; 95% CI 1.04 to 2.08), the dry powder inhaler did not (Peto OR 0.92; 95% CI 0.80 to 1.05) [[Karner2014]].
- TIOSPIR was designed to settle that safety signal. It randomised 17,135 patients to tiotropium Respimat 2.5 or 5 µg or HandiHaler 18 µg, with a mean follow-up of 2.3 years. Respimat 5 µg was noninferior to HandiHaler for death (HR 0.96; 95% CI 0.84 to 1.09), as was 2.5 µg (HR 1.00; 95% CI 0.87 to 1.14) [[Wise2013]].
- TIOSPIR found that Respimat 5 µg was not superior to HandiHaler for first exacerbation (HR 0.98; 95% CI 0.93 to 1.03). Causes of death and major cardiovascular events were similar across the groups [[Wise2013]].
- The Spiriva HandiHaler label gives the dose as "two inhalations of the powder contents of one SPIRIVA capsule, once-daily" and warns "Do not swallow SPIRIVA capsules". Spiriva Respimat is "two inhalations once-daily" [[FDA-SpirivaHandiHaler,FDA-SpirivaRespimat]].

### inhalers · LABA, and LAMA versus LABA (POET-COPD)
- Cochrane (26 RCTs, 14,939 people): twice-daily LABA versus placebo improved SGRQ (MD −2.32; 95% CI −3.09 to −1.54). It reduced exacerbations needing hospitalisation (OR 0.73; 95% CI 0.56 to 0.95), which is 18 fewer per 1000 over about 7 months [[Kew2013]].
- In the same review, LABA did not significantly affect mortality [[Kew2013]].
- POET-COPD randomised 7376 patients with moderate-to-very-severe COPD and a recent exacerbation to tiotropium 18 µg once daily or salmeterol 50 µg twice daily for 1 year [[Vogelmeier2011]].
- POET-COPD primary end point: tiotropium lengthened the time to first moderate or severe exacerbation from 145 to 187 days (HR 0.83; 95% CI 0.77 to 0.90; P<0.001) [[Vogelmeier2011]].
- In POET-COPD the annual rate of moderate or severe exacerbations was 0.64 with tiotropium versus 0.72 with salmeterol (rate ratio 0.89; 95% CI 0.83 to 0.96). For severe exacerbations it was 0.09 versus 0.13 (rate ratio 0.73; 95% CI 0.66 to 0.82) [[Vogelmeier2011]].
- POET-COPD reported 64 deaths (1.7%) with tiotropium and 78 (2.1%) with salmeterol. Serious adverse events were similar [[Vogelmeier2011]].
- Cochrane (7 studies, 12,223 participants): tiotropium versus LABA reduced the number of people with one or more exacerbations (OR 0.86; 95% CI 0.79 to 0.93) and exacerbation-related hospitalisation (OR 0.87; 95% CI 0.77 to 0.99). Mortality did not differ [[Chong2012]].

### inhalers · Dual bronchodilation (LAMA + LABA)
- The ATS 2020 guideline makes "a strong recommendation for the use of long-acting β2-agonist (LABA)/long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA) combination therapy over LABA or LAMA monotherapy in patients with COPD and dyspnea or exercise intolerance" [[Nici2020]].
- GOLD 2023 changed Group B: "a dual long-acting bronchodilator combination ... is now recommended since dual therapy is more effective than monotherapy, with similar side effects" [[Agusti2023]].
- GOLD 2023: for Group E, "LAMA + LABA is also the recommended initial therapy, except for those patients with blood eosinophils ≥300 cells/μl, in whom starting triple therapy (LABA + LAMA + ICS) can be considered". GOLD calls this "a practical recommendation; direct evidence is not available" [[Agusti2023]].
- DYNAGITO randomised 7880 patients to tiotropium-olodaterol or tiotropium alone. The exacerbation rate ratio was 0.93 (99% CI 0.85 to 1.02; P=0.0498), which missed the trial's 0.01 significance target. The authors concluded the combination "did not reduce exacerbation rate as much as expected" [[Calverley2018]].
- FLAME (3362 patients, all with at least one exacerbation in the previous year) compared indacaterol-glycopyrronium (LABA+LAMA) with salmeterol-fluticasone (LABA+ICS) over 52 weeks. The primary end point was the annual rate of all exacerbations: 3.59 versus 4.03 (rate ratio 0.89; 95% CI 0.83 to 0.96; P=0.003). The LABA+LAMA was both noninferior and superior [[Wedzicha2016]].
- In FLAME the moderate-or-severe exacerbation rate was 0.98 versus 1.19 (rate ratio 0.83; 95% CI 0.75 to 0.91). Pneumonia occurred in 3.2% versus 4.8% (P=0.02). The benefit did not depend on baseline blood eosinophil count [[Wedzicha2016]].
- Cochrane 2023 update (19 studies, 22,354 participants): LAMA+LABA and LABA+ICS had similar odds of exacerbation (OR 0.91; 95% CI 0.78 to 1.06). LAMA+LABA gave a greater trough FEV1 gain (MD 0.07 L; 95% CI 0.05 to 0.08) [[Fukuda2023]].
- Dual-bronchodilator products, as labelled: Anoro Ellipta (umeclidinium/vilanterol) "for the maintenance treatment of patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease" and "NOT indicated ... for the treatment of asthma". Stiolto Respimat (tiotropium/olodaterol) for "long-term, once-daily maintenance treatment" of COPD [[FDA-Anoro,FDA-Stiolto]].

### escalation · Inhaled corticosteroids: TORCH, SUMMIT and pneumonia
- TORCH randomised 6112 patients for 3 years to salmeterol 50 µg plus fluticasone propionate 500 µg twice daily, either component alone, or placebo. The primary outcome was all-cause death, combination versus placebo [[Calverley2007]].
- TORCH mortality was 12.6% (combination), 15.2% (placebo), 13.5% (salmeterol) and 16.0% (fluticasone). The combination-versus-placebo HR was 0.825 (95% CI 0.681 to 1.002; P=0.052), so it did not reach significance [[Calverley2007]].
- In TORCH the combination cut the annual exacerbation rate from 1.13 to 0.85 versus placebo. The probability of pneumonia reported as an adverse event was 19.6% (combination) and 18.3% (fluticasone) versus 12.3% (placebo) [[Calverley2007]].
- SUMMIT enrolled 16,485 patients with moderate COPD and heightened cardiovascular risk. Fluticasone furoate/vilanterol did not change all-cause mortality versus placebo (HR 0.88; 95% CI 0.74 to 1.04; P=0.137). Composite cardiovascular events were also unchanged (HR 0.93; 95% CI 0.75 to 1.14) [[Vestbo2016]].
- In SUMMIT, FEV1 declined by 38 mL/year with the combination versus 46 mL/year with placebo (difference 8 mL/year; 95% CI 1 to 15). The authors noted that secondary outcomes should be read with caution because the primary outcome was neutral [[Vestbo2016]].
- SUMMIT reported no excess pneumonia: 5% placebo, 6% combination, 5% fluticasone furoate, 4% vilanterol [[Vestbo2016]].
- Cochrane (43 studies): fluticasone increased serious (hospitalised) pneumonia (OR 1.78; 95% CI 1.50 to 2.12), which is 18 more per 1000 treated over 18 months (high quality). Budesonide also raised it (OR 1.62; 95% CI 1.00 to 2.62; 6 more per 1000 over 9 months), but less precisely [[Kew2014]].
- In that review, neither ICS significantly changed overall mortality versus controls (high-quality evidence). Pneumonia deaths were too rare to draw conclusions [[Kew2014]].
- ICS-containing labels carry a pneumonia warning. The Trelegy label says "Physicians should remain vigilant for the possible development of pneumonia in patients with COPD as clinical features of pneumonia and exacerbations frequently overlap" [[FDA-Trelegy]].

### escalation · The blood eosinophil count as an ICS biomarker
- In a secondary analysis of two fluticasone furoate/vilanterol trials (3177 patients), adding the ICS cut exacerbations by 29% in patients with eosinophils ≥2% (0.91 vs 1.28 per year). Below 2% the cut was 10% (0.79 vs 0.89; P=0.28) [[Pascoe2015]].
- The ICS effect in that analysis rose with eosinophil percentage: 24% at ≥2–<4%, 32% at 4–<6% and 42% at ≥6%. The pneumonia increase with ICS did not depend on eosinophils [[Pascoe2015]].
- In a pooled analysis of three budesonide-formoterol trials (4528 patients), the ICS reduced exacerbations at eosinophil counts of 0.10 × 10⁹/L (100 cells/µL) or more (rate ratio 0.75; 95% CI 0.57 to 0.99). Eosinophil count and smoking history were the only independent predictors of response [[Bafadhel2018]].
- GOLD 2023: "Adding ICS has little or no effect at a blood eosinophil count of <100 cells/μl, whereas blood eosinophils ≥300 cells/μl identify patients with a strong likelihood of treatment benefit". Between 100 and 300 there is "a continuous gradation" [[Agusti2023]].
- GOLD 2023 adds that decisions "can be based on historical eosinophil counts" because repeatability is reasonable, though variability is greater at higher thresholds [[Agusti2023]].
- GOLD 2023: "The use of LABA + ICS in COPD is no longer encouraged. If there is an indication for an ICS, then LABA + LAMA + ICS has been shown to be superior to LABA + ICS and is therefore the preferred choice" [[Agusti2023]].
- GOLD 2023: if a patient with COPD has concomitant asthma, "they should be treated as if they have asthma" [[Agusti2023]].

### escalation · Triple therapy: IMPACT and ETHOS
- IMPACT randomised 10,355 patients to once-daily single-inhaler fluticasone furoate/umeclidinium/vilanterol 100/62.5/25 µg (triple), fluticasone furoate/vilanterol, or umeclidinium/vilanterol for 52 weeks. All used the Ellipta inhaler [[Lipson2018]].
- IMPACT primary end point (annual moderate or severe exacerbations): 0.91 with triple versus 1.07 with FF/VI (rate ratio 0.85; 95% CI 0.80 to 0.90) and 1.21 with UMEC/VI (rate ratio 0.75; 95% CI 0.70 to 0.81), both P<0.001 [[Lipson2018,NCT02164513]].
- In IMPACT, severe exacerbations leading to hospitalisation were 0.13 per year with triple versus 0.19 with UMEC/VI (rate ratio 0.66; 95% CI 0.56 to 0.78) [[Lipson2018]].
- Clinician-diagnosed pneumonia was more frequent with triple than with UMEC/VI in IMPACT (HR 1.53; 95% CI 1.22 to 1.92) [[Lipson2018]].
- ETHOS randomised patients with at least one exacerbation in the past year to twice-daily budesonide 320 or 160 µg with glycopyrrolate 18 µg and formoterol 9.6 µg (triple), glycopyrrolate-formoterol, or budesonide-formoterol. The modified intention-to-treat population was 8509 (registry enrolment 8588) [[Rabe2020,NCT02465567]].
- ETHOS annual moderate-or-severe exacerbation rates were 1.08 (triple, budesonide 320), 1.07 (triple, budesonide 160), 1.42 (glycopyrrolate-formoterol) and 1.24 (budesonide-formoterol) [[Rabe2020]].
- Triple therapy with budesonide 320 µg versus glycopyrrolate-formoterol in ETHOS: rate ratio 0.76 (95% CI 0.69 to 0.83). Versus budesonide-formoterol: 0.87 (95% CI 0.79 to 0.95). The budesonide 160 µg triple gave 0.75 (0.69 to 0.83) and 0.86 (0.79 to 0.95) [[Rabe2020]].
- In ETHOS, confirmed pneumonia occurred in 3.5–4.5% of the ICS-containing groups versus 2.3% with glycopyrrolate-formoterol [[Rabe2020]].
- Triple products, as labelled: Trelegy Ellipta COPD dose is "1 actuation of TRELEGY ELLIPTA 100/62.5/25 mcg once daily". Breztri Aerosphere COPD dose is "2 inhalations of BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 160 mcg/9 mcg/4.8 mcg twice daily" (US labelling states the glycopyrrolate dose per actuation differently from ETHOS's 18 µg) [[FDA-Trelegy,FDA-Breztri,Rabe2020]].
- The ATS 2020 guideline makes "a conditional recommendation for the use of triple therapy ... over dual therapy with LABA/LAMA in patients with COPD and dyspnea or exercise intolerance who have experienced one or more exacerbations in the past year" [[Nici2020]].

### escalation · Triple therapy and mortality
- IMPACT's final vital-status analysis (99.6% of 10,355) counted 98 deaths (2.36%) with triple, 109 (2.64%) with FF/VI and 66 (3.19%) with UMEC/VI. Triple versus UMEC/VI: HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.53 to 0.99; P=0.042). Triple versus FF/VI: HR 0.89 (95% CI 0.67 to 1.16) [[Lipson2020]].
- The IMPACT mortality analysis was a secondary analysis, and the extra vital-status collection was done post hoc [[Lipson2020]].
- In ETHOS's final retrieved dataset (vital status for 99.6%), budesonide 320 µg triple lowered the risk of death versus glycopyrrolate-formoterol (HR 0.51; 95% CI 0.33 to 0.80; unadjusted P=0.0035). It was not significantly different from budesonide-formoterol (HR 0.72; 95% CI 0.44 to 1.16) [[Martinez2021]].
- The lower-dose (budesonide 160 µg) triple in ETHOS did not significantly reduce death versus either dual comparator. Cardiovascular deaths were 0.5%, 0.8%, 1.4% and 0.5% across the four arms [[Martinez2021]].
- GOLD 2023 includes IMPACT and ETHOS in its figure of "Evidence supporting a reduction in mortality with pharmacotherapy and nonpharmacotherapy in patients with COPD" [[Agusti2023]].
- Synthesis (Early evidence): in people who exacerbate, two large trials found lower all-cause mortality with ICS-containing triple therapy than with LAMA+LABA. Both were secondary end points with low event rates, and the ETHOS signal appeared only at the higher ICS dose [[Lipson2020,Martinez2021]].

### escalation · Stepping ICS down: WISDOM, SUNSET and the ERS guideline
- WISDOM gave 2485 patients with a history of exacerbations tiotropium + salmeterol + fluticasone propionate 500 µg twice daily. Fluticasone was then withdrawn in three steps over 12 weeks, or continued [[Magnussen2014]].
- WISDOM primary end point: time to first moderate or severe exacerbation with withdrawal versus continuation, HR 1.06 (95% CI 0.94 to 1.19). This met the noninferiority margin of 1.20 [[Magnussen2014]].
- At week 18 in WISDOM, trough FEV1 fell 38 mL more in the withdrawal group than with continued ICS (P<0.001), and 43 mL more at week 52 [[Magnussen2014]].
- SUNSET switched 1053 non-frequently-exacerbating patients from long-term triple therapy to indacaterol/glycopyrronium. Trough FEV1 fell by 26 mL (95% CI −53 to 1), which crossed the −50 mL noninferiority bound. The exacerbation rate did not differ (rate ratio 1.08; 95% CI 0.83 to 1.40) [[Chapman2018]].
- In SUNSET, patients with ≥300 eosinophils/µL at baseline lost more lung function and had higher exacerbation risk after ICS withdrawal [[Chapman2018]].
- The ERS 2020 guideline made a conditional recommendation for "the withdrawal of ICS in patients with COPD without a history of frequent exacerbations". It made a strong recommendation "not to withdraw ICS in patients with blood eosinophil counts ≥300 eosinophils·µL-1" [[Chalmers2020]].
- The ERS guideline also strongly recommends treatment "with one or two long-acting bronchodilators if ICS are withdrawn" [[Chalmers2020]].
- ATS 2020: "a conditional recommendation for ICS withdrawal for patients with COPD receiving triple therapy (ICS/LABA/LAMA) if the patient has had no exacerbations in the past year" [[Nici2020]].
- GOLD 2023: "ICS de-escalation can be considered if pneumonia or other considerable side effects develop. In case of a blood eosinophil count ≥300 cells/μl, ICS de-escalation is more likely to be associated with development of exacerbations" [[Agusti2023]].

### escalation · GOLD 2023 follow-up algorithm (as published)
- GOLD 2023 bases follow-up treatment on two treatable traits, "dyspnea and occurrence of exacerbations" [[Agusti2023]].
- Persistent dyspnoea on one long-acting bronchodilator: "a step up to LABA + LAMA is recommended". If that fails, "clinicians should consider switching inhaler device or molecules, as well as investigating and treating other causes of dyspnea" [[Agusti2023]].
- Exacerbations on monotherapy: "escalation to LABA + LAMA is recommended, except for patients with blood eosinophils ≥300 cells/μl, who may be escalated to LABA + LAMA + ICS" [[Agusti2023]].
- Exacerbations on LABA + LAMA: "escalation to LABA + LAMA + ICS is recommended if they have blood eosinophils ≥100 cells/μl" [[Agusti2023]].
- Exacerbations despite triple therapy, or eosinophils <100 cells/µL: "the addition of roflumilast (particularly in patients with chronic bronchitis and an FEV1 < 50% predicted) or a macrolide (particularly in patients who are not current smokers) may be considered" [[Agusti2023]].
- GOLD 2023 notes that "Treatment escalation has not been systematically tested, and trials of de-escalation are limited to withdrawing ICS" [[Agusti2023]].
- The ATS 2020 guideline makes "a conditional recommendation against the use of maintenance oral corticosteroids in patients with COPD and a history of severe and frequent exacerbations" [[Nici2020]].

### escalation · Roflumilast (oral PDE4 inhibitor)
- The Daliresp label explains that roflumilast and its active metabolite inhibit PDE4, "a major cyclic-3′,5′-adenosine monophosphate (cyclic AMP)-metabolizing enzyme in lung tissue". The exact therapeutic mechanism "is not well defined" [[FDA-Daliresp]].
- In two 52-week trials (M2-124/125; 3091 patients with severe airflow limitation, bronchitic symptoms and prior exacerbations), roflumilast 500 µg daily raised pre-bronchodilator FEV1 by 48 mL versus placebo. Moderate-or-severe exacerbations were 1.14 versus 1.37 per patient-year (17% reduction; 95% CI 8 to 25) [[Calverley2009]].
- Mean weight change with roflumilast differed from placebo by −2.17 kg in the pooled analysis. Discontinuations because of adverse events were 14% versus 12% [[Calverley2009]].
- Added to salmeterol or tiotropium (M2-127/128), roflumilast improved pre-bronchodilator FEV1 by 49 mL and 80 mL. Nausea, diarrhoea, weight loss and headache were more frequent [[Fabbri2009]].
- REACT (1945 patients with severe COPD, chronic bronchitis and ≥2 exacerbations on ICS/LABA): the primary Poisson analysis gave rate ratio 0.868 (95% CI 0.753 to 1.002; P=0.0529). The predefined negative-binomial sensitivity analysis gave 0.858 (0.740 to 0.995; P=0.0424) [[Martinez2015]].
- In REACT, withdrawals due to adverse events were 11% with roflumilast versus 5% with placebo [[Martinez2015]].
- Cochrane (roflumilast 28 trials, 18,046 participants): PDE4 inhibitors lowered the odds of exacerbation (OR 0.78; 95% CI 0.73 to 0.84; high certainty), or 5 more people exacerbation-free per 100 treated. The FEV1 gain was 49.33 mL, called "small, clinically insignificant" [[Janjua2020]].
- The Daliresp indication: "to reduce the risk of COPD exacerbations in patients with severe COPD associated with chronic bronchitis and a history of exacerbations". The label states "DALIRESP 250 mcg is a starting dose, for the first 4 weeks of treatment only and is not the effective (therapeutic) dose" [[FDA-Daliresp]].
- Daliresp label adverse reactions (8 controlled trials, n=4438 vs 4192): diarrhoea 9.5% versus 2.7%, weight decreased 7.5% versus 2.1%, nausea 4.7% versus 1.4%, insomnia 2.4% versus 1.0% [[FDA-Daliresp]].
- The Daliresp label warns of psychiatric events including suicidality: 5.9% with roflumilast versus 3.3% with placebo reported psychiatric adverse reactions [[FDA-Daliresp]].

### escalation · Long-term azithromycin
- Albert 2011 randomised 1142 people with COPD and increased exacerbation risk to azithromycin 250 mg daily or placebo for 1 year. People with hearing impairment, resting tachycardia or apparent risk of QTc prolongation were excluded [[Albert2011]].
- Albert 2011 primary end point: median time to first exacerbation was 266 days (95% CI 227 to 313) with azithromycin versus 174 days (95% CI 143 to 215) with placebo (P<0.001) [[Albert2011,NCT00325897]].
- Exacerbations were 1.48 versus 1.83 per patient-year (P=0.01). The hazard ratio for an exacerbation per patient-year was 0.73 (95% CI 0.63 to 0.84) [[Albert2011]].
- Hearing decrements were more common with azithromycin: 25% versus 20% (P=0.04). The authors noted the effect on microbial resistance patterns "is not known" [[Albert2011]].
- In a secondary analysis, azithromycin did not reduce exacerbations in current smokers (HR 0.99; 95% CI 0.71 to 1.38). Older age and milder GOLD stage were linked with better response [[Han2014]].
- Cochrane (14 studies, 3932 participants): prophylactic antibiotics reduced the number of people with one or more exacerbations (OR 0.57; 95% CI 0.42 to 0.78), from 61% to 47%. NNTB was 8 (95% CI 5 to 17) [[Herath2018]].
- Azithromycin has no US label indication for COPD exacerbation prevention. Its use here comes from trials and guidelines (no COPD indication was found in openFDA label searches; see Provenance).

### escalation · Biologics for eosinophilic COPD: dupilumab
- The Dupixent label describes dupilumab as a human IgG4 antibody that binds IL-4Rα, the subunit shared by the IL-4 and IL-13 receptor complexes, inhibiting IL-4 and IL-13 signalling [[FDA-Dupixent]].
- BOREAS randomised 939 patients with COPD, blood eosinophils ≥300/µL and high exacerbation risk despite triple therapy to dupilumab 300 mg or placebo every 2 weeks [[Bhatt2023]].
- BOREAS primary end point: annual moderate-or-severe exacerbations were 0.78 (95% CI 0.64 to 0.93) with dupilumab versus 1.10 (95% CI 0.93 to 1.30) with placebo (rate ratio 0.70; 95% CI 0.58 to 0.86; P<0.001) [[Bhatt2023,NCT03930732]].
- In BOREAS, pre-bronchodilator FEV1 rose by 160 mL versus 77 mL at week 12 (difference 83 mL; 95% CI 42 to 125), sustained to week 52. SGRQ improved by 3.4 points more than placebo (95% CI −5.5 to −1.3) [[Bhatt2023]].
- NOTUS (935 patients, same design) confirmed the result: 0.86 versus 1.30 exacerbations per year (rate ratio 0.66; 95% CI 0.54 to 0.82; P<0.001) [[Bhatt2024,NCT04456673]].
- In NOTUS, the FEV1 difference was 82 mL at week 12 and 62 mL at week 52 (P=0.02). Unlike BOREAS, SGRQ did not differ significantly at 52 weeks [[Bhatt2024]].
- The FDA approved Dupixent "as an add-on maintenance treatment in adult patients with inadequately controlled chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and an eosinophilic phenotype" on 2024-09-27 (BLA 761055, supplement S-064) [[FDA-Dupixent]].
- The Dupixent label COPD dose: "Recommended dosage for adult patients is 300 mg given every 2 weeks (Q2W)" [[FDA-Dupixent]].

### escalation · Biologics for eosinophilic COPD: mepolizumab
- The Nucala label describes mepolizumab as an IL-5 antagonist that blocks IL-5 from binding the IL-5 receptor α chain on eosinophils [[FDA-Nucala]].
- MATINEE randomised 804 patients (ClinicalTrials.gov lists 806 enrolled) with COPD, a history of exacerbations and blood eosinophils ≥300/µL on triple therapy to mepolizumab 100 mg or placebo every 4 weeks for 52 to 104 weeks [[Sciurba2025,NCT04133909]].
- MATINEE primary end point: 0.80 versus 1.01 moderate-or-severe exacerbations per year (rate ratio 0.79; 95% CI 0.66 to 0.94; P=0.01) [[Sciurba2025]].
- In MATINEE, median time to first moderate or severe exacerbation was 419 versus 321 days (HR 0.77; 95% CI 0.64 to 0.93). Quality-of-life and symptom differences were not significant [[Sciurba2025]].
- The MATINEE paper says eosinophilic inflammation is present in 20 to 40% of patients with COPD [[Sciurba2025]].
- The FDA approved Nucala for "add-on maintenance treatment of adult patients with inadequately controlled chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and an eosinophilic phenotype" on 2025-05-22 (BLA 125526, S-007). The approval letter calls this the complete response to an FDA action letter of 7 September 2018 on the same supplement [[FDA-Nucala]].
- The Nucala label COPD dose: "100 mg administered subcutaneously once every 4 weeks" [[FDA-Nucala]].
- Synthesis (Established for the trial populations): two biologic classes have reduced moderate-or-severe exacerbation rates by about 20–34% in people already on triple therapy with eosinophils ≥300/µL. No head-to-head trial exists [[Bhatt2023,Bhatt2024,Sciurba2025]].

### escalation · Ensifentrine (inhaled PDE3/PDE4 inhibitor)
- The Ohtuvayre label describes ensifentrine as an inhibitor of PDE3 and PDE4. PDE3 hydrolyses cAMP and cGMP, PDE4 only cAMP. Inhibiting both raises intracellular cAMP and/or cGMP [[FDA-Ohtuvayre]].
- ENHANCE-1 (760 patients) and ENHANCE-2 (789) tested nebulised ensifentrine versus placebo in people aged 40–80 with moderate-to-severe symptomatic COPD. 69% and 55% were also taking a LAMA or LABA [[Anzueto2023]].
- ENHANCE primary end point: average FEV1 AUC over 0–12 h improved by 87 mL (95% CI 55 to 119) in ENHANCE-1 and 94 mL (65 to 124) in ENHANCE-2, both P<0.001 [[Anzueto2023,NCT04535986,NCT04542057]].
- Over 24 weeks, ensifentrine reduced the rate of moderate-or-severe exacerbations: rate ratio 0.64 (0.40 to 1.00; P=0.050) in ENHANCE-1 and 0.57 (0.38 to 0.87; P=0.009) in ENHANCE-2. Symptoms and quality of life improved in ENHANCE-1 but not ENHANCE-2 [[Anzueto2023]].
- The FDA approved Ohtuvayre (NDA 217389) on 2024-06-26 as a Type 1 new molecular entity, "for the maintenance treatment of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) in adult patients" [[FDA-Ohtuvayre]].
- The Ohtuvayre label dose is "3 mg (one unit-dose ampule) twice daily ... by oral inhalation using a standard jet nebulizer with a mouthpiece" [[FDA-Ohtuvayre]].
- Ohtuvayre label adverse reactions in the pooled 24-week population (n=975 vs 574): back pain 1.8% versus 1.0%, hypertension 1.7% versus 0.9%, urinary tract infection 1.3% versus 1.0%, diarrhoea 1.0% versus 0.7%. The label also warns of paradoxical bronchospasm and psychiatric events [[FDA-Ohtuvayre]].

### technique · Device types
- Dry powder inhalers have an internal resistance. They depend on the patient's own inspiratory flow to break the powder into particles smaller than 5 µm that can reach the lower airways. This de-aggregation happens inside the device and is helped by fast acceleration at the start of the breath [[Mahler2017]].
- Peak inspiratory flow depends on effort and respiratory-muscle strength. Both can be reduced in COPD by hyperinflation, hypoxaemia and muscle wasting [[Mahler2017]].
- Soft mist inhalers (Respimat) need priming. The Spiriva Respimat label says to actuate "toward the ground until an aerosol cloud is visible and then repeat the process three more times" before first use [[FDA-SpirivaRespimat]].
- pMDIs need priming too. The Breztri Aerosphere label says to "Prime ... before first time use and re-prime if not used for more than 7 days" [[FDA-Breztri]].
- The only ensifentrine formulation is a nebuliser solution, for "a standard jet nebulizer equipped with a mouthpiece, connected to an air compressor". The label says it should not be mixed with other drugs [[FDA-Ohtuvayre]].
- GOLD 2023: device choice depends on "availability, patient preferences, and ability to perform the correct inhalation maneuver". Regular assessment at follow-up "is necessary to maintain their effective use" [[Agusti2023]].

### technique · Peak inspiratory flow (PIF)
- A handheld inspiratory flow meter with an adjustable dial can mimic a DPI's resistance. It checks whether a patient reaches an optimal PIF of at least 60 L/min [[Mahler2017]].
- Observational studies cited by Mahler found suboptimal PIF (<60 L/min) in 19–78% of stable outpatients with COPD and 32–47% of inpatients before discharge after an exacerbation [[Mahler2017]].
- Mahler suggests that if PIF is below 60 L/min, "a different delivery system, such as a metered-dose or soft mist inhaler or nebulized therapy, should be considered" (author's review, not a guideline) [[Mahler2017]].
- In 123 patients hospitalised for an exacerbation, 52% had suboptimal PIF. They had higher 90-day COPD readmission rates (28.1% vs 13.6%; P=0.048) and fewer median days to COPD readmission (63.5 vs 144) [[Loh2017]].
- In that cohort, PIF was the only variable that predicted days to COPD readmission in a multivariable model (P=0.041) (single-centre observational; Early evidence) [[Loh2017]].

### technique · How often technique goes wrong
- A systematic review of 144 articles (54,354 subjects, 59,584 observed technique tests) found correct technique in only 31% (95% CI 28% to 35%) [[Sanchis2016]].
- In that review, the commonest pMDI errors were coordination (45%), speed and/or depth of inspiration (44%) and no breath-hold after inhaling (46%) [[Sanchis2016]].
- The commonest DPI errors were incorrect preparation (29%), no full breath out before inhaling (46%) and no breath-hold after inhaling (37%) [[Sanchis2016]].
- Technique did not improve between the first and second 20-year periods studied (about 40 years in all) [[Sanchis2016]].
- A review of DPIs found 4% to 94% of patients do not use their inhalers correctly, depending on device and assessment method. As many as 25% had never received verbal technique instruction [[Lavorini2008]].
- In 1664 experienced chest-clinic patients (52% COPD), critical errors ranged from 12% with MDIs to 35% with Diskus and HandiHaler and 44% with Turbuhaler [[Melani2011]].
- In that study, misuse was associated with more hospitalisation (P=0.001), emergency visits, oral steroid courses and antibiotic courses (all P<0.001) [[Melani2011]].
- The study's only modifiable factor linked to less misuse was instruction from health caregivers (P<0.001). Older age and less schooling were also associated with misuse [[Melani2011]].
- A systematic review of critical errors found 299 different descriptions of "critical error". It found an association between errors and exacerbations, plus a greater health-economic burden [[Usmani2018]].

### technique · Carbon footprint of inhalers
- HFC propellants used in pMDIs since the CFC phase-out do not deplete ozone but "are potent greenhouse gases" [[Janson2020]].
- The annual carbon footprint per patient was 17 kg CO2e for a DPI regimen (Relvar Ellipta plus Ventolin Accuhaler) versus 439 kg CO2e for a pMDI regimen (Seretide Evohaler plus Ventolin Evohaler) [[Janson2020]].
- In 2017, 70% of inhalers sold in England were MDIs versus 13% in Sweden. Applying Sweden's mix to England would cut 550 kt CO2e a year [[Janson2020]].
- In NHS England data, some salbutamol pMDIs (e.g. Ventolin) exceed 25 kg CO2e per inhaler, while lower-volume HFA-134a ones (e.g. Salamol) are below 10 kg. Each 10% of MDIs switched to DPIs would save 58 kt CO2e a year [[Wilkinson2019]].
- Whether that switch saves or costs money depends on brand choice: switching to the cheapest equivalent DPIs saved £8.2M per 10% switched, whereas keeping 2017 DPI brand patterns cost £12.7M more [[Wilkinson2019]].
- Low-carbon propellant: per salbutamol inhaler, the total footprint was 26.91 kg CO2e (HFA-134a pMDI), 2.06 kg (HFA-152a pMDI) and 0.69 kg (DPI). That is a 92% reduction for HFA-152a [[King2026]].
- In pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic studies, salbutamol with HFA-152a was bioequivalent to salbutamol with HFA-134a, with a similar safety profile (Early evidence: healthy-volunteer and small PD studies) [[Moreira2026]].
- Janson et al. conclude the lower carbon footprint of DPIs "should be considered alongside other factors when choosing inhalation devices". Patient ability, such as PIF, is one of those factors [[Janson2020,Mahler2017]].

### inhalers · Step sequence
1. Kicker: Rescue. Title: A short-acting puffer opens the airways for a few hours. Regular SABA in stable COPD raised FEV1 by about 0.14 L and eased breathlessness versus placebo [[Sestini2002]].
2. Kicker: One long-acting. Title: A LAMA blocks M3 receptors for 24 hours. Tiotropium blocks smooth-muscle M3 receptors, and its protective effect lasted over 24 hours in preclinical studies, which supports once-daily dosing [[FDA-SpirivaHandiHaler]].
3. Kicker: LAMA vs LABA. Title: Tiotropium beat salmeterol on flare-ups. In POET-COPD, tiotropium delayed the first exacerbation from 145 to 187 days (HR 0.83) [[Vogelmeier2011]].
4. Kicker: Two long-acting. Title: LABA + LAMA together. ATS 2020 strongly recommends LABA/LAMA over either alone for breathless patients, and GOLD 2023 makes it initial therapy for groups B and E [[Nici2020,Agusti2023]].
5. Kicker: What it does not do. Title: Bronchodilators do not slow decline. In UPLIFT, tiotropium lifted FEV1 by 87–103 mL for 4 years but did not change the rate of FEV1 decline [[Tashkin2008]].

### escalation · Step sequence
1. Kicker: Check the count. Title: Eosinophils predict who gains from ICS. The ICS effect on exacerbations grew from 24% to 42% as eosinophils rose from 2–4% to ≥6% [[Pascoe2015]].
2. Kicker: Add the steroid. Title: Triple therapy for exacerbators. In IMPACT, triple therapy cut moderate-or-severe exacerbations to 0.91 per year versus 1.21 with LAMA/LABA (rate ratio 0.75) [[Lipson2018]].
3. Kicker: Weigh the cost. Title: More pneumonia with ICS. Clinician-diagnosed pneumonia was higher with triple therapy than with LAMA/LABA in IMPACT (HR 1.53) [[Lipson2018]].
4. Kicker: Still flaring. Title: Roflumilast or azithromycin. GOLD 2023 suggests roflumilast for chronic bronchitis with FEV1 <50%, or a macrolide in people who do not smoke, when exacerbations continue on triple therapy [[Agusti2023]].
5. Kicker: Type 2 COPD. Title: Biologics for eosinophils ≥300. Dupilumab (rate ratio 0.70 and 0.66) and mepolizumab (0.79) cut exacerbations when added to triple therapy [[Bhatt2023,Bhatt2024,Sciurba2025]].
6. Kicker: Step down. Title: Withdrawing ICS when it is not needed. ERS conditionally recommends ICS withdrawal without frequent exacerbations, but strongly advises against it when eosinophils are ≥300/µL [[Chalmers2020]].

### technique · Step sequence
1. Kicker: Pick the device. Title: Match the device to the breath. Device choice depends on availability, preference and the ability to perform the inhalation manoeuvre, according to GOLD 2023 [[Agusti2023]].
2. Kicker: Breathe out first. Title: Empty the lungs away from the device. Not breathing out fully before inhaling was a mistake in 46% of DPI technique checks [[Sanchis2016]].
3. Kicker: The breath in. Title: The right breath for the device. Coordination (45%) and speed or depth of inspiration (44%) were the commonest pMDI errors, while a DPI needs about ≥60 L/min peak flow [[Sanchis2016,Mahler2017]].
4. Kicker: Hold it. Title: Breath-hold after inhaling. No breath-hold after inhaling was missed in 46% of pMDI and 37% of DPI checks [[Sanchis2016]].
5. Kicker: Check again. Title: Teach and re-check at every visit. Instruction from health caregivers was the only modifiable factor linked to less inhaler misuse [[Melani2011]].

### Exam hooks
- UPLIFT: tiotropium improved FEV1, quality of life and exacerbations over 4 years but did not slow FEV1 decline [[Tashkin2008]].
- POET-COPD: LAMA (tiotropium) beat LABA (salmeterol) for time to first exacerbation, HR 0.83 [[Vogelmeier2011]].
- FLAME: LABA+LAMA beat LABA+ICS on exacerbations (rate ratio 0.89), with less pneumonia (3.2% vs 4.8%) [[Wedzicha2016]].
- TORCH: salmeterol/fluticasone missed significance for mortality (HR 0.825; P=0.052), and pneumonia was more frequent with fluticasone [[Calverley2007]].
- IMPACT and ETHOS: triple therapy reduced moderate-or-severe exacerbations versus dual therapy. Mortality reductions versus LAMA/LABA were secondary findings [[Lipson2018,Rabe2020,Lipson2020,Martinez2021]].
- GOLD 2023 eosinophil cut-offs: <100 cells/µL means little or no ICS benefit; ≥300 cells/µL means a strong likelihood of benefit [[Agusti2023]].
- GOLD 2023 no longer encourages LABA+ICS in COPD. If an ICS is indicated, triple therapy is preferred [[Agusti2023]].
- WISDOM: stepwise ICS withdrawal on LAMA+LABA was noninferior for first exacerbation (HR 1.06), but FEV1 fell by about 40 mL [[Magnussen2014]].
- Roflumilast is not a bronchodilator. It is labelled for severe COPD with chronic bronchitis and prior exacerbations; watch for weight loss and psychiatric effects [[FDA-Daliresp]].
- Azithromycin 250 mg daily lengthened time to first exacerbation (266 vs 174 days) but caused hearing decrements (25% vs 20%), and showed no benefit in current smokers [[Albert2011,Han2014]].
- Dupilumab (IL-4Rα) and mepolizumab (IL-5) are FDA-approved add-ons for COPD with an eosinophilic phenotype (approved 2024 and 2025) [[FDA-Dupixent,FDA-Nucala]].
- Ensifentrine is a nebulised PDE3/PDE4 inhibitor, FDA-approved 2024-06-26 [[FDA-Ohtuvayre]].
- DPIs need adequate inspiratory flow, about ≥60 L/min. Suboptimal PIF is common after exacerbations [[Mahler2017,Loh2017]].
- Only about 31% of patients use their inhaler correctly, and this has not improved in 40 years [[Sanchis2016]].

### In plain words
- COPD medicines mostly come as inhalers, so the medicine goes straight to the airways [[Agusti2023]].
- A "reliever" inhaler works within minutes and lasts a few hours. "Maintenance" inhalers are taken every day, even on good days [[Sestini2002,FDA-Trelegy]].
- Long-acting inhalers relax the muscles around the airways for 12 to 24 hours. Two different kinds together usually help breathing more than one alone [[Nici2020,FDA-SpirivaHandiHaler]].
- Steroid inhalers mainly help people who have flare-ups and a higher number of a white blood cell called the eosinophil. They slightly raise the chance of pneumonia [[Agusti2023,Kew2014]].
- If flare-ups keep happening, doctors may add a tablet (roflumilast), a daily antibiotic (azithromycin) or, for some people, an injection (dupilumab or mepolizumab) [[Agusti2023,FDA-Dupixent,FDA-Nucala]].
- Most people make at least one mistake with their inhaler. Asking a nurse, pharmacist or doctor to watch you use it is one of the most useful checks [[Sanchis2016,Melani2011]].
- Powder inhalers need a strong, fast breath in. Spray inhalers need a slow, steady breath timed with the press. Some people use a spray inhaler with a spacer [[Mahler2017,Sanchis2016,Agusti2023]].
- None of these medicines cures COPD or stops the lungs from slowly changing. They ease breathing and lower the chance of flare-ups [[Tashkin2008]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Does triple therapy lower mortality? Lipson 2020 and Martinez 2021 report lower all-cause death with triple versus LAMA/LABA (HR 0.72 and 0.51) [[Lipson2020,Martinez2021]]. Both were secondary end points with low event rates. ETHOS showed it only at the higher budesonide dose, and neither trial showed significance versus ICS/LABA [[Martinez2021]]. A published exchange questioned whether the IMPACT result was "Confounded by Asthma?" (title of the authors' reply; the critique letter itself was not read) [[Lipson2020Reply]]. Status: debated; GOLD 2023 includes both trials in its mortality-evidence figure [[Agusti2023]].
- LABA+LAMA or LABA+ICS for exacerbators? FLAME found LABA+LAMA superior [[Wedzicha2016]], but the 2023 Cochrane review found similar exacerbation odds overall (OR 0.91; 95% CI 0.78 to 1.06) [[Fukuda2023]]. GOLD 2023 sidesteps the comparison by recommending triple, not LABA+ICS, when an ICS is indicated [[Agusti2023]].
- Eosinophil thresholds. GOLD uses 100 and 300 cells/µL [[Agusti2023]]. Pascoe used percentages (≥2%) [[Pascoe2015]], and Bafadhel found benefit from 100 cells/µL upward [[Bafadhel2018]]. FLAME found no eosinophil interaction for LABA+LAMA versus LABA+ICS [[Wedzicha2016]]. Status: thresholds are pragmatic cut-points on a continuum.
- Roflumilast's REACT result sits on the significance line: P=0.0529 in the primary analysis, P=0.0424 in the sensitivity analysis [[Martinez2015]]. Tolerability is poor, with more withdrawals [[Martinez2015,FDA-Daliresp]].
- Long-term azithromycin: benefit in non-smokers [[Han2014]] against hearing loss, cardiac exclusion criteria and unknown resistance effects [[Albert2011]].
- Biologic selection. BOREAS and NOTUS (dupilumab) [[Bhatt2023,Bhatt2024]] and MATINEE (mepolizumab) [[Sciurba2025]] used similar eosinophil ≥300 criteria. No head-to-head trial exists, and quality-of-life results were inconsistent: SGRQ improved in BOREAS but not in NOTUS or MATINEE [[Bhatt2023,Bhatt2024,Sciurba2025]].
- Device choice and climate: DPIs have far lower carbon footprints [[Janson2020,Wilkinson2019]], but up to 78% of stable outpatients may not generate the 60 L/min some DPIs need [[Mahler2017]]. Low-GWP propellants (HFA-152a) may narrow the gap [[King2026,Moreira2026]]. Status: Early evidence on clinical equivalence.
- The Respimat safety signal from Cochrane subgroup data [[Karner2014]] was not confirmed in TIOSPIR [[Wise2013]].

Two records (Moreira2026, Lipson2020Reply) were validated in Europe PMC only. Abstracts for every trial and review were read from the Europe PMC core records (MEDLINE text). Numbers were copied as printed.
- GOLD 2023 wording was quoted from the Europe PMC full text of PMC10111975. GOLD 2024–2026 full reports (goldcopd.org) were not used because they are not indexed by the required services. Statements tagged GOLD here are therefore GOLD 2023.
- Labels: 11 US labels were read from openFDA drug/label. The Nucala, Ohtuvayre and Breztri SPL records have empty openfda brand fields, so they were found by product-data and text search, and the brand was confirmed in the label text. Approval dates come from openFDA drugsfda submission records (ORIG and EFFICACY supplements).
- FDA approval letters for Dupixent S-064, Nucala S-007 and Breztri S-009 were downloaded from accessdata.fda.gov (linked from the openFDA application_docs) and text-extracted to confirm what each supplement approved. The Nucala letter states it answers an FDA action letter of 7 September 2018. The content of the second Breztri NDA (220290, approved 2026-10-08) was not read.
- Breztri dose units: ETHOS reports glycopyrrolate 18 µg and formoterol 9.6 µg per dose, while the US label states 160/9/4.8 mcg per actuation (two actuations). The difference is a labelling convention (per actuation versus per dose and delivered versus metered). It was not reconciled further, and both are quoted as published.
- Azithromycin: openFDA label searches found no COPD indication. The statement that it has no US COPD indication rests on that search, not on a regulator statement.
- ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 confirmed status, enrolment and primary-outcome titles for 8 trials. MATINEE's registry enrolment (806) differs from the 804 randomised in the NEJM abstract, and both are reported.
- No facts are marked (unverified). Canonical keys used as given: Agusti2023, Lipson2018, Rabe2020, Bhatt2023, Bhatt2024, Sciurba2025, Anzueto2023. The other companions' src/sources.json files were grepped for every PMID here, with no overlaps found.
- Not used: NICE NG115 (not indexed by required services); spacer-specific deposition studies (none validated in time, so spacer use is quoted only from GOLD 2023's exacerbation section); SAMA-specific (ipratropium) trials.
