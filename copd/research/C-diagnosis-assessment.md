# C · Diagnosis & assessment

Pass date: 2026-11-07. Modules served: spirometry, assessment, lookalikes.

This pass covers how COPD is confirmed and graded: the post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio (fixed 0.70 versus the lower limit of normal), how spirometry is done and quality-graded (ATS/ERS 2019), reference equations (GLI 2012, race-neutral GLI Global), z-score severity, bronchodilator responsiveness, the GOLD 1–4 grades, the mMRC and CAT (now also called CAAT) symptom scores, the ABE groups from GOLD 2023, blood eosinophil thresholds, multidimensional scores (BODE, 6-minute walk, IC/TLC), case-finding versus screening, under- and overdiagnosis, chest CT, and the look-alikes: asthma and asthma–COPD overlap, bronchiectasis, heart failure, PRISm and pre-COPD. Treatment choices that follow from the eosinophil count and ABE group belong to pass D; exacerbation definitions (Rome) belong to pass E; alpha-1 antitrypsin testing belongs to pass B.

## Findings

### spirometry · The diagnostic rule
- GOLD 2023 defines COPD as "a heterogeneous lung condition" with chronic respiratory symptoms (dyspnoea, cough, expectoration and/or exacerbations) caused by abnormalities of the airways (bronchitis, bronchiolitis) and/or alveoli (emphysema), which cause persistent, often progressive, airflow obstruction [[Agusti2023]].
- GOLD 2023 says a COPD diagnosis should be considered in anyone with dyspnoea, chronic cough or sputum, recurrent lower respiratory tract infections and/or exposure to risk factors. A post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio < 0.7 on forced spirometry "is needed to establish the diagnosis" [[Agusti2023]].
- GOLD 2023 points out that airflow obstruction that is not fully reversible is not specific to COPD: asthma and other diseases can show it too, so the clinical context and risk factors must also be weighed [[Agusti2023]].
- If a single post-bronchodilator ratio falls between 0.60 and 0.80, GOLD 2023 advises repeating spirometry on a separate occasion, because biological variation can move the ratio. An initial ratio < 0.6 is "very unlikely" to rise spontaneously above 0.7 [[Agusti2023]].
- GOLD 2023 no longer recommends using the degree of reversibility to guide treatment. Reversibility varies over time in the same patient, does not separate COPD from asthma, and does not predict long-term response to bronchodilators or corticosteroids. The one exception: if airflow limitation disappears after bronchodilator, that is incompatible with COPD [[Agusti2023]].
- Because reversibility is not used for treatment decisions, GOLD 2023 says it is neither necessary nor advised to stop inhaled medicines before follow-up spirometry [[Agusti2023]].

### spirometry · Fixed ratio 0.70 versus the lower limit of normal (LLN)
- GOLD prefers the fixed ratio < 0.7 because it is simple, needs no reference values, and was used in all the clinical trials behind its treatment recommendations. GOLD 2023 also acknowledges that the fixed ratio may underdiagnose young adults and overdiagnose the elderly, especially in mild disease, compared with the LLN [[Agusti2023]].
- The LLN is set at the bottom 5% of a healthy reference population. It therefore depends on the reference equation chosen and on race/ethnicity, and GOLD notes there are no longitudinal studies validating LLN use [[Agusti2023,Bhakta2023]].
- In 40,646 adults from US, English and Dutch surveys, the LLN of FEV1/FVC fell steadily with age. The median age at which the LLN dropped below 0.70 in healthy people was 42 years for men and 48 years for women [[Swanney2008]].
- In healthy never-smokers over 60, the GOLD fixed ratio labelled 17–45% of men and 7–26% of women as obstructed (range across reference equations). Applied to whole populations, it gave false-positive rates of up to 60% [[Swanney2008]].
- In Bergen, Norway, about 35% of 71 healthy, asymptomatic never-smokers aged > 70 had FEV1/FVC < 70%. Above age 80, the figure was about 50% [[Hardie2002]].
- In the NHLBI Pooled Cohorts (24,207 adults followed for a median 15 years; 3,925 COPD-related hospitalisations or deaths), the best-discriminating fixed threshold was 0.71 (C statistic 0.696). This was no different from 0.70 and more accurate than the GLI LLN (C-statistic difference 0.034) [[Bhatt2019]].
- The 2021 pre-COPD perspective from GOLD Science Committee members cites the pooled-cohort data as showing that the fixed 0.70 ratio predicts COPD hospitalisation and mortality as well as, or better than, the LLN [[Han2021,Bhatt2019]].

### spirometry · How the test is done (ATS/ERS 2019 standard)
- An FVC manoeuvre has four phases: maximal inspiration, a "blast" of expiration, continued complete expiration for up to 15 seconds, and a maximal inspiration back to full lungs [[Graham2019]].
- Time zero is found by back-extrapolation. To show a maximal start, the back-extrapolated volume must be < 5% of FVC or 0.100 L, whichever is greater [[Graham2019]].
- End of forced expiration requires one of three signs: a plateau (≤ 0.025 L change in the last 1 second), an expiratory time of ≥ 15 seconds, or an FVC within the repeatability tolerance of (or greater than) the largest previous FVC [[Graham2019]].
- The aim is at least three acceptable FEV1 and three acceptable FVC measurements, before and after bronchodilator. For age > 6 years, the two largest FVC values and the two largest FEV1 values must each agree within 0.150 L [[Graham2019]].
- The largest FEV1 and the largest FVC from all acceptable manoeuvres are reported, and their ratio is used as FEV1/FVC even if they come from different blows. The ratio is reported to two decimal places [[Graham2019]].
- Eight manoeuvres is "generally a practical upper limit" for adults. Testing should stop if FEV1 falls below 80% of its starting value during repeated attempts [[Graham2019]].
- Quality grades (graded separately for FEV1 and FVC, age > 6): A = ≥ 3 acceptable within 0.150 L; B = 2 acceptable within 0.150 L; C = ≥ 2 acceptable within 0.200 L; D = ≥ 2 acceptable within 0.250 L; E = ≥ 2 acceptable but > 0.250 L apart, or only 1 acceptable; U = 0 acceptable but ≥ 1 usable; F = none acceptable or usable [[Graham2019]].
- Suggested bronchodilator withholding times before diagnostic testing: SABA 4–6 h, SAMA 12 h, LABA 24 h, ultra-LABA (indacaterol, vilanterol, olodaterol) 36 h, LAMA 36–48 h. Inhaled corticosteroids and leukotriene modifiers need not be withheld [[Graham2019]].
- All first diagnostic spirometry should be done before and after bronchodilator, because normal baseline spirometry does not rule out a bronchodilator response. The 2019 standard leaves the bronchodilator drug, dose and route to each facility's protocol [[Graham2019]].
- In a patient survey for the 2019 standard (52 countries), 31% rated being told to keep blowing when nothing more seemed to come out as a moderate or serious issue [[Graham2019]].

### spirometry · Reference equations, z-scores and race
- The GLI 2012 equations were built from 97,759 records of healthy non-smokers aged 2.5–95 years from 72 centres in 33 countries. They give continuous, age-dependent LLNs for ages 3–95. Ethnic groups differed in FEV1 and FVC but proportionally, so FEV1/FVC was "virtually independent" of ethnic group [[Quanjer2012]].
- NHANES III reference values came from 7,429 asymptomatic lifelong non-smokers aged 8–80 years (Caucasian, African-American and Mexican-American) [[Hankinson1999]].
- The race-neutral GLI Global (2022) equations reanalysed 74,185 GLI records with inverse-probability weights. They fit about as well as the GLI 2012 "other" equations, with wider limits of normal [[Bowerman2023]].
- A 2023 official ATS statement recommends replacing race- and ethnicity-specific equations with race-neutral average equations. It pairs this with a call to re-evaluate how lung-function tests are used in clinical, employment and insurance decisions, and with a caution about uncertain effects [[Bhakta2023]].
- ATS worked example: a 40-year-old man who is 6 feet tall has a predicted FEV1 of 4.50 L (5th percentile 3.56 L) by GLI White, and 3.84 L (5th percentile 2.95 L) by GLI African American. A measured FEV1 of 3.50 L is 91% predicted and above the LLN on one equation, but 78% predicted and below the LLN on the other [[Bhakta2023]].
- In 8,431 people tested at one US centre, switching to GLI Global did not significantly change the prevalence of obstruction (Black individuals 19.9% vs 19.5%; White 23.9% vs 25.1%). Restriction in Black individuals rose from 26.8% to 37.5%, and severity increased in 22.8% of Black individuals [[Moffett2023]].
- ERS/ATS severity bands by FEV1 z-score, as applied by Moffett and colleagues: > −1.645 normal; −1.645 to −2.5 mild; −2.5 to −4.0 moderate; < −4.0 severe [[Moffett2023,Stanojevic2022]].
- The 2021–2022 ERS/ATS interpretation standard redefined a positive bronchodilator response as a rise in FEV1 or FVC of > 10% of the predicted value. This replaced the 2005 criterion of ≥ 12% and > 200 mL from baseline [[Zhao2025,Stanojevic2022]].
- The 2019 spirometry standard notes that expressing the bronchodilator change as a percentage of predicted FEV1, or as a z-score, avoids sex and height bias [[Graham2019]].

### spirometry · Bronchodilator responsiveness in COPD
- In 5,756 UPLIFT patients (baseline FEV1 1.10 L, 39.3% predicted; FVC 2.63 L), 80 µg ipratropium followed by 400 µg salbutamol raised FEV1 by a mean 229 mL and FVC by 407 mL [[Tashkin2008]].
- In UPLIFT, the proportion classed as "responsive" depended on the criterion: 53.9% had ≥ 12% and ≥ 200 mL, 65.6% had ≥ 15%, and 38.6% had ≥ 10% absolute rise in FEV1 % predicted [[Tashkin2008]].
- In ECLIPSE, 400 µg salbutamol raised FEV1 by a mean 0.12 L in COPD, 0.14 L in smoking controls and 0.08 L in non-smoking controls. Reversibility status changed between four tests over a year [[Albert2012]].
- In ECLIPSE, the absolute FEV1 response fell with severity (GOLD II 0.16 L, III 0.10 L, IV 0.05 L). Consistently reversible patients (n = 227) did not differ in mortality, hospitalisation or exacerbations from irreversible patients once baseline FEV1 was allowed for [[Albert2012]].

### spirometry · GOLD grades of airflow obstruction
- FEV1 sets the severity of airflow obstruction as GOLD grade 1, 2, 3 or 4 (mild, moderate, severe, very severe) once the post-bronchodilator ratio is < 0.7 [[Agusti2023]].
- GOLD 2023 cut-offs as restated in a 2025 registry analysis: GOLD 1 mild, FEV1 ≥ 80% predicted; GOLD 2 moderate, 50% ≤ FEV1 < 80%; GOLD 3 severe, 30% ≤ FEV1 < 50%; GOLD 4 very severe, FEV1 < 30% predicted [[Lin2025,Agusti2023]].
- In a European primary-care sample of 1,817 patients, mean CAT scores by GOLD stage were I 16.2 ± 8.8, II 16.3 ± 7.9, III 19.3 ± 8.2 and IV 22.3 ± 8.7. Stages I and II did not differ (p = 0.88), which shows how loosely FEV1 tracks symptoms [[Jones2011]].
- In 227 Japanese patients followed for 5 years, ATS FEV1-based staging did not significantly predict survival (p = 0.08), but dyspnoea level did (p < 0.001) [[Nishimura2002]].
- In 100 rehabilitation patients, FEV1 was not associated with MRC dyspnoea grade, while shuttle-walk distance, SGRQ, CRQ, mood and daily-living scores were [[Bestall1999]].

### assessment · Goals of the initial assessment
- After spirometry confirms COPD, GOLD 2023 lists four assessment goals: (1) severity of airflow limitation (GOLD grade); (2) nature and size of current symptoms; (3) previous moderate and severe exacerbations, "the best estimate of the risk of future exacerbations"; and (4) presence and type of multimorbidity [[Agusti2023]].
- GOLD 2017 first separated the spirometric grade from the ABCD groups, which from then on came only from symptoms and exacerbation history [[Vogelmeier2017]].

### assessment · The mMRC dyspnoea scale (published content, paraphrased)
- The mMRC is a five-point (0–4) self-rating of breathlessness during activity [[Lin2025,Sandberg2026]]. Grades as published (paraphrased; verbatim text in [[Xiong2026]] Table 1 and the GOLD mMRC figure):
  - Grade 0: breathless only with strenuous exercise [[Xiong2026,Sandberg2026]].
  - Grade 1: short of breath when hurrying on level ground or walking up a slight hill [[Xiong2026]].
  - Grade 2: walks slower than people of the same age on the level because of breathlessness, or has to stop for breath when walking at their own pace on the level [[Xiong2026]].
  - Grade 3: stops for breath after walking about 100 metres or after a few minutes on level ground [[Xiong2026]].
  - Grade 4: too breathless to leave the house, or breathless when dressing or undressing [[Xiong2026,Sandberg2026]].
- Scoring: the person picks the single grade that fits best. There are no sub-scores [[Xiong2026]]. GOLD uses mMRC 0–1 as "less symptomatic" and ≥ 2 as "more symptomatic" [[Han2013,Cheng2023]].
- The scale descends from the original MRC dyspnoea scale, which ran from grade 1 to 5 [[Bestall1999]]. The mMRC renumbers the same steps 0–4, so MRC grade 3 corresponds to mMRC grade 2 (the mapping is inferred from the two numbering schemes, not stated by these sources).
- In 100 COPD rehabilitation patients graded MRC 3 (n = 32), 4 (n = 34) and 5 (n = 34), MRC grade was significantly associated with shuttle distance, SGRQ, CRQ, mood and extended activities of daily living [[Bestall1999]].
- In 153 patients with various respiratory diseases, the MRC scale, baseline dyspnoea index and oxygen-cost diagram correlated with one another (r = 0.48 to 0.70) and showed satisfactory agreement between observers [[Mahler1988]].

### assessment · The COPD Assessment Test (CAT / CAAT) (published content, paraphrased)
- The CAT has eight items. They were chosen by psychometric and Rasch analysis from 21 candidate items in three studies (Europe and the USA, n = 1,503). Cronbach's α was 0.88, test–retest ICC 0.8, and correlation with the COPD-specific SGRQ r = 0.80 [[Jones2009]].
- Each item is a 6-point scale scored 0 to 5 (0 = no impairment) between two opposite statements. The total is the sum of the eight items, 0–40, and higher scores mean worse health status [[Minov2015,Jones2011]].
- The eight items (domain: what the 0 end and the 5 end describe; paraphrased, verbatim in [[Minov2015]]):
  - 1 Cough: never coughs → coughs all the time [[Minov2015]].
  - 2 Phlegm: no phlegm (mucus) in the chest → chest completely full of phlegm [[Minov2015]].
  - 3 Chest tightness: chest not tight at all → chest very tight [[Minov2015]].
  - 4 Breathlessness: not breathless walking up a hill or one flight of stairs → very breathless doing so [[Minov2015]].
  - 5 Home activities: not limited in activities at home → very limited [[Minov2015]].
  - 6 Confidence: confident leaving home despite the lung condition → not at all confident leaving home because of it [[Minov2015]].
  - 7 Sleep: sleeps soundly → does not sleep soundly because of the lung condition [[Minov2015]].
  - 8 Energy: lots of energy → no energy at all [[Minov2015]].
- Impact bands as reported with the CAT: < 10 low; 10–20 medium; > 20 high; > 30 very high. A score of 5 is the upper limit of normal in healthy non-smokers [[Minov2015]].
- GOLD uses CAT < 10 as "less symptomatic" and CAT ≥ 10 as "more symptomatic" [[Han2013,Cheng2023]].
- The minimum clinically important difference for the CAT is 2 points. ROC curves consistently gave −2. Mean change was −2.5 after 8 weeks of pulmonary rehabilitation (n = 565) and −3.0 from discharge to 3 months after an exacerbation admission (n = 147) [[Kon2014]].
- In the development study, scores differed by 5 units (12% of the 40-point range) between stable patients (n = 229) and those having an exacerbation (n = 67) [[Jones2009]]. In the European study, means were 17.2 ± 8.3 when stable and 21.3 ± 8.4 during an exacerbation [[Jones2011]].
- After pulmonary rehabilitation (n = 64), the CAT improved by a mean −2.2 ± 5.3 (p = .002), as responsive as longer health-status questionnaires [[Jones2012]].
- The Chronic Airways Assessment Test (CAAT) adapts the CAT by replacing references to "COPD" with "lung disease". The items and scoring stay identical, and it is used in COPD and asthma [[Choate2026]]. A 2026 UK cohort applied the GOLD 2026 ABE tool with the CAAT [[Adesoye2026]].

### assessment · GOLD ABE groups (GOLD 2023)
- GOLD 2023 replaced the ABCD tool with ABE. The symptom thresholds (x-axis) and exacerbation-history thresholds (y-axis) are unchanged, groups A and B are unchanged, and the old C and D are merged into a single group "E" (for Exacerbations). GOLD says the practical value of this change "needs to be validated" [[Agusti2023]].
- ABE rules as applied in a 2023 cohort analysis citing GOLD 2023:
  - Group A: fewer than two moderate exacerbations that did not lead to hospital admission, and mMRC < 2 or CAT < 10.
  - Group B: the same exacerbation history, with mMRC ≥ 2 or CAT ≥ 10.
  - Group E: two or more moderate exacerbations, or one or more leading to hospitalisation, whatever the symptom level [[Cheng2023,Agusti2023]].
- Exacerbation history counts exacerbations in the previous year [[Agusti2023]].
- The earlier ABCD tool (GOLD 2017) used the same axes. C was high exacerbation risk with mMRC < 2 or CAT < 10, and D was high risk with mMRC ≥ 2 or CAT ≥ 10 [[Cheng2023,Vogelmeier2017]].
- A GOLD grade and an ABE group can be combined into 12 subgroups (1A–4E), replacing the 16 ABCD subgroups (A1–4D) [[Cheng2023]].
- In 2,499 Chinese outpatients, 210 were group A, 1,119 B and 1,170 E. Group E had more first-year hospitalisations (adjusted OR 1.57 vs A) and higher mortality than A and B. Mortality prediction was slightly better for GOLD 2017 ABCD than for 2023 ABE [[Cheng2023]].
- In a Taiwanese registry, 30.2% were group A, 46.4% B and 23.5% E. Group E had the highest 5-year mortality (22.6%) and the highest hazards of death (HR 1.727) and of moderate-to-severe exacerbation (HR 2.127) versus A [[Lin2025]].
- The symptom instrument changes who lands in A or B. In COPDGene, mMRC assigned 33.6% A, 20.5% B, 7.9% C and 38.0% D, while SGRQ (as a CAT surrogate) assigned 29.4%, 24.7%, 4.9% and 41.0% (κ = 0.77) [[Han2013]].
- In the UK ERICA cohort (n = 664), groups A/B/E were 7%/27%/66% using the CAAT and 22%/12%/66% using the mMRC. 57% of those in group B by CAAT were group A by mMRC [[Adesoye2026]].
- In ERICA, both versions predicted hospitalised exacerbations. With CAAT: OR 3.72 for B vs A and 8.13 for E vs A. With mMRC: 2.69 and 4.05 [[Adesoye2026]].
- GOLD 2026 updated the ABE tool. According to an ERICA analysis, exacerbations are now assessed separately from symptoms, and for people with no exacerbation in the past 12 months the assessment focuses on symptom burden. The exact 2026 thresholds were not seen in a primary GOLD source (unverified: GOLD 2026 report not accessed) [[Adesoye2026]].

### assessment · Blood eosinophils
- GOLD 2023: adding an inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) has little or no effect at a blood eosinophil count < 100 cells/µL. Counts ≥ 300 cells/µL identify a strong likelihood of benefit, and between 100 and 300 cells/µL there is a continuous gradation of effect [[Agusti2023]].
- GOLD 2023 allows treatment decisions to use historical eosinophil counts, as repeatability in a large primary-care population "appears reasonable", though variability is greater at higher thresholds [[Agusti2023]].
- In GOLD 2023's follow-up algorithm, persistent exacerbations on LABA + LAMA lead to escalation to LABA + LAMA + ICS if eosinophils are ≥ 100 cells/µL. For group E at first presentation, triple therapy "can be considered" if eosinophils are ≥ 300 cells/µL (full treatment detail in pass D) [[Agusti2023]].
- The GOLD 2019 science committee described blood eosinophils as a biomarker to target ICS more precisely in patients who keep having exacerbations despite bronchodilators [[Singh2019]].
- Pooling three budesonide–formoterol trials (4,528 patients, no asthma history), exacerbations rose non-linearly with eosinophil count on formoterol alone. At ≥ 0.10 × 10⁹ cells/L, adding budesonide reduced exacerbations (rate ratio 0.75) [[Bafadhel2018]].
- In IMPACT, the moderate/severe exacerbation rate ratio for triple therapy versus umeclidinium–vilanterol was 0.88 at eosinophils < 90 cells/µL and 0.56 at ≥ 310 cells/µL. Former smokers responded more to ICS than current smokers at any eosinophil count [[Pascoe2019]].
- In the Copenhagen General Population Study (7,225 with spirometric COPD), eosinophils above 0.34 × 10⁹ cells/L carried adjusted incidence rate ratios of 1.76 for severe and 1.15 for moderate exacerbations. A 2% cut-point predicted severe exacerbations only in clinical COPD [[VedelKrogh2016]].

### assessment · Multidimensional scores, walking tests and hyperinflation
- BODE (Body-mass index, airflow Obstruction, Dyspnoea, Exercise capacity) is a 10-point scale. In a validation cohort of 625 patients (162 deaths), each 1-point rise carried a hazard ratio of 1.34 for death from any cause and 1.62 for respiratory death. The C statistic was 0.74 versus 0.65 for FEV1 [[Celli2004]].
- BODE point table, as reproduced from Celli 2004:
  - FEV1 % predicted: ≥ 65 = 0; 50–64 = 1; 36–49 = 2; ≤ 35 = 3.
  - 6-minute walk distance: ≥ 350 m = 0; 250–349 = 1; 150–249 = 2; ≤ 149 = 3.
  - mMRC: 0–1 = 0; 2 = 1; 3 = 2; 4 = 3.
  - BMI: > 21 = 0; ≤ 21 = 1.
  - Quartiles: 0–2, 3–4, 5–6, 7–10 [[Kamath2020,Guerra2018,Celli2004]].
- In the original BODE validation, 61% of deaths were from respiratory insufficiency, 14% myocardial infarction, 12% lung cancer and 13% other causes [[Celli2004]].
- A 2018 external validation compared BODE, updated BODE, ADO, e-BODE, BODEx, DOSE, SAFE, B-AE-D and GOLD-based scores for predicting COPD mortality, and tabulated each score's points [[Guerra2018]].
- The ERS/ATS field walking test standard covers the 6-minute walk test and the incremental and endurance shuttle walk tests. It says results are sensitive to small changes in method and that two tests should be done for the 6MWT and ISWT [[Holland2014]].
- In ECLIPSE (n = 2,112), 6MWD fell 29.7 m more over the year before death in those who died than in survivors. A fall of more than 30 m carried a hazard ratio of 1.93 for death, so 30 m is proposed as the minimal clinically important difference [[Polkey2013]].
- Static hyperinflation measured as inspiratory capacity to total lung capacity (IC/TLC) predicted all-cause and respiratory mortality independently of BODE in 689 patients (183 deaths over a mean 34 months) [[Casanova2005]].

### assessment · Case-finding versus screening; under- and overdiagnosis
- GOLD 2023: screening spirometry is "probably not indicated" in asymptomatic people without significant exposure. In those with symptoms and/or risk factors (for example > 20 pack-years, recurrent chest infections, prematurity or other significant early-life events), diagnostic yield is relatively high and spirometry "should be considered" for case-finding [[Agusti2023]].
- The USPSTF (2022 reaffirmation) recommends against screening asymptomatic adults for COPD (grade D), concluding with moderate certainty that screening has no net benefit [[USPSTF2022]].
- In BOLD and other population surveys (30,874 adults aged ≥ 40 at 44 sites in 27 countries), 81.4% of spirometrically defined COPD (post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC < LLN) was undiagnosed. Underdiagnosis ranged from 50.0% in Lexington, Kentucky to 98.3% in Ile-Ife, Nigeria, and only 26.4% had ever had a lung function test [[Lamprecht2015]].
- A 2018 review estimates that about 70% of COPD worldwide is undiagnosed, while 30–60% of people with a physician diagnosis of COPD do not have it [[Diab2018]].
- In BOLD, 61.9% of 919 people reporting a doctor's diagnosis of COPD had unobstructed post-bronchodilator spirometry (55.3% by the fixed ratio). 45.7% of these false positives were using respiratory medicines [[Sator2019]].
- The CAPTURE tool (five questions plus peak expiratory flow, with low PEF defined as < 350 L/min in males and < 250 L/min in females) had 89.7% sensitivity and 78.1% specificity for COPD needing treatment in the development case–control study [[Martinez2017]].
- In 4,325 US primary-care patients without a COPD diagnosis, 110 (2.5%) had undiagnosed clinically significant COPD. CAPTURE had a sensitivity of 48.2% and a specificity of 88.6% (AUC 0.81) [[Martinez2023]].

### lookalikes · Imaging
- GOLD 2023: a chest X-ray cannot confirm COPD. It may show hyperinflation (flattened diaphragm, increased retrosternal air space), hyperlucency and rapid tapering of vessels, and it helps exclude alternatives such as fibrosis, bronchiectasis, pleural disease, kyphoscoliosis and cardiomegaly [[Agusti2023]].
- GOLD 2023 recommends chest CT for patients with persistent exacerbations, symptoms out of proportion to airflow limitation, severe obstruction with significant hyperinflation and gas trapping, or who meet lung cancer screening criteria [[Agusti2023]].
- On CT, emphysema extent bears on lung volume reduction and is associated with faster FEV1 decline, higher mortality and lung cancer risk. About 30% of COPD patients have bronchiectasis on CT. CT also shows coronary calcification, pulmonary artery enlargement, bone density and muscle mass [[Agusti2023]].
- The Fleischner Society classifies emphysema on CT as centrilobular (trace, mild, moderate, confluent, advanced destructive), panlobular, or paraseptal (mild or substantial). It also lists airway wall thickening, inflammatory small airways disease, tracheal abnormalities, interstitial lung abnormalities, pulmonary arterial enlargement and bronchiectasis as visual features [[Lynch2015]].
- A pulmonary artery to aorta diameter ratio > 1 on CT was associated with future severe exacerbations (OR 3.44 in the trial cohort and 2.80 in an external validation cohort). Among all variables analysed, it had the strongest association [[Wells2012]].

### lookalikes · Asthma and asthma–COPD overlap
- In 597 primary-care patients aged ≥ 40 in Aberdeen and Denver, 235 met the spirometric definition of COPD. Of these, 51.5% had previously been labelled asthma only, 37.9% chronic bronchitis/emphysema, and 10.6% had no diagnosis [[Tinkelman2006]].
- GOLD 2023 notes that abnormal lung development in childhood can cause asthma-like symptoms, so some children labelled "asthma" may in fact have been on a low lung-function trajectory [[Agusti2023]].
- GOLD 2023 says patients with COPD and concomitant asthma should be treated as if they have asthma [[Agusti2023]].
- A 2015 meta-analysis of 19 studies found a pooled prevalence of asthma–COPD overlap among people with COPD of 27% in population studies and 28% in hospital studies. Compared with COPD alone, overlap patients were younger, had higher BMI and healthcare use, and had worse quality of life [[Alshabanat2015]].
- The non-reversibility of airflow obstruction does not by itself separate asthma from COPD [[Agusti2023,Albert2012]].

### lookalikes · Bronchiectasis
- Among 201 patients with moderate-to-severe COPD who had high-resolution CT, 57.2% had bronchiectasis. It was independently associated with all-cause mortality (adjusted HR 2.54) over a median 48 months [[MartinezGarcia2013]].
- The EMBARC Delphi "ROSE" definition of the COPD–bronchiectasis association needs four things. **R**adiology: bronchial dilatation in ≥ 1 segment and > 1 lobe. **O**bstruction: FEV1/FVC < 0.7. **S**ymptoms: at least two of cough, expectoration, dyspnoea, fatigue and frequent infections. **E**xposure: ≥ 10 pack-years or other toxic agents such as biomass [[Traversi2021]].
- In 16,730 patients in the European Bronchiectasis Registry, 4,336 carried a COPD co-diagnosis. 22.2% of those had no airflow obstruction and 31.9% lacked ≥ 10 pack-years, so only 55.4% met ROSE [[Polverino2024]].
- An international consensus (2022) holds that clinically significant bronchiectasis needs both clinical and radiological criteria [[Aliberti2022]].

### lookalikes · Heart failure
- Of 405 people aged ≥ 65 with a GP diagnosis of COPD, 83 (20.5%) had previously unrecognised heart failure (42 systolic, 41 isolated diastolic). Only 244 (60.2%) actually met GOLD COPD criteria [[Rutten2005]].
- A 2009 review advises echocardiography and lung function testing in patients with both conditions. Airflow obstruction should be shown when the patient is euvolaemic. Very high or very low natriuretic peptides are informative, but intermediate values are not [[Hawkins2009]].
- In the Rotterdam Study, 70.0% of PRISm participants who died within a year had cardiovascular comorbidity (heart failure or coronary heart disease) [[Wijnant2020]].

### lookalikes · PRISm (preserved ratio impaired spirometry)
- GOLD 2023 defines PRISm as post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ≥ 0.7 with FEV1 < 80% of reference. Reported prevalence is 7.1–20.3%, higher in current and former smokers, and it is associated with more all-cause mortality. It can move to normal, obstructive or restrictive spirometry over time [[Agusti2023]].
- In early COPDGene data (smokers with ≥ 10 pack-years, aged 45–80), "GOLD-unclassified" smokers (FEV1/FVC ≥ 0.7, FEV1 < 80%) were 9%. They had higher BMI, disproportionately reduced TLC, more diabetes and thicker airway walls [[Wan2011]].
- In COPDGene's 5-year follow-up, PRISm prevalence was 12.4–12.5%. Of those with PRISm at the first visit, 22.2% moved to normal and 25.1% progressed to GOLD 1–4 [[Wan2018]].
- People with persistent PRISm in COPDGene lost FEV1 more slowly (−27.3 ± 42.1 mL/yr) than stable normal-spirometry smokers (−33.0 ± 41.7 mL/yr), while incident PRISm showed accelerated decline [[Wan2018]].
- In the Rotterdam Study (n = 5,487, mean age 69), 7.1% had PRISm. Of those re-examined after 4.5 years, 15.7% had normal spirometry and 49.4% had COPD. Adjusted all-cause mortality HR was 1.6 and cardiovascular mortality HR 2.8 versus controls [[Wijnant2020]].
- In 53,701 US adults from nine pooled cohorts, 8.5% had PRISm. It was associated with obesity (prevalence ratio 1.68) and with all-cause mortality of 29.6 versus 18.0 per 1,000 person-years (adjusted HR 1.50) [[Wan2021]].

### lookalikes · Pre-COPD, "GOLD 0" and smokers with normal spirometry
- GOLD 2023 uses "pre-COPD" for people of any age with respiratory symptoms and/or structural changes (for example emphysema) or functional changes (hyperinflation, reduced diffusing capacity, rapid FEV1 decline) without airflow obstruction (FEV1/FVC ≥ 0.7). It says they should already be considered patients, but there is no evidence yet on the best treatment [[Agusti2023]].
- GOLD 2001 proposed an "at-risk" stage 0 (risk factors plus chronic cough and sputum with normal spirometry). It was later dropped because not all such people progressed to COPD [[Han2021]].
- GOLD 2023 terminology: "early COPD" only for the biological first steps of disease studied experimentally; "mild" only for spirometric severity; "young COPD" for patients aged 20–50 [[Agusti2023]].
- In SPIROMICS (2,736 participants), 50% of current or former smokers with preserved spirometry had symptoms (CAT ≥ 10). They had more exacerbations than asymptomatic smokers (0.27 vs 0.08 per year), and 42% used bronchodilators and 23% inhaled glucocorticoids without an evidence base [[Woodruff2016]].
- In COPDGene, 54.1% of smokers with normal spirometry ("GOLD 0", n = 4,388) had one or more respiratory impairments, and 42.3% of 300 with CT had emphysema or airway thickening [[Regan2015]].
- Across three cohorts, about half of the 332 people with COPD at the end of follow-up had a normal FEV1 before age 40 and then a rapid decline (53 ± 21 mL/yr). The other half started from a low FEV1 and declined at only 27 ± 18 mL/yr [[Lange2015]].
- Of 657 people with FEV1 < 80% predicted before age 40, 26% had COPD after 22 years, compared with 7% of 2,207 people who started at ≥ 80% [[Lange2015]].

### spirometry · Step sequence
1. Kicker "Breathe in" · Title "Fill to the top": the FVC manoeuvre starts with a maximal inspiration to total lung capacity, followed by a "blast" of expiration [[Graham2019]].
2. Kicker "Blast" · Title "Time zero and the first second": time zero is found by back-extrapolation, and the back-extrapolated volume must be under 5% of FVC or 0.100 L [[Graham2019]].
3. Kicker "Keep going" · Title "Empty completely": expiration continues until a plateau (≤ 0.025 L in 1 s) or 15 s, then a full inspiration closes the loop [[Graham2019]].
4. Kicker "Repeat" · Title "Three good blows": at least three acceptable blows, with the two largest FEV1 and FVC each within 0.150 L, earn quality grade A [[Graham2019]].
5. Kicker "Bronchodilator" · Title "Test again after the inhaler": post-bronchodilator spirometry is required, for example 400 µg salbutamol as used in UPLIFT and ECLIPSE [[Agusti2023,Tashkin2008,Albert2012]].
6. Kicker "The ratio" · Title "Below 0.70 means obstruction": a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC < 0.7 confirms airflow obstruction, and FEV1 % predicted then sets GOLD grade 1–4 [[Agusti2023,Lin2025]].

### assessment · Step sequence
1. Kicker "Confirm" · Title "Spirometry first": the assessment starts only after spirometry has confirmed COPD [[Agusti2023]].
2. Kicker "Grade" · Title "How blocked: GOLD 1–4": FEV1 ≥ 80%, 50–79%, 30–49% and < 30% predicted give grades 1 to 4 [[Lin2025]].
3. Kicker "Symptoms" · Title "mMRC or CAT": mMRC 0–1 or CAT < 10 is "less symptomatic"; mMRC ≥ 2 or CAT ≥ 10 is "more symptomatic" [[Han2013,Cheng2023]].
4. Kicker "History" · Title "Count last year's flare-ups": two or more moderate exacerbations, or one leading to hospital, places the patient in group E whatever their symptoms [[Cheng2023,Agusti2023]].
5. Kicker "Group" · Title "A, B or E": with fewer flare-ups, low symptoms give group A and high symptoms give group B. C and D were merged into E in 2023 [[Agusti2023]].
6. Kicker "Biomarker" · Title "Eosinophils": blood eosinophils < 100 cells/µL predict little ICS benefit, and ≥ 300 cells/µL a strong likelihood of benefit [[Agusti2023]].

### lookalikes · Step sequence
1. Kicker "Ratio ≥ 0.70" · Title "Not obstructed, not necessarily well": PRISm (FEV1 < 80% with ratio ≥ 0.7) affects 7.1–20.3% and carries higher mortality [[Agusti2023]].
2. Kicker "Symptoms, normal test" · Title "Pre-COPD": symptoms or CT or functional abnormalities with a normal ratio are now called pre-COPD [[Agusti2023]].
3. Kicker "Reversible?" · Title "Asthma": if airflow limitation disappears after bronchodilator, that is incompatible with COPD; otherwise reversibility does not separate the two [[Agusti2023]].
4. Kicker "Dilated airways" · Title "Bronchiectasis": about 30% of COPD patients have bronchiectasis on CT, and ROSE defines when the two coexist [[Agusti2023,Traversi2021]].
5. Kicker "Wet lungs" · Title "Heart failure": 20.5% of older people labelled COPD had unrecognised heart failure [[Rutten2005]].
6. Kicker "Wrong label" · Title "Over- and underdiagnosis": in BOLD, 81.4% of spirometric COPD was undiagnosed, and 61.9% of reported COPD diagnoses had unobstructed spirometry [[Lamprecht2015,Sator2019]].

### Exam hooks
- COPD needs a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC < 0.70 in the right clinical context; symptoms and exposure alone do not make the diagnosis [[Agusti2023]].
- GOLD grade (1–4) comes from post-bronchodilator FEV1 % predicted: ≥ 80, 50–79, 30–49, < 30 [[Lin2025,Agusti2023]].
- ABE: group E = ≥ 2 moderate exacerbations or ≥ 1 hospitalised exacerbation in the past year; A vs B splits at mMRC 2 / CAT 10 [[Cheng2023,Agusti2023]].
- mMRC grade 2: slower than peers on the level, or stops at own pace. Grade 3: stops after about 100 m or a few minutes [[Xiong2026]].
- The CAT has 8 items scored 0–5, a total of 0–40, and an MCID of 2 points [[Minov2015,Kon2014]].
- A GOLD 2023 repeat-spirometry rule: an initial ratio of 0.60–0.80 should be confirmed on another occasion [[Agusti2023]].
- The fixed ratio overdiagnoses older people. The LLN of FEV1/FVC falls below 0.70 at a median age of 42 (men) and 48 (women) [[Swanney2008]].
- Bronchodilator reversibility varies from test to test and does not predict outcomes in COPD [[Albert2012,Agusti2023]].
- Eosinophils < 100/µL mean little ICS benefit; ≥ 300/µL mean a strong likelihood of benefit [[Agusti2023]].
- BODE ranges 0–10, and each point raises the hazard of death by 1.34 [[Celli2004]].
- PRISm = ratio ≥ 0.7 with FEV1 < 80% predicted. It is not COPD but carries excess mortality [[Agusti2023,Wan2021]].
- The ATS/ERS spirometry repeatability target is that the two best FEV1 and FVC values agree within 150 mL (age > 6) [[Graham2019]].
- A positive bronchodilator response under the 2022 standard is an FEV1 or FVC rise > 10% of predicted [[Zhao2025]].
- USPSTF recommends against screening asymptomatic adults (grade D). GOLD supports case-finding in people with symptoms or risk factors [[USPSTF2022,Agusti2023]].

### In plain words
- To check for COPD, you blow out as hard and as long as you can into a machine called a spirometer, before and after a puff of reliever medicine [[Graham2019,Agusti2023]].
- The test compares how much air you blow out in the first second with how much you blow out in total. If the first second is less than 70% of the total after the medicine, your airways are narrowed [[Agusti2023]].
- How much you blow out in that first second, compared with someone like you, gives a grade from 1 (mild) to 4 (very severe) [[Lin2025]].
- Your doctor or nurse also asks how breathless you get (the mMRC scale, 0 to 4) or gives you an 8-question form (the CAT, 0 to 40) [[Xiong2026,Minov2015]].
- A change of 2 points on the CAT is about the smallest change people notice [[Kon2014]].
- Flare-ups matter: two or more in a year, or one that put you in hospital, puts you in the highest-risk group, E [[Cheng2023]].
- A blood test that counts eosinophils (a type of white blood cell) helps decide whether an inhaled steroid is likely to help [[Agusti2023]].
- Many people with COPD do not know they have it, and some people told they have COPD turn out to have normal breathing tests [[Lamprecht2015,Sator2019]].
- Other conditions can feel like COPD, including asthma, widened airways (bronchiectasis) and heart failure, so a breathing test and sometimes a heart scan or a CT scan help sort them out [[Agusti2023,Rutten2005]].
- Having a normal breathing test does not always mean your lungs are fine if you smoke or have symptoms. Doctors now talk about "pre-COPD" and "PRISm" for these in-between states [[Agusti2023]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Fixed ratio or LLN? GOLD keeps < 0.70 for simplicity and trial continuity, and pooled cohorts found it discriminates COPD events as well as or better than the LLN [[Agusti2023,Bhatt2019]]. Others show the fixed ratio labels up to 45% of healthy older never-smokers as obstructed and argue for the LLN [[Swanney2008,Hardie2002]]. Status: unresolved. GOLD acknowledges the age bias.
- Race-neutral reference equations: the ATS recommends race-neutral averages [[Bhakta2023]]. Switching to GLI Global left obstruction prevalence unchanged but reclassified restriction and severity, especially in Black patients, with unknown downstream effects on care [[Moffett2023,Bowerman2023]]. Status: being adopted; outcome effects not yet known.
- mMRC versus CAT for ABE: the two are used interchangeably but are not equivalent. In ERICA, 57% of CAAT-defined group B became group A by mMRC [[Adesoye2026]]. In COPDGene, agreement was κ = 0.77 [[Han2013]]. Status: GOLD allows either.
- Does ABE predict better than ABCD? Group E carries more hospitalisation and death, but one cohort found mortality prediction slightly better with the 2017 ABCD scheme [[Cheng2023,Lin2025]]. GOLD itself says validation is needed [[Agusti2023]]. GOLD 2026 changed the tool again (details not verified from primary GOLD text) [[Adesoye2026]].
- Pre-COPD and PRISm: should they be treated? Symptomatic smokers with preserved spirometry have exacerbations and use inhalers without evidence [[Woodruff2016]]. GOLD 2023 says there is no evidence yet on treatment and calls for RCTs [[Agusti2023]]. Labelling them risks conferring disease status on people who may never progress [[Han2021]]. Status: research question.
- Case-finding versus screening: USPSTF says no net benefit of screening asymptomatic adults [[USPSTF2022]]. GOLD supports case-finding in symptomatic or exposed people [[Agusti2023]]. The CAPTURE tool had a sensitivity of only 48.2% in real primary care [[Martinez2023]]. Status: open whether case-finding improves outcomes.
- Eosinophil thresholds: GOLD uses 100 and 300 cells/µL, but the ICS effect is continuous [[Agusti2023,Bafadhel2018,Pascoe2019]]. Counts vary more at higher thresholds [[Agusti2023]]. Status: thresholds are pragmatic cut-points.
