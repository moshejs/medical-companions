Inhaler devices & technique

An inhaler only works if the medicine reaches the lungs, and each device needs a different breath. Pick a device and play one use of it, step by step, with how often each step goes wrong in published studies.

Study material, not instructionsSteps and error rates are quoted from labels and studies. Each product's own leaflet, and the person's nurse, pharmacist or doctor, give the instructions for a specific inhaler.

Peak inspiratory flow: a slow breath or a fast one

A handheld inspiratory flow meter with an adjustable dial can mimic a DPI's resistance and check whether a person reaches an optimal peak inspiratory flow (PIF) of at least 60 L/min [[Mahler2017]]. Move the slider to see where a flow sits against that threshold.

Teaching toolThe 60 L/min threshold and the device suggestion come from one author's review, not a guideline [[Mahler2017]]. This is not for diagnosis or treatment decisions, and it does not measure anyone's breath.

How common

Low flow is frequent

Observational studies cited by Mahler found suboptimal PIF (<60 L/min) in 19–78% of stable outpatients with COPD and 32–47% of inpatients before discharge after an exacerbation [[Mahler2017]].

Established
After a flare-up

Low flow and readmission

In 123 people hospitalised for an exacerbation, 52% had suboptimal PIF. They had higher 90-day COPD readmission (28.1% vs 13.6%; P=0.048) and fewer median days to readmission (63.5 vs 144). PIF was the only variable predicting days to COPD readmission in a multivariable model [[Loh2017]].

Early evidence: single centre
Why it falls

Effort and muscle strength

PIF depends on effort and respiratory-muscle strength, both of which can be reduced in COPD by hyperinflation, low oxygen (hypoxaemia) and muscle wasting [[Mahler2017]].

Established

Common errors, as published

The only modifiable factor linked to less inhaler misuse in Melani 2011 was instruction from health caregivers (P<0.001) [[Melani2011]]. Nursing inhaler teaching is covered in module 5.4.

The carbon footprint of inhalers

HFC propellants used in pMDIs since the CFC phase-out do not deplete ozone but "are potent greenhouse gases" [[Janson2020]]. In 2017, 70% of inhalers sold in England were MDIs against 13% in Sweden; applying Sweden's mix to England would cut 550 kt CO2e a year [[Janson2020]].

Switching

Savings depend on brand

In NHS England data, each 10% of MDIs switched to DPIs would save 58 kt CO2e a year. Switching to the cheapest equivalent DPIs saved £8.2M per 10% switched, whereas keeping 2017 DPI brand patterns cost £12.7M more [[Wilkinson2019]].

Established
Low-carbon propellant

HFA-152a

Per salbutamol inhaler, HFA-152a cut the footprint by 92% versus HFA-134a [[King2026]], and was bioequivalent in pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic studies [[Moreira2026]].

Early evidence: healthy-volunteer and small PD studies
Balance

Carbon is one factor

Janson et al. conclude the lower carbon footprint of DPIs "should be considered alongside other factors when choosing inhalation devices" [[Janson2020]]. A person's ability to reach the needed peak flow is one of those factors [[Mahler2017]].
