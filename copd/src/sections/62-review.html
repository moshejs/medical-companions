Every card below is tagged memory aid: a short phrase written for this guide to hold a published fact in mind. None is an official grouping from GOLD, the ATS, ERS, BTS or NICE, a drug label or any guideline, and none replaces the source it points to. Doses, thresholds and oxygen targets are quoted as published, never as instructions.

Mnemonic in motion: the GOLD follow-up

At follow-up, GOLD 2023 reviews treatment by two traits, breathlessness and exacerbations, and uses the blood eosinophil count to judge whether an inhaled steroid is likely to help [[Agusti2023]]. As a memory aid, five words walk the published path: double, count, triple, add, step back. Schematic of the published GOLD 2023 follow-up algorithm, not a treatment plan; the needle positions are examples, not measurements. [[Agusti2023]]

These cards are for learning, not for deciding anything about one person's care. Whether an inhaler, a tablet, oxygen or a procedure suits someone depends on their own tests, history and wishes, and only their own care team can judge that. Survival and risk figures on the cards are population statistics from registries and trials; they cannot predict what will happen to any one person.

Sources

Every reference behind the COPD Companion: journal articles, randomised trials, cohort and registry studies, systematic reviews, GOLD, ATS, ERS, BTS and other guidance, US drug and device labels and trial registrations. The small numbers throughout the app point here; filter by research area or search.

Research passes ran in parallel, one per area: epidemiology, risk factors and history; mechanism, pathology and alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency; diagnosis and assessment; medicines for stable COPD and inhalers; exacerbations and hospital care; smoking cessation, vaccines, rehabilitation, oxygen and advanced therapies; living with COPD, comorbidities and nursing care; and the research frontier. Before inclusion each source was checked, where possible, against PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, ClinicalTrials.gov or the regulator's label, and the green tag on an entry names the service that confirmed it. Works found by more than one pass appear once, with any extra keys listed beside them.

Entries marked %t:sources.check% are pages with no PMID or DOI, or works that could not be confirmed. They are not cited for any fact in this guide.
