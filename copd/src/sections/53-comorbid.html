Heart, mood, bones & other conditions

COPD rarely travels alone. GOLD 2023 states that it "almost invariably" coexists with other diseases, which can mimic its symptoms and should generally be treated to usual standards [[Agusti2023]]. Follow the heart's risk through the weeks after a flare-up, then tap the body map.

In the BODE cohort (1,664 people followed for a median of 51 months), 79 comorbidities were recorded and 12 predicted death. They form the COTE index: a score of 4 or more roughly doubled the risk of death (HR 2.26–2.68) in every BODE quartile, and each point raised the risk of both COPD-related death (HR 1.13) and non-COPD death (HR 1.18) [[Divo2012]].

Heart medicines: what the trials found

GOLD 2023 states that cardiovascular disease in COPD should be treated per established guidelines, including selective β1-blockers when there is a clear cardiovascular indication [[Agusti2023]]. Trials that gave heart medicines to people without such an indication, hoping to prevent exacerbations, did not show that benefit.

Heart

Hidden heart failure

Among 405 people aged 65 or over with a GP diagnosis of COPD, 20.5% had previously unrecognised heart failure on full work-up, split almost evenly between systolic (42) and isolated diastolic (41) dysfunction [[Rutten2005]].

Heart

More of every kind

A meta-analysis of 29 datasets found higher odds of cardiovascular disease with COPD (OR 2.46), with a two- to five-fold higher risk of ischaemic heart disease, arrhythmia, heart failure, pulmonary circulation and arterial disease; hypertension (OR 1.33) and diabetes (OR 1.36) were also more common [[Chen2015]].

Blood clots

The unexplained flare-up

In 7 CT-angiography studies (880 people), pulmonary embolism was found in 16.1% of exacerbations with no clear cause. Two-thirds of the clots were in main, lobar or interlobar arteries; pleuritic chest pain and signs of heart failure were more common with PE [[Aleva2017]].

Lung cancer and screening

COPD (GOLD 2+) was present in 50% of 602 people with lung cancer, compared with 8% of community controls matched for age, sex and pack-years (OR 11.6) [[Young2009]]. In 2,507 outpatients with COPD followed for a median of 60 months, lung cancer developed at 16.7 per 1,000 person-years; squamous cell carcinoma was the commonest type (44%), and cancer was more frequent with milder obstruction (GOLD I–II), older age, lower BMI and DLCO below 80% [[deTorres2011]].

In the US National Lung Screening Trial (53,454 high-risk people), three annual low-dose CT scans cut lung-cancer deaths by 20.0% compared with chest X-ray (247 vs 309 per 100,000 person-years) [[Aberle2011]]. In NELSON (13,195 men aged 50–74), volume CT lowered 10-year lung-cancer mortality (2.50 vs 3.30 per 1,000 person-years; rate ratio 0.76) [[deKoning2020]].

The USPSTF 2021 recommends yearly low-dose CT for adults aged 50–80 with a 20 pack-year history who smoke or quit within the past 15 years (B recommendation) [[Krist2021]]. GOLD 2023 recommends annual low-dose CT in COPD due to smoking, as in the general population, and says data are insufficient for COPD not due to smoking [[Agusti2023]].

Bones, muscle, weight and frailty

Bones

Often missed

A systematic review found osteoporosis in 9–69% and osteopenia in 27–67% of people with COPD; no treatment trials had been done in COPD-only samples [[GraatVerboom2009]]. With bone scans plus spine X-rays, half of 255 outpatients had osteoporosis, at every GOLD stage, and most were untreated [[GraatVerboom2011]]. GOLD 2023 notes that recurrent systemic corticosteroids raise the risk and should be avoided if possible [[Agusti2023]].

Frailty

Common, and often reversible

Frailty affected 19% and pre-frailty 56% of people with COPD in 27 studies; COPD roughly doubled the odds of frailty (OR 1.97) [[Marengoni2018]]. Of 816 people referred for rehabilitation, 25.6% were frail; they had twice the odds of not completing (OR 2.20), but 61.3% of frail completers no longer met frailty criteria afterwards [[Maddocks2016]].

Muscle

Limb muscles matter

The ATS/ERS 2014 statement calls limb muscle dysfunction a key systemic consequence of COPD, linked to poorer exercise tolerance, quality of life and survival, and names exercise training as the most potent treatment [[Maltais2014]].

Weight

Thin is a warning sign

In 400 patients, low BMI independently predicted death, with mortality rising below 25 kg/m²; gaining more than 2 kg in 8 weeks predicted survival [[Schols1998]]. Supplements added 1.65 kg in undernourished patients [[Ferreira2012]] and, across 13 RCTs, 14.8 g protein and 236 kcal a day, 1.94 kg and 5.3% grip strength [[Collins2012]].

Mood, thinking, sleep and reflux

Depression

About twice as common

Depressive symptoms affected 24.6% of 39,587 people with COPD vs 11.7% of controls (OR 2.81) [[Zhang2011]]. Reported ranges run from 8–80% for depression and 6–74% for anxiety, depending on tools and methods [[Yohannes2010]].

Anxiety

Linked to more flare-ups

In the FLOW cohort, COPD was linked to anxiety (OR 1.85), and anxiety predicted a shorter 6-minute walk, more functional limitation (OR 2.41) and more exacerbations (HR 1.39) [[Eisner2010G]]. Cognitive behavioural therapy modestly improved anxiety scores (Beck −4.41; low-quality evidence) [[Usmani2017]].

Thinking

Low oxygen and memory

A review concluded that cognition is only mildly impaired without hypoxaemia, that cognitive dysfunction is more common with hypoxaemia, and that it may be linked to higher mortality and disability [[Dodd2010]].

Sleep

The overlap syndrome

GOLD 2023 puts sleep apnoea at about 14% of people with COPD, with more night-time desaturation [[Agusti2023]]. In a Spanish cohort, overlap syndrome not treated with CPAP carried higher mortality (RR 1.79) and more severe exacerbations; those on CPAP had no excess risk Early evidence [[Marin2010]].

Reflux

Strong link, thin treatment evidence

Reflux was linked to frequent exacerbations in ECLIPSE [[Hurst2010]] and to higher exacerbation odds (OR 5.37) in 10 observational studies [[Huang2020]]. The only RCT of a proton pump inhibitor was small: 12 of 50 on lansoprazole vs 26 of 50 had an exacerbation (low to very low certainty) [[Kikuchi2020]].

After a stay in hospital

A serious marker

Of 171 people admitted with an exacerbation, 8% died in hospital and 23% by one year (35% after ICU). Maintenance oral steroids (RR 5.07), higher PaCO2 and older age predicted death [[Groenewegen2003]].

SupportFeeling anxious or low is common with COPD; an ACCP workshop found it is underdiagnosed and treated effectively in only a small proportion of people [[Maurer2008]]. It is part of the illness, help is available, and talking therapies have been tested [[Zhang2011,Usmani2017]].
