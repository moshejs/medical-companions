Part 1 · Understanding COPD

Air trapping & breathlessness

Why does walking up stairs feel so hard with COPD? Narrow airways empty slowly, so when breathing speeds up, air gets trapped and each breath starts fuller than the last. Step through the chain from slow emptying to low oxygen and a strained heart, then set a breathing rate yourself and watch the trapped air build up.

Try it: breathing rate during exercise

Teaching toolA simple model of a lung that empties at a fixed speed (a time constant), with volumes drawn as fractions of total lung capacity. Its numbers illustrate the idea of dynamic hyperinflation; they are not measurements, they are not for diagnosis or treatment decisions, and real lungs vary widely. The published figure: inspiratory capacity fell by a mean 0.37 L during cycling in 105 patients. [[ODonnell2001]]

What to look for

Too little time to breathe out

At 12 breaths a minute both model lungs empty fully between breaths. Push the rate up with the COPD lung: each breath out is cut short, the red staircase climbs, and inspiratory capacity, the room left for the next breath, shrinks. Red dots mark breaths that hit the ceiling. In real patients, dyspnoea during exercise correlates with end-expiratory lung volume, and anything that lowers it should ease breathlessness. [[ODonnell2006]]

In plain wordsThe faster you breathe, the less time there is to get the air out, so air builds up and the lungs get too full. [[ODonnell2001,ODonnell2006]]

Long-acting bronchodilators reduce hyperinflation at rest and during exercise in moderate to severe COPD, so tidal volume can expand further for the same effort. [[ODonnell2006]]

Neural drive

Older lungs work harder too

In healthy people, neural drive was higher at ages 51–80 than at 18–50 (11.4% vs 8.2% of maximum). [[Jolley2009]]

Sensation

Not one feeling

The 2012 ATS statement distinguishes several qualitatively and mechanistically distinct breathing sensations, notes that endogenous and exogenous opioids may modulate how dyspnoea is felt, and says treatment has lagged behind understanding of the mechanisms. [[Parshall2012]]

Low oxygen: air and blood out of step

V/Q mismatch

Not a diffusion problem

In advanced COPD, V/Q mismatch and shunt explained all the hypoxaemia at rest and on exercise. People with similar blood gases often had different V/Q patterns, so the pattern cannot be inferred from PaO2 and PaCO2. [[Wagner1977]]

Already in GOLD 1

Early mismatch

In 150 patients across GOLD 1–4, the alveolar–arterial PO2 gradient and V/Q mismatch were already clearly abnormal in GOLD 1 and rose only modestly with severity. FEV1 correlated with PaO2 (r=0.62) and PaCO2 (r=−0.59). [[RodriguezRoisin2009]]

Where it sits matters

Apical or basal

In alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, basal-predominant emphysema went with worse FEV1 (by 9.9% predicted) but better gas exchange than apical-predominant disease of the same volume. [[Parr2004]]

Thick blood

Secondary polycythaemia

GOLD 2023 notes that secondary polycythaemia is associated with pulmonary hypertension, venous thromboembolism and mortality. It became less common after long-term oxygen therapy, but one study found it in 8.4% of people with severe COPD on oxygen. [[Agusti2023]]

Pulmonary hypertension and cor pulmonale

QuestionWhat the research reports
How high?Usually mild: resting mean PAP 20–35 mmHg in stable disease, rising further during exercise, sleep and exacerbations. Fewer than 5% have severe or “disproportionate” PH (mean PAP above 40 mmHg). [[Weitzenblum2009]]
Severe PH from COPD alone?Of 998 people catheterised for chronic respiratory failure, 27 had a mean PAP of 40 mmHg or more, and in only 11 (1.1%) was COPD the sole cause. That group had mild-to-moderate obstruction, severe hypoxaemia, low PaCO2, very low DLCO and shorter survival. [[Chaouat2005]]
How fast does it progress?In 131 mildly to moderately hypoxaemic people catheterised twice about 6.8 years apart, mean PAP rose by 0.4 mmHg a year, and 25% developed resting PH, usually mild (20–42.5 mmHg). [[Kessler2001]]
A CT clueA pulmonary artery-to-aorta diameter ratio above 1 was independently associated with future severe exacerbations (OR 3.44 in COPDGene, 2.80 in ECLIPSE). [[Wells2012]]
Treatment evidenceLong-term oxygen is the only treatment shown to slow the progression of PH in COPD, though pressures rarely return to normal; vasodilators are not recommended because benefit is minimal and they worsen gas exchange. [[Barbera2003]]

How fast lung function falls

Group averages

About half of people with COPD at the end of three cohorts had a normal FEV1 before age 40 followed by rapid decline (53 mL a year); the other half started low and declined at 27 mL a year. Accelerated decline is not obligatory for COPD. [[Lange2015]]
