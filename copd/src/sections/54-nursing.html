Nursing care

Follow a nurse assessing a breathless person with COPD on admission: count the breathing, score it on the right oxygen scale, give controlled oxygen to a target, know what the oximeter cannot tell you, teach the inhaler, and plan a safe discharge with home oxygen.

Schematic. The patient's readings (breathing rate 32, SpO2 87% on air, then 90% on controlled oxygen) are made up for teaching. Oxygen targets and devices are quoted from the BTS guideline [[ODriscoll2017G]]; NEWS2 scale 2 as described by Echevarria [[Echevarria2019]]; oximeter bias [[Fawzy2022]]; inhaler teaching [[Press2012]]; burns and fires [[Murabit2012,Cooper2015]].

Early warning scores in COPD

NEWS2 has two oxygen saturation scales; the second sets the target at 88–92% for people with hypercapnic respiratory failure [[Echevarria2019]]. Who should be scored on scale 2 is still debated: the two large studies below reached different conclusions [[Echevarria2019,Pimentel2019]]. The point bands of each scale are not reproduced here because the sources in this companion do not state them.

Sputum, flare-ups and going home

Sputum colour

Green means bacteria, more often

In 121 people with an exacerbation in primary care, purulent (green) sputum grew bacteria in 84% vs 38% for mucoid sputum; green was 94.4% sensitive and 77.0% specific for a high bacterial load, and everyone with white sputum improved without antibiotics [[Stockley2000]].

Who flares up

The past predicts the future

In ECLIPSE, first-year exacerbation rates were 0.85, 1.34 and 2.00 per person for GOLD 2, 3 and 4, and the best single predictor of future exacerbations was a history of them [[Hurst2010]].

Home oxygen

Fire risk

Tobacco smoking is the greatest fire risk for people on home oxygen; any naked flame is a hazard, and the European Industrial Gases Association considers e-cigarettes unsafe with home oxygen [[Cooper2015]]. The BTS home oxygen guideline includes risk assessment of users who smoke as a key area [[Hardinge2015]].

Burns

Long stays, lasting harm

In a Canadian burn centre (1999–2008), 17 people, all with COPD, were burned while smoking on home oxygen: two needed intubation, mean stay was 42.8 days, two died, and fewer returned home [[Murabit2012]]. A US model found a national mandate for thermal fuses on oxygen tubing cost-effective Early evidence [[Sheckter2024]].

Alert cards

Follow the card first

If a patient has an oxygen alert card, the BTS guideline says initial oxygen should follow the card until blood gas results are available [[ODriscoll2017G]].
