Part 1 · Understanding COPD

Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency

The inherited form of COPD. The liver makes alpha-1 antitrypsin (AAT), a protein that travels in the blood to shield the lungs from neutrophil elastase. Follow the normal M protein and the faulty Z protein side by side, then work out what two parents can pass on and see what the augmentation trials found.

How common is it?

Severe AATD affects about 1 in 2,000–5,000 people [[Stoller2005]]; a WHO memorandum puts it at about 1 in 2,000 to 1 in 7,000 in Europe and North America, comparable with cystic fibrosis. [[WHO1997]] It has been found in populations of all racial groups, not only white Europeans. [[deSerres2002]]

GOLD 2023 states that PiZZ is found in 0.12% of COPD patients, from 1 in 408 in Northern Europe to 1 in 1,274 in Eastern Europe [[Agusti2023]]; Greulich and colleagues put AATD as the cause of about 1% of COPD cases. [[Greulich2013]] The figures probably differ in what they count and which population was sampled. Blanco and colleagues report the Z allele in 98% of severe cases [[Blanco2017]], while the label’s 95% counts ZZ homozygotes. [[FDA-ProlastinC]]

Smoking changes the story

ZZ, New Zealand

Decades earlier

In 69 ZZ individuals followed from 1970 to 1983, mean FEV1 was 77% predicted in non-smokers and 38% in smokers. FEV1 fell by 80 mL/yr in non-smokers, 317 mL/yr in smokers and 61 mL/yr in ex-smokers. [[Janus1985]]

ZZ, Sweden

Dose–response

In 608 PiZZ adults, adjusted FEV1 decline was 47 mL/yr in never-smokers, 41 in ex-smokers and 70 in current smokers, with a dose–response to cigarette consumption. [[Piitulainen1999]] In the NHLBI registry (1,129 adults), mean decline was 54 mL/yr and 5-year mortality 19%. [[AATDRegistry1998]]

Never-smokers

Found by screening

Never-smoking PiZZ adults found by family or population screening had an SMR of 0.70 (95% CI 0.14–2.04) [[Tanash2008]], and compared with random controls showed no clear excess risk of death (HR 1.2). Overall, PiZZ carried a 3.2-fold adjusted risk of death. [[Tanash2017]] Some never-smokers still develop progressive lung disease. [[Miravitlles2017]]

Not always basal

Where the emphysema sits

Of 102 PiZ people with emphysema on CT, 65 had a basal-predominant and 37 (36%) an apical-predominant pattern. [[Parr2004]]

The liver

Infants

Newborn screening in Sweden

Screening 200,000 newborns found 120 Pi Z infants; 14 had prolonged obstructive jaundice (9 with severe liver disease), and many apparently healthy Pi Z infants had abnormal liver tests at 3 and 6 months. [[Sveger1976]] Infant cholestasis usually remits by adolescence, and AATD is the commonest cause of chronic liver disease in childhood. [[WHO1997]]

Adults

Fibrosis, cirrhosis and cancer

Cirrhosis and liver carcinoma affect at least 25% of deficient adults over 50 (WHO memorandum). [[WHO1997]] Malmö autopsies linked AATD with cirrhosis (OR 7.8) and primary liver cancer (OR 20), significant only in men. [[Eriksson1986]] Liver fibrosis was not related to lung function. [[Hamesch2019]]

Pipeline

Silencing Z protein

In a 16-patient phase 2 trial, the RNAi drug fazirsiran cut liver Z-AAT by a median of 83%. Early evidence [[Strnad2022Fazirsiran]]

Testing, as published

Teaching toolA summary of published testing guidance, not a testing protocol for any person and not for diagnosis or treatment decisions.

Who to test

The 2003 ATS/ERS statement made a strong recommendation to test all symptomatic adults with COPD. The 2016 Alpha-1 Foundation guidance endorses testing all adults with symptomatic fixed airflow obstruction, whether labelled COPD or asthma, plus people with unexplained bronchiectasis or liver disease. [[Sandhaus2016]]

Family

Testing first-degree relatives is the most efficient way to find cases. [[Sandhaus2016]]

Level and genotype

In three European laboratories, the AAT level and genotyping were the main diagnostic steps, with isoelectric focusing (phenotyping) kept for confirmation. [[Miravitlles2010]]

Rare alleles

Genotyping only for S and Z can miss rare deficiency alleles; an algorithm sending low-level samples on to isoelectric focusing was tested on 50,020 samples. [[Bornhorst2007]]

Diagnostic delay

Years from first symptoms

In US patient surveys, the mean delay from first symptoms to diagnosis was 7.2 years in 1994 and 5.6 years in 2003, longer in women and older people. [[Stoller2005Chest]] In Germany and Italy the median delay was 6 to 7 years, and only 18–25% of surveyed physicians tested all COPD patients. [[Greulich2013]]

Undiagnosed

Registries and expert centres

The ERS statement says a large share of people with AATD remain undiagnosed, and recommends registries and management through expert centres. [[Miravitlles2017]]

Augmentation therapy: what the trials show

Augmentation means weekly intravenous infusions of AAT purified from donated plasma. The Prolastin-C label states a dose of 60 mg/kg once weekly by intravenous infusion, notes that efficacy on exacerbations and emphysema progression “has not been demonstrated” in adequately powered randomised trials, and contraindicates the product in IgA-deficient patients with antibodies against IgA. The label version cited is dated 2009 and may be older than the current US label. [[FDA-ProlastinC]]

Longer bars mean faster loss of lung tissue on CT. Lung density is a sensitive surrogate; its link to patient outcomes is inferred.
