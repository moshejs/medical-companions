Part 1 · Understanding COPD

What COPD is

COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease) is a long-term lung condition in which damaged airways, damaged air sacs, or both make it hard to breathe out fully. The GOLD 2023 definition

"a heterogeneous lung condition characterized by chronic respiratory symptoms (dyspnea, cough, expectoration, and/or exacerbations) due to abnormalities of the airways (bronchitis, bronchiolitis) and/or alveoli (emphysema) that cause persistent, often progressive, airflow obstruction" [[Agusti2023]]

GOLD 2023 deliberately keeps this definition apart from epidemiology, causes, risk factors and diagnostic criteria, which it discusses on their own [[Agusti2023]]. It describes COPD as the result of dynamic, cumulative and repeated gene–environment interactions over a lifetime that damage the lungs or change how they develop and age, which it calls "GETomics" [[Agusti2023]].

How it is confirmed. Diagnosis needs a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio below 0.7 on forced spirometry, in someone with symptoms (breathlessness, chronic cough or sputum, recurrent lower respiratory infections) and/or exposure to risk factors [[Agusti2023]]. If a single test gives a ratio between 0.60 and 0.80, GOLD 2023 advises repeating spirometry on a separate occasion [[Agusti2023]].

Not specific on its own. Airflow obstruction that does not fully reverse also occurs in asthma and other diseases, so GOLD 2023 says clinical context and risk factors must be considered [[Agusti2023]]. The spirometry module shows the test step by step.

Two faces: airways and air sacs

Airways

Chronic bronchitis and bronchiolitis

The major sites of obstruction are small airways under 2 mm across [[McDonough2011]]. Their walls thicken with inflammatory cells and laid-down connective tissue, and mucous exudate collects in the lumen [[Hogg2004Lancet]].

Chronic bronchitis comes from mucus overproduction by goblet cells. The mucus can plug small airways and make them collapse more easily [[Kim2013]]. In 1962 the ATS defined chronic bronchitis clinically: chronic cough for at least three months in at least two years [[Petty2006]].

Air sacs

Emphysema

The 1962 ATS definition was anatomical: enlarged airspaces with loss of alveolar walls [[Petty2006]]. Centrilobular emphysema, most closely linked to smoking, starts around the respiratory bronchioles; its lesions merge to destroy large volumes of lung [[Hogg2004Lancet]].

Narrowed small airways plus a floppier emphysematous lung make the lung slow to empty [[Hogg2004Lancet]]. Laënnec described such lungs as over-inflated and failing to collapse [[Petty2006]].

Together

Usually both

A 2015 review describes COPD as small-airway obstruction (chronic obstructive bronchiolitis) plus emphysema, which together cause air trapping and breathlessness on exertion [[Barnes2015]]. The balance varies from person to person, which is one reason GOLD calls COPD heterogeneous [[Agusti2023]].

How many people have COPD

In 2019 about 1 in 10 people aged 30–79 worldwide had COPD: 10.3% (95% CI 8.2–12.8) by the GOLD fixed ratio, or 391.9 million people, and 7.6% by the lower limit of normal (LLN), or 292.0 million [[Adeloye2022]]. Of the 391.9 million, 315.5 million (80.5%) lived in low- and middle-income countries [[Adeloye2022]]. These are modelled estimates for populations, not predictions for any one person.

Worldwide, prevalence in people aged 30–79 was more than twice as high in men (14.1%) as in women (6.5%) [[Adeloye2022]]. By the LLN definition, though, it was marginally higher in women (7.4%) than men (7.1%) in low- and middle-income countries [[Adeloye2022]], and GOLD 2023 says prevalence in developed countries is now almost equal in men and women [[Agusti2023]]. At the same airflow limitation, women report more breathlessness and worse health status, and have more exacerbations [[Agusti2023]].

In the BOLD study (12 sites, 9,425 people aged 40 or over), each extra 10 years of age raised the odds of COPD by a pooled factor of 1.94 [[Buist2007]]. A modelling study projects cases among people aged 25 and over rising 23% from 2020 to 2050, to nearly 600 million, with a 47.1% rise in women against 9.4% in men [[Boers2023]].

Who to test

Case finding, not screening

GOLD 2023 does not recommend screening spirometry for people without symptoms or risk factors, but says spirometry should be considered for case finding in people with symptoms and/or risk factors, such as more than 20 pack-years, recurrent chest infections or prematurity [[Agusti2023]].

Deaths and disability

COPD caused 3.3 million deaths and 74.4 million disability-adjusted life years in 2019 [[Safiri2022]]. Deaths rose from 2.5 million in 1990, while the age-standardised death rate (42.5 per 100,000 in 2019) fell by 41.7%: more, older people, but a lower risk at any given age [[Safiri2022]].

About 80% of COPD deaths occur in low- and middle-income countries [[Stolz2022]]. The burden is highest in middle-development settings, not the poorest or the richest: a reversed-V pattern with the sociodemographic index [[Safiri2022]]. In 2017, chronic respiratory diseases were the third leading cause of death, with COPD causing most of them [[GBDCRD2020]].

Despite this toll, the Lancet Commission counted only 780 ongoing therapeutic COPD trials, against more than 41,000 cancer treatment trials [[Stolz2022]].

Many causes: the etiotypes

GOLD 2023 proposes classifying COPD by cause ("etiotypes"), to raise awareness of COPD in people who never smoked and to push research into its mechanisms, prevention and treatment [[Agusti2023]]. The 2022 Lancet Commission proposed five types Early evidence; the Commission concedes some aspects are speculative [[Stolz2022]]. The taxonomy is a proposal, not a validated classification [[Agusti2023,Stolz2022]].

Questions people ask

What is COPD?

A long-term lung condition in which damaged airways and/or air sacs make it hard to breathe out fully. It causes breathlessness, cough, phlegm and flare-ups, and the narrowing does not fully go away [[Agusti2023]].

Can people who never smoked get it?

Yes. Never smokers made up about a quarter of people with moderate or worse COPD in the BOLD study. Other causes include smoke from cooking fires, polluted air, dust and fumes at work, past tuberculosis and problems with lung growth early in life [[Lamprecht2011,Salvi2009,Agusti2023]].

Is it contagious or inherited?

It is not an infection that spreads. A small number of people inherit a strong risk, mainly alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, and smaller gene effects add up in others [[Agusti2023,Stoller2005]].

Does stopping smoking still help?

In the Lung Health Study, people who quit regained some lung function in the first year, then lost it at about half the rate of people who kept smoking. A quit-smoking programme also lowered deaths over 14.5 years [[Scanlon2000,Anthonisen2005]].

Can it be cured?

Lung damage that has already happened does not reverse. Some treatments, including certain medicines, oxygen for low blood oxygen and quitting smoking, have been shown to lower the risk of death in selected people [[Agusti2023,Anthonisen2005,NOTT1980]].

What is the outlook?

It varies a lot. In a large US study, COPD shortened life expectancy only modestly in never smokers with mild-to-moderate disease, and more in smokers with severe disease. These are group averages, not predictions for one person [[Shavelle2009]].

Open questions

Unresolved

Fixed ratio or lower limit of normal?

The fixed ratio gives a global prevalence of 10.3%, the LLN 7.6% [[Adeloye2022]]. GOLD 2023 keeps the fixed ratio for diagnosis [[Agusti2023]]. The choice changes who is counted, especially at older ages.

Method

392 million or 212 million?

The Global Burden of Disease study counted 212.3 million prevalent cases in 2019 [[Safiri2022]], against 391.9 million in the spirometry-based estimate. They use different case definitions and methods: a meta-regression for ages 30–79 versus GBD modelling of all ages [[Adeloye2022,Safiri2022]].

Early evidence

Pre-COPD and PRISm

There is no trial evidence yet on how to treat people with pre-COPD or PRISm [[Agusti2023]].
