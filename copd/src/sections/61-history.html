Part 6 · Context & review

History & people

For three centuries COPD was seen at autopsy as "voluminous lungs". Then came a machine to measure breath, a one-second timing, agreed names, the causes, the first treatment proven to save lives and a global strategy. Scrub the timeline from 1679 to 2023, filter it by theme, or jump straight to a year.

The main history review behind this timeline is Petty's 2006 "The history of COPD" [[Petty2006]]; the story of the one-second test comes from Yernault's 1997 tribute to Robert Tiffeneau [[Yernault1997]]. Laënnec's 1819 treatise and Tiffeneau and Pinelli's 1947 paper are not indexed, so their content is cited through those reviews. The axis is compressed before 1940.

From vital capacity to the first second

John Hutchinson, a surgeon, published his spirometer in 1846 and named the volume exhaled from fully inflated lungs the "vital capacity", the capacity to live [[Hutchinson1846,Petty2002]]. His instrument measured only that volume; it took about 100 more years before airflow over time was added [[Petty2006]]. In December 1947 Tiffeneau and Pinelli in Paris described the maximal volume exhaled in the first second after a deep breath, which they called the CPUE [[Yernault1997]]. 1954 · 1957

CPUE, VEMS, FEV

A group of European experts renamed the CPUE "VEMS" on 13 February 1954, and the English term "forced expiratory volume" was adopted in 1957 [[Yernault1997]].

1950 · 1951

Gaensler in the United States

Gaensler introduced the "air velocity index" in 1950 and the "timed vital capacity" in 1951, foundations of today's FEV1 and FEV1/FVC [[Gaensler1950,Yernault1997,Petty2006]].

Today

The ratio GOLD uses

GOLD 2023 confirms COPD with a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC below 0.7 on forced spirometry [[Agusti2023]].

Two hypotheses

1960

The "Dutch hypothesis"

Orie emphasised inherited bronchial hyperreactivity [[Petty2006]].

Britain

The "British hypothesis"

Emphasised repeated chest infections and air pollution [[Petty2006]]. Fletcher and Peto later found that infections and chronic mucus hypersecretion did not speed up airflow obstruction, and described them as two largely unrelated processes [[Fletcher1977]].

The trials that changed care

YearTrialWhoWhat it found
1980NOTT203 people with low blood oxygenMortality was 1.94 times higher with 12-hour nocturnal oxygen than with continuous oxygen (P = 0.01) [[NOTT1980]].
1981MRC oxygen trial87 people19 of 42 given oxygen for at least 15 hours a day died over 5 years, against 30 of 45 controls [[MRC1981]].
2000Lung Health Study3,926 smokers with mild to moderate obstructionQuitting improved FEV1 by 47 mL in the first year; later decline in sustained quitters was half that of continuing smokers (31 vs 62 mL a year) [[Scanlon2000]].
2003NETT1,218 people with severe emphysemaNo overall survival benefit from lung volume reduction surgery, but lower mortality in upper-lobe emphysema with low exercise capacity (risk ratio 0.47) [[Fishman2003]].
2005Lung Health Study, 14.5 yearsSame cohortAll-cause mortality 8.83 vs 10.38 per 1,000 person-years, cessation group vs usual care [[Anthonisen2005]].
2016LOTT738 people with moderate desaturationNo difference in time to death or first hospitalisation with long-term oxygen (hazard ratio 0.94, 95% CI 0.79–1.12) [[Albert2016]].

In the early 1960s, oxygen was considered contraindicated in COPD and exercise was discouraged for fear of straining the right heart [[Petty2006]]. Within twenty years, NOTT and the MRC trial showed that, in people with low blood oxygen, more hours of oxygen a day went with fewer deaths [[NOTT1980,MRC1981]].

GOLD, from 2001 to 2023

ReportWhat changed
2001The NHLBI/WHO workshop summary, the first GOLD global strategy for diagnosis, management and prevention, with a new severity classification [[Pauwels2001,Petty2006]]. The 2006 history review, following it, defined COPD as chronic airflow obstruction that is progressive and only partly reversible [[Petty2006]].
2017Spirometric grading separated from the ABCD groups, which were now derived only from symptoms and exacerbation history [[Vogelmeier2017]].
2023A new definition and cause-based taxonomy; groups C and D merged into a single E (exacerbations) group, creating the ABE tool [[Agusti2023]].

People to know

1819

Laënnec

Inventor of the stethoscope. He described emphysematous lungs as hyperinflated, failing to collapse and not emptying well, often with mucus-filled bronchi: an early account of emphysema and chronic bronchitis together [[Petty2006]].

1846

John Hutchinson

A surgeon who invented the spirometer and named the "vital capacity" [[Hutchinson1846,Petty2002]].

1947

Robert Tiffeneau and Pinelli

Described the forced expiratory manoeuvre in Paris: the volume exhaled in one second after a deep breath [[Yernault1997]].

1954

Doll and Hill

Their 1954 preliminary report linked doctors' smoking habits to their mortality; the British Doctors Study ran from 1951 to 2001 in 34,439 men [[Doll1954,Doll2004]].

1960

Reid

Proposed measuring the bronchial mucous gland layer, later called the Reid index, as a diagnostic yardstick in chronic bronchitis [[Reid1960,Petty2006]].

1963

Laurell and Eriksson

In Sweden, noticed the missing α1-globulin band on serum electrophoresis, described alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency and linked it to emphysema [[Laurell1963,Perciaccante2018]]. With Gross's 1964 papain experiments, this founded the protease–antiprotease theory [[Petty2006]].

1966

Burrows, Fletcher and Briscoe

Burrows and Fletcher presented the emphysematous and bronchial types of chronic airways obstruction seen in London and Chicago at the 9th Aspen Emphysema Conference, published in 1966; William Briscoe is believed to have first used the term COPD there [[Petty2006,Burrows1966]].

1977

Fletcher and Peto

Their study of London working men described the natural history of chronic airflow obstruction and showed the value of quitting in susceptible smokers [[Fletcher1977]]. The Lancet Commission argues their picture of COPD as a smoker's disease of fast decline is now undermined by non-smoking causes and by people who reach COPD without fast decline [[Stolz2022,Lange2015]].
