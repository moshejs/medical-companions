Part 2 · Diagnosis & assessment

Spirometry & the FEV1/FVC ratio

COPD is confirmed by one breathing test: a forced blow into a spirometer, before and after a reliever inhaler. Watch a single blow build the two curves, switch between a healthy blow and one narrowed by COPD, then work a ratio and a GOLD grade yourself.

The rule GOLD uses

GOLD 2023 describes COPD as a heterogeneous lung condition with chronic respiratory symptoms (breathlessness, cough, sputum and/or exacerbations) caused by abnormalities of the airways and/or the air sacs, which cause persistent, often progressive, airflow obstruction [[Agusti2023]]. It says a diagnosis should be considered in anyone with breathlessness, chronic cough or sputum, recurrent lower respiratory tract infections and/or exposure to risk factors, and that a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio below 0.7 "is needed to establish the diagnosis" [[Agusti2023]].

The test result is not enough on its own. Airflow obstruction that is not fully reversible also occurs in asthma and other diseases, so GOLD asks for the clinical context and risk factors to be weighed too [[Agusti2023]]. How COPD is told apart from its look-alikes is in .

Repeat if borderline

A ratio between 0.60 and 0.80

If a single post-bronchodilator ratio falls between 0.60 and 0.80, GOLD 2023 advises repeating spirometry on a separate occasion, because biological variation can move the ratio. An initial ratio below 0.6 is "very unlikely" to rise spontaneously above 0.7 [[Agusti2023]].

Reversibility

Not used to guide treatment

GOLD 2023 no longer uses the degree of reversibility to guide treatment: it varies over time in the same person, does not separate COPD from asthma and does not predict long-term response to bronchodilators or corticosteroids. The one exception: airflow limitation that disappears after bronchodilator is incompatible with COPD [[Agusti2023]].

Follow-up tests

No need to stop inhalers first

Because reversibility is not used for treatment decisions, GOLD 2023 says it is neither necessary nor advised to stop inhaled medicines before follow-up spirometry [[Agusti2023]]. For a first diagnostic test, the 2019 standard suggests withholding times (table below) [[Graham2019]].

Try it: ratio, fixed ratio or LLN, and the GOLD grade

Teaching toolThis works through the published arithmetic only: the ratio, GOLD's fixed 0.70 threshold and the GOLD 2023 grade cut-offs (FEV1 ≥ 80%, 50–79%, 30–49% and < 30% predicted) [[Agusti2023,Lin2025]]. It is not for diagnosis or treatment decisions. A diagnosis needs post-bronchodilator spirometry of acceptable quality, the person's symptoms and exposures, and a clinician's judgement.

Default values: the UPLIFT mean after bronchodilator (1.33 L and 3.04 L, derived) [[Tashkin2008]] and a percent predicted derived from UPLIFT's 39.3% before bronchodilator.

Fixed ratio or lower limit of normal?

GOLD prefers the fixed ratio below 0.7 because it is simple, needs no reference values and was used in all the clinical trials behind its treatment recommendations. It also acknowledges that, compared with the lower limit of normal (LLN), the fixed ratio may underdiagnose young adults and overdiagnose the elderly, especially in mild disease [[Agusti2023]]. The LLN is the bottom 5% of a healthy reference population, so it depends on the reference equation chosen, and GOLD notes there are no longitudinal studies validating its use [[Agusti2023,Bhakta2023]].

Overdiagnosis

Healthy older never-smokers

Over 60, the fixed ratio labelled 17–45% of healthy never-smoking men and 7–26% of women as obstructed, and applied to whole populations it gave false-positive rates of up to 60% [[Swanney2008]]. In Bergen, about 35% of 71 healthy never-smokers over 70 had a ratio below 70%, and about 50% above 80 [[Hardie2002]].

Outcomes

But the fixed ratio predicts events

In the NHLBI Pooled Cohorts (24,207 adults followed for a median 15 years; 3,925 COPD-related hospitalisations or deaths), the best-discriminating fixed threshold was 0.71 (C statistic 0.696). It was no different from 0.70 and more accurate than the GLI LLN [[Bhatt2019]]. GOLD Science Committee members cite this as showing the 0.70 ratio predicts hospitalisation and death as well as, or better than, the LLN [[Han2021,Bhatt2019]].

Status

Unresolved

GOLD keeps the fixed ratio for simplicity and continuity with the trials, and acknowledges the age bias [[Agusti2023]]. Researchers who favour the LLN point to the false positives in healthy older people [[Swanney2008,Hardie2002]]. Which threshold serves people better remains debated.

How a good test is done

The 2019 ATS/ERS standard sets what counts as an acceptable blow and how a session is graded. The aim is at least three acceptable FEV1 and three acceptable FVC measurements, before and after bronchodilator. The largest FEV1 and the largest FVC from all acceptable blows are reported, even if they come from different blows, and the ratio is reported to two decimal places [[Graham2019]]. Eight blows is "generally a practical upper limit" for adults, and testing should stop if FEV1 falls below 80% of its starting value during repeated attempts [[Graham2019]].

"Predicted" for whom? Reference equations and z-scores

GLI 2012

One equation for ages 3–95

Built from 97,759 records of healthy non-smokers aged 2.5–95 from 72 centres in 33 countries, with continuous, age-dependent LLNs. Ethnic groups differed in FEV1 and FVC but proportionally, so FEV1/FVC was "virtually independent" of ethnic group [[Quanjer2012]]. Earlier US values (NHANES III) came from 7,429 asymptomatic lifelong non-smokers aged 8–80 [[Hankinson1999]].

GLI Global, 2022

Race-neutral equations

GLI Global reanalysed 74,185 GLI records with inverse-probability weights. It fits about as well as the GLI 2012 "other" equations, with wider limits of normal [[Bowerman2023]]. A 2023 official ATS statement recommends replacing race- and ethnicity-specific equations with race-neutral averages, together with a re-evaluation of how lung-function tests are used in clinical, employment and insurance decisions, and a caution about uncertain effects [[Bhakta2023]].

Worked example

Same blow, two answers

For a 40-year-old man 6 feet tall, GLI White predicts an FEV1 of 4.50 L (5th percentile 3.56 L) and GLI African American 3.84 L (5th percentile 2.95 L). A measured FEV1 of 3.50 L is 91% predicted and above the LLN on one, but 78% predicted and below the LLN on the other [[Bhakta2023]].

What switching changed

Obstruction steady, severity shifted

In 8,431 people at one US centre, switching to GLI Global did not significantly change the prevalence of obstruction (Black individuals 19.9% vs 19.5%; White 23.9% vs 25.1%). Restriction in Black individuals rose from 26.8% to 37.5%, and severity increased in 22.8% of Black individuals [[Moffett2023]]. Effects on care are not yet known Hypothesis [[Moffett2023,Bowerman2023]].

QuestionWhat the studies found
Who counts as "responsive"?It depends on the rule. In UPLIFT, 53.9% met ≥ 12% and ≥ 200 mL, 65.6% met ≥ 15%, and 38.6% met a ≥ 10% absolute rise in FEV1 % predicted [[Tashkin2008]].
Does it change with severity?In ECLIPSE the absolute FEV1 response fell with severity: GOLD II 0.16 L, III 0.10 L, IV 0.05 L [[Albert2012]].
Is it stable?No. In ECLIPSE, reversibility status changed between four tests over a year, and consistently reversible patients (n = 227) did not differ in mortality, hospitalisation or exacerbations once baseline FEV1 was allowed for [[Albert2012]].
The current definitionThe 2021–2022 ERS/ATS interpretation standard defines a positive response as a rise in FEV1 or FVC of more than 10% of the predicted value, replacing the 2005 criterion of ≥ 12% and > 200 mL from baseline [[Zhao2025,Stanojevic2022]]. Expressing the change as a share of predicted, or as a z-score, avoids sex and height bias [[Graham2019]].

What the grade does and does not tell you

GOLD 1–4

Severity of airflow obstruction

Once the post-bronchodilator ratio is below 0.7, FEV1 % predicted sets the grade: GOLD 1 mild (≥ 80%), GOLD 2 moderate (50–79%), GOLD 3 severe (30–49%) and GOLD 4 very severe (< 30%) [[Lin2025,Agusti2023]].

Symptoms

FEV1 tracks symptoms loosely

In 1,817 European primary-care patients, mean CAT scores were 16.2 (GOLD I), 16.3 (II), 19.3 (III) and 22.3 (IV); stages I and II did not differ (p = 0.88) [[Jones2011]]. In 100 rehabilitation patients, FEV1 was not associated with MRC breathlessness grade [[Bestall1999]].

Survival

Breathlessness predicted better

In 227 Japanese patients followed for 5 years, FEV1-based staging did not significantly predict survival (p = 0.08), but the level of breathlessness did (p < 0.001) [[Nishimura2002]]. This is why GOLD assesses symptoms and flare-ups separately: .

In plain words

To check for COPD, you blow out as hard and as long as you can into a machine called a spirometer, before and after a puff of reliever medicine [[Graham2019,Agusti2023]].

The test compares how much air you blow out in the first second with how much you blow out in total. If the first second is less than 70% of the total after the medicine, your airways are narrowed [[Agusti2023]].

How much you blow out in that first second, compared with someone like you, gives a grade from 1 (mild) to 4 (very severe) [[Lin2025]].
