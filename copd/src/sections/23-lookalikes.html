Asthma and asthma–COPD overlap

Mislabelled

Half had been called asthma

In 597 primary-care patients aged 40 or over in Aberdeen and Denver, 235 met the spirometric definition of COPD. Of these, 51.5% had previously been labelled asthma only, 37.9% chronic bronchitis or emphysema, and 10.6% had no diagnosis [[Tinkelman2006]].

Overlap

About a quarter of people with COPD

A 2015 meta-analysis of 19 studies found asthma–COPD overlap in 27% of people with COPD in population studies and 28% in hospital studies. Overlap patients were younger, had higher BMI and more healthcare use, and worse quality of life [[Alshabanat2015]]. GOLD 2023 says people with COPD and asthma together should be treated as if they have asthma [[Agusti2023]].

Childhood

Early lungs leave a mark

GOLD 2023 notes that abnormal lung development in childhood can cause asthma-like symptoms, so some children labelled "asthma" may have been on a low lung-function trajectory [[Agusti2023]]. The trajectory chart below shows why that matters.

Bronchiectasis and heart failure

ROSE

When COPD and bronchiectasis coexist

The EMBARC Delphi definition needs all four: Radiology (bronchial dilatation in ≥ 1 segment and > 1 lobe), Obstruction (FEV1/FVC < 0.7), Symptoms (at least two of cough, expectoration, breathlessness, fatigue and frequent infections) and Exposure (≥ 10 pack-years or other toxic agents such as biomass) [[Traversi2021]].

Registry

Labels that do not fit

Of 16,730 people in the European Bronchiectasis Registry, 4,336 carried a COPD co-diagnosis. 22.2% of them had no airflow obstruction and 31.9% lacked ≥ 10 pack-years, so only 55.4% met ROSE [[Polverino2024]]. An international consensus holds that clinically significant bronchiectasis needs both clinical and radiological criteria [[Aliberti2022]].

Heart failure

One in five, unrecognised

Of 405 people aged 65 or over with a GP diagnosis of COPD, 83 (20.5%) had previously unrecognised heart failure (42 systolic, 41 isolated diastolic), and only 244 (60.2%) actually met GOLD COPD criteria [[Rutten2005]]. A 2009 review advises echocardiography and lung function tests in people with both, with airflow obstruction shown when euvolaemic; very high or very low natriuretic peptides are informative, intermediate values are not [[Hawkins2009]].

What a CT scan can show

A chest X-ray cannot confirm COPD. It may show hyperinflation (a flattened diaphragm, more air behind the breastbone), hyperlucency and rapidly tapering vessels, and it helps exclude alternatives such as fibrosis, bronchiectasis, pleural disease, kyphoscoliosis and an enlarged heart [[Agusti2023]]. GOLD 2023 recommends chest CT for people with persistent exacerbations, symptoms out of proportion to airflow limitation, severe obstruction with significant hyperinflation and gas trapping, or who meet lung cancer screening criteria [[Agusti2023]].

What CT adds

More than emphysema

On CT, emphysema extent bears on lung volume reduction and is associated with faster FEV1 decline, higher mortality and lung cancer risk. About 30% of COPD patients have bronchiectasis on CT, and CT also shows coronary calcification, pulmonary artery enlargement, bone density and muscle mass [[Agusti2023]].

MESA

Emphysema in smokers with normal tests

In 318 smokers aged 50–79 with at least 10 pack-years, 27% had emphysema on CT, and 17% of smokers with normal spirometry had emphysema. Centrilobular and panlobular emphysema came with more breathlessness, shorter walks, more hyperinflation and lower diffusing capacity [[Smith2014]].

PA:A ratio

A wide pulmonary artery

A pulmonary artery to aorta diameter ratio above 1 was associated with future severe exacerbations (OR 3.44 in the trial cohort, 2.80 in an external validation cohort), the strongest association among all variables analysed [[Wells2012]].

PRISm and pre-COPD over a lifetime

GOLD 2023 defines PRISm (preserved ratio impaired spirometry) as a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ≥ 0.7 with FEV1 below 80% of reference. Reported prevalence is 7.1–20.3%, higher in current and former smokers; it is associated with more all-cause mortality and can move to normal, obstructive or restrictive spirometry over time [[Agusti2023]]. Pre-COPD is GOLD's term for people of any age with respiratory symptoms and/or structural changes (such as emphysema) or functional changes (hyperinflation, reduced diffusing capacity, rapid FEV1 decline) without airflow obstruction. GOLD says they should already be considered patients, but there is no evidence yet on the best treatment [[Agusti2023]].

Symptoms, normal test

Smokers with preserved spirometry

In SPIROMICS (2,736 participants), 50% of current or former smokers with preserved spirometry had symptoms (CAT ≥ 10). They had more exacerbations than asymptomatic smokers (0.27 vs 0.08 per year), and 42% used bronchodilators and 23% inhaled glucocorticoids without an evidence base [[Woodruff2016]].

"GOLD 0"

Not as normal as it looks

In COPDGene, 54.1% of smokers with normal spirometry (n = 4,388) had one or more respiratory impairments, and 42.3% of 300 with CT had emphysema or airway thickening [[Regan2015]]. GOLD 2001's "at-risk" stage 0 was dropped because not all such people progressed to COPD [[Han2021]].

Words matter

Early, mild, young

GOLD 2023 keeps "early COPD" for the biological first steps of disease studied experimentally, "mild" for spirometric severity only, and "young COPD" for people aged 20–50 [[Agusti2023]]. Whether pre-COPD and PRISm should be treated is a research question; labelling them risks giving disease status to people who may never progress [[Han2021,Agusti2023]] Hypothesis.

Wrong label, both ways

Underdiagnosis

81.4% undiagnosed

In BOLD and other surveys (30,874 adults aged 40 or over, 27 countries), 81.4% of spirometrically defined COPD was undiagnosed [[Lamprecht2015]].

Overdiagnosis

61.9% unobstructed

In BOLD, 61.9% of 919 people reporting a doctor's diagnosis of COPD had unobstructed post-bronchodilator spirometry (55.3% by the fixed ratio), and 45.7% of these were using respiratory medicines [[Sator2019]]. A 2018 review estimates that 30–60% of people with a physician diagnosis of COPD do not have it [[Diab2018]].

In plain words

Other conditions can feel like COPD, including asthma, widened airways (bronchiectasis) and heart failure, so a breathing test and sometimes a heart scan or a CT scan help sort them out [[Agusti2023,Rutten2005]].

Having a normal breathing test does not always mean your lungs are fine if you smoke or have symptoms. Doctors now talk about "pre-COPD" and "PRISm" for these in-between states [[Agusti2023]].

Many people with COPD do not know they have it, and some people told they have COPD turn out to have normal breathing tests [[Lamprecht2015,Sator2019]].
