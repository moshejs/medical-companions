Part 4 · Flare-ups & hospital

Hospital treatment

What guidelines describe for a flare-up treated in hospital, hour by hour and then day by day: controlled oxygen and a blood gas, bronchodilators, a short steroid course, antibiotics when the sputum changes, non-invasive ventilation when the blood turns acidic, and a plan for going home. Get helpGOLD 2023 lists reasons for hospital care: severe symptoms such as sudden worsening of breathlessness at rest, a high breathing rate, low oxygen saturation, confusion or drowsiness; acute respiratory failure; new signs such as blue lips (cyanosis) or swollen ankles; a flare-up that does not respond to first treatment; serious other illness such as heart failure or a new irregular heartbeat; and too little support at home. [[Agusti2023]] Anyone with these signs needs urgent medical help: in an emergency, call your local emergency number, and show any oxygen alert card to the ambulance crew. [[ODriscoll2017Summary]]

Early evidence

Steroids guided by eosinophils

CORTICO-COP (318 inpatients) gave prednisolone only on days with blood eosinophils ≥0.3 × 10⁹/L: days alive and out of hospital 8.9 against 9.3 (non-inferior), with a median of 2 against 5 steroid days. [[Sivapalan2019]] In an earlier pilot, flare-ups with eosinophils ≤2% failed treatment in 15% on prednisolone against 2% on placebo. [[Bafadhel2012]] GOLD 2023 says only that steroids "may be less efficacious" with lower eosinophils. [[Agusti2023]]

Open question

Antibiotics on the ward

In non-ICU inpatients the Cochrane estimate for current antibiotics was not significant (RR 0.65, 95% CI 0.38–1.12), while GOLD recommends them when sputum is purulent. [[Vollenweider2018,Agusti2023]]

ICU

Ventilated patients

In the one ICU trial (93 ventilated patients), ofloxacin 400 mg daily for 10 days cut in-hospital deaths from 22% to 4%. [[Nouira2001]] Cochrane: treatment failure RR 0.19. [[Vollenweider2018]]

Try it: the DECAF score

Teaching toolDECAF predicts death in hospital from five findings on admission. It is shown here as published so you can see how the points add up; it is not for diagnosis or treatment decisions, and it cannot predict what happens to any one person. [[Steer2012,Echevarria2016]]

Validation cohort of 1725 patients; AUROC 0.83 internally and 0.82 externally. Score 6 had only 2 patients. The authors suggest DECAF 0–1 identifies low-risk patients who may suit hospital at home or early supported discharge, and 3–6 high-risk patients for escalation planning or early palliation. [[Echevarria2016]] In the 920-patient derivation cohort, 10.4% died and AUROC was 0.86. [[Steer2012]]

Non-invasive ventilation in practice

NIV is breathing support given through a mask rather than a tube in the windpipe: it can help people breathe and often avoids a breathing tube. [[Osadnik2017]] GOLD 2023 calls it "the preferred initial mode of ventilation", and once patients tolerate at least 4 hours of unassisted breathing it can be stopped without weaning. [[Agusti2023]] ERS/ATS 2017 made a strong recommendation for NIV in acute or acute-on-chronic respiratory failure in COPD flare-ups. [[Wedzicha2017,Rochwerg2017]]

How common

About 1 in 5 flare-ups

Acute hypercapnic respiratory failure complicates around 20% of COPD flare-ups; about 12% of those patients died in hospital, and 33% if the acidosis developed after admission. [[Davidson2016Summary]] A UK audit of 9716 admissions found 20% acidotic on arrival and 6% more later. [[Roberts2011]]

Audit gap

Real-world NIV

In that audit, hospital mortality was 25% on NIV and 39% when acidosis developed late, and 30% of patients with persisting respiratory acidosis did not get NIV. 55% of the 1077 NIV patients had pH <7.26. [[Roberts2011]]

Failure risk

Low pH, high risk

In 1033 patients, NIV failure risk was above 70% with GCS <11, APACHE II ≥29, breathing rate ≥30 and admission pH <7.25; pH <7.25 after 2 hours of NIV raised it above 90%. [[Confalonieri2005]] Improving acidosis and a falling breathing rate at 4 h predicted success. [[Plant2001]]

By acidosis

90-day mortality

European COPD Audit (16,016 admissions), as reported in a UK review: 9.1% when pH >7.35, 16.1% at 7.25–7.35 and 28.1% below 7.25. [[Trethewey2018,Hartl2016]] Spirometry results were found for only 49.7% of admissions. [[Hartl2016]]

Going home

GOLD 2023

Before discharge

Good practice covers inhaler education, home support and a follow-up plan. Follow-up within one month is linked to fewer readmissions, and again at three months. Early readmission in the first 90 days is frequent; risk factors are other illnesses, previous flare-ups and admissions, and a longer stay. [[Agusti2023]]

Bundles

RR 0.80 for readmission

14 studies of COPD discharge bundles: pooled readmission RR 0.80 (95% CI 0.65–0.99), from four trials at moderate-to-high risk of bias, with no clear effect on mortality or quality of life. [[Ospina2017]] One UK team built its bundle from guidelines, expert opinion and patient consultation. [[Hopkinson2012]]

After high CO2

The BTS/ICS checklist

Early specialist review, pulmonary rehabilitation, help to stop smoking, future care planning, a warning card or ambulance alert for controlled oxygen, and consideration of home NIV. [[Davidson2016Summary]]

Rehabilitation

OR 0.44 for readmission

Cochrane (20 trials): rehabilitation after a flare-up cut readmissions (OR 0.44, 95% CI 0.21–0.91; heterogeneous) and improved SGRQ by 7.80 points. [[Puhan2016]] ERS/ATS 2017 suggests starting within 3 weeks of discharge, not during the stay [[Wedzicha2017]]; GOLD 2023 calls the effects of starting in the first 4 weeks unclear. [[Agusti2023]]

Home NIV

HOT-HMV

116 people still with PaCO2 >53 mmHg 2–4 weeks after acidosis resolved: home NIV plus oxygen lengthened median time to readmission or death from 1.4 to 4.3 months (adjusted HR 0.49). [[Murphy2017]]

Hospital at home

RR 0.76 for readmission

Cochrane (8 trials, 870 patients): fewer readmissions than inpatient care; the mortality trend (RR 0.65, 0.40–1.04) was not significant. The trials excluded people with pH <7.35. [[Jeppesen2012]]
