Pulmonary rehabilitation

Pulmonary rehabilitation is a programme of exercise training, education and behaviour change, tailored after an assessment. Play the cycle to see what it is meant to interrupt and what the trials measured, then explore the effect sizes and the question of timing after a hospital stay.

What a programme is, and who is offered it

The ATS/ERS 2013 statement defines pulmonary rehabilitation as "a comprehensive intervention based on a thorough patient assessment followed by patient-tailored therapies that include, but are not limited to, exercise training, education, and behavior change, designed to improve the physical and psychological condition of people with [chronic respiratory disease] and to promote the long-term adherence to health-enhancing behaviors" [[Spruit2013,Rochester2023]].

The 2023 ATS guideline makes a strong recommendation for pulmonary rehabilitation in adults with stable COPD and after hospitalisation for an exacerbation (moderate-quality evidence for both), and a strong recommendation to offer a choice of centre-based rehabilitation or telerehabilitation [[Rochester2023]]. GOLD 2023 states that people with high symptom burden and exacerbation risk (groups B and E) "should be recommended to take part in a formal PR program" [[Agusti2023]].

< 5%of people with COPD who may benefit receive rehabilitation (ATS 2023) [[Rochester2023]]
1.5%of 197,376 Medicare patients started within 90 days of a COPD admission [[Lindenauer2020]]
65 RCTsin the 2015 Cochrane review; most programmes ran 8 or 12 weeks [[McCarthy2015]]

The Cochrane authors concluded that "additional RCTs comparing pulmonary rehabilitation and conventional care in COPD are not warranted" [[McCarthy2015]]. Exercise-only programmes and programmes with more complex add-ons did not differ significantly; hospital-based programmes showed larger CRQ effects than community ones, which the authors warn may be confounded [[McCarthy2015]].

Home, telehealth and keeping the gains

Home-based

One visit plus phone calls

In a 166-person equivalence trial, home rehabilitation (one home visit and seven weekly physiotherapist phone calls) was non-inferior to centre-based rehabilitation for 6-minute walk distance at the end of the programme (difference +18.6 m favouring home). "Neither group maintained postrehabilitation gains at 12 months" [[Holland2017]].

Telerehabilitation

Similar gains, more finish

The 2021 Cochrane review (15 studies, 1904 participants, 99% with COPD) found probably little or no difference from centre-based rehabilitation in 6MWD (MD 0.06 m, 95% CI −10.82 to 10.94; moderate certainty). 93% completed telerehabilitation versus 70% for centre-based programmes [[Cox2021Cochrane]].

One equivalence trial

Breathlessness not confirmed

In 142 people, telerehabilitation was equivalent to centre-based rehabilitation for 6MWD (MD −6 m), but equivalence for CRQ dyspnoea could not be confirmed (MD −1 point, 95% CI −3 to 1). The authors still describe it as safe with clinically meaningful benefits [[Cox2022]]. GOLD 2023 says tele-rehabilitation "is safe and has similar benefits to those of center-based PR" [[Agusti2023]].

Maintenance

After the first programme

The 2023 ATS guideline makes a conditional recommendation for either supervised maintenance or usual care after the first programme (low-quality evidence) [[Rochester2023]]. The ATS/ERS statement highlights health behaviour change as the way to keep the benefits [[Spruit2013]].
