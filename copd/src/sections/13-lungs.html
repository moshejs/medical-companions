Inside the lungs

COPD damages two places: the small airways, which narrow, clog and then vanish, and the air sacs, whose walls break down in emphysema. Zoom from the whole lung into one small airway and on to the air sacs, step by step, and watch which cells do the damage.

Two lesions, one disease

GOLD 2023 defines COPD as “a heterogeneous lung condition” with chronic respiratory symptoms caused by abnormalities of the airways (bronchitis, bronchiolitis) and/or the alveoli (emphysema) that cause persistent, often progressive, airflow obstruction. It describes COPD as the result of gene–environment interactions over a lifetime that damage the lungs or change their normal development or ageing. [[Agusti2023]]

A 2015 Nature Reviews primer describes COPD as obstruction of the small airways (chronic obstructive bronchiolitis) plus emphysema, which together cause air trapping and breathlessness on exertion. The inflammation is usually corticosteroid resistant, and the disease involves accelerated lung ageing and abnormal repair. Established [[Barnes2015]] Hogg explains the airflow limitation as a lung that takes longer to empty: narrowed small airways add resistance, and emphysema makes the lung more compliant. [[Hogg2004Lancet]]

Not every smoker develops COPD, and why some people are susceptible is not fully explained. [[Barnes2015]] In the London working-men study, most non-smokers and many smokers never developed clinically significant obstruction. [[Fletcher1977]]

Small airways

Narrowed, then lost

On CT, airways 2.0–2.5 mm across were fewer than in controls at GOLD 1 (P=0.001), GOLD 2 (P=0.02) and GOLD 3–4 (P<0.001). In GOLD 4 lungs, the total cross-sectional area of terminal bronchioles was 81–99.7% smaller. [[McDonough2011]] Surviving small airways had thickened walls and narrowed lumens. [[Koo2018]]

Emphysema

Walls between air sacs break down

The Fleischner Society classes CT emphysema as centrilobular, panlobular and paraseptal, and lists airway-wall thickening, small airways disease, pulmonary arterial enlargement and bronchiectasis as further CT features. [[Lynch2015]] Comparing CT with lung morphometry, −950 HU was the only threshold whose relative area matched measured emphysema. [[Gevenois1995]]

Chronic bronchitis

Too much mucus

Mucus overproduction by goblet cells plugs small airways, remodels the epithelium and makes airways collapse more easily. It goes with faster lung-function decline, more infections, more exacerbations and higher mortality, and most patients lie between “pure” emphysema and “pure” chronic bronchitis. [[Kim2013]]

An open puzzle

Which comes first?

Narrowing and loss of terminal bronchioles came before emphysematous destruction in resected lungs (P<0.001). [[McDonough2011]] Small-airway thickening happens close to tissue undergoing destruction, and Hogg and Timens call the mechanism behind this pairing “a puzzle” still to be solved. Mucus: more than a nuisance

COPDGene: airway-blocking mucus plugs on CT, 4,363 people Established

After adjustment, including for FEV1 and CT emphysema, plugs in 1–2 segments carried a hazard ratio for death of 1.15, and in 3 or more segments 1.24. [[Diaz2023]]

Mucin in sputum

In SPIROMICS, total sputum mucin was 3,166 µg/mL in smokers with severe COPD and 1,515 µg/mL in never-smoking controls; 4,194 µg/mL with two or more exacerbations a year and 2,458 µg/mL with none. MUC5B was about 3 times and MUC5AC about 10 times never-smoker levels. [[Kesimer2017]]

Mucus and lung-function decline

In the Copenhagen City Heart Study, mucus hypersecretion at both surveys went with an excess FEV1 decline of 22.8 mL/yr in men and 12.6 mL/yr in women, and an adjusted relative risk of COPD hospitalisation of 5.3 and 5.1. [[Vestbo1996]] In the 1946 British birth cohort, hypersecretion usually resolved after quitting. [[Allinson2016]]

Why the damage keeps going

Oxidants

Oxidative stress

Antioxidant capacity is reduced by smoking and exacerbations, and oxidative stress persists after quitting. Antioxidant strategies have so far failed in trials, and the authors argue no single “magic bullet” exists. [[Kirkham2013]]

Steroid resistance

A switched-off brake

Barnes links oxidative stress to NF-κB activation, weaker antiprotease defences, DNA damage, cellular senescence and corticosteroid resistance through inactivation of histone deacetylase 2. [[Barnes2016]] HDAC activity in peripheral lung fell step by step with GOLD stage, while IL-8 rose. [[Ito2005]]

Vessels too

Early changes in the arteries

Pulmonary arteries in mild COPD relaxed less and carried a T-cell infiltrate, which tracked intimal thickness. Early evidence [[Peinado1999]] Some vascular changes are present even in smokers with normal lung function. [[Barbera2003]]

Not one pattern

Eosinophils and non-smokers

A subset of patients has eosinophilic inflammation and may respond better to corticosteroids. [[Barnes2016]] GOLD 2023 states that COPD in non-smokers shows more small-airway obstruction, less emphysema, fewer sputum neutrophils and more sputum eosinophils than COPD in smokers. [[Agusti2023]]
