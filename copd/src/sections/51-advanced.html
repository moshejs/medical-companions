NETT subgroup explorer

NETT randomised 1218 people with severe emphysema, all after pulmonary rehabilitation, to lung volume reduction surgery or continued medical treatment. Overall mortality was the same in both groups (0.11 deaths per person-year), but the effect depended on where the emphysema was and how much exercise people could do [[Fishman2003]]. NETT at 24 months

Exercise capacity

Exercise capacity improved by more than 10 W in 15% of the surgery group versus 3% of the medical group. A high-risk subgroup of 140 patients was identified at an interim analysis [[Fishman2003]].

NETT long-term

Durable benefit

At a median 4.3 years, intention-to-treat analysis showed an overall survival advantage for surgery (5-year risk ratio 0.86, P = 0.02); upper-lobe, low-exercise patients had a 5-year risk ratio of 0.67. The authors concluded that "Effects of LVRS are durable" [[Naunheim2006]].

GOLD 2023

Who is considered

In selected patients with heterogeneous or homogeneous emphysema and significant hyperinflation refractory to optimised care, surgical or bronchoscopic lung volume reduction "may be considered"; bullectomy is an option for a large bulla. Homogeneous means a < 10% difference in destruction between the target and neighbouring lobe on quantitative CT [[Agusti2023]].

The FDA approved the Zephyr Endobronchial Valve System on 29 June 2018 for "adult patients with hyperinflation associated with severe emphysema in regions of the lung that have little to no collateral ventilation" [[FDA-Zephyr]], and the Spiration Valve System on 3 December 2018 for regions "that have low collateral ventilation" [[FDA-Spiration]].

Earlier valve trials had modest results because collateral ventilation from neighbouring lobes stopped the target lobe from collapsing [[Davey2015]]. Later trials selected people with intact fissures or no collateral ventilation on measurement [[Klooster2015,Kemp2017,Valipour2016]].

ISHLT 2021 · referral

When to talk to a transplant centre

The consensus suggests referral with BODE 5–6 and FEV1 20–25% predicted, a BODE increase of more than 1 over 24 months, or a pulmonary artery-to-aorta diameter ratio > 1 on CT [[Leard2021]].

ISHLT 2021 · listing

When to list

BODE 7–10 identifies people whose predicted survival without transplant is below the observed 6.0-year median after transplant for COPD. FEV1 < 20% predicted is an additional consideration; pulmonary hypertension, chronic hypercapnia and severe exacerbations may also influence listing [[Leard2021]].

A caution on BODE

It overestimated risk

BODE is "the most familiar" model, but in US transplant candidates median survival in BODE quartile 4 was 59 months, against 37 months in the original BODE cohort [[Leard2021]]. Bilateral transplant gave little benefit over single for people aged 60 or older (HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.81–1.13) [[Thabut2008]].

SupportBeing assessed for transplant, or deciding not to pursue it, is a big decision for people and their families. Questions and worries about it are a normal part of the conversation with the care team.

Alpha-1 antitrypsin augmentation

Established

RAPID: slower density loss

In 180 non-smokers with severe deficiency (serum < 11 μM) and FEV1 35–70% predicted, weekly intravenous A1PI 60 mg/kg for 24 months slowed CT lung-density loss at TLC (−1.45 vs −2.19 g/L per year, P = 0.03). The combined TLC+FRC endpoint missed significance (P = 0.06) [[Chapman2015]].

Established

RAPID-OLE: lost density stays lost

People who switched from placebo slowed from −2.26 to −1.26 g/L per year; early starters stayed near −1.5 to −1.6 through month 48. "Lost lung density was never recovered" [[McElvaney2017]].

Early evidence

Clinical outcomes

EXACTLE (77 PiZ subjects) found a trend toward slower density loss and no change in exacerbation frequency [[Dirksen2009]]. No trial has shown a mortality or exacerbation benefit [[Chapman2015,Dirksen2009]]. The genetics are in Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency.

Palliative care and breathlessness

GOLD 2023 states: "All patients with advanced COPD should be considered for end-of-life and palliative care support to optimize symptom control and allow patients and their families to make informed choices about future management" [[Agusti2023]].

Breathlessness service

A combined team

In 105 people with advanced disease and refractory breathlessness, a service combining palliative care, respiratory medicine, physiotherapy and occupational therapy improved breathlessness mastery (MD 0.58, 95% CI 0.01–1.15) [[Higginson2014]].

Early evidence Six-month survival was 94% (50/53) versus 75% (39/52), a safety finding the authors say "requires further investigation" [[Higginson2014]].

Handheld fan

Air on the face

A handheld fan directed at the face for 5 minutes reduced breathlessness compared with directing it at the legs (P = 0.003; 50 participants, crossover) [[Galbraith2010]].

Oxygen

Not better than air without low oxygen

In 239 people with refractory breathlessness and PaO2 > 7.3 kPa, oxygen at 2 L/min gave no more relief than room air through the same concentrator (−0.9 vs −0.7 points; P = 0.504) [[Abernethy2010]].
