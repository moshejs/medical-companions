Stopping smoking & vaccines

In the Lung Health Study, ipratropium gave a small FEV1 benefit that disappeared when the inhaler was stopped; it "did not influence the long-term decline of FEV1". The benefit came from quitting [[Anthonisen1994]]. Respiratory symptoms did not predict who would gain: smokers with airflow obstruction benefited "despite previous heavy smoking, advanced age, poor baseline lung function, or airway hyperresponsiveness" [[Scanlon2000]].

GOLD 2023 states that "Approximately 40% of people with COPD continue to smoke despite knowing that they have the disease", and that "All patients who continue to smoke should be offered help and treatment to quit" [[Agusti2023]]. An ERS statement adds that many people with lung disease are not given specific advice or referred for intensive treatment, and so continue smoking [[JimenezRuiz2015]].

Label · CHANTIX

Varenicline dosing as published

The Pfizer label states: start one week before the quit date, or quit between days 8 and 35; 0.5 mg once daily on days 1–3, 0.5 mg twice daily on days 4–7, then 1 mg twice daily, for a total of 12 weeks. An additional 12 weeks "is recommended for successful quitters" [[FDA-Chantix]].

Label · warnings

No boxed warning now

The current CHANTIX label (effective 2026-01-23) has no boxed warning. Warnings and Precautions list postmarketing reports of neuropsychiatric events, including mood changes, psychosis and suicidal ideation (5.1), seizures (5.2) and interaction with alcohol [[FDA-Chantix]].

EAGLES

8144 smokers, 16 countries

Varenicline 1 mg twice a day, bupropion 150 mg twice a day, nicotine patch 21 mg/day with taper, or placebo for 12 weeks, in people with and without psychiatric disorders. No varenicline–placebo or bupropion–placebo difference in moderate and severe neuropsychiatric events was statistically significant [[Anthenelli2016]].

Side effects

What people reported

Most frequent in EAGLES: nausea on varenicline (25%), insomnia on bupropion (12%), abnormal dreams on the patch (12%) and headache on placebo (10%) [[Anthenelli2016]]. Across trials, varenicline probably raised serious adverse events (RR 1.23, 95% CI 1.01–1.48) [[LivingstoneBanks2023]]; NRT can rarely cause chest pain or palpitations (OR 1.88) [[HartmannBoyce2018]].

E-cigarettes: what is known and what is not

Established

More quitters than with NRT

The 2024 Cochrane living review (88 studies, 27,235 participants) found high-certainty evidence that nicotine e-cigarettes increase quit rates compared with NRT (RR 1.59, 95% CI 1.29–1.93), which might mean four more quitters per 100 [[Lindson2024]].

Established

One NHS trial

In 886 adults with weekly behavioural support, 1-year validated abstinence was 18.0% with a refillable e-cigarette versus 9.9% with NRT (RR 1.83, 95% CI 1.30–2.58). Cough and phlegm fell more in the e-cigarette group; throat or mouth irritation was more common (65.3% vs 51.2%) and nausea less common (31.3% vs 37.9%) [[Hajek2019]].

Early evidence

Long-term safety

The review found no evidence of serious harm, but "the longest follow-up was two years and the number of studies was small" [[Lindson2024]]. Of 1-year abstainers in the NHS trial, 80% were still using e-cigarettes at 52 weeks, against 9% still using NRT [[Hajek2019]].

Hypothesis

Lungs of people with COPD

Long-term respiratory safety of e-cigarettes specifically in COPD has not been settled by trials of the length needed [[Lindson2024,Hajek2019]].

Vaccines named in guidance

GOLD 2023 states that, "Depending on local guidelines, patients should be offered vaccination against influenza, pneumococcus, COVID-19, pertussis, and herpes zoster, if they have not already received these", and that people with COPD "should have COVID-19 vaccination in line with national guidelines" [[Agusti2023]].
