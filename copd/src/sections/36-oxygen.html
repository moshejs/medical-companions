Long-term oxygen & home ventilation

Two trials from around 1980 showed that oxygen for many hours a day helps people with COPD live longer when blood oxygen is very low at rest. Later trials showed where it does not help. Play the survival curves, then try the threshold explainer.

Threshold explainer: the published eligibility bands

The ATS 2020 guideline recommends long-term oxygen for at least 15 hours a day in adults with COPD and severe chronic resting room-air hypoxaemia (strong recommendation, moderate-quality evidence) [[Jacobs2020]]. It defines severe hypoxaemia by either of two criteria, based on the NOTT and MRC entry criteria. GOLD 2023 says its criteria "remain unchanged and are described in detail in the GOLD 2023 report" [[Agusti2023]]. Who oxygen helped

Long-term home oxygen "improved survival in a selected group of COPD patients with severe hypoxaemia"; there was no survival benefit in mild to moderate hypoxaemia or with night-time desaturation only [[Cranston2005]]. The review writes 55 mm Hg as 8.0 kPa; ATS 2020 pairs 55 mm Hg with 7.3 kPa, the correct conversion [[Jacobs2020]].

ATS 2020 · other suggestions

Moving about with oxygen

ATS suggests ambulatory oxygen for severe exertional room-air hypoxaemia (SpO2 ≤ 88% on exertion; conditional) and portable liquid oxygen for people mobile outside the home who need continuous flow above 3 L/min on exertion (conditional, very-low-quality evidence) [[Jacobs2020]].

Open question

24 hours or 15?

Textbooks recommended 24 h/day after NOTT [[NOTT1980]], but the NOTT continuous arm averaged about 17.7 h/day [[Arif2022]], and the 24-hour advice rested on a non-randomised NOTT-versus-MRC contrast. REDOX found 24 h no better than 15 h at 1 year; it was not powered for longer-term survival [[Ekstrom2024]].

Oxygen safety at home

Safety events per 100 person-years in LOTT, as reported by ATS 2020 [[Jacobs2020]].

ATS 2020 makes a best-practice statement that patients and caregivers be trained in oxygen equipment and safety, "including smoking cessation, fire prevention, and tripping hazards" [[Jacobs2020]]. In LOTT, the commonest event was tripping or falling over equipment; burns came from smoking near oxygen, from open flames and from liquid-oxygen frost [[Jacobs2020]].

The BTS 2015 home oxygen guideline covers risk assessment, "particularly in the clinically challenging area of home oxygen users who smoke" [[Hardinge2015]]. Help with stopping smoking is in Stopping smoking & vaccines.

Home non-invasive ventilation (NIV)

Some people with COPD keep a high carbon dioxide level when stable. ATS 2020 defines chronic hypercapnic COPD as FEV1/FVC < 0.70 with resting PaCO2 > 45 mm Hg, outside an exacerbation [[Macrea2020]]. The ATS guideline discusses nocturnal NIV: a ventilator that delivers breaths through a mask during sleep [[Macrea2020]].
