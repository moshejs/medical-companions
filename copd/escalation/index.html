Inhaled steroids, triple therapy & beyond

When flare-ups keep coming, the next steps depend on one blood test: the eosinophil count. Follow an inflamed airway through an inhaled steroid, its pneumonia trade-off, tablet add-ons, biologic injections and stepping down, then explore the trials yourself.

Check the count

Eosinophils predict who gains from ICS

In a secondary analysis of two fluticasone furoate/vilanterol trials (3177 people), adding the inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) cut exacerbations by 29% in people with blood eosinophils ≥2%, but by only 10% below 2% (P=0.28). The effect grew with the count: 24% at ≥2–<4%, 32% at 4–<6% and 42% at ≥6%, while the pneumonia increase did not depend on eosinophils 358. GOLD 2023: "Adding ICS has little or no effect at a blood eosinophil count of <100 cells/μl, whereas blood eosinophils ≥300 cells/μl identify patients with a strong likelihood of treatment benefit", with "a continuous gradation" in between 12.

In plain wordsA blood test counts a white blood cell called the eosinophil. People with more of them are more likely to be helped by a steroid inhaler 12,358.
Exam hookGOLD 2023 eosinophil cut-offs: <100 cells/µL little or no ICS benefit; ≥300 cells/µL strong likelihood of benefit 12.

100 to 299 cells/µL. GOLD 2023 describes "a continuous gradation" of ICS effect between 100 and 300 12. In a pooled analysis of three budesonide-formoterol trials, the ICS reduced exacerbations from 100 cells/µL upward (rate ratio 0.75; 95% CI 0.57 to 0.99) 30. GOLD 2023 recommends escalation from LABA + LAMA to triple for people with exacerbations at ≥100 cells/µL 12.

IMPACT and ETHOS: triple therapy, explored

Two large trials compared a single triple inhaler with dual inhalers in people who exacerbate. Pick a trial and an outcome.

Trial Outcome

Bars are rates or percentages in each arm; intervals are ratios with 95% confidence intervals, where 1 means no difference.

IMPACT compared three once-daily Ellipta inhalers in 10,355 people: fluticasone furoate/umeclidinium/vilanterol 100/62.5/25 µg (triple), FF/VI and UMEC/VI. Both comparisons on the primary end point had P<0.001; severe exacerbations leading to hospitalisation had a rate ratio of 0.66 (95% CI 0.56 to 0.78) versus UMEC/VI 256,304.

Open question

Does triple therapy lower mortality?

IMPACT (HR 0.72 vs UMEC/VI) and ETHOS (HR 0.51 vs GLY/FOR, higher budesonide dose only) both reported fewer deaths than with LAMA/LABA. Both were secondary end points with low event rates, and neither was significant versus ICS/LABA 257,278. GOLD 2023 includes both in its figure of evidence for reduced mortality 12.

Early evidence
Guidelines

When triple is recommended

ATS 2020: "a conditional recommendation for the use of triple therapy ... over dual therapy with LABA/LAMA in patients with COPD and dyspnea or exercise intolerance who have experienced one or more exacerbations in the past year" 339. GOLD 2023: with concomitant asthma, people "should be treated as if they have asthma" 12.

Established

Inhaled steroids and pneumonia

The Cochrane review of 43 studies put the extra risk of serious (hospitalised) pneumonia in absolute terms. Each dot is one of 1000 people treated; red dots are the extra cases. The review found no significant change in overall mortality with either ICS, and pneumonia deaths were too rare to judge 222.

Beyond inhalers: add-on medicines

Roflumilast · oral PDE4 inhibitor

Fewer exacerbations, more side effects

The Daliresp label says roflumilast inhibits PDE4, "a major cyclic-3′,5′-adenosine monophosphate (cyclic AMP)-metabolizing enzyme in lung tissue", and that its exact mechanism "is not well defined" 136. Indication: "to reduce the risk of COPD exacerbations in patients with severe COPD associated with chronic bronchitis and a history of exacerbations" 136. Exacerbations 1.14 vs 1.37 per patient-year; weight −2.17 kg vs placebo 61. Label: diarrhoea 9.5% vs 2.7%, weight decreased 7.5% vs 2.1%, psychiatric adverse reactions 5.9% vs 3.3% 136. REACT sat on the significance line (P=0.0529 primary, P=0.0424 sensitivity) 275.

Established
Azithromycin · daily macrolide

Longer to the first flare-up

1142 people, 250 mg daily for 1 year: median time to first exacerbation 266 vs 174 days, and an exacerbation hazard ratio of 0.73. Hearing decrements 25% vs 20%; the effect on microbial resistance "is not known". People with hearing impairment, resting tachycardia or QTc risk were excluded 13. No benefit in current smokers (HR 0.99) 177. Cochrane: prophylactic antibiotics cut the share of people with an exacerbation from 61% to 47% (NNTB 8) 187. Azithromycin has no US label indication for COPD; its use comes from trials and guidelines.

Established
Dupilumab · anti-IL-4Rα

BOREAS and NOTUS

939 and 935 people on triple therapy with eosinophils ≥300/µL: rate ratios 0.70 and 0.66 40,41. FEV1 differences of 83 mL (BOREAS) and 82 mL (NOTUS) at week 12; SGRQ improved in BOREAS but not in NOTUS at 52 weeks 40,41. FDA approval for COPD 2024-09-27; label dose "300 mg given every 2 weeks (Q2W)" 137.

Established for the trial populations
Mepolizumab · anti-IL-5

MATINEE

804 people randomised (806 in the registry) on triple therapy with eosinophils ≥300/µL: 0.80 vs 1.01 exacerbations per year (rate ratio 0.79); median time to first exacerbation 419 vs 321 days. Quality-of-life differences were not significant 310,402. Eosinophilic inflammation is present in 20 to 40% of people with COPD, the paper says 402. FDA approval for COPD 2025-05-22; label dose "100 mg administered subcutaneously once every 4 weeks" 138.

Established for the trial population
Ensifentrine · inhaled PDE3/PDE4 inhibitor

A nebulised newcomer

Inhibiting both enzymes raises intracellular cAMP and/or cGMP, the Ohtuvayre label says 139. ENHANCE-1 and -2 (760 and 789 people): FEV1 AUC0–12h +87 and +94 mL; exacerbation rate ratios 0.64 and 0.57 over 24 weeks 25. FDA-approved 2024-06-26; label dose "3 mg (one unit-dose ampule) twice daily ... by oral inhalation using a standard jet nebulizer with a mouthpiece" 139.

COPD Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), emphysema and chronic bronchitis, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people with COPD, their families and caregivers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change an inhaler, a medicine, oxygen or a care plan without your own care team. The spirometry explainer, GOLD grade and group tools, mMRC and CAT scores, the blood-gas explainer and the other calculators are for learning, never for diagnosis, self-triage or treatment decisions. Doses, oxygen targets, inhaler routines and guideline statements are quoted as published (GOLD, NICE, ATS/ERS, BTS and drug labels), never as instructions, and survival and risk figures are population statistics. If breathing becomes much worse than usual, or there is confusion, drowsiness, chest pain or blue lips, follow your action plan or seek urgent care.
