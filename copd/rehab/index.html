Pulmonary rehabilitation

Pulmonary rehabilitation is a programme of exercise training, education and behaviour change, tailored after an assessment. Play the cycle to see what it is meant to interrupt and what the trials measured, then explore the effect sizes and the question of timing after a hospital stay.

Schematic, not to scale. The loop is a teaching picture of how breathlessness and inactivity feed each other; it is not a measured model. The numbers in the side panel come from the Cochrane reviews 84,283,374, the Medicare cohort 254 and the early-rehabilitation trial 170.

What a programme is, and who is offered it

The ATS/ERS 2013 statement defines pulmonary rehabilitation as "a comprehensive intervention based on a thorough patient assessment followed by patient-tailored therapies that include, but are not limited to, exercise training, education, and behavior change, designed to improve the physical and psychological condition of people with [chronic respiratory disease] and to promote the long-term adherence to health-enhancing behaviors" 383,422.

The 2023 ATS guideline makes a strong recommendation for pulmonary rehabilitation in adults with stable COPD and after hospitalisation for an exacerbation (moderate-quality evidence for both), and a strong recommendation to offer a choice of centre-based rehabilitation or telerehabilitation 383. GOLD 2023 states that people with high symptom burden and exacerbation risk (groups B and E) "should be recommended to take part in a formal PR program" 12.

< 5%of people with COPD who may benefit receive rehabilitation (ATS 2023) 383
1.5%of 197,376 Medicare patients started within 90 days of a COPD admission 254
65 RCTsin the 2015 Cochrane review; most programmes ran 8 or 12 weeks 283

The Cochrane authors concluded that "additional RCTs comparing pulmonary rehabilitation and conventional care in COPD are not warranted" 283. Exercise-only programmes and programmes with more complex add-ons did not differ significantly; hospital-based programmes showed larger CRQ effects than community ones, which the authors warn may be confounded 283.

After a hospital stay: does timing matter?

Move the slider to choose when rehabilitation starts. The panel shows what the studies of that timing found. The early-start finding comes from a randomised trial; the after-discharge finding is an association in an observational cohort.

Within 90 days after discharge: linked with lower 1-year mortality

In the Medicare cohort, 1-year mortality was 7.3% versus 19.6% (HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.57–0.69), and every 3 additional sessions was associated with lower risk (HR 0.91); residual confounding is possible 254. In trials of rehabilitation after exacerbations, readmissions fell (OR 0.44), SGRQ improved by 7.80 points and 6MWD by 62 m; mortality did not change significantly (OR 0.68, 95% CI 0.28–1.67) 374. ATS 2023 strongly recommends rehabilitation after hospitalisation 383.

Home, telehealth and keeping the gains

Home-based

One visit plus phone calls

In a 166-person equivalence trial, home rehabilitation (one home visit and seven weekly physiotherapist phone calls) was non-inferior to centre-based rehabilitation for 6-minute walk distance at the end of the programme (difference +18.6 m favouring home). "Neither group maintained postrehabilitation gains at 12 months" 195.

Telerehabilitation

Similar gains, more finish

The 2021 Cochrane review (15 studies, 1904 participants, 99% with COPD) found probably little or no difference from centre-based rehabilitation in 6MWD (MD 0.06 m, 95% CI −10.82 to 10.94; moderate certainty). 93% completed telerehabilitation versus 70% for centre-based programmes 84.

One equivalence trial

Breathlessness not confirmed

In 142 people, telerehabilitation was equivalent to centre-based rehabilitation for 6MWD (MD −6 m), but equivalence for CRQ dyspnoea could not be confirmed (MD −1 point, 95% CI −3 to 1). The authors still describe it as safe with clinically meaningful benefits 85. GOLD 2023 says tele-rehabilitation "is safe and has similar benefits to those of center-based PR" 12.

Maintenance

After the first programme

The 2023 ATS guideline makes a conditional recommendation for either supervised maintenance or usual care after the first programme (low-quality evidence) 383. The ATS/ERS statement highlights health behaviour change as the way to keep the benefits 422.

COPD Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), emphysema and chronic bronchitis, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people with COPD, their families and caregivers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change an inhaler, a medicine, oxygen or a care plan without your own care team. The spirometry explainer, GOLD grade and group tools, mMRC and CAT scores, the blood-gas explainer and the other calculators are for learning, never for diagnosis, self-triage or treatment decisions. Doses, oxygen targets, inhaler routines and guideline statements are quoted as published (GOLD, NICE, ATS/ERS, BTS and drug labels), never as instructions, and survival and risk figures are population statistics. If breathing becomes much worse than usual, or there is confusion, drowsiness, chest pain or blue lips, follow your action plan or seek urgent care.
