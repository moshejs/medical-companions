# F · Other medicines: anticonvulsants, antipsychotics, antidepressants, ECT and guidelines

Pass date: 2026-11-03. Modules served: stabilisers, antipsychotics, depression.

This pass covers the medicines for bipolar disorder other than lithium (lithium itself is pass E): the guideline hierarchies (CANMAT/ISBD 2018 and its 2021 mixed-features and 2023 evidence updates, NICE CG185 as last updated 2 September 2025, BAP 2016), valproate, lamotrigine, carbamazepine, the antipsychotics for mania, bipolar depression and maintenance, long-acting injectables, metabolic harms and monitoring, antidepressants and switch risk, ECT and ketamine. Trial numbers come from PubMed abstracts. Label statements come from current US labels in openFDA (effective dates 2025–2026). Every dose, level and titration below is quoted as published in a label or guideline. None of it is an instruction, and none of it is for working out one's own dose.

## Findings

### stabilisers · Guideline hierarchy: where anticonvulsants sit
- The CANMAT/ISBD 2018 guidelines grade treatments first-, second- or third-line by weighing efficacy evidence, clinical experience, and consensus ratings of safety, tolerability and treatment-emergent switch risk. They introduced hierarchical rankings within first- and second-line options for acute mania, acute bipolar I depression and maintenance [[Yatham2018]].
- CANMAT/ISBD 2018 first-line monotherapy for acute mania, in the order listed: lithium, quetiapine, divalproex, asenapine, aripiprazole, paliperidone (for doses >6 mg), risperidone and cariprazine. All are level 1 evidence, with "comparable efficacy (Cohen's d 0.32‐0.66)" [[Yatham2018]].
- The guideline says lithium "should be considered first for acute mania unless there are specific reasons not to, such as mixed features, comorbid substance use or previous non‐response to lithium" [[Yatham2018]].
- The guideline states that about 50% of patients respond to monotherapy within 3–4 weeks. Combination therapy (an atypical antipsychotic plus lithium or divalproex) helps about 20% more patients respond, on average [[Yatham2018]].
- Carbamazepine, olanzapine, ziprasidone and haloperidol have level 1 evidence in mania, but CANMAT/ISBD 2018 downgrades them to second-line "due to safety/tolerability risks" [[Yatham2018]].
- Lamotrigine is "Not recommended" for acute mania in CANMAT/ISBD 2018 (level 1 negative). So are gabapentin (level 2 negative) and omega-3 fatty acids (level 1 negative) [[Yatham2018]].
- CANMAT/ISBD 2018 first-line choices for bipolar I depression, as listed in the abstract's hierarchy order: quetiapine, lurasidone plus lithium or divalproex, lithium, lamotrigine, lurasidone, and adjunctive lamotrigine. Divalproex is second-line [[Yatham2018]].
- CANMAT/ISBD 2018 first-line maintenance monotherapies: lithium, quetiapine, divalproex and lamotrigine (all level 1), then asenapine and aripiprazole (oral or once monthly, level 2). First-line combinations: quetiapine or aripiprazole with lithium/divalproex [[Yatham2018]].
- For bipolar II maintenance, CANMAT/ISBD 2018 lists quetiapine (level 1), lithium (level 2) and lamotrigine (level 2) monotherapy as first-line [[Yatham2018]].
- CANMAT/ISBD's 2021 mixed-presentations update found that "No agents met threshold for first-line treatment of DSM-5 manic or depressive episodes with mixed features". For mania with mixed features it lists asenapine, cariprazine, divalproex and aripiprazole as second-line. For depression with mixed features it lists cariprazine and lurasidone as second-line [[Yatham2021Mixed]].
- A 2023 CANMAT/ISBD summary paper restates the 2018 first-, second- and third-line recommendations and adds newer evidence across the lifespan. It is not a new full guideline [[Keramatian2023]].
- NICE CG185 (last updated 2025) says: "Do not offer lamotrigine to treat mania" [[NICE2014CG185F]].
- NICE CG185 says that for long-term treatment, clinicians should "explain that lithium is the most effective long‑term treatment for bipolar disorder". If lithium is ineffective, poorly tolerated or unsuitable, NICE suggests considering an antipsychotic. If an alternative antipsychotic is ineffective, it suggests considering valproate combined with an antipsychotic or lithium [[NICE2014CG185F]].
- NICE CG185 says "Do not start valproate in primary care to treat bipolar disorder" [[NICE2014CG185F]].
- NICE CG185 says that if stopping valproate, lamotrigine or an antipsychotic, the dose should be reduced "gradually over at least 4 weeks to minimise the risk of relapse" [[NICE2014CG185F]].
- NICE CG185 says plasma valproate and lamotrigine levels should not be measured routinely "unless there is evidence of ineffectiveness, poor adherence or toxicity" [[NICE2014CG185F]].
- BAP 2016 states that the risk/benefit of valproate "contraindicates its use in women of child bearing potential under normal circumstances" (evidence category I) [[Goodwin2016BAP]].

### stabilisers · Valproate: efficacy
- In the 1994 three-arm mania trial (179 hospitalised patients, 21 days), ≥50% improvement occurred in 48% on divalproex, 49% on lithium and 25% on placebo. Divalproex worked as well in rapid-cycling mania as in other patients [[Bowden1994Divalproex]].
- In the Cipriani 2011 network meta-analysis of 68 acute mania trials (16,073 participants), valproate beat placebo with a standardised mean difference (SMD) of −0.20 (95% CI −0.37 to −0.04). It ranked below haloperidol (−0.56), risperidone (−0.50) and olanzapine (−0.43) [[Cipriani2011Mania]].
- In the 12-month divalproex maintenance trial (n = 372, randomised 2:1:1 to divalproex, lithium or placebo), divalproex did not differ from placebo on time to any mood episode, the primary outcome. It did better than placebo on some secondary outcomes, including fewer discontinuations for a depressive episode [[Bowden2000Divalproex]].
- In BALANCE (330 people with bipolar I, up to 24 months), a primary outcome event occurred in 54% on lithium plus valproate, 59% on lithium and 69% on valproate. The hazard ratio (HR) for combination vs valproate was 0.59 (95% CI 0.42–0.83). For lithium vs valproate it was 0.71 (0.51–1.00) [[Geddes2010BALANCE]].
- BALANCE "could neither reliably confirm nor refute a benefit of combination therapy compared with lithium monotherapy" (HR 0.82, 0.58–1.17) [[Geddes2010BALANCE]].
- The US Depakote label indicates divalproex for "the manic episodes associated with bipolar disorder". The label adds that "there are no data to support the benefits of Depakote in such longer-term treatment" [[FDA-Depakote]].
- Depakote label dosing for mania, quoted as published: initial dose "750 mg daily in divided doses". In the acute mania trials, patients were dosed to a trough plasma concentration "between 50 and 125 mcg/mL". The maximum recommended dosage is 60 mg/kg/day [[FDA-Depakote]].

### stabilisers · Valproate: boxed warnings and safety
- The Depakote boxed warning ("Life threatening adverse reactions") covers three hazards: hepatotoxicity, fetal risk and pancreatitis [[FDA-Depakote]].
- Hepatotoxicity: fatal hepatic failure "usually" occurred in the first six months. The label calls for serum liver tests before therapy "and at frequent intervals thereafter, especially during the first six months". Children under two and people with POLG mitochondrial disease are at higher risk [[FDA-Depakote]].
- Pancreatitis: the label reports life-threatening and sometimes haemorrhagic pancreatitis, with "2 cases of pancreatitis without alternative etiology in 2,416 patients, representing 1,044 patient-years" in clinical trials [[FDA-Depakote]].
- Thrombocytopenia: in an epilepsy trial, 27% (34/126) of patients on about 50 mg/kg/day had at least one platelet count ≤75 × 10⁹/L. The risk "appeared to increase significantly at total valproate concentrations of ≥110 mcg/mL (females) or ≥135 mcg/mL (males)" [[FDA-Depakote]].
- The label also warns of hyperammonaemia and hyperammonaemic encephalopathy. It advises measuring ammonia if unexplained lethargy, vomiting or mental status changes occur [[FDA-Depakote]].
- NICE CG185 advises measuring weight or BMI, full blood count and liver function at the start of valproate, again at 6 months, and then annually [[NICE2014CG185F]].

### stabilisers · Valproate in pregnancy and the Pregnancy Prevention Programme
- The Depakote boxed warning: "Valproate can cause major congenital malformations, particularly neural tube defects (e.g., spina bifida). In addition, valproate can cause decreased IQ scores and neurodevelopmental disorders following in utero exposure" [[FDA-Depakote]].
- The label says valproate "should not be used to treat women with epilepsy or bipolar disorder who are pregnant or who plan to become pregnant unless other medications have failed to provide adequate symptom control or are otherwise unacceptable" [[FDA-Depakote]].
- Label figures: spina bifida risk is about 0.06–0.07% (6–7 in 10,000 births) in the general population vs about 1–2% (100–200 in 10,000) after in utero valproate exposure. The NAAED registry reported a major malformation rate of 9–11% at an average 1,000 mg/day of valproate monotherapy [[FDA-Depakote]].
- In the EURAP registry (7,355 monotherapy pregnancies, 1999–2016), major congenital malformations at 1 year occurred in 10.3% (142/1381) with valproate. Rates were 5.5% with carbamazepine, 2.9% with lamotrigine and 2.8% with levetiracetam. Risk rose with valproate dose (p<0.0001) [[Tomson2018]].
- In the updated EURAP analysis (9,840 pregnancies, 1999–2022), malformation rates were 9.9% (95% CI 8.5–11.5%) for valproate, 5.4% for carbamazepine, 3.1% for lamotrigine and 2.5% for levetiracetam. Overall malformation prevalence fell from 6.1% (1998–2004) to 3.7% (2015–2022) as prescribing moved away from valproate and carbamazepine [[Battino2024EURAP]].
- In a Cochrane review, children exposed to valproate had the highest malformation risk of any antiepileptic drug: 10.93% (95% CI 8.91–13.13). The risk ratio vs children of women without epilepsy was 5.69 (3.33–9.73). Lamotrigine showed no increased risk of major malformation [[Weston2016]].
- In the NEAD cohort, mean IQ at age 6 was 97 (95% CI 94–101) after valproate exposure vs 105 for carbamazepine, 108 for lamotrigine and 108 for phenytoin. Higher valproate doses correlated with lower IQ (r = −0.56) [[Meador2013NEAD]].
- In a Cochrane review, children exposed to valproate had IQ about 8.94 points lower (95% CI −11.96 to −5.92) than children of women without epilepsy [[Bromley2014]].
- In a Danish population study of 655,615 children, the 14-year absolute risk of autism spectrum disorder was 4.42% (95% CI 2.59–7.46%) after prenatal valproate vs 1.53% overall (adjusted HR 2.9, 1.7–4.9) [[Christensen2013]].
- In April 2018 the UK MHRA banned valproate prescribing for women and girls of childbearing potential who are not in its Pregnancy Prevention Programme [[Iacobucci2018]].
- NICE CG185 reproduces the later MHRA measures (Box 1). Do not start valproate for the first time in anyone (male or female) under 55 "unless 2 specialists independently agree and document that there is no other effective and tolerated treatment, or there are compelling reasons that the reproductive risks do not apply" [[NICE2014CG185F]].
- NICE CG185 adds MHRA precautionary advice for men: effective contraception is recommended throughout valproate treatment and for 3 months after stopping. NICE also notes a "possible association" between paternal valproate use around conception and neurodevelopmental disorders in children (2025 recommendation) [[NICE2014CG185F]].
- A Taiwanese nationwide cohort (2,583,503 births; 1,701 with paternal valproate exposure) found no association between paternal valproate and offspring autism (HR 0.86, 0.61–1.22), ADHD (HR 1.02) or congenital malformations (OR 1.07). The findings held in sibling comparisons (Early evidence) [[Feng2026]].
- For patients, guidelines frame valproate and pregnancy as a matter for advance planning with the care team. Stopping valproate abruptly is itself flagged as a risk in the label, so any change is planned with the prescriber, not made alone [[FDA-Depakote,NICE2014CG185F]].

### stabilisers · Lamotrigine: what it does and does not treat
- The US Lamictal label indicates lamotrigine for "Maintenance treatment of bipolar I disorder to delay the time to occurrence of mood episodes". It also states: "Treatment of acute manic or mixed episodes is not recommended" [[FDA-Lamictal]].
- In the 18-month trial in recently depressed patients (463 randomised), median time to intervention for any mood episode was 200 days on lamotrigine, 170 on lithium and 93 on placebo [[Calabrese2003Lamotrigine]].
- In the same trial, intervention for depression was nearly three times more frequent than intervention for mania. At 1 year, 57% on lamotrigine, 46% on lithium and 45% on placebo were free of intervention for depression [[Calabrese2003Lamotrigine]].
- At 1 year in that trial, 77% on lamotrigine, 86% on lithium and 72% on placebo were free of intervention for mania. The authors concluded lamotrigine was "predominantly effective against depression" and lithium "predominantly effective against mania" [[Calabrese2003Lamotrigine]].
- In the companion trial in recently manic or hypomanic patients (175 randomised), lamotrigine was superior to placebo in prolonging time to a depressive episode (p = .02). Lithium was superior in prolonging time to a manic, hypomanic or mixed episode (p = .006) [[Bowden2003Lamotrigine]].
- In the pooled analysis of both trials (638 randomised), median time to intervention for any mood episode was 86 days on placebo, 184 on lithium and 197 on lamotrigine. There was no evidence that either drug caused an affective switch [[Goodwin2004Lamotrigine]].
- In the Cipriani 2011 mania network meta-analysis, lamotrigine was not more effective than placebo. Haloperidol beat lamotrigine (SMD −0.48, −0.77 to −0.19) [[Cipriani2011Mania]].
- In an individual-patient meta-analysis of five acute bipolar depression trials (n = 1072), lamotrigine increased response modestly (HRSD RR 1.27, 95% CI 1.09–1.47). It helped when baseline HRSD was >24 (RR 1.47) but not when it was ≤24 (RR 1.07, 0.90–1.27) [[Geddes2009Lamotrigine]].
- In the 2023 bipolar depression network meta-analysis (101 trials, 20,081 participants), lamotrigine had the smallest effect among the agents that beat placebo: SMD 0.16 (0.03–0.29) [[Yildiz2023]].
- In the Miura 2014 maintenance network meta-analysis, lamotrigine was better tolerated than carbamazepine, lithium, or lithium plus valproate [[Miura2014]].
- In the Kishi 2021 maintenance network meta-analysis (41 trials, n = 9821), lamotrigine beat placebo for depressive relapse but not for manic relapse [[Kishi2021]].

### stabilisers · Lamotrigine: rash boxed warning and the slow titration
- The Lamictal boxed warning says serious rash, including Stevens–Johnson syndrome, occurs in about 0.3% to 0.8% of patients aged 2–17 and 0.08% to 0.3% of adults [[FDA-Lamictal]].
- Label risk factors for serious rash: coadministration with valproate, exceeding the recommended initial dose, exceeding the recommended dose escalation, and the HLA-B*1502 allele. "However, cases have occurred in the absence of these factors" [[FDA-Lamictal]].
- The label states: "Nearly all cases of life-threatening rashes caused by LAMICTAL have occurred within 2 to 8 weeks of treatment initiation". It says lamotrigine "should ordinarily be discontinued at the first sign of rash, unless the rash is clearly not drug related" [[FDA-Lamictal]].
- Label Table 5 (adults with bipolar disorder, no valproate and no enzyme inducers): 25 mg daily in weeks 1–2, 50 mg daily in weeks 3–4, 100 mg daily in week 5, and 200 mg daily from week 6 [[FDA-Lamictal]].
- Label Table 5 (taking valproate): 25 mg every other day in weeks 1–2, 25 mg daily in weeks 3–4, 50 mg daily in week 5, and 100 mg daily in weeks 6–7. Valproate "has been shown to inhibit glucuronidation and decrease the apparent clearance of lamotrigine" [[FDA-Lamictal]].
- Label Table 5 (taking carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital or primidone, and not valproate): 50 mg daily in weeks 1–2, 100 mg daily in weeks 3–4, 200 mg in week 5, 300 mg in week 6, and up to 400 mg daily in week 7, in divided doses [[FDA-Lamictal]].
- The label's bipolar target dose is 200 mg/day: 100 mg/day with valproate, or 400 mg/day with enzyme-inducing drugs and no valproate. Trials found "no additional benefit" at 400 mg vs 200 mg/day, so "doses above 200 mg/day are not recommended" [[FDA-Lamictal]].
- Per the label, if valproate is stopped, the lamotrigine dose "should be doubled over a 2-week period in equal weekly increments". If an inducer is stopped, the dose is held for a week and then halved over 2 weeks [[FDA-Lamictal]].
- Estrogen-containing oral contraceptives increase lamotrigine clearance. The label says the maintenance dose "will in most cases need to be increased by as much as 2-fold" when they are taken, and decreased by as much as 50% when they are stopped [[FDA-Lamictal]].
- The label advises that people taking lamotrigine for more than 16 weeks "should be periodically reassessed to determine the need for maintenance treatment" [[FDA-Lamictal]].
- NICE CG185 advises people taking lamotrigine to "contact their doctor immediately if they develop a rash while the dose of lamotrigine is being increased" [[NICE2014CG185F]].
- Lamotrigine's slow titration means it cannot be judged by an early (2-week) response the way other bipolar depression treatments can. CANMAT/ISBD 2018 suggests a lamotrigine target of "a minimum of 200 mg/day" [[Yatham2018]].

### stabilisers · Carbamazepine: efficacy, HLA-B*15:02 and enzyme induction
- In a 3-week trial of extended-release carbamazepine for manic or mixed episodes (N = 239), carbamazepine improved YMRS scores more than placebo from day 7. Dizziness (39.3%), somnolence (30.3%) and nausea (23.8%) were more common on carbamazepine, and total cholesterol rose [[Weisler2005Carbamazepine]].
- In the Cipriani 2011 network meta-analysis, carbamazepine beat placebo for acute mania (SMD −0.36, −0.60 to −0.11) [[Cipriani2011Mania]].
- In Miura 2014, carbamazepine did not significantly lower the risk of any mood relapse vs placebo (RR 0.68, 95% credible interval 0.44–1.06) [[Miura2014]].
- The Equetro (extended-release carbamazepine) label indicates it for "acute manic or mixed episodes associated with bipolar I disorder" [[FDA-Equetro]].
- The Equetro boxed warning: SJS/TEN are "estimated to occur in 1 to 6 per 10,000 new users in countries with mainly Caucasian populations, but the risk in patients of Asian descent is estimated to be about 10 times higher" [[FDA-Equetro]].
- The label says to test for HLA-B*1502 before starting in patients with "an increased likelihood of carrying this allele", and to avoid use in carriers "unless the benefit clearly outweighs the risk" [[FDA-Equetro]].
- The boxed warning also covers aplastic anaemia and agranulocytosis. The label puts the risk at 5–8 times that of the general population, where it is low (agranulocytosis 6 per million per year, aplastic anaemia 2 per million per year). It calls for a complete blood count before starting [[FDA-Equetro]].
- The HLA-B*1502 association with carbamazepine-induced SJS was first reported in Han Chinese (Established) [[Chung2004HLA]].
- In a prospective Taiwanese screening study (4,877 people), 7.7% carried HLA-B*1502 and were advised to avoid carbamazepine. No SJS/TEN occurred in non-carriers given carbamazepine, against about 10 cases expected from a historical incidence of 0.23% (P<0.001) [[Chen2011HLA]].
- In people of Northern European ancestry, HLA-A*3101 was linked to carbamazepine hypersensitivity, with OR 25.93 (4.93–116.18) for SJS-TEN. Carrying the allele raised the risk of a hypersensitivity reaction from 5.0% to 26.0% [[McCormack2011HLA]].
- Carbamazepine is metabolised by CYP3A4, and the Equetro label states it "may decrease the effectiveness of hormonal contraceptives" [[FDA-Equetro]].
- Carbamazepine and other enzyme inducers roughly double the lamotrigine titration schedule and target dose (see the lamotrigine table) [[FDA-Lamictal]].

### antipsychotics · Acute mania: comparative efficacy
- In Cipriani 2011 (68 RCTs, 16,073 participants), the SMDs vs placebo were: haloperidol −0.56, risperidone −0.50, olanzapine −0.43, lithium −0.37, quetiapine −0.37, aripiprazole −0.37, carbamazepine −0.36, asenapine −0.30, valproate −0.20 and ziprasidone −0.20. Gabapentin, lamotrigine and topiramate were no better than placebo [[Cipriani2011Mania]].
- Cipriani 2011 concluded that "antipsychotic drugs were significantly more effective than mood stabilisers". Risperidone, olanzapine and haloperidol "should be considered as among the best of the available options" [[Cipriani2011Mania]].
- In the same analysis, olanzapine, risperidone and quetiapine led to significantly fewer discontinuations than lithium, lamotrigine, placebo, topiramate and gabapentin [[Cipriani2011Mania]].
- In the Yildiz 2011 meta-analysis (38 trials, 10,800 patients), 13 of 17 agents tested beat placebo for mania. Pooled response was 48% on drug vs 31% on placebo, a number needed to treat (NNT) of 6 [[Yildiz2011Mania]].
- In Yildiz 2011, second-generation antipsychotics did not differ from haloperidol in direct comparisons, and lithium did not differ from valproate [[Yildiz2011Mania]].
- In a 3-week trial of risperidone 1–6 mg/day for mania (290 inpatients, mean baseline YMRS 37.2), risperidone beat placebo from week 1. Extrapyramidal symptoms were its most frequent adverse events [[Khanna2005Risperidone]].
- In a 3-week cariprazine mania trial (497 patients), YMRS fell 6.1 points more than placebo at 3–6 mg/day (95% CI −8.4 to −3.8) and 5.9 points more at 6–12 mg/day. Akathisia was more common on cariprazine [[Calabrese2015Cariprazine]].
- NICE CG185: if mania develops in someone not taking an antipsychotic or mood stabiliser, "offer haloperidol, olanzapine, quetiapine or risperidone". If the first antipsychotic fails, offer another. Then consider adding lithium, then valproate [[NICE2014CG185F]].
- BAP 2016: "haloperidol, olanzapine, risperidone and quetiapine are particularly effective in short-term reduction of symptoms". Valproate is described as an alternative with less motor risk that "should not be used for women of child bearing potential" [[Goodwin2016BAP]].
- Labels quoted as published: quetiapine for bipolar mania in adults starts at 50 mg twice daily, with a recommended 400–800 mg/day and a maximum of 800 mg/day [[FDA-Seroquel]]. Cariprazine for bipolar mania starts at 1.5 mg daily, with a recommended 3–6 mg daily and a maximum of 6 mg [[FDA-Vraylar]].

### antipsychotics · Bipolar depression: the FDA-approved options
- Five US-approved treatments for bipolar depression have published phase 3 evidence:
  - quetiapine [[FDA-Seroquel]]
  - lurasidone (bipolar I, monotherapy or with lithium/valproate) [[FDA-Latuda]]
  - cariprazine (bipolar I) [[FDA-Vraylar]]
  - lumateperone (bipolar I or II, monotherapy or with lithium/valproate) [[FDA-Caplyta]]
  - olanzapine–fluoxetine combination (bipolar I) [[FDA-OlanzapineFluoxetine]]
- In BOLDER I (542 outpatients, 8 weeks), response rates were 58.2% on quetiapine 600 mg, 57.6% on quetiapine 300 mg and 36.1% on placebo. Remission was 52.9% on either dose vs 28.4% on placebo. Treatment-emergent mania was 3.2% vs 3.9% [[Calabrese2005BOLDER]].
- In BOLDER II (509 patients), quetiapine beat placebo on MADRS from week 1, with week-8 effect sizes of 0.61 (300 mg) and 0.54 (600 mg). There were no major differences between the two doses [[Thase2006BOLDER2]].
- Seroquel label dosing for bipolar depression (adults), as published: once daily at bedtime, 50 mg on day 1, 100 mg on day 2, 200 mg on day 3 and 300 mg on day 4. The recommended and maximum dose is 300 mg/day [[FDA-Seroquel]].
- In the lurasidone monotherapy trial (505 patients, 6 weeks), MADRS fell 15.4 points in both lurasidone dose groups (effect size 0.51) vs 10.7 on placebo, with "minimal changes in weight, lipids, and measures of glycemic control" [[Loebel2014Mono]].
- In the adjunctive lurasidone trial (348 patients on lithium or valproate), MADRS fell 17.1 points vs 13.5 on placebo (effect size 0.34) [[Loebel2014Adj]].
- Latuda label, as published: bipolar depression in adults starts at 20 mg per day, with a recommended 20–120 mg per day, taken "with food (at least 350 calories)" [[FDA-Latuda]].
- In the first cariprazine bipolar depression trial (571 patients), 1.5 mg/day reduced MADRS 4.0 points more than placebo (95% CI −6.3 to −1.6). The 3.0 mg/day effect (−2.5) was not significant after multiplicity adjustment, and 0.75 mg/day was similar to placebo [[Durgam2016Cariprazine]].
- In a phase 3 cariprazine trial (480 patients), both 1.5 and 3.0 mg/day beat placebo on MADRS (LS mean differences −2.5 and −3.0) [[Earley2019Cariprazine]].
- In a second phase 3 trial (493 patients), 1.5 mg/day beat placebo (MADRS LSMD −2.5) but 3.0 mg/day did not. Mean weight increase was ≤0.5 kg in all groups [[Earley2020Cariprazine]].
- Vraylar label, as published: bipolar depression in adults starts at 1.5 mg daily, with a recommended 1.5 mg or 3 mg daily and a maximum of 3 mg [[FDA-Vraylar]].
- In the lumateperone 42 mg trial (377 patients with bipolar I or II, 6 weeks), MADRS improved 4.6 points more than placebo (effect size −0.56), with benefit in both bipolar I and bipolar II. Weight, metabolic and endocrine changes were minimal [[Calabrese2021Lumateperone]].
- Caplyta label, as published: 42 mg once daily, no titration needed. It is indicated for bipolar I or II depression as monotherapy or with lithium or valproate [[FDA-Caplyta]].
- In the 8-week olanzapine–fluoxetine trial (833 patients), MADRS fell 11.9 points on placebo, 15.0 on olanzapine and 18.5 on olanzapine–fluoxetine. Remission was 24.5%, 32.8% and 48.8%, and treatment-emergent mania did not differ (6.7%, 5.7%, 6.4%) [[Tohen2003OFC]].
- Olanzapine–fluoxetine label, as published: adults start at 6 mg/25 mg once daily in the evening, with a maximum of 12 mg/50 mg [[FDA-OlanzapineFluoxetine]].
- In the 2023 network meta-analysis, olanzapine plus fluoxetine, quetiapine, olanzapine, lurasidone, lumateperone, cariprazine and lamotrigine beat placebo with moderate confidence. SMDs ranged from 0.41 (olanzapine plus fluoxetine) to 0.16 (lamotrigine) [[Yildiz2023]].
- NNTs for response vs placebo (Ketter 2014): olanzapine–fluoxetine 4, quetiapine 6, lurasidone 5 (monotherapy) and 7 (adjunctive), lamotrigine 12, adjunctive antidepressants 29. Matching harms: olanzapine–fluoxetine weight gain NNH 6, quetiapine sedation/somnolence NNH 5 [[Ketter2014]].
- For lurasidone (Citrome 2014), the NNT for response was 5 (monotherapy) and 7 (adjunctive). The NNH for ≥7% weight gain was 29 (20–60 mg/d), 5550 (80–120 mg/d) and 42 (adjunctive) [[Citrome2014Lurasidone]].
- For cariprazine 1.5–3 mg/day (pooled), response was 46.3% vs 35.9% on placebo (NNT 10, 95% CI 7–21) and remission 30.2% vs 20.9% (NNT 11) [[Citrome2019Cariprazine]].
- In CANMAT/ISBD 2018, quetiapine is "the only recommended first‐line treatment for BDII depression (level 1)". Lithium, sertraline, venlafaxine, lamotrigine and ECT are second-line [[Yatham2018]].
- The ISBD's 2025 task force defined treatment-resistant bipolar depression as failure of at least two approved, adequately dosed treatments. For bipolar I it named quetiapine 300–600 mg/day (≥8 weeks), lurasidone 20–120 mg/day (≥6 weeks), olanzapine 6–12 mg plus fluoxetine 25–75 mg (≥8 weeks), cariprazine 1.5–3 mg/day (≥6 weeks) and lumateperone 42 mg/day (≥6 weeks) [[Vieta2025TRBD]].
- NICE CG185: for moderate or severe bipolar depression in someone not on treatment, "offer fluoxetine combined with olanzapine, or quetiapine on its own". Olanzapine alone or lamotrigine alone are the options if the person prefers [[NICE2014CG185F]].
- CANMAT/ISBD 2018 does not recommend aripiprazole monotherapy for bipolar depression (level 1 negative). It failed two trials, and its pooled MADRS advantage of 1.12 points was judged not clinically meaningful [[Yatham2018]].

### antipsychotics · Maintenance and long-acting injectables
- In the olanzapine maintenance trial (361 responders), median time to any relapse was 174 days on olanzapine vs 22 days on placebo. Relapse rates were 46.7% vs 80.1% [[Tohen2006Olanzapine]].
- In that trial, patients gained a mean of 3.1 kg during open-label olanzapine. In the double-blind phase, those continuing olanzapine gained another 1.0 kg and those switched to placebo lost 2.0 kg [[Tohen2006Olanzapine]].
- In Trial 144 (1,172 in the intent-to-treat population, up to 104 weeks), quetiapine prolonged time to any mood event vs placebo (HR 0.29, 0.23–0.38), as did lithium (HR 0.46, 0.36–0.59). Quetiapine protected against both manic (HR 0.29) and depressive (HR 0.30) events [[Weisler2011Quetiapine]].
- In the Miura 2014 maintenance network meta-analysis (33 trials, 6,846 participants), most drugs beat placebo. The exceptions were aripiprazole (RR 0.62, 0.38–1.03), carbamazepine, imipramine and paliperidone. The authors concluded lithium "should remain the first-line treatment" [[Miura2014]].
- In Kishi 2021, aripiprazole plus valproate, lamotrigine, lamotrigine plus valproate, lithium, olanzapine and quetiapine beat placebo for depressive relapse. Most other active treatments beat placebo for manic relapse [[Kishi2021]].
- In Kishi 2021, among antipsychotics added to lithium or valproate, only lurasidone and quetiapine add-ons beat placebo add-on for depressive relapse [[Kishi2021]].
- CANMAT/ISBD 2018: after a combination of an antipsychotic plus lithium or divalproex treats acute mania, continuing the antipsychotic for 6 months offered "clear benefit", but "the benefits beyond 6 months remain uncertain" [[Yatham2018]].
- CANMAT/ISBD 2018 places olanzapine as second-line for maintenance "because of safety issues such as metabolic syndrome", despite efficacy against any mood, manic or depressive episode [[Yatham2018]].
- In the risperidone long-acting injectable (LAI) trial (303 randomised responders, up to 24 months), risperidone LAI prolonged time to recurrence of elevated-mood episodes (p<0.001) but not depressive episodes (p = .805). Most (77%) received 25 mg every 2 weeks [[Quiroz2010]].
- In the aripiprazole once-monthly 400 mg trial (266 randomised, 52 weeks), recurrence was 26.5% (35/132) vs 51.1% (68/133) on placebo (HR 0.45, 0.30–0.68), "predominantly on manic episodes" [[Calabrese2017AOM]].
- Risperdal Consta label, as published: 25 mg IM every 2 weeks for bipolar I maintenance, as monotherapy or with lithium or valproate. Some patients may benefit from 37.5 or 50 mg, and doses above 50 mg have not been studied [[FDA-RisperdalConsta]].
- Abilify Maintena label, as published: 400 mg IM monthly for maintenance monotherapy of bipolar I in adults. Tolerability should first be established with oral aripiprazole in people who have not taken it [[FDA-AbilifyMaintena]].
- CANMAT/ISBD 2018 says that if psychosocial strategies do not fix non-adherence, "long‐acting injectable medications should be offered" [[Yatham2018]].
- BAP 2016 says to consider depot formulations "if prophylaxis against recurrence of mania is required and adherence to oral medication is erratic or injection preferred", noting only risperidone had RCT support at the time [[Goodwin2016BAP]].

### antipsychotics · Metabolic effects, boxed warnings and monitoring
- In a network meta-analysis of 18 antipsychotics (100 RCTs, 25,952 patients with schizophrenia, median 6 weeks), weight gain vs placebo ranged from −0.23 kg for haloperidol to 3.01 kg for clozapine. BMI change was highest with olanzapine (1.07 kg/m²) [[Pillinger2020]].
- In the same analysis, olanzapine and clozapine had the worst metabolic profiles. Aripiprazole, brexpiprazole, cariprazine, lurasidone and ziprasidone had the most benign. Higher baseline weight and male sex predicted greater glucose rises [[Pillinger2020]].
- CANMAT/ISBD 2018: metabolic syndrome affects 20–65% of people with bipolar disorder. Weight gain contributes to "upwards of 60%" of treatment non-adherence [[Yatham2018]].
- CANMAT/ISBD 2018 ranks clozapine and olanzapine highest for metabolic risk, followed by quetiapine (particularly at higher doses) and risperidone. Aripiprazole, ziprasidone, asenapine and lurasidone have more minimal impact [[Yatham2018]].
- From the Seroquel label weight table, the share of adults gaining ≥7% body weight was 21% vs 7% on placebo in bipolar mania monotherapy (up to 12 weeks) and 8% vs 2% in bipolar depression (up to 8 weeks) [[FDA-Seroquel]].
- Every antipsychotic label read carries a boxed warning of increased mortality in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis [[FDA-Seroquel,FDA-Latuda,FDA-Vraylar,FDA-Caplyta,FDA-AbilifyMaintena,FDA-RisperdalConsta]].
- The labels of the antipsychotics approved for depression also carry the antidepressant-class boxed warning on suicidal thoughts and behaviours in children, adolescents and young adults. They advise close monitoring for clinical worsening [[FDA-Seroquel,FDA-Latuda,FDA-Vraylar,FDA-Caplyta,FDA-OlanzapineFluoxetine]].
- NICE CG185: before starting an antipsychotic, measure and record weight or BMI, pulse, blood pressure, fasting blood glucose or HbA1c, and blood lipid profile [[NICE2014CG185F]].
- NICE CG185 monitoring during treatment: weight or BMI weekly for the first 6 weeks and then at 12 weeks. Fasting glucose or HbA1c and lipids at 12 weeks. Also pulse and blood pressure after each dose change, movement disorders and adherence [[NICE2014CG185F]].
- NICE CG185 recommends offering a combined healthy-eating and physical-activity programme to people with bipolar disorder, "especially those taking antipsychotics and long‑term medication" [[NICE2014CG185F]].
- NICE CG185 advises against regular combined antipsychotic medication "except for short periods (for example, when changing medication)" [[NICE2014CG185F]].

### depression · Antidepressants in bipolar depression
- In STEP-BD (366 people with bipolar depression on a mood stabiliser, up to 26 weeks), durable recovery occurred in 23.5% (42/179) with an added antidepressant vs 27.3% (51/187) with added placebo (P = 0.40). Rates of treatment-emergent affective switch were similar [[Sachs2007STEPBD]].
- In a 2016 meta-analysis of six trials (1,383 patients), adjunctive second-generation antidepressants gave a small improvement in symptom scores (SMD 0.165). They did not improve response (RR 1.158, 0.840–1.597) or remission [[McGirr2016]].
- In that meta-analysis, acute treatment did not raise switch risk (0.926, 0.576–1.491), but 52-week extension periods did (1.774, 1.018–3.091) [[McGirr2016]].
- In a 2004 systematic review (12 trials, 1,088 patients), antidepressants beat placebo short-term. Switch rates were 3.8% on antidepressants vs 4.7% on placebo, and 10% for tricyclics vs 3.2% for all other antidepressants combined [[Gijsman2004]].
- A 2010 meta-analytic review (109 trials, 114,521 adults with bipolar disorder or major depression) found mania risk averaged 12.5% with antidepressants vs 7.5% without. Tricyclics were riskier than serotonin-reuptake inhibitors [[Tondo2010]].
- In a Swedish within-person registry study (3,240 people with bipolar disorder starting an antidepressant), antidepressant monotherapy raised the 0–3 month mania risk (HR 2.83, 1.12–7.19). With a concurrent mood stabiliser there was no rise (HR 0.79, 0.54–1.15) [[Viktorin2014]].
- In the same study, nearly 35% of patients started an antidepressant without a mood stabiliser [[Viktorin2014]].
- In a 2025 network meta-analysis of 13 RCTs (1,362 patients), no antidepressant had a significantly higher switch risk than placebo. Venlafaxine had the highest point estimate (RR 4.53, 0.47–43.25), and overall confidence was low [[Oliva2025]].
- In the 2023 bipolar depression network meta-analysis, antidepressants as a class "seem to be efficacious, but had a higher risk for manic switch compared to antipsychotics" [[Yildiz2023]].
- The ISBD task force found "striking incongruity between the wide use of and the weak evidence base" for antidepressants in bipolar disorder. It reached consensus on 12 statements [[Pacchiarotti2013]].
- ISBD task force: SSRIs and bupropion "may have lower rates of manic switch" than tricyclics, tetracyclics and SNRIs. In bipolar I, antidepressants "should be prescribed only as an adjunct to mood-stabilizing medications" [[Pacchiarotti2013]].
- CANMAT/ISBD 2018 moved adjunctive SSRIs/bupropion from first-line (2013) to second-line in bipolar I depression because of weak efficacy data. It states "Antidepressant monotherapy should NOT be used for the treatment of BDI depression" [[Yatham2018]].
- CANMAT/ISBD 2018 says antidepressants should ideally be avoided, or used cautiously, after antidepressant-induced mania or hypomania, with current mixed features, or with recent rapid cycling [[Yatham2018]].
- NICE CG185: if mania or hypomania develops on antidepressant monotherapy, consider stopping the antidepressant and offer an antipsychotic "regardless of whether the antidepressant is stopped" [[NICE2014CG185F]].
- In a 2021 network meta-analysis of adjunctive treatments (69 trials, 8,007 participants), adjunctive fluoxetine had "the most consistent evidence base for both efficacy and tolerability" (response RR 1.51, 1.11–2.06). None of the agents raised switch rates [[Bahji2021]].

### depression · ECT
- In the only RCT of ECT in treatment-resistant bipolar depression (73 inpatients, Norway, 6 weeks), ECT beat algorithm-based medication by 6.6 MADRS points (95% CI 2.5–10.6). Response was 73.9% vs 35.0%, but remission did not differ (34.8% vs 30.0%) [[Schoeyen2015]].
- In a meta-analysis of six cohort studies, ECT remission was 53.2% (168/316) in bipolar depression and 50.9% (402/790) in unipolar depression (OR 1.08, 0.75–1.57) [[Dierckx2012]].
- CANMAT/ISBD 2018 lists ECT as second-line for bipolar I depression. It is "particularly for treatment‐refractory patients and those for whom a rapid treatment response is needed", including severe depression with imminent suicidal risk, catatonia or psychotic depression [[Yatham2018]].
- CANMAT/ISBD 2018 also lists ECT as second-line for acute mania (level 3). It cites evidence "that up to 80% of patients will show marked clinical improvements", and prefers bifrontal over bitemporal placement [[Yatham2018]].
- NICE CG185 notes ECT as an option for "rapid short-term treatment of a prolonged or severe manic episode when other treatments have not worked or the condition is life-threatening" (NICE TA59) [[NICE2014CG185F]].

### depression · Ketamine (Early evidence)
- In a crossover add-on RCT (18 people with treatment-resistant bipolar depression on lithium or valproate), one IV ketamine infusion of 0.5 mg/kg improved depression within 40 minutes (d = 0.52). The peak effect was at day 2 (d = 0.80), and benefit lasted through day 3 (Early evidence) [[Diazgranados2010]].
- In that trial, 71% responded to ketamine and 6% to placebo at some point. The most common adverse effect was dissociation, seen only at the 40-minute time point [[Diazgranados2010]].
- In the replication trial (15 participants), 79% responded to ketamine and 0% to placebo. Suicidal ideation also improved rapidly (d = 0.98) (Early evidence) [[Zarate2012]].
- In a network meta-analysis of adjunctive treatments, IV racemic ketamine had the largest response RR (12.49, 95% CI 3.06–50.93). The very wide interval reflects small trials (Early evidence) [[Bahji2021]].
- CANMAT/ISBD 2018 places IV ketamine as third-line adjunctive (level 3) "due to its invasive nature, short duration of effect, and lack of long‐term safety data" [[Yatham2018]].

### Exam hooks
- Lamotrigine is a maintenance drug that mainly prevents depressive relapse. It does not treat acute mania [[FDA-Lamictal,Calabrese2003Lamotrigine,Cipriani2011Mania]].
- Lamotrigine with valproate: halve the titration (valproate inhibits lamotrigine glucuronidation). With carbamazepine: roughly double it [[FDA-Lamictal]].
- Lamotrigine serious rash in adults: 0.08–0.3%, nearly always within 2–8 weeks of starting [[FDA-Lamictal]].
- Valproate boxed warnings: hepatotoxicity, fetal risk and pancreatitis [[FDA-Depakote]].
- Valproate major malformation rate is about 10% (EURAP 10.3%, Cochrane 10.93%) vs about 3% for lamotrigine [[Tomson2018,Weston2016]].
- Carbamazepine plus HLA-B*15:02 (Asian ancestry) means SJS/TEN risk: test before starting [[FDA-Equetro,Chen2011HLA]].
- Most effective antimanic drugs in Cipriani 2011: haloperidol, risperidone, olanzapine [[Cipriani2011Mania]].
- FDA-approved for bipolar depression: quetiapine, lurasidone, cariprazine, lumateperone, olanzapine–fluoxetine. Lumateperone and quetiapine cover bipolar II [[FDA-Caplyta,FDA-Seroquel,Calabrese2021Lumateperone]].
- Quetiapine is the only CANMAT/ISBD 2018 first-line treatment for bipolar II depression [[Yatham2018]].
- STEP-BD: adding an antidepressant to a mood stabiliser gave no added recovery (23.5% vs 27.3%) and no increased switch [[Sachs2007STEPBD]].
- Antidepressant monotherapy in bipolar disorder raises mania risk (HR 2.83). With a mood stabiliser it does not (HR 0.79) [[Viktorin2014]].
- Tricyclics carry the highest switch risk (10% vs 3.2% for other antidepressants) [[Gijsman2004]].
- Olanzapine is effective but second-line in CANMAT/ISBD because of metabolic harm [[Yatham2018,Pillinger2020]].
- NICE antipsychotic metabolic monitoring: weight weekly for 6 weeks, then at 12 weeks. Glucose/HbA1c and lipids at 12 weeks [[NICE2014CG185F]].
- Long-acting injectable risperidone and aripiprazole mainly prevent manic, not depressive, recurrence [[Quiroz2010,Calabrese2017AOM]].
- ECT in treatment-resistant bipolar depression: response 73.9% vs 35.0% with medication alone [[Schoeyen2015]].
- BALANCE: lithium alone or with valproate prevents relapse better than valproate alone [[Geddes2010BALANCE]].

### In plain words
- Lithium is not the only medicine for bipolar disorder. Some seizure medicines and some antipsychotic medicines also help prevent or treat mood episodes [[Yatham2018]].
- Lamotrigine helps most with keeping depression away over the long term. It does not calm mania [[FDA-Lamictal,Calabrese2003Lamotrigine]].
- Lamotrigine has to be started very slowly to lower the chance of a serious rash. A new rash while the dose is going up is a reason to call the doctor right away [[FDA-Lamictal,NICE2014CG185F]].
- Valproate can harm an unborn baby. Anyone who could become pregnant should plan medicines and pregnancy with their care team well ahead of time, and should not stop a medicine suddenly on their own [[FDA-Depakote,NICE2014CG185F]].
- Several antipsychotic medicines treat bipolar depression, not only mania. Each has different side effects [[Yildiz2023]].
- Some of these medicines can cause weight gain and raise blood sugar or cholesterol. Regular weight and blood checks help catch this early [[Pillinger2020,NICE2014CG185F]].
- Healthy eating and physical activity programmes are part of good care, especially for people taking antipsychotics [[NICE2014CG185F]].
- Standard antidepressants on their own can tip some people with bipolar disorder into mania. Doctors usually use them, if at all, only together with a mood stabiliser [[Viktorin2014,Pacchiarotti2013]].
- A monthly or two-weekly injection is an option for people who find daily pills hard to keep up with [[Yatham2018,Calabrese2017AOM]].
- ECT is a safe, well-studied treatment that can help when depression is severe or other treatments have not worked [[Schoeyen2015,Yatham2018]].
- Ketamine is still being studied for bipolar depression. Its effects so far are fast but short-lived [[Diazgranados2010,Yatham2018]].
- Medicines work: in trials, many people improve within weeks, and long-term treatment greatly cuts the chance of a new episode. These are averages across groups of people [[Weisler2011Quetiapine,Goodwin2004Lamotrigine]].
- If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

## Open questions and controversies
- Antidepressants in bipolar depression: STEP-BD found no added benefit and no added switch [[Sachs2007STEPBD]]. A meta-analysis found a small symptom benefit without better response, with longer use linked to more switching [[McGirr2016]]. Register data put the switch risk on antidepressant monotherapy, not on combination with a mood stabiliser [[Viktorin2014]]. A 2025 network meta-analysis found no single antidepressant with significant switch risk, at low certainty [[Oliva2025]]. Status: CANMAT/ISBD 2018 keeps adjunctive SSRIs/bupropion as second-line, and monotherapy is not recommended in bipolar I [[Yatham2018]].
- Valproate in men: NICE (2025) reproduces the MHRA precaution on a possible link between paternal valproate and neurodevelopmental disorders [[NICE2014CG185F]]. A large 2026 Taiwanese sibling-controlled cohort found no association [[Feng2026]]. Status: unresolved, and regulators have kept their precautions.
- Where olanzapine belongs: NICE lists olanzapine among the first antipsychotics to offer for mania and with fluoxetine for bipolar depression [[NICE2014CG185F]]. CANMAT/ISBD 2018 ranks it second-line for mania and maintenance because of metabolic risk [[Yatham2018]]. Network meta-analysis ranks olanzapine among the worst for metabolic effects [[Pillinger2020]]. Status: a values trade-off between efficacy and metabolic harm.
- How much lamotrigine helps acute bipolar depression: the IPD meta-analysis showed a modest overall effect, mainly in more severe depression [[Geddes2009Lamotrigine]]. The 2023 network meta-analysis gave it the smallest effect among effective agents (SMD 0.16) [[Yildiz2023]]. CANMAT/ISBD still lists lamotrigine first-line for bipolar I depression [[Yatham2018]]. Status: an accepted option, valued mainly for prevention and tolerability.
- Weight of relapse-prevention (enriched withdrawal) trials: BAP 2016 accepts them "for what they are while recognizing their limitations" and notes that NICE "effectively discounted much of the longer term data generated by relapse-prevention studies" [[Goodwin2016BAP]]. This partly explains why the guidelines differ on antipsychotic maintenance.
- How long to continue an antipsychotic added for mania: there is clear benefit for 6 months after response, but "the benefits beyond 6 months remain uncertain" [[Yatham2018]].
- Ketamine: rapid but short-lived benefit in small crossover trials [[Diazgranados2010,Zarate2012]]. It is third-line in CANMAT/ISBD 2018 because of invasiveness, short duration and lack of long-term safety data [[Yatham2018]]. Status: Early evidence.
- Treatment-resistant bipolar depression: the ISBD's 2025 consensus definition (failure of two approved, adequately dosed treatments) is new, and it is meant to standardise future trials [[Vieta2025TRBD]].
- Valproate's maintenance evidence: its pivotal 12-month trial missed its primary endpoint [[Bowden2000Divalproex]] and its US label offers no long-term efficacy data [[FDA-Depakote]]. CANMAT/ISBD still rates divalproex first-line for maintenance (level 1) [[Yatham2018]]. BALANCE found it less effective than lithium [[Geddes2010BALANCE]].
