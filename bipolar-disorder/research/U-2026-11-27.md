## Findings

### frontier · A second aspirin trial, with the opposite result
- The site reported one aspirin trial: in A-Bipolar (250 people newly diagnosed), aspirin 150 mg did not reduce mood instability at 6 months or any secondary outcome [[Bruun2026]].
- ASPIRIN BD (EudraCT 2021-006635-25) was a double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2 trial at four Polish centres. 100 adults aged 18–60 in a bipolar depressive episode were randomised 1:1 to aspirin 150 mg/day or placebo for 12 months, added to usual treatment. [[Dominiak2026]]
- At month 2, HAMD-17 was lower with aspirin: adjusted difference −4.24 (95% CI −7.20 to −1.28; p = 0.005; d = −0.51). The primary analysis used participants with a month-2 rating; an intention-to-treat sensitivity analysis (N = 100) agreed (p = 0.008). [[Dominiak2026]]
- Relapse or clinical worsening over months 4–12 was less frequent with aspirin (p = 0.026; NNT 5). Safety and adherence were comparable. The authors conclude that the findings "warrant phase III confirmation". [[Dominiak2026]]
- Disagreement written out: A-Bipolar was larger (250), enrolled people newly diagnosed and measured self-rated daily mood instability, with a 6-item Hamilton scale as a secondary outcome: no effect [[Bruun2026]]. ASPIRIN BD was smaller (100), enrolled people in a depressive episode and used clinician-rated HAMD-17: positive [[Dominiak2026]]. Same dose. Status on the site stays "not supported for routine use". (Early evidence)

### Exam hooks
- Aspirin 150 mg in bipolar disorder: A-Bipolar (n = 250) negative; ASPIRIN BD (n = 100, phase 2) positive on HAMD-17 at 2 months. [[Bruun2026,Dominiak2026]]

### In plain words
- Not changed: bigger trials have not confirmed a benefit, so anti-inflammatory add-ons are not routine treatments.

## Open questions and controversies
- Does low-dose aspirin help bipolar depression? One larger negative trial in newly diagnosed patients [[Bruun2026]]; one smaller positive phase 2 trial in a depressive episode [[Dominiak2026]]. Status: open; a phase 3 trial is needed.
