## Findings

### lithium · Lithium and suicide: a 2026 umbrella review and re-analysis of the randomised trials
- Yano and colleagues searched five databases to 6 April 2026 for systematic reviews with meta-analyses of randomised trials comparing lithium with placebo, treatment as usual or active comparators in people with psychiatric disorders, re-extracted every eligible trial, and updated the primary-trial search from January 2024. Ten systematic reviews, one reanalysis and 20 trial publications (22 strata; 3,675 participants) were included. [[Yano2026]]
- Completed suicide (11 informative comparisons, 2,292 participants): 6 events with lithium vs 16 with comparators; Mantel-Haenszel OR 0.39 (95% CI 0.15 to 1.00; I² = 0%). A Peto sensitivity estimate was OR 0.42 (0.18 to 0.98); excluding the most influential comparison gave OR 0.43 (0.13 to 1.40). [[Yano2026]]
- In an exploratory subgroup, the signal was concentrated in trials enrolling mixed mood-disorder populations (four trials; OR 0.25, 0.07 to 0.90). [[Yano2026]]
- No significant effects were found for suicide attempt, deliberate self-harm, all-cause mortality or composite suicide-related events. Treatment discontinuation favoured lithium over placebo but active comparators over lithium. GRADE certainty was low to very low. [[Yano2026]]
- The authors conclude that the evidence shows "greater uncertainty than suggested by earlier syntheses" and that lithium remains a treatment option when clinically indicated. (Established that the direction favours lithium; the size of the effect is uncertain.) [[Yano2026]]
- Disagreement written out: Cipriani 2013 reported OR 0.13 (0.03 to 0.66) for suicide and OR 0.38 (0.15 to 0.95) for all-cause death against placebo [[Cipriani2013]]; Yano 2026, with a different comparator set (placebo, usual care and active drugs) and estimator, reports OR 0.39 (0.15 to 1.00) for suicide and no significant effect on all-cause mortality [[Yano2026]].

### Exam hooks
- A 2026 re-analysis of randomised trials found a weaker lithium suicide signal than Cipriani 2013: OR 0.39 (0.15 to 1.00), 6 vs 16 suicides, GRADE low to very low. [[Yano2026]]

### In plain words
- Studies still lean towards fewer suicides in people who take lithium, but a new, careful recount of the trials shows the evidence is less certain than older summaries suggested. Suicides in trials are rare, so the numbers are small.

## Open questions and controversies
- Does lithium specifically prevent suicide? Earlier meta-analyses and registers favour it [[Cipriani2013,Song2017]]; the VA add-on trial was negative [[Katz2022]]; the 2026 re-analysis finds a sparse, estimator-sensitive signal and calls for adequately powered pragmatic trials [[Yano2026]]. Status: open.
