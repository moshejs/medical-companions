Bipolar disorder, explained

Bipolar disorder is a long-term illness in which mood and energy move between depression and highs called mania or hypomania; about 2.4% of adults worldwide have a bipolar spectrum disorder at some point in life, usually starting in the teens or twenties. 191,329 The low periods usually last much longer than the highs, 246,247 and with treatment most episodes end: 98% of people treated for a first mania recovered from the episode within 2 years. 486 This guide animates the science, from mood episodes and lithium levels to therapy, safety and history.

Every animation can be driven from the keyboard: Tab to its timeline, then arrow keys to scrub and Enter to play or pause.

For students

Every module has an Exam hook box and a citation trail. The criteria counter, screening tools and lithium level explainer are teaching aids, not self-tests. Finish with flashcards, mnemonics and a quiz.

For people living with bipolar disorder, families and carers

Look for the In plain words boxes. Bipolar disorder is treatable, and many people stay well for long stretches. Bring questions to your own care team: the app reports what labels, guidelines and trials say, not what you should take or change.

How sure is each claim?

Research claims carry a label: Established Early evidence Hypothesis. Small numbers link to the Sources list.

Bipolar Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on bipolar disorder and lithium therapy, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people living with bipolar disorder, their families and carers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine without your own care team. Criteria, screening tools and the lithium level explainer are for learning, not self-diagnosis or reading your own results. Doses, levels and monitoring schedules are quoted as published in labels and guidelines, never as instructions; suspected lithium toxicity is an emergency. Please check the original sources, which are listed in full in the Sources module.
