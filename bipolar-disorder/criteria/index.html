Criterion B

Three of seven symptoms

At least 3 of 7 symptoms, present to a significant degree and a noticeable change from usual behaviour: grandiosity, decreased need for sleep, more talkative or pressured, flight of ideas or racing thoughts, distractibility, increased goal-directed activity or agitation, and risky activities with painful consequences. 15,525

In plain wordsSleeping much less but not feeling tired, talking fast, thoughts racing, starting many projects: a cluster of changes, not one.
Exam hookDIGFAST (a memory aid): Distractibility, Irresponsibility, Grandiosity, Flight of ideas, Activity, Sleep need down, Talkativeness.

Rules that often come up

Bipolar I

One manic episode is enough

At least one lifetime manic episode is required for bipolar I disorder, and is sufficient. Hypomanic or major depressive episodes may come before or after it but are not needed. 15,320 How episodes combine into bipolar I, II and cyclothymic disorder is in 2.2.

Antidepressants

Mania that starts on treatment

A full manic episode that emerges during antidepressant treatment (medicine or ECT) and persists at a fully syndromal level beyond the treatment's physiological effect is sufficient evidence for a manic episode, and so for bipolar I. DSM-IV did not allow this. 15,525

DSM-5 change

Energy joined mood in Criterion A

DSM-5 (2013) added "abnormally and persistently increased activity or energy"; in DSM-IV a mood change alone was enough. 197,525 Early evidence Grunze and colleagues report that the extra requirement cuts the prevalence of manic or hypomanic episodes by at least a third; whether that helps practice is unresolved. 197

Bipolar depression

No separate checklist

Bipolar depression is a major depressive episode in someone with bipolar disorder; DSM-5 criteria for it are unchanged from DSM-IV. No single feature tells it apart from unipolar depression. 342,525 The clues that raise suspicion are in 2.4.

Mixed features

Both poles at once

DSM-5 removed the categorical "mixed episode" and replaced it with a "mixed features" specifier, which can also apply in major depressive disorder. ICD-11 still keeps a mixed episode of at least 2 weeks. 475,525

Hypomania length

Why 4 days?

DSM-5-TR requires 4 days and lists 2–3-day hypomania with major depressive episodes as "other specified bipolar and related disorder". In bipolar II, people with 2–6-day hypomanias did not differ from those with longer ones, and ICD-11 says only "at least several days". 247,452,475

Criteria describe episodes, not people. Most people with bipolar disorder spend much more of their time low than high, and getting the diagnosis right matters because effective treatment exists and helps many people live well. 226,246,298

Bipolar Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on bipolar disorder and lithium therapy, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people living with bipolar disorder, their families and carers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine without your own care team. Criteria, screening tools and the lithium level explainer are for learning, not self-diagnosis or reading your own results. Doses, levels and monitoring schedules are quoted as published in labels and guidelines, never as instructions; suspected lithium toxicity is an emergency.
