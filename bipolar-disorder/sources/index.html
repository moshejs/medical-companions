An animated, cited guide for students, people living with bipolar disorder, and their families and carers. Educational only, not medical advice. Your place is saved on this device.

Not medical adviceThe Bipolar Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. It does not replace a psychiatrist, nurse, pharmacist or other clinician. The criteria counter, screening tools and lithium level explainer are teaching aids, not self-tests, and doses and lithium levels are quoted as published, never as instructions. Suspected lithium toxicity is an emergency. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123).

Sources

Every reference behind the Bipolar Companion: journal articles, Cochrane and other systematic reviews, guidelines, drug labels and trial registrations. The small numbers throughout the app point here; filter by research area or search.

Eight research passes ran in parallel, one per area: epidemiology, presentation, burden and history; causes, genetics and environment; mechanisms, from the body clock to how lithium works; diagnosis, screening and the differential; lithium; the other medicines; psychological care, suicide safety, living well, families and nursing; and course, comorbidity, pregnancy and research toward better treatment. Before inclusion each source was checked, where possible, against PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, ClinicalTrials.gov or openFDA, and the green tag on an entry names the service that confirmed it. Works found by more than one pass appear once, with any extra keys listed beside them.

Entries marked not matched in a research database are pages with no PMID or DOI, or works that could not be confirmed. They are not cited for any fact in this guide.

Bipolar Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on bipolar disorder and lithium therapy, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people living with bipolar disorder, their families and carers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine without your own care team. Criteria, screening tools and the lithium level explainer are for learning, not self-diagnosis or reading your own results. Doses, levels and monitoring schedules are quoted as published in labels and guidelines, never as instructions; suspected lithium toxicity is an emergency. Please check the original sources, which are listed in full in the Sources module.
