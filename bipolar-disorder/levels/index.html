Lithium levels, toxicity & interactions

The amount of lithium in the blood matters: too little may not work and too much can harm, so blood tests are part of the treatment. Watch a week of dosing build to a steady level, see how the kidney handles lithium, and learn what the published ranges mean. 132,372

Absorption peak

Lithium is reported to be completely absorbed in the upper gut. The peak serum level comes 0.25–3 hours after an immediate-release dose and 2–6 hours after a sustained-release one. 132 Sustained-release forms lower the peak by 30–50% without much change in the total exposure (the area under the curve): switch the tablet type above to compare. 192 Lithium spreads through roughly total body water, 0.7–1 L/kg, and barely binds to plasma proteins. 132

In plain wordsAfter a tablet, the blood level rises within a few hours and then slowly falls. Slow-release tablets give a lower, flatter peak.
Exam hookTime to peak: immediate release 0.25–3 h, sustained release 2–6 h; sustained release lowers the peak by 30–50%. 132,192

The kidney: lithium is handled like sodium

Lithium is not broken down by the liver. The kidney filters it and about 80% of what is filtered is reabsorbed in the proximal tubule, much as sodium is; when the body is short of sodium or water, it holds on to more lithium. Lithium follows sodium

The glomerulus filters lithium, and about 80% of the filtered lithium is reabsorbed by passive diffusion in the proximal tubule; the rest leaves in the urine, in proportion to the serum level. Lithium reduces sodium reabsorption, and a low-sodium state makes the kidney hold on to more lithium, so the labels stress a normal salt intake and, while levels settle, adequate fluid (2500–3000 mL in the generic label). 132,133 Switch on a situation or medicine above.

In plain wordsThe kidney cannot tell lithium from sodium very well. When the body is short of salt or water, it keeps both.

Pattern

Chronic poisoning is the common one

Lithium poisoning is acute, acute-on-chronic or chronic. Chronic poisoning is the most common and usually unintentional: intake outstrips what the kidneys clear, often during volume depletion, nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, illness or a new interacting drug. In 97 cases, 26 of 28 with severe neurotoxicity came from chronic therapeutic use (OR 136 against overdose). 22,385

Who is at risk

Older age, kidneys, thyroid

Independent risk factors for chronic poisoning were nephrogenic diabetes insipidus (adjusted OR 26.96), thyroid dysfunction (9.30) and age over 50 (6.20). In Ontario, 3.9% of adults aged 66 or over taking lithium were admitted at least once for toxicity over 1992–2001. 248,385

Perspective

Rare with monitoring

In a Swedish cohort of 1,340 people, levels of 1.5 mmol/L or more occurred 0.01 times per patient-year; of 91 episodes, 34% needed intensive care and 13% haemodialysis, with no deaths. The authors said physicians "should not withhold lithium for fear of intoxication" but should screen for it readily. 391

Lasting harm

SILENT

The syndrome of irreversible lithium-effectuated neurotoxicity means sequelae lasting at least 2 months after lithium stops; a 2005 review found 90 published cases, most often with persistent cerebellar problems. 2,132

How toxicity is treated (as published)

No antidote; charcoal does not help

There is no specific antidote. Mild toxicity can usually be managed by lowering or stopping the dose. Activated charcoal is not recommended because it does not bind lithium ions significantly. 132

Haemodialysis

The generic label calls haemodialysis "the treatment of choice" for severe toxicity; levels can rebound as lithium leaves the tissues. EXTRIP graded lithium as dialyzable. 98,132

EXTRIP thresholds

Recommended with impaired kidney function and a level above 4.0 mEq/L, or with decreased consciousness, seizures or life-threatening dysrhythmias at any level; suggested above 5.0, with significant confusion, or if the level is expected to take more than 36 hours to fall below 1.0. The evidence is very low quality, mostly case reports. 22,98

Sick days

NICE advises people taking lithium to seek medical attention if they develop diarrhoea or vomiting or become acutely ill for any reason, and to keep up fluids after sweating, in hot climates, with fever, during long immobility or with a chest infection. 372
