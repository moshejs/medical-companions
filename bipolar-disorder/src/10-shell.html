Bipolar disorder, explained

Bipolar disorder is a long-term illness in which mood and energy move between depression and highs called mania or hypomania; about 2.4% of adults worldwide have a bipolar spectrum disorder at some point in life, usually starting in the teens or twenties. [[Grande2016,Merikangas2011]] The low periods usually last much longer than the highs, [[Judd2002,Judd2003]] and with treatment most episodes end: 98% of people treated for a first mania recovered from the episode within 2 years. [[Tohen2003]] This guide animates the science, from mood episodes and lithium levels to therapy, safety and history.

Every animation can be driven from the keyboard: Tab to its timeline, then arrow keys to scrub and Enter to play or pause.

For students

Every module has an Exam hook box and a citation trail. The criteria counter, screening tools and lithium level explainer are teaching aids, not self-tests. Finish with flashcards, mnemonics and a quiz.

For people living with bipolar disorder, families and carers

Look for the In plain words boxes. Bipolar disorder is treatable, and many people stay well for long stretches. Bring questions to your own care team: the app reports what labels, guidelines and trials say, not what you should take or change.
