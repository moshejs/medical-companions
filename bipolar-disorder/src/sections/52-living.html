Your own mood chart

The NIMH Life Chart Method rates mood every day by how much it gets in the way of daily life, and its ratings tracked clinicians' scales closely in 270 people (depression r = −0.785 against the IDS-C; mania r = 0.656 against the YMRS). [[Denicoff2000]] The tracker below borrows that idea in a simple form: one mood rating and one sleep figure a day.

Your entries stay in this browser on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere. This is a teaching tool for keeping a chart to share with your care team, not a test: it does not diagnose, score or predict anything.

Using a chart well

A chart is a conversation starter

  • Write your own warning signs on your plan. Sleep change is the commonest, but each person's list is different. [[Jackson2003]]
  • Bring the chart to appointments. Self-monitoring of mood, thoughts and behaviour is part of the relapse-prevention care NICE recommends. [[NICE2014CG185]]
  • Monitoring alone is not treatment: in the MONARCA I trial, daily smartphone self-monitoring with feedback did not reduce symptoms compared with a control phone. What helps is the plan and the people acting on it. [[FaurholtJepsen2015]]
In plain wordsKeeping a daily mood chart helps you and your team spot changes early. [[Denicoff2000,Perry1999BMJ]]

Staying well: what the evidence says

Plans

A relapse-prevention plan, made while well

NICE CG185 says to develop a plan jointly with the person and their carer covering triggers and early warning signs, the person's own coping strategies, how services will respond, and who to contact in a crisis (recommendation 1.4.1). [[NICE2014CG185]]

Joint crisis plans cut compulsory admission or treatment from 27% to 13% in one trial; the larger CRIMSON trial found no significant effect (18% vs 20%), possibly because plans were not fully put into practice. [[Henderson2004BMJ,Thornicroft2013CRIMSON]] Across 13 trials, advance statements reduced compulsory admissions by 23% (RR 0.77). [[deJong2016]]

NICE advises discussing a lasting power of attorney when mania or hypomania has caused money problems (1.1.5). [[NICE2014CG185]] See also Families, work & school.

Sleep & routine

Protecting sleep and a regular day

Hypothesis Sleep loss can trigger a switch into mania, and mania cuts sleep further: a self-reinforcing loop, with sleep reduction proposed as a "final common pathway". [[Wehr1987]]

Early evidence In IPSRT, people who made their daily rhythms more regular were less likely to have a recurrence. In a pilot of insomnia-focused CBT, days in a mood episode over 6 months were 3.3 vs 25.5 with psychoeducation. [[Frank2005IPSRT,Harvey2015CBTI]] More in talking therapies and the body clock.

Substances

Alcohol, drugs and smoking

In national surveys, about a third of people with bipolar disorder (33%) had a substance use disorder: 24% alcohol and 17% illicit drugs. In clinical samples alcohol use disorder reached 42% and cannabis 20%, and substance use went with earlier onset and more hospital stays. [[Hunt2016Surveys,Hunt2016Clinical]]

Smoking is more common than in the general population (OR 3.5), and quitting is less common (OR 0.34). [[Jackson2015Smoking]]

Physical health

The yearly health check

NICE CG185 says the annual physical health check should cover weight or BMI, diet, nutrition and activity; pulse and blood pressure; fasting glucose or HbA1c and lipids; and liver, kidney and thyroid function and calcium (1.2.12). People on antipsychotics and long-term medicines should be offered a healthy-eating and activity programme (1.8.2). [[NICE2014CG185]]

Metabolic syndrome affects 37.3% of people with bipolar disorder (45.3% on antipsychotics, 32.4% without). [[Vancampfort2013]] Severe mental illness carries a higher rate of cardiovascular disease (HR 1.78). [[Correll2017CVD]]

The mortality gap

Why the heart matters

As a population statistic, bipolar disorders are associated with about 10–20 potential years of life lost, mainly from excess cardiovascular deaths and suicide. [[McIntyre2020Lancet]] Natural causes matter, not only unnatural ones: the standardised mortality ratio is 1.64 for natural causes, 1.73 for circulatory and 2.92 for respiratory deaths. [[Hayes2015]] These are averages across large groups; regular checks and treatment of each risk factor are how the gap is narrowed for a person. [[NICE2014CG185,Correll2017CVD]]

Work & study

Working with bipolar disorder

Across 25 studies, about 40–60% of people with established bipolar disorder were employed, more so early in the illness; 40–50% may slide in job status over time, which argues for early support. [[Marwaha2013]] Better verbal memory and executive function and more years of education went with better work outcomes; more hospital stays and depression with poorer ones. [[Tse2014]]

Getting back to work often lags behind feeling better: leftover low mood and thinking problems are the strongest links to poorer functioning. [[Gitlin2017]] NICE recommends supported employment for people who want to work (1.9.6). [[NICE2014CG185]]

Driving

Driving

A review states that people with acute mania are not fit to drive, but that driving ability can usually be confirmed after remission under psychiatric supervision; about 17% of people in remission had impaired driving performance in testing. [[Laux2014]] The lithium label cautions that lithium "may impair mental and/or physical abilities" and advises care with tasks that need alertness, such as driving. [[FDA-Lithobid]] Rules differ by country: check with your care team and licensing authority.

Stigma

Self-stigma

Across 25 studies, internalised (self-)stigma in bipolar disorder was moderate to high. [[Ellison2013Stigma]] In GAMIAN-Europe, 21.7% of people with bipolar disorder or depression reported moderate or high self-stigma and 71.6% perceived discrimination, but 59.7% also showed moderate or high stigma resistance. [[Brohan2011]]

Many people with bipolar disorder work [[Marwaha2013]], and adding psychosocial treatments and involving caregivers "have been shown to improve health outcomes". [[McIntyre2020Lancet]]

CrisisIf you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123). Help works [[Nuij2021]], and you do not have to wait for a planned appointment. See Suicide risk & crisis safety.
