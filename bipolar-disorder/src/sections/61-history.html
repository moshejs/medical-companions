History & people

The idea that mania and depression can be two sides of one illness is almost two thousand years old. Its name, its borders and its treatment changed many times. Play the timeline from Aretaeus to the genome studies of the 2020s, or tap a year to jump.

The years before 1850 are compressed on the wide timeline. Approval years are as given in the cited reviews.

How the name changed

Each name drew the border of the illness in a different place: around alternating mania and melancholia, around all mood disorders, or around two kinds of bipolar disorder kept separate from unipolar depression. Choose a name to see what it included.

How the manuals wrote it down

ManualYearTerm and change
DSM-I1952"Manic depressive reaction", classed as a psychotic disorder
DSM-II1968"Manic-depressive illness", moved under the affective disorders
DSM-III1980Specific criteria and the term "bipolar disorder"; a manic episode alone is enough; "bipolar II" appears only as an example of atypical bipolar disorder
DSM-III-R1987A "hypomanic syndrome" for symptoms without marked impairment
DSM-IV1994A stand-alone hypomanic episode (at least 4 days, no marked impairment, no psychosis), the building block of bipolar II; a "mixed episode" added
DSM-52013Increased activity or energy becomes a required core symptom of mania and hypomania; the mixed episode is replaced by a "with mixed features" specifier
ICD-112019 / 2022Approved by the World Health Assembly in May 2019, in use from 1 January 2022; keeps the bipolar type I / type II split
DSM-5-TR2022Text revision published in March 2022

Sources: DSM-I to DSM-5 [[Mason2016]]; ICD-11 [[Reed2019,Angst2020ICD11]]; DSM-5-TR [[APA2022]]. Adding the energy criterion cut the number of people with a manic or hypomanic visit by 34% in one network and by at least a third in a review, without changing longitudinal outcomes in STEP-BD. [[Fredskild2019,Grunze2021,MachadoVieira2017]] ICD-11 and DSM-5 are "closer than at any time since the ICD-8 and DSM-II". [[First2021]] For today's criteria see DSM-5-TR criteria.

People behind the ideas

1854

Falret and Baillarger: a quarrel over priority

On 31 January 1854 Jules Baillarger lectured to the French Imperial Academy of Medicine on "la folie à double forme". Jean-Pierre Falret replied that he had already described the same illness as "folie circulaire" and lectured on it on 14 February. Baillarger accused Falret of plagiarism; a later study of the printed record supports Falret's priority and finds his description the closer to modern ideas. [[Pichot2004]] Falret even hinted that brief hypomanias at the end of depressions might be mild forms of the illness, an early glimpse of the bipolar spectrum. [[Haustgen2006]]

1899

Emil Kraepelin

Kraepelin brought all types of affective disorder together as "manic-depressive insanity", a unitary concept adopted worldwide, [[Angst2001]] and separated it from dementia praecox mainly by the deterioration seen in the latter. [[Pichot2004]] His descriptions of mood symptoms "have arguably never been surpassed". [[Mondimore2005]] His final views stressed mixed features and a temperament seen between episodes and in unaffected relatives. [[Kendler2021]]

1949–1970

John Cade and Mogens Schou

Lithium had been used in psychiatry in the mid-19th century, but that work was forgotten. [[Shorter2009]] In 1949 John Cade published "Lithium salts in the treatment of psychotic excitement" in the Medical Journal of Australia. [[Cade1949,Shorter2009]] In 1954 Mogens Schou and colleagues reported a randomly controlled trial in manic psychoses, [[Schou1954,Shorter2009]] and in 1967 Baastrup and Schou reported lithium as a preventive treatment. [[Baastrup1967]] See Lithium: how it works and the evidence.

1957–1966

Leonhard, Angst, Perris and Winokur

Karl Leonhard's 1957 classification distinguished bipolar from unipolar ("monopolar") forms. [[Haustgen2006,Perris1992]] In 1966 Jules Angst and Carlo Perris independently published family, clinical and course data separating bipolar from unipolar depressive illness, and George Winokur's work supported the same split. [[Angst1966,Perris1966,Angst2001]] In Pichot's words, Falret's illness "regained its autonomy only in 1966". [[Pichot2004]]
