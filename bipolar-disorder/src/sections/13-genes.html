Genes & family risk

Bipolar disorder runs in families, but no single gene causes it and most relatives never develop it. Step through one family, from the general population to an identical twin, and watch the numbers build from twin, family and register studies.

Population statistics, not a prediction for any one person. How heritable? Twins against whole populations

Heritability is the share of the differences in liability between people in a population that is linked to genetic differences. It is not the chance that a given person inherits the condition. Clinical twin series give the highest figures; whole-population Swedish registers give lower ones, probably because of how severe cases were found, the diagnostic method and small twin samples with wide confidence intervals. The usual summary is "about 60–85%". [[McGuffin2003,Kieseppa2004,Lichtenstein2009,Mullins2021]]

The gap between 60–85% from twins and about 16–23% from measured common variants is the "missing heritability". Rare variants, undiscovered common variants and non-additive effects are candidate explanations; the question is open. [[Stahl2019,Mullins2021,OConnell2025,Palmer2022Exome]]

Genes and environment: a liability threshold

A classic way to picture a condition with many small causes: everyone sits somewhere on a scale of liability, and an episode or onset happens past a threshold. Genes set where a person starts; experiences can push liability up for a while. Switch factors on to see the curve shift. Each one is an association from the studies named, not proof of cause, and the size of each shift is drawn, not calculated.

Schematic liability-threshold model. Shifts are illustrative and do not add up to any person's risk. Sleep loss and the postpartum period mostly act as triggers in people who already have bipolar disorder; the others are linked with onset.

What this means for families

Established

Risk, not destiny

In the Danish register, 95.6% of children with one parent admitted with bipolar disorder had not been diagnosed with it by age 52. By the 22-year Dutch follow-up of children of a parent with bipolar disorder, 87–89% had not developed it. Even an identical co-twin develops bipolar I in fewer than half of cases (concordance 0.43). [[Gottesman2010,Helmink2025,Kieseppa2004]]

Established

Shared genetics with schizophrenia and depression

From genome-wide SNPs, the genetic correlation of bipolar disorder is about 0.68–0.70 with schizophrenia and 0.44–0.47 with major depression. In Swedish families, 63% of the overlap between schizophrenia and bipolar disorder came from shared genetic effects. Bipolar I leans toward schizophrenia (driven by psychosis); bipolar II leans toward depression. [[PGCCDG2013NatGenet,PGCCDG2019,Mullins2021,Lichtenstein2009,Stahl2019]]

In children at family risk

Low mood usually comes first

In the Dutch offspring study, 88% of those who developed a bipolar spectrum disorder began with a depressive episode. In Pittsburgh, subthreshold manic or hypomanic episodes were the strongest precursor of a full episode (hazard ratio 7.57). In the Canadian cohort the median age at onset was 20.7 years. [[Mesman2013,Axelson2015,Duffy2019AJP]]

Established

No DNA test can diagnose it

The best polygenic score separates cases from controls with an AUC of about 0.65–0.70; its authors call the liability explained "insufficient for diagnostic prediction in the general population". A polygenic score alone will never establish a diagnosis. There are no clinical practice guidelines for genetic testing in bipolar disorder. [[Mullins2021,OConnell2025,Murray2021PRS,Austin2020GC]]

Early evidence

Rare variants: a smaller part

Large rare copy-number variants matter less than in schizophrenia; a 16p11.2 duplication is the best supported. Exome sequencing of 13,933 cases found an excess of ultra-rare damaging variants and named AKAP11 (odds ratio 7.06), a gene shared with schizophrenia whose protein interacts with GSK3B, a proposed lithium target. [[Grozeva2010,Green2016CNV,Palmer2022Exome]]

Support

Genetic counselling helps

Genetic counselling is not genetic testing. In a pilot randomised trial, both counselling and a booklet improved knowledge, but only counselling made people's own risk estimates more accurate. Real risk is often lower than families fear. [[Hippman2016,Austin2020GC]] For how episodes look and are diagnosed, see Mania, hypomania & depression; for the body clock, Brain, sleep & the body clock.
