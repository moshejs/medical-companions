Part 2 · Diagnosis

Bipolar or something else?

Low moods, mood swings, restlessness and highs all have more than one cause. The shape of mood over time is often what tells them apart: distinct episodes, a lifelong pattern, hour-to-hour shifts, or a change that starts and stops with a substance.

Schematic courses, not real people. Each pattern is the typical teaching contrast; real presentations overlap, and two conditions can be present together. [[Paris2015,Yatham2018,Kitsune2016]]

Clues to hidden bipolarity in depression

No single feature separates bipolar from unipolar depression; guidelines suggest weighing several clues together. [[Mitchell2008,Yatham2018]] Tap the clues to see what each one is linked to. This is a teaching list, not a score or a self-test: the clues raise suspicion and call for a careful history, a family member's account and charting over time.

Medical and substance causes to rule out

"Secondary mania" can follow drugs, infection, tumours, epilepsy and metabolic disturbance in people with no history of mood disorder. [[Krauthammer1978]] Satzer and Bond advise suspecting it with neurological deficits, a history atypical for bipolar disorder, or a first manic episode after age 40. [[Satzer2016]]

Missed, delayed and over-made diagnoses

Delay

Years to the right diagnosis

A meta-analysis of 27 studies found a pooled 5.8 years between onset and management. In a patient-association survey, 69% had been misdiagnosed, most often with unipolar depression, and over a third waited 10 years or more. [[Dagani2017,Hirschfeld2003Perceptions]]

Over-diagnosis

A problem in the other direction

In 700 outpatients interviewed with the SCID, fewer than half of those who reported a previous bipolar diagnosis met criteria; the authors call over-diagnosis a problem "equal if not greater" than under-diagnosis. CANMAT names borderline personality disorder, substance use disorder and ADHD as the usual sources, and advises to "strictly adhere to diagnostic criteria". [[Zimmerman2008,Yatham2018]]

Borderline PD

Both can be present

Borderline personality disorder was found in 21.6% of people with bipolar disorder (37.7% in bipolar II samples), and bipolar disorder in 18.5% of people with borderline personality disorder. Having one does not rule out the other. [[Fornaro2016]] More in the Borderline Personality Disorder Companion.

ADHD

Overlap in adults and children

17.11% of adults with bipolar disorder had ADHD, and comorbid ADHD brought bipolar onset 3.96 years earlier. In 1999–2003, 32.2% of US youth bipolar visits also carried an ADHD diagnosis. [[Schiweck2021,Moreno2007]] More in the ADHD Companion.

Children

Irritability is not enough

Severe, non-episodic irritability in youth predicts later anxiety and unipolar depression, not bipolar disorder, the work behind disruptive mood dysregulation disorder. NICE: diagnose mania in children only after prospective monitoring, with euphoria on most days for at least 7 days; irritability is "not a core diagnostic criterion". [[Leibenluft2011,APA2022,NICE2014CG185]]

Psychosis

Schizophrenia and schizoaffective disorder

Psychotic disorders are the second most frequent initial misdiagnosis, in up to 30%. Mood-incongruent psychotic symptoms were the only factor linked to an initial diagnosis of schizophrenia. In bipolar disorder with psychotic features, psychosis occurs only during mood episodes. [[Yatham2018,GonzalezPinto1998,APA2022]]
