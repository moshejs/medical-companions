Treating bipolar depression

Depression takes up more of the illness than mania does, and the treatments that work for depression on its own do not carry straight over. See what happened when antidepressants were tested in STEP-BD, what the ISBD task force concluded, and how CANMAT/ISBD 2018 ranks the first-line options.

Switch risk: an antidepressant alone, or with a mood stabiliser?

A "switch" is a turn from depression into mania or hypomania after starting a treatment. A Swedish registry study followed 3,240 people with bipolar disorder who started an antidepressant, comparing each person with themselves over time. Nearly 35% started it without a mood stabiliser. Beyond tablets

ECT

Electroconvulsive therapy

In the only randomised trial of ECT in treatment-resistant bipolar depression (73 inpatients in Norway), ECT beat algorithm-based medication by 6.6 MADRS points; response was 73.9% vs 35.0%, though remission did not differ (34.8% vs 30.0%). Across cohort studies, remission was similar in bipolar (53.2%) and unipolar (50.9%) depression. [[Schoeyen2015,Dierckx2012]]

CANMAT/ISBD 2018 lists it second-line, "particularly for treatment‐refractory patients and those for whom a rapid treatment response is needed". ECT is a well-studied treatment that can help when depression is severe or other treatments have not worked. [[Yatham2018]]

Early evidence

Ketamine

In a small crossover trial (18 people already on lithium or valproate), one intravenous ketamine infusion improved depression within 40 minutes (d = 0.52), peaking at day 2 (d = 0.80); 71% responded at some point vs 6% on placebo. A replication (15 people) found 79% vs 0%. [[Diazgranados2010,Zarate2012]]

CANMAT/ISBD 2018 places it third-line "due to its invasive nature, short duration of effect, and lack of long‐term safety data". Its effects so far are fast but short-lived. [[Yatham2018]]

Early evidence

Bright light therapy

In a 6-week trial of people on antimanic medication, midday 7,000-lux light gave remission in 68.2% vs 22.2% with dim red light, with no switches. A meta-analysis of 6 trials was inconclusive overall. The ISBD Chronobiology Task Force judges adjunctive light likely efficacious and recommends anti-manic cover and clinical monitoring. The hardest phase

Why depression is the hardest part

Over 12.8 years of weekly follow-up in bipolar I, depressive symptoms took up 31.9% of weeks vs 8.9% for manic or hypomanic symptoms; in bipolar II, 50.3% vs 1.3%. About 75% of symptomatic time is depressive. For response, add-on antidepressants have an NNT of 29, against 4–6 for the most effective options. [[Judd2002,Judd2003,Nierenberg2023,Ketter2014]]

When treatments fail

Treatment-resistant bipolar depression

The ISBD's 2025 task force defined it as failure of at least two approved, adequately dosed treatments, for example quetiapine for at least 8 weeks or lurasidone, cariprazine or lumateperone for at least 6 weeks. The definition is meant to standardise future trials. [[Vieta2025TRBD]]

Recovery

Episodes do end

In 219 people with bipolar I followed for up to 25 years, the median mood episode lasted 13 weeks, and more than 75% recovered from their episodes within a year of onset. These are group figures.
