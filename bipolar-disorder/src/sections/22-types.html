Specifiers: describing the course

Specifiers add detail to the diagnosis. Some describe an episode (anxious distress, mixed features, psychotic features, catatonia, peripartum onset; melancholic or atypical features for depression); two describe the illness course (rapid cycling, seasonal pattern). Beyond the three main types

Bipolar II

Not "bipolar lite"

CANMAT calls the idea of bipolar II as a less severe form "long-standing but discredited": disability is comparable to bipolar I, the economic burden up to four times greater, and people spend as much time symptomatic, mostly depressed. Rates of attempted suicide are similar in the two types, a population statistic that is a reason for regular, kind check-ins and early help, not for fear. The diagnosis is generally stable, with a possibly higher chance of conversion to bipolar I early in the illness. [[Yatham2018]]

How common

Population figures

In WHO World Mental Health surveys of 61,392 adults in 11 countries, lifetime prevalence was 0.6% for bipolar I, 0.4% for bipolar II and 1.4% for subthreshold bipolar disorder: 2.4% for the whole spectrum. These describe populations, not anyone's personal chance. [[Merikangas2011]]

Early evidence

Cyclothymia is under-recognised

Van Meter and colleagues describe cyclothymic disorder as rarely diagnosed despite evidence that it "may be the most prevalent form of bipolar disorder", as highly impairing, and as a possible diathesis for major mood disorders. [[VanMeter2012]]

Other categories

Other specified and unspecified

DSM-5 replaced "bipolar disorder not otherwise specified" with "other specified" and "unspecified" bipolar and related disorder. Examples of other specified include short hypomanias (2–3 days) with major depressive episodes, and hypomanic episodes with too few symptoms, with major depressive episodes. Severus and Bauer warn that a diagnosis made after treatment has started can be shaped by that treatment. [[Severus2014,Yatham2018]]

Other causes

Substance, medicine or medical condition

DSM-5 has separate categories for substance/medication-induced bipolar and related disorder and for bipolar and related disorder due to another medical condition. A manic episode needs Criterion D: not attributable to a substance or another medical condition. The exception is a full manic episode that emerges on antidepressant treatment and persists at a fully syndromal level beyond the treatment's effect, which counts towards bipolar I. [[APA2022,Yatham2018]]

Hypothesis

A wider "soft" spectrum?

In the Zurich cohort, softer criteria gave a "soft bipolar spectrum" of 23.7%, and the authors argued that hypomania should be defined by 3 of 7 symptoms plus a change in functioning, whatever its length. In the US NCS-R, nearly 40% of people with major depression had a history of subthreshold hypomania (early evidence). These ideas are not current criteria. [[Angst2003,Angst2010NCSR]]
