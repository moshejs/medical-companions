Part 1 · Understanding bipolar disorder

Brain, sleep & the body clock

No one yet knows exactly what causes bipolar disorder, but sleep and daily rhythm are among the clearest links. Scrub through three weeks of one illustrative person's nights, then look at what brain scans and brain chemistry do and do not show.

What brain scans and brain chemistry show

Schematic. Region positions are approximate and the synapse is a cartoon. Effect sizes are group averages from large pooled studies (ENIGMA); they overlap heavily with people without bipolar disorder. [[Hibar2018,Hibar2016,Ashok2017]]

Why a scan cannot diagnose one person

Curves drawn from the published Cohen's d assuming normal distributions; the overlap figure is computed from d, not reported by the studies. [[Hibar2018,Hibar2016]]

The largest cortical effect in 1,837 adults with bipolar disorder was in the left pars opercularis, Cohen's d −0.293; the hippocampus was d −0.232 and the thalamus d −0.148. Effects this size mean the two groups overlap almost completely: most people with bipolar disorder have measures well inside the range of people without it. [[Hibar2018,Hibar2016]]

Medicines also confound scans. In a 2026 ENIGMA mega-analysis, people not taking medication had only mild ventricular enlargement (d 0.07), while antiepileptic and antipsychotic use went with smaller hippocampal and thalamic volumes. [[King2026ENIGMA]]

There is no brain-scan or blood test for bipolar disorder. Diagnosis rests on a careful history of mood episodes: see DSM-5-TR criteria. [[Hibar2018,Ching2022]]

Does each episode make the next more likely?

Direction only. The Danish register abstracts give no recurrence percentages, so the bars have no scale. [[Kessing1998Recurrence,Kessing2004Course]]

Other threads

Early evidence

Daily routines as clock-setters

The social zeitgeber theory proposes that people, meals and tasks help set the body clock, and that losing them can destabilise it. In 39 people, routine-disrupting life events were more common in the 8 weeks before a manic onset, but not before depression. In a trial of 175 adults with bipolar I, interpersonal and social rhythm therapy in the acute phase lengthened time well over 2 years, and people whose routines became more regular were less likely to relapse. [[Ehlers1988,MalkoffSchwartz1998,Frank2005IPSRT]] More in Psychoeducation & talking therapies.

Established

Evening types and the seasons

Across 42 studies, circadian disruption appeared even in people never treated and between episodes, with a delayed melatonin and cortisol rhythm and more evening chronotypes. Mania peaks in spring and summer and depression in early winter; a seasonal pattern was found for 15% of manic and 25% of depressive episodes. [[Melo2017,Geoffroy2014]]

Early evidence

Clock genes, mostly in animals

Mice with a Clock gene mutation are hyperactive, sleep less and value rewards more; lithium returned many behaviours to normal, and restoring the clock protein in a dopamine area rescued them. In people, the largest GWAS did not list core clock genes among its headline findings, and whether clock problems cause mood episodes remains controversial. [[Roybal2007,Mullins2021,McCarthy2012]]

Early evidence

Inflammation and stress hormones

CRP, IL-6 and TNF-α are raised on average; CRP and TNF-α behave like state markers, rising during episodes, while IL-6 stays raised. Cortisol and ACTH are higher, more so in mania. BDNF, a nerve-growth protein, falls in mania and depression and normalises between episodes. Whether any of this causes episodes or follows them is not settled. [[Solmi2021Inflammation,BelvederiMurri2016,Fernandes2015]]

Hypothesis

Many layers, no single cause

Genes, the body clock, brain networks, cell energy, inflammation and stress are best seen as interacting layers rather than rival single causes. The cause of bipolar disorder is not known; heritability is around 70% in a Lancet review. [[McIntyre2020Lancet,Berk2011]] See Genes & family risk.

For everyday life

Protecting sleep

Regular times for waking, eating, working and sleeping help keep the body clock steady. A change of more than about 3 hours from usual sleep can come a day before a large mood change, so it is worth noticing and telling the care team.
