DSM-5-TR criteria

Bipolar disorder is diagnosed from episodes: a manic, hypomanic or major depressive episode each has its own checklist of mood, symptoms, duration, severity and exclusions. Watch each definition assemble gate by gate, then try a teaching counter.

DIGFAST is a widely taught memory aid, not part of DSM. Each letter points to one of the seven Criterion B symptoms of mania and hypomania. [[APA2022,Yatham2018]]

Rules that often come up

Bipolar I

One manic episode is enough

At least one lifetime manic episode is required for bipolar I disorder, and is sufficient. Hypomanic or major depressive episodes may come before or after it but are not needed. [[APA2022,McIntyre2020Lancet]] How episodes combine into bipolar I, II and cyclothymic disorder is in 2.2.

Antidepressants

Mania that starts on treatment

A full manic episode that emerges during antidepressant treatment (medicine or ECT) and persists at a fully syndromal level beyond the treatment's physiological effect is sufficient evidence for a manic episode, and so for bipolar I. DSM-IV did not allow this. [[APA2022,Yatham2018]]

DSM-5 change

Energy joined mood in Criterion A

DSM-5 (2013) added "abnormally and persistently increased activity or energy"; in DSM-IV a mood change alone was enough. [[Yatham2018,Grunze2021]] Early evidence Grunze and colleagues report that the extra requirement cuts the prevalence of manic or hypomanic episodes by at least a third; whether that helps practice is unresolved. [[Grunze2021]]

Bipolar depression

No separate checklist

Bipolar depression is a major depressive episode in someone with bipolar disorder; DSM-5 criteria for it are unchanged from DSM-IV. No single feature tells it apart from unipolar depression. [[Yatham2018,Mitchell2008]] The clues that raise suspicion are in 2.4.

Mixed features

Both poles at once

DSM-5 removed the categorical "mixed episode" and replaced it with a "mixed features" specifier, which can also apply in major depressive disorder. ICD-11 still keeps a mixed episode of at least 2 weeks. [[Yatham2018,Stein2020BMC]]

Hypomania length

Why 4 days?

DSM-5-TR requires 4 days and lists 2–3-day hypomania with major depressive episodes as "other specified bipolar and related disorder". In bipolar II, people with 2–6-day hypomanias did not differ from those with longer ones, and ICD-11 says only "at least several days". [[Severus2014,Judd2003,Stein2020BMC]]

Criteria describe episodes, not people. Most people with bipolar disorder spend much more of their time low than high, and getting the diagnosis right matters because effective treatment exists and helps many people live well. [[Judd2002,Lish1994,Hirschfeld2003Perceptions]]
