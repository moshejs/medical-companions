Screening & rating scales

A screening questionnaire sorts people into "look closer" and "probably not". How well it sorts depends on the setting: follow 1,000 people through the Mood Disorder Questionnaire or the HCL-32 and watch what a positive result really means.

Why the same questionnaire behaves differently

MDQ

Mood Disorder Questionnaire

13 yes/no items on lifetime hypomanic or manic symptoms, a question on whether several happened in the same period, and a rating of how much of a problem they caused. The standard positive screen is 7 or more items, plus co-occurrence, plus a "moderate" or "serious" problem. [[Hirschfeld2000MDQ,Palmer2021]]

Setting

Clinic versus community

In mood clinics a score of 7 or more gave sensitivity 0.73 and specificity 0.90; in the US general population, 0.281 and 0.972. The authors note the figures "differ substantially" from those in psychiatric outpatients. [[Hirschfeld2000MDQ,Hirschfeld2003AJP]]

HCL-32

Hypomania Checklist

32 hypomania items. In its first study it had sensitivity 80% and specificity 51%, and could not separate bipolar I from bipolar II. In a meta-analysis it detected bipolar II better than the MDQ in mental-health settings. [[Angst2005HCL32,Carvalho2015]]

False positives

Who else screens positive

Among outpatients without bipolar disorder, 15.2% still screened positive on the MDQ, linked to PTSD, substance use, eating, impulse-control and attention-deficit disorders. Of 98 positive screens, 23.5% had bipolar disorder and 27.6% borderline personality disorder. [[Zimmerman2011MDQ,Zimmerman2010Screening]]

Overcounting

Screens inflate prevalence

In primary care, bipolar disorder was found in 3.4–9% of patients with depression or psychiatric complaints by structured interview, but 20.9–30.8% by screening measures. Using the MDQ as a "diagnostic proxy" has distorted conclusions about how common bipolar disorder is. [[Cerimele2013,Zimmerman2012MDQ]]

Guidelines differ

NICE and CANMAT

NICE: "Do not use questionnaires in primary care to identify bipolar disorder in adults", and the same for children and young people. CANMAT allows tools like the MDQ only as an adjunct for screening clinical populations, not for diagnosis or treatment planning, and recommends screening all women with depressive symptoms in pregnancy and postpartum, followed by a clinical interview. [[NICE2014CG185,Yatham2018]]

Rating scales measure severity, not diagnosis

A rating scale is filled in by a trained clinician to track how severe symptoms are and how they change with treatment. The Young Mania Rating Scale (YMRS) has 11 items and scores 0–60; inter-rater correlation was 0.93 for the total score and 0.66–0.92 for individual items, and it distinguished patients before and after two weeks of treatment. The item wording is not reproduced here, and the scale is not something to fill in for yourself. [[Young1978,Pigott2016Cochrane]]

A mood chart shows what memory misses

Diagnosis is more confident "when episodes are prospectively observed", and CANMAT recommends collateral information from family and friends plus ongoing mood charting. The NIMH Life Chart Method rates mood every day by how much it impairs functioning; its mania ratings correlated with the YMRS (r = 0.656). [[Yatham2018,Denicoff2000]]

Getting to a diagnosis

Structured interviews

How research makes the diagnosis

The SCID is semistructured and clinician-administered, built in modules that test diagnostic hypotheses through a decision tree. The WMH-CIDI is fully structured, given by trained lay interviewers, and scores diagnoses by computer; brief CIDI-based screens detected 67–96% of true cases, with a positive predictive value of 31–52%. [[Spitzer1992,Kessler2004CIDI,Kessler2006CIDI]]

In primary care

Ask about overactivity

NICE: when adults present with depression, ask about previous periods of overactivity or disinhibited behaviour; if it lasted 4 days or more, consider referral for a specialist assessment. Refer urgently if mania or severe depression is suspected, or if the person is a danger to themselves or others. [[NICE2014CG185]]

For patients and families

What to bring to an appointment

  • Any times of unusual energy, little need for sleep or racing thoughts, and how long they lasted
  • Periods of low mood, what seemed to trigger episodes, and how you were between them
  • Family history of mood problems
  • A family member or friend, who often remembers the highs more clearly
  • A mood chart, if you keep one

Based on NICE's list for assessing suspected bipolar disorder. [[NICE2014CG185,Ghaemi2002]]

Why it matters

The right diagnosis opens the right treatment

It often takes years: a pooled 5.8 years from onset to management. Effective treatment exists, and in a patient-association survey social difficulties were "diminished by effective treatment". [[Dagani2017,Lish1994]] What a screen can be mistaken for is in 2.4.
