Mnemonic in motion: DIGFAST and the episode gates

A manic or hypomanic episode is defined by a two-part Criterion A, seven Criterion B symptoms, a minimum duration and, for mania, a Criterion C that hypomania lacks [[APA2022,Yatham2018]]. Play the animation to pass each gate in turn: mood and energy, the DIGFAST letters, the 3-or-4 count, the 4-day and 1-week clocks, and the line between mania and hypomania that separates bipolar I from bipolar II. Criterion wording shortened for study. DIGFAST is a memory aid, not DSM-5-TR wording. A teaching tool, not for diagnosing yourself or anyone else. [[APA2022,Yatham2018]]

These cards are for learning, not for self-diagnosis or for interpreting your own test results or lithium levels. Suspected lithium toxicity is an emergency [[FDA-Lithium]]. Sources

Every reference behind the Bipolar Companion: journal articles, Cochrane and other systematic reviews, guidelines, drug labels and trial registrations. The small numbers throughout the app point here; filter by research area or search.

Eight research passes ran in parallel, one per area: epidemiology, presentation, burden and history; causes, genetics and environment; mechanisms, from the body clock to how lithium works; diagnosis, screening and the differential; lithium; the other medicines; psychological care, suicide safety, living well, families and nursing; and course, comorbidity, pregnancy and research toward better treatment. Before inclusion each source was checked, where possible, against PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, ClinicalTrials.gov or openFDA, and the green tag on an entry names the service that confirmed it. Works found by more than one pass appear once, with any extra keys listed beside them.

Entries marked check are pages with no PMID or DOI, or works that could not be confirmed. They are not cited for any fact in this guide.
