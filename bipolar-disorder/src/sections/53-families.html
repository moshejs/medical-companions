Families, work & school

Bipolar disorder is lived in families, classrooms and workplaces. Follow a family through family-focused therapy, from the home climate that research calls expressed emotion to the two-year relapse results, then sort what helps from what hurts.

What helps, what hurts: 12 cards for families and carers

Read each situation, decide, then tap to turn it over. The answers come from trials, reviews and NICE guidance, not opinion.

Families, children, school and work

Carers

Carer wellbeing and burden

In the STEP-BD Family Experience Study (caregivers of 500 patients), many carers reported moderate or greater burden:

Source of burdenCarers
Problem behaviours89%
Disrupted household routine61%
Role dysfunction52%

High-burden carers reported more physical health problems, depressive symptoms and health-risk behaviours, and less social support. [[Perlick2007]] Early evidence Family-focused therapy for carers reduced carers' depressive symptoms and health-risk behaviour. [[Perlick2010]] NICE says to offer all carers an education and support programme "with a positive message about recovery" (1.1.8). [[NICE2014CG185]]

Children

Children of a parent with bipolar disorder

In a meta-analysis of 33 family high-risk studies, offspring of parents with severe mental illness had a 32% probability of a severe mental illness by adulthood, about 2.5 times the risk of control offspring. [[Rasic2014]]

In the Dutch Bipolar Offspring Study (22 years, n=100), lifetime bipolar disorder was 11–13%, so most children (about 87–89%) did not develop it; any mood disorder reached 65%, which supports early help. [[Helmink2025]] NICE asks services to identify children who depend on or care for a person with bipolar disorder and to offer support (1.1.9). [[NICE2014CG185]] Twin, family and gene findings: Genes & family risk.

Adolescents & school

Teenagers and school

In 58 teenagers, family-focused therapy for adolescents did not change recovery or recurrence rates, but they recovered from depressive symptoms faster (HR 1.85). [[Miklowitz2008FFTA]] In 127 high-risk young people aged 9–17, 12 sessions of FFT lengthened the time to the next mood episode (HR 0.55), delaying depressive but not manic episodes. Early evidence [[Miklowitz2020FFT]]

In the COBY study, 81.5% of young people recovered from their index episode, but 62.5% had a recurrence, mostly depression, so ongoing support matters. [[Birmaher2009COBY]] With the person's agreement, NICE supports talking with education staff about how they can be supported (1.1.4). [[NICE2014CG185]] Constant irritability in a child is not by itself bipolar disorder. [[Leibenluft2011,Copeland2013DMDD]]

Work

Workplace adjustments

About 40–60% of people with established bipolar disorder are employed, more often early in the illness. [[Marwaha2013]] NICE recommends supported employment programmes for people who wish to work (1.9.6) and, with the person's agreement, talking with employers about how the person can be supported (1.1.4). [[NICE2014CG185]]

Return to work often lags behind symptom recovery; leftover low mood and thinking problems are the strongest links to poorer functioning. [[Gitlin2017]] More in Living well.

Planning ahead

Advance statements

Across 13 trials, advance statements reduced compulsory admissions by 23% (RR 0.77). [[deJong2016]] Peer-worker-supported psychiatric advance directives cut compulsory admissions at 12 months from 39.9% to 27.0%. [[Tinland2022]] Joint crisis plans were positive in one trial (13% vs 27%) but not in the larger CRIMSON trial (18% vs 20%). [[Henderson2004BMJ,Thornicroft2013CRIMSON]]

In a UK survey, 82% of people with bipolar disorder who explained their view endorsed "self-binding" directives, made while well, for times they are unwell; the legal position varies by country. [[Gergel2021]] NICE advises discussing a lasting power of attorney if mania has caused money problems (1.1.5). [[NICE2014CG185]]

NICE CG185 says to give carers written and verbal information on the diagnosis, "positive outcomes and recovery", support and getting help in a crisis (1.1.6), and to agree early how information will be shared, stressing information about risk (1.1.7). [[NICE2014CG185]]
