Suicide risk & crisis safety

Suicide risk in bipolar disorder is real, it rises at predictable times, and it can be lowered. This page shows when risk is higher, what protects, and how to write a safety plan.

Help now
If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line (in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland Samaritans 116 123). Help works.

Write a safety plan

A safety plan is a brief, written, prioritised list of coping strategies and sources of support that a person can use in a crisis. [[Stanley2012SPI,Stanley2018ED]] It is best written when you are well, ideally with your care team and the people close to you. NICE describes a plan made jointly with the person and their carer that covers triggers and early warning signs, the person's own coping strategies, how services will respond, and who to contact in a crisis. [[NICE2014CG185]]

Does it help? In a meta-analysis of 6 studies (3536 people), safety-planning-type interventions reduced suicidal behaviour (RR 0.57; number needed to treat 16). [[Nuij2021]] Plans work on paper or digitally, with or without a clinician. [[Ferguson2022]]

In a crisis
Contact local emergency services or a crisis line: in the US call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland call Samaritans on 116 123. These numbers are printed on your plan.

For families, friends and anyone worried

Families

Talk openly, and plan together

NICE asks services to give carers written and spoken information about getting help in a crisis, to agree early how information will be shared, stressing information about risk, and to make crisis plans jointly with the person and their carer. [[NICE2014CG185]] If you are worried, you can ask the person calmly and directly how they are and whether they are having thoughts of suicide, listen, and help them contact their care team or a crisis line.

Emergency

When it cannot wait

  • If someone may act on thoughts of suicide, or is unsafe right now: call local emergency services.
  • Crisis lines: 988 (US, call or text); Samaritans 116 123 (UK & Ireland).
  • On lithium with shaking, unsteadiness, slurred speech or confusion: suspected toxicity is an emergency too. [[FDA-Lithobid]]
During mania

Other risks: money, driving, decisions

The criteria for a manic episode include "excessive involvement in activities with a high potential for painful consequences". [[APA2022]] NICE advises people with mania not to make important decisions until they recover, and to discuss a lasting power of attorney if mania has caused financial problems. [[NICE2014CG185]] People with acute mania are not fit to drive; fitness can usually be confirmed after remission under psychiatric supervision. [[Laux2014]]

Established

Low and mixed moods: ask for extra help

Suicidal behaviour is associated with depressed or mixed states, not with mania. [[Miller2020Suicide]] Low moods and mixed moods are the times to ask for extra help, and shortening time in these phases is a prevention target. [[Valtonen2008,Holma2014]]

Established

Making surroundings safer

Restricting access to lethal means prevents suicide, and the evidence for this has strengthened. [[Zalsman2016,Mann2021Suicide]] Talk with the care team about what this means for your household; this page gives no specifics.

Open question

Can risk be predicted for one person?

Not well. Pooled across cohorts, "high-risk" categories had 56% sensitivity and 79% specificity, and most people labelled high-risk did not die by suicide (5.5% over about 5 years). [[Large2016]] "There is no effective algorithm to predict suicide in clinical practice", so reviews favour good care and safety planning for everyone. [[Turecki2016Lancet,Bolton2015BMJ]]

In plain words. Most people with bipolar disorder never die by suicide, and treatment, support and planning lower the risk further.
