Part 4 · Living, caring, preventing

Nursing care

Four parts of nursing care for a person living with dementia: assessment, giving the medicines, communication and care approaches, and documentation and safety. Each part has an animation or a tool to try, and each claim links to its source.

ScopeThis module describes what guidelines, drug labels and published studies say. It is not a care plan. It does not replace local protocols, your scope of practice, or the prescriber. Where the sources say no dementia-specific evidence exists, the module says so instead of filling the gap.

1 · Assessment

Delirium on top of dementia, hour by hour

Dementia changes over months and years. Delirium changes over hours and comes and goes. When the two overlap, the sudden change can hide inside the slow one. Press play to watch two weeks pass.

Step 1 · The baseline

Dementia drifts slowly

Over two weeks, the usual level of a person living with dementia barely moves. Knowing that baseline, from people who know the person and from earlier notes, is what lets a sudden change stand out. NICE NG97 lists delirium, depression, sensory impairment and medicines with anticholinergic burden among reversible causes of cognitive impairment to investigate. 385

In plain wordsDementia changes slowly. A change over hours or a day points to something else.

The 4AT, scored

Teaching tool, not a clinical record

A score of 0 does not definitively exclude delirium or cognitive impairment. 1

Accuracy: a 2021 meta-analysis (17 studies, 3,702 observations) found pooled sensitivity and specificity of 0.88 each. 488 In 785 acute medical patients aged 70 or over, the 4AT had sensitivity 76% and specificity 94%, while the CAM used in routine conditions had 40% and 100%. 461

Guidelines differ NICE CG103 (2023 update) names the 4AT when indicators of delirium are present, and the CAM-ICU or ICDSC in critical care. 386 NICE NG97 (2018) suggests the long CAM or the Observational Scale of Level of Arousal for inpatients with cognitive impairment of unknown cause, says instruments alone should not separate delirium from delirium on dementia, and says that when the two cannot be told apart, delirium is treated first. 385 A 2012 review found only 9 studies, with 50 participants who had delirium on dementia, validating any delirium tool in people with dementia. 369

The rest of the assessment

Cutoff disputed

Cognitive screening

The Mini-Cog (three-word recall plus clock drawing) had 99% sensitivity in its 2000 development sample and took about 3 minutes against 7 for the MMSE; education and language did not affect it. 59 At a cutoff of 26, the MoCA detected 90% of MCI and 100% of mild Alzheimer's, against 18% and 78% for the MMSE, with specificity 87% against 100%. 376 A 2018 meta-analysis found 23/30 gave better accuracy than 26. 69

NICE NG97 lists brief tools for non-specialist settings (10-point cognitive screener, 6CIT, 6-item screener, Memory Impairment Screen, Mini-Cog, Test Your Memory) and says dementia should not be ruled out on a normal score alone. 385 On screening people without symptoms, the USPSTF found insufficient evidence, while the Medicare Annual Wellness Visit requires a cognitive assessment. 90,397

NPI and CMAI

The Neuropsychiatric Inventory takes caregiver report on 10 domains, scoring frequency and severity, with screening questions to shorten it. 103 The Cohen-Mansfield Agitation Inventory was developed with 408 nursing home residents: nurses on each shift rated 29 agitated behaviours on a 7-point frequency scale, and agitation peaked on the day shift. 87

Exam hookNPI = caregiver interview, 10 domains, frequency × severity. CMAI = staff-rated, 29 behaviours.
One RCT

The unmet-needs model

Agitation is read as communicating a need such as pain, boredom or discomfort. In a trial of the TREA approach, which hypothesises the unmet need and matches an intervention to it (89 intervention residents, 36 placebo), total, physical non-aggressive and verbal agitation fell significantly, and pleasure and interest rose. 88 NG97 recommends a structured assessment of causes of distress, including pain, delirium and inappropriate care, before any drug or non-drug treatment, with psychosocial and environmental approaches first. 385

In plain wordsBehaviour that looks difficult is often the only way a person can say "I hurt", "I'm bored" or "this frightens me".

Function

The Katz Index of ADL (1963) and the Lawton-Brody IADL scale (1969) are the original functional measures. Secondary summaries describe Katz as 6 self-care items (bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring, continence, feeding) and Lawton as 8 instrumental domains (telephone, shopping, food preparation, housekeeping, laundry, transport, medicines, finances). Those counts were not checked against the originals. 273,304

Nutrition and swallowing

The MNA short form uses six questions and allows calf circumference in place of BMI; it was validated against the full MNA in 2,032 people. 266 In 395 people with Alzheimer's, 33.4% had progressive weight loss and 10.2% severe loss; hospitalisation, acute illness or a move raised the odds of severe loss (OR 6.8). 215 Swallowing difficulty affected 13% to 57%. 12

Falls

People with dementia had nearly 8 times the fall incidence of controls (9,118 against 1,023 per 1,000 person-years); Lewy body disorders, orthostatic hypotension, autonomic symptoms and depression predicted falls. 16 A multifactorial intervention after an emergency visit for a fall did not reduce falls in 274 cognitively impaired people (74% against 80% fell). 460 NICE NG249 (2025) advises against falls risk prediction tools, favouring multifactorial assessment. No falls risk tool validated specifically for dementia was found. 387

Exam hookNG249: multifactorial assessment, not a risk-prediction score.

Cornell Scale for Depression in Dementia

19 items, rated by a clinician from interviews with both the person and nursing staff. In the 1988 paper, interrater kappa was 0.67 and internal consistency 0.84. The commonly quoted cutoffs could not be verified from the source. 14

Other pain tools

The Abbey Pain Scale, from Australian residential care, takes about a minute, and mean scores fell by more than half after pain relief. Reproduced forms describe six items scored 0 to 3 (total 0 to 18); those details were not checked against the paper. 4 PACSLAC, from interviews with caregivers, has four subscales (alpha 0.92) and separated painful events from non-painful distress. 189

Antipsychotic reduction programmes

Antipsychotics used for distress in dementia carry an increased risk of death, and programmes in the UK and the US set out to cut their use. The bars show the published figures.

Established

The death risk

A meta-analysis of 15 trials found atypical antipsychotics in dementia increased deaths (3.5% against 2.3%; OR 1.54). 453 In the DART-AD withdrawal trial, survival on continued treatment against placebo was 46% against 71% at 24 months and 30% against 59% at 36 months. 33

What NICE NG97 allows

Antipsychotics only for people at risk of harming themselves or others, or in severe distress from agitation, hallucinations or delusions; then the lowest dose for the shortest time, reassessed at least every 6 weeks (recs 1.7.3 to 1.7.7). 385

Exam hookNG97: antipsychotic reassessment at least every 6 weeks.
Cluster RCT

Training, not just stopping

In 12 UK nursing homes, staff training in person-centred care cut neuroleptic use to 23.0% against 42.1% (a 19.1-point difference) with no increase in agitation. 186

Secondary sources

UK: the Banerjee report

The 2009 report is widely cited as estimating 180,000 people with dementia on antipsychotics and up to 1,800 excess deaths a year, and as setting a two-thirds reduction target. The 2012 national audit is reported to show a 52% fall from 2008 to 2011. An independent local study found 15.3% use against 10.5% in the audit, where only 48.9% of practices took part. The primary reports were not accessed. 35,456

US: the National Partnership

CMS launched the National Partnership to Improve Dementia Care in Nursing Homes in 2012. Long-stay antipsychotic use fell 40.1%, from 23.9% (2011) to 14.3% (Q2 2019). 96 From 2015, two antipsychotic quality measures that exclude schizophrenia, Huntington's disease and Tourette syndrome entered Five-Star ratings. 81

Contested

The critique

Use was already falling before the Partnership, and the decline slowed afterward, while mood-stabiliser use rose and accelerated, suggesting substitution. 342 The HHS OIG found 5% of long-stay enrollees with antipsychotic claims (12,091 people) were not recorded as receiving them in the MDS, and nearly one third of residents recorded with schizophrenia had no Medicare claims for it. 392

In plain wordsThe numbers fell, but some of the fall may be other sedating drugs, or labels, taking their place.

In plain wordsFurther right means a bigger benefit was found. An empty row means the trials were too few to say.
Established

Restraint minimisation

A guideline-based programme in 36 German nursing homes reduced physical restraint at 6 months (22.6% against 29.1%; OR 0.71) with no increase in falls, fractures or psychotropic use. 289 The 2023 Cochrane review of long-term care (11 studies, 19,003 participants) found least-restraint programmes probably reduce restraint (RR 0.86) and belt use (RR 0.54), with little or no effect on falls. 363 In general hospitals the evidence is very low certainty. 5

Harms of restraint

Among 27,353 forensic autopsies in Munich (1997 to 2010), 22 deaths were caused solely by physical restraint: strangulation 11, chest compression 8, head-down suspension 3. All occurred in patients who were not continuously observed, and 19 involved incorrectly applied restraints. 50 Antipsychotics used as chemical restraint carry the death risk shown in part 2. 33,453

Exam hookRestraint reduction did not increase falls in the trials.

Hospital outcomes

Of 617 people over 70 with emergency medical admissions, 42.4% had dementia, and only half had been diagnosed before admission. Among those with severe cognitive impairment, 24.0% died during the admission. 449 52 of 60 studies found longer stays for people with dementia. 364 A review of 104 papers found higher risks of death, care home admission, delirium, falls, dehydration and functional decline. 179 NG97 lists these harms to weigh before admitting someone with severe dementia. 385

John's Campaign and visiting

John's Campaign was founded by Nicci Gerrard and Julia Jones after Dr John Gerrard declined during five weeks in hospital in 2014, cut off from his family. Its site lists 677 hospital pledges. 262 In England, Regulation 9A, in force since 6 April 2024, requires care homes, hospitals and hospices to facilitate visits "unless there are exceptional circumstances", and lets people attending hospital without an overnight stay be accompanied. 495

No outcome trial

"This is me" and routines

"This is me" is an Alzheimer's Society leaflet recording background, important people and places, preferences and routines; the RCN has endorsed it since 2010. No outcome trial of it was identified. 22 No dementia-specific trial was found for "maintaining routine" on its own; NG97 recommends considering a structured tool to record likes, dislikes, routines and personal history (rec 1.1.3). 385

Environment

A review of 169 studies of building design in long-term care found specific design features helped behaviour, function, wellbeing and orientation, but not cognition. 338

In plain wordsA familiar, readable place can make the day easier, even if it does not improve memory.

4 · Documentation and safety

Build an ABC chart entry

An ABC chart records the Antecedent (what came just before), the Behaviour (described as seen and heard) and the Consequence (what happened next). Over many entries, a pattern can point to a need. Choose one of each to assemble a worked entry. The example is fictional.

Worked example · fictional
07:20 · bedroom
A · Antecedent
Morning wash offered by two staff she does not know.
B · Behaviour
Pushed the carer's arm away and shouted "no".
C · Consequence
Care paused. One familiar carer returned later and explained each step; wash accepted.
Possible need to test
Fear or discomfort during personal care. The unmet-needs model reads behaviour as communicating a need such as pain, boredom or discomfort. 88

A Cochrane review of 18 trials found that programmes built on functional analysis, of which ABC recording is the basis, reduced how often challenging behaviour happened and improved caregivers' reactions after the intervention, but not at follow-up. The analysis part could not be separated from the rest of each programme. No study was found that validates the ABC chart itself as a nursing documentation tool in dementia. 366

Capacity, best interests and surrogates

England and Wales: Mental Capacity Act 2005

Section 1 sets five principles: 344

  1. Capacity is presumed.
  2. All practicable steps to support the person's own decision come first.
  3. An unwise decision does not show incapacity.
  4. Acts must be in the person's best interests.
  5. The less restrictive option must be considered.

NICE NG97 recommends early and repeated offers of advance care planning: lasting power of attorney, advance statements, advance decisions to refuse treatment, and preferred place of care and death. 385

Exam hookMCA s.1: presume capacity, support the decision, unwise is not incapable, best interests, least restrictive.

In institutions and at home

In institutional settings, 64.2% of staff reported having committed some form of abuse in the past year; residents reported psychological abuse at 33.4% and physical at 14.1%. 536 Among 220 family carers of people with dementia, 52% reported some abusive behaviour, mostly verbal, 34% at important levels, and physical abuse was rare (1.4%). 89

In plain wordsMost harm reported by family carers was shouting or harsh words, often under strain. Support for carers is part of safeguarding.
Sources disagree

Mouth care and pneumonia

A Japanese trial in 417 nursing home residents reported fewer pneumonia episodes, febrile days and pneumonia deaths with intensive oral care. 537 The 2022 Cochrane review (6 RCTs, 6,244 participants, all at high risk of bias) found insufficient evidence that professional oral care reduces pneumonia, with low-certainty evidence of lower pneumonia mortality at 24 months (RR 0.43). 68 Resistance to mouth care is a known barrier; a 7-resident pilot of a threat-reduction approach (MOUTh) improved oral health scores from 7.29 to 1.00. 253

The course of advanced dementia

In nursing home residents with advanced dementia followed for 18 months, 54.8% died. Eating problems affected 85.8%, febrile episodes 52.6%, dyspnoea 46.0%, pneumonia 41.1% and pain 39.1%. When proxies understood the prognosis, burdensome interventions were much less likely (adjusted OR 0.12). 359

Nursing home quality measures (US)

Five-Star quality measures are calculated from MDS 3.0 data and include pressure ulcers, restraint use, falls with major injury and, since February 2015, short- and long-stay antipsychotic use. 81 The MDS is reported by the facilities themselves, which the HHS OIG judged insufficiently validated. 392
