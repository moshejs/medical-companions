Glossary

116 of 116 terms

4ATA short delirium test with four items: Alertness, AMT4 (four orientation questions), Attention (months backwards) and Acute change or fluctuating course. Scored 0-12; 4 or more suggests delirium.
ABC chartA behaviour record of the Antecedent (what came before), the Behaviour itself and the Consequence (what followed). It is the basis of functional analysis.
ABC scoreThe 2012 NIA-AA neuropathology score that combines A (Thal amyloid phase), B (Braak tangle stage) and C (CERAD neuritic plaque density) into Not, Low, Intermediate or High Alzheimer's change.
Aβ42/40 ratioThe ratio of Aβ42 to Aβ40 in spinal fluid or blood. An expert group recommended it over Aβ42 alone for CSF classification.
Acetylcholine (ACh)A neurotransmitter made by choline acetyltransferase and broken down in the synapse by acetylcholinesterase. Cholinergic input to the cortex is progressively lost in Alzheimer's disease.
Acetylcholinesterase (AChE)The enzyme in the synaptic cleft that quickly breaks acetylcholine into acetate and choline. Cholinesterase inhibitors block it.
ADADAutosomal dominant Alzheimer's disease, caused by mutations in APP, PSEN1 or PSEN2 or by APP duplication. Each child of a carrier has a 50% chance of inheriting the mutation.
ADAS-CogAlzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale, cognitive subscale. A cognition measure used in drug trials; the version in the donepezil Cochrane review runs from 0 to 70.
AducanumabAn antibody against aggregated Aβ. It received FDA accelerated approval in June 2021 based on amyloid reduction, and Biogen ended its development and sales in January 2024.
Advance care planningDiscussing and recording future wishes while the person can still take part, such as lasting power of attorney, advance statements, advance decisions to refuse treatment and preferred place of care and death.
AHEAD 3-45A prevention programme testing lecanemab for 4 years in cognitively unimpaired people aged 55-80 with raised brain amyloid. Its primary completion is estimated for December 2028.
AICDAPP intracellular domain: the fragment released inside the cell when gamma-secretase cuts C83 or C99.
Amyloid cascade hypothesisThe model, proposed in 1991-1992, that build-up of Aβ is the primary driver of Alzheimer's disease and leads to tangles, synapse loss and dementia. It is supported by genetics and debated on several points.
Amyloid-beta (Aβ)A peptide of 38-43 amino acids cut from APP. Aβ42 is the main plaque component and Aβ40 the most common form made.
Anticholinergic burdenThe combined effect of medicines that block acetylcholine, such as some antihistamines, bladder drugs and tricyclic antidepressants. High cumulative use is associated with dementia in observational studies.
Antisense oligonucleotideA short synthetic strand of nucleic acid that lowers production of a target protein. BIIB080 (diranersen) is a tau antisense drug given into the spinal fluid.
APOEApolipoprotein E, the strongest common genetic risk factor for late-onset Alzheimer's disease. Its three common alleles are ε2 (protective), ε3 (reference) and ε4 (raises risk).
APOE3 ChristchurchA rare APOE variant (R136S). A woman with a PSEN1 mutation and two copies of it stayed free of MCI until her seventies, about three decades later than expected.
APPAmyloid precursor protein, a membrane protein encoded on chromosome 21. Aβ is cut from it by beta- and gamma-secretase.
ARIAAmyloid-related imaging abnormalities: MRI changes seen with anti-amyloid antibodies, attributed to treatment-related loss of vessel-wall integrity. Usually without symptoms, but they can be serious or fatal.
ARIA-EARIA with oedema or effusion: vasogenic oedema or sulcal effusion seen on FLAIR MRI. It usually resolves.
ARIA-HARIA with haemosiderin: microhaemorrhages or superficial siderosis seen on T2* or susceptibility-weighted MRI. It persists.
ATNThe 2018 research framework grouping biomarkers into A (amyloid), T (tau) and (N) neurodegeneration. N is in parentheses because it is not specific to Alzheimer's.
Auguste D.Alois Alzheimer's patient, admitted in Frankfurt in November 1901. He described her brain's plaques and tangles in Tübingen on 3 November 1906.
BACE1Beta-site APP-cleaving enzyme 1 (beta-secretase). It cuts APP at the start of the Aβ sequence and is thought to be the rate-limiting step in Aβ production.
Beers CriteriaThe American Geriatrics Society's list of potentially inappropriate medicines for older adults. The 2023 version advises avoiding benzodiazepines, Z-drugs and strong anticholinergics in dementia, and avoiding antipsychotics except in narrow circumstances.
Best interestsUnder the Mental Capacity Act 2005, any act or decision for a person who lacks capacity must be in that person's best interests, and the less restrictive option must be considered.
Biomarker cascadeThe sequence in which Alzheimer's biomarkers turn abnormal: amyloid first, then tau and neurodegeneration markers, then symptoms.
Boxed warningThe most prominent safety warning on a US drug label. Antipsychotics carry one for increased death in older people with dementia, and lecanemab and donanemab carry one for ARIA.
BPSDBehavioural and psychological symptoms of dementia, also called neuropsychiatric symptoms: for example apathy, depression, agitation, anxiety, sleep problems and psychosis.
Braak stagesSix stages of tangle spread: I-II transentorhinal, III-IV limbic, V-VI isocortical.
BrexpiprazoleAn antipsychotic approved by the FDA on 11 May 2023 for agitation associated with dementia due to Alzheimer's disease. It keeps the class mortality warning.
Butyrylcholinesterase (BuChE)A second enzyme that breaks down acetylcholine. Its activity rises as dementia progresses. Rivastigmine inhibits it as well as AChE.
C1qA complement protein. In mouse models it builds up at synapses before plaques and tags them for removal by microglia.
CAACerebral amyloid angiopathy: Aβ, rich in Aβ40, deposited in the walls of brain blood vessels. About 85-95% of people with Alzheimer's have some.
CAMConfusion Assessment Method: delirium requires acute onset with fluctuating course and inattention, plus either disorganised thinking or an altered level of consciousness.
CDRClinical Dementia Rating: rates six domains from an interview with the person and an informant. Global scores are 0, 0.5, 1, 2 and 3.
CDR-SBCDR Sum of Boxes: the six CDR domain scores added up, from 0 to 18. It was the primary endpoint of CLARITY AD.
CentiloidA standard scale for amyloid PET across tracers, from 0 (mean of young controls) to 100 (mean of typical mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease).
Cholinergic hypothesisThe idea, set out in 1982, that loss of cholinergic input from the basal forebrain to the cortex contributes to memory loss.
Cholinesterase inhibitorA drug that blocks the breakdown of acetylcholine: donepezil, rivastigmine and galantamine. The labels state no evidence that they change the course of the disease.
CJDCreutzfeldt-Jakob disease, a prion disease. The sporadic form causes rapidly progressive dementia, with a median of 4-5 months from onset to death.
CMAICohen-Mansfield Agitation Inventory: 29 agitated behaviours rated on a 7-point frequency scale. Used as an outcome in agitation trials.
Cognitive stimulation therapy (CST)Group sessions of themed activities for people with mild to moderate dementia. NICE NG97 recommends offering it.
Core 1 biomarkerIn the Alzheimer's Association 2024 criteria, a marker that turns abnormal around the same time as amyloid PET: amyloid PET, approved CSF assays or accurate plasma assays such as p-tau217.
Covert administrationGiving medicine hidden in food or drink without the person knowing. NICE SC1 says it should not be done to a resident who has capacity.
CSFCerebrospinal fluid, sampled by lumbar puncture. CSF Aβ42 (or the Aβ42/40 ratio) falls and phosphorylated tau rises in Alzheimer's disease.
Delirium superimposed on dementia (DSD)Delirium occurring in a person who already has dementia. It is common, often missed, and linked to faster decline.
DIANDominantly Inherited Alzheimer Network: a study of families with APP, PSEN1 or PSEN2 mutations that timed biomarker changes against expected age of onset.
DICEDescribe, Investigate, Create, Evaluate: a caregiver-centred approach to behavioural symptoms that looks for causes before drugs.
Disease-associated microglia (DAM)A microglial state containing Aβ, identified by single-cell mapping. It switches on in two steps, the second dependent on TREM2.
Disease-targeted therapy (DTT)A drug aimed at the underlying disease process rather than symptoms. The pipeline authors prefer this term to disease-modifying, which they call aspirational.
DLBDementia with Lewy bodies: core features are fluctuating cognition, recurrent visual hallucinations, REM sleep behaviour disorder and parkinsonism.
DonanemabAn antibody against N-terminal pyroglutamate Aβ in plaques (brand name Kisunla), approved by the FDA on 2 July 2024. Dosing can stop once amyloid is cleared.
DonepezilA selective acetylcholinesterase inhibitor taken once daily, with a half-life of about 70 hours. It is labelled for mild, moderate and severe Alzheimer's dementia.
Down syndromeTrisomy 21. Because APP is on chromosome 21, virtually all adults with Down syndrome show Alzheimer's pathology by age 40; this is now described as a genetic form of Alzheimer's.
ElderspeakInfantilising talk to older people. In videotaped nursing-home care it was followed by resistance to care more often than normal talk.
Entorhinal cortexThe cortex that relays information into and out of the hippocampus. Its layer II neurons are lost early in Alzheimer's disease.
Episodic memoryMemory for personal events and recent conversations. It is usually the first ability affected in typical Alzheimer's disease.
FASTFunctional Assessment Staging: rates functional decline over the whole course of Alzheimer's, from stage 1 to 7, with substages 6a-6e and 7a-7f.
FDG-PETPET imaging of glucose use in the brain. Reduced posterior cingulate uptake with relatively preserved occipital uptake is typical of Alzheimer's.
FINGERA Finnish trial of a 2-year multidomain lifestyle programme (diet, exercise, cognitive training, vascular monitoring) in at-risk older adults. It found a small cognitive benefit.
GalantamineA competitive, reversible acetylcholinesterase inhibitor with a half-life of about 7 hours, labelled for mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's dementia.
Gamma-secretaseA four-part enzyme complex with presenilin at its core. It cuts APP fragments inside the membrane to release Aβ.
GFAPGlial fibrillary acidic protein, a blood marker of astrocyte activation. It is raised in amyloid-positive people but is not used for diagnosis.
Glymphatic systemA proposed route by which spinal fluid flows through the brain along blood vessels and clears waste such as Aβ, especially during sleep. The evidence is mainly from rodents and is mixed.
GUIDE modelGuiding an Improved Dementia Experience: an 8-year US Medicare model launched on 1 July 2024 offering care navigation, caregiver training, a 24/7 support line and respite.
HippocampusA medial temporal lobe structure needed to form new episodic memories. Its atrophy on MRI is a marker of Alzheimer's disease.
HyperphosphorylationExcess phosphate groups added to tau. Hyperphosphorylated tau detaches from microtubules and assembles into tangles.
iADRSIntegrated Alzheimer's Disease Rating Scale (0-144, lower is worse), combining cognition and daily function. It was the primary endpoint of TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2.
IWGInternational Working Group. Its 2024 recommendation treats Alzheimer's as a clinical-biological construct and calls most biomarker-positive people without symptoms at risk, not as having the disease.
LATELimbic-predominant age-related TDP-43 encephalopathy: a TDP-43 disease of older adults that causes an amnestic dementia resembling Alzheimer's.
LecanemabAn antibody that binds soluble Aβ protofibrils (brand name Leqembi). FDA traditional approval came on 6 July 2023 after the CLARITY AD trial.
Logopenic variant PPAA primary progressive aphasia with word-finding pauses and impaired sentence repetition. Its most common pathology is Alzheimer's disease.
MAPTThe gene for tau, on chromosome 17.
MCIMild cognitive impairment: measurable cognitive decline with daily activities largely preserved. Many people with MCI do not progress to dementia.
MemantineAn uncompetitive NMDA receptor antagonist labelled for moderate-to-severe Alzheimer's dementia.
Mental Capacity Act 2005The law in England and Wales on decisions for people who may lack capacity. It starts from a presumption of capacity and sets five principles.
MicrogliaThe brain's resident immune cells. They cluster around plaques and can prune synapses.
Mini-CogA brief screen combining three-word recall with a clock drawing, scored 0-5. It takes about 3 minutes.
Mixed pathologyMore than one cause of dementia in the same brain, such as Alzheimer's plus infarcts or Lewy bodies. A 2024 history review calls it the most frequent cause of dementia in the oldest old.
MMSEMini-Mental State Examination: an 11-item, 30-point cognitive screen. It is affected by age and education and should not be used alone to diagnose.
MoCAMontreal Cognitive Assessment: a 10-minute, 30-point screen designed for MCI. The cut-off is disputed: 26 in the original validation, 23 in a 2018 meta-analysis.
Neuritic plaqueA dense-core amyloid plaque surrounded by swollen, tau-positive neurites, activated microglia and reactive astrocytes.
Neurofibrillary tangle (NFT)An aggregate of hyperphosphorylated tau filaments inside a neuron, flame-shaped in pyramidal cells.
NfLNeurofilament light, a blood and spinal fluid marker of nerve damage from any cause. It is not specific to Alzheimer's.
NMDA receptorA glutamate receptor channel. Memantine blocks it when it is excessively open.
NPINeuropsychiatric Inventory: a caregiver interview scoring the frequency and severity of behavioural and psychological symptoms.
Nucleus basalis of MeynertA basal forebrain nucleus that is the main source of acetylcholine for the cortex. More than 75% of its neurons are lost in Alzheimer's.
OligomerA small soluble cluster of Aβ molecules. Reviews propose that oligomers, more than plaques, drive synapse damage.
p-tau217Tau phosphorylated at position 217. In plasma it is one of the most accurate blood markers of Alzheimer's pathology.
PAINADPain Assessment in Advanced Dementia: an observation scale of breathing, negative vocalisation, facial expression, body language and consolability, scored 0-10.
Paired helical filament (PHF)The main tau filament in tangles: two filaments twisted around each other.
Person-centred careCare organised around the person's identity, history and preferences rather than tasks. Associated with Kitwood.
PETPositron emission tomography: imaging with radioactive tracers that bind amyloid or tau or show glucose use.
Pittsburgh compound B (PiB)The first amyloid PET tracer studied in people, reported in 2004 in 16 people with mild Alzheimer's disease and 9 controls.
Population attributable fraction (PAF)The share of cases that would theoretically be avoided if a risk factor were removed, assuming it is causal.
Posterior cortical atrophyAn atypical presentation, usually caused by Alzheimer's pathology, with problems in visual and spatial perception rather than memory.
Prion-like spreadThe idea that misfolded tau seeds pass from neuron to connected neuron and template misfolding there.
ProtofibrilA curved, soluble intermediate between Aβ oligomers and mature fibrils. Lecanemab binds it with high affinity.
PSEN1Presenilin 1, on chromosome 14, the catalytic part of gamma-secretase. It has the most known pathogenic variants, and its mutations give the earliest onsets.
PSEN2Presenilin 2, on chromosome 1. Its mutations cause autosomal dominant Alzheimer's with a wide range of onset ages.
Pseudo-atrophyExtra brain volume loss on MRI after amyloid-lowering antibodies. Some authors attribute it to plaque removal; others read it as possible harm.
Regression discontinuityA natural-experiment design that compares people just either side of an eligibility cutoff, such as a birth date for shingles vaccination.
Respite careShort breaks for family caregivers. A Cochrane review found no clear benefit or harm, mainly because of a lack of good studies.
RivastigmineA pseudo-irreversible inhibitor of both AChE and BuChE, given as capsules or a daily skin patch.
Superficial siderosisBlood-derived iron (haemosiderin) deposited on the surface of the brain, seen on T2* or susceptibility-weighted MRI. With antibodies it counts as ARIA-H.
Synapse lossLoss of connections between neurons. Neocortical synapse density correlated most strongly with cognition in a classic 1991 study.
TauA neuronal protein that stabilises microtubules. In Alzheimer's it becomes hyperphosphorylated and forms tangles.
Tau PETPET imaging of tau tangles, used for biological staging in the 2024 criteria.
TDP-43A protein that forms inclusions in LATE and in some frontotemporal dementias.
Thal phasesFive phases of amyloid spread: neocortex, allocortex, then diencephalon, striatum and basal forebrain, then brainstem, then cerebellum.
TREM2A receptor on microglia. Rare variants such as R47H raise Alzheimer's risk.
TrontinemabAn anti-amyloid antibody fused to a transferrin-receptor brain shuttle that carries it across the blood-brain barrier. Phase 3 trials started in late 2025.
Vascular dementiaDementia caused by cerebrovascular disease, about 15% of cases. Its course can be stepwise or variable, and diagnosis needs imaging evidence of cerebrovascular disease.
Young-onset dementiaDementia beginning before age 65. Early-onset Alzheimer's is about 5% of all Alzheimer's disease, and most cases are not familial.
