Part 1 · The disease

Genes and inheritance

Most Alzheimer's is not inherited in a simple way. One common gene, APOE, shifts the odds. A few rare genes all but guarantee it, and an extra chromosome 21 does too. Pick a genotype, then play the family below.

APOE: one gene, three common versions

Everyone carries two copies of APOE, one from each parent. The three common versions, ε2, ε3 and ε4, differ at only two positions in the protein (112 and 158). 535 Choose a pair to see how often it turns up in people with Alzheimer's compared with ε3/ε3.

Genotype

Odds ratios compare the odds of Alzheimer's with ε3/ε3, on a log scale. They are group averages, not anyone's personal chance.

APOE ε3/ε4

Higher odds than ε3/ε3

The commonest risk genotype. Odds ratio 3.2 (2.8–3.8) in 1997 and 6.13 in autopsy-confirmed cases. A review puts one ε4 at about 3–4-fold risk and 2–5 years earlier onset. The effect is strongest in East Asian and weakest in Hispanic participants, and in 1997 it weakened after age 70. 48,149,432,535

In plain wordsThese numbers compare groups. A longer bar to the right means the pair is found more often in people with Alzheimer's. It does not say what will happen to any one person.
Exam hookWhy the two bars differ: clinically diagnosed groups include some people misdiagnosed with Alzheimer's, which dilutes the effect. Autopsy confirmation makes the odds ratios larger. 432

Why they disagree. Case-control and memory-clinic samples carry referral bias; the population cohorts measured MCI or dementia rather than Alzheimer's alone; and most cohorts are of European ancestry, where the ε4 effect is larger than in African ancestry. 21,48,423

What both agree on. ε4 raises risk but does not decide it: many ε4/ε4 people never develop dementia, and Alzheimer's often occurs without ε4. Only 38% of APOE alleles in people with Alzheimer's are ε4. 197,423,535

Dominant inheritance: APP, PSEN1 and PSEN2

A mutation in APP, PSEN1 or PSEN2, or an extra copy of APP, is the only known cause that all but guarantees Alzheimer's. 41 Pick the gene, play the family, and press "Another family" to see that 50% is a chance for each child, not a quota.

Step 1 · The family

One parent carries the mutation

In this family one parent carries a mutation in a dominant Alzheimer's gene. Apart from the earlier onset, dominantly inherited Alzheimer's is clinically and pathologically very similar to the common late-onset form. 41

In plain wordsOne parent has a rare gene change that causes Alzheimer's at a younger age.

Down syndrome: a third copy of APP

APP, the gene for the protein amyloid beta is cut from, sits on chromosome 21. People with Down syndrome usually have three copies of chromosome 21, so three copies of APP. Drag the age, then switch to the rare case with only two APP copies.

Down syndrome · age 50

AD pathology in virtually all

Virtually all adults with Down syndrome show Alzheimer's pathology by age 40, partly because APP lies on chromosome 21 and is overexpressed. 327 In 388 adults, spinal-fluid and blood markers changed in the 20s and amyloid PET in the 30s; prodromal Alzheimer's was diagnosed at a median 50.2 years and dementia at 53.7, and symptomatic Alzheimer's reached 90–100% in the 60s. 184 In an Irish cohort followed for 20 years, 75 of 77 people (97.4%) developed dementia, at a mean age of 55. 346 It is now described as a genetic form of Alzheimer's and the leading cause of death in adults with Down syndrome. 185

In plain wordsPeople with Down syndrome have an extra copy of the gene that makes amyloid. Almost all develop the brain changes of Alzheimer's by 40, and most develop dementia in their 50s.
Exam hookAPP is on chromosome 21: trisomy 21 = three APP copies = early, near-universal amyloid. Partial trisomy without the extra APP: no Alzheimer's (case report). 327,420

Resilience, risk scores and testing

Case reports + cohort

APOE3 Christchurch

A woman with the Colombian PSEN1 E280A mutation did not develop MCI until her seventies, about three decades late. She had two copies of APOE3 Christchurch, very high amyloid, and little tau. Among 1,077 carriers, the 27 with one Christchurch copy began to decline at a median 52 years against 47, about 5 years later. 26,424

Single case

RELN-COLBOS, 2023

A man with the same PSEN1 mutation stayed cognitively intact until 67 without Christchurch. He carried a gain-of-function variant in Reelin, which binds the same receptors as APOE; in mice it reduced tau phosphorylation. Both resilient people had very high amyloid but little entorhinal tau, suggesting protection acted at the tau stage. 26,326

Established

A673T: protection in APP itself

An Icelandic APP variant next to the beta-secretase site cut amyloid production by about 40% in the lab, and protected against Alzheimer's and against decline in older people without it. 265 See Amyloid, animated.

Established

The Colombian kindred

A 1997 survey of five extended families in Antioquia (nearly 3,000 people) found 128 affected, with onset at a mean 46.8 years. PSEN1 E280A traces to one founder about 15 generations ago. In 449 carriers, median ages were 44 for MCI, 49 for dementia and 59 for death. 8,299,325

Established

GWAS: from 75 to 118 places in the genome

Bellenguez 2022 in Nature Genetics (111,326 cases, 677,663 controls) found 75 risk loci, 42 new, pointing to amyloid and tau processing, microglia and TNF-α signalling. The largest study so far, a multi-ancestry analysis by Uffelmann 2026 in Nature (109,479 cases and 74,141 proxy cases, 2,131,799 controls and 499,708 proxy controls), found 118 loci, plus 9 more in single-ancestry analyses, 48 of them new; genes more active in microglia and less active in neurons in people with the disease carried more of the genetic risk. Rare TREM2 R47H raised risk about 2.9-fold. 47,266,495

Limited use

Polygenic scores

A score built with APOE, age and sex reached an AUC of 78.2%. Beyond age and APOE ε4, the Bellenguez score separated the top and bottom tenths by only 1.6- to 1.9-fold. Scores discriminate poorly in the general population, work less well outside European ancestry, and have no established clinical use. 47,146,312

Established

Twins: heritability 58–79%

In 11,884 Swedish twin pairs, heritability of Alzheimer's was 58% in the full model and 79% in the best-fitting one; the rest came from non-shared environment. Identical twins also differed less in age at onset. The authors stress non-genetic factors still matter. 193

Debated

Is ε4/ε4 its own genetic form?

In 2024, Fortea and colleagues reported Alzheimer's biology in almost all ε4/ε4 people, with symptoms at a mean of 65, and proposed it as a distinct genetic form. Critics answer that near-full penetrance of biology is not near-full penetrance of dementia, citing lifetime risks of about 30–55%. 21,186

Testing: what the guidelines say

Published guidance

Predictive APOE testing

A 1995 ACMG/ASHG statement said APOE should not be used for predictive testing. The 2011 ACMG/NSGC guideline calls APOE susceptibility testing "not clinically recommended" and advises against direct-to-consumer testing; if a person still insists after counselling, it says testing should follow the Huntington disease protocol. 7,204,205

Published guidance

Families with a dominant gene

The guideline says testing for APP, PSEN1 and PSEN2 should be offered to people with early-onset Alzheimer's and a family history, to families with a dominant pattern, and to relatives of a known carrier, using a Huntington-style protocol. Children should not be tested. Only 8.4% of at-risk people in one series asked for predictive testing. 41,204

Treatment context

Genotyping before anti-amyloid drugs

US labels for lecanemab and donanemab say ApoE ε4 testing "should be performed prior to initiation of treatment", because ARIA (brain swelling or bleeding on MRI) is more common in ε4/ε4. This is a different purpose from predicting Alzheimer's. 105,156,157,426 See Anti-amyloid antibodies.

Trial evidence

What disclosure did: REVEAL

162 adult children of people with Alzheimer's were randomised to learn their APOE result or not. At 1 year anxiety, depression and distress did not differ. ε4-positive people more often changed health behaviours (adjusted OR 2.73) and were 5.76 times more likely to change long-term care insurance. 73,211,543
