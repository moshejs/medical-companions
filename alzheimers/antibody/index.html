Part 3 · Treatment

Anti-amyloid antibodies over time

Lecanemab and donanemab are antibodies infused into the blood that help the brain's immune cells clear amyloid plaque. Follow the published trials month by month: plaque falls fast, ARIA (brain swelling or small bleeds on MRI) shows up early, and the clinical benefit appears slowly and stays modest.

Published trial and label information only. Everything here reports what the trials, labels and regulators published. Whether a particular person is eligible, and whether treatment is right for them, are decisions for specialist clinicians together with the person and their family.

Baseline

Baseline

CLARITY AD randomized 1,795 people aged 50 to 90 with early Alzheimer's disease (MCI or mild dementia) and amyloid confirmed by PET or spinal fluid: 898 to lecanemab 10 mg/kg IV every 2 weeks and 897 to placebo. Amyloid PET averaged 77.9 centiloids (CL) on lecanemab and 75.0 on placebo. About 69% carried APOE4. 305,404,497

In plain wordsEveryone in the trial had early memory symptoms and confirmed amyloid. Half received the drug and half a dummy infusion, and nobody knew which.

How big is the benefit?

The same trial numbers are read in two very different ways. Pick a scale, then pick any row to see what it is and how each side reads it. This is a live debate among specialists, so both readings are shown side by side.

Scale

Bars: point estimate. Whiskers: published 95% confidence interval or range. Vertical lines: proposed thresholds.

CLARITY AD

Lecanemab: 0.45 points

The 18-month difference was 0.45 (95% CI 0.23 to 0.67) on a 0 to 18 scale, 27% slowing, with all key secondary endpoints also favouring lecanemab. Supporters stress the consistency across scales; critics stress that the absolute gap is under half a point. 305,497

In plain wordsThe bars show how much less the treated group declined than the placebo group, on an 18-point scale. The dashed lines are the smallest changes people are thought to notice in one person.
Exam hookWithin-person MCID (about 1 to 2 CDR-SB points) versus between-group difference (0.45 to 0.70): the debate is partly about whether these can be compared.

One readingA real, growing benefit

  • Both trials met their primary endpoints, with consistent results across secondary scales: all of lecanemab's key secondaries, and 23 of 24 gated donanemab outcomes. 465,497
  • Within-person thresholds (MCIDs) were not designed to judge a between-group average; a 0.98-point group gap would need about 60% slowing. 298,406
  • Translated into time, estimates run from about 4 to 13 months, and modelled independence gains of 10 to 13 months. 25,224
  • Lower post-treatment amyloid tracked slower decline (CDR-SB R² 0.87), supporting plaque removal as the mechanism. 329
  • Sponsor extensions report gaps that keep widening against external cohorts. 498,542

Another readingA small benefit at real cost

  • Differences of 0.45 to 0.70 sit below anchor-based MCIDs of about 1 to 2 points; a meta-analysis found no antibody exceeded the MCID. 23,123
  • Benefit should be judged on whether it is noticeable, valuable and worthwhile given cost and risk; responder-style analyses can mislead. 316,317
  • Extensions lost randomization and blinding; delayed-start groups appear to catch up, which argues against lasting disease modification. 225,226
  • NICE judged a 4 to 6 month delay too costly; Leqembi's US list price was $26,500 a year. 276,409
  • ARIA, frequent MRI and infusions are a burden, and extra brain volume loss is unexplained. 18,305,465

Eligibility, ARIA, dosing and the history

Established

Few people are eligible

  • Population study, amyloid-positive MCI or mild dementia: 8% met CLARITY AD criteria 408
  • Swedish memory clinic, AUR criteria: 6%, or 3% excluding two APOE4 copies 437
  • Paris memory clinic: 28.6% of biomarker-confirmed patients, 2.9% of all patients 505
  • Korea, amyloid-positive patients: 24.6% lecanemab, 28.0% donanemab 258

The denominators differ (everyone in a population, everyone in a clinic, or only those with confirmed amyloid), so the numbers are not directly comparable.

Label information

ARIA: what it is and who is at risk

ARIA-E is vasogenic oedema or sulcal effusion on FLAIR MRI; ARIA-H is microhaemorrhage or superficial siderosis. Reported symptoms include headache, confusion, visual change, dizziness and gait trouble; seizures occur and ARIA can be fatal. Risk rises with two APOE4 copies (about 15% of people with Alzheimer's) and with signs of cerebral amyloid angiopathy. 86,279,305

ARIA-E can mimic stroke; fatal bleeds have followed clot-busting drugs, and both labels advise considering ARIA first. The AURs exclude people on anticoagulants and the EU makes it a contraindication. 103,139,279,305,423

Exam hookARIA-E = oedema (FLAIR). ARIA-H = haemorrhage (T2*/SWI). Highest risk: APOE4 homozygotes, early in treatment.
Label information

How the drugs are given

  • Lecanemab: 10 mg/kg IV every 2 weeks; after 18 months, IV every 4 weeks or a 360 mg weekly autoinjector (Leqembi Iqlik). Since July 2026 treatment can also start under the skin at 500 mg weekly, as two 250 mg injections. The injectable approvals rest on matched drug exposure and modelling, not a separate efficacy trial. 132,134,305
  • Donanemab: IV every 4 weeks. The label's modified titration (350, 700, 1,050, then 1,400 mg) cut ARIA-E at 24 weeks from 23.7% to 13.7% with similar amyloid lowering. 279,509
  • MRI schedules differ: the 2026 Leqembi label checks at 1, 2, 3 and 6 months; the 2023 AUR and EU label before the 5th, 7th and 14th infusions. 103,139,305

Published label dosing, quoted for education. It is not a guide for any individual.

Contested

Aducanumab: the approval that split the field

EMERGE and ENGAGE were stopped for futility in 2019. In a larger dataset EMERGE's high dose met its endpoint (CDR-SB -0.39) and ENGAGE did not (+0.03). At high dose 35.2% had ARIA-E. An FDA advisory committee voted 0 yes, 10 no, 1 uncertain, yet the FDA granted accelerated approval on 7 June 2021, and three committee members resigned. Biogen stopped selling it in January 2024, saying safety and efficacy were not the reason. 54,66,416,445

Established

Gantenerumab and solanezumab failed

Gantenerumab lowered amyloid but only about 27 to 28% became amyloid-negative, and CDR-SB differences (-0.31, -0.19) were not significant. Solanezumab, which binds monomers, did not slow decline in amyloid-positive people without symptoms, and their amyloid kept rising. The lesson drawn: only deep, fast plaque clearance has gone with clinical benefit. 42,329,471

In plain wordsGrabbing loose amyloid did not help. Clearing plaques only partly did not help either. The two drugs that cleared plaque deeply and quickly slowed decline a little.
Contested

The brain-volume paradox

On MRI, amyloid-lowering antibodies are followed by extra brain volume loss, not less. One group calls this amyloid-removal-related pseudo-atrophy: limited to amyloid-lowering drugs, compatible with plaque removal and not so far linked to harm. A meta-analysis found ventricles enlarged 2.1 mL more than placebo, tracking ARIA frequency (r = 0.86), and read it as possible harm. Both sides call for more patient-level and post-mortem data. 18,44

Published figures

Cost and coverage

Leqembi's US list price was $26,500 a year; with 20% coinsurance, people on traditional Medicare would pay over $5,000 a year. Kisunla was about $32,000 for 12 months (trade press). Since 6 July 2023 Medicare covers traditionally approved antibodies when the clinician joins a registry. 83,276,352

Real-world US clinic (234 people): ARIA in 22% over about 6.5 months, symptomatic in 5.7%, and far more often in mild dementia (27%) than in MCI or very mild dementia (1.8%). 396
