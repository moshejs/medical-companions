Part 4 · Living, caring, preventing

Toward a cure

Researchers are trying to interrupt the disease at every step, from the first protein cut to the loss of synapses. Pick a route to drop a barrier at its target and see what the best evidence found. Every route carries an evidence label, and failures are shown as plainly as successes.

Established · best evidence

Slowed decline by a quarter to a third in early disease; did not stop it

In CLARITY AD, CDR-SB worsened 1.21 points with lecanemab against 1.66 with placebo at 18 months, about 27% less decline; ARIA with oedema occurred in 12.6%. 499 In TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2, donanemab slowed CDR-SB worsening by 0.70 points in the combined group at 76 weeks; ARIA-E occurred in 24.0%, and 3 deaths were considered treatment-related. 467 Antibodies that do not clear plaque well, solanezumab and crenezumab, have not worked at any stage tested. 473,481

In plain wordsRemoving plaque slows the decline a little in early disease. It does not stop it, and brain swelling or bleeding (ARIA) is a real risk.
Exam hookOnly plaque-clearing antibodies have slowed symptomatic decline. Antibodies that do not clear plaque (solanezumab, crenezumab) failed.

Prevention trials: treating before symptoms

These trials give a drug to people who have amyloid or a disease-causing gene but no symptoms yet. The timeline runs from the first readout to the last expected one; solid dots are published results, hollow dots are estimated completion dates from the trial registry.

Dates are publication years for results and registry estimates for trials still running.

Established · phase 3, published 2023

A4: no slowing

A4 randomized 1,169 cognitively unimpaired people aged 65 to 85 with elevated amyloid. At 240 weeks, PACC change was −1.43 with solanezumab and −1.13 with placebo (difference −0.30; P=0.26). Amyloid PET rose by 11.6 centiloids on the drug and 19.3 on placebo, so plaque was not removed. ARIA-E occurred in under 1% of each group. 473

In plain wordsAn antibody that does not clear plaque did not help, even when given years before any symptoms.
Exam hookA4 (solanezumab in preclinical AD): negative.

Open questions

Early evidence

Is removing amyloid enough?

Antibodies that clear plaque slow decline by about a quarter to a third in early symptomatic AD; antibodies that do not clear plaque failed even before symptoms. In inherited AD, plaque removal did not visibly change tangles or glial activation at autopsy. 75,467,481,499

Hypothesis

Amyloid first, then tau?

Many investigators expect combination or sequential therapy: amyloid clearance plus a tau-directed drug. The first randomized tests add etalanetug to lecanemab, in DIAN-TU Tau NexGen and Eisai study 202. 100,132

Early evidence

Is tau a workable target?

N-terminal tau antibodies failed. Mid-region antibodies and tau antisense lowered tau biomarkers or tau PET but missed primary clinical endpoints in phase 2. 53,178,464,483,493

Early evidence

Is inflammation a workable target?

TREM2 agonism (AL002) and peripheral anti-inflammatory effects (semaglutide) did not slow decline. A positive phase 2b/3 signal for masitinib, an oral mast-cell and microglia inhibitor, awaits a confirmatory trial. 13,108,122

Established

Rescue by subgroup

Many claims rest on subgroups after a missed primary endpoint (valiltramiprosate in MCI, bepranemab in low tau, blarcamesine, HMTM delayed start). Regulators have been sceptical: the EMA recommended refusing blarcamesine, and the application was withdrawn in March 2026. 142,416,493,523

Established

Who can actually receive treatment?

Applying CLARITY AD criteria to a population cohort of 237 amyloid-positive people left only 8% eligible for lecanemab. That cohort was 97.5% White, so equity remains a concern. 410

Established

Blood tests are changing trials

The FDA cleared the first blood test to aid AD diagnosis on 16 May 2025. In primary care, clinicians' diagnostic accuracy rose from 61% to 91% with a p-tau217 blood test. Blood tests now screen people into AHEAD 3-45, TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 3 and PrevenTRON. 154,381,400,426,532
