After a diagnosis of MCI: who progresses?

Mild cognitive impairment (MCI) means thinking has declined more than expected for age while daily life is still independent. It is a risk state, not a sentence. Two groups of 100 people with MCI are followed for five years below.

Illustration. Hollow figure: no dementia yet. Solid figure: progressed to dementia. It applies each published annual rate to those not yet converted, every year, which is a simplification: real risk differs from person to person.

Not always memory first

Typical Alzheimer's starts with memory, because the first tangles form in the medial temporal lobe. Some people start with vision, language or planning instead. These atypical forms are much more common when the illness starts before 65. [[GraffRadford2021]]

How long, and how the illness ends

Established

Survival is measured in years

People aged 65 and older live on average 4 to 8 years after a diagnosis of Alzheimer's dementia, and some live as long as 20 years. Across studies, median survival from onset was 3.3 to 11.7 years, most often 7 to 10. [[AA2025Facts,Todd2013]]

Established

Age at diagnosis matters most

Median survival was 8.3 years when diagnosed at 65 and 3.4 years at 90, with no significant difference between men and women. Across studies, age was a consistent predictor of death; male sex was a less consistent one. [[Brookmeyer2002,Todd2013]]

Established

Pneumonia is the most common final event

Pneumonia is the most commonly identified immediate cause of death, and swallowing problems can lead to aspiration. In one autopsy series, respiratory causes accounted for 55.5% of deaths in Alzheimer's. [[AA2025Facts,Brunnstrom2009]]

Advanced dementia in nursing homes

In the CASCADE study of 323 residents with advanced dementia, 54.8% died over 18 months. Eating problems affected 85.8% and pneumonia 41.1%. See Living with dementia for planning and end-of-life care. [[Mitchell2009NEJM]]
