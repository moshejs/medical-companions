The biomarker toolbox

Established

Amyloid PET

Florbetapir visual reads found moderate-to-frequent neuritic plaques with 92% sensitivity and 100% specificity against autopsy within 2 years. The Centiloid scale runs from 0 (young controls) to 100 (typical mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's); about 12 to 24 Centiloids marked plaque thresholds at autopsy. [[Clark2012,Klunk2015,LaJoie2019]]

Established

Spinal fluid (CSF)

In Alzheimer's, CSF total tau is about 2.5 times and p-tau about 1.9 times control levels, and Aβ42 is about half. The Aβ42/40 ratio classifies better than Aβ42 alone. The FDA permitted the Lumipulse CSF ratio test in May 2022. [[Olsson2016,Hansson2019,FDA2022CSF]]

Established

Blood p-tau217

Plasma p-tau217 separated clinical Alzheimer's from other neurodegenerative diseases with AUC 0.96. In 1,213 Swedish patients, a p-tau217 test was about 90% accurate, against 61% for primary care doctors and 73% for specialists using standard workup. In May 2025 the FDA cleared the first blood test, for specialised care, "not intended as a screening or stand-alone diagnostic test". [[Palmqvist2020,Palmqvist2024,FDA2025]]

Early evidence

A rule-out test for primary care

In October 2025 the FDA cleared Roche's Elecsys pTau181 plasma test for primary care, to help rule out Alzheimer's amyloid pathology. Its reported negative predictive value was 97.9%, a figure that depends on the low prevalence of the people studied. [[Roche2025]]

Established

Tau PET and FDG-PET

Flortaucipir tau PET separated Alzheimer's from other neurodegenerative disorders with about 90% sensitivity and specificity, outperforming MRI volumes. FDG-PET patterns correctly classified 95% of Alzheimer's, 92% of Lewy body and 94% of frontotemporal dementia in a 7-centre study. [[Ossenkoppele2018,Mosconi2008]]

Not specific

NfL and GFAP

Neurofilament light rises with neurodegeneration of any cause, including MS, ALS and brain injury. GFAP, an astrocyte marker, rises with amyloid even before symptoms. The 2024 criteria use neither for diagnosis. [[Mattsson2019,Pereira2021,Jack2024]]

Screening tests: what they can and cannot do

MMSE

  • 11 items, 30 points; about 7 minutes. [[Creavin2016,Borson2000]]
  • At a cut-point of 24 in community settings: sensitivity 0.85, specificity 0.90 for dementia. [[Creavin2016]]
  • Strongly affected by schooling: median 29 with 9 or more years, 22 with 0 to 4 years. [[Crum1993]]
  • Highly verbal and insensitive to mild impairment. [[Tombaugh1992]]
  • Under copyright; its authors began enforcing their rights in 2000, and licensed forms cost about USD 1.23 each in 2011. [[Newman2011]]

MoCA

  • About 10 minutes, 30 points; adds executive and visuospatial tasks. In the original study a cut-off of 26 caught 90% of MCI, against 18% for the MMSE; specificity was 87%, against 100% for the MMSE. [[Nasreddine2005]]
  • The cut-off dispute: the owner keeps 26 or above as normal, with 1 point added for 12 or fewer years of education. A meta-analysis found 26 gives too many false positives, especially in older or less educated people, and the best accuracy at 23. [[MoCA2025FAQ,Carson2018]]
  • 2025 change: since December 2025, certification is optional for total-score screening but still required to interpret sub-scores. [[MoCA2025FAQ]]

Mini-Cog

  • Three-word recall (0 to 3) plus a clock drawing (0 or 2), total 0 to 5. A score of 0 to 2 means a higher likelihood of important impairment; a normal score does not rule it out. [[MiniCog2026]]
  • About 3 minutes, and its accuracy was not affected by education or language in the original multilingual study. [[Borson2000]]
  • In a meta-analysis: sensitivity 0.91, specificity 0.86 for dementia. [[Tsoi2015]]
Established

The limit they share

Screening tools grade severity and prompt further assessment. They do not establish the cause. A highly educated person with early disease can score normally; low education, poor hearing or vision, or a language barrier can lower a score without dementia. [[Crum1993,Tombaugh1992,Creavin2016,MiniCog2026]]

Exam hookMoCA for MCI, MMSE for grading dementia, Mini-Cog for speed. None of them names the cause.
