Delirium or dementia?

Delirium is a medical emergency that can look like dementia, and it often happens on top of dementia. The Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) asks four questions. Switch the features on and off to see how the algorithm combines them.

Published guidance

Routine in the AAN parameter

Screening for depression, B12 deficiency and hypothyroidism, and a non-contrast CT or MRI, in the initial evaluation. This is a 2001 guideline. [[Knopman2001]]

Only when suspected

Syphilis serology when neurosyphilis is suspected. CSF or genetic testing is considered for atypical cases: onset before 65, rapid onset, or non-memory presentations. [[Knopman2001,Arvanitakis2019]]

Medicines

A medication review. The heaviest cumulative use of strong anticholinergics, most commonly tricyclic antidepressants, first-generation antihistamines and bladder antimuscarinics, was associated with dementia (hazard ratio 1.54). [[Gray2015]]

Sleep

Sleep-disordered breathing was associated with incident MCI or dementia in 298 older women (odds ratio 1.85), following low oxygen rather than broken sleep. [[Yaffe2011]]

Gait first

When walking changes first, imaging looks for the enlarged ventricles and DESH pattern of normal-pressure hydrocephalus; a CSF tap test may follow. [[Nakajima2021]]

A sudden change

A sudden worsening points to delirium and a search for medical and drug causes, even in someone already living with dementia. [[Inouye1990,Fick2002]]
