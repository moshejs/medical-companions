Part 4 · Living, caring, preventing

Nursing care

Four parts of nursing care for a person living with dementia: assessment, giving the medicines, communication and care approaches, and documentation and safety. Each part has an animation or a tool to try, and each claim links to its source.

ScopeThis module describes what guidelines, drug labels and published studies say. It is not a care plan. It does not replace local protocols, your scope of practice, or the prescriber. Where the sources say no dementia-specific evidence exists, the module says so instead of filling the gap.

Accuracy: a 2021 meta-analysis (17 studies, 3,702 observations) found pooled sensitivity and specificity of 0.88 each. [[Tieges2021]] In 785 acute medical patients aged 70 or over, the 4AT had sensitivity 76% and specificity 94%, while the CAM used in routine conditions had 40% and 100%. [[Shenkin2019]]

Guidelines differ NICE CG103 (2023 update) names the 4AT when indicators of delirium are present, and the CAM-ICU or ICDSC in critical care. [[NICE2023CG103]] NICE NG97 (2018) suggests the long CAM or the Observational Scale of Level of Arousal for inpatients with cognitive impairment of unknown cause, says instruments alone should not separate delirium from delirium on dementia, and says that when the two cannot be told apart, delirium is treated first. [[NICE2018NG97]] A 2012 review found only 9 studies, with 50 participants who had delirium on dementia, validating any delirium tool in people with dementia. [[Morandi2012]]

4 · Documentation and safety

Build an ABC chart entry

An ABC chart records the Antecedent (what came just before), the Behaviour (described as seen and heard) and the Consequence (what happened next). Over many entries, a pattern can point to a need. Choose one of each to assemble a worked entry. The example is fictional.

A Cochrane review of 18 trials found that programmes built on functional analysis, of which ABC recording is the basis, reduced how often challenging behaviour happened and improved caregivers' reactions after the intervention, but not at follow-up. The analysis part could not be separated from the rest of each programme. No study was found that validates the ABC chart itself as a nursing documentation tool in dementia. [[MonizCook2012]]
