Microglia and the lost synapse

Memory depends on synapses, the contact points between neurons. In Alzheimer's they are lost early, and the brain's own immune cells help remove them. Press play to watch a patch of tissue change, then try the two switches.

The other cells, and the other diseases

Established

Astrocytes react too

Activated microglia release IL-1α, TNF and C1q, which push astrocytes into a reactive state, named "A1" in 2017, that supports neurons less well. Such astrocytes were abundant in Alzheimer's brains. [[Liddelow2017]]

Debated

But "A1" and "A2" are too simple

A 2021 consensus statement criticised binary astrocyte labels (A1 versus A2, good versus bad) as oversimplified, and recommended describing reactive astrocytes with several measures at once. [[Escartin2021]]

Established

Microglial genes carry risk

TREM2 R47H raised risk about 2.9-fold. Other rare variants in microglial genes point the same way: PLCG2 P522R is protective (odds ratio 0.68), ABI3 S209F (1.43) and TREM2 R62H (1.67) raise risk. [[Jonsson2013,Sims2017]] More in Genes and inheritance.

Association only

A leaky barrier, early

Breakdown of the blood–brain barrier in the hippocampus was found in people with early cognitive problems, whatever their amyloid and tau status. The authors propose it as an independent early marker; the study shows association, not cause. [[Nation2019]]

Sleep and the "glymphatic" system

Mostly rodent · contested

The mouse findings

In mice, fluid enters the brain along spaces around arteries and leaves around veins. Deleting the astrocyte water channel AQP4 cut solute clearance by about 70%. Sleep widened the space between cells by about 60% and sped amyloid clearance, and sleep restriction increased plaques. [[Iliff2012,Xie2013,Kang2009]]

Contrary evidence

Other labs disagree

An independent group found transport in brain tissue fitted diffusion, not flow, and did not depend on AQP4. A 2024 mouse study found clearance was lower, not higher, during sleep and anaesthesia. [[Smith2017,Miao2024]]

Small human studies

What people show

One night without sleep (7 people against 17 controls) slowed clearance of a tracer from most brain regions. Sleep deprivation raised spinal-fluid amyloid by 25–30%, but mainly through more production, not less clearance. [[Eide2021,Lucey2018]]

Where this leaves it

That sleep affects amyloid levels is better supported than any specific glymphatic mechanism in human Alzheimer's. That is a synthesis across the studies above, not any single study's conclusion. Meningeal lymphatic vessels, described in 2015, may be another drainage route; their role in humans is unknown. [[Lucey2018,Smith2017,TarasoffConway2015]]

In plain wordsPoor sleep and amyloid are linked. How the brain "washes" itself at night is still argued about, mostly from mouse studies.
