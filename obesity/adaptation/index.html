Not medical adviceThe Obesity Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. Obesity is a chronic disease with genetic, biological, environmental and social causes, not a failure of willpower. The guide does not replace a doctor, nurse, dietitian, psychologist, surgeon or the rest of a care team. BMI, waist and staging tools are teaching aids, never a diagnosis or a target. Doses, schedules and guideline statements are quoted as published, never as instructions: do not start, stop or change a medicine without a clinician. Weight-change results are averages from trials, not predictions for any person.

Part 1 · Understanding obesity

Why lost weight comes back

After weight loss the body pushes back: it burns a little less energy than expected and hunger signals rise, and both can last for years. 165,530 The Endocrine Society describes obesity as a disorder of the energy homeostasis system in which the defended level of fat mass is "reset" upward. 499 Regain is biology at work, not a failure of willpower.

Weeks 0 to 30

Weight comes off

Sixteen contestants in a televised weight-loss competition (baseline BMI 49.4) lost 38 ± 9% of their weight by week 30, 83% of it fat. 266 The mean loss was 58.3 kg. 165 On the see-saw, energy out outweighs energy in, so stored fat is used up.

In plain wordsFor a while, the body uses more energy than it takes in, and weight falls. 266
Exam hookHuge losses in this small cohort: 58.3 kg on average by week 30. 165

Regain is biologyMost people who lose weight regain some or most of it, and the body's defence of its fat stores is a main reason obesity is treated as a long-term condition. 472,499 Weight stigma causes physical and psychological harm and poorer care; experts call for a public narrative "coherent with modern scientific knowledge". 477

One year later: the hormones had not gone back

In 50 adults with overweight or obesity, a 10-week very-low-energy diet programme led to a mean loss of 13.5 kg. Appetite hormones were measured at the start, at 10 weeks and again at 62 weeks, a year after the weight loss. 530 Choose a hormone. The study abstract reports the direction of each change and its P value, not its size, so the bars show direction only.

Fell with weight loss

Leptin: still lower a year later

Leptin fell with weight loss and was still lower at 62 weeks (P<0.001). 530 Restoring leptin with injections in people holding a 10% loss brought energy expenditure and thyroid hormones back to pre-loss values, so the reduced-weight state behaves like relative leptin deficiency. 470

How is body weight regulated? Competing models

Hypothesis

Set point

Active feedback, presumably encoded in the brain, links fat stores to intake and expenditure. It fits much of the biology but struggles to explain the rise in obesity since the 1980s. 520

Hypothesis

Settling point

Weight settles where passive feedback from the environment and expenditure balances out. It fits social and environmental effects but struggles with the genetics. Hybrid models such as the dual intervention point model try to bridge the two. 520

Hypothesis

Which fits best?

Hall and Guo judged the set-point model most consistent with current data, and note that weight loss is resisted by counterbalancing physiology, so "eat less, move more" misreads the energy-balance equation. 209

Hypothesis

Energy balance or carbohydrate-insulin?

The energy balance model puts the brain at the centre, mostly below conscious awareness. 212 The carbohydrate-insulin model proposes that hormonal responses to a high-glycaemic-load diet drive fat storage first. 344 In 32 controlled feeding studies, fat loss was 16 g/day greater on lower-fat diets at equal calories. 209 An active debate.

Slow by nature

Half-time~1 year

Body weight responds slowly to a lasting change in intake, with a half-time of about a year; people with more body fat take longer to reach a new steady state. 207

The "3500 kcal per pound" rule32.2 MJ/kg

Roughly matches the energy in lost weight only when starting body fat is above 30 kg; it overestimates the deficit needed in people with less body fat. 206 Hall's simulator is a population model, not a personal prescription. 207

Relapse>80%

Rosenbaum and Leibel report relapse to pre-weight-loss body fat in over 80% after otherwise successful weight loss, driven by coordinated metabolic, behavioural, neuroendocrine and autonomic responses. 472 MacLean describes them as comprehensive, persistent and redundant. 347

If eating or weight is distressingIf eating or weight causes distress, or someone restricts, binges or purges, talk to a clinician or an eating-disorder service. The diets described here were research protocols, not advice.

In plain wordsAfter weight loss, hunger hormones rise, fullness hormones fall, and the body burns a little less energy. These changes can last for years. That is why keeping weight off is hard, and why long-term care, covered in Diet, activity & behaviour and Living with obesity, matters. 165,530
