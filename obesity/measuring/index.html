BMI, waist & body fat

BMI is a quick, cheap screening number with real blind spots. Follow a point across the height and weight plane through the WHO classes, see what BMI misses, and why waist and a second measure now matter.

Step 1 · the formula

Quetelet's formula

BMI is weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared 133,280. In 1832 Adolphe Quetelet concluded from growth studies that, outside the growth spurts of infancy and puberty, "the weight increases as the square of the height" 133. In 1972 Ancel Keys and colleagues compared indices in 7,424 healthy men in 12 cohorts and named weight/height² the "body mass index": it correlated little with height and well with body fat 133,280.

In plain wordsBMI is your weight divided by your height squared. It is a quick screening number, but it cannot tell fat from muscle, so on its own it is not a diagnosis 412,480.

Keep in mind
  • BMI cannot tell fat from muscle and misses about half of adults with excess body fat 412.
  • Risk can start at lower BMI in south Asian, Chinese, Arab and Black adults 60.
  • A fixed waist-to-height cut-off over-classifies shorter adults 399.
  • These are population cut-offs: a teaching result, not a diagnosis or a target.

Sarcopenic obesity

Excess adiposity together with low skeletal muscle mass and function (ESPEN/EASO 2022). Screening uses BMI or waist plus signs of low muscle; diagnosis rests on muscle-function tests, then body composition. Stage I has no complications, stage II has complications 124.

The open question

Meta-analyses favour waist-to-height ratio over waist or BMI 30,55, but waist cut-offs remain population-specific and international harmonisation is unfinished 13,474. Status: unresolved. How a clinician puts measures together is covered in Assessment & staging; children in Children & adolescents.
