Not medical adviceThe Obesity Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. Obesity is a chronic disease with genetic, biological, environmental and social causes, not a failure of willpower. The guide does not replace a doctor, nurse, dietitian, psychologist, surgeon or the rest of a care team. BMI, waist and staging tools are teaching aids, never a diagnosis or a target. Doses, schedules and guideline statements are quoted as published, never as instructions: do not start, stop or change a medicine without a clinician. Weight-change results are averages from trials, not predictions for any person.

Diet, activity & behaviour

A comprehensive lifestyle intervention has three parts: a moderately reduced-calorie diet, more physical activity, and behavioural strategies to help people stick to both. 262 Here is what the big trials found, as group averages over months and years.

If eating or weight is distressingIf eating or weight causes distress, or someone restricts, binges, purges or exercises compulsively, talk to a clinician or an eating-disorder service before changing how they eat. Some diet trials excluded people with eating disorders, so their results may not apply.

Year 1

Any diet you can keep to

POUNDS LOST randomised 811 adults to four reduced-calorie diets with different fat, protein and carbohydrate. All lost about 6 kg by 6 months and began to regain after 12; at 2 years the diets did not differ, and each group session attended added about 0.2 kg of loss. 484 In DIETFITS, low-fat and low-carbohydrate diets gave −5.3 vs −6.0 kg at 12 months, and neither genotype nor insulin secretion predicted who did better on which. 178 A network meta-analysis "supports the practice of recommending any diet that a patient will adhere to". 268

In plain wordsNo single "best diet" exists. Low-fat, low-carbohydrate and Mediterranean diets gave similar average results; the one a person can keep up matters most. 178,268,484

Macronutrients

Fat vs carbohydrate: small differences

Large trials find small or no differences between macronutrient patterns; pooled analyses give low-carbohydrate about a 1 kg edge over low-fat (1.15 kg in 53 RCTs). 548 Adherence dominates. 268 In DIRECT, low-carbohydrate cut the total:HDL cholesterol ratio by 20% vs 12% on low-fat. 502 Weight loss diminished by 12 months in a network meta-analysis of 121 trials. 191

Activity: small for weight, large for health

Aerobic exercise alone−1.6 kg

At 6 months in 14 RCTs (−1.7 kg at 12 months); the authors called it "not an effective weight loss therapy" on its own. 547 Exercise without weight loss still lowered blood pressure, triglycerides and fasting glucose. 507

Guidelines, as published150–300 min

Weekly moderate-intensity aerobic activity, or 75 to 150 minutes vigorous, plus muscle strengthening on 2 or more days; the least active gain most from modest increases. 428

Keeping it off>250 min

Per week went with better maintenance in observational studies, but no well-designed RCT shows it prevents regain. 125 Women who kept off ≥10% reported about 275 min a week. 248

Protecting muscle−5% vs −2%

Lean mass lost with aerobic vs resistance training during about 9% weight loss in older adults; function improved most with combined training. 566 With tirzepatide, resistance exercise reduced lean loss by 1.09 kg. Early evidence 195

Everyday movement2 h

More sitting a day in sedentary people with mild obesity than in lean people, unchanged by weight loss or gain, which suggests posture is partly biologically set. Early evidence 324 Non-exercise movement explained a 10-fold range in fat gain with overfeeding. 323

Weight is not the only outcome

Diabetes Prevention Program58%

Lower diabetes incidence with the lifestyle programme (goals ≥7% weight loss, ≥150 min/week activity) vs placebo over 2.8 years; 31% with metformin. 126,293 Still 27% lower at 15 years. 128 More in Obesity & type 2 diabetes.

Look AHEAD heart outcomesHR 0.95

No difference in the primary cardiovascular outcome (95% CI 0.83 to 1.09); stopped for futility at a median 9.6 years. 335 People who lost ≥10% in year 1 had fewer events in a post-hoc, non-randomised analysis. Early evidence 337

Benefits that outlast the weight5 years

Across 124 trials, behavioural programmes still lowered cholesterol ratio and HbA1c 5 years after they ended, despite regain, though benefits dwindled as weight returned. 219

Regain is not failureProgrammes are followed by a plateau at about 6 months and gradual regain; reviews attribute this to biological, behavioural and environmental factors and say long-term clinical care is needed. 168,210 Why it happens is in Why lost weight comes back. Binge-eating disorder is a treatable condition; see Living with obesity.

Exam hookDPP: lifestyle −58%, metformin −31% diabetes incidence; NNT over 3 years 6.9 and 13.9. 293 Look AHEAD: 8.6% vs 0.7% loss at 1 year, 6.0% vs 3.5% at the end, cardiovascular HR 0.95. 333,335
In plain wordsProgrammes with regular support work better than advice alone, and support can be in person, by phone or online. 21,262 All these numbers are averages; weight is only one part of health. 262

Obesity Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on obesity, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people living with obesity, their families and caregivers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose. Nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine, a diet, an operation or a care plan without your own doctor or care team. BMI, waist-to-height, the Edmonton Obesity Staging System and other tools are for learning, never a diagnosis or a target for any person. Doses, escalation schedules, thresholds and guideline statements are quoted as published by drug labels, the AGA, AAP, ASMBS/IFSO, the Lancet Commission and other guidelines, never as instructions, and weight-change figures are averages from trials, not predictions for any person. Compounded or online "GLP-1" products are described only as the FDA describes them. If eating or weight causes distress, or someone restricts, binges or purges, talk to a clinician or an eating-disorder service.
