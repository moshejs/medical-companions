Other weight-loss medicines

Before the weekly incretin injections there were other medicines, and they are still prescribed: one blocks fat digestion in the gut, two act on appetite and reward in the brain, one replaces a missing signal in rare genetic obesity, and one is a daily GLP-1 injection. Gut lumen · capsule

Orlistat

Orlistat reversibly blocks gastric and pancreatic lipases inside the gut, so undigested dietary fat (triglyceride) is not absorbed. 151 It is the one medicine here that does not act on the brain. In XENDOS, 4 years of orlistat with lifestyle change gave 5.8 kg loss vs 3.0 kg on placebo, and diabetes incidence of 6.2% vs 9.0%, a 37.3% risk reduction confined to people with impaired glucose tolerance. Only 52% on orlistat and 34% on placebo completed treatment. 547

In plain wordsOrlistat stops some of the fat in food from being digested, so it passes out of the body in the stool instead of being absorbed. It works in the gut, not the brain.
Exam hookOrlistat = gastric and pancreatic lipase inhibitor. XENDOS: diabetes risk down 37.3% over 4 years, only in impaired glucose tolerance. 151,547
Exam hookMatch drug to site: orlistat, gut lipases; 151 phentermine, hypothalamic catecholamines; 146 naltrexone–bupropion, hypothalamus and mesolimbic reward circuit; 141 setmelanotide, the MC4 receptor; 144 liraglutide, the GLP-1 receptor. 148
In plain wordsThese medicines work in different places, which is why their side effects differ too. None of them is a test of willpower: they act on the body's own systems for hunger, fullness and digestion.

In plain wordsEach label sets a check-in point. If the medicine has not helped enough by then, the label says to stop it, so nobody stays on a medicine that is not working for them.

Guidelines weigh these options differently. The AGA's 2022 guideline suggested semaglutide 2.4 mg, liraglutide 3.0 mg, phentermine–topiramate ER and naltrexone–bupropion ER for long-term management (moderate-certainty evidence), and phentermine or diethylpropion with low certainty; it suggested against orlistat. 201 Yet orlistat is the one medicine shown in XENDOS to cut diabetes incidence over 4 years in people with impaired glucose tolerance. 547

Orforglipron (Foundayo) label

Orforglipron, a small-molecule, non-peptide oral GLP-1 receptor agonist, is FDA-approved as Foundayo; it carries the same class boxed warning as the injectable incretin medicines. 143

Exam hookFenfluramine and dexfenfluramine: withdrawn in 1997 for heart-valve disease. Sibutramine: withdrawn after SCOUT (more non-fatal heart attacks and strokes). Rimonabant: withdrawn in Europe for psychiatric harm. 69,77,251
In plain wordsSome past weight-loss medicines turned out to harm the heart or mood and were taken off the market. That history is why new ones now go through very large safety studies before and after approval.
