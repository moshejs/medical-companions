Part 1 · Understanding obesity

What obesity is

Obesity is a chronic disease of excess body fat with many causes, now split into clinical and preclinical obesity. Watch how common it became between 1990 and 2022, then try the Lancet Commission's classification for yourself.

Step 1 · definition

Excess adiposity

The 2025 Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology Commission defines obesity as "a condition characterised by excess adiposity, with or without abnormal distribution or function of adipose tissue, and with causes that are multifactorial and still incompletely understood" 477. Its 58 experts, who included people with lived experience of obesity, reached 90–100% agreement, and 76 organisations endorsed it 477.

In plain wordsObesity means carrying more body fat than is healthy for you. It is a long-term condition with many causes, including genes, biology, surroundings and life circumstances, not a failure of willpower 53,477.

Clinical or preclinical? A teaching explorer

The Commission first asks whether excess body fat is confirmed, then whether it is already changing how organs work or limiting daily life 477. Choose how adiposity was confirmed and tick any problems to see how the framework sorts an imaginary person.

Teaching toolThis shows the logic of a published framework. It is not a diagnosis. The Commission's 18 adult criteria carry exact clinical definitions and require that the problem be caused by excess adiposity; the short names below summarise them 477.

Commission framework, as a teaching tool

Preclinical obesity

BMI plus at least one anthropometric criterion (waist circumference, waist-to-hip or waist-to-height ratio) confirms excess adiposity 477.

With no organ dysfunction and no limits on daily activities, the framework calls this preclinical obesity: preserved function, with a varying but generally increased risk of clinical obesity, type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, some cancers and mental disorders 477.

In plain wordsThe label is about how the body is working, not about size. Two people with the same BMI can be in different categories.
Exam hookConfirm adiposity first (BMI + 1 body measure, 2 body measures, or direct fat measurement; assumed above BMI 40), then look for organ dysfunction 477.

"Metabolically healthy" obesity is not risk-free: in 3.5 million UK adults, people with obesity but no diabetes, hypertension or hyperlipidaemia had more coronary heart disease (HR 1.49) and heart failure (HR 1.96) than metabolically healthy people of normal weight 59. The Commission's preclinical category likewise means preserved organ function with increased risk 477. Why "disease"?

What the label recognises

The Commission says the causes include genetic, environmental, psychological, nutritional and metabolic factors that alter the biology maintaining fat tissue (agreement 95%) 477. Supporters argue the disease label reflects biology and reduces blame, and the AMA's 2013 recognition has been linked to more access to care 53,305. Established

The debate

The Commission sets out the counter-argument that the label may reduce emphasis on personal responsibility, rejects it as a non-medical reason, and grounds disease status in organ dysfunction 477. AACE's term "adiposity-based chronic disease" reflects a separate debate about naming and stigma 362. Why weight bias harms care is covered in Weight stigma & bias.

Not a failure of willpowerObesity is a long-term condition shaped by genes, biology, surroundings and life circumstances 53,477. Weight that returns after weight loss reflects the body defending its weight, which is why it is treated as chronic; see Why lost weight comes back 527.
