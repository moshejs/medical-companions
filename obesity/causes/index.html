Not medical adviceThe Obesity Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. Obesity is a chronic disease with genetic, biological, environmental and social causes, not a failure of willpower. The guide does not replace a doctor, nurse, dietitian, psychologist, surgeon or the rest of a care team. BMI, waist and staging tools are teaching aids, never a diagnosis or a target. Doses, schedules and guideline statements are quoted as published, never as instructions: do not start, stop or change a medicine without a clinician. Weight-change results are averages from trials, not predictions for any person.

Part 1 · Understanding obesity

Causes: genes & environment

Obesity is a chronic disease with many causes. An international consensus states that it results "primarily from a combination of genetic, epigenetic, and environmental factors", and that the idea of obesity as a choice is a misconception. 474 Build the picture layer by layer.

Genes · families, adoptees and twins

Heritability

In 540 Danish adoptees, adult weight class tracked the BMI of their biological parents, not their adoptive parents. 524 Identical twins reared apart had BMI correlations of 0.70 (men) and 0.66 (women). 525 Across 88 twin estimates (140,525 twins), BMI heritability ranged from 0.47 to 0.90, median 0.75; family studies gave lower figures, median 0.46. 134 In 87,782 twin pairs, the genetic share was lowest at age 4 (0.42 in boys) and rose to 0.75 by age 19. 506

In plain wordsBody size runs in families mostly because of shared genes, not shared kitchens. Heritability describes differences between people in one setting; it does not mean weight is fixed. 134,524
Exam hookTwin heritability of BMI 0.47–0.90 (median 0.75); family studies lower (median 0.46). Adoptees resemble their biological, not adoptive, parents. 134,524

Medicines that can cause weight gain

Many medicines for diabetes, depression and other chronic diseases affect weight. The Endocrine Society guideline states that, where possible, options with favourable weight profiles should be chosen; that is quoted as published, not as an instruction. 20 The figures below are trial averages compared with placebo. 123

MedicinesThese medicines treat serious conditions. Do not start, stop or change any medicine without a clinician. Weight effects can be discussed with them, and other options may exist.

More influences across a lifetime

Before birth and in infancyOR 4.47

Maternal obesity before pregnancy was linked to overweight or obesity in children aged 10–18 (OR 4.47). 565 Each 1 SD of faster weight gain in the first year roughly doubled the odds of childhood obesity (OR 1.97). 130 Established

Sleep, tested−270 kcal

Two nights of short sleep lowered leptin by 18% and raised ghrelin by 28%. 518 In a randomised trial, adults who usually slept under 6.5 hours slept about 1.2 hours more and ate 270 kcal a day less than controls. 537

Stopping smoking4.67 kg

People who quit without treatment gained 4.67 kg on average at 12 months, mostly in the first 3 months; 16% lost weight and 13% gained more than 10 kg. 33 These are averages; people vary widely.

Neighbourhood−3.38 points

In a randomised social experiment, offering 4,498 women in high-poverty housing a voucher to move to a lower-poverty area lowered the prevalence of BMI 40 or more by 3.38 percentage points. 343

Gut microbesRR 1.21

Microbes from a twin with obesity made germ-free mice gain more fat. 458 Early-life antibiotics are linked to childhood obesity (RR 1.21), 500 but a placebo-controlled faecal transplant did not change insulin sensitivity or body composition. 615 Hypothesis in humans

Hormone conditionsa few kg

Across thyroid function within the normal range, weight differences were modest, a few kilograms at most. 167 Cushing's syndrome, from chronic excess glucocorticoids, is rare compared with common obesity. 306

Obesity Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on obesity, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people living with obesity, their families and caregivers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose. Nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine, a diet, an operation or a care plan without your own doctor or care team. BMI, waist-to-height, the Edmonton Obesity Staging System and other tools are for learning, never a diagnosis or a target for any person. Doses, escalation schedules, thresholds and guideline statements are quoted as published by drug labels, the AGA, AAP, ASMBS/IFSO, the Lancet Commission and other guidelines, never as instructions, and weight-change figures are averages from trials, not predictions for any person. Compounded or online "GLP-1" products are described only as the FDA describes them.
