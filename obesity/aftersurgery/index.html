Obesity is a chronic disease with genetic, biological, environmental and social causes, not a failure of willpower. The guide does not replace a doctor, nurse, dietitian, psychologist, surgeon or the rest of a care team. BMI, waist and staging tools are teaching aids, never a diagnosis or a target. Part 3 · Treatment

Life after surgery

Surgery is the start of lifelong care, not the end of treatment. The 1991 NIH consensus already called for lifelong medical surveillance after surgery. 402 Scrub from the day of the operation to 20 years later to see when each problem tends to appear.

UrgentAfter surgery, get urgent medical help for a fast heartbeat that does not settle, fever, belly or shoulder pain, or changes in breathing: these can be signs of a leak. 213,284 Persistent vomiting needs prompt care because it can cause thiamine deficiency, which can damage the brain. 345 Confusion or seizures can be a sign of very low blood sugar. 284 If you feel low, have thoughts of harming yourself, or feel unsafe, contact your care team or an emergency service straight away.

The first month

In LABS (4776 first operations, 2005 to 2007), 30-day mortality after bypass or band was 0.3% and 4.3% had at least one major adverse outcome. 161 Current perioperative mortality is reported at 0.03% to 0.2%. 29,132 A 2026 review gives leaks in about 1% after sleeve and 0.6% after bypass, early bleeding in 0.6 to 2.5% and 1 to 1.5%, and blood clots in veins in 0.3 to 2.4%; 70 to 80% of these clots are diagnosed after discharge. 284 Signs of a leak can be vague: a fast heart rate that persists, fever, belly or shoulder pain and changes in breathing. 284

In plain wordsMost people recover from the operation without serious problems. A fast heartbeat, fever, belly or shoulder pain or trouble breathing in the first weeks needs urgent care.
Exam hookIn 210 bypass patients, breathing difficulty and a heart rate above 120 were the most sensitive signs of a leak; a routine contrast swallow caught only 2 of 9 leaks. 213
In plain wordsSurgery is safe for most people, but it has risks: leaks, bleeding, clots, ulcers, reflux, gallstones and blocked bowel. Many of these are found early when follow-up continues. 284
Exam hookLABS 30-day mortality 0.3%; modern perioperative mortality 0.03 to 0.2%. 29,161 Predictors of a bad early outcome: previous clots, sleep apnoea, impaired function and extreme BMI, but not age, sex or race. 161

Vitamins and minerals, for life

Many people already have at least one vitamin or mineral deficiency before surgery, and the ASMBS guideline covers screening, prevention and repletion of thiamine, B12, folate, iron, vitamins A, E, K and D, calcium, copper and zinc. 417 Deficiencies are becoming more common while follow-up monitoring declines. 417 Pick a nutrient and an operation; rows about other operations fade.

Iron

Anaemia is common

Anaemia affects 33 to 49% within 2 years of surgery: about 17% after sleeve and 45 to 50% after bypass or biliopancreatic diversion; low ferritin occurs in more than 30% at 5 years. When iron deficiency is established, oral iron may not be enough and intravenous iron may be needed. 345 Five years after bypass, low ferritin was found in 48% of adolescents versus 29% of adults. 246 Calcium interferes with the absorption of iron, zinc and copper, so iron and calcium supplements are taken apart. 284,345

In plain wordsLow iron is one of the most common problems after surgery. Blood tests pick it up; your team decides the treatment.
Exam hookIron and calcium supplements are separated in time, because calcium blocks iron absorption. 345

Next steps

Revisional surgery achieves further weight loss in selected people but carries more complications than a first operation. 132 Medicines are another option; see How GLP-1 medicines work and Why lost weight comes back.

If eating or weight is distressingIf eating or weight causes distress, or someone restricts, binges or purges, talk to a clinician or an eating-disorder service. Some weight returning after surgery is common and is not a personal failure.

Alcohol and mental health

Alcohol after bypass20.8%

Cumulative new alcohol use disorder symptoms by 5 years after bypass, versus 11.3% after a band (AHR 2.08). 286 In SOS, bypass raised the risk of alcohol abuse diagnoses (adjusted HR 4.97); banding did not. 533

Self-harmRR 1.54

Self-harm emergencies rose from 2.33 to 3.63 per 1000 patient-years after surgery in Ontario; 93% of events were in people with a mental health diagnosis in the 5 years before. 46 A meta-analysis found 3.8 times the odds of self-harm or suicide attempt compared with matched controls. 67

Suicide4.1

Estimated suicides per 10,000 person-years after bariatric surgery, higher than in the general population. 424 Whether surgery causes this or reflects earlier risk is unresolved. 46

Support is part of careThese findings support psychological screening and follow-up before and after surgery, and alcohol education and screening. 286,424 Feeling low, struggling with drinking or with how you eat are reasons to reach out, not reasons for shame. More in Living with obesity.

Pregnancy after surgery

A 2019 international consensus advises postponing pregnancy until weight is stable, typically about 1 year after sleeve or bypass and 2 years after a band, and contraceptive counselling before surgery. 508 In a Swedish register, pregnancies after surgery had less gestational diabetes (1.9% versus 6.8%) and fewer large babies, but more small-for-gestational-age babies (15.6% versus 7.6%). 267 Details are in Pregnancy & fertility.

Exam hookThiamine deficiency with vomiting after surgery risks Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome. 345 Alcohol use disorder risk is about twice as high after bypass as after a band. 285,286
In plain wordsAfter surgery you need vitamins and minerals for life, and regular blood tests. Your team will tell you which ones. Do not start, stop or change any medicine or supplement without your clinician. 417
