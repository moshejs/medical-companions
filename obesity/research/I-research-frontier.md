# I · Research frontier

Pass date: 2026-11-11. Modules served: frontier, glp.

This pass covers the obesity treatment pipeline as of November 2026: next-generation incretin and amylin agents (retatrutide, cagrilintide-semaglutide, amycretin, survodutide, mazdutide, maridebart cafraglutide), oral options (orforglipron, oral semaglutide 25 and 50 mg), higher-dose semaglutide 7.2 mg, muscle-preserving approaches (bimagrumab), outcome trials beyond weight (heart, kidney, liver, knee, sleep apnoea, alcohol use, Alzheimer's disease), melanocortin-pathway therapy for rare genetic and hypothalamic obesity, endoscopic and device therapies, phenotype-guided prescribing, and cost, access, shortages and compounded products. Approved-drug basics (mechanisms, standard dosing, core safety) belong to pass F; surgery to pass G.

Access notes:

- Several of the newest papers (TRIUMPH-4/1-OA, retatrutide OSA, SYNCHRONIZE-CVOT, SYNCHRONIZE-2) were "online ahead of print" in PubMed, without volume or pages yet.

## Findings

### frontier · How to read the pipeline (evidence labels)
- In this file, "Established" means at least one completed phase 3 randomised trial or a regulatory approval for the stated use; "Early evidence" means phase 1–2 trials, single trials in small groups, or observational data; "Hypothesis" means a mechanism or signal not yet tested in an adequately powered human outcome trial.
- Obesity is a chronic, relapsing disease; the WHO 2025 guideline states that it requires lifelong care and that medication alone cannot solve the global obesity burden (Established) [[Celletti2026WHO]].
- After diet-induced weight loss (mean 13.5 kg), hormones that drive hunger (raised ghrelin, lowered leptin, peptide YY and cholecystokinin) and subjective hunger were still shifted at 1 year, which is why regain is framed as biology and why long-term treatment strategies are being studied (Established) [[Sumithran2011]].
- An updated systematic review (38 RCTs, n = 25,816, adults without diabetes, searched to 25 March 2026) reported placebo-subtracted weight loss of up to -5.8% for liraglutide, -14.8% for subcutaneous semaglutide, -14.3% for oral semaglutide, -12.4% for orforglipron and -19.0% for tirzepatide, with numerically larger reductions for emerging multi-agonists: -23.9% for amycretin and -22.1% for retatrutide (Established for marketed agents; Early evidence for comparisons across agents) [[Moiz2026SR]].
- In that review, gastrointestinal adverse events occurred in 76.0% on GLP-1-based drugs versus 40.1% on placebo; discontinuation for adverse events was 10.7% versus 3.4%; serious adverse events 6.5% versus 5.2% [[Moiz2026SR]].
- A network meta-analysis (58 trials, n = 24,214, adults without diabetes, to 6 March 2026) ranked retatrutide first versus placebo (-22.10%, 95% CI -25.60 to -18.60), then tirzepatide (-19.28%) and CagriSema (-17.32%), but the confidence intervals overlapped for several comparisons and head-to-head evidence is limited (Early evidence for the ranking) [[Chen2026NMA]].
- The same network meta-analysis found low-certainty evidence of higher discontinuation with danuglipron and retatrutide and better tolerability with mazdutide [[Chen2026NMA]].

### frontier · Retatrutide (GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptor triple agonist)
- Retatrutide is a single molecule that activates three receptors: GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon [[Jastreboff2023Retatrutide]].
- Phase 2 (n = 338, adults with obesity or overweight plus a weight-related condition, 48 weeks): mean weight change at 48 weeks was -8.7% (1 mg), -17.1% (4 mg combined), -22.8% (8 mg combined) and -24.2% (12 mg) versus -2.1% with placebo (Early evidence, now superseded by phase 3) [[Jastreboff2023Retatrutide]].
- In phase 2 at 48 weeks, 100%, 93% and 83% of those on 12 mg lost at least 5%, 10% and 15% of body weight, versus 27%, 9% and 2% on placebo [[Jastreboff2023Retatrutide]].
- Phase 2 side effects were mainly gastrointestinal, dose-related and partly reduced by a lower starting dose (2 mg vs 4 mg); heart rate rose in a dose-dependent way, peaked at 24 weeks and then declined [[Jastreboff2023Retatrutide]].
- TRIUMPH-1 (phase 3, n = 2,339, adults with obesity without diabetes, 80 weeks, treatment-regimen estimand): mean weight change -17.6% (4 mg), -23.7% (9 mg) and -25.0% (12 mg) versus -3.9% with placebo; differences -19.8 and -21.0 percentage points for 9 and 12 mg (Established) [[Jastreboff2026TRIUMPH1]].
- TRIUMPH-1 also tested knee osteoarthritis pain (574 participants) and obstructive sleep apnoea (243 participants) inside the same master protocol; the most common adverse events were gastrointestinal [[Jastreboff2026TRIUMPH1]].
- TRIUMPH-2 (phase 3, n = 1,152, adults with obesity and type 2 diabetes, 80 weeks): weight change -11.9% (4 mg), -16.8% (9 mg), -18.8% (12 mg) versus -5.1% with placebo; treatment differences -6.9%, -11.8% and -13.8% (Established) [[Bellido2026TRIUMPH2]].
- TRIUMPH-2 safety: nausea in 14%, 21% and 28% (4, 9, 12 mg) versus 8% on placebo; diarrhoea 27–34% versus 13%; hypotension up to 6% and dysaesthesia (abnormal skin sensation) up to 7% versus under 1% and 1% on placebo; permanent stopping for adverse events or death was 12% on 9 mg and 8% on 12 mg versus 5% on placebo [[Bellido2026TRIUMPH2]].
- TRANSCEND-T2D-1 (phase 3, n = 537, type 2 diabetes on diet and exercise alone, 40 weeks): HbA1c fell by 1.69–1.94 percentage points with retatrutide versus 0.81 with placebo, and weight by 11.5–15.3% versus 2.6% [[Bajaj2026TRANSCEND]].
- TRIUMPH-4 (knee osteoarthritis, n = 445, 68 weeks): WOMAC pain score (0–10) fell by 3.96 (9 mg) and 3.74 (12 mg) versus 2.12 with placebo (hybrid estimand), and weight by 20.0% and 23.7% versus 4.6% (Established for this outcome) [[Schnitzer2026TRIUMPH4]].
- In both retatrutide knee osteoarthritis trials, serious adverse events were more common with retatrutide than with placebo [[Schnitzer2026TRIUMPH4]].
- TRIUMPH-1-OSA (n = 243, baseline apnoea-hypopnoea index 58.6 events/h, 80 weeks): AHI fell by 34.3 (9 mg) and 31.7 (12 mg) events per hour versus 9.9 with placebo (hybrid estimand) [[Grunstein2026RetaOSA]].
- In TRIUMPH-2-OSA (people with type 2 diabetes, n = 448), AHI fell by 24.7 (9 mg) and 25.4 (12 mg) events per hour versus 10.3 with placebo [[Grunstein2026RetaOSA]].
- Retatrutide is still described as investigational in its 2026 phase 3 reports, and no openFDA label existed for it on 2026-11-11 (status check: openFDA) [[Schnitzer2026TRIUMPH4,Grunstein2026RetaOSA]].
- Ongoing retatrutide outcome work on ClinicalTrials.gov: TRIUMPH-3 (severe obesity plus established cardiovascular disease, n = 1,946, completed April 2026, no results posted) and an event-driven trial of major cardiovascular and kidney events (n ≈ 10,000, primary completion February 2029) [[NCT05882045,NCT06383390]].

### frontier · Cagrilintide-semaglutide (CagriSema, amylin plus GLP-1)
- Cagrilintide is a long-acting amylin analogue; CagriSema combines cagrilintide 2.4 mg and semaglutide 2.4 mg once weekly [[Garvey2025REDEFINE1]].
- REDEFINE 1 (phase 3a, n = 3,417, adults without diabetes, 68 weeks, treatment-policy estimand): weight change -20.4% with CagriSema versus -3.0% with placebo (difference -17.3 percentage points; 95% CI -18.1 to -16.6) (Established) [[Garvey2025REDEFINE1]].
- REDEFINE 1 gastrointestinal adverse events: 79.6% with CagriSema versus 39.9% with placebo, mostly transient and mild to moderate [[Garvey2025REDEFINE1]].
- REDEFINE 2 (phase 3a, n = 1,206, obesity with type 2 diabetes, 68 weeks): weight change -13.7% versus -3.4% with placebo; 73.5% versus 15.9% reached HbA1c 6.5% or less [[Davies2025REDEFINE2]].
- REDEFINE 5 (Japan and Taiwan, n = 331, head-to-head, 68 weeks, trial-product estimand): -18.4% with CagriSema versus -11.9% with semaglutide 2.4 mg alone (difference -6.5 points; 95% CI -8.4 to -4.6) (Established) [[Yamauchi2026REDEFINE5]].
- A completed phase 3 head-to-head trial of CagriSema versus tirzepatide 15 mg in obesity (n = 809, primary completion 8 December 2025) is registered; no peer-reviewed results were found in PubMed on 2026-11-11 [[NCT06131437]].
- REDEFINE 3, a cardiovascular outcome trial of CagriSema in established cardiovascular disease (n = 7,101, primary outcome 3-point MACE), is active with primary completion September 2027 [[NCT05669755]].
- CagriSema had no openFDA label on 2026-11-11, so it was not a US-approved product on that date (status check: openFDA).

### frontier · Amycretin (single molecule acting on GLP-1 and amylin receptors)
- Amycretin is a unimolecular GLP-1 and amylin receptor agonist, tested both as a weekly injection and as a daily tablet (Early evidence) [[Dahl2025Amycretin,Gasiorek2025Amycretin]].
- Subcutaneous phase 1b/2a (n = 125, BMI 27–39.9): weight change -24.3% with 60 mg versus -1.1% with placebo at 36 weeks; -22.0% with 20 mg (36 weeks); -16.2% with 5 mg (28 weeks); -9.7% with 1.25 mg (20 weeks) (Early evidence) [[Dahl2025Amycretin]].
- The authors reported a high rate of study withdrawal (much of it unrelated to adverse events), which limits these early numbers [[Dahl2025Amycretin]].
- Oral amycretin first-in-human phase 1 (n = 144): all treatment-emergent adverse events were mild or moderate; gastrointestinal events made up 49% of events (Early evidence) [[Gasiorek2025Amycretin]].
- No phase 3 amycretin trial was found by name on ClinicalTrials.gov on 2026-11-11 (searched "amycretin" and the code NNC0487-0111; status check: ClinicalTrials.gov).

### frontier · Glucagon/GLP-1 dual agonists: survodutide and mazdutide
- Survodutide activates glucagon and GLP-1 receptors; the glucagon arm is thought to add effects on liver fat and energy expenditure (Hypothesis for the added benefit beyond weight) [[leRoux2026SYNCHRONIZE1,Sanyal2024Survodutide]].
- SYNCHRONIZE-1 (phase 3, n = 725, obesity without diabetes, 76 weeks): weight change -12.2% (3.6 mg) and -13.0% (6.0 mg) versus -5.4% with placebo (Established) [[leRoux2026SYNCHRONIZE1]].
- SYNCHRONIZE-1 gastrointestinal adverse events: 80.9% (3.6 mg) and 89.7% (6.0 mg) versus 47.9% with placebo [[leRoux2026SYNCHRONIZE1]].
- SYNCHRONIZE-2 (phase 3, n = 752, obesity with type 2 diabetes, 76 weeks): weight change -8.2% and -9.8% versus -3.9% with placebo; HbA1c fell 0.9 and 0.8 points versus 0.2 [[Wharton2026SYNCHRONIZE2]].
- SYNCHRONIZE-CVOT (n = 5,507 treated, median 22 months): the composite of cardiovascular death, stroke, myocardial infarction, ischaemia-driven revascularisation or heart-failure event occurred in 3.7% on survodutide versus 4.1% on placebo (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.67–1.18), meeting non-inferiority; this is a safety result, not proof of benefit (Established for cardiovascular safety) [[Bozkurt2026SYNCHRONIZECVOT]].
- In SYNCHRONIZE-CVOT, nausea affected 48–49% on survodutide versus 12.1% on placebo, vomiting 31.7–34.0% versus 4.8%, and gastrointestinal events led about one in five people on survodutide to stop (19.3% and 20.8%) versus 1.9% on placebo [[Bozkurt2026SYNCHRONIZECVOT]].
- Survodutide phase 2 in biopsy-confirmed MASH with fibrosis F1–F3 (n = 293, 48 weeks): MASH improved without worse fibrosis in 47%, 62% and 43% (2.4, 4.8, 6.0 mg) versus 14% with placebo (Early evidence) [[Sanyal2024Survodutide]].
- SYNCHRONIZE-MASLD (phase 3, n = 216, 48 weeks): a 30% or greater fall in MRI liver fat in 84.2% on survodutide 6.0 mg versus 24.3% on placebo (efficacy estimand; 68.5% vs 28.6% by treatment-regimen estimand) [[Kaplan2026SYNCHRONIZEMASLD]].
- Survodutide phase 3 liver outcome trials are recruiting: one in MASH with F2–F3 fibrosis (n = 1,800, primary completion December 2031) and one in compensated MASH cirrhosis (n = 1,590, primary completion June 2029) [[NCT06632444,NCT06632457]].
- Mazdutide (glucagon/GLP-1 dual agonist) GLORY-1 (phase 3, China, n = 610, BMI 24 or more with a condition or 28 or more): weight change at 48 weeks -11.00% (4 mg) and -14.01% (6 mg) versus +0.30% with placebo (Established in Chinese adults) [[Ji2025GLORY1]].
- GLORY-2 (phase 3, China, n = 461, BMI 30 or more, 60 weeks): mazdutide 9 mg gave -16.65% versus -1.50% with placebo; vomiting 53.1% versus 1.3%, nausea 46.9% versus 3.2% [[Gao2026GLORY2]].
- A US phase 2 (n = 179) tested mazdutide up to 16 mg: weight change at 32 weeks -18.1% (16 mg) versus -0.9% with placebo, with 20% stopping the 16 mg dose for adverse events (Early evidence outside China) [[Hsia2026Mazdutide]].

### frontier · Maridebart cafraglutide (MariTide, monthly antibody-peptide conjugate)
- MariTide joins a GLP-1 receptor agonist peptide to an antibody that blocks the GIP receptor; it is dosed every 4 weeks or less often (Early evidence) [[Jastreboff2025MariTide]].
- That design (blocking GIP) is the opposite of tirzepatide (activating GIP), and both approaches lower weight; why both work is not settled (Hypothesis) [[Jastreboff2025MariTide,Jastreboff2022SURMOUNT1]].
- Phase 2 (n = 592, 52 weeks, treatment-policy estimand): in people with obesity, weight change ranged from -12.3% to -16.2% across doses versus -2.5% with placebo; in people with obesity and type 2 diabetes, -8.4% to -12.3% versus -1.7% [[Jastreboff2025MariTide]].
- In the phase 2 diabetes cohort HbA1c fell by 1.2 to 1.6 points versus a 0.1 rise with placebo; gastrointestinal events were common but less frequent with dose escalation and a lower starting dose [[Jastreboff2025MariTide]].
- Phase 3 programme on ClinicalTrials.gov: MARITIME-1 (obesity without diabetes, n = 3,853, 72-week weight endpoint, primary completion January 2027) and MARITIME-CV (n = 12,800, 3-point MACE, primary completion June 2028) [[NCT06858839,NCT07037433]].

### glp · Oral options: orforglipron and oral semaglutide
- Orforglipron is a small-molecule (non-peptide) GLP-1 receptor agonist taken once daily [[Wharton2023Orforglipron]].
- Orforglipron phase 2 (n = 272, 36 weeks): weight change -9.4% to -14.7% across doses versus -2.3% with placebo; gastrointestinal events led 10–17% to stop (Early evidence) [[Wharton2023Orforglipron]].
- ATTAIN-1 (phase 3, n = 3,127, obesity without diabetes, 72 weeks): weight change -7.5% (6 mg), -8.4% (12 mg) and -11.2% (36 mg) versus -2.1% with placebo (Established) [[Wharton2025ATTAIN1]].
- In ATTAIN-1 on 36 mg, 54.6%, 36.0% and 18.4% lost at least 10%, 15% and 20% of weight versus 12.9%, 5.9% and 2.8% on placebo; adverse events led 5.3–10.3% to stop versus 2.7% [[Wharton2025ATTAIN1]].
- ATTAIN-2 (phase 3, n = 1,613, obesity with type 2 diabetes, 72 weeks): weight change -5.1%, -7.0% and -9.6% (6, 12, 36 mg) versus -2.5% with placebo [[Horn2026ATTAIN2]].
- ATTAIN-MAINTAIN (n = 376 who had lost weight on tirzepatide or semaglutide in SURMOUNT-5, 52 weeks): switching to oral orforglipron kept a model-based 74.7% (after tirzepatide) and 79.3% (after semaglutide) of the earlier weight reduction, versus 49.2% and 37.6% with placebo (Early evidence; no arm stayed on the injection) [[Aronne2026ATTAINMAINTAIN]].
- ATTAIN-OSA (two trials, n = 363 and 349, 52 weeks): AHI fell 17.8 and 17.3 events per hour versus 4.5 and 5.3 with placebo [[Malhotra2026ATTAINOSA]].
- The FDA approved orforglipron as FOUNDAYO on 1 April 2026 (NDA 220934), for weight reduction and long-term maintenance in adults with obesity, or overweight with at least one weight-related condition, with a reduced-calorie diet and more physical activity (Established) [[FDA-Foundayo]].
- The FOUNDAYO label states a start of 0.8 mg once daily, stepping up at intervals of at least 30 days through 2.5 mg and 5.5 mg, with optional steps to 9, 14.5 or 17.2 mg; the maximum is 17.2 mg once daily; it is taken with or without food. Quoted from the label, not as instructions [[FDA-Foundayo]].
- The FOUNDAYO label says its trials used an investigational formulation, and it shows the trial arms as "equivalent dosages" of 5.5, 9 and 17.2 mg; so the 36 mg trial arm corresponds to the 17.2 mg label dose. Students should not compare trial and label milligrams directly [[FDA-Foundayo,Wharton2025ATTAIN1]].
- FOUNDAYO carries the class boxed warning on thyroid C-cell tumours, but the label notes orforglipron is not pharmacologically active in rats or mice and did not produce tumours in rodents; human relevance is undetermined [[FDA-Foundayo]].
- Oral semaglutide 50 mg (OASIS 1, n = 667, no diabetes, 68 weeks): weight change -15.1% versus -2.4% with placebo; gastrointestinal events 80% versus 46% [[Knop2023OASIS1]].
- Oral semaglutide 25 mg (OASIS 4, n = 307, no diabetes, 64 weeks): weight change -13.6% versus -2.2% with placebo (difference -11.4 points); gastrointestinal events 74.0% versus 42.2% (Established) [[Wharton2025OASIS4]].
- The WEGOVY label now includes tablets: 1.5 mg daily for 30 days, then monthly steps to a 25 mg once-daily maintenance dose, taken on an empty stomach with up to 4 ounces of water and at least 30 minutes before food, drink or other oral medicines. Quoted from the label, not as instructions; Drugs@FDA lists the tablet NDA 218316 as approved on 22 December 2025 [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- WEGOVY tablets are labelled for weight reduction and for lowering the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events in adults with established cardiovascular disease and obesity or overweight [[FDA-Wegovy]].

### glp · Higher-dose semaglutide 7.2 mg
- STEP UP (phase 3b, n = 1,407, BMI 30 or more without diabetes, 72 weeks): weight change -18.7% with semaglutide 7.2 mg versus -15.6% with 2.4 mg (difference -3.1 points; 95% CI -4.7 to -1.6) and -3.9% with placebo (Established) [[Wharton2025STEPUP]].
- In STEP UP, dysaesthesia (tingling or altered skin sensation) affected 22.9% on 7.2 mg, 6.0% on 2.4 mg and 0.5% on placebo; gastrointestinal events 70.8%, 61.2% and 42.8% [[Wharton2025STEPUP]].
- STEP UP T2D (n = 512, obesity with type 2 diabetes, 72 weeks): -13.2% with 7.2 mg versus -3.9% with placebo; dysaesthesia 18.9% versus 4.9% (2.4 mg) and none (placebo) [[Lingvay2025STEPUPT2D]].
- The current WEGOVY label allows the injection to be increased to a maximum of 7.2 mg once weekly for people who tolerate 2.4 mg for at least 4 weeks when more weight reduction is clinically indicated. Quoted from the label, not as instructions [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- In a 55-person MRI sub-study of STEP UP, semaglutide reduced adipose tissue by 11.1 L versus placebo; thigh muscle volume changed by -1.1 L (95% CI -2.3 to 0.1, not significant), muscle fat infiltration fell, and sit-to-stand repetitions were preserved (13 at baseline, 15 at week 72) (Early evidence) [[Hjelmesaeth2026STEPUPMuscle]].

### frontier · Preserving muscle during weight loss
- Bimagrumab is an antibody that blocks activin type II receptors, a signal that normally limits muscle growth (Early evidence) [[Heymsfield2021Bimagrumab]].
- Bimagrumab phase 2 (n = 75, type 2 diabetes with BMI 28–40, 48 weeks, IV every 4 weeks): fat mass fell 20.5% (-7.5 kg) versus 0.5%, lean mass rose 3.6% (+1.7 kg) versus -0.8%, and weight fell 6.5% versus 0.8% (Early evidence; analysis only in completers) [[Heymsfield2021Bimagrumab]].
- Bimagrumab with semaglutide phase 2 (n = 507, 48 weeks): absolute weight change -9.3 kg (bimagrumab 30 mg/kg), -14.2 kg (semaglutide 2.4 mg) and -17.8 kg (both) versus -3.3 kg with placebo (Early evidence) [[Heymsfield2026Bimagrumab]].
- Common bimagrumab adverse events were muscle spasms, diarrhoea and acne [[Heymsfield2026Bimagrumab]].
- Whether keeping lean mass during drug-induced weight loss improves strength, falls or long-term health has not been shown in outcome trials (Hypothesis) [[Heymsfield2026Bimagrumab,Hjelmesaeth2026STEPUPMuscle]].

### frontier · Head-to-head and reference points (approved incretins)
- STEP 1 (semaglutide 2.4 mg, n = 1,961, 68 weeks): -14.9% versus -2.4% with placebo (Established; reference arm for the chart) [[Wilding2021STEP1]].
- SURMOUNT-1 (tirzepatide, n = 2,539, 72 weeks): -15.0% (5 mg), -19.5% (10 mg) and -20.9% (15 mg) versus -3.1% with placebo (Established; reference arm for the chart) [[Jastreboff2022SURMOUNT1]].
- SURMOUNT-5 (open-label head-to-head, n = 751, 72 weeks): -20.2% with tirzepatide versus -13.7% with semaglutide; waist -18.4 cm versus -13.0 cm (Established) [[Aronne2025SURMOUNT5]].
- The ZEPBOUND label now also covers moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnoea in adults with obesity, with a maximum dose of 15 mg weekly (Established) [[FDA-Zepbound]].

### frontier · Outcomes beyond weight: heart and kidney
- SELECT (n = 17,604, established cardiovascular disease, BMI 27 or more, no diabetes, mean follow-up 39.8 months): cardiovascular death, heart attack or stroke occurred in 6.5% on semaglutide 2.4 mg versus 8.0% on placebo (HR 0.80; 95% CI 0.72–0.90) (Established) [[Lincoff2023SELECT]].
- In SELECT, adverse events leading to permanent stopping occurred in 16.6% on semaglutide versus 8.2% on placebo [[Lincoff2023SELECT]].
- SURMOUNT-MMO (tirzepatide versus placebo, n = 15,374 enrolled, BMI 27 or more without diabetes, with or at risk of cardiovascular disease) has a five-part primary outcome that includes death from any cause; it is the first incretin outcome trial to test both primary and secondary prevention (results pending; Hypothesis until reported) [[Lam2025SURMOUNTMMO,NCT05556512]].
- ClinicalTrials.gov lists SURMOUNT-MMO as active, not recruiting, with primary completion October 2027 [[NCT05556512]].
- FLOW (n = 3,533, type 2 diabetes with chronic kidney disease, median 3.4 years): semaglutide 1.0 mg lowered major kidney disease events by 24% (HR 0.76; 95% CI 0.66–0.88), cardiovascular death (HR 0.71) and death from any cause (HR 0.80) (Established, in diabetes) [[Perkovic2024FLOW]].
- A pooled analysis of SELECT, FLOW and SOUL (n = 30,787) found fewer kidney composite events with semaglutide (HR 0.84; 95% CI 0.77–0.91), including people without diabetes from SELECT (Early evidence for people without diabetes; pooled secondary analysis) [[Mann2026KidneyPooled]].

### frontier · Outcomes beyond weight: liver (MASH)
- ESSENCE part 1 (n = 800 at week 72, biopsy MASH with F2–F3 fibrosis): steatohepatitis resolved without worse fibrosis in 62.9% on semaglutide 2.4 mg versus 34.3% on placebo; fibrosis improved without worse steatohepatitis in 36.8% versus 22.4% (Established for histology) [[Sanyal2025ESSENCE]].
- The WEGOVY injection is FDA-labelled for noncirrhotic MASH with F2–F3 fibrosis under accelerated approval, which depends on confirming clinical benefit; ESSENCE part 2 (cirrhosis-free survival) runs to April 2029 [[FDA-Wegovy,NCT04822181]].
- Tirzepatide phase 2 SYNERGY-NASH (n = 190, F2–F3, 52 weeks): MASH resolved without worse fibrosis in 44%, 56% and 62% (5, 10, 15 mg) versus 10% with placebo (Early evidence) [[Loomba2024SYNERGY]].

### frontier · Outcomes beyond weight: joints and sleep apnoea
- STEP 9 (n = 407, obesity with moderate knee osteoarthritis, 68 weeks): WOMAC pain (0–100) fell 41.7 points with semaglutide 2.4 mg versus 27.5 with placebo; weight -13.7% versus -3.2% (Established) [[Bliddal2024STEP9]].
- Knee pain benefit has now been replicated with retatrutide in two phase 3 trials (TRIUMPH-4, TRIUMPH-1-OA) [[Schnitzer2026TRIUMPH4]].
- Sleep apnoea benefit is now reported for tirzepatide (labelled), retatrutide and oral orforglipron (Established for tirzepatide; phase 3 results for the others) [[FDA-Zepbound,Grunstein2026RetaOSA,Malhotra2026ATTAINOSA]].

### frontier · GLP-1 and alcohol and other substance use
- A large US Veterans Affairs cohort of people with diabetes (215,970 starting GLP-1 receptor agonists) linked them with lower risk of substance use disorders, psychotic disorders, seizures and neurocognitive disorders, and higher risk of gastrointestinal disorders, hypotension, syncope, kidney stones and drug-induced pancreatitis, versus usual care (Hypothesis-generating; observational) [[Xie2025NatMed]].
- A Swedish registry study (227,866 people with alcohol use disorder) linked semaglutide use with lower risk of alcohol-related hospital admission (adjusted HR 0.64; 95% CI 0.50–0.83) (Early evidence; observational) [[Lahteenvuo2025]].
- A 9-week phase 2 RCT (n = 48, alcohol use disorder, low-dose semaglutide) reduced alcohol drunk in a laboratory task and drinks per drinking day, but not average drinks per day or drinking days (Early evidence) [[Hendershot2025Semaglutide]].
- In that trial, people who smoked cut cigarettes per day more on semaglutide than on placebo (subsample) (Hypothesis) [[Hendershot2025Semaglutide]].
- A 26-week RCT (n = 108, alcohol use disorder with obesity, semaglutide 2.4 mg plus cognitive behavioural therapy) cut heavy drinking days by 41.1 percentage points versus 26.4 on placebo (difference -13.7; 95% CI -22.0 to -5.4) (Early evidence; single centre) [[Klausen2026Lancet]].
- No GLP-1 receptor agonist is FDA-approved for alcohol or other substance use disorders as of 2026-11-11 [[FDA-Wegovy,FDA-Zepbound,FDA-Foundayo]].

### frontier · GLP-1 and the brain: the Alzheimer's test
- evoke and evoke+ (two phase 3 trials, n = 3,808, amyloid-confirmed early Alzheimer's disease, oral semaglutide up to 14 mg daily): CDR-SB change at 104 weeks did not differ from placebo (differences -0.08 and 0.10; p = 0.57 and 0.46); both trials were discontinued for a negative outcome [[Cummings2026Evoke]].
- This negative result is a lesson in evidence levels: observational links between GLP-1 drugs and lower dementia risk did not hold up in a randomised trial in people who already had early Alzheimer's disease [[Xie2025NatMed,Cummings2026Evoke]].

### frontier · Rare genetic and hypothalamic obesity (melanocortin pathway)
- Setmelanotide activates the melanocortin-4 receptor (MC4R), downstream of the leptin pathway; it targets obesity caused by breaks in that pathway, not common (polygenic) obesity (Established) [[Clement2020Setmelanotide,FDA-Imcivree]].
- In POMC deficiency (n = 10) and LEPR deficiency (n = 11), 80% and 45% lost at least 10% of body weight at about 1 year on open-label setmelanotide; "most hunger" scores fell 27.1% and 43.7% [[Clement2020Setmelanotide]].
- In Bardet-Biedl syndrome (phase 3 with 14-week placebo period, n = 38), 32.3% of patients aged 12 or older lost at least 10% of body weight after 52 weeks; results in Alström syndrome were inconclusive [[Haqq2022BBS]].
- In acquired hypothalamic obesity (after a hypothalamic tumour, lesion or injury; phase 3, n = 120, ages 4–66, 52 weeks), BMI changed -16.5% with setmelanotide versus +3.3% with placebo; serious adverse events 28% versus 8% (Established) [[Miller2026HO]].
- The current IMCIVREE label covers acquired hypothalamic obesity from age 4 and Bardet-Biedl syndrome or POMC, PCSK1 or LEPR deficiency (confirmed genetically) from age 2; it states the drug is not indicated for general (polygenic) obesity [[FDA-Imcivree]].
- IMCIVREE label warnings include depression and suicidal ideation, sexual arousal disturbance, hypersensitivity, skin hyperpigmentation and new or darker moles, and (in hypothalamic obesity) acute adrenal insufficiency and sodium imbalance [[FDA-Imcivree]].
- First FDA approval of setmelanotide was 25 November 2020 (NDA 213793), with efficacy supplements approved in 2022, 2024 and 2026 [[FDA-Imcivree]].

### frontier · Endoscopic and device therapies
- Endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty (MERIT, n = 209, class 1–2 obesity, 52 weeks): total body weight loss 13.6% with ESG plus lifestyle versus 0.8% with lifestyle alone; ESG-related serious adverse events in 2% (3 of 131) (Established for class 1–2 obesity) [[AbuDayyeh2022MERIT]].
- In MERIT, 68% of the ESG group kept at least 25% excess weight loss at 104 weeks [[AbuDayyeh2022MERIT]].
- Vagal nerve blockade (ReCharge, n = 239, sham-controlled, 12 months): 9.2% versus 6.0% initial body weight loss; it missed both prespecified efficacy targets though it met the safety target (device-related serious adverse events 3.7%) (Early evidence; modest effect) [[Ikramuddin2014ReCharge]].
- Duodenal mucosal resurfacing (REVITA-2, sham-controlled, type 2 diabetes): overall HbA1c change at 24 weeks did not differ significantly from sham (p = 0.147), with benefit seen only in the European subgroup and in people with high fasting glucose (Early evidence; a diabetes procedure, not a weight-loss procedure) [[Mingrone2022REVITA2]].

### frontier · Precision medicine: phenotype-guided treatment
- A Mayo Clinic study grouped 383 of 450 adults (85%) into four phenotypes: "hungry brain" (abnormal satiation), "emotional hunger", "hungry gut" (abnormal satiety) and "slow burn" (lower metabolic rate); 27% had two or more (Early evidence) [[Acosta2021Phenotype]].
- In a 12-month pragmatic trial (n = 312) using older medicines, phenotype-guided prescribing gave 15.9% weight loss versus 9.0% without it (difference -6.9%); 79% versus 34% lost more than 10% (Early evidence; single centre, before modern incretins) [[Acosta2021Phenotype]].

### frontier · Cost, access, shortages and compounded products
- A US lifetime microsimulation (2023 dollars) estimated incremental cost-effectiveness ratios of $197,023 per QALY for tirzepatide and $467,676 per QALY for semaglutide; reaching $100,000 per QALY would need further net price cuts of 30.5% and 81.9% (Early evidence; model) [[Hwang2025CostEffect]].
- The same model projected that, per 100,000 people treated, tirzepatide would avert 10,655 and semaglutide 8,263 cardiovascular disease cases over a lifetime [[Hwang2025CostEffect]].
- A 2026 commentary reports that the Institute for Clinical and Economic Review (ICER) found semaglutide and tirzepatide cost-effective versus lifestyle alone, but that even limited uptake exceeds ICER's budget-impact threshold [[Ramachandran2026ICER]].
- In US health-system records (n = 125,474 starting a GLP-1 drug, 2018–2023), 64.8% of people without type 2 diabetes stopped within 1 year, versus 46.5% with diabetes; higher income and greater weight loss were linked to staying on treatment [[Rodriguez2025Discontinuation]].
- The WHO 2025 guideline made conditional recommendations for long-term GLP-1 therapy and for intensive behavioural therapy alongside it, citing limited long-term data, cost, health-system readiness and equity as reasons for the conditional grade [[Celletti2026WHO]].
- The AGA 2022 guideline recommended adding medication to lifestyle intervention for adults with BMI 30 or more, or 27 or more with weight-related complications, who have not responded adequately to lifestyle alone (Established) [[Grunvald2022AGA]].
- In UK prescribing data, a GLP-1 receptor agonist shortage from July 2023 was analysed as a distinct event in prescribing trends [[Ibrahim2024Shortage]].
- After the US semaglutide and tirzepatide shortages ended, a 2025 secret-shopper study of 75 weight-loss clinics and medical spas in two states found 56.0% still offered compounded GLP-1 products combined with B vitamins; 4 of 21 supplying pharmacies were not licensed for sterile compounding [[DiStefano2026Compounded]].
- A poison-centre case series described 10-fold dosing errors with compounded semaglutide drawn from vials with syringes, partly from confusion between millilitres, units and milligrams [[Lambson2023Compounded]].

### Exam hooks
- Retatrutide = GIP + GLP-1 + glucagon receptor agonist; TRIUMPH-1 (no diabetes, 80 weeks): -25.0% on 12 mg versus -3.9% placebo [[Jastreboff2026TRIUMPH1]].
- People with type 2 diabetes lose less weight on the same incretin than people without diabetes (TRIUMPH-2 12 mg -18.8%; REDEFINE 2 -13.7%) [[Bellido2026TRIUMPH2,Davies2025REDEFINE2]].
- CagriSema = cagrilintide (amylin analogue) + semaglutide; REDEFINE 1: -20.4% versus -3.0% at 68 weeks [[Garvey2025REDEFINE1]].
- Amycretin is one molecule acting on both GLP-1 and amylin receptors (unlike CagriSema, which is two molecules) [[Dahl2025Amycretin]].
- Survodutide and mazdutide are glucagon/GLP-1 dual agonists; survodutide has a completed cardiovascular safety trial (HR 0.89, non-inferior) [[Bozkurt2026SYNCHRONIZECVOT]].
- MariTide blocks the GIP receptor (antagonist) while activating GLP-1; tirzepatide activates both [[Jastreboff2025MariTide]].
- Orforglipron is a non-peptide oral GLP-1 agonist taken with or without food; oral semaglutide must be taken fasting with a small amount of water [[FDA-Foundayo,FDA-Wegovy]].
- FOUNDAYO label doses (max 17.2 mg) are expressed differently from trial doses (36 mg) [[FDA-Foundayo]].
- STEP UP: semaglutide 7.2 mg gave -18.7% versus -15.6% with 2.4 mg; dysaesthesia was a new signal (22.9%) [[Wharton2025STEPUP]].
- SELECT: semaglutide cut major cardiovascular events by 20% in people with cardiovascular disease and no diabetes [[Lincoff2023SELECT]].
- FLOW: semaglutide cut major kidney events by 24% in type 2 diabetes with CKD [[Perkovic2024FLOW]].
- evoke/evoke+: oral semaglutide did not slow early Alzheimer's disease [[Cummings2026Evoke]].
- Setmelanotide (MC4R agonist) works in POMC, PCSK1 and LEPR deficiency, Bardet-Biedl syndrome and acquired hypothalamic obesity, not in common obesity [[FDA-Imcivree]].
- Skin hyperpigmentation is a characteristic setmelanotide adverse effect [[Haqq2022BBS,FDA-Imcivree]].
- Vagal nerve blockade in ReCharge did not meet its prespecified efficacy goals [[Ikramuddin2014ReCharge]].
- A noninferiority cardiovascular safety trial (SYNCHRONIZE-CVOT) shows a drug does not raise risk; it does not prove benefit [[Bozkurt2026SYNCHRONIZECVOT]].
- Observational signals (dementia, substance use) need RCTs: evoke was negative; alcohol RCTs are small but positive [[Xie2025NatMed,Cummings2026Evoke,Klausen2026Lancet]].

### In plain words
- New medicines in testing act on two or three gut hormone signals at once. In large trials, people lost on average about a fifth to a quarter of their body weight over about a year and a half. These are averages; some people lose much more, some much less [[Jastreboff2026TRIUMPH1,Garvey2025REDEFINE1]].
- Most of these medicines are not approved yet. Trial results do not mean a drug is available or right for you [[NCT05882045,NCT06858839]].
- There are now daily tablets for obesity as well as weekly injections. Tablets on average lead to a bit less weight loss than the strongest injections [[Wharton2025ATTAIN1,Wharton2025OASIS4]].
- The most common side effects are feeling sick, vomiting, diarrhoea and constipation, mostly when the dose goes up [[Moiz2026SR]].
- Weight often comes back after stopping. That is the body's biology defending its weight, not a personal failure [[Sumithran2011,Aronne2026ATTAINMAINTAIN]].
- Some of these medicines also protect the heart, kidneys, liver and knees, not just weight [[Lincoff2023SELECT,Perkovic2024FLOW,Sanyal2025ESSENCE,Bliddal2024STEP9]].
- A rare group of people have obesity from a gene change or brain injury that breaks the body's fullness signal. A different medicine, setmelanotide, is made for them [[FDA-Imcivree]].
- Do not start, stop or change a medicine without talking to your clinician. That includes doses you read about in trials.
- "Compounded" or online versions are not the same as approved products. The FDA has not reviewed them for safety or quality, and dosing mistakes have happened [[Lambson2023Compounded,DiStefano2026Compounded]].
- If eating or weight causes you distress, or you restrict, binge or purge, please talk to a clinician or an eating-disorder service.
- For children, the focus is healthy habits and health, not the number on the scale.

## Open questions and controversies
- Are the newest agents better than tirzepatide? Cross-trial numbers favour retatrutide (about -25% at 80 weeks) [[Jastreboff2026TRIUMPH1]], but network meta-analysis confidence intervals overlap and head-to-head trials are few [[Chen2026NMA]]; the CagriSema versus tirzepatide trial has no published results yet [[NCT06131437]]. Status: open.
- Tolerability versus potency: retatrutide 9 mg had more stopping for adverse events than placebo (12% vs 5%) and new signals (dysaesthesia, hypotension) [[Bellido2026TRIUMPH2]]; semaglutide 7.2 mg also raised dysaesthesia [[Wharton2025STEPUP]]; about 20% stopped survodutide for gastrointestinal events in the CVOT [[Bozkurt2026SYNCHRONIZECVOT]]. Whether more weight loss is worth more side effects depends on the person. Status: open.
- Do the new agents reduce heart attacks, strokes and deaths, not just weight? Proven for semaglutide [[Lincoff2023SELECT]]; tirzepatide (SURMOUNT-MMO), retatrutide, CagriSema and MariTide outcome trials report 2027–2029 [[NCT05556512,NCT06383390,NCT05669755,NCT07037433]]. Survodutide has safety, not benefit, data [[Bozkurt2026SYNCHRONIZECVOT]]. Status: pending.
- GIP: activate or block? Tirzepatide activates the GIP receptor [[Jastreboff2022SURMOUNT1]]; MariTide blocks it [[Jastreboff2025MariTide]]; both lower weight. Mechanism unresolved (Hypothesis).
- Muscle loss: does lean tissue lost on incretins matter clinically? MRI sub-study found a non-significant fall in thigh muscle with preserved function [[Hjelmesaeth2026STEPUPMuscle]]; bimagrumab adds fat loss and muscle gain in phase 2 [[Heymsfield2021Bimagrumab,Heymsfield2026Bimagrumab]]. No outcome trial yet. Status: open.
- Lifelong treatment versus stepping down: regain after stopping is expected biology [[Sumithran2011]]; switching to an oral agent kept most weight loss over 1 year without an injectable comparator arm [[Aronne2026ATTAINMAINTAIN]]; most real-world users stop within a year [[Rodriguez2025Discontinuation]]. Status: open.
- Value: one US model finds semaglutide and tirzepatide not cost-effective at current net prices [[Hwang2025CostEffect]]; ICER, as reported in a 2026 commentary, found them cost-effective but unaffordable at scale [[Ramachandran2026ICER]]. Different assumptions (prices, time horizon, persistence) explain the gap. Status: depends on price.
- GLP-1 drugs for addiction: observational data are strong [[Lahteenvuo2025,Xie2025NatMed]] and RCTs are small but positive for alcohol [[Hendershot2025Semaglutide,Klausen2026Lancet]]; no approval. Status: Early evidence.
- GLP-1 drugs for dementia: observational signal [[Xie2025NatMed]] was not confirmed in early Alzheimer's disease [[Cummings2026Evoke]]. Prevention in people without dementia is untested. Status: negative for treatment; prevention a Hypothesis.
- Phenotype-guided prescribing: promising single-centre result with older medicines [[Acosta2021Phenotype]]; not tested with modern incretins. Status: Early evidence.
- Compounded products: after the shortages ended, compounded GLP-1 products with added ingredients persist, some from pharmacies not licensed for sterile compounding [[DiStefano2026Compounded]]. Regulatory status is evolving. Status: open.
