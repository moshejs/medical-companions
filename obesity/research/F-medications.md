# F · Anti-obesity medications

Pass date: 2026-11-11. Modules served: glp, medtrials, othermeds, sideeffects, pregnancy.

This pass covers the medicines used to treat obesity: how each one works, the pivotal trials (mean % weight change, comparator, timepoint, share of people reaching 5/10/15/20% loss), what happens when treatment stops, outcome trials (heart, heart failure, sleep apnoea, knee osteoarthritis, diabetes prevention), the current US labels (indications, dose escalation, boxed warnings, contraindications, pregnancy), adverse events with rates, lean-mass loss, persistence in real-world practice, and the older drugs that were withdrawn or failed safety trials. Every weight result is a population average from a trial, not a prediction for one person. Doses and escalation schedules are quoted from the labels as published, never as instructions: nobody should start, stop or change a medicine without their clinician.

## Findings

### glp · How the incretin medicines work
- Semaglutide is a GLP-1 analogue with 94% sequence homology to human GLP-1. It binds and activates the GLP-1 receptor, which is present in brain areas that regulate appetite. In animals it reached and activated neurons in regions that control food intake [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Native GLP-1 is released from gut enteroendocrine cells. It increases glucose-dependent insulin secretion, lowers glucagon, slows gastric emptying and reduces food intake [[Drucker2018GLP1]].
- Tirzepatide is a single molecule that activates both the GIP and GLP-1 receptors. A C20 fatty diacid lets it bind albumin, which prolongs its half-life to about 5 days. Nonclinical studies suggest the GIP action may add to appetite regulation [[FDA-Zepbound]].
- Liraglutide resists breakdown by peptidases and has a plasma half-life of 13 hours after subcutaneous injection, so it is given once daily. Native GLP-1 lasts only minutes [[FDA-Saxenda]].
- The Wegovy label says to stop semaglutide at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy "because of the long half-life of semaglutide" [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Orforglipron (FOUNDAYO) is a small-molecule, non-peptide oral GLP-1 receptor agonist. Its elimination half-life is about 29–49 hours, and it can be taken with or without food [[FDA-Foundayo,Wharton2025ATTAIN1]].
- The Wegovy tablet (oral semaglutide) is taken on an empty stomach in the morning with up to 4 ounces of water. The person then waits at least 30 minutes before eating, drinking or taking other oral medicines [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- In STEP 1–3, mean weight loss with semaglutide 2.4 mg was similar in people with GI adverse events (11.4–17.7%) and without them (9.6–17.1%). Mediation analysis found that less than 1 percentage point of the extra weight loss was explained by GI adverse events. Weight loss is not just the result of nausea [[Wharton2022GItolerability]].

### glp · Semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly: the STEP programme
- STEP 1: 1961 adults with BMI ≥30 (or ≥27 with a weight-related condition) and no diabetes were randomised 2:1 to semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo for 68 weeks, both with lifestyle intervention. Mean weight change was −14.9% vs −2.4% (difference −12.4 points; 95% CI −13.4 to −11.5) [[Wilding2021STEP1]].
- STEP 1, week 68, proportions reaching each threshold (semaglutide vs placebo): ≥5% 86.4% vs 31.5%; ≥10% 69.1% vs 12.0%; ≥15% 50.5% vs 4.9%. Mean loss was −15.3 kg vs −2.6 kg [[Wilding2021STEP1]].
- STEP 1: 4.5% on semaglutide vs 0.8% on placebo stopped treatment because of GI events. Nausea and diarrhoea were the most common and were typically transient and mild to moderate [[Wilding2021STEP1]].
- STEP 2 (adults with type 2 diabetes, BMI ≥27, HbA1c 7–10%; n=1210; 68 weeks): weight change was −9.6% with semaglutide 2.4 mg vs −3.4% with placebo (difference −6.2 points; 95% CI −7.3 to −5.2). 68.8% vs 28.5% reached ≥5% loss. Weight loss is smaller in people with diabetes [[Davies2021STEP2]].
- STEP 3 (with intensive behavioural therapy: 30 counselling visits plus an 8-week low-calorie diet; n=611): weight change was −16.0% vs −5.7% at 68 weeks. 86.6% vs 47.6% reached ≥5%, 75.3% vs 27.0% ≥10%, and 55.8% vs 13.2% ≥15% [[Wadden2021STEP3]].
- STEP 5 (2 years; n=304): weight change was −15.2% vs −2.6% at week 104 (difference −12.6 points; 95% CI −15.3 to −9.8). 77.1% vs 34.4% reached ≥5%. GI adverse events occurred in 82.2% vs 53.9% [[Garvey2022STEP5]].
- STEP 8 (open-label head-to-head; n=338; 68 weeks): weight change was −15.8% with semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly vs −6.4% with liraglutide 3.0 mg daily (difference −9.4 points; 95% CI −12.0 to −6.8). Pooled placebo was −1.9%. 13.5% on semaglutide and 27.6% on liraglutide stopped treatment for any reason [[Rubino2022STEP8]].
- STEP 8, ≥10/≥15/≥20% loss, semaglutide vs liraglutide: 70.9% vs 25.6%; 55.6% vs 12.0%; 38.5% vs 6.0% [[Rubino2022STEP8]].
- STEP UP (semaglutide 7.2 mg; adults with BMI ≥30 and no diabetes; n=1407; 72 weeks): weight change was −18.7% with 7.2 mg, −15.6% with 2.4 mg and −3.9% with placebo (7.2 vs 2.4 mg difference −3.1 points; 95% CI −4.7 to −1.6) [[Wharton2025STEPUP]].
- STEP UP safety: GI adverse events occurred in 70.8% (7.2 mg), 61.2% (2.4 mg) and 42.8% (placebo). Dysaesthesia (abnormal skin sensation) occurred in 22.9%, 6.0% and 0.5% [[Wharton2025STEPUP]].
- STEP TEENS (ages 12 to <18 with obesity; n=201; 68 weeks): BMI change was −16.1% with semaglutide 2.4 mg vs +0.6% with placebo. 73% vs 18% lost ≥5% of body weight. 4% had gallstones (cholelithiasis) on semaglutide vs none on placebo [[Weghuber2022STEPTEENS]].
- STEP 9 (knee osteoarthritis with obesity; n=407; 68 weeks): weight change was −13.7% vs −3.2%. WOMAC pain score fell by 41.7 vs 27.5 points (0–100 scale) [[Bliddal2024STEP9]].

### glp · Oral semaglutide and orforglipron
- OASIS 1 (oral semaglutide 50 mg daily; n=667; 68 weeks): weight change was −15.1% vs −2.4% (difference −12.7 points; 95% CI −14.2 to −11.3). Reaching ≥5/10/15/20%: 85/69/54/34% vs 26/12/6/3% [[Knop2023OASIS1]].
- OASIS 1: GI adverse events occurred in 80% on oral semaglutide 50 mg vs 46% on placebo [[Knop2023OASIS1]].
- OASIS 4 (oral semaglutide 25 mg daily, the dose marketed as the Wegovy tablet; n=307): weight change at week 64 was −13.6% vs −2.2% (difference −11.4 points; 95% CI −13.9 to −9.0). GI adverse events occurred in 74.0% vs 42.2% [[Wharton2025OASIS4]].
- ATTAIN-1 (orforglipron; n=3127; 72 weeks; obesity without diabetes): weight change was −7.5% (6 mg), −8.4% (12 mg) and −11.2% (36 mg) vs −2.1% with placebo [[Wharton2025ATTAIN1]].
- ATTAIN-1, 36 mg vs placebo: ≥10% loss in 54.6% vs 12.9%; ≥15% in 36.0% vs 5.9%; ≥20% in 18.4% vs 2.8%. Adverse events led 5.3–10.3% to stop orforglipron vs 2.7% on placebo [[Wharton2025ATTAIN1]].
- The FOUNDAYO label doses (0.8 → 2.5 → 5.5 → 9 → 14.5 → 17.2 mg once daily, at least 30 days per step) are "equivalent dosages" of the investigational formulation used in the trials. In the label, the trial arms appear as 5.5, 9 and 17.2 mg [[FDA-Foundayo]].

### glp · Tirzepatide: the SURMOUNT programme
- SURMOUNT-1: 2539 adults with BMI ≥30 (or ≥27 with a complication) and no diabetes were randomised to tirzepatide 5, 10 or 15 mg or placebo for 72 weeks, with a 20-week dose escalation. Mean baseline weight was 104.8 kg and BMI 38.0 [[Jastreboff2022SURMOUNT1]].
- SURMOUNT-1, week 72: weight change was −15.0% (5 mg), −19.5% (10 mg) and −20.9% (15 mg) vs −3.1% with placebo [[Jastreboff2022SURMOUNT1]].
- SURMOUNT-1: ≥5% loss in 85%, 89% and 91% (5/10/15 mg) vs 35% on placebo. ≥20% loss in 50% (10 mg) and 57% (15 mg) vs 3% [[Jastreboff2022SURMOUNT1]].
- SURMOUNT-1: adverse events caused discontinuation in 4.3% (5 mg), 7.1% (10 mg) and 6.2% (15 mg) vs 2.6% on placebo [[Jastreboff2022SURMOUNT1]].
- SURMOUNT-1, 3-year analysis (1032 people with prediabetes, 176 weeks): weight change was −12.3% (5 mg), −18.7% (10 mg) and −19.7% (15 mg) vs −1.3% with placebo. Type 2 diabetes developed in 1.3% vs 13.3% (HR 0.07) [[Jastreboff2025SURMOUNT1]].
- SURMOUNT-1 3-year: 17 weeks after stopping, 2.4% of the tirzepatide groups vs 13.7% of the placebo group had type 2 diabetes (HR 0.12) [[Jastreboff2025SURMOUNT1]].
- SURMOUNT-2 (obesity with type 2 diabetes; n=938; 72 weeks): weight change was −12.8% (10 mg) and −14.7% (15 mg) vs −3.2% with placebo. 79–83% vs 32% reached ≥5% [[Garvey2023SURMOUNT2]].
- SURMOUNT-3: after a 12-week intensive lifestyle programme in which participants lost ≥5.0%, tirzepatide gave a further −18.4% vs +2.5% with placebo over 72 weeks (difference −20.8 points) [[Wadden2023SURMOUNT3]].
- SURMOUNT-5 (open-label head-to-head; adults with obesity without diabetes; n=751; 72 weeks): weight change was −20.2% with tirzepatide (10/15 mg) vs −13.7% with semaglutide (1.7/2.4 mg). Waist change was −18.4 vs −13.0 cm [[Aronne2025SURMOUNT5]].

### glp · Liraglutide 3.0 mg daily: the SCALE trial
- SCALE Obesity and Prediabetes: 3731 adults without diabetes were randomised 2:1 for 56 weeks. Mean loss was 8.4 kg with liraglutide 3.0 mg vs 2.8 kg with placebo (difference −5.6 kg). 63.2% vs 27.1% lost ≥5%, and 33.1% vs 10.6% lost >10% [[PiSunyer2015SCALE]].
- The Saxenda label reports the same study (Study 1) as −7.4% vs −3.0% (difference −4.5 points; 95% CI −5.2 to −3.8). 62.3% vs 34.4% lost ≥5% [[FDA-Saxenda]].
- SCALE 3-year arm (people with prediabetes; 160 weeks): diabetes was diagnosed on treatment in 2% vs 6% (HR 0.21; 95% CI 0.13–0.34). Weight change was −6.1% vs −1.9%. About half of participants withdrew (47% liraglutide, 55% placebo) [[leRoux2017SCALE]].

### medtrials · Stopping treatment: withdrawal trials
- STEP 4 design: 902 adults took semaglutide in a 20-week run-in and lost a mean 10.6%. 803 who reached 2.4 mg were randomised 2:1 to continue semaglutide or switch to placebo for 48 weeks [[Rubino2021STEP4]].
- STEP 4 result, week 20 to week 68: weight changed −7.9% with continued semaglutide vs +6.9% after switching to placebo (difference −14.8 points; 95% CI −16.0 to −13.5) [[Rubino2021STEP4]].
- STEP 1 extension (327 people; all treatment, including lifestyle support, stopped at week 68): the semaglutide group had lost 17.3% by week 68 and regained 11.6 percentage points by week 120, leaving a net loss of 5.6%. The placebo group's net change was −0.1% [[Wilding2022STEP1ext]].
- STEP 1 extension: one year off treatment, people "regained two-thirds of their prior weight loss", and most cardiometabolic gains reverted towards baseline. The authors conclude this confirms the chronicity of obesity [[Wilding2022STEP1ext]].
- SURMOUNT-4: in a 36-week open-label lead-in on maximum tolerated tirzepatide, participants lost a mean 20.9%. 670 were then randomised to continue or switch to placebo [[Aronne2024SURMOUNT4]].
- SURMOUNT-4, week 36 to 88: weight changed −5.5% with continued tirzepatide vs +14.0% with placebo (difference −19.4%). Total change from week 0 to 88 was −25.3% vs −9.9%. 89.5% vs 16.6% kept at least 80% of their lead-in loss [[Aronne2024SURMOUNT4]].
- Meta-analysis of 37 studies (9341 participants): after any weight management medication stopped, weight was regained at an average 0.4 kg per month (95% CI 0.3–0.5). Cardiometabolic markers were projected to return to baseline within 1.4 years. Regain was faster than after behavioural programmes by 0.3 kg per month [[West2026BMJ]].
- Meta-analysis of 18 RCTs: in people with obesity, stopping a GLP-1 RA was followed by 5.63 kg weight gain (95% CI 3.52–7.73) and an HbA1c rise of 0.25%. Regain was greater with longer follow-up (>26 weeks: 7.31 vs 2.51 kg) and after semaglutide than liraglutide (8.21 vs 4.29 kg) (Established) [[Tzang2025Rebound]].

### medtrials · Outcome trials beyond weight
- SELECT: 17,604 adults aged ≥45 with established cardiovascular disease, BMI ≥27 and no diabetes. The primary outcome (cardiovascular death, non-fatal MI or non-fatal stroke) occurred in 6.5% on semaglutide 2.4 mg vs 8.0% on placebo over a mean 39.8 months (HR 0.80; 95% CI 0.72–0.90) [[Lincoff2023SELECT]].
- SELECT: adverse events led to permanent discontinuation in 16.6% on semaglutide vs 8.2% on placebo [[Lincoff2023SELECT]].
- SELECT weight analysis: at 208 weeks (4 years), weight change was −10.2% vs −1.5%, and waist change −7.7 vs −1.3 cm. Weight loss continued over 65 weeks and was sustained for up to 4 years [[Ryan2024SELECT]].
- SELECT weight analysis: serious adverse event rates were lower on semaglutide in every BMI category. For BMI ≥40, the rates were 47.06 vs 60.85 events per 100 years of observation [[Ryan2024SELECT]].
- STEP-HFpEF (heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and BMI ≥30; n=529; 52 weeks): KCCQ symptom score rose 16.6 vs 8.7 points (difference 7.8). Weight change was −13.3% vs −2.6%, and 6-minute walk distance improved 21.5 vs 1.2 m [[Kosiborod2023STEPHFpEF]].
- STEP-HFpEF: CRP fell 43.5% vs 7.3%. Serious adverse events occurred in 13.3% on semaglutide vs 26.7% on placebo [[Kosiborod2023STEPHFpEF]].
- SUMMIT (tirzepatide; HFpEF with EF ≥50% and BMI ≥30; n=731; median follow-up 104 weeks): cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure occurred in 9.9% vs 15.3% (HR 0.62; 95% CI 0.41–0.95) [[Packer2025SUMMIT]].
- SUMMIT: worsening heart-failure events occurred in 8.0% vs 14.2% (HR 0.54). Cardiovascular death was 2.2% vs 1.4% (HR 1.58; 95% CI 0.52–4.83; too few events to interpret). KCCQ rose 19.5 vs 12.7 points [[Packer2025SUMMIT]].
- SURMOUNT-OSA (moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnoea with obesity; 52 weeks): baseline AHI was about 50 events/hour. Without PAP, AHI fell by 25.3 vs 5.3 events/hour (difference −20.0). On PAP, it fell by 29.3 vs 5.5 events/hour (difference −23.8) [[Malhotra2024SURMOUNTOSA]].
- XENDOS (orlistat 120 mg three times daily vs placebo, both with lifestyle change, 4 years; n=3305): cumulative diabetes incidence was 6.2% vs 9.0%, a 37.3% risk reduction. The effect was confined to people with impaired glucose tolerance [[Torgerson2004XENDOS]].
- XENDOS: weight loss at 4 years was 5.8 vs 3.0 kg. Only 52% on orlistat and 34% on placebo completed treatment [[Torgerson2004XENDOS]].

### othermeds · Phentermine–topiramate, naltrexone–bupropion, orlistat
- Phentermine is a sympathomimetic amine whose weight effect is likely mediated by catecholamine release in the hypothalamus, reducing appetite. The label says topiramate's weight mechanism is not known [[FDA-Qsymia]].
- CONQUER (phentermine–topiramate ER; adults with BMI 27–45 and ≥2 comorbidities; n=2487; 56 weeks): weight change was −1.2% (placebo), −7.8% (7.5/46 mg) and −9.8% (15/92 mg) [[Gadde2011CONQUER]].
- CONQUER: ≥5% loss in 21%, 62% and 70%, and ≥10% loss in 7%, 37% and 48% (placebo, 7.5/46 mg, 15/92 mg) [[Gadde2011CONQUER]].
- CONQUER adverse events (placebo / 7.5/46 / 15/92 mg): dry mouth 2/13/21%; paraesthesia 2/14/21%; constipation 6/15/17%; dysgeusia (altered taste) 1/7/10%. Depression-related events 4/4/7%; anxiety-related 3/5/8% [[Gadde2011CONQUER]].
- Naltrexone–bupropion: naltrexone is an opioid antagonist and bupropion a weak dopamine and noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor. Nonclinical studies suggest effects on the hypothalamus (appetite) and the mesolimbic dopamine reward circuit [[FDA-Contrave]].
- COR-I (naltrexone 32 mg + bupropion 360 mg daily; n=1742; 56 weeks): weight change was −6.1% vs −1.3% with placebo. 48% vs 16% reached ≥5% loss. Only 50% completed 56 weeks [[Greenway2010CORI]].
- COR-I: nausea occurred in 29.8% vs 5.3%. Blood pressure rose about 1.5 mm Hg at first and later fell about 1 mm Hg below baseline [[Greenway2010CORI]].
- LIGHT (naltrexone–bupropion cardiovascular outcomes; n=8910): the trial was stopped early after confidential interim data were released publicly. At 50% of events, MACE was 2.0% vs 2.3% (HR 0.88), and cardiovascular safety "remains uncertain" [[Nissen2016LIGHT]].
- Orlistat reversibly inhibits gastric and pancreatic lipases in the gut lumen, so undigested dietary triglyceride is not absorbed [[FDA-Xenical]].
- AGA 2022 suggested semaglutide 2.4 mg, liraglutide 3.0 mg, phentermine–topiramate ER and naltrexone–bupropion ER (moderate-certainty evidence), and phentermine and diethylpropion (low certainty), for long-term management. It suggested **against** orlistat [[Grunvald2022AGA]].
- AGA 2022 strongly recommended adding pharmacotherapy to lifestyle intervention for adults with BMI ≥30, or ≥27 with weight-related complications, whose response to lifestyle intervention alone is inadequate [[Grunvald2022AGA]].
- Endocrine Society 2015: medicines approved for chronic weight management are useful adjuncts to lifestyle change. Many medicines for diabetes, depression and other chronic diseases can cause weight gain or loss, so choosing weight-favourable options can help [[Apovian2015EndoSoc]].

### othermeds · Rare genetic and hypothalamic obesity: setmelanotide
- Setmelanotide is an MC4 receptor agonist with 20-fold less activity at MC3 and MC1 receptors. It may restore signalling in the leptin–melanocortin pathway when that pathway is under-activated [[FDA-Imcivree]].
- Imcivree indications (as published): age ≥4 years with acquired hypothalamic obesity, and age ≥2 years with Bardet-Biedl syndrome or POMC, PCSK1 or LEPR deficiency confirmed by genetic testing. It is not a treatment for common obesity [[FDA-Imcivree]].
- POMC and LEPR deficiency phase 3 trials (open-label): ≥10% weight loss at about 1 year in 8/10 (80%) with POMC deficiency and 5/11 (45%) with LEPR deficiency. "Most hunger" scores fell by 27.1% and 43.7% [[Clement2020Setmelanotide]].
- Bardet-Biedl syndrome (randomised phase 3): 32.3% of patients aged ≥12 reached ≥10% weight reduction at 52 weeks. Skin hyperpigmentation occurred in 61% [[Haqq2022Setmelanotide]].
- TRANSCEND (acquired hypothalamic obesity; ages 4–66; n=120): BMI change at 52 weeks was −16.5% vs +3.3% with placebo [[Miller2026TRANSCEND]].
- Imcivree label, Bardet-Biedl syndrome trial (14-week placebo-controlled period): hyperpigmentation disorders 67% vs 0%, and vomiting 11% vs 0%. The label also warns about depression and suicidal ideation, sexual arousal disturbances and darkening of naevi (moles) [[FDA-Imcivree]].

### othermeds · Drugs that failed safety tests (history)
- SCOUT (sibutramine; n=9804 at high cardiovascular risk; mean 3.4 years): the primary outcome occurred in 11.4% vs 10.0% (HR 1.16; 95% CI 1.03–1.31), driven by non-fatal MI (HR 1.28) and non-fatal stroke (HR 1.36). Extra weight loss after randomisation averaged only 1.7 kg [[James2010SCOUT]].
- CAMELLIA-TIMI 61 (lorcaserin, a serotonin 2C agonist; n=12,000): major cardiovascular events were 2.0% vs 2.1% per year (HR 0.99), so lorcaserin was non-inferior on cardiovascular safety. 38.7% vs 17.4% lost ≥5% at 1 year [[Bohula2018CAMELLIA]].

### glp · US labels: indications as published
- Wegovy injection: indicated to reduce major adverse CV events in adults with established CV disease and obesity or overweight; for long-term weight reduction in adults and children ≥12 with obesity, and adults with overweight plus ≥1 weight-related condition; and (accelerated approval) for noncirrhotic MASH with F2–F3 fibrosis [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Wegovy tablets: indicated for CV risk reduction and weight reduction in adults (not children) [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Zepbound: indicated for long-term weight reduction in adults with obesity, or overweight plus ≥1 weight-related condition, and for moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnoea in adults with obesity [[FDA-Zepbound]].
- Saxenda: indicated for adults, and for children aged ≥12 with body weight >60 kg and obesity [[FDA-Saxenda]].
- Qsymia and Xenical: indicated for adults and children ≥12 with obesity, and for adults with overweight plus a weight-related condition. Contrave and Foundayo: adults only [[FDA-Qsymia,FDA-Xenical,FDA-Contrave,FDA-Foundayo]].
- Alli (orlistat 60 mg, over the counter): for weight loss in overweight adults aged 18 and over, alongside a reduced-calorie, low-fat diet [[FDA-Alli]].
- All the incretin labels say not to combine them with another GLP-1 receptor agonist or another product containing the same drug [[FDA-Wegovy,FDA-Zepbound,FDA-Saxenda,FDA-Foundayo]].

### glp · Dose escalation as published in the labels (quoted, not instructions)
- Wegovy injection: 0.25 mg weekly for weeks 1–4, 0.5 mg weeks 5–8, 1 mg weeks 9–12, 1.7 mg weeks 13–16, then maintenance from week 17. For adult weight reduction, maintenance is 1.7 mg or 2.4 mg (recommended), with an optional increase to a maximum of 7.2 mg after at least 4 weeks on 2.4 mg [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Wegovy tablets: 1.5 mg daily for days 1–30, 4 mg days 31–60, 9 mg days 61–90, then 25 mg daily maintenance [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Wegovy: if 2 or more consecutive weekly doses are missed, the label says to restart escalation at a lower dose to reduce GI adverse reactions [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Zepbound: start at 2.5 mg weekly for 4 weeks (a starting dose, not a maintenance dose), then rise in 2.5 mg steps at least 4 weeks apart. Maintenance is 5, 10 or 15 mg (10 or 15 mg for OSA), and the maximum is 15 mg [[FDA-Zepbound]].
- Saxenda: start at 0.6 mg daily and increase weekly until 3 mg daily. The label says to stop if an adult has not lost at least 4% of baseline weight after 16 weeks [[FDA-Saxenda]].
- Qsymia: 3.75/23 mg for 14 days, then 7.5/46 mg. At 12 weeks, if an adult has lost less than 3%, the dose goes up to 11.25/69 mg and then 15/92 mg. If less than 5% is lost after 12 weeks on 15/92 mg, the label says to stop. The 15/92 mg dose is tapered over at least 1 week because of seizure risk [[FDA-Qsymia]].
- Contrave: 1 tablet (8/90 mg) in week 1, rising to 2 tablets twice daily from week 4 (32 mg naltrexone / 360 mg bupropion per day). The label says to stop if less than 5% is lost after 12 weeks at maintenance [[FDA-Contrave]].
- Xenical: 120 mg three times daily with each main meal containing fat, plus a daily multivitamin taken at least 2 hours apart from the dose. Alli: 60 mg per capsule, at most 3 a day [[FDA-Xenical,FDA-Alli]].
- Foundayo: 0.8 mg daily, then 2.5 mg and 5.5 mg, each after at least 30 days, and optionally 9, 14.5 and 17.2 mg (maximum) [[FDA-Foundayo]].

### sideeffects · Boxed warnings and contraindications
- Wegovy, Zepbound and Saxenda carry a boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumours: semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide caused them in rodents, and human relevance "has not been determined". All are contraindicated with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or MEN 2 [[FDA-Wegovy,FDA-Zepbound,FDA-Saxenda]].
- The labels say routine calcitonin or thyroid ultrasound monitoring is "of uncertain value" for early detection of MTC. Patients are counselled about a neck mass, trouble swallowing, shortness of breath or persistent hoarseness [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Foundayo carries the same class boxed warning and contraindications. Its label notes that orforglipron is not pharmacologically active in rats or mice and did not produce tumours there [[FDA-Foundayo]].
- Contrave carries a boxed warning on suicidal thoughts and behaviours because it contains bupropion. It is not approved for use in children [[FDA-Contrave]].
- Contrave contraindications include uncontrolled hypertension; seizure disorder; bulimia or anorexia nervosa; chronic opioid use or acute opiate withdrawal; abrupt stopping of alcohol, benzodiazepines, barbiturates or antiepileptics; and MAOI use within 14 days [[FDA-Contrave]].
- Qsymia contraindications: pregnancy, glaucoma, hyperthyroidism, and MAOI use within 14 days [[FDA-Qsymia]].
- Xenical contraindications: chronic malabsorption syndrome and cholestasis [[FDA-Xenical]].
- In 02/2026, the Suicidal Behavior and Ideation warning was removed from the Wegovy, Saxenda and Zepbound labels [[FDA-Wegovy,FDA-Saxenda,FDA-Zepbound]].

### sideeffects · Gastrointestinal adverse events with rates
- Wegovy 2.4 mg (pooled adult weight trials, n=2116 vs 1261 placebo): nausea 44% vs 16%; diarrhoea 30% vs 16%; vomiting 24% vs 6%; constipation 24% vs 11%; abdominal pain 20% vs 10%; hair loss 3% vs 1% [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Wegovy 2.4 mg: 6.8% vs 3.2% stopped because of adverse reactions, most often nausea (1.8% vs 0.2%) and vomiting (1.2% vs 0%) [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- STEP 1–3 pooled: 99.5% of GI adverse events on semaglutide were non-serious and 98.1% were mild to moderate. They were most frequent during or shortly after dose escalation, and 4.3% stopped because of them [[Wharton2022GItolerability]].
- Zepbound (Studies 1–2; placebo / 5 / 10 / 15 mg): nausea 8/25/29/28%; diarrhoea 8/19/21/23%; vomiting 2/8/11/13%; constipation 5/17/14/11%; hair loss 1/5/4/5% [[FDA-Zepbound]].
- Saxenda (n=3384 vs 1941): nausea 39.3% vs 13.8%; diarrhoea 20.9% vs 9.9%; constipation 19.4% vs 8.5%; vomiting 15.7% vs 3.9% [[FDA-Saxenda]].
- Qsymia (placebo / 3.75/23 / 7.5/46 / 15/92 mg): paraesthesia 2/4/14/20%; dry mouth 3/7/14/19%; constipation 6/8/15/16%; dysgeusia 1/1/7/9% [[FDA-Qsymia]].
- Wegovy label warning: GI adverse reactions can be severe, and Wegovy is not recommended in severe gastroparesis. The label also warns of acute kidney injury from fluid loss (volume depletion) [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Meta-analysis of 21 cardiovascular-outcome trials (99,599 patients): GLP-1 RAs increased GI disorders by 63% and gallbladder disorders by 26%, with no difference in pancreatitis or neoplasms (cancers) [[Galli2025]].

### sideeffects · Gallbladder, pancreas, heart rate and low blood sugar
- Wegovy, adults in weight trials: cholelithiasis 1.6% (injection) vs 0.7% (placebo), and 2.5% (tablets) vs 1%. Cholecystitis 0.6% vs 0.2% [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Wegovy, adolescents: cholelithiasis 3.8% vs 0%. The label says acute gallbladder disease was more common than placebo even after accounting for the degree of weight loss [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Meta-analysis of 76 RCTs (103,371 patients): GLP-1 RA use was linked to more gallbladder or biliary disease (RR 1.37; 95% CI 1.23–1.52), and more so in weight-loss trials (RR 2.29), at higher doses and with longer use [[He2022Gallbladder]].
- Adjudicated acute pancreatitis: Wegovy 4 cases (0.2 per 100 patient-years) vs 1 on placebo; Saxenda 9/3291 (0.3%). Labels say to stop the drug if pancreatitis is suspected [[FDA-Wegovy,FDA-Saxenda]].
- In the Zepbound OSA studies, adjudicated pancreatitis occurred at 0.84 patients per 100 years vs 0 on placebo [[FDA-Zepbound]].
- Wegovy heart rate: mean resting heart rate rose 1–4 bpm vs placebo. A peak rise of ≥20 bpm at any visit occurred in 26% vs 16% of adults, and 54% vs 39% of adolescents [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Hypoglycaemia (blood glucose <54 mg/dL) in people with type 2 diabetes not on insulin: 6% on Wegovy vs 2% on placebo. Combining with insulin or a sulfonylurea raises the risk, and the label says those doses may need reducing [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Contrave can raise blood pressure or heart rate, especially in the first 3 months [[FDA-Contrave]].

### sideeffects · Anaesthesia, contraception, eyes, mood, cancer
- Wegovy label: there are rare postmarketing reports of pulmonary aspiration under general anaesthesia or deep sedation in people taking GLP-1 RAs who had food left in the stomach despite fasting. The label says the data are insufficient to recommend holding the drug or changing fasting, and patients should tell their care team before any procedure [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Zepbound can reduce the effect of oral hormonal contraceptives because it slows gastric emptying. The label advises switching to a non-oral method, or adding a barrier method for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose increase [[FDA-Zepbound]].
- NAION (a type of sudden optic-nerve damage): in a single-centre neuro-ophthalmology cohort, semaglutide was linked to higher risk (HR 4.28 with diabetes; HR 7.64 with overweight or obesity). The observational design cannot show causation (Early evidence) [[Hathaway2024NAION]].
- Suicidal ideation: in a 240,618-person electronic health record cohort with overweight or obesity, semaglutide was linked to **lower** risk of new (HR 0.27) and recurrent (HR 0.44; 95% CI 0.32–0.60) suicidal ideation than other anti-obesity medicines (Early evidence) [[Wang2024Suicidal]].
- Thyroid cancer: in a Scandinavian cohort of 145,410 GLP-1 RA users vs 291,667 DPP-4 inhibitor users (mean follow-up 3.9 years), the HR was 0.93 (95% CI 0.66–1.31). For medullary thyroid cancer it was 1.19 (0.37–3.86) [[Pasternak2024Thyroid]].
- Meta-analysis of 48 RCTs (94,245 participants): GLP-1 RAs "probably have little or no effect" on thyroid (OR 1.37; 95% CI 0.82–2.31), pancreatic (OR 0.84) or breast cancer (moderate certainty). The trials were short and not designed for cancer outcomes [[Ko2026Cancer]].

### sideeffects · Lean mass and muscle
- SURMOUNT-1 DXA substudy (n=160): weight changed −21.3%, fat mass −33.9% and lean mass −10.9% with tirzepatide. About 75% of the weight lost was fat and 25% was lean mass, the same proportions as with placebo [[Look2025SURMOUNT1DXA]].
- STEP 1 DXA substudy, as summarised in a 2026 review: total fat mass fell 19.3%, visceral fat 27.4% and lean body mass 9.7%. The lean share of total body mass rose by about 3% [[DeGirolamo2026]].
- A 2025 expert meeting report said STEP 1 showed that "up to 40%" of weight lost could be lean mass, compared with about 25% often seen with caloric restriction [[vonHaehling2025]].
- Across GLP-1-based trials, lean mass made up anywhere from about 15% or less to 40–60% of weight lost. "Lean mass" includes organs, bone and water as well as muscle, and MRI studies suggest muscle changes may be adaptive, with better muscle quality (Early evidence) [[Neeland2024LeanMass]].

### medtrials · Persistence and results in everyday practice
- US commercial and public insurance data (195,915 new users in 2021): GLP-1 RA discontinuation was 26.2% at 3 months, 30.8% at 6 months and 36.5% at 12 months. For people with obesity but no diabetes it was 50.3% at 12 months (Europe PMC full text) [[Do2024Discontinuation]].
- US electronic health records (125,474 adults starting semaglutide, liraglutide or tirzepatide in 2018–2023): 1-year discontinuation was 64.8% without type 2 diabetes vs 46.5% with it. Moderate or severe GI events increased discontinuation (HR 1.19–1.38) [[Rodriguez2025Discontinuation]].
- Same cohort: among those who stopped, 36.3% (no diabetes) and 47.3% (with diabetes) restarted within 1 year. Higher income was linked to staying on treatment, raising equity concerns [[Rodriguez2025Discontinuation]].
- Ohio/Florida health system (7881 adults without diabetes, injectable semaglutide or tirzepatide): mean 1-year weight loss was 8.7% overall. It was 3.6% for those who stopped within 3 months, 6.8% for those who stopped at 3–12 months and 11.9% for those who stayed on. 80.8% were on low maintenance doses [[Gasoyan2025Weight]].
- Reasons for stopping within a year (random sample of 288): cost or insurance 47.6%; side effects 14.6%; shortages 11.8%; switch to a compounded product 2.4%; unsatisfactory weight loss 1.7% [[Gasoyan2025Reasons]].
- Compounded GLP-1 RAs in FAERS (707 of 81,078 reports, 2018–2024), compared with approved products: higher reporting odds of preparation errors (ROR 48.9), contamination (19.0), hospitalisation (2.35) and suicidality (6.34). This is reporting-signal data and does not show cause (Early evidence) [[McCall2026Compounded]].

### pregnancy · Pregnancy, contraception and breastfeeding
- Wegovy: weight loss offers no benefit in pregnancy and may cause fetal harm. The label says to stop Wegovy when pregnancy is recognised, and at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Wegovy: semaglutide in pregnant rats caused embryofetal death and malformations at clinically relevant exposures, and rabbits and monkeys had early pregnancy loss. There is a pregnancy exposure registry [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Wegovy tablets: breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Zepbound: the label advises stopping when pregnancy is recognised. In a single-dose lactation study, tirzepatide in breast milk was undetectable or low [[FDA-Zepbound]].
- Qsymia is contraindicated in pregnancy: first-trimester topiramate exposure raises the risk of major malformations, including oral clefts. A negative pregnancy test is advised before starting and monthly during treatment, and it is dispensed only through a REMS programme of certified pharmacies [[FDA-Qsymia]].
- Contrave, Xenical and Saxenda: the labels say to stop when pregnancy is recognised. The Xenical label notes decades of cohort data without an increased risk of birth defects [[FDA-Contrave,FDA-Xenical,FDA-Saxenda]].
- Alli (over the counter): "If pregnant or breast-feeding, do not use" [[FDA-Alli]].
- Early pregnancy exposure (mothers with type 2 diabetes in Nordic, US and Israeli data; 938 GLP-1 RA-exposed infants): the adjusted RR for major congenital malformations vs insulin was 0.95 (95% CI 0.72–1.26). This is reassuring but imprecise (Early evidence) [[Cesta2024Pregnancy]].

### Exam hooks
- STEP 1: semaglutide 2.4 mg −14.9% vs placebo −2.4% at 68 weeks [[Wilding2021STEP1]].
- SURMOUNT-1: tirzepatide 15 mg −20.9% vs −3.1% at 72 weeks, and 57% lost ≥20% [[Jastreboff2022SURMOUNT1]].
- SURMOUNT-5: tirzepatide −20.2% vs semaglutide −13.7% head-to-head at 72 weeks [[Aronne2025SURMOUNT5]].
- SELECT: semaglutide reduced MACE by 20% (HR 0.80) in people with CVD and BMI ≥27 without diabetes [[Lincoff2023SELECT]].
- STEP 4: after switching to placebo, weight rose 6.9% while those continuing lost a further 7.9% [[Rubino2021STEP4]].
- STEP 1 extension: two-thirds of the weight lost came back within 1 year of stopping [[Wilding2022STEP1ext]].
- SURMOUNT-4: +14.0% regain on placebo vs −5.5% further loss on tirzepatide [[Aronne2024SURMOUNT4]].
- People with type 2 diabetes lose less weight on the same drug: STEP 2 −9.6% vs STEP 1 −14.9% [[Davies2021STEP2,Wilding2021STEP1]].
- Tirzepatide is a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist, and setmelanotide is an MC4 receptor agonist used only for specific genetic or hypothalamic obesity [[FDA-Zepbound,FDA-Imcivree]].
- Thyroid C-cell tumour boxed warning: incretin drugs are contraindicated with a personal or family history of MTC or MEN 2 [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Contrave's boxed warning is about suicidality, from its bupropion component [[FDA-Contrave]].
- Qsymia is teratogenic (oral clefts), so it needs a REMS programme and monthly pregnancy tests [[FDA-Qsymia]].
- The Wegovy label says to stop at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Zepbound can reduce oral contraceptive effect, so a backup method is advised for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose increase [[FDA-Zepbound]].
- Orlistat (XENDOS) reduced diabetes incidence by 37.3% over 4 years, with the effect confined to impaired glucose tolerance [[Torgerson2004XENDOS]].
- Stopping rules: Saxenda <4% at 16 weeks; Contrave <5% at 12 weeks; Qsymia <5% at 12 weeks on the top dose [[FDA-Saxenda,FDA-Contrave,FDA-Qsymia]].
- Sibutramine increased non-fatal MI and stroke in SCOUT (HR 1.16 for the primary outcome) [[James2010SCOUT]].
- GLP-1 RAs raise the risk of gallbladder disease, especially at weight-loss doses (RR 2.29 in weight-loss trials) [[He2022Gallbladder]].

### In plain words
- These medicines act on the brain's appetite system so that people feel full sooner and think about food less. They are not a test of willpower [[FDA-Wegovy,FDA-Zepbound]].
- In the big trials, people on semaglutide lost about 15% of their weight on average, and on tirzepatide about 20%. Some people lose more, some less, and averages cannot predict one person's result [[Wilding2021STEP1,Jastreboff2022SURMOUNT1]].
- Obesity is a long-term condition. When the medicine stops, the body tends to regain weight. This is biology, not failure [[Wilding2022STEP1ext,Aronne2024SURMOUNT4]].
- Feeling sick, loose stools or constipation are common, mostly early on and when the dose goes up, and they usually settle [[Wharton2022GItolerability]].
- Severe stomach pain that does not go away, sometimes spreading to the back, needs urgent medical review [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- If you are having surgery or a scope, tell the team you take one of these medicines [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- If you could become pregnant, talk with your clinician about birth control and about planning a pregnancy before starting [[FDA-Wegovy,FDA-Zepbound,FDA-Qsymia]].
- Some of the weight lost is muscle and other lean tissue, so ways to protect muscle are worth discussing with a clinician [[Neeland2024LeanMass]].
- Never start, stop or change a dose without your clinician. Products sold online or "compounded" are not the same as FDA-approved medicines [[McCall2026Compounded]].
- If eating or weight is causing you distress, or you restrict, binge or purge, please talk to a clinician or an eating-disorder service. Contrave must not be used with bulimia or anorexia nervosa [[FDA-Contrave]].
- Cost is the most common reason people stop. Ask your care team about coverage before starting [[Gasoyan2025Reasons]].
- Weight-loss medicine can also help the heart, sleep apnoea and knee pain in some people [[Lincoff2023SELECT,Malhotra2024SURMOUNTOSA,Bliddal2024STEP9]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Should treatment be lifelong? Withdrawal trials show substantial regain within a year [[Rubino2021STEP4,Aronne2024SURMOUNT4,Wilding2022STEP1ext]], and a meta-analysis projects cardiometabolic markers returning to baseline within 1.4 years of stopping [[West2026BMJ]]. Dose-reduction or intermittent strategies are untested in published phase 3 trials. Status: open.
- Lean mass: some trials report lean mass as 40–60% of weight lost and others ≤15% [[Neeland2024LeanMass]]; the SURMOUNT-1 DXA substudy found about 25% [[Look2025SURMOUNT1DXA]]. Whether this matters clinically, especially in older adults, is debated, and MRI data suggest muscle quality may improve [[Neeland2024LeanMass,vonHaehling2025]]. Status: early evidence.
- NAION: a single-centre cohort found HRs of 4.28–7.64 [[Hathaway2024NAION]]. The design is observational and confounding is possible. Status: early evidence, unresolved.
- Suicidality: the Wegovy, Saxenda and Zepbound labels dropped the warning in 02/2026 [[FDA-Wegovy,FDA-Zepbound]], and a large EHR cohort found lower risk [[Wang2024Suicidal]]. Contrave keeps a bupropion boxed warning [[FDA-Contrave]], and the Imcivree and Qsymia labels keep depression/suicidal-ideation warnings [[FDA-Imcivree,FDA-Qsymia]]. Status: labels differ by molecule.
- Thyroid cancer: rodent C-cell tumours drive the boxed warning [[FDA-Wegovy]]. Human cohort and RCT meta-analysis data do not show a substantial increase [[Pasternak2024Thyroid,Ko2026Cancer]], but follow-up is short. Status: the warning stands, and the human risk appears small or absent.
- Orlistat: XENDOS showed diabetes prevention [[Torgerson2004XENDOS]], yet AGA 2022 suggested against it because of its smaller benefit and GI effects [[Grunvald2022AGA]]. Status: it is still labelled and available over the counter.
- Naltrexone–bupropion cardiovascular safety: LIGHT was stopped early after interim data were released, and safety "remains uncertain" [[Nissen2016LIGHT]]. Status: unresolved.
- Trials vs practice: real-world 1-year loss averages 8.7%, below the trials, because many people stop and many stay on lower doses [[Gasoyan2025Weight]]. Cost is the main reason for stopping [[Gasoyan2025Reasons]], and access differs by income [[Rodriguez2025Discontinuation]]. Status: an equity and policy question.
- Compounded products: FAERS reporting signals for contamination and preparation errors [[McCall2026Compounded]] cannot show cause, and there are no RCTs. Status: early evidence.
- Perioperative aspiration: the label says the data are insufficient to recommend holding the drug or changing fasting [[FDA-Wegovy]]. Society guidance exists but could not be read for this pass. Status: open.
