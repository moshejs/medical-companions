# D · Complications, comorbidities & clinical assessment

Pass date: 2026-11-11. Modules served: complications, diabetes, assessment, measuring.

This pass covers the diseases that travel with obesity (with relative risks by BMI category or per 5 kg/m² where the source gives them), what modest weight loss changes in those diseases, the diabetes-prevention and diabetes-remission trials, and the tools used to assess a person living with obesity in clinic: history, medication review, examination, laboratory tests, sleep apnoea screening and staging systems. Every relative risk and percentage is a population statistic from the study named: it describes groups, not what will happen to any one person. BMI, waist, STOP-Bang and staging tools below are teaching tools with population caveats, never a diagnosis or a target on their own.

## Findings

### complications · The big picture: mortality and global burden
- In 900 000 adults in 57 prospective studies (Prospective Studies Collaboration), all-cause mortality was lowest at a BMI of about 22.5–25 kg/m². Above that range, each 5 kg/m² higher BMI meant about 30% higher overall mortality (HR 1.29, 95% CI 1.27–1.32) [[PSC2009]].
- In the same analysis, each 5 kg/m² meant about 40% higher vascular mortality (HR 1.41) and 60–120% higher diabetic, renal and hepatic mortality (HRs 2.16, 1.59 and 1.82). Neoplastic mortality was about 10% higher (HR 1.10) [[PSC2009]].
- Prospective Studies Collaboration estimate: median survival is reduced by 2–4 years at BMI 30–35 kg/m² and by 8–10 years at 40–45 kg/m², "comparable with the effects of smoking". These are population averages [[PSC2009]].
- In 3.95 million never-smokers without chronic disease at baseline (239 studies, four continents), the all-cause mortality HR versus BMI 22.5–<25 was 1.45 for BMI 30–<35, 1.94 for 35–<40 and 2.76 for 40–<60 kg/m² [[GBMC2016]].
- The excess risk per 5 kg/m² above 25 was greater in younger people: HR 1.52 when BMI was measured at age 35–49 versus 1.21 at age 70–89. It was also greater in men (1.51) than in women (1.30) [[GBMC2016]].
- In 2015, high BMI accounted for an estimated 4.0 million deaths worldwide. Nearly 40% of these deaths were in people whose BMI was below the obesity range, and more than two-thirds were due to cardiovascular disease (GBD modelled estimates) [[GBD2015Obesity2017]].
- An AHA scientific statement says obesity contributes directly to dyslipidaemia, type 2 diabetes, hypertension and sleep disorders. It also leads to cardiovascular disease and cardiovascular death independently of other risk factors. Waist circumference is a CVD risk marker independent of BMI [[PowellWiley2021]].
- Moderate adult weight gain (2.5 to <10 kg from age 18/21 to 55) was associated with higher type 2 diabetes incidence than stable weight: 207 vs 110 per 100 000 person-years in women and 258 vs 147 in men (Nurses' Health Study and Health Professionals Follow-up Study) [[Zheng2017]].

### complications · Relative risks for 18 comorbidities (Guh 2009 meta-analysis)
- Guh 2009 pooled 89 prospective cohort studies and found significant associations between obesity (BMI ≥30) and incident type 2 diabetes, most cancers, all cardiovascular diseases, asthma, gallbladder disease, osteoarthritis and chronic back pain. The comparator was normal BMI [[Guh2009]].
- Type 2 diabetes was the strongest association. Pooled incidence rate ratios (IRR) were 2.40 (overweight) and 6.74 (obesity) in men, and 3.92 and 12.41 (95% CI 9.03–17.06) in women [[Guh2009]].
- Hypertension (BMI): IRR 1.28 (overweight) and 1.84 (obesity) in men; 1.65 and 2.42 in women (full text, Table 2) [[Guh2009]].
- Coronary artery disease (BMI): RR 1.29 and 1.72 in men; 1.80 and 3.10 in women (full text) [[Guh2009]].
- Congestive heart failure (BMI, obesity): 1.79 in men and 1.78 in women. Stroke (obesity): 1.51 in men and 1.49 in women (full text) [[Guh2009]].
- Pulmonary embolism, from a single study: RR 1.91 (overweight) and 3.51 (obesity) (full text) [[Guh2009]].
- Asthma (obesity): 1.43 in men and 1.78 in women. Gallbladder disease (obesity, BMI): 1.43 in men and 2.32 in women (full text) [[Guh2009]].
- Osteoarthritis, defined as joint replacement (obesity): 4.20 in men and 1.96 in women. Chronic back pain (early retirement due to back pain): 2.81 (full text) [[Guh2009]].
- Cancers (obesity vs normal BMI): endometrial 3.22, kidney 2.64 (women) and 1.82 (men), colorectal 1.95 (men) and 1.66 (women), pancreatic 2.29 (men) and 1.60 (women), postmenopausal breast 1.13, ovarian 1.28 (full text) [[Guh2009]].

### diabetes · Obesity and the risk of type 2 diabetes
- In 51 529 US male health professionals, men with BMI ≥35 had a multivariate RR of type 2 diabetes of 42.1 (95% CI 22.0–80.6) compared with BMI <23.0. Even men of average relative weight had significantly raised risk [[Chan1994]].
- In 114 281 US female nurses, BMI was the dominant predictor of diabetes risk. Risk was raised even at an average BMI of 24.0 kg/m² [[Colditz1995]].
- In that cohort, compared with stable weight since age 18, a gain of 5.0–7.9 kg gave RR 1.9 (1.5–2.3) and a gain of 8.0–10.9 kg gave RR 2.7 (2.1–3.3). Women who lost more than 5.0 kg cut their diabetes risk by 50% or more [[Colditz1995]].
- Mendelian randomisation studies (5 studies, 881 692 participants) support a causal role of obesity in type 2 diabetes (OR 1.67, 1.30–2.14) and coronary artery disease (OR 1.20, 1.02–1.41). They found no association with stroke (OR 1.02) [[Riaz2018]].
- Each 5 kg/m² higher BMI was associated with roughly 2.2 times higher diabetic mortality (HR 2.16, 1.89–2.46) [[PSC2009]].

### diabetes · Preventing type 2 diabetes: DPP, DPS and DPPOS
- DPP randomised 3234 adults with raised fasting and post-load glucose (mean BMI 34.0) to placebo, metformin 850 mg twice daily, or lifestyle with goals of at least 7% weight loss and at least 150 minutes of activity a week. Mean follow-up was 2.8 years [[Knowler2002DPP]].
- Diabetes incidence was 11.0 (placebo), 7.8 (metformin) and 4.8 (lifestyle) cases per 100 person-years. Lifestyle cut incidence by 58% (95% CI 48–66) and metformin by 31% (17–43) versus placebo [[Knowler2002DPP]].
- DPP: 6.9 people would need the lifestyle programme, and 13.9 would need metformin, to prevent one case of diabetes over three years [[Knowler2002DPP]].
- In the DPP lifestyle arm (n=1079), weight loss was the dominant predictor of lower diabetes incidence. HR per 5 kg lost was 0.42 (0.35–0.51), and every kilogram lost meant a 16% lower risk after adjustment for diet and activity changes [[Hamman2006]].
- Among lifestyle participants who missed the year-1 weight-loss goal, those who met the activity goal still had 44% lower diabetes incidence [[Hamman2006]].
- In the 10-year DPP Outcomes Study, cumulative diabetes incidence was 34% lower with lifestyle and 18% lower with metformin than placebo. The original lifestyle group lost weight, then partly regained it [[DPPRG2009]].
- After the trial phase, incidence rates converged at 5.9 (lifestyle), 4.9 (metformin) and 5.6 (placebo) per 100 person-years, because all groups were then offered group lifestyle intervention [[DPPRG2009]].
- Over about 21 years, diabetes incidence stayed lower with lifestyle (HR 0.76, 0.68–0.85) and metformin (HR 0.83, 0.74–0.93). Median diabetes-free survival rose by 3.5 and 2.5 years respectively [[Knowler2025DPPOS]].
- Finnish Diabetes Prevention Study (522 adults with impaired glucose tolerance, mean BMI 31): 4-year cumulative incidence of diabetes was 11% with lifestyle counselling versus 23% in controls, a 58% risk reduction [[Tuomilehto2001]].
- USPSTF 2018 (B recommendation): clinicians should offer or refer adults with BMI ≥30 to intensive, multicomponent behavioural interventions. It found these interventions lower diabetes incidence in adults with obesity and raised glucose, with harms "small to none" [[Curry2018USPSTF]].

### diabetes · Remission of type 2 diabetes: DiRECT
- DiRECT enrolled 306 people aged 20–65 with type 2 diabetes of less than 6 years' duration and BMI 27–45, not on insulin. The intervention withdrew diabetes and blood-pressure drugs, then used total diet replacement (825–853 kcal/day formula for 3–5 months), stepped food reintroduction and structured maintenance support. These are the trial's published methods, not instructions [[Lean2018DiRECT]].
- Remission was defined as HbA1c <6.5% (<48 mmol/mol) after at least 2 months off all glucose-lowering drugs [[Lean2018DiRECT]].
- At 12 months, remission occurred in 46% of the intervention group (68/149) versus 4% of controls (6/149) (OR 19.7). Mean weight change was −10.0 kg versus −1.0 kg [[Lean2018DiRECT]].
- Remission at 12 months by weight change across the whole study: 0% of 76 who gained weight, 7% with 0–5 kg loss, 34% with 5–10 kg, 57% with 10–15 kg and 86% (31/36) with ≥15 kg [[Lean2018DiRECT]].
- At 24 months, remission was 36% (53/149) in the intervention group versus 3% (5/149) in controls (adjusted OR 25.82) [[Lean2019DiRECT]].
- In a post hoc analysis at 24 months, 64% (29/45) of participants in the whole study who maintained at least 10 kg loss were in remission [[Lean2019DiRECT]].
- The 5-year paper restates the 2-year figure differently: of 36 intervention participants who kept off more than 10 kg at 2 years, 29 (81%) were in remission. Both figures are as published [[Lean2024DiRECT]].
- At 5 years, 13% (11/85) of extension-group participants were in remission. Of those in remission at year 2, 26% were still in remission at 5 years. This is consistent with obesity and type 2 diabetes being chronic, relapsing conditions [[Lean2024DiRECT]].
- Over 5 years, the original intervention group had fewer serious adverse events than controls (4.8 vs 10.2 per 100 patient-years) [[Lean2024DiRECT]].

### complications · Hypertension, dyslipidaemia and the benefit of modest weight loss
- Framingham, up to 44 years of follow-up: age-adjusted RR for new hypertension with overweight was 1.46 in men and 1.75 in women. Excess weight (BMI ≥25) accounted for 26% (men) and 28% (women) of new hypertension [[Wilson2002Framingham]].
- Framingham: the RR for cardiovascular disease with obesity was 1.46 in men and 1.64 in women. New hypercholesterolaemia and diabetes were less strongly associated with excess adiposity than hypertension in that cohort [[Wilson2002Framingham]].
- Meta-analysis of 25 RCTs (4874 participants): a net weight reduction of 5.1 kg lowered systolic BP by 4.44 mmHg and diastolic BP by 3.57 mmHg. Per kilogram lost, that is about 1.05 mmHg systolic and 0.92 mmHg diastolic [[Neter2003]].
- In Look AHEAD (5145 adults with type 2 diabetes), losing 5 to <10% of body weight at 1 year raised the odds of a 0.5-point HbA1c fall (OR 3.52), a 5 mmHg systolic fall (OR 1.56), a 5 mg/dL HDL rise (OR 1.69) and a 40 mg/dL triglyceride fall (OR 2.20), compared with stable weight [[Wing2011DiabetesCare]].
- Weight loss in Look AHEAD was not associated with change in LDL cholesterol (P=0.79). Odds of improvement were even greater with 10–15% loss [[Wing2011DiabetesCare]].
- At 1 year, Look AHEAD intensive lifestyle produced 8.6% mean weight loss versus 0.7% with diabetes support and education. Mean HbA1c fell from 7.3% to 6.6% versus 7.3% to 7.2% [[LookAHEAD2007]].
- Look AHEAD's main result: after a median 9.6 years, intensive lifestyle did not reduce cardiovascular events (HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.83–1.09). Weight loss was 6.0% versus 3.5% at study end [[LookAHEAD2013]].
- In a post hoc Look AHEAD analysis, people who lost at least 10% of body weight in year 1 had a 21% lower risk of the primary cardiovascular outcome (adjusted HR 0.79, 0.64–0.98) than those with stable weight or gain. This is observational, not randomised (Early evidence) [[LookAHEAD2016LDE]].
- The AHA statement notes that clinical trials of medical weight loss have not shown fewer coronary events. Prospective studies of bariatric surgery versus non-surgical care have shown lower coronary risk [[PowellWiley2021]].

### complications · Heart failure, HFpEF, atrial fibrillation, stroke and VTE
- Framingham (5881 adults, 14 years): heart failure risk rose 5% in men and 7% in women for each 1-unit increase in BMI. Obesity roughly doubled the risk (HR 2.12 in women, 1.90 in men) [[Kenchaiah2002]].
- Compared with non-obese HFpEF, "obese HFpEF" (BMI ≥35) showed greater plasma volume (3907 vs 2772 mL), more epicardial fat, greater pericardial restraint and worse peak VO2 (7.7 vs 10.0 mL/min/kg), despite lower NT-proBNP. The authors call it a distinct phenotype [[Obokata2017]].
- In STEP-HFpEF (529 people with HFpEF and BMI ≥30), semaglutide 2.4 mg improved KCCQ-CSS by 16.6 points versus 8.7 with placebo, with −13.3% vs −2.6% body weight at 52 weeks [[Kosiborod2023STEPHFpEF]].
- In SUMMIT (731 people with HFpEF and BMI ≥30), tirzepatide lowered cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure: 9.9% vs 15.3% with placebo (HR 0.62, 0.41–0.95) over a median 104 weeks [[Packer2025SUMMIT]].
- Framingham (5282 adults, 13.7 years): AF risk rose 4% per 1-unit BMI increase. Obesity HRs were 1.52 (men) and 1.46 (women), and the excess risk appeared to be mediated by left atrial enlargement [[Wang2004AF]].
- Meta-analysis of 51 studies (626 603 people): every 5-unit BMI increase meant 29% higher incident AF in cohorts (OR 1.29), 10% higher post-operative AF and 13% higher post-ablation AF [[Wong2015AF]].
- LEGACY cohort (355 people with AF and BMI ≥27): sustained weight loss of ≥10% was associated with a 6-fold greater probability of arrhythmia-free survival. Weight fluctuation >5% partly offset the benefit. This was an observational cohort (Early evidence) [[Pathak2015LEGACY]].
- Stroke meta-analysis (25 cohorts, 2.27 million people): RR for ischaemic stroke was 1.22 (overweight) and 1.64 (obesity). The haemorrhagic-stroke association was weaker and not significant (RR 1.24 for obesity, 95% CI 0.99–1.54) [[Strazzullo2010]].
- Venous thromboembolism (21 studies, 63 552 patients): obesity was associated with VTE (OR 2.33, 1.68–3.24) [[Ageno2008]].

### complications · MASLD/MASH: liver disease
- In 2023, a multisociety Delphi process (236 panellists, 56 countries) renamed NAFLD as metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD). The new definition requires steatosis plus at least 1 of 5 cardiometabolic risk factors, and it added MetALD for higher alcohol intake [[Rinella2023MASLD]].
- In that survey, the terms "nonalcoholic" and "fatty" were felt to be stigmatising by 61% and 66% of respondents [[Rinella2023MASLD]].
- In a meta-analysis of 151 studies, NAFLD prevalence was 69.99% in people with overweight and 75.27% in people with obesity. NASH prevalence was about 33.5% in both groups [[Quek2023]].
- Among people with obesity and NAFLD, 21.60% had clinically significant fibrosis (F2–F4) and 6.85% had advanced fibrosis (F3–F4) [[Quek2023]].
- AASLD 2023 guidance: everyone with hepatic steatosis or clinically suspected NAFLD because of obesity and metabolic risk factors "should undergo primary risk assessment with FIB-4". High-risk groups listed include T2DM and "medically complicated obesity" (full text) [[Rinella2023AASLD]].
- AASLD 2023 FIB-4 cut-points for advanced fibrosis: <1.3 rules out and ≥2.67 rules in. If FIB-4 ≥1.3, elastography (VCTE or MRE) or the ELF test may be used (full text, as published) [[Rinella2023AASLD]].
- AASLD notes that a "true normal" ALT in NAFLD is 29–33 U/L in men and 19–25 U/L in women, lower than many laboratory reference limits. A normal-range ALT does not exclude the disease (full text) [[Rinella2023AASLD]].
- AASLD: weight loss of 3–5% improves steatosis, but greater loss (>10%) is generally needed to improve NASH and fibrosis (full text) [[Rinella2023AASLD]].
- In 293 people with biopsy-proven NASH who tried lifestyle change for 52 weeks, all who lost ≥10% of body weight had lower NAS scores. Among them, 90% had NASH resolution and 45% had fibrosis regression [[VilarGomez2015]].
- In the same study, 58% of those who lost ≥5% had NASH resolution [[VilarGomez2015]].

### complications · Obstructive sleep apnoea
- Wisconsin Sleep Cohort (690 adults, 4-year intervals): compared with stable weight, a 10% weight gain predicted a 32% rise in AHI and a 10% weight loss a 26% fall [[Peppard2000]].
- In the same cohort, a 10% weight gain predicted about a 6-fold increase in the odds of developing moderate-to-severe sleep-disordered breathing (AHI ≥15) (95% CI 2.2–17.0) [[Peppard2000]].
- Estimated US prevalence of moderate-to-severe sleep-disordered breathing (AHI ≥15) in 2007–2010: 10% of men aged 30–49, 17% of men aged 50–70, 3% of women aged 30–49 and 9% of women aged 50–70. The authors call obesity "a strong causal factor" [[Peppard2013]].
- Sleep AHEAD (264 adults with T2DM, mean BMI 36.7): intensive lifestyle produced 10.8 kg vs 0.6 kg weight loss and lowered AHI by 9.7 events/h relative to control. More than three times as many people had total OSA remission [[Foster2009SleepAHEAD]].
- SURMOUNT-OSA (two trials): tirzepatide reduced AHI by 20.0 and 23.8 events/h more than placebo at 52 weeks in adults with moderate-to-severe OSA and obesity (baseline AHI about 50/h) [[Malhotra2024SURMOUNTOSA]].
- The US Zepbound label lists an indication "to treat moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) in adults with obesity", in combination with reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity [[FDA-Zepbound]].

### complications · Knee osteoarthritis and pain
- Knee OA meta-analysis (21 studies): each 5-unit BMI increase raised the risk of knee OA by 35% (RR 1.35, 1.21–1.51). The rise was stronger in women (RR 1.38) than in men (1.22) [[Jiang2012]].
- Framingham women: a BMI decrease of 2 units or more (about 5.1 kg) over 10 years cut the odds of symptomatic knee OA by more than 50% (OR 0.46, 0.24–0.86) [[Felson1992]].
- In adults with knee OA, each 1 kg of weight lost was associated with about 40.6 N lower compressive knee force per step, which the authors summarise as about a 4-fold reduction in knee load per unit of weight lost [[Messier2005]].
- IDEA trial (454 adults aged ≥55, BMI 27–41, with knee OA, 18 months): diet + exercise produced 10.6 kg (11.4%) mean weight loss, diet alone 8.9 kg (9.5%) and exercise alone 1.8 kg (2.0%) [[Messier2013IDEA]].
- In IDEA, diet + exercise gave less pain (WOMAC 3.6 vs 4.7 with exercise alone) and better function (14.1 vs 18.4). Knee compressive force was lower with diet than with exercise alone (2487 vs 2687 N) [[Messier2013IDEA]].

### complications · Digestive, gallbladder, kidney and urinary conditions
- GERD: pooled adjusted ORs for GERD symptoms were 1.43 at BMI 25–30 and 1.94 at BMI >30. For oesophageal adenocarcinoma they were 1.52 and 2.78 [[Hampel2005]].
- Gallbladder disease (17 cohorts, 1.9 million people): RR 1.63 per 5-unit BMI increase, and 1.46 per 10 cm of waist. Risk nearly doubled even within the "normal" BMI range [[Aune2015Gallbladder]].
- Kidney disease meta-analysis: RR 1.40 with overweight and 1.83 with obesity compared with normal weight, with a stronger association in women (1.92) than men (1.49) [[Wang2008Kidney]].
- Kaiser Permanente (320 252 adults): adjusted RR of end-stage renal disease was 1.87 with overweight, 3.57 with class I obesity, 6.12 with class II and 7.07 with BMI ≥40 [[Hsu2006]].
- PRIDE trial (338 women with ≥10 incontinence episodes a week, mean BMI 36): a 6-month weight-loss programme (8.0% loss) cut weekly incontinence episodes by 47% versus 28% in controls (P=0.01). The gain was mainly in stress incontinence [[Subak2009PRIDE]].

### complications · Reproductive health and PCOS
- Women with PCOS had a higher prevalence of obesity than women without PCOS (RR 2.77, 1.88–4.10) and of central obesity (RR 1.73) [[Lim2012PCOS]].
- The 2023 international PCOS guideline stresses PCOS's metabolic and cardiovascular features, sleep apnoea and adverse pregnancy outcomes. It keeps the emphasis on healthy lifestyle "with awareness and consideration of weight stigma" [[Teede2024PCOS]].
- Nurses' Health Study II: an estimated 25% (95% CI 20–31%) of ovulatory infertility in US women may be attributable to BMI ≥25 [[RichEdwards2002]].
- Danish National Birth Cohort (47 835 couples): rising BMI group was linked to subfecundity (time to pregnancy >12 months), OR 1.32 per group in women and 1.19 in men. Couples in which both partners had obesity had the highest risk [[RamlauHansen2007]].

### complications · Mental health
- Meta-analysis of 15 longitudinal studies (58 745 people): obesity at baseline raised the odds of later depression (OR 1.55, 1.22–1.98), and depression raised the odds of later obesity (OR 1.58, 1.33–1.87). The link runs both ways [[Luppino2010]].
- UK Biobank Mendelian randomisation: a genetically 1 SD higher BMI (4.9 kg/m²) raised the odds of depression (OR 1.18 overall; 1.24 in women). Higher BMI is likely partly causal for depression (Early evidence) [[Tyrrell2019]].
- Binge eating disorder (WHO World Mental Health Surveys, 14 countries): median lifetime prevalence was 1.4%, higher than bulimia nervosa (0.8%). Fewer than half of people with BED ever received treatment, and the authors stress asking about eating problems [[Kessler2013BED]].
- The Lancet Commission notes that eating disorders, especially binge eating disorder and night eating syndrome, are common among people living with obesity. The links may run both ways (full text) [[Rubino2025Commission]].

### complications · Cancer
- The IARC Working Group (2016) found sufficient evidence that the absence of excess body fatness lowers the risk of cancers at 13 sites: oesophageal adenocarcinoma, gastric cardia, colon and rectum, liver, gallbladder, pancreas, postmenopausal breast, corpus uteri, ovary, kidney (renal cell), meningioma, thyroid and multiple myeloma (full text) [[LaubySecretan2016]].
- IARC: for endometrial cancer, RRs were about 1.5 (overweight), 2.5 (class 1 obesity), 4.5 (class 2) and 7.1 (class 3). For oesophageal adenocarcinoma, RR was up to 4.8 at BMI ≥40 (full text) [[LaubySecretan2016]].
- IARC judged evidence that intentional weight loss lowers cancer risk in humans as not yet sufficient for formal evaluation, though animal and mechanistic data support it (Early evidence; full text) [[LaubySecretan2016]].
- In 5.24 million UK adults, each 5 kg/m² was linearly associated with higher risk of uterine cancer (HR 1.62), gallbladder (1.31), kidney (1.25) and liver (1.19). An estimated 41% of uterine cancers could be attributable to excess weight, assuming causality [[Bhaskaran2014]].
- In the United States in 2014, about 631 000 diagnoses (40% of all cancers diagnosed) were cancers associated with overweight and obesity [[Steele2017MMWR]].

### complications · Infection: COVID-19
- In 6.9 million adults in England, the risk of COVID-19 hospital admission rose 5% per kg/m² above a BMI of 23 (HR 1.05). ICU admission rose 10% per kg/m² across the whole BMI range [[Gao2021COVID]].
- The BMI-linked risk was greater in people under 40 (HR 1.09 per kg/m² for admission at age 20–39 vs 1.01 at 80–100) and in Black people (1.07 vs 1.04) [[Gao2021COVID]].
- Among 540 667 US adults hospitalised with COVID-19 (March 2020–March 2021), obesity was among the strongest risk factors for death (adjusted RR 1.30, 1.27–1.33). Obesity was recorded in 33.0% of these patients [[Kompaniyets2021]].

### complications · Atypical and "clinical obesity" framing
- The 2025 Lancet Commission defines clinical obesity as a chronic, systemic illness in which excess adiposity alters organ or tissue function, or limits daily activities. Preclinical obesity is excess adiposity with preserved organ function [[Rubino2025Commission]].
- The Commission lists 18 adult diagnostic criteria (consensus 90–100%), including: raised intracranial pressure; sleep apnoeas or hypopnoeas; hypoventilation or breathlessness; HFrEF; HFpEF; chronic or recurrent AF; pulmonary hypertension; recurrent DVT or PE; and raised blood pressure (full text, Table 2) [[Rubino2025Commission]].
- The remaining adult criteria are: the cluster of hyperglycaemia, high triglycerides and low HDL; NAFLD with hepatic fibrosis; microalbuminuria with reduced eGFR; recurrent or chronic urinary incontinence; anovulation, oligomenorrhoea and PCOS; male hypogonadism; chronic severe knee or hip pain with stiffness; lower-limb lymphoedema; and significant limitation of daily activities (full text) [[Rubino2025Commission]].

### assessment · History, medication review and drivers
- The modified 5As for obesity counselling are Ask, Assess, Advise, Agree and Assist. Ask means asking permission to discuss weight, without judgement, and exploring readiness. Assess covers BMI, waist circumference, obesity stage, and the drivers and complications of excess weight [[Vallis2013]].
- In the 5As, Advise covers health risks, the benefits of modest weight loss, the need for a long-term strategy and treatment options. Agree covers realistic expectations and the plan. Assist covers barriers, resources, referrals and regular follow-up [[Vallis2013]].
- Meta-analysis of 257 RCTs: drugs associated with weight gain versus placebo included olanzapine (2.4 kg), gabapentin (2.2 kg), pioglitazone (2.6 kg), glyburide (2.6 kg), amitriptyline (1.8 kg) and mirtazapine (1.5 kg). Population averages; no one should stop a medicine without a clinician [[Domecq2015]].
- In the same meta-analysis, drugs associated with weight loss included metformin (1.1 kg), liraglutide (1.7 kg), topiramate (3.8 kg) and bupropion (1.3 kg) [[Domecq2015]].
- Endocrine Society 2015: many medicines prescribed for diabetes, depression and other chronic diseases affect weight, and "knowledgeable prescribing", choosing weight-favourable options where possible, can help prevent and manage obesity [[Apovian2015]].
- The Lancet Commission notes that secondary obesity can occur with Cushing's syndrome, hypothyroidism and medicines such as steroids, antidepressants and antipsychotics (full text) [[Rubino2025Commission]].
- Weight history matters. In the Aerobics Center Longitudinal Study, lower weight at age 21, lower fitness and reported dieting were each associated with being in EOSS stage 2 or 3 [[Kuk2011EOSS]].
- The 2016 AACE/ACE guideline frames obesity as an "adiposity-based chronic disease". It says management should target both weight-related complications and adiposity [[Garvey2016AACE]].

### assessment · Examination and measuring
- Lancet Commission: BMI should be used only as a surrogate for health risk at population level, in epidemiology or for screening. Excess adiposity should be confirmed by direct fat measurement or at least one anthropometric criterion (waist, waist-to-hip or waist-to-height) in addition to BMI. Above BMI 40, excess adiposity can be assumed [[Rubino2025Commission]].
- The IAS/ICCR consensus recommends measuring waist circumference routinely as a "vital sign" alongside BMI. It argues BMI alone is not enough to assess cardiometabolic risk [[Ross2020Waist]].
- IAS/ICCR, citing a meta-regression of 18 European studies (>680 000 people): waist circumference was associated with higher all-cause mortality above 95 cm in men and 80 cm in women (full text) [[Ross2020Waist]].
- AHA 2019: a cuff that is too small gives an artificially high reading. The cuff bladder length should be 75–100% of the measured arm circumference and its width 37–50% (full text) [[Muntner2019]].
- AHA illustrative cuff sizes by arm circumference: small adult 22–26 cm, adult 27–34 cm, large adult 35–44 cm, extra-large adult 45–52 cm. Measure arm circumference at the midpoint between acromion and olecranon (full text) [[Muntner2019]].
- AHA 2019: choosing the right cuff "is an essential prerequisite" for accurate BP in people with obesity. Most kiosk BP machines have a single cuff that is too small for most US adults. If an extra-large cuff does not fit, a validated wrist device held at heart level is an option (full text) [[Muntner2019]].
- Acanthosis nigricans was found in 74% of 34 randomly chosen adults attending an obesity clinic. It was more frequent with greater BMI and marked higher fasting insulin. The authors call it "a reliable cutaneous marker of hyperinsulinemia" [[Hud1992]].

### assessment · Laboratory tests
- Lancet Commission (consensus, 98%): standard laboratory tests for people with confirmed excess adiposity "should include at least" a full blood count, glycaemia, lipid profile, and renal and liver function tests (full text, as published) [[Rubino2025Commission]].
- The Commission adds that specific tests may be needed "if clinically indicated" to rule out secondary obesity (for example hypothyroidism or Cushing's syndrome). It says people with preclinical or clinical obesity should be regularly screened for type 2 diabetes (full text) [[Rubino2025Commission]].
- AASLD: FIB-4 uses age, ALT, AST and platelet count. In people with prediabetes, T2DM or 2 or more metabolic risk factors, FIB-4 should be repeated every 1–2 years (full text) [[Rinella2023AASLD]].
- AASLD: NAFLD carries a 2- to 5-fold risk of incident diabetes, so people with NAFLD should be screened for type 2 diabetes (full text) [[Rinella2023AASLD]].

### assessment · Sleep apnoea screening: STOP-Bang
- STOP stands for Snoring, Tiredness, Observed apnoea and high blood Pressure. Adding BMI, Age, Neck circumference and male Gender ("Bang") raised sensitivity for AHI >15 from 74.3% to 92.9%, and for AHI >30 from 79.5% to 100%, in surgical patients [[Chung2008STOP]].
- STOP-Bang scores range from 0 to 8. A score of 0–2 is low risk and 5–8 is high risk for moderate-to-severe OSA. In the mid-range (3–4), a score of ≥2 plus BMI >35 classifies a person as high risk [[Chung2016STOPBang]].
- A STOP-Bang score ≥3 has 93% sensitivity for moderate-to-severe OSA and 100% for severe OSA, with negative predictive values of 90% and 100% [[Chung2016STOPBang]].
- As the score rises from 0–2 to 7–8, the probability of moderate-to-severe OSA rises from 18% to 60%, and of severe OSA from 4% to 38% [[Chung2016STOPBang]].
- Meta-analysis (17 studies, 9206 patients): in sleep clinics, sensitivity was 90%, 94% and 96% for any, moderate-to-severe and severe OSA. The negative predictive value for any OSA was only 46% [[Nagappa2015]].
- In surgical patients (median BMI 30), a STOP-Bang score of 5 gave an OR of 4.8 for moderate/severe OSA and 10.4 for severe OSA [[Chung2012STOPBang]].

### assessment · Staging systems: EOSS and King's
- The Edmonton Obesity Staging System (EOSS) is a clinical and functional staging system meant to be used alongside anthropometric classification. It describes the comorbidity and functional limitation linked to excess weight [[Sharma2009EOSS]].
- EOSS is a 5-point ordinal scale (0–4). In NHANES adults with overweight or obesity, over 75% scored 1 or 2 [[Padwal2011EOSS]].
- NHANES III: EOSS stage 2 (HR 1.57) and stage 3 (HR 2.69) predicted higher mortality than stages 0–1, even after adjustment for BMI and metabolic syndrome. Survival curves separated by EOSS stage but not by obesity class alone [[Padwal2011EOSS]].
- In 29 533 adults followed for 16 years, people with obesity at EOSS stage 2 or 3 had higher all-cause mortality than people of normal weight (HR 1.6 and 1.7). Stage 0/1 was not associated with higher mortality [[Kuk2011EOSS]].
- The King's Obesity Staging Criteria score nine domains, A to I: Airways, BMI, Cardiovascular, Diabetes, Economic, Functional, Gonadal, Health status (perceived) and body Image. Each domain is scored 0 (normal health), 1 (at risk), 2 (established disease) or 3 (advanced disease) [[Aasheim2011]].
- In 144 people before bariatric surgery, 84% were at stage 2 or 3 in at least one domain other than BMI. Clinicians agreed on the same score in more than 75% of cases for most domains [[Aasheim2011]].

### assessment · Cardiovascular-treatment labels relevant to complications
- The US Wegovy label (openFDA) includes an indication to reduce the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events in adults with established cardiovascular disease and either obesity or overweight, with reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity [[FDA-Wegovy]].

### Exam hooks
- DPP: lifestyle (goal ≥7% weight loss, 150 min/week activity) cut type 2 diabetes incidence by 58% and metformin by 31%, versus placebo, over 2.8 years [[Knowler2002DPP]].
- In the DPP lifestyle arm, each kilogram lost meant 16% lower diabetes risk [[Hamman2006]].
- DiRECT remission at 12 months: 46% vs 4%. By weight loss: 86% of those who lost ≥15 kg [[Lean2018DiRECT]].
- DiRECT remission at 24 months: 36% vs 3% [[Lean2019DiRECT]].
- Look AHEAD: intensive lifestyle did not reduce cardiovascular events (HR 0.95) in adults with type 2 diabetes [[LookAHEAD2013]].
- Modest weight loss of 5 to <10% improves glycaemia, BP, triglycerides and HDL, but not LDL [[Wing2011DiabetesCare]].
- A 10% weight gain predicted about 6-fold higher odds of moderate-to-severe sleep-disordered breathing [[Peppard2000]].
- STOP-Bang 0–2 is low risk and 5–8 high risk for moderate-to-severe OSA. Score ≥3: 93% sensitivity for AHI >15 [[Chung2016STOPBang]].
- IARC lists 13 cancers linked to excess body fatness, including meningioma, thyroid and multiple myeloma [[LaubySecretan2016]].
- Obesity and depression are bidirectional: obesity → depression OR 1.55, depression → obesity OR 1.58 [[Luppino2010]].
- Obesity-associated AF risk was mediated by left atrial enlargement in Framingham [[Wang2004AF]].
- Each 1-unit BMI increase raised heart-failure risk by 5% (men) and 7% (women) in Framingham [[Kenchaiah2002]].
- NAFLD has been renamed MASLD and requires at least 1 of 5 cardiometabolic risk factors [[Rinella2023MASLD]].
- FIB-4 <1.3 rules out advanced fibrosis and ≥2.67 rules it in (AASLD) [[Rinella2023AASLD]].
- EOSS stages 2–3, not BMI class alone, predicted higher mortality in NHANES [[Padwal2011EOSS]].
- A blood-pressure cuff that is too small overestimates BP. The bladder length should be 75–100% of arm circumference [[Muntner2019]].
- Each unit of body weight lost (1 kg, 9.8 N) cut knee compressive force by about 4 units (40.6 N) per step [[Messier2005]].
- Acanthosis nigricans signals hyperinsulinaemia [[Hud1992]].
- USPSTF: offer or refer adults with BMI ≥30 to intensive multicomponent behavioural interventions (grade B) [[Curry2018USPSTF]].
- The 5As are Ask (permission), Assess, Advise, Agree and Assist [[Vallis2013]].

### In plain words
- Living with obesity raises the chance of many other health problems, such as type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, sleep apnoea, liver disease and knee pain. These numbers describe large groups of people, not you personally [[Guh2009,PSC2009]].
- Obesity is a long-term disease with genetic, biological and social causes. It is not a failure of willpower, and weight coming back after weight loss is biology, not weakness [[Rubino2025Commission,Lean2024DiRECT]].
- Losing even a small amount of weight, around 5 in every 100 kilograms, can lower blood sugar, blood pressure and blood fats for many people [[Wing2011DiabetesCare]].
- In a large study of people at high risk, a lifestyle programme cut the chance of getting type 2 diabetes by more than half over about three years [[Knowler2002DPP]].
- For some people who have had type 2 diabetes for only a few years, a supported weight programme put diabetes into remission. Remission can wear off over time, so check-ups still matter [[Lean2018DiRECT,Lean2024DiRECT]].
- Loud snoring, stopping breathing at night or feeling very tired in the day can be signs of sleep apnoea. A short questionnaire can help your clinician decide whether you need a sleep test [[Chung2016STOPBang]].
- A good check-up asks your permission before talking about weight. It looks at your whole health, not just the number on the scale [[Vallis2013,Rubino2025Commission]].
- Some medicines can cause weight gain. Never stop or change a medicine on your own: ask your clinician whether there is another option [[Domecq2015]].
- Your blood pressure should be measured with a cuff that fits your arm. A cuff that is too small can show a falsely high reading [[Muntner2019]].
- If eating or weight causes you distress, or you find yourself bingeing, restricting or purging, please talk to a clinician or an eating-disorder service. Help works, and many people with binge eating never get treatment [[Kessler2013BED]].
- Low mood and weight can affect each other. Getting help for one can help with the other [[Luppino2010]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Do intentional weight loss and lower cardiovascular events go together? Look AHEAD found no reduction in events with intensive lifestyle (HR 0.95) [[LookAHEAD2013]]. A post hoc analysis found 21% lower risk among people who lost ≥10% [[LookAHEAD2016LDE]], but that comparison is not randomised. The AHA statement notes that medical weight-loss trials have not reduced coronary events, while surgical cohorts suggest benefit [[PowellWiley2021]]. Status: unresolved for lifestyle; for drug trials see pass F.
- How durable is diabetes remission? DiRECT remission fell from 46% at 1 year to 36% at 2 years [[Lean2018DiRECT,Lean2019DiRECT]]. Only 26% of those in remission at 2 years were still in remission at 5 years [[Lean2024DiRECT]]. This is consistent with weight regain being biological, not a lack of effort (Established that remission often wanes; how to sustain it is open).
- How large is the remission rate among people who keep off ≥10 kg? The 2-year paper gives 64% (29/45, whole study) [[Lean2019DiRECT]], and the 5-year paper gives 81% (29/36, intervention group) [[Lean2024DiRECT]]. The denominators differ, so the figure to show depends on the population chosen.
- Is the link between obesity and stroke causal? Observational meta-analyses show higher ischaemic-stroke risk (RR 1.64 with obesity) [[Strazzullo2010]]. Pooled Mendelian randomisation found no association (OR 1.02) [[Riaz2018]]. Status: unresolved.
- Is BMI the right clinical measure? The Lancet Commission says BMI alone should be used only for population risk and screening, with adiposity confirmed by waist or body-fat measures [[Rubino2025Commission]]. IAS/ICCR also urge routine waist measurement [[Ross2020Waist]]. Function-based staging (EOSS) predicted mortality better than BMI class [[Padwal2011EOSS]]. Status: guidelines are moving away from BMI-only diagnosis; validated ethnicity- and sex-specific waist cut-offs are still being refined [[Ross2020Waist]].
- What does the direction of the obesity–depression link mean? Longitudinal data show it runs both ways [[Luppino2010]], and Mendelian randomisation suggests higher BMI is partly causal for depression [[Tyrrell2019]]. Weight stigma is a plausible contributor that these designs do not isolate (Hypothesis).
- Does intentional weight loss lower cancer risk? IARC found insufficient human evidence for a formal evaluation, though mechanistic and animal data support it [[LaubySecretan2016]]. Status: Early evidence.
- The BMI–COVID-19 risk gradient was steeper in younger and Black adults [[Gao2021COVID]]. Whether this reflects biology, social determinants or both is not settled (Hypothesis).
