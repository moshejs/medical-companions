# H · Children, pregnancy, stigma, living with obesity & nursing

Pass date: 2026-11-11. Modules served: children, pregnancy, stigma, living, nursing.

This pass covers obesity in children and adolescents (growth-chart definitions, the 2023 AAP guideline, adolescent medication and surgery trials, tracking into adulthood), obesity before and during pregnancy (risks, gestational weight gain, preconception medicines), weight stigma (in society, in health care, in language and media), living with obesity (mood, binge eating, quality of life, maintenance, daily practicalities) and nursing care (blood pressure cuffs, safe handling, skin, airway, dosing scalars, VTE prophylaxis, post-bariatric warning signs, pen teaching, the 5As). Trial and cohort numbers come from PubMed abstracts or open full texts. Label statements come from current US labels in openFDA.

## Findings

### children · Defining obesity in children: percentiles, z-scores and the extended charts
- In children and adolescents, obesity is defined as BMI at or above the 95th percentile of BMI-for-age, and severe obesity as BMI at or above 120% of the 95th percentile or a BMI of 35 or more [[Ogden2023]].
- The 2023 AAP guideline recommends using percentages of the 95th percentile (120% for class 2 severe obesity, 140% for class 3) to describe levels of severe obesity [[Ogden2023,Hampl2023AAP]].
- KAS 1 of the AAP guideline: screen all children aged 2 years and older for overweight and obesity at least once a year, using BMI percentile for age and sex [[Orr2026,Hampl2023AAP]].
- The 2000 CDC growth charts revised the 1977 NCHS charts. They cover birth to 36 months and 2 to 20 years, and they introduced BMI-for-age charts built from five US national examination surveys [[Kuczmarski2002]].
- The 2000 CDC charts did not support percentiles or z-scores above the 97th percentile, so they could not grade severe obesity well [[Ogden2023]].
- In December 2022, CDC released Extended BMI-for-age growth charts. They reach a BMI of 60 and add four curves: the 98th, 99th, 99.9th and 99.99th percentiles [[Ogden2023]].
- From about age 7, the extended 98th percentile approximates 120% of the 95th percentile. At ages 2 to 6, both 120% and 140% of the 95th percentile sit higher than the extended 98th and 99th percentiles [[Ogden2023]].
- In NHANES 2017 to March 2020, among US children and adolescents aged 2 to 19:
  - 7.7% (95% CI 6.5-9.0) had BMI at or above the extended 98th percentile.
  - 6.7% (5.5-8.1) had BMI at or above 120% of the 95th percentile.
  - At ages 12-19 the two measures nearly matched: 8.7% vs 8.5% [[Ogden2023]]
- The WHO 2007 growth reference for ages 5-19 was built to line up with adult cut-offs. At age 19, +1 SD equals a BMI of 25.4 for boys and 25.0 for girls (adult overweight ≥25). +2 SD equals 29.7 for both sexes (adult obesity ≥30) [[deOnis2007]].
- An AHA scientific statement put severe obesity at between 4% and 6% of all US youth and recommended a standardized definition for it [[Kelly2013AHA]].
- About 21% of US adolescents have obesity. Obesity in adolescents aged 12 to under 18 is commonly defined as BMI at or above the 95th age- and sex-adjusted percentile [[Kelly2024JAMA]].

### children · Health risks and tracking into adulthood
- In NHANES 1999-2012, 8579 children and young adults aged 3-19 had BMI at or above the 85th percentile. Of these:
  - 46.9% had overweight
  - 36.4% had class I obesity
  - 11.9% had class II obesity
  - 4.8% had class III obesity [[Skinner2015]]
- The more severe the obesity, the higher the risk of low HDL cholesterol, high systolic and diastolic blood pressure, high triglycerides and high HbA1c. The gradient was strongest in boys and young men [[Skinner2015]].
- Obesity in adolescence is associated with insulin resistance, hypertension, dyslipidaemia, sleep disorders, depression and musculoskeletal problems. It is also associated with higher adult mortality from cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes [[Kelly2024JAMA]].
- In a meta-analysis of 15 prospective cohorts (200,777 participants), children and adolescents with obesity were around five times more likely to have obesity as adults [[Simmonds2016]].
- Around 55% of children with obesity still have it in adolescence. Around 80% of adolescents with obesity still have it in adulthood, and around 70% have it over age 30 [[Simmonds2016]].
- The reverse also holds: 70% of adults with obesity did not have obesity in childhood or adolescence. Targeting only children with higher weight would therefore not remove most adult obesity [[Simmonds2016]].
- AAP KAS 3-8 recommend checking for comorbidities. For children aged 10 or older with obesity, this means lipids, glucose and liver enzymes (ALT). The AAP also recommends measuring blood pressure at every visit from age 3 in children with overweight or obesity [[Orr2026,Hampl2023AAP]].
- KAS 4: treat overweight or obesity and its comorbidities at the same time (strong recommendation, grade A) [[Orr2026,Hampl2023AAP]].

### children · The AAP 2023 treatment pathway (quoted as published, not as instructions)
- The AAP guideline recommends against "watchful waiting". Treatment should begin when obesity is diagnosed [[Cuda2024OMA,Orr2026,Hampl2023AAP]].
- KAS 9: care is delivered through a family-centred, non-stigmatising medical home that addresses biological, social and structural drivers [[Orr2026,Hampl2023AAP]].
- KAS 10: use motivational interviewing [[Orr2026,Hampl2023AAP]].
- KAS 11: refer children with overweight and obesity to intensive health behaviour and lifestyle treatment (IHBLT). The guideline describes it as most effective with ≥26 hours of face-to-face, family-based, multicomponent treatment over 3 to 12 months [[Cuda2024OMA,Orr2026,Hampl2023AAP]].
- KAS 11 carries a moderate recommendation, graded B for age 6 and older and C for ages 2-5 [[Orr2026,Hampl2023AAP]].
- KAS 12: offer adolescents aged 12 and older with obesity weight-loss pharmacotherapy as an adjunct to lifestyle treatment, weighing risks and benefits (grade B) [[Orr2026,Hampl2023AAP]].
- KAS 13: refer adolescents aged 13 and older with severe obesity for evaluation for metabolic and bariatric surgery (grade C) [[Orr2026,Hampl2023AAP]].
- Lifestyle therapy alone needs more than 26 contact hours over a year to produce a mean BMI fall of about 3% [[Kelly2024JAMA]].
- Uptake is low. Fewer than 2% of eligible adolescents receive pharmacotherapy, and referrals to IHBLT and to surgery are infrequent [[Orr2026]].
- In a meta-analysis of 36 studies (2589 participants aged 7.8-16.9), structured, professionally run obesity treatment with a dietary component was followed by fewer eating-disorder symptoms. Bulimic symptoms fell (SMD -0.326), binge eating fell (SMD -0.588), and ED risk was lower at follow-up (SMD -0.313) [[Jebeile2019]]. This applies to supervised programmes, not to self-directed dieting.

### children · Adolescent medicines: trial results (population averages)
- STEP TEENS randomised 201 adolescents aged 12 to under 18 with obesity (or overweight plus a comorbidity) 2:1 to weekly semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo for 68 weeks, both with lifestyle intervention [[Weghuber2022STEPTEENS]].
- In STEP TEENS, the mean BMI change at week 68 was -16.1% with semaglutide and +0.6% with placebo (difference -16.7 percentage points; 95% CI -20.3 to -13.2) [[Weghuber2022STEPTEENS]].
- Weight loss of 5% or more occurred in 73% (95/131) on semaglutide vs 18% (11/62) on placebo [[Weghuber2022STEPTEENS]].
- At baseline, STEP TEENS participants had a mean age of 15.4 years, a mean weight of 107.5 kg and a mean BMI of 37.0. 62% were girls [[Weghuber2022STEPTEENS]].
- Other STEP TEENS results at week 68:
  - mean absolute BMI change: -5.8 (semaglutide) vs +0.1 (placebo)
  - BMI as a percentage of the 95th percentile: -24.6 vs -4.2 percentage points
  - body weight: -14.7% vs +2.7% [[Weghuber2022STEPTEENS]]
- Seven weeks after stopping treatment (week 75), BMI change was -13.2% with semaglutide vs +1.2% with placebo. The authors describe this as a small regain [[Weghuber2022STEPTEENS]].
- STEP TEENS safety:
  - gastrointestinal adverse events: 62% (semaglutide) vs 42% (placebo)
  - cholelithiasis: 5 (4%) on semaglutide, none on placebo
  - serious adverse events: 11% vs 9% [[Weghuber2022STEPTEENS]]
- In the liraglutide 3.0 mg trial (251 adolescents, 56 weeks), BMI SDS improved by 0.22 more than with placebo (95% CI -0.37 to -0.08) [[Kelly2020Liraglutide]].
- In the same trial, a BMI reduction of at least 5% occurred in 43.3% on liraglutide vs 18.7% on placebo. After the drug was stopped, BMI SDS rose more in the liraglutide group (difference 0.15) [[Kelly2020Liraglutide]].
- In adolescents aged 12 to under 17, phentermine/topiramate top dose (15/92 mg) reduced BMI at week 56 by 10.44 percentage points more than placebo. The mid dose reduced it by 8.11 points more [[Kelly2022PHENTPM]].
- Added to lifestyle therapy, newer medicines reduce mean BMI by about 5% to 17% at one year in adolescents. Severe adverse events are rare [[Kelly2024JAMA]].
- Labels:
  - Wegovy is indicated for "adults and pediatric patients aged 12 years and older with obesity". Its pediatric maintenance dosage is 2.4 mg (recommended) or 1.7 mg once weekly.
  - Pediatric patients on Wegovy had more cholelithiasis, cholecystitis, hypotension, rash and urticaria than adults on it [[FDA-Wegovy]].
  - Saxenda is indicated for pediatric patients aged 12 and older "with body weight greater than 60 kg and obesity" [[FDA-Saxenda]].
  - The Zepbound label states that safety and effectiveness "have not been established in pediatric patients" [[FDA-Zepbound]].

### children · Adolescent metabolic and bariatric surgery
- Teen-LABS studied 242 adolescents (mean age 17, mean BMI 53). Three years after surgery, mean weight had fallen 27% (95% CI 25-29): 28% after gastric bypass and 26% after sleeve gastrectomy [[Inge2016TeenLABS]].
- At 3 years, remission rates were:
  - type 2 diabetes: 95%
  - abnormal kidney function: 86%
  - prediabetes: 76%
  - elevated blood pressure: 74%
  - dyslipidaemia: 66%
- Also at 3 years, 57% had hypoferritinaemia and 13% had had further intra-abdominal procedures [[Inge2016TeenLABS]].
- Five years after gastric bypass, weight change was similar in adolescents (-26%) and adults (-29%) [[Inge2019TeenLABS]].
- Adolescents were more likely than adults to have remission of type 2 diabetes (86% vs 53%) and of hypertension (68% vs 41%) [[Inge2019TeenLABS]].
- Adolescents had more abdominal reoperations than adults (19 vs 10 per 500 person-years) and more low ferritin (48% vs 29%) [[Inge2019TeenLABS]].
- In the Swedish AMOS study (81 adolescents, mean BMI 45.5), BMI fell by 13.1 kg/m² over 5 years after gastric bypass. Over the same period, BMI rose 3.3 kg/m² in matched adolescents treated without surgery [[Olbers2017AMOS]].
- In AMOS, 25% of surgical patients needed further abdominal surgery and 72% had some nutritional deficiency. 25% of the non-surgical controls went on to have surgery within 5 years [[Olbers2017AMOS]].
- In adolescents with severe obesity (BMI ≥120% of the 95th percentile), surgery reduces mean BMI by about 30% at one year. About 15% have minor and 8% major perioperative complications [[Kelly2024JAMA]].
- AAP policy calls metabolic and bariatric surgery an underused option for youth with severe obesity and notes that access is unequal, especially in underserved groups [[Armstrong2019AAP]].

### children · Talking with children and families: health, not weight
- The AAP stigma policy statement makes 6 clinical and 4 advocacy recommendations. They include modelling non-biased language, using motivational interviewing, and addressing weight stigma and bullying at the visit [[Pont2017AAP]].
- Weight stigma in youth contributes to binge eating, social isolation, avoiding health care, less physical activity and more weight gain. It does not motivate change (Established) [[Pont2017AAP,PuhlLatner2007]].
- In a survey of 361 adolescents at weight-loss camps:
  - 64% reported weight-based victimisation at school.
  - 36% had been teased or bullied for 5 years.
  - Perpetrators included peers (92%), PE teachers or coaches (42%) and parents (37%) [[Puhl2013Bullying]]
- In a 2021 survey of 2032 youth aged 10-17 and 1936 parents, youth preferred terms such as "healthy weight". They disliked "obese", "fat" and "large", which made them feel sad, ashamed and embarrassed [[Puhl2022Terms]].

### pregnancy · Risks before and during pregnancy (population statistics)
- Obesity is the most common medical condition in women of reproductive age [[ACOG2021PB230,Catalano2017]].
- Obesity reduces fertility and increases the time taken to conceive [[Poston2016]].
- A review of 22 systematic reviews found these more likely with maternal obesity:
  - gestational diabetes, pre-eclampsia, gestational hypertension and depression
  - instrumental and caesarean birth, and surgical site infection
  - preterm birth, large-for-gestational-age babies, congenital anomalies and perinatal death
  - lower breastfeeding initiation and earlier stopping [[Marchi2015]]
- Odds of gestational diabetes compared with normal weight (unadjusted ORs) were 2.14 for overweight, 3.56 for obesity and 8.56 for severe obesity [[Chu2007]].
- In 13 cohorts (nearly 1.4 million women), pre-eclampsia risk typically doubled with each 5-7 kg/m² rise in pre-pregnancy BMI [[OBrien2003]].
- Compared with BMI 20-25, the crude odds of caesarean delivery in first pregnancies were 1.53 for overweight, 2.26 for BMI 30-35 and 3.38 for BMI over 35 [[Poobalan2009]].
- Compared with mothers of recommended BMI, mothers with obesity had higher odds of:
  - neural tube defects: OR 1.87 (95% CI 1.62-2.15)
  - spina bifida: OR 2.24
  - cardiovascular anomalies: OR 1.30
  - hydrocephaly: OR 1.68
- Gastroschisis was less common (OR 0.17). The authors note the absolute increase in anomalies is likely small [[Stothard2009]].
- Each 5-unit rise in maternal BMI was associated with a relative risk of 1.24 for stillbirth and 1.21 for fetal death [[Aune2014]].
- Absolute stillbirth risk was 40 per 10,000 pregnancies at BMI 20, 48 at BMI 25 and 59 at BMI 30 [[Aune2014]].
- Pregnant women with obesity are also at higher risk of early pregnancy loss, shoulder dystocia, stillbirth and post-partum haemorrhage [[Poston2016]].
- After birth, risks include venous thromboembolism, depression and difficulty breastfeeding [[Catalano2017]].
- Maternal obesity before conception was associated with higher odds of obesity in the child: OR 3.64 (95% CI 2.68-4.95), described as a 264% increase. Maternal overweight had OR 1.89 [[Heslehurst2019]].
- The proposed mechanisms are higher pre-pregnancy insulin resistance, hyperinsulinaemia, inflammation and oxidative stress, which may affect early placental and fetal development (Early evidence) [[Catalano2017]].

### pregnancy · Gestational weight gain (IOM 2009 ranges, quoted as published)
- The IOM 2009 total gestational weight gain ranges, by pre-pregnancy BMI:
  - underweight (BMI <18.5): 12.5-18 kg
  - normal weight (18.5-24.9): 11.5-16 kg
  - overweight (25-29.9): 7-11 kg
  - obesity (≥30): 5-9 kg [[Rasmussen2009IOM,Goldstein2017]]
- In a meta-analysis of 1,309,136 women, gestational weight gain was below the IOM range in 23% of pregnancies and above it in 47% [[Goldstein2017]].
- Gain above the range was associated with more large-for-gestational-age babies (OR 1.85; absolute risk difference 4%), macrosomia (OR 1.95) and caesarean delivery (OR 1.30) [[Goldstein2017]].
- Gain below the range was associated with more small-for-gestational-age babies (OR 1.53) and preterm birth (OR 1.70) [[Goldstein2017]].
- 50-60% of women with overweight or obesity gain more than the IOM recommends. Weight kept on after the birth raises later cardiometabolic risk [[Catalano2017]].
- A pooled analysis of 196,670 women in 25 cohorts reported "any adverse outcome" in 37.2% overall. The rate ranged from 34.7% in underweight women to 61.1% in women with obesity grade 3 [[Voerman2019LifeCycle]].
- The ranges linked to the lowest risk in that analysis were:
  - obesity grade 1: 2 to under 6 kg
  - grade 2: weight loss or gain of 0 to under 4 kg
  - grade 3: gain of 0 to under 6 kg
- These ranges predicted outcomes only modestly (AUC 0.55-0.76) [[Voerman2019LifeCycle]].

### pregnancy · Preconception care, medicines and folic acid
- Wegovy label: "discontinue WEGOVY in patients at least 2 months before they plan to become pregnant to account for the long half-life of semaglutide" [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Zepbound label:
  - Advise pregnant patients that weight loss is not recommended during pregnancy, and stop Zepbound when a pregnancy is recognised.
  - Zepbound may reduce the efficacy of oral hormonal contraceptives. Advise switching to a non-oral method, or adding a barrier method for 4 weeks after starting and for 4 weeks after each dose escalation [[FDA-Zepbound]].
- RCOG Green-top Guideline No. 72 is the UK guideline on care of women with obesity in pregnancy. It is widely cited as recommending 5 mg folic acid daily before conception and in the first trimester for women with BMI ≥30 (unverified) [[Denison2019RCOG]].
- Uptake of 5 mg folic acid is low. In Northern Ireland booking data (255,117 pregnancies), use of 5 mg folic acid supplements rose only from 3.6% to 5.0% between 2015 and 2020. Most folic acid use started after conception [[Cassinelli2026]].
- The 2023 international PCOS guideline:
  - recognises the metabolic and cardiovascular features of PCOS and a high risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes
  - recommends a healthy lifestyle with "awareness and consideration of weight stigma"
  - calls for cheaper and safer fertility management [[Teede2024PCOS]]
- ACOG recommends that obesity management begin before pregnancy and continue through the postpartum period [[ACOG2021PB230]].
- In 670 Swedish pregnancies after bariatric surgery, compared with matched controls:
  - gestational diabetes was lower: 1.9% vs 6.8% (OR 0.25)
  - large-for-gestational-age babies were fewer: 8.6% vs 22.4%
  - small-for-gestational-age babies were more common: 15.6% vs 7.6% (OR 2.20)
  - stillbirth or neonatal death was 1.7% vs 0.7%, a non-significant difference (OR 2.39; 95% CI 0.98-5.85) [[Johansson2015NEJM]]

### stigma · What weight stigma is and how common it is
- The 2020 joint international consensus defines weight stigma as "social devaluation and denigration of individuals because of their excess body weight". The consensus was signed by representatives of ten scientific organisations through a modified Delphi process [[Rubino2020Stigma]].
- It lists weight-based stereotypes as being seen as lazy, gluttonous, lacking willpower, incompetent, unmotivated, non-compliant, and personally to blame [[Rubino2020Stigma]].
- The consensus was unanimous that weight stigma causes physical and psychological harm and that people who experience it are less likely to seek and receive adequate care [[Rubino2020Stigma]].
- The consensus agreed (above 90%) that the common belief that obesity is a choice, reversible by eating less and exercising more, misleads policy and undermines access to treatment [[Rubino2020Stigma]].
- All signatories agreed that weight stigma should not be tolerated in education, health care or public policy, and that clear policies should prohibit weight-based discrimination [[Rubino2020Stigma]].
- Among adults with obesity, 19-42% report weight discrimination. Rates are higher with higher BMI and in women [[Rubino2020Stigma]].
- About 40-50% of US adults with overweight or obesity internalise weight bias, and about 20% do so at high levels [[Rubino2020Stigma]].
- In a US national sample (MIDUS, 1995-96), weight or height discrimination was reported by 5% of men and 10% of women overall, but by 40% of adults with BMI 35 or more [[Puhl2008Discrimination]].
- Women with BMI 30-35 were three times as likely as men of similar weight to report this discrimination [[Puhl2008Discrimination]].
- Some forms of weight discrimination are more common than discrimination based on race or ethnicity [[Tomiyama2018,Puhl2008Discrimination]].

### stigma · Effects on health
- Weight discrimination was associated with nearly 60% higher mortality: HR 1.57 in the Health and Retirement Study (n=13,692) and HR 1.59 in MIDUS (n=5079). This was not explained by common physical or psychological risk factors [[Sutin2015]].
- In 33 studies of adults, weight stigma was associated with:
  - higher obesity and diabetes risk
  - higher cortisol, oxidative stress and CRP
  - more disordered eating, depression and anxiety
  - lower self-esteem [[Wu2018]]
- Across 74 studies, internalised weight bias showed strong negative relationships with mental health. Links with physical health were less consistent [[Pearl2018WBI]].
- Weight stigma raises the risk of obesity through several pathways and harms health over and above BMI (Early evidence) [[Tomiyama2018]].
- Stigma is not a useful public-health tool. It threatens health, creates disparities and interferes with obesity treatment (Established) [[Puhl2010AJPH,Rubino2020Stigma]].

### stigma · Bias in health care and care avoidance
- Among 2284 doctors who took the Weight Implicit Association Test, implicit anti-fat bias was strong (Cohen's d = 0.93), similar to the general public (d = 1.0) [[Sabin2012]].
- Many clinicians hold negative attitudes toward people with obesity. These attitudes affect judgement, behaviour and decisions, and can lower the quality of care despite good intentions [[Phelan2015]].
- Expecting poor treatment leads to stress, avoiding care, mistrust and poorer adherence [[Phelan2015]].
- In 13,996 adults in a weight-management programme across six countries, 56-61% reported having experienced weight stigma. Two-thirds of these reported stigma from doctors [[Puhl2021SixCountries]].
- In that study, higher internalised weight bias went with more avoidance of care, fewer routine check-ups and lower perceived quality of care, independent of BMI [[Puhl2021SixCountries]].
- In a US survey (n=1064), 19% would avoid future appointments and 21% would seek a new doctor if they felt stigmatised about their weight by their doctor [[Puhl2013Language]].

### stigma · Language and imagery
- People-first language means "people with obesity" rather than "obese people", so that the disease does not define the person [[Kyle2014,Puhl2020Words]].
- A systematic review of 33 studies found that neutral terms such as "weight" or "unhealthy weight" are preferred. "Obese" and "fat" were least acceptable, especially in conversations with providers, though preferences vary between people [[Puhl2020Words]].
- In a US national survey, "weight" and "unhealthy weight" were rated the most desirable terms. "Morbidly obese", "fat" and "obese" were rated the most undesirable, stigmatising and blaming terms for providers to use [[Puhl2013Language]].
- In 371 online news videos about obesity, 65% of adults and 77% of youth with higher weight were shown in a negative, stigmatising way. Common framings were headless images, isolated body parts, rear views, unhealthy eating and ill-fitting clothes [[Puhl2013Headless]].
- In three experiments (n=1251), stereotyped images in news stories increased anti-fat attitudes and social distance. Positive, non-stereotyped images improved attitudes [[Puhl2013Media]].
- The consensus calls on media to produce "fair, accurate, and non-stigmatizing portrayals of obesity" (unanimous) [[Rubino2020Stigma]].

### living · Mood, binge eating and quality of life
- The link between obesity and depression runs both ways. Obesity at baseline raised the odds of later depression (OR 1.55; 95% CI 1.22-1.98), and depression raised the odds of later obesity (OR 1.58; 95% CI 1.33-1.87) [[Luppino2010]].
- Lifetime prevalence of binge eating disorder in the US NCS-R was 3.5% in women and 2.0% in men. BED was associated with current severe obesity (BMI ≥40), and only a minority ever sought treatment [[Hudson2007]].
- The 7-item Binge-Eating Disorder Screener (BEDS-7) is a brief patient-reported tool to flag possible BED for full evaluation. In development it had 100% sensitivity and 38.7% specificity, so it screens rather than diagnoses [[Herman2016BEDS7]].
- The IWQOL-Lite is a 31-item weight-specific quality-of-life questionnaire with five scales: Physical Function, Self-Esteem, Sexual Life, Public Distress and Work. Reliability is 0.90-0.94 for the scales and 0.96 for the total [[Kolotkin2001]].
- In STEP TEENS, semaglutide improved the IWQOL-Kids total score, mainly through the physical comfort domain [[Weghuber2022STEPTEENS]].
- Weight stigma and internalised weight bias are linked to depression, anxiety and disordered eating. Addressing them is part of mental-health care for people living with obesity (Established) [[Wu2018,Pearl2018WBI]].

### living · Maintenance and regain as biology
- After an average 13.5 kg loss on a very-low-energy diet, levels of leptin, peptide YY, cholecystokinin, insulin, ghrelin, GIP and pancreatic polypeptide, along with hunger, had still not returned to baseline one year later. These changes favour regain [[Sumithran2011]].
- In adolescents, BMI rose again after liraglutide was stopped (BMI SDS +0.15 vs placebo) and slightly after semaglutide was stopped (-16.1% at week 68 to -13.2% at week 75). This is consistent with obesity as a chronic, relapsing disease [[Kelly2020Liraglutide,Weghuber2022STEPTEENS]].
- In the US National Weight Control Registry (2886 adults who had lost at least 13.6 kg and kept it off for a year), mean loss was 31.3 kg at entry, 23.8 kg at 5 years and 23.1 kg at 10 years. More than 87% were still maintaining at least a 10% loss [[Thomas2014NWCR]].
- In the registry, less physical activity, less dietary restraint, less frequent self-weighing and more disinhibition were associated with more regain. The registry is a self-selected group of successful maintainers, not a typical population [[Thomas2014NWCR]].

### living · Work, daily life and safety
- Weight discrimination is common at work. Institutional forms (such as in employment) and interpersonal forms (such as name-calling) were sometimes more common than gender or race discrimination [[Puhl2008Discrimination]].
- In US fatal crashes 2003-2009, drivers of normal weight had 67% higher odds of wearing a seatbelt than drivers with the highest BMI (OR 1.67). Poor seatbelt fit is one practical reason that seatbelt extenders matter [[Jehle2014]].
- Kiosk blood pressure machines usually have a single cuff size that is too small for most US adults [[Muntner2019]].

### nursing · Blood pressure measurement
- Measure arm circumference at the midpoint between the acromion and olecranon. Choose a cuff whose bladder length is 75%-100% and width 37%-50% of the arm circumference (about 2:1), placed on bare skin [[Muntner2019]].
- The AHA lists illustrative cuff sizes by arm circumference:
  - small adult: 22-26 cm (bladder 12×22 cm)
  - adult: 27-34 cm (16×30)
  - large adult: 35-44 cm (16×36)
  - extra-large adult: 45-52 cm (16×42) [[Muntner2019]]
- A tronco-conical (cone-shaped) cuff may suit some people with obesity. A thigh cuff can be used if arm circumference exceeds the largest arm cuff available [[Muntner2019]].
- A cuff that is too small overestimates blood pressure; this is a common error in people with larger arms (Established) [[Muntner2019]].
- Children with obesity (BMI ≥95th percentile) should have blood pressure measured at every health care encounter [[Muntner2019]].

### nursing · Safe patient handling and equipment
- The Revised NIOSH Lifting Equation gives a recommended maximum of 35 lb (about 16 kg) for patient-handling tasks. When the weight to be lifted exceeds this, assistive devices should be used [[Waters2007]].
- In a review of incidents reported to the UK National Patient Safety Agency, many obesity-related incidents involved a lack of suitable equipment. Hospitals should invest in appropriately rated beds, operating tables, trolleys and lifting aids [[Nightingale2015]].
- The consensus statement says appropriate infrastructure for people with obesity, including severe obesity, should be a standard requirement for accrediting medical facilities (unanimous) [[Rubino2020Stigma]].
- For people with a high BMI who are immobile, more staff are needed to reposition them and prevent pressure injuries [[Nightingale2015]].

### nursing · Skin: pressure injuries and intertrigo
- In a dose-response meta-analysis (11 studies, 31,389 hospitalised adults), the relationship between BMI and pressure injury risk was J-shaped. Compared with normal weight, the odds ratios were:
  - underweight: 1.70
  - obesity: 1.12
  - morbid obesity: 1.70 [[Jia2024]]
- In ICU patients, pressure ulcer incidence by weight group was:
  - underweight: 8.6%
  - normal weight: 5.5%
  - obesity: 2.8%
  - extreme obesity: 9.9%
- Adding BMI to the Braden scale did not meaningfully improve prediction (AUC 0.71 vs 0.68) [[Hyun2014]].
- Obesity changes skin barrier function, sweat and sebum production, lymphatic flow, collagen and wound healing. It is linked to intertrigo, skin infections, acanthosis nigricans, lymphoedema, hidradenitis suppurativa and cellulitis [[Yosipovitch2007]].
- A systematic review of intertrigo in large skin folds (68 studies) found no evidence at all on prevention and no firm evidence on any treatment. Even patients on placebo improved [[Mistiaen2010]].

### nursing · Airway and positioning
- Using the intubation difficulty scale, difficult intubation occurred in 15.5% of patients with BMI ≥35 vs 2.2% of lean patients. Mallampati III-IV was the only independent risk factor [[Juvin2003]].
- In 100 patients with BMI over 40, neither BMI nor weight predicted difficult intubation, but a large neck circumference and Mallampati score ≥3 did [[Brodsky2002]].
- A neck circumference over 60 cm is associated with a 35% probability of difficult laryngoscopy [[Nightingale2015]].
- The ramped position (tragus of the ear level with the sternum) improves the laryngoscopy view and is recommended as the default for induction [[Nightingale2015]].
- After surgery, patients should be managed sitting or with 45° head-up tilt [[Nightingale2015]].
- STOP-BANG is the best-validated sleep apnoea screen in people with obesity. A score of 5 or more suggests significant sleep-disordered breathing [[Nightingale2015]].

### nursing · Drug dosing by body-size descriptor
- No single size descriptor works for all drugs. Drug volume of distribution guides the loading dose and clearance guides the maintenance dose, and both change with obesity in drug-specific ways [[Hanley2010]].
- The AAGBI/SOBA guideline defines four weight terms:
  - total body weight (TBW): actual weight
  - ideal body weight (IBW): height (cm) minus 105 (women) or minus 100 (men), as given in the guideline
  - lean body weight (LBW): weight without fat
  - adjusted body weight (ABW): IBW + 0.4 × (TBW − IBW) [[Nightingale2015]]
- Lean body weight rarely exceeds 100 kg in men or 70 kg in women, whatever the total body weight [[Nightingale2015]].
- The AAGBI/SOBA suggested initial dosing scalars, quoted as published:
  - lean body weight for induction propofol, fentanyl and rocuronium
  - adjusted body weight for propofol infusion, antibiotics and low-molecular-weight heparin
  - ideal body weight for tidal volume and tracheal tube size [[Nightingale2015]]
- The guideline advises avoiding intramuscular drug administration because of unpredictable pharmacokinetics [[Nightingale2015]].

### nursing · VTE prophylaxis and enoxaparin (published regimens, not instructions)
- Lovenox label: "The safety and efficacy of prophylactic doses of Lovenox in obese patients (BMI >30 kg/m2) has not been fully determined and there is no consensus for dose adjustment" [[FDA-Lovenox]].
- The AAGBI/SOBA weight-banded enoxaparin prophylaxis schedule:
  - under 50 kg: 20 mg once daily
  - 50-100 kg: 40 mg once daily
  - 100-150 kg: 40 mg twice daily
  - over 150 kg: 60 mg twice daily [[Nightingale2015]]
- In 3928 inpatients weighing over 100 kg with BMI ≥40, high-dose prophylaxis (enoxaparin 40 mg twice daily or heparin 7500 units three times daily) was compared with standard doses:
  - VTE was 0.77% vs 1.48% (OR 0.52; 95% CI 0.27-1.00)
  - bleeding did not increase (OR 0.84) [[Wang2014]]
- In 223 people having gastric bypass, a BMI-stratified regimen gave a VTE rate of 0.45%, and 74% reached target anti-factor Xa levels. The regimen was enoxaparin 40 mg (BMI ≤50) or 60 mg (BMI >50) every 12 hours in hospital, then once daily for 10 days [[Borkgren2008]].
- The guideline recommends VTE prophylaxis for all people with obesity having anything more than minor surgery [[Nightingale2015]].

### nursing · Intramuscular injections
- Deltoid fat pad thickness, measured by ultrasound in 220 adults, was 11.7 mm in women vs 8.3 mm in men. A standard 16 mm (5/8 in) needle would not have reached 5 mm into muscle in 48.4% of women and 17% of men [[Poland1997]].
- Poland 1997 proposed deltoid needle lengths, quoted as published:
  - men 59-118 kg: 25 mm (1 in)
  - women under 60 kg: 16 mm
  - women 60-90 kg: 25 mm
  - women over 90 kg: 38 mm (1.5 in) [[Poland1997]]
- In 100 adults with pelvic CT, ventrogluteal depth exceeded 35 mm (the length of a green needle) in 12. Dorsogluteal depth exceeded 35 mm in 43 and exceeded 25 mm (blue needle) in 72. Standard needles often fail to reach gluteal muscle [[Nisbet2006]].

### nursing · After bariatric surgery: leak warning signs and team care
- In 210 patients after laparoscopic gastric bypass, 9 leaks occurred (4.3%). Respiratory distress and a heart rate above 120 beats per minute were the most sensitive signs [[Hamilton2003]].
- Routine contrast swallow detected only 2 of those 9 leaks (22%) [[Hamilton2003]].
- Perioperative bariatric guidelines (AACE/TOS/ASMBS/OMA/ASA 2019 update) cover enhanced recovery protocols. They state that a team approach to perioperative care is mandatory, with attention to nutritional and metabolic issues [[Mechanick2019]].
- Rhabdomyolysis is a rare but serious complication after surgery in people with high BMI. It should be considered if there is deep tissue pain after surgery, classically in the buttocks [[Nightingale2015]].
- Pre-operative discussion should cover the importance of thromboprophylaxis and early mobilisation, and remind patients to bring their own CPAP machine [[Nightingale2015]].

### nursing · Patient teaching for weekly GLP-1-based injections (label statements, not instructions)
- Wegovy is injected subcutaneously in the abdomen, thigh or upper arm. If 2 or more consecutive doses are missed, the label says to reinitiate dose escalation at a lower dosage [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Wegovy storage:
  - single-dose pens are refrigerated at 2-8 °C and may be kept at 8-30 °C for up to 28 days
  - FlexTouch pens can be stored for 56 days after first use [[FDA-Wegovy]]
- Zepbound injection sites and missed doses:
  - inject in the abdomen or thigh, or another person may inject in the back of the upper arm
  - a missed dose is given within 4 days (96 hours); if more than 4 days have passed, skip it [[FDA-Zepbound]]
- Zepbound single-dose pens are refrigerated and may be kept unrefrigerated (up to 30 °C) for a total of 21 days [[FDA-Zepbound]].
- Saxenda is a daily injection in the abdomen, thigh or upper arm. A missed dose is not made up with an extra or larger dose [[FDA-Saxenda]].
- Wegovy, Zepbound and Saxenda all carry a boxed warning on the risk of thyroid C-cell tumours, seen in rodents. Human relevance has not been determined [[FDA-Wegovy,FDA-Zepbound,FDA-Saxenda]].

### nursing · Communication without stigma: the 5As
- The modified 5As for obesity counselling are:
  - Ask permission to discuss weight, without judgement.
  - Assess BMI, waist and obesity stage, and look at drivers and complications.
  - Advise on health risks, the benefits of modest loss and treatment options.
  - Agree on realistic expectations and a plan.
  - Assist with barriers, resources, referral and follow-up [[Vallis2013]]
- The AAP recommends motivational interviewing and empathetic, empowering counselling, and asks clinicians to model non-biased behaviour and language [[Pont2017AAP,Orr2026]].
- Stigma reduction should target the behaviour and attitudes of those who stigmatise, starting in health-professional training, rather than the people who experience stigma [[Tomiyama2018,Rubino2020Stigma]].

### Exam hooks
- Pediatric obesity = BMI ≥95th percentile. Class 2 = ≥120% of the 95th percentile (or BMI ≥35). Class 3 = ≥140% of the 95th percentile (or BMI ≥40) [[Ogden2023,Hampl2023AAP]].
- The CDC extended charts (Dec 2022) add the 98th, 99th, 99.9th and 99.99th percentiles and reach a BMI of 60 [[Ogden2023]].
- WHO 2007: +1 SD at age 19 ≈ BMI 25, and +2 SD ≈ BMI 29.7 [[deOnis2007]].
- AAP 2023 has no "watchful waiting". IHBLT is ≥26 hours over 3-12 months. Pharmacotherapy is offered from age 12, and surgery referral for severe obesity from age 13 [[Orr2026,Cuda2024OMA,Hampl2023AAP]].
- STEP TEENS BMI change at 68 weeks: -16.1% (semaglutide) vs +0.6% (placebo) [[Weghuber2022STEPTEENS]].
- About 80% of adolescents with obesity still have it as adults, but 70% of adults with obesity did not have it as children [[Simmonds2016]].
- Teen-LABS at 5 years: type 2 diabetes remission was 86% in adolescents vs 53% in adults [[Inge2019TeenLABS]].
- IOM 2009 total gain for pre-pregnancy BMI ≥30 is 5-9 kg [[Rasmussen2009IOM,Goldstein2017]].
- Maternal obesity and neural tube defects: OR 1.87. Gastroschisis is less common (OR 0.17) [[Stothard2009]].
- Pre-eclampsia risk roughly doubles with each 5-7 kg/m² rise in pre-pregnancy BMI [[OBrien2003]].
- The Wegovy label says to stop at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy [[FDA-Wegovy]].
- Zepbound can reduce oral contraceptive efficacy. The label advises a non-oral method or a barrier method for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose increase [[FDA-Zepbound]].
- Weight discrimination is associated with about 60% higher mortality (HR 1.57-1.59) [[Sutin2015]].
- The 2019 AHA statement gives cuff bladder length as 75%-100% and width as 37%-50% of arm circumference [[Muntner2019]].
- NIOSH recommends a 35 lb limit for manual patient handling [[Waters2007]].
- A heart rate above 120 after gastric bypass is a leak warning sign. Contrast studies missed 78% of leaks [[Hamilton2003]].
- Adjusted body weight = IBW + 0.4 × (TBW − IBW) [[Nightingale2015]].
- Difficult intubation occurred in 15.5% of patients with BMI ≥35 vs 2.2% of lean patients. Neck circumference and Mallampati score predict difficulty better than BMI [[Juvin2003,Brodsky2002]].
- 19% of people said they would avoid future appointments if their doctor stigmatised their weight [[Puhl2013Language]].

### In plain words
- Obesity is a long-term health condition. Genes, body chemistry, surroundings and life circumstances all play a part. It is not a lack of willpower.
- For children, doctors use growth charts that compare a child with others of the same age and sex. A chart is a tool, not a judgement, and talking about healthy habits for the whole family matters more than talking about weight.
- Children who are teased about weight do not become healthier. Teasing makes things harder. Families, schools and clinics can stop it.
- Some teenagers can now be offered medicines, or surgery for very high weight. These decisions are made with a specialist team and the family.
- Before pregnancy, ask your clinician about folic acid and about any weight medicines. Some weight medicines need to be stopped two months before trying to get pregnant.
- Pregnancy at a higher weight carries some extra risks, such as diabetes in pregnancy and high blood pressure. Regular check-ups help catch these early.
- If eating or weight causes you distress, or you restrict, binge or purge, talk to a clinician or an eating-disorder service. Help works.
- Low mood and higher weight can feed each other. Asking for help with mood is part of looking after your health.
- After weight loss, the body raises hunger signals for a long time. Regain is biology, not failure.
- You can ask your nurse for a larger blood pressure cuff. A small cuff can give a falsely high reading.
- You can ask for equipment that fits you: a wider chair, a stronger bed, a seatbelt extender. Good care includes the right equipment.
- If you use a weekly injection pen, follow the leaflet and your clinician's advice. Do not start, stop or change a medicine without a clinician.
- After weight-loss surgery, tell the team at once if you have a racing heart, trouble breathing or new belly pain.
- You have the right to respectful words. "A person with obesity" or "higher weight" puts the person first.

## Open questions and controversies
- Severe obesity in young children: the AAP and CDC use 120%/140% of the 95th percentile [[Hampl2023AAP,Ogden2023]]. The extended 98th percentile classifies more children aged 2-5 as severe (4.5% vs 2.5%), and whether risk is equal along the age span is unknown [[Ogden2023]]. Status: open.
- Medicines in adolescents: STEP TEENS and the liraglutide and phentermine/topiramate trials show large short-term BMI effects [[Weghuber2022STEPTEENS,Kelly2020Liraglutide,Kelly2022PHENTPM]]. Regain after stopping, and how long treatment should last in a growing child, are unresolved [[Kelly2024JAMA]]. Status: long-term data needed.
- Treatment and eating disorders: some fear that weight treatment in youth triggers eating disorders. A meta-analysis found that structured, supervised programmes reduced ED symptoms on average [[Jebeile2019]]. Individual risk still needs screening. Status: reassuring on average, with vigilance advised.
- Gestational weight gain in obesity: IOM advises a total gain of 5-9 kg [[Rasmussen2009IOM]]. The LifeCycle IPD analysis found the lowest risk with 0 to under 6 kg, or even small losses, in grades 2-3, but with limited predictive value [[Voerman2019LifeCycle]]. Status: no change to IOM ranges.
- Pregnancy after bariatric surgery: less gestational diabetes and fewer large babies, but more small-for-gestational-age babies and a possible rise in stillbirth or neonatal death (non-significant) [[Johansson2015NEJM]]. Status: counselling and nutritional monitoring.
- Enoxaparin dose in high BMI: the label says there is no consensus [[FDA-Lovenox]]. Weight-banded or BMI-stratified regimens appear to lower VTE without more bleeding in observational data [[Wang2014,Borkgren2008,Nightingale2015]]. Status: no definitive RCT.
- Intertrigo care: products are used widely, but a systematic review found no evidence on prevention and no firm evidence on treatment [[Mistiaen2010]]. Status: evidence gap.
- Pressure injury and BMI: risk is J-shaped in pooled hospital data [[Jia2024]], but one ICU cohort found the lowest incidence in the obesity group [[Hyun2014]]. Status: mixed.
- Terminology: "people with obesity" is the professional standard [[Kyle2014]]. Many youth and adults prefer "weight" or "healthy weight", and some prefer culturally specific words, so asking each person works best [[Puhl2020Words,Puhl2022Terms]]. Status: individualise.
