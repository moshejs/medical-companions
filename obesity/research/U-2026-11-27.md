## Findings

### children · WHO guidelines on managing obesity in children and adolescents
- The WHO developed two guidelines, one for children (younger than 10 years) and one for adolescents (10 to 19 years), on integrated management of obesity delivered through primary health care by trained multiprofessional teams. [[GarciaCasal2026WHO]]
- WHO strongly recommends structured dietary, physical activity and behaviour-change interventions, alone or as part of multimodal interventions, for both age groups. Digital health interventions may be considered under strict parental supervision. [[GarciaCasal2026WHO]]
- Pharmacological interventions and bariatric surgery are not recommended for children. For adolescents they may be considered only after complete pubertal development, final adult height and psychological stability, and are to be delivered in a specialised multidisciplinary setting with close monitoring for adverse events. [[GarciaCasal2026WHO]]
- This is more cautious than the AAP 2023 guideline, which offers pharmacotherapy from age 12 and surgical evaluation from age 13 with severe obesity. [[Hampl2023AAP,Orr2026]]

### pregnancy · ACOG practice advisory on GLP-1 receptor agonists (October 2026)
- Prepregnancy: ACOG says clinicians should advise patients about the current lack of sufficient safety data on GLP-1 receptor agonist use in pregnancy and to consider discontinuation 1 to 2 months before conception, and should encourage lifestyle interventions and weight surveillance after stopping. [[ACOG2026GLP1]]
- Pregnancy: clinicians should advise against the use of GLP-1 receptor agonists during pregnancy. [[ACOG2026GLP1]]
- Lactation: clinicians may use shared decision-making on a postpartum plan that could include a GLP-1 receptor agonist, with close surveillance of the infant's weight gain; ACOG recommends against use when breastfeeding preterm infants and neonates. [[ACOG2026GLP1]]
- The advisory also says patients who can become pregnant should be counselled about effective contraception while using these medicines, because weight loss may improve fertility. [[ACOG2026GLP1]]
- The Wegovy label states at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy; the advisory's 1 to 2 months covers the class. [[FDA-Wegovy,ACOG2026GLP1]]

### Exam hooks
- WHO 2026: no medicines or surgery for children under 10; in adolescents only after puberty, adult height and psychological stability. AAP 2023: medicines offered from 12. [[GarciaCasal2026WHO,Hampl2023AAP]]
- ACOG, October 2026: consider stopping GLP-1 receptor agonists 1 to 2 months before conception; advise against use in pregnancy. [[ACOG2026GLP1]]

### In plain words
- The World Health Organization says weight medicines and surgery are not for young children, and for teenagers only once they have finished growing and are doing well emotionally. American children's doctors allow medicines from age 12. Guidelines differ.
- Doctors who look after pregnancy advise against GLP-1 medicines during pregnancy, and suggest thinking about stopping them one to two months before trying to conceive.

## Open questions and controversies
- When should medicines or surgery be offered to adolescents? AAP offers medicines from 12 and surgical evaluation from 13 [[Hampl2023AAP]]; WHO waits for completed puberty, adult height and psychological stability [[GarciaCasal2026WHO]]. Status: open.
- Does stopping a GLP-1 medicine before pregnancy cause harm through weight regain? The ACOG advisory describes cohort studies with more gestational weight gain after prepregnancy use and suggests rebound after stopping as one explanation [[ACOG2026GLP1]]. Status: open.
