## Findings

### frontier · Preserving muscle during weight loss: trevogrumab and garetosmab (COURAGE)
- COURAGE was a two-part, phase 2, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial at 60 US sites in adults with BMI ≥ 30 kg/m². 975 participants were enrolled. Everyone received semaglutide titrated to 2.4 mg weekly. Trevogrumab is an antibody against GDF8 (myostatin); garetosmab is an antibody against activin A. (Early evidence) [[Mosenzon2026COURAGE]]
- Part A (n = 605; 26 weeks): lean mass changed −6.6% (95% CI −7.4 to −5.7) with semaglutide plus placebo. The estimated treatment difference was +3.1% (1.9 to 4.3) with trevogrumab 200 mg, +2.7% (1.5 to 4.0) with trevogrumab 400 mg, and +4.5% (3.1 to 5.9) with trevogrumab 400 mg plus garetosmab 10 mg/kg. (Early evidence) [[Mosenzon2026COURAGE]]
- Part B (n = 376; 52 weeks): lean mass changed −7.3% (−8.8 to −5.8) with semaglutide plus placebo. The difference was +1.5% (−0.3 to 3.3) with trevogrumab 25 mg and +3.1% (1.3 to 4.9) with trevogrumab 75 mg. (Early evidence) [[Mosenzon2026COURAGE]]
- Fat mass in part A changed −15.6% with semaglutide plus placebo; the extra fat loss was significant with trevogrumab 400 mg (−3.4%) and with the triple combination (−11.7%). Body weight changed −10.6% with semaglutide plus placebo, and only the triple combination lost significantly more (−2.7 points). [[Mosenzon2026COURAGE]]
- Safety: semaglutide plus trevogrumab had a safety profile similar to semaglutide plus placebo. The triple combination had serious adverse events in 14 of 149 (9%), treatment discontinuation in 48 of 149 (32%) and muscle spasms in 61 of 149 (41%), versus 7 of 151 (5%) with semaglutide plus placebo. Two deaths occurred, both in the triple-combination group; the authors did not identify a causal link. [[Mosenzon2026COURAGE]]
- The authors state that further study is needed to learn whether better body composition preserves physical function or metabolic health. This matches the site's existing open question [[Heymsfield2026Bimagrumab,Hjelmesaeth2026STEPUPMuscle]].

### frontier · Setmelanotide outside its labelled groups: EMANATE registry results
- EMANATE was a phase 3, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial made of four independent sub-studies in people aged 6 to 65 with obesity and a heterozygous POMC or PCSK1 variant, a heterozygous LEPR variant, an NCOA1 (SRC1) variant, or an SH2B1 variant or 16p11.2 deletion. 296 were enrolled; results were first posted on ClinicalTrials.gov on 2026-11-12. (Early evidence; registry results, no peer-reviewed paper found) [[NCT05093634]]
- Primary outcome, percent change in BMI at week 52 (least-squares mean difference, setmelanotide minus placebo, modified intention to treat): POMC/PCSK1 −4.29 (95% CI −10.11 to 1.52; p = 0.147; n = 38 vs 40); LEPR −3.57 (−90.22 to 83.07; p = 0.935; n = 11 vs 12); SRC1 −4.05 (−9.22 to 1.12; p = 0.125; n = 36 vs 37); SH2B1 −1.73 (−6.07 to 2.61; p = 0.433; n = 61 vs 60). None was statistically significant. [[NCT05093634]]
- About half of participants completed the 52-week treatment period (152 of 296 by the participant-flow table). [[NCT05093634]]
- Context: the label covers POMC, PCSK1 or LEPR deficiency (confirmed genetically), Bardet-Biedl syndrome and acquired hypothalamic obesity, and states the drug is not indicated for general obesity [[FDA-Imcivree]]. EMANATE does not change the label; it shows that the benefit seen in those groups did not extend to these wider variant groups.

### frontier · How common GLP-1 and compounded GLP-1 use is in the US
- CDC Rapid Surveys System data, July to August 2025: approximately one in 10 US adults reported taking a GLP-1 medication in the past year. Among GLP-1 users, approximately one in five reported taking a compounded version, and approximately two in five modified their use from the way it was prescribed, often citing cost and availability. Modification was noted particularly among younger adults. [[Pierce2026MMWR]]
- The abstract gives these figures as approximate fractions; exact percentages and confidence intervals are in the full text, which could not be reached (see Provenance).

### Exam hooks
- Blocking myostatin (GDF8) with trevogrumab reduced semaglutide-related lean mass loss by about 3 percentage points in phase 2; adding activin A blockade (garetosmab) spared more lean mass but was poorly tolerated. [[Mosenzon2026COURAGE]]
- Setmelanotide did not significantly lower BMI versus placebo in heterozygous POMC/PCSK1 or LEPR carriers or in SRC1 or SH2B1 variant carriers (EMANATE, registry results). [[NCT05093634]]

### In plain words
- A new kind of medicine helped people keep a little more muscle while losing weight on semaglutide. A stronger two-antibody mix kept more muscle but caused more problems. Nobody yet knows if keeping that muscle makes people stronger or healthier.
- Setmelanotide helps people with a few rare, clear-cut gene conditions. A trial in people with milder or different gene changes did not show a clear benefit.
- About 1 in 10 US adults used a GLP-1 medicine in a year, and about 1 in 5 of them used a compounded version, which the FDA does not review.

## Open questions and controversies
- Does sparing lean mass improve strength, falls or long-term health? Still untested; COURAGE measured body composition, not function [[Mosenzon2026COURAGE]]. Status: open.
- Is activin A blockade usable alongside GLP-1 medicines? The trevogrumab plus garetosmab arm had unfavourable safety and tolerability [[Mosenzon2026COURAGE]]; bimagrumab, which blocks activin type II receptors, reported muscle spasms as a common event [[Heymsfield2026Bimagrumab]]. Status: open.
