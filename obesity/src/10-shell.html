Obesity Companion · animated and cited

Obesity, explained

Obesity is a chronic, relapsing disease of excess body fat with genetic, biological, environmental and social causes, not a failure of willpower [[Rubino2025Commission,Bray2017WOF]]. In 2022 more than a billion people were living with it [[NCDRisC2024]]. This guide follows it in moving pictures you can scrub, from the gut-brain loop that sets appetite and the biology of regain to complications, GLP-1 medicines and surgery, with every claim cited.

Every animation can be driven from the keyboard: Tab to its timeline, then arrow keys to scrub and Enter to play or pause.

Schematic, not to scale.

A chronic disease, not a choiceThis guide treats obesity as a chronic, relapsing disease shaped by genes, biology, environment and social circumstances, never as a failure of willpower. It uses person-first language: people with obesity, not "the obese".

For students

Every module has an Exam hook box and a citation trail. BMI, waist-to-height and Edmonton staging tools are teaching aids, worked exactly as published. Finish with flashcards, memory aids and a quiz.

For people living with obesity, families and caregivers

Look for the In plain words boxes. Trial results are averages for groups, not predictions or targets for one person. The guide reports what guidelines, trials and drug labels say, not what you should eat, take, stop or change: bring questions to your own care team.

If eating or weight is distressingIf eating or weight causes distress, or someone restricts, binges or purges, talk to a clinician or an eating-disorder service.

How sure is each claim?

Research claims carry a label: Established Early evidence Hypothesis. Small numbers link to the Sources list.
