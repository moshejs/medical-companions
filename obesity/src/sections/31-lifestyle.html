Diet, activity & behaviour

A comprehensive lifestyle intervention has three parts: a moderately reduced-calorie diet, more physical activity, and behavioural strategies to help people stick to both. [[Jensen2014Guideline]] Here is what the big trials found, as group averages over months and years.

If eating or weight is distressingIf eating or weight causes distress, or someone restricts, binges, purges or exercises compulsively, talk to a clinician or an eating-disorder service before changing how they eat. Some diet trials excluded people with eating disorders, so their results may not apply.

Numbers, not instructionsCalorie levels, session counts and activity minutes on this page are quoted as the trials and guidelines published them; they are not targets for anyone. Weight changes are trial averages: individual results vary widely and cannot be predicted. [[Jensen2014Guideline]]

Activity: small for weight, large for health

Aerobic exercise alone−1.6 kg

At 6 months in 14 RCTs (−1.7 kg at 12 months); the authors called it "not an effective weight loss therapy" on its own. [[Thorogood2011]] Exercise without weight loss still lowered blood pressure, triglycerides and fasting glucose. [[Shaw2006Exercise]]

Guidelines, as published150–300 min

Weekly moderate-intensity aerobic activity, or 75 to 150 minutes vigorous, plus muscle strengthening on 2 or more days; the least active gain most from modest increases. [[Piercy2018]]

Keeping it off>250 min

Per week went with better maintenance in observational studies, but no well-designed RCT shows it prevents regain. [[Donnelly2009ACSM]] Women who kept off ≥10% reported about 275 min a week. [[Jakicic2008]]

Protecting muscle−5% vs −2%

Lean mass lost with aerobic vs resistance training during about 9% weight loss in older adults; function improved most with combined training. [[Villareal2017]] With tirzepatide, resistance exercise reduced lean loss by 1.09 kg. Early evidence [[Green2026Tirzepatide]]

Everyday movement2 h

More sitting a day in sedentary people with mild obesity than in lean people, unchanged by weight loss or gain, which suggests posture is partly biologically set. Early evidence [[Levine2005]] Non-exercise movement explained a 10-fold range in fat gain with overfeeding. [[Levine1999]]

Weight is not the only outcome

Diabetes Prevention Program58%

Lower diabetes incidence with the lifestyle programme (goals ≥7% weight loss, ≥150 min/week activity) vs placebo over 2.8 years; 31% with metformin. [[Knowler2002DPP,DPPRG2002Lifestyle]] Still 27% lower at 15 years. [[DPPRG2015]] More in Obesity & type 2 diabetes.

Look AHEAD heart outcomesHR 0.95

No difference in the primary cardiovascular outcome (95% CI 0.83 to 1.09); stopped for futility at a median 9.6 years. [[LookAHEAD2013]] People who lost ≥10% in year 1 had fewer events in a post-hoc, non-randomised analysis. Early evidence [[LookAHEAD2016LDE]]

Benefits that outlast the weight5 years

Across 124 trials, behavioural programmes still lowered cholesterol ratio and HbA1c 5 years after they ended, despite regain, though benefits dwindled as weight returned. [[HartmannBoyce2023]]

Regain is not failureProgrammes are followed by a plateau at about 6 months and gradual regain; reviews attribute this to biological, behavioural and environmental factors and say long-term clinical care is needed. [[Hall2018Maintenance,Franz2007]] Why it happens is in Why lost weight comes back. Binge-eating disorder is a treatable condition; see Living with obesity.

Exam hookDPP: lifestyle −58%, metformin −31% diabetes incidence; NNT over 3 years 6.9 and 13.9. [[Knowler2002DPP]] Look AHEAD: 8.6% vs 0.7% loss at 1 year, 6.0% vs 3.5% at the end, cardiovascular HR 0.95. [[LookAHEAD2007,LookAHEAD2013]]
In plain wordsProgrammes with regular support work better than advice alone, and support can be in person, by phone or online. [[Appel2011POWER,Jensen2014Guideline]] All these numbers are averages; weight is only one part of health. [[Jensen2014Guideline]]
