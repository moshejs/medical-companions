People behind the ideas

1832

Adolphe Quetelet

A Belgian statistician who, from growth studies, concluded that outside the growth spurts of infancy and puberty "the weight increases as the square of the height". He also organised the first International Statistical Congress in 1853, which began work on a uniform naming of causes of death, a forerunner of the International Classification of Diseases. [[Eknoyan2008]]

1972

Ancel Keys and colleagues

Compared indices of relative weight in 7,424 healthy men in 12 cohorts in five countries. Weight divided by height squared correlated least with height and well with body fat, and they named it the "body mass index". [[Keys1972,Eknoyan2008]]

1967

Mason and Ito

Described gastric bypass for obesity; today's operations are covered in Metabolic & bariatric surgery. [[Mason1967]] A 1991 NIH consensus advised trying a nonsurgical programme first and offering surgery to well-informed people with acceptable operative risk, chosen by a multidisciplinary team; ASMBS and IFSO published major updates in 2022. [[NIH1991Consensus,Eisenberg2022ASMBS]]

1994

Friedman's laboratory

Zhang and colleagues cloned the mouse obese (ob) gene and its human counterpart. Its product, leptin, was proposed as a signal from fat tissue that helps regulate the size of the body's fat store. [[Zhang1994]] More in Appetite: the gut-brain loop.
