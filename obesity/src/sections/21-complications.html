Complications across the body

Excess adiposity touches almost every organ: the heart, liver, airway, pancreas, kidneys, joints, reproductive organs, and the risk of 13 cancers. Scrub through the body organ by organ and watch the published relative risks build beside it.

Across 900 000 adults in 57 prospective studies, deaths from all causes were lowest at a BMI of about 22.5–25 kg/m². Above that, each 5 kg/m² higher BMI meant about 30% higher overall mortality (HR 1.29). [[Whitlock2009PSC]] Among 3.95 million never-smokers without chronic disease at baseline, the hazard ratio was 1.45 at BMI 30–<35, 1.94 at 35–<40 and 2.76 at 40–<60, and the excess was larger in younger people and in men. [[GBMC2016]] High BMI accounted for an estimated 4.0 million deaths worldwide in 2015, more than two-thirds of them cardiovascular. [[Afshin2017]]

Every figure on this page describes large groups of people. None of them predicts what will happen to one person, and obesity itself is a chronic disease with genetic, biological and social causes, not a failure of willpower. [[Rubino2025Commission]]

Schematic body, not to scale. Bars: relative risks as published, each against its own reference group (usually normal BMI), on a log scale; IRR = incidence rate ratio, OR = odds ratio, HR = hazard ratio. Population statistics. Sources in the note panel.

Exam hookOf 18 comorbidities pooled from 89 cohorts, type 2 diabetes had the strongest link to obesity: IRR 12.41 in women and 6.74 in men. [[Guh2009]] Each 5 kg/m² above 25 meant about 30% higher all-cause mortality. [[Whitlock2009PSC]]
In plain wordsLiving with obesity raises the chance of many other health problems, such as type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, sleep apnoea, liver disease and knee pain. These numbers describe large groups of people, not you personally. [[Guh2009,Whitlock2009PSC]]

Relative risk, made visible

A relative risk of 3 sounds alarming, but what it means depends on how common the disease is to begin with. Pick a condition: each block counts the cases in a group for every 10 cases in an equal-sized reference group. The last choice is different: it shows how common fatty liver disease is, as people out of 100.

Teaching toolThese blocks turn published ratios into counts so they are easier to compare. They are population statistics from the studies cited, not a forecast for anyone, and BMI groups are a screening measure, not a diagnosis.

What modest weight loss improves

In Look AHEAD, people with type 2 diabetes who lost 5 to under 10% of their weight in the first year were far more likely to improve their blood sugar, blood pressure and blood fats than people whose weight stayed stable, though LDL cholesterol did not follow. [[Wing2011DiabetesCare]] Other trials show the same direction in the liver, the bladder and sleep.

If eating or weight is distressingIf eating or weight causes distress, or someone restricts, binges or purges, talk to a clinician or an eating-disorder service. Binge eating disorder is more common than bulimia nervosa, and fewer than half of people with it ever receive treatment. [[Kessler2013BED]]

Does weight loss prevent heart attacks?

Randomised: no difference

HR 0.95

Look AHEAD, 5145 adults with type 2 diabetes, median 9.6 years: intensive lifestyle (6.0% vs 3.5% weight loss at the end) did not reduce cardiovascular events (95% CI 0.83–1.09). [[LookAHEAD2013]]

Observational: lower risk

HR 0.79

In the same cohort, people who lost at least 10% in year 1 had 21% fewer cardiovascular events than those with stable weight or gain, a comparison that is not randomised. Early evidence [[LookAHEAD2016LDE]]

What the AHA says

Trials of medical weight loss have not shown fewer coronary events, while prospective studies of bariatric surgery have shown lower coronary risk. Waist size is a cardiovascular risk marker independent of BMI. [[PowellWiley2021]]

Is the link to stroke causal? Observational studies show a higher ischaemic-stroke risk (RR 1.64 with obesity) [[Strazzullo2010]], but pooled Mendelian randomisation found no association (OR 1.02), while supporting causal links to type 2 diabetes (OR 1.67) and coronary artery disease (OR 1.20). [[Riaz2018]] Status: unresolved.

Mood matters tooLow mood and weight can affect each other in both directions. [[Luppino2010]] Genetic studies suggest higher BMI is partly causal for depression. Early evidence [[Tyrrell2019]] Getting help for one can help with the other.
