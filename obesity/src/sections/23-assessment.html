Assessment & staging

A good assessment starts by asking permission and ends with a plan made together. In between, it looks at the whole person: their story, their medicines, sleep and mood, the right measurements and tests, and how weight is actually affecting health and daily life.

In plain wordsA good check-up asks your permission before talking about weight. It looks at your whole health, not just the number on the scale. [[Vallis2013,Rubino2025Commission]]

Teaching toolBMI, waist, STOP-Bang and staging tools on this page are teaching tools with population caveats. None is a diagnosis or a target on its own; a clinician puts them together with the person. [[Rubino2025Commission]] The measuring module, 1.2, explains BMI classes and waist cut-offs.

Staging with EOSS: a teaching sketch

EOSS grades comorbidity and functional status on a 5-point scale, 0 to 4, alongside BMI. [[Sharma2009EOSS,Padwal2011EOSS]] In a modified version used with cohort data, stage 0 means no risk factors or comorbidities, stage 1 mild conditions, and stages 2–3 moderate to severe conditions. [[Kuk2011EOSS]] Set each domain below; this sketch takes the most severe one.

Teaching toolA simplified sketch of the idea, not the official EOSS scoring form, and never a diagnosis or a prognosis. Weight history matters too: lower weight at age 21, lower fitness and reported dieting were each associated with being at EOSS stage 2 or 3. [[Kuk2011EOSS]]

Clinical or preclinical obesity? The Lancet Commission criteria

The 2025 Lancet Commission defines clinical obesity as a chronic, systemic illness in which excess adiposity alters organ or tissue function or limits daily activities; preclinical obesity is excess adiposity with organ function preserved. It lists 18 adult diagnostic criteria, agreed with 90–100% consensus. [[Rubino2025Commission]]

Teaching toolA checklist for learning the criteria, not a diagnosis. Each criterion needs a clinician to confirm both the problem and that excess adiposity is its cause.

Screening for sleep apnoea: STOP-Bang

STOP stands for Snoring, Tiredness, Observed apnoea and high blood Pressure; adding BMI, Age, Neck circumference and male Gender ("Bang") raised sensitivity for an AHI over 15 from 74.3% to 92.9% in surgical patients. [[Chung2008STOP]] In sleep clinics the negative predictive value for any OSA was only 46%. [[Nagappa2015]]
