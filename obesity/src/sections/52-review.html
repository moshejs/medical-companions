Every answer carries its source. Tap a card or press Space to flip it; arrow keys move through the deck. Filter by part, or shuffle.

Trial results on these cards are population averages, not predictions for any one person, and BMI, waist and staging figures are teaching tools, never a diagnosis or a target. Doses, schedules and thresholds are quoted as published, never as instructions: do not start, stop or change a medicine without a clinician. If eating or weight causes you distress, or you restrict, binge or purge, please talk to a clinician or an eating-disorder service.

The 5As and STOP-Bang are published names, and their cards are tagged so. Every other card is a memory aid written for this guide: a short phrase to hold a published fact in mind. None of those is an official grouping from any guideline, drug label or patient organisation, and none replaces the source it points to.

Mnemonic in motion: the 5As of obesity counselling

The 5As (Ask, Assess, Advise, Agree, Assist) are a published framework for talking about weight in a clinic, adapted for obesity by Vallis and colleagues [[Vallis2013]]. Play the animation to see each step in turn, with numbers from the trials behind it, and why the first step, asking permission, matters so much. A teaching tool that explains how a published counselling framework is built. The curve is schematic, shaped on pooled trial averages, and is not a target for anyone. [[Vallis2013,Wing2011DiabetesCare,Franz2007,Puhl2013Language]]

These cards are for learning, not for deciding anything about one person's care. Obesity is a chronic disease with genetic, biological, environmental and social causes, not a failure of willpower, and weight coming back after weight loss is biology [[Rubino2020Stigma,Rubino2025Commission]].

Sources

Every reference behind the Obesity Companion: randomised trials such as the DPP, Look AHEAD, DiRECT, STEP, SURMOUNT and SELECT, cohorts such as SOS and LABS, meta-analyses, the 2025 Lancet Commission, guidelines from the AHA/ACC/TOS, USPSTF, AGA, AAP and ASMBS/IFSO, and US drug labels. The small numbers throughout the app point here; filter by research area or search.

Research passes ran in parallel, one per area: definitions, measurement, epidemiology and history; causes, genetics and environment; physiology of appetite, fat tissue and energy balance; complications and clinical assessment; lifestyle and behavioural treatment; anti-obesity medications; metabolic and bariatric surgery; children, pregnancy, stigma, living with obesity and nursing; and the research frontier. Before inclusion each source was checked, where possible, against PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, ClinicalTrials.gov or openFDA, and the green tag on an entry names the service that confirmed it. Works found by more than one pass appear once, with any extra keys listed beside them.

Some guideline texts could only be read in part; where a module relies on a statement reported through a secondary source, it says so. Drug labels change, so check the current label on the regulator's site. Entries marked %t:sources.check% are pages with no PMID or DOI, or works that could not be confirmed.
