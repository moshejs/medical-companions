Metabolic & bariatric surgery

Metabolic and bariatric surgery changes the stomach and gut, and with them the hormones that tell the brain about hunger and fullness. [[leRoux2006,Karamanakos2008]] Scrub the timeline to watch a normal stomach become a sleeve, then a bypass, and see what the gut sends to the brain afterwards.

Schematic, not to scale. Arrows show the direction of hormone changes reported in trials, not measured levels. [[leRoux2006,Karamanakos2008]] Limb lengths are those used in one trial; surgeons vary them. [[Robert2019YOMEGA]]

In plain wordsThe two most common operations are the sleeve, where part of the stomach is removed, and the gastric bypass, where food skips most of the stomach and the first part of the gut. Both change hunger and fullness signals, not only stomach size. [[Eisenberg2022ASMBS,leRoux2006]]
Exam hookThe gut-hormone rise after bypass did not occur after a band with equal weight loss, so restriction alone does not explain it. [[leRoux2006]]

Who is offered surgery

In 1991 a US NIH consensus panel said that people with severe obesity should first be considered for a non-surgical programme, and that surgery could be considered for well-informed patients with acceptable operative risk, selected by a multidisciplinary team, with lifelong medical follow-up. [[NIH1991Consensus]] Its BMI thresholds, above 40, or above 35 with comorbidities, became the standard for three decades. [[Eisenberg2022ASMBS,Arterburn2020JAMA]] In 2022 the ASMBS and IFSO replaced them. The explainer below quotes both, as published.

Teaching toolThis explainer only shows which published statement a BMI band falls under. It is not an eligibility check, a diagnosis or a target. Real decisions weigh health, risks, preferences and much more than BMI; a JAMA review advises offering people with severe obesity a conversation about surgery, driven by informed preference. [[Arterburn2020JAMA]] In SOS, baseline glucose and insulin, not baseline BMI, predicted who benefited. [[Sjostrom2013JIM]]

Children and adolescents

ASMBS/IFSO 2022: "Appropriately selected children and adolescents should be considered" for surgery, and surgery does not harm pubertal development or linear growth, so no specific Tanner stage or bone age should be required. [[Eisenberg2022ASMBS]] The 2018 ASMBS pediatric guideline says adolescents with cognitive disabilities, treated mental illness or eating disorders "should not be denied treatment". [[Pratt2018ASMBS]] More in Children & adolescents.

Older adults

There is "no evidence to support an age limit"; surgery has been done in people over 70, with slightly higher complication rates, and frailty assessment is recommended. [[Eisenberg2022ASMBS]]

Required weight loss before surgery

The 2022 statement finds "no data to support" insurance-mandated preoperative weight loss and calls it "discriminatory, arbitrary, and scientifically unfounded". [[Eisenberg2022ASMBS]]

Type 2 diabetes

The 2016 Diabetes Surgery Summit, endorsed by 45 societies, set separate thresholds for type 2 diabetes, lowered by 2.5 kg/m² for Asian patients. [[Rubino2016DSS]] See Obesity & type 2 diabetes.

Twenty years of weight change: the SOS study

The Swedish Obese Subjects (SOS) study followed 2010 people who chose surgery and 2037 matched controls who had usual care. It was prospective and matched, not randomised. [[Sjostrom2007SOS]] Most had operations rarely used today: vertical banded gastroplasty 68%, banding 19% and gastric bypass 13%. [[Sjostrom2013JIM]] Play the years and switch the lines on and off.

Dots are published means; the lines between them are smoothed and schematic. All surgery and controls to 20 years [[Sjostrom2013JIM]]; by operation to 10 years, with the maximum loss (reached at 1 to 2 years) plotted at 1.5 years. [[Sjostrom2007SOS]] Population averages, not a prediction for any person.

In plain wordsOn average, people who had surgery lost more weight and kept more of it off than people with usual care. Results vary a lot from person to person, and some weight usually comes back after the first two years. [[Sjostrom2013JIM]]
Exam hookSOS maximum loss at 1 to 2 years: bypass 32%, gastroplasty 25%, banding 20%; at 10 years 25%, 16% and 14%. Adjusted mortality hazard ratio 0.71. [[Sjostrom2007SOS]]

If eating or weight is distressingIf eating or weight causes distress, or someone restricts, binges or purges, talk to a clinician or an eating-disorder service. Weight-change figures on this page are group averages from studies, not goals.

Diabetes after surgery: STAMPEDE

STAMPEDE randomised 150 people with type 2 diabetes and BMI 27 to 43 to intensive medical therapy alone or with gastric bypass or sleeve gastrectomy. [[Schauer2017STAMPEDE]] Pick a measure.

5-year results: HbA1c 6.0% or less in 5%, 29% and 23%; weight change −5%, −23% and −19%; insulin use fell by 13%, 35% and 34%. [[Schauer2017STAMPEDE]]

Diabetes remission after biliopancreatic diversion, gastric bypass and medical therapy. Of those in remission at 2 years, 58.8% relapsed by 10 years, but all kept adequate control. [[Mingrone2021Lancet]]

ARMMS-T2D, 4 pooled trials12.7% vs 0%

Remission at 12 years after surgery versus medical and lifestyle care (18.2% versus 6.2% at 7 years). [[Courcoulas2024ARMMS]]

SOS, people without diabetesHR 0.17

New type 2 diabetes: 6.8 versus 28.4 per 1000 person-years after surgery versus usual care. [[Carlsson2012NEJM]]

GATEWAY, blood pressure51%

of the bypass group were off all blood pressure medicines at 12 months; 83.7% versus 12.8% cut medicines by at least 30% with controlled pressure. [[Schiavon2018GATEWAY]]

Do not start, stop or change a diabetes or blood pressure medicine without a clinician. Diabetes can come back over the years after surgery, so monitoring continues. [[Mingrone2021Lancet,Courcoulas2018LABS]]

How much Barrett's oesophagus follows a sleeve is disputed: 4% in the 10-year randomised trial, [[Salminen2022SLEEVEPASS]] but 11.6% pooled across mostly observational studies, unrelated to reflux symptoms. [[Qumseya2021]] Reflux, ulcers and other late problems are covered in Life after surgery.

Survival, heart disease and cancer

SOS, mean 10.9 yearsHR 0.71

Adjusted hazard ratio for death: 101 deaths after surgery versus 129 in controls. [[Sjostrom2007SOS]]

Meta-analysis, 174,772 people+6.1 years

Longer median life expectancy with surgery: 9.3 years with diabetes and 5.1 without, in matched observational data. [[Syn2021Lancet]]

Type 2 diabetes cohort, 8 years30.8% vs 47.7%

Major adverse cardiovascular events after surgery versus matched controls (adjusted HR 0.61); observational, and the authors call for randomised confirmation. [[Aminian2019JAMA]]

SPLENDID, 10 years2.9% vs 4.9%

Obesity-associated cancer after surgery versus matched controls (adjusted HR 0.68). [[Aminian2022SPLENDID]] In SOS the cancer reduction was seen in women but not in men. [[Sjostrom2009LancetOncol]]

These are group statistics from cohorts, mostly not randomised. They describe averages, not a promise for any one person. The 24-year SOS life-expectancy result is in Life after surgery.

Endoscopic options

Endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty13.6% vs 0.8%

An "endolumenal, organ-sparing" procedure done through the mouth. In MERIT (209 people with class 1 or 2 obesity), total body weight loss at 52 weeks was 13.6% with ESG plus lifestyle versus 0.8% with lifestyle alone; serious ESG-related adverse events in 2%. [[AbuDayyeh2022MERIT]]

Intragastric balloon10.2% vs 3.3%

Weight loss at 6 months with an Orbera balloon plus lifestyle versus lifestyle alone; 7.6% versus 3.1% at 12 months, 6 months after removal. Nausea in 86.9%, vomiting in 75.6%, early removal in 18.8%. [[Courcoulas2017IGB]]

Endoscopic therapies give smaller weight loss, tested over shorter times, than surgery. [[AbuDayyeh2022MERIT,Courcoulas2017IGB]] Medicines are covered in How GLP-1 medicines work.

Exam hookASMBS/IFSO 2022: surgery recommended at BMI above 35 regardless of comorbidity; considered at 30 to 34.9 with metabolic disease; Asian threshold above 27.5. The 1991 NIH thresholds were above 40, or above 35 with comorbidities. [[Eisenberg2022ASMBS]]
In plain wordsSurgery is safe for most people, but it has risks, and it means lifelong vitamins, blood tests and follow-up. Your team will talk through what fits you. [[Mechanick2020SOARD,Parrott2017ASMBS]]
