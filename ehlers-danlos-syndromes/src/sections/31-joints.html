Complications by age in classical EDS

In 75 people with molecularly confirmed classical EDS, joint complications were far more common in adults than in children. In hEDS, by contrast, an Italian cohort of 189 found that the rate and sites of instability did not change with age at diagnosis, even though the Beighton score fell. [[Ritelli2020C,Morlino2017]]

What the studies show

Words

Subluxation or dislocation?

In a subluxation the joint surfaces slide partly apart and keep some contact; in a dislocation they lose contact. In classical EDS, 29.3% had had subluxations and 21.3% dislocations; the dislocations were mostly of the shoulder, digits, hip, radius and clavicle, and usually went back by themselves. In 100 adults with HSD and shoulder complaints, 67 described mechanical symptoms; 25% of them had a previous dislocation and 64% said the shoulder felt loose. [[Ritelli2020C,Liaghat2022APMR]]

Early evidence

Position sense is blurred

Ten women with hypermobility syndrome needed bigger knee movements than controls before they noticed them, at 30° and 5° of flexion, and lost the usual gain in acuity near full extension. A meta-analysis of 5 studies (254 people) confirmed poorer lower-limb position sense; upper-limb results were mixed. Children are affected too. [[Hall1995,Smith2013BJHS,Fatoye2009]]

An uncontrolled 8-week home programme of closed-chain exercise improved knee acuity in 16 of 18 people. [[Ferrell2004]]

Early evidence

Weaker, but not smaller

Women with hEDS had 30% to 49% lower knee strength and endurance than matched controls, with similar muscle mass on DXA. Weakness persisted over 8 years of follow-up, and strength tracked chair-rise, walking and function scores. In 22 women with hEDS, 95% had fallen in the past year. [[Rombaut2012Strength,Coussens2021,Scheper2017Strength,Rombaut2011Balance]]

Early evidence

Muscles work differently

With generalised hypermobility, knee muscles co-contract more (a higher coactivation ratio) and adults hold force less steadily. In hEDS/HSD with multidirectional shoulder instability, the humeral head moved more during simple loaded exercises, and the shoulder blade rotated upward less. [[Jensen2013GJH,Spanhove2020,Spanhove2022]]

Hypothesis

Early osteoarthritis?

Poor position sense may let joints sit in unsound positions and speed wear (a hypothesis). Thumb-base osteoarthritis is the clearest link. Large population cohorts point the other way for the knee and hand, but they studied mild hypermobility in older adults: no hEDS cohort with X-ray osteoarthritis outcomes was found. [[Hall1995,Jonsson1996,Dolan2003,Kraus2004,Flowers2018]]

Early evidence

Bone: size more than density?

Results are mixed. One study of 50 adults with classical or hypermobile EDS found vertebral fractures in 32% vs 8% of controls; another found normal density but smaller cortical bone and more fractures. A review concludes that in hEDS and cEDS bone density is at most mildly lower. [[EllerVainicher2016,Banica2020,Basalom2020]]

Exam hook · capsule laxity. Hypermobility is partly a property of the restraints themselves: in 100 hip-arthroscopy patients a Beighton score of 4 or more went with a capsule thinner than 10 mm, and in hEDS the Achilles tendon is less stiff than in controls. But not all range is laxity: in benign joint hypermobility, hamstrings absorbed the same energy at a given stretch, and the extra range came with a higher tolerance to stretch. [[Devitt2017,Rombaut2012Tendon,Magnusson2001]]
