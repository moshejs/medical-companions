What the gut specialists advise

AGA 2025, in brief

  • Test for functional defecation disorders in hEDS/HSD with lower GI symptoms.
  • Timely gastric-function testing when POTS and chronic upper GI symptoms occur together.
  • Consider coeliac testing earlier; routine disaccharidase testing is not supported.

Eating and nutrition

When food becomes hard

In 680 people with hEDS/HSD, 62.1% had changed their diet in the past year, and 31.7% of those needed nutrition support. A positive screen for ARFID-type fear of eating predicted it (OR 4.97). [[Topan2024]]

MCAS: weigh the evidence

Mast cell activation syndrome (MCAS) is often mentioned with hEDS and POTS as a "triad". The published picture is unsettled: supportive reviews propose a mechanism, critics find the evidence inadequate, and gut specialists advise targeted, not universal, testing. Each square below is an original study, sized by its number of participants. Reviews and guidance interpret data but add no participants, so they hang as tags. [[Kucharik2020,Monaco2022,Aziz2025]]

Square size reflects participants only, not study quality or design. Lyons 2016 counted families, not individuals. [[Wang2021AAP,Lyons2016,Vazquez2022]]

The plain verdict

No controlled study using consensus MCAS criteria has shown that MCAS is more common in hEDS. The NIH POTS consensus says accurate data on how often MCAS occurs in POTS are not available. [[Kucharik2020,Aziz2025,Vazquez2022,Vernino2021]]

Tryptase genetics

Hereditary alpha-tryptasemia

Extra copies of the TPSAB1 gene raise baseline tryptase and were first linked to dysautonomia, IBS-like symptoms and hypermobility. When people with HSD, hEDS and axial hypermobility were genotyped, it was no more common than in the general population. [[Lyons2016,Vazquez2022]]

How it is tested

Targeted, with a strict lab rule

The AGA reserves mast-cell testing for episodic symptoms in 2 or more organ systems. The AAAAI framed MCAS criteria to avoid "overdiagnosing the disorder in patients who likely have other conditions". Medicines used for mast-cell symptoms are covered, with their evidence labels, in 4.2. [[Aziz2025,Weiler2019JACI]]

Try the consensus "20% + 2" rule

The consensus criterion for confirming mast cell activation is an event-related rise in serum tryptase of at least 20% plus 2 ng/mL over the person's own baseline. A teaching example only. [[Valent2019IAAI,Aziz2025]]
