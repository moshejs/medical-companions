Part 4 · Treatment

Exercise & physiotherapy

Exercise with a physiotherapist who understands hypermobility is the main treatment for hypermobile EDS and hypermobility spectrum disorder. Watch a published 16-week strengthening programme build week by week, compare paced and boom-bust activity, and see what the trials actually found.

A teaching picture, not a prescriptionProgrammes in trials were supervised and adjusted to each person. Almost every trial enrolled people with hEDS or HSD, so results should not be carried over to the rarer genetic types without care, and exercise plans should be made with a clinician. [[Malfait2017,Castori2017,Engelbert2017]]

Lower chart

Load bars: the HEAVY arm of the Danish shoulder trial, as published. The joint, the gauges, the stage names and the activity and pain lines are schematic, not trial data. [[Liaghat2022BJSM]]

Significant is not the same as meaningful

In the 100-person shoulder trial, the WOSI score (0–2100, lower is better) favoured high-load training at 16 weeks. The whole confidence interval sat below zero, so the result was statistically significant. But the minimal important difference is 252 points, and the average gap (−174.5) was smaller than that. At one year the gap had shrunk and its interval crossed zero. [[Liaghat2022BJSM,Liaghat2024BJSM]]

Between-group differences, HEAVY minus LIGHT; negative favours HEAVY. [[Liaghat2022BJSM,Liaghat2024BJSM]]

What the trials tested

The evidence base is small. A 2021 systematic review of 11 exercise studies found only 3 small controlled trials showing benefit over a control, all in one body area and all in women, with no long-term follow-up; improvement over time was consistent, but superiority over comparators was "weak". Attrition ran from 0% to 44%. Filter the cards by the outcome you care about. [[Palmer2021]]

Type

Beyond exercise: supports and therapies

Early evidence

Splints and hand orthoses

In 169 adults with HSD or hEDS and hand symptoms, a progressive wrist-stabilising exercise programme and wearing a hand orthosis during chosen activities gave the same results at 12 weeks. A 14-person study of finger orthoses found faster timed hand tasks, but the authors call the evidence inconclusive. No trial of finger "ring" splints was found. [[Susanne2025,Jensen2021Orth]]

Early evidence

Compression garments

In a 36-person RCT, garments worn alongside 12 physiotherapy sessions improved dynamic balance more than physiotherapy alone (effect size 0.93), but pain fell equally in both groups. Uncontrolled cohorts report less pain (most painful joint 71.5 to 45.7 mm on a VAS over 2 years), open to placebo and selection bias. [[Benistan2024,Benistan2023,Boulu2025]]

Early evidence

Foot orthoses and insoles

Custom foot orthoses in 36 adults with EDS improved foot pain, foot function and fatigue over 3 months, with no control group. In 53 children with leg pain, custom orthotics cut pain by a mean 27/100 at 1 month. No orthosis had been tested in an RCT in children by 2018. [[ReinaBueno2020,Maarj2023,Peterson2018]]

No trial found

Hydrotherapy and braces for the neck

No trial of hydrotherapy in hEDS or HSD was found. For POTS, the heart rhythm consensus advises starting exercise in non-upright forms such as rowing, a recumbent bike or swimming. For upper cervical instability, experts say a neck brace helps some highly irritable patients, while noting "no published guidelines" on neck braces. [[Sheldon2015,Russek2022UCI]]

Occupational therapy

Work, home and school

OT guidance for EDS with POTS covers equipment, orthoses, energy conservation and pacing, sleep hygiene and routines: a descriptive paper, not a trial. Patients and clinicians rank OT support "to maintain independence at work and home" as a priority. In the Bendy Study, a physiotherapist and an OT visited each child's school together. [[Levine2022OT,Bennett2022,Bale2019]]

Early evidence

CBT-based pain management

A UK programme (8 days over 6 weeks, 87 people) improved self-efficacy, catastrophising, mood and pain at 1 month; by 5 months scores had drifted back, though most stayed better. An online 8-week programme was feasible, with a moderate effect on worst pain (d 0.63). The most helpful psychological approaches ran alongside physiotherapy. [[Rahman2014,Chew2025,Clark2024Psych]]

Early evidence

Breathing muscle training

Inspiratory muscle strength was below 80% of predicted in 77% of 104 women with hEDS. In a 6-week RCT (20 per group), training raised the 6-minute walk from 455 to 515 m. [[Reychler2019]]

Hypothesis

Pacing

Activity pacing is recommended by patients and clinicians and was a prioritised behaviour-change technique, but no trial has tested pacing alone in hEDS or HSD. Fear of movement is common: 93% of 42 people with JHS/EDS-HT scored above the Tampa cut-off. [[Bennett2022,Celletti2013]]

Pain, fatigue and POTS have their own modules: see Pain & fatigue, POTS & the autonomic link and, for the medicines, Medicines. The Beighton score is a teaching tool; only a clinician can diagnose hEDS or HSD. [[Castori2017]]
