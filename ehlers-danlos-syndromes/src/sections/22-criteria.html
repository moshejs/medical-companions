Part 2 · Diagnosis

The 2017 hEDS criteria

Hypermobile EDS is the only one of the 13 types without a known gene, so it is diagnosed with a clinical checklist: three criteria, all of which must be met. Follow one illustrative person through the three gates, then try the checklist on worked examples.

A teaching tool, not self-diagnosisThe checklist below shows how the published rules fit together. Only a clinician who examines a person and excludes other conditions can apply them. The 2017 criteria are also being revised: the Ehlers-Danlos Society has announced new criteria for late 2026, but until a revision is published, the 2017 criteria remain the published standard. [[Malfait2017,Fairweather2025]]

Try it: the checklist on worked examples

Pick a fictional case to load its findings, or choose free mode and tick items yourself. The panel reports which gates pass under the 2017 wording. It never says "you have": the findings here are made up, and in real life each item needs a clinician to examine, measure and exclude. [[Fairweather2025,Thwaites2022]]

Example
Criterion 1 · generalised joint hypermobility

The cut-off depends on age: 6 or more before puberty, 5 or more from puberty to 50, 4 or more over 50. Children should not be diagnosed with hEDS before biological maturity. [[Tofts2023,Fairweather2025]]

Feature A · systemic signs (need 5 of 12)

Several items have conditions: striae only at set sites and without significant weight change; prolapse only in children, men or women who have never given birth; scars at 2 or more sites and not paper-thin or haemosiderotic. [[Fairweather2025,Thwaites2022]]

Feature B · family history

"Mum is bendy too" is not Feature B: the relative must independently meet the current criteria. [[Fairweather2025,Thwaites2022]]

Feature C · musculoskeletal complications (need 1)

"Recurrent" has a definition: 3 or more atraumatic dislocations of one joint, 2 or more in two different joints at different times, or medically confirmed instability at 2 or more sites. [[Fairweather2025]]

Criterion 3 · exclusions (need all 3)

With an autoimmune rheumatological condition, hEDS needs Features A and B; Feature C cannot be counted. [[Fairweather2025,Thwaites2022]]

Where students go wrong

Misreading 1

"A high Beighton score means hEDS"

Generalised joint hypermobility is only Criterion 1. All three criteria must be met, and hypermobility on its own is common and is not a disease. [[Fairweather2025,Castori2017]]

Misreading 2

"Soft skin and stretch marks are enough for Feature A"

Feature A needs at least 5 of its 12 items, and Criterion 2 needs two of Features A, B and C. [[Fairweather2025,Thwaites2022]]

Misreading 3

"Widespread pain points to hEDS"

Chronic widespread pain is one Feature C item, so pain alone cannot separate hEDS from fibromyalgia; the skin, systemic and family-history features carry the distinction. Since 2016, fibromyalgia can be diagnosed alongside another condition. [[Thwaites2022,Fairweather2025,Wolfe2016]]

Misreading 4

"Fragile skin makes hEDS more likely"

The reverse: unusual skin fragility is an exclusion in Criterion 3 and should prompt consideration of other EDS types, which are confirmed by genetic testing. [[Fairweather2025,Malfait2017]]

Misreading 5

"A normal gene panel rules hEDS out"

No gene is known for hEDS, so no genetic test can confirm it. Testing is used to exclude other conditions "as indicated". [[Yew2021,Gensemer2021,Fairweather2025]]

Misreading 6

"Not hEDS means nothing is wrong"

Symptomatic joint hypermobility that does not meet the hEDS criteria is described as a hypermobility spectrum disorder. See hEDS or HSD? [[Castori2017]]

How the criteria perform, and the criticisms

Swiss cohort

Which part separates the groups?

In 97 adults with symptomatic hypermobility (61 hEDS, 36 HSD), Criterion 1 was met by 90% vs 71%, Feature B by 93% vs 33%, Feature C by 98% vs 75%, and Feature A by 18% vs 0%. The median Beighton score was 7 in both groups. [[AubryRozier2021]]

US clinic

Most people assessed get another label

Of 2,695 adults at the Mayo Clinic Florida EDS clinic, the 2017 criteria gave HSD in 60.6%, hEDS in 18.3%, localised or historical HSD in 10.7%, and none of these in 10.4%. [[Fairweather2025]]

A gap

Inter-rater agreement is unknown

No study of inter-rater agreement for the full 2017 checklist was found by this guide's research pass. Published reliability data cover only the Beighton score and the five-part questionnaire, and a COSMIN review rated those studies "fair" or "poor". [[Juul-Kristensen2017,Bockhorn2021]]

Established

"Too stringent"

The 2017 criteria have been criticised as too stringent and as missing the multisystem picture. An Italian centre that re-examined 327 patients proposes loosening them, and a 2026 review calls them inadequate for the clinical variety of hEDS/HSD. [[Ritelli2024,Motlak2026]]

Early evidence

Are the boundaries real?

Severity clusters in 105 Italian adults "do not mirror either the old or new criteria". Heel piezogenic papules were seen in 76% of adolescents with HSD/hEDS and 60% of controls, so their diagnostic value is not established. [[Copetti2019,Schubert-Hjalmarsson2026]]

The wait

Years to a diagnosis

In a global survey, people with hEDS reported an average delay of 22.1 years and people with HSD 17.5 years (self-report). Of one registry's respondents, 95.2% rated getting a diagnosis as important or highly important. [[Daylor2025,Wang2024AJMG]]
