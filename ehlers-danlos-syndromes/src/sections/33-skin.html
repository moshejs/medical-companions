Skin, scars & bruising

Skin is where several EDS types show themselves most clearly. Pinch, stretch, cut and bruise four kinds of skin in cross-section, and see why classical EDS skin stretches and scars, vascular EDS skin is thin and see-through, and hypermobile EDS skin is soft but rarely fragile. Classical EDS skin, by the numbers

In 75 people with molecularly confirmed classical EDS, skin findings were common but not universal: 16 did not meet the combined major skin criterion and 6 had no skin hyperextensibility at all. [[Ritelli2020C]]

Which skin sign belongs to which type?

Pick a type for each sign. One sign is a trap: it is common in people without EDS.

Signs in detail

Established

Piezogenic papules

Small herniations of fat into the dermis that appear on the heels when standing. 72% of 322 healthy Dutch children had at least one, none painful, and hypermobile children had no more than others. They were found in 86.7% of people with cEDS and were painful in 10 of 29 people with EDS (34.5%). [[vanStraaten1991,Ritelli2020C,Kahana1987]]

hEDS checklist item

Striae

Unexplained stretch marks on the back, groin, thighs, breasts or abdomen are among the 2017 hEDS skin items. In cEDS, striae were present in 40%, more often in adults and females. [[Stembridge2023,Malfait2017,Ritelli2020C]]

Established

Molluscoid pseudotumours and spheroids

Molluscoid pseudotumours are fleshy, fibrotic nodules up to 3 cm across, found with scars at pressure points such as elbows and knees. Subcutaneous spheroids are hard, pea-sized calcified fat lobules over the ulna and tibia, visible on X-ray. Both are rare but highly diagnostic of cEDS. [[Stembridge2023,Ritelli2020C]]

Established

Why bruises come easily

Bruising is attributed to fragile capillaries and perivascular tissue. Clotting factors, platelet aggregation and bleeding time are usually normal; only the Hess capillary-fragility test may be abnormal. In 52 people with several EDS types, a bleeding score was abnormal in 62% vs 0% of controls. [[DePaepe2004,Kumskova2023]]

Children

Bruises and safeguarding

In cEDS, bruising often appears when children start crawling and walking, on the shins and forearms. The differential includes clotting disorders and non-accidental injury, so the skin picture needs careful, informed assessment. [[Stembridge2023]]

Early evidence

Thinner skin, non-specific biopsies

20-MHz ultrasound showed thinner skin in cEDS and hEDS, most at the chest and lower leg. Skin electron microscopy is no longer subtype-specific: of 177 biopsies at the London EDS service, none was specific, although collagen "flowers" were seen in most confirmed cEDS. [[Eisenbeiss2003,Angwin2020C]]

Vascular EDSThin, translucent skin and a bleeding tendency are diagnostic clues alongside arterial, bowel and uterine rupture. In a Dutch cohort of 142, 54.3% of index patients had a highly suggestive appearance, and in them major events came younger. See Vascular EDS. [[Germain2002,Byers2017,Demirdas2024]]
