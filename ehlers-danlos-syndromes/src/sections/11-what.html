Part 1 · The syndromes

What EDS is

The Ehlers-Danlos syndromes are a family of inherited conditions of connective tissue, the body's "glue". The 2017 International Classification names 13 types: one very common type with no known gene, and twelve rare ones confirmed by a genetic test. [[Malfait2017,Tinkle2017]]

How rare is each type?

Published frequencies differ in kind: some are estimates of how many people carry a type, some are counts of reported individuals, and the Welsh figure counts everyone with a coded diagnosis. For hypermobile EDS there is no type-specific population figure, because there is no gene test to count it by. [[VanDijk2024,Demmler2019,Malfait2017]]

What EDS is not

Not just bendy

Being flexible is not a disease

Generalised joint hypermobility is common. Among 6,022 UK 14-year-olds, 27.5% of girls and 10.6% of boys scored 4 or more on the Beighton score. Hypermobility is the shared feature that hEDS, HSD and many other conditions build on, but on its own it is not a diagnosis. [[Clinch2011,Castori2017]]

Not one disease

Thirteen types, many genes

The 2017 classification lists 13 types. Every type except hEDS needs a causative variant for a definite diagnosis. By 2020 a primer counted 14 types and 20 genes, most of them for collagens I, III and V or the enzymes that process them. [[Malfait2017,Malfait2020]]

Not the same as HSD

hEDS and HSD are separate labels

Hypermobility spectrum disorder is the 2017 label for symptomatic joint hypermobility that does not meet the criteria for hEDS or another syndrome. Living with HSD can be just as hard. See hEDS or HSD? [[Castori2017]]

Not a home test

A Beighton score is a starting point

In 1,000 Australians aged 3 to 101, the common cut-off of 4 or more gave a 60% false-positive rate across the lifespan. The score and the hEDS checklist in this guide are teaching tools: only a clinician can diagnose EDS or HSD. Try it in Beighton score. [[Singh2017,Juul-Kristensen2017]]

Not "in the head"

Real symptoms, long waits

In the Ehlers-Danlos Society's Global Registry survey, people reported an average of 10.45 alternative diagnoses before hEDS, and the mean time to diagnosis was 10.39 years. Interviews describe clinicians dismissing pain and the harm that follows. [[Halverson2023,Halverson2021]]

Early evidence

Is EDS rare?

As a group, diagnosed EDS and joint hypermobility syndrome in Wales sat at about four times the EU rare-disease threshold of 50 per 100,000, so the authors argued they "are not rare". Each molecular type remains rare or ultra-rare. The much-repeated "1 in 5,000" has no traceable origin. [[Demmler2019,VanDijk2024]]
