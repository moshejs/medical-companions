Part 1 · The syndromes

The 13 types (2017)

The 2017 International Classification names 13 types of Ehlers-Danlos syndrome and groups them by the pathway their proteins work in. Twelve need a genetic test to confirm them; hypermobile EDS has no known gene and is a clinical diagnosis. Build the map group by group. [[Malfait2017]]

Old names, new names

EDS has been classified three times. The Berlin nosology (agreed 1986, published 1988) numbered 11 types with Roman numerals. The Villefranche nosology (1997, published 1998) named 6 types, mainly by cause. The 2017 classification has 13, and a 2020 primer counts 14 with classical-like type 2. Tap a card to see the new name. [[Beighton1988,Forghani2019,Beighton1998,Malfait2017,Malfait2020]]

Established

hEDS is not the same as HSD

People with symptomatic joint hypermobility who do not meet the criteria for hEDS or another syndrome are diagnosed with hypermobility spectrum disorder. The 2017 hEDS criteria are aimed at adults. [[Castori2017,VanDijk2024]] See hEDS or HSD?

Debated

Are the 2017 hEDS criteria too strict?

An Italian reference centre that re-assessed 327 people from 213 families argued the criteria should be relaxed. A shared plasma pattern of matrix fragments in hEDS and HSD has been proposed as evidence they are one disorder (early evidence). [[Ritelli2024,Ritelli2025]]

Proposed

A missing type?

COL1-related overlap disorder (21 people from 13 families with COL1A1 or COL1A2 variants) fits neither osteogenesis imperfecta nor the three COL1-related EDS types. It was proposed in 2020 but is not in the 2017 classification. [[Morlino2020,Venable2023]]
