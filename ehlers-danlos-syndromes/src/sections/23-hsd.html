In plain words

HSD is not a lesser diagnosis

If you have troublesome flexible joints but do not meet every hEDS rule, the diagnosis is usually hypermobility spectrum disorder, and HSD can be just as hard to live with. In a Swiss centre, hEDS and HSD differed on only 3 of 16 severity items, and at a US clinic people with HSD reported more symptoms above controls than people with hEDS did. [[Castori2017,AubryRozier2021,Fairweather2025]]

One condition or two?

Early evidence

The case for one spectrum

Bone parameters, neuropathic pain scores and suspected mast cell symptoms were alike in hEDS and HSD. Skin fibroblasts showed the same extracellular-matrix pattern, and a shared plasma pattern of matrix fragments has been reported; the authors argue for a single entity. [[AubryRozier2021,Coussens2023,Ritelli2022,Ritelli2025]]

Early evidence

The case for a difference

In a US clinic analysis of 2,088 patients, conditions more frequent in hEDS were "structural" (such as prolapses) and those more frequent in HSD "functional" (such as irritable bowel syndrome). [[Fairweather2025]]

Children

Labels that are meant to change

The paediatric working group proposes not diagnosing hEDS before biological maturity. Its framework has 8 subgroups, and a child can move back to typical, to asymptomatic hypermobility, or on to paediatric generalised HSD as signs change. [[Tofts2023]]

Under review

The boundary may move

Some centres propose loosening the 2017 hEDS criteria so that more people now classed as HSD would qualify, and a new international revision has been announced. Until it is published, the 2017 framework applies. [[Ritelli2024,Castori2017]]
