Medicines

No medicine treats EDS itself. Medicines are used for pain, for POTS and, in vascular EDS, to protect the arteries. Climb each treatment ladder rung by rung to see how each medicine works and how strong its evidence is.

Doses quoted as published, not instructionsEvery dose on this page is reported as a trial, label or guideline states it. Many uses shown here are off-label. Medicines are chosen and adjusted by a person's own clinicians. [[Malfait2020,Vernino2021]]

Ladder

The order of the rungs follows the published guidance (non-drug care first); it is not a fixed sequence for any one person. Mechanism drawings are schematic; numbers are from the cited studies.

The celiprolol trial, person by person

BBEST randomised 53 people with clinical vascular EDS to celiprolol or no treatment, with events judged by assessors blind to the arm. It was stopped early for benefit after a mean of 47 months. ARCADE later tested irbesartan against placebo in 57 people already taking celiprolol. Each figure below is one participant; event timing within each trial is drawn evenly for teaching, but the totals are as published. [[Ong2010,Jeunemaitre2025]]

Good to know

Early evidence

Numbing that wears off early

In a randomised crossover trial (91 people with EDS, 44 controls), lidocaine numbed both groups equally at 5 minutes, but at 30 minutes only 53% with EDS were still numb vs 80% of controls. Tell the dentist or surgeon if numbing has failed before. More in Surgery & anaesthesia. [[Bourne2026RAPM,Wiesmann2014]]

Low-level evidence

One medicine at a time

A 2026 expert review found the evidence "low-level" and not supportive of "any disease-specific, repurposed, or novel drug" for hypermobility-related pain. It advises one drug at a time, usually at a low dose, reassessed after a set trial period. [[Castori2026EOP]]

Consensus

Non-drug care comes first in POTS

The EDS consortium reserves drugs for moderate-to-severe impairment that has not responded to, or cannot tolerate, conservative care. The NIH consensus adds that no medicine is FDA-approved for POTS. See POTS & the autonomic link. [[Hakim2017,Vernino2021]]

Hypothesis

Mast cells

A systematic search found only 4–9 original articles across hEDS, POTS and MCAS, none studying all three, and no proven shared mechanism. Treatments for mast-cell symptoms have no EDS-specific trial evidence. See Gut, bladder & MCAS. [[Kucharik2020,Hakim2024GR]]
