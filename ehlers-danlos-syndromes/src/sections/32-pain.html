What else to know

Established

As heavy as fibromyalgia

In 206 women, physical and overall function in hEDS was similar to fibromyalgia and worse than rheumatoid arthritis. Pain severity sat between the two. [[Rombaut2011]]

Early evidence

Fear of movement

In 42 people with JHS/hEDS, 93% scored above the Tampa cut-off for fear of movement, and it tracked fatigue rather than pain intensity. Programmes that combine physical and cognitive-behavioural approaches have reduced it. [[Celletti2013,Bathen2013,Hakimi2023]]

Early evidence

Pain and mood travel together

In 106 people with classical or hypermobile EDS, 42.5% had a psychiatric disorder and any pain symptom raised the odds (OR 9.68). In 59 people with HSD/hEDS, depressive symptoms correlated with pain catastrophising and fatigue. [[Hershenfeld2016,Fletcher2025]]

Established

Fatigue has causes to look for

An expert review lists sleep disorders, chronic pain, deconditioning, autonomic dysfunction, bowel and bladder problems, psychological issues and nutritional deficiencies, and advises ruling out anaemia, hypothyroidism and chronic infection. No specific drug treats fatigue in EDS. [[Hakim2017Fatigue]]

Early evidence

Pain programmes

A UK cognitive behavioural pain-management programme (8 days over 6 weeks, 87 people) improved self-efficacy, mood and pain at 1 month; most gains held at 5 months, except average pain intensity. An online programme improved worst pain in a pilot trial. [[Rahman2014,Chew2025]]

Hypothesis

Headache and the neck

Migraine is more common and starts earlier in JHS/hEDS. Craniocervical instability has no agreed imaging criteria; a systematic review found level III–V evidence, and fusion surgery remains unproven for chronic pain. [[Bendik2011,Puledda2015,Lohkamp2022,Mao2022]]
