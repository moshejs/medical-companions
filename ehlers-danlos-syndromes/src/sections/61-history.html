Names then and now

Each classification renamed the types. Berlin numbered them with Roman numerals, mainly by inheritance and clinical picture; Villefranche gave six of them descriptive names, mainly by cause; 2017 kept those names, added types defined by their genes and removed several conditions from EDS altogether. People who were diagnosed under an older system keep that diagnosis. [[Forghani2019,Beighton1998,Malfait2017,Malek2021,VanDijk2024]]

Why the lists changed

1960s

One syndrome became many

In the 1960s it became clear that EDS was several disorders with different patterns of Mendelian inheritance, and classification began in the late 1960s. [[VanDijk2024,Beighton1998]]

1997

Classifying by cause

Villefranche cut eleven numbered types to six named ones, chosen mainly by cause, and gave major and minor criteria for each. Four Berlin types left EDS: the X-linked type, occipital horn syndrome, the fibronectin-deficient type and familial hypermobility. [[Beighton1998,Forghani2019,Malek2021]]

2017

Two pressures for a new list

After 1998, many new molecularly defined EDS variants were described, and there was concern that the hypermobile type had an expanded picture, might have several genetic causes and had inadequate criteria. The International Consortium met for about two years to write new criteria and management guidelines. [[Bloom2017]]

2017

JHS and hEDS folded together

Joint hypermobility syndrome (Brighton) and the hypermobility type (Villefranche) overlapped so much that the 2017 nosology folded them into hEDS, with hypermobility spectrum disorder (HSD) for people who do not meet the stricter criteria. See hEDS or HSD? [[Tinkle2017,Castori2017]]

Early evidence

Are the 2017 criteria too strict?

An Italian reference centre reassessed 327 people (213 families) diagnosed under Villefranche or Brighton and concluded that the 2017 criteria should be made less stringent. One genetics team finds adults rarely meet them when strictly applied, and the criteria are seen as unsuitable for children. The current state of the criteria is in Toward better treatment. [[Ritelli2024,VanDijk2024]]

Hypothesis

Will HEDGE find a gene?

The HEDGE study, begun by the Ehlers-Danlos Society in 2018, aims to sequence the whole genomes of 1,000 adults who meet the 2017 hEDS criteria. Experts expect most people with hEDS will not have a single-gene cause, and no causal gene has been confirmed. [[Douglas2026,VanDijk2024,Forghani2025]]

The zebra

Medical students are taught "when you hear hoofbeats, think of horses, not zebras": the common explanation is usually right. People with rare diseases adopted the zebra to say that sometimes the rare diagnosis is the right one, and it became the EDS community's symbol. Meeting other people with EDS, often called zebras, helps many people feel less alone. [[Park2025,Bashe2025,Callen2025,Bennett2021]]

In 500 short rare-disease videos on social media, EDS was the condition named most often (7.8% of videos). More on the zebra is in What EDS is. [[Park2025]]
