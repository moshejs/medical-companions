Look-alikes & red flags

Bendy joints, stretchy skin, aortic problems and widespread pain are each shared by several conditions. The pattern of features tells them apart, and some patterns are red flags for a heritable disorder of the arteries. Only hypermobile EDS lacks a known gene; the other types are confirmed by a genetic test. [[Malfait2017,Fairweather2025]]

Red flags for a heritable vascular disorder

These features are proposed as reasons to think of vascular EDS, or of another heritable arterial disorder, and to ask about genetic testing. They are a teaching list, not a self-diagnosis checklist.

  • Arterial rupture or dissection under age 40, spontaneous carotid-cavernous sinus fistula, spontaneous sigmoid colon perforation, or unexplained third-trimester uterine rupture. [[Demirdas2024]]
  • A family history of vEDS, or spontaneous pneumothorax with other features consistent with vEDS. [[Wang2026OJRD,Malfait2017]]
  • Thin, translucent skin with visible veins and easy bruising, and a characteristic face (thin lips, small chin, narrow nose, prominent eyes). [[Byers2025GeneReviews]]
  • Arterial tortuosity and aneurysms with wide-set eyes and a bifid uvula or cleft palate (the Loeys-Dietz triad). [[Loeys2006]]
  • Aortic root enlargement with a dislocated lens (the cardinal Marfan features). [[Loeys2010]]

The look-alikes, one by one

vEDSCOL3A1 · autosomal dominant

Vascular EDS

Arterial, bowel and uterine rupture; thin translucent skin. Joint hypermobility is often limited: 40.7% of people with a confirmed variant had it. A diagnosis made on clinical grounds alone "cannot be trusted": 9.8% of clinically diagnosed people had non-pathogenic changes. [[Shalhub2020]] See Vascular EDS.

cEDSCOL5A1 / COL5A2

Classical EDS

Skin hyperextensibility with atrophic scarring, plus generalised joint hypermobility. Only 58.66% of 75 confirmed patients had a Beighton score of 5 or more, so a low score does not exclude it. Common minor features: skin fragility (89.33%), easy bruising (86.66%). [[Ritelli2020C]]

hEDSno known gene

Hypermobile EDS

Diagnosed clinically with all three 2017 criteria; unusual skin fragility points elsewhere. Its scars must not be the papyraceous or haemosiderotic scars of classical EDS. [[Fairweather2025]] See The 2017 hEDS criteria and hEDS or HSD?

MarfanFBN1 (fibrillin-1)

Marfan syndrome

Cardinal features: aortic root aneurysm and ectopia lentis (lens dislocation, in about 60%). A systemic score of 7 or more out of 20 counts. About 1 in 5,000–10,000 people. [[Loeys2010,DietzGR-Marfan,Pollock2021,Dietz1991]]

Loeys-DietzTGFBR1/2, SMAD2/3, TGFB2/3

Loeys-Dietz syndrome

Triad of arterial tortuosity and aneurysms, wide-set eyes and bifid uvula or cleft palate; no lens dislocation. Velvety, translucent skin overlaps with vEDS, but vascular surgery is far better tolerated: one death in 59 operations. [[Loeys2006,MacCarrick2014,LoeysGR-LDS]]

OICOL1A1 / COL1A2 (≈85%)

Osteogenesis imperfecta

The hallmark is bone fragility, unlike EDS. The hEDS criteria list skeletal dysplasias such as OI among the diagnoses to exclude. A proposed COL1-related overlap disorder sits between OI and EDS. [[Marini2017,Fairweather2025,Morlino2020]]

Cutis laxaacquired or inherited

Cutis laxa

Loose, redundant skin from fragmented elastic fibres: it hangs in folds. In classical EDS, by contrast, the skin stretches and snaps back. [[Beyens2021,Malfait2024GeneReviews]]

SticklerCOL2A1, COL11A1/2, COL9A1–3

Stickler syndrome

A type II/XI collagenopathy: myopia, retinal detachment, hearing loss, cleft palate and early joint disease. The commonest inherited cause of rhegmatogenous retinal detachment. [[MortierGR-Stickler,Snead2011]]

Fibromyalgiaclinical criteria (2016)

Fibromyalgia

Widespread pain is also an hEDS Feature C item, so pain alone cannot separate them; skin, systemic and family features do. Since 2016 fibromyalgia can be diagnosed alongside another condition. [[Wolfe2016,Thwaites2022,Fairweather2025]]

Inflammatory arthritisACR/EULAR 2010

Rheumatoid and other arthritis

Overt synovitis is unusual in HSD. With an autoimmune rheumatic disease present, hEDS needs Features A and B, and Feature C cannot be counted. [[Carroll2023,Aletaha2010,Fairweather2025]]
