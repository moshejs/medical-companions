# F · Treatment: medicines, surgery & anaesthesia

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: meds, surgery, autonomic, vascular.

This pass covers drug treatment, surgery and peri-procedural care in the Ehlers-Danlos syndromes (EDS), as published. It covers pain medicines and their evidence, the POTS treatment ladder (non-drug first, then drugs), vascular EDS (vEDS) drug prevention and its trials, blood-pressure and imaging surveillance, the emergency "passport", open versus endovascular repair, local anaesthetic response, anaesthesia (airway, positioning, lines, neuraxial blocks), bleeding management, dental and periodontal EDS, and mast-cell treatments with evidence labels. Physiotherapy, exercise programmes and psychological care belong to pass E. Natural history and comorbidity belong to pass G.

Corrections and refinements to the brief:
- The BBEST celiprolol maximum dose is 400 mg/day, reached by 100 mg steps every 6 months. The Lancet abstract carries a "[DOSAGE ERROR CORRECTED]" note at this sentence.
- "Salt 3–10 g" in the brief is, in the NIH 2019 consensus, 3–10 g of sodium per day. The HRS 2015 consensus instead gives 10–12 g of sodium chloride per day. The units differ (see Open questions).
- Since the brief was written, a second randomized vEDS drug trial (ARCADE, irbesartan added to celiprolol) has reported a positive primary outcome (Circulation 2025). DiSCOVER (NCT05432466) is still recruiting, with estimated primary completion in March 2029.

## Findings

### meds · Pain medicines: what the evidence is
- The Chopra 2017 international consortium paper states that nearly 90% of people with EDS report some form of chronic pain in one study. It says treatment studies "are few and insufficient to guide management" [[Chopra2017]].
- Chopra 2017 groups management into physical therapy, medications and durable medical equipment such as cushions, compressive garments and braces. It frames pain treatment around both treating the cause (for example a dislocation) and reducing the sensation of pain (Established as expert consensus; low-level evidence) [[Chopra2017]].
- A 2026 expert review of drug treatment for hypermobility-related pain searched PubMed, Web of Science, EMBASE, ClinicalTrials.gov and WHO ICTRP. It concluded that current evidence "is low-level and does not support the efficacy of any disease-specific, repurposed, or novel drug" [[Castori2026EOP]].
- The same review advises one drug at a time, usually at a low dose, with benefit and adverse effects reassessed after a predefined trial. It says drug-only strategies are limited when pain is chronic and mixes nociceptive, neuropathic and nociplastic mechanisms (Early evidence; expert opinion) [[Castori2026EOP]].
- In a cross-sectional study of 79 people with hEDS (then "EDS hypermobility type"), 92.4% used medications, and analgesics were the most common [[Rombaut2011F]].
- In the same cohort, heavier analgesic use was associated with greater dysfunction in daily life. The study design cannot say which came first [[Rombaut2011F]].
- In a retrospective chart review of 98 people with EDS at one US rehabilitation clinic, occupational therapy and bracing were the most effective options, with 70% reporting improvement [[Song2020PainPhysician]].
- In the same review, neuropathic modulators were the least well tolerated, with 47% of patients reporting adverse effects [[Song2020PainPhysician]].
- The GeneReviews hEDS chapter says chronic pain in hEDS is "often neuropathic in nature". It recommends "pain management tailored to cause and symptoms" [[Hakim2024GR]].
- The same chapter warns that autonomic and gastrointestinal problems may limit drug choices. Its examples are common analgesics in someone with slow gut transit, and vasodilators in a person with orthostatic intolerance [[Hakim2024GR]].
- The GeneReviews classic EDS chapter states that anti-inflammatory drugs may relieve joint pain in classic EDS (expert recommendation) [[Malfait2024GR]].
- A US commercial-claims study (2005–2014) compared 4,294 people with EDS with matched controls. In adults with EDS, 62% had an opioid prescription claim versus 34.1% of controls [[Schubart2019Opioid]].
- In children in the same study, 27.5% with EDS had an opioid claim versus 13.5% of controls. Among people prescribed opioids, those with EDS had higher cumulative doses over 2 years [[Schubart2019Opioid]].
- In a national database of total hip arthroplasty (2015–2020; 238 people with EDS, propensity-matched), people with EDS were more often prescribed opioids in the 90 days before surgery (49.1% vs 34.7%) and after it (59.7% vs 41.2%) [[Kelly2024THA]].
- In that THA cohort, 14.8% of people with EDS who received opioids in the 90 days after surgery had a hip dislocation, versus 2.1% of those who did not (OR 8.13, 95% CI 1.87–35.7). The authors caution against reading this as cause and effect [[Kelly2024THA]].
- One surgical team describes opioid-induced hyperalgesia and central sensitisation in hEDS with craniocervical instability. For occipito-cervical fusion they propose opioid-minimising care: total intravenous opioid-free anaesthesia, multimodal analgesia, and lidocaine, ketamine and dexmedetomidine as opioid-sparing adjuncts (Early evidence; case-series-based protocol) [[RamirezPaesano2023]].
- A 2026 modified-Delphi panel of 15 anaesthesia and EDS experts recommends multimodal analgesia and regional anaesthesia strategies for hEDS (consensus; low-level evidence) [[Amaral2026]].

### meds · Local anaesthetics: the "doesn't go numb" problem
- In a 1990 controlled study (8 people with EDS type III, now hEDS, and 8 controls), intradermal lidocaine abolished laser pain in both groups at 5 minutes. At 1 hour only the controls' skin was still numb [[ArendtNielsen1990]].
- In the same study, EMLA cream applied for 30, 60 or 120 minutes gave no patient sufficient analgesia. Controls had full analgesia after 60 and 120 minutes [[ArendtNielsen1990]].
- In an online survey of 980 people with EDS who had dental local anaesthetic, 88% (860) recalled inadequate pain prevention. Among 249 respondents without EDS the figure was 33% (83) (p<0.001). This was a hypothesis-generating survey, prone to recall and selection bias [[Schubart2019LA]].
- Among EDS respondents in that survey who named their agent, articaine had the highest reported success rate (30%), followed by bupivacaine (25%) and mepivacaine (22%) [[Schubart2019LA]].
- In a randomized crossover trial (NCT05603741; 91 people with EDS and 44 healthy controls), subcutaneous lidocaine numbed both groups similarly at 5 minutes (p=0.2) [[Bourne2026RAPM,NCT05603741]].
- In the same trial, fewer people with EDS had "anaesthesia" at 15 minutes (60% vs 84%, p=0.006) and 30 minutes (53% vs 80%, p=0.003). The authors interpret this as a shorter duration of lidocaine effect (Early evidence; first controlled trial of this size) [[Bourne2026RAPM]].
- The registered primary outcome of NCT05603741 is the delta pain score at 5 minutes. The positive findings were at 15 and 30 minutes [[NCT05603741,Bourne2026RAPM]].
- Wiesmann 2014 advises recording any history of failed local anaesthesia (for example during dental work) and earlier failed regional blocks in the pre-operative assessment [[Wiesmann2014]].

### autonomic · POTS in EDS: how often, and what the guidelines say
- In 91 people with POTS assessed against the 2017 hEDS checklist, 28 (31%) met clinical criteria for hEDS. A further 24% had generalised joint hypermobility without meeting hEDS criteria [[Miller2020AutonNeurosci]].
- The NIH 2019 POTS consensus states that about 20–30% of people with POTS meet hEDS criteria. It adds that orthostatic intolerance and tachycardia can be found in up to 40% of people with HSD or hEDS. Both figures come from small samples or uncontrolled survey data [[Vernino2021]].
- Hakim 2017 (international EDS consortium) states that the incidence, prevalence and natural history of autonomic dysfunction in hEDS "remain unquantified". It also states that drug-response evidence in hEDS comes from case and cohort observations and short-term physiological studies, with "limited clinical trials" [[Hakim2017]].
- Hakim 2017 reserves drug treatment for moderate-to-severe impairment of daily function with an inadequate response to, or intolerance of, conservative treatment [[Hakim2017]].
- The NIH 2019 consensus states there are no FDA-approved medicines for POTS, and that evidence for many medicines used is not robust [[Vernino2021]].
- The HRS 2015 consensus explains why EDS predisposes to orthostatic intolerance. Abnormal connective tissue in dependent blood vessels can let veins distend excessively under ordinary hydrostatic pressure [[Sheldon2015]].
- The Canadian Cardiovascular Society 2020 position statement used a GRADE systematic review. It flags that many people without true POTS (symptoms without tachycardia, tachycardia without symptoms, or another overt cause) are given the label anyway [[Raj2020CCS]].

### autonomic · POTS ladder, step 1: non-drug measures
- HRS 2015 Class IIa (level B-R): a regular, structured and progressive exercise programme "can be effective" for POTS [[Sheldon2015]].
- HRS 2015 Class IIb (level E): consuming up to 2–3 L of water and 10–12 g of NaCl daily "may be considered" [[Sheldon2015]].
- HRS 2015 Class IIa (level C): an acute IV infusion of up to 2 L of saline is reasonable for short-term decompensation. Class III (level E): regular IV saline infusions are not recommended, and chronic or repeated IV cannulation is potentially harmful [[Sheldon2015]].
- HRS 2015 advises starting exercise in non-upright forms (rowing machine, recumbent cycle, swimming) to reduce orthostatic stress on the heart [[Sheldon2015]].
- The NIH 2019 consensus recommends at least 2–3 L of water per day. Sodium intake can be increased to 3–10 g daily, using table salt (1 teaspoon ≈ 2.3 g sodium), salt tablets or electrolyte solutions. It adds that the true benefit and optimal dose of salt loading still need research [[Vernino2021]].
- In a 6-day crossover diet study (14 people with POTS, 13 controls), high sodium (300 mEq/day) versus low sodium (10 mEq/day) increased plasma volume, lowered standing norepinephrine and reduced the orthostatic heart-rate rise in POTS [[Garland2021JACC]].
- Even on the high-sodium diet, upright heart rate in POTS stayed higher than in controls: median 117 vs 85 beats/min [[Garland2021JACC]].
- In a randomized crossover tilt study of 30 adults with POTS, compression lowered heart rate in a dose-dependent way. Mean heart rate was 109 beats/min with no compression, 103 with leg-only, 97 with abdomen/thigh and 92 with full abdominal-leg compression (p<0.001). Symptoms also improved [[Bourne2021JACC]].
- A commercially available abdominal-only compression garment reduced standing heart rate (median 103 vs 118 beats/min, p=0.002) and symptoms during morning stand tests. Heart rate and symptoms rose again when the garment was removed after several hours of wear [[Bourne2026CJC]].
- After a 3-month exercise-training programme in POTS (19 completers), left ventricular mass rose about 12% and blood volume about 7%. Upright heart rate fell by 9 beats/min, and 10 of 19 patients no longer met POTS criteria [[Fu2010JACC]].
- In a trial of 4 weeks of propranolol or placebo followed by 3 months of training (19 patients), training improved SF-36 physical functioning (33 to 50) and social functioning (37 to 48). Propranolol did not improve quality of life [[Fu2011Hypertension]].
- HRS 2015 Class III (level B-R): drugs that block the norepinephrine reuptake transporter can worsen POTS and should not be given [[Sheldon2015]].

### autonomic · POTS ladder, step 2: medicines (all off-label for POTS in the US)
- HRS 2015 Class IIb (level C): it "seems reasonable" to treat POTS with fludrocortisone or pyridostigmine. Class IIb (level B-R): midodrine or low-dose propranolol "may be considered" [[Sheldon2015]].
- HRS 2015 Class IIb (level E): clonidine or alpha-methyldopa for prominent hyperadrenergic features [[Sheldon2015]].
- Fludrocortisone: HRS 2015 notes that its effects on sodium retention and plasma volume might last only 1–2 days, and that it has not been tested in randomized trials in POTS [[Sheldon2015]].
- The NIH 2019 consensus adds that there is no evidence fludrocortisone actually increases blood volume. It works only alongside a high-sodium diet, with serum potassium monitored [[Vernino2021]].
- The US fludrocortisone label indication is partial replacement therapy in Addison's disease and salt-losing adrenogenital syndrome. The usual Addison dose is 0.1 mg daily. POTS is not a labelled use [[FDA-Fludrocortisone]].
- Low-dose propranolol: in a randomized crossover study of 54 people with POTS, propranolol 20 mg lowered supine and standing heart rate versus placebo. It also improved symptom burden at 2 hours (median −4.5 vs 0 units, p=0.044) [[Raj2009Circ]].
- In the high-versus-low-dose part of that study (18 patients), 80 mg lowered standing heart rate more than 20 mg. Symptoms improved more with 20 mg (−6 vs −2 units, p=0.041): "less is more" [[Raj2009Circ]].
- HRS 2015 summarises that low-dose propranolol (10–20 mg orally) acutely lowers standing heart rate and improves symptoms. Higher doses are less well tolerated, and long-acting propranolol does not improve quality of life [[Sheldon2015]].
- Propranolol's US labels (for example Inderal LA) list hypertension, angina, migraine prophylaxis and hypertrophic subaortic stenosis, not POTS [[FDA-InderalLA]].
- Ivabradine: in a double-blind placebo-controlled crossover trial in 22 people with hyperadrenergic POTS (plasma norepinephrine >600 pg/mL), ivabradine lowered heart rate (p<0.001) and improved SF-36 physical (p=0.008) and social (p=0.021) functioning. No significant bradycardia or hypotension occurred [[Taub2021JACC]].
- In a 2026 three-phase crossover trial (28 women with POTS, 4 weeks per phase), peak tilt heart rate was 99 beats/min on ivabradine, 100 on propranolol and 118 on placebo (both p<0.001 vs placebo) [[Uppal2026]].
- In the same trial, ivabradine raised systolic blood pressure more than propranolol (Δ 4.9 vs 1.9 mm Hg, p=0.001). Every participant who stated a preference chose an active drug over placebo, with no difference between the two drugs (59% vs 41%, p=0.52) [[Uppal2026]].
- The US ivabradine label (Corlanor) covers heart failure, not POTS. The adult starting dose is 5 mg twice daily (2.5 mg in vulnerable adults), with a maximum of 7.5 mg twice daily [[FDA-Corlanor]].
- The Corlanor label warns of fetal toxicity and advises effective contraception for females of reproductive potential. This matters because most people with POTS are women of childbearing age [[FDA-Corlanor,Vernino2021]].
- Corlanor (ivabradine) was first approved by the FDA on 15 April 2015 (NDA 206143, priority review) [[FDA-Corlanor]].
- Pyridostigmine: in a randomized crossover study of 17 people with POTS, a single 30 mg dose cut standing heart rate from 119 beats/min at baseline to 104 at 2 hours and 100 at 4 hours (p<0.001), with no change in blood pressure. Symptoms improved more than with placebo [[Raj2005Circ]].
- In a retrospective single-centre series of 203 people with POTS given pyridostigmine, 168 tolerated titration. Symptoms improved in 43% of all treated patients (51% of those who tolerated it). Gastrointestinal side effects affected 19% [[Kanjwal2011]].
- In a 3-month 2×2 factorial randomized trial (77 completers), propranolol and bisoprolol, each with or without pyridostigmine, all improved the orthostatic intolerance score. Mean score fell from 18.5 to 12.5 at 1 month and 7.8 at 3 months. Efficacy was comparable across regimens, and adding pyridostigmine gave no clear extra benefit [[Moon2018Neurotherapeutics,Vernino2021]].
- The US pyridostigmine label (Mestinon) indication is myasthenia gravis. POTS is not a labelled use [[FDA-Mestinon]].
- Midodrine: in a double-blind crossover trial in 20 adolescents with POTS (2.5–10 mg three times daily), midodrine reduced orthostatic tachycardia in neuropathic POTS but not in hyperadrenergic POTS [[Ross2014ClinSci]].
- HRS 2015 notes that midodrine acts briefly, must be dosed 3 times daily, and reduces orthostatic tachycardia less than IV saline [[Sheldon2015]].
- The US midodrine label carries a boxed warning: midodrine "can cause marked elevation of supine blood pressure". Its orthostatic-hypotension indication rests on a surrogate endpoint (1-minute standing systolic pressure), and clinical benefit has not been verified [[FDA-Midodrine]].
- The midodrine label recommends 10 mg three times daily in daytime hours, not after the evening meal or within 4 hours of bedtime. At single doses of 20 mg, severe and persistent supine hypertension occurs in about 45% [[FDA-Midodrine]].
- Midodrine (ProAmatine) was first approved by the FDA on 6 September 1996 (NDA 019815, priority review) [[FDA-Midodrine]].
- Droxidopa (Northera) is labelled only for neurogenic orthostatic hypotension due to primary autonomic failure, DBH deficiency and non-diabetic autonomic neuropathy. It carries a boxed warning for supine hypertension and has no POTS or EDS indication [[FDA-Northera]].
- HRS 2015 Class III (level B-NR): radiofrequency sinus-node modification, surgical correction of Chiari I malformation and jugular-vein dilation or stenting are not recommended for routine POTS care and are potentially harmful [[Sheldon2015]].

### autonomic · Mast-cell activation: treatments only with evidence labels
- The AAAAI 2019 work group framed MCAS criteria to diagnose most cases "without overdiagnosing the disorder in patients who likely have other conditions" [[Weiler2019JACI]].
- The consensus MCAS-confirming lab criterion is an event-related rise in serum tryptase of at least 20% + 2 ng/mL over the person's baseline (the "20% + 2" formula, proposed in 2012) [[Valent2019IAAI]].
- Seneviratne 2017 (EDS consortium) reviews mast-cell biology. It proposes mast-cell dysregulation as a factor in a cluster of EDS, non-IgE hypersensitivity and dysautonomia (Hypothesis) [[Seneviratne2017]].
- A 2020 systematic search found only 4–9 original research articles across hEDS, POTS and MCAS. None studied all three together, and the authors concluded no evidence-based common mechanism has been shown (Hypothesis for the "triad") [[Kucharik2020]].
- In a retrospective coded-records study of 195 people, MCAS was recorded in 31% of those with POTS plus EDS versus 2% of others (OR 32.46). Coding-based ascertainment limits this finding (Early evidence) [[Wang2021AAP]].
- A 2026 Mayo Clinic study is titled "Lack of evidence for increased mast cell activation syndrome prevalence in POTS and/or hEDS". No abstract was available to check its numbers [[Valdez2026]].
- The NIH 2019 POTS consensus states that accurate data on the frequency of MCAS in POTS are not available [[Vernino2021]].
- The GeneReviews hEDS chapter lists "avoidance of triggers for mast cell activation disorder and pharmacotherapy or monoclonal biologic therapy as needed". This is expert-level guidance, and no EDS-specific trials of mast-cell drugs were found (Hypothesis for EDS-specific benefit) [[Hakim2024GR]].
- Oral cromolyn sodium (Gastrocrom) is FDA-labelled for mastocytosis, with reported improvement in diarrhoea, flushing, headaches, vomiting, urticaria, abdominal pain, nausea and itching "in some patients". It has no EDS or MCAS-in-EDS indication [[FDA-Gastrocrom]].

### meds · Bleeding, bruising and haemostasis
- In a retrospective review of 26 children with EDS referred for bleeding symptoms, 19 (73%) had a desmopressin challenge. Mean bleeding time fell from 11.26 to 5.95 minutes (p<0.01) [[Mast2009]].
- Wiesmann 2014 reports a retrospective series of 41 surgical procedures in 26 patients. There were no bleeding complications when desmopressin (DDAVP) was given before surgery, versus 30% when it was not [[Wiesmann2014]].
- Wiesmann 2014 recommends desmopressin for people with a positive bleeding history. It suggests considering prophylactic tranexamic acid before surgery, as in other bleeding disorders [[Wiesmann2014]].
- The 2026 anaesthesia Delphi panel recommends bleeding-risk evaluation, and considering desmopressin, in classical, classical-like, kyphoscoliotic and vascular EDS [[Amaral2026]].
- In a small Toronto cohort (17 adults: 10 hEDS, 7 classical), clinically significant bleeding scores were common (86% classical, 90% hypermobile), yet ROTEM testing showed no abnormalities. This suggests bleeding in EDS is multifactorial (Early evidence) [[Lam2025CJA]].
- GeneReviews (classic EDS) states that ascorbic acid (vitamin C) "may reduce bruising" and desmopressin "may be useful to normalize bleeding time" (expert recommendation; low-level evidence) [[Malfait2024GR]].
- GeneReviews (hEDS) states that platelet disorders may respond to tranexamic or mefenamic acid [[Hakim2024GR]].

### vascular · Drug prevention in vascular EDS: celiprolol
- Celiprolol is a β1-adrenoceptor antagonist with β2-adrenoceptor agonist action [[Ong2010]].
- Its rationale is to lower arterial wall stress without excessive vasodilation or heart-rate reduction. Classical β-blockers were set aside partly to avoid chronic fatigue in young, active patients (Hypothesis for mechanism) [[Buso2024]].
- BBEST trial design: multicentre, randomised, open, with blinded endpoint assessment, at 8 centres in France and 1 in Belgium. People with clinical vEDS were assigned to 5 years of celiprolol or no treatment. 33 were COL3A1-positive (NCT00190411) [[Ong2010,NCT00190411]].
- BBEST dosing: celiprolol twice daily, uptitrated by 100 mg every 6 months to a maximum of 400 mg/day [[Ong2010]].
- BBEST result: 53 patients were randomised (25 celiprolol, 28 control). Mean follow-up was 47 months (SD 5), and the trial was stopped early for benefit [[Ong2010]].
- BBEST primary endpoint (arterial rupture or dissection, fatal or not): 5 of 25 (20%) on celiprolol versus 14 of 28 (50%) controls. HR 0.36, 95% CI 0.15–0.88, p=0.040 [[Ong2010]].
- BBEST adverse events: severe fatigue in 1 patient after starting 100 mg, and mild fatigue in 2 during uptitration [[Ong2010]].
- In a French national referral cohort (144 people with molecularly confirmed vEDS, 2000–2017, median follow-up 5.3 years), 90.3% were on celiprolol alone or combined by the end of the study [[Frank2019JACC]].
- In that cohort, people treated with celiprolol had better survival than others (p=0.0004). Protection appeared dose-dependent, with 400 mg/day better than <400 mg/day (p=0.003). This is an observational study, open to confounding [[Frank2019JACC]].
- In a Swedish referral cohort (40 COL3A1-positive patients offered celiprolol, 2011–2019, 106 patient-years), 26 of 38 (65%) reached 400 mg/day. Six could not uptitrate because of side effects [[Baderkhan2021]].
- The Swedish cohort's annual risk of a major vascular event was 4.7% (5/106 patient-years). This is similar to BBEST's celiprolol arm (5%) and lower than its control arm (12%). Four of the 5 events were fatal [[Baderkhan2021]].
- A 2025 systematic review (323 participants, studies from 2010–2023, mostly European) concluded celiprolol "appears to be a promising treatment". It noted that dizziness and hypotension sometimes limit treatment (Early evidence: one open RCT plus cohorts) [[Saputra2025]].
- The 2022 ACC/AHA aortic guideline states that celiprolol studies "have suggested a benefit … but data were considered to be insufficient" for FDA approval [[Isselbacher2022]].
- The same guideline notes that other β-blockers, sometimes with vasodilatory properties, are often prescribed instead, without efficacy data in vEDS [[Isselbacher2022]].
- Regulatory status (US): celiprolol has no FDA approval for any indication. openFDA's label and Drugs@FDA endpoints returned no celiprolol records on 2026-10-25 [[openFDA-Celiprolol,Buso2024]].
- A 2018 analysis reported that Acer Therapeutics had obtained exclusive rights to the BBEST data and intended to file for FDA approval of celiprolol (to be called Edsivo) in early 2018 [[David2018F1000]].
- Celiprolol is not available in several countries [[Buso2024]].

### vascular · Newer and ongoing drug trials
- ARCADE (NCT02597361) was a multicentre double-blind placebo-controlled RCT of irbesartan (150 mg/day, titrated to 300 mg/day) for 2 years in adults with vEDS on stable background celiprolol: 29 on irbesartan, 28 on placebo [[Jeunemaitre2025,NCT02597361]].
- ARCADE primary composite outcome (any vEDS-related arterial event, or new or worsening arterial lesions on systematic imaging): 8 of 29 (27.6%) on irbesartan versus 15 of 28 (53.6%) on placebo. HR 0.42, 95% CI 0.17–0.99, p<0.05 [[Jeunemaitre2025]].
- In ARCADE, the risk of recurrent symptomatic or non-symptomatic arterial events was lower with irbesartan (risk ratio 0.37, 95% CI 0.19–0.68, p=0.002) [[Jeunemaitre2025]].
- Irbesartan lowered systolic blood pressure by 5.4 mm Hg (baseline-adjusted), but this reduction was not related to the outcome benefit. Eleven hypotension episodes led to down-titration in 4 patients [[Jeunemaitre2025]].
- A 2026 review states that randomized evidence now supports irbesartan added to celiprolol, with expert-centre use of an ARB when celiprolol is not tolerated [[Corrao2026]].
- The 2022 ACC/AHA guideline, written before ARCADE reported, stated "There are no studies showing a benefit of ARBs in vascular Ehlers-Danlos syndrome" [[Isselbacher2022]].
- DiSCOVER (NCT05432466; sponsor Acer Therapeutics) is a phase 3, randomized, double-blind, decentralised trial of celiprolol (ACER-002) 200 mg twice daily versus placebo in COL3A1-positive vEDS, ages 15–64 [[NCT05432466]].
- DiSCOVER status: recruiting. Started 7 Nov 2022, estimated enrolment 150, estimated primary completion 1 Mar 2029, last registry update 24 Aug 2025 [[NCT05432466]].
- DiSCOVER primary endpoint: time to first vEDS-related clinical event requiring medical attention. This covers cardiac or arterial events, uterine rupture, intestinal rupture, or unexplained sudden death, over a double-blind period estimated at 40 months [[NCT05432466]].
- PREVEnt (NCT05463679) was a phase 3 placebo-controlled RCT of the PKCβ inhibitor enzastaurin 500 mg once daily (planned n=260, ages 18–60). Its status is "SUSPENDED", with the reason "Indefinite hold by company" [[NCT05463679]].
- In two vEDS mouse models with glycine substitutions, inhibitors of ERK1/2 or PKCβ prevented death from spontaneous aortic rupture. Pregnancy- and puberty-related risk was rescued by blunting oxytocin and androgen signalling (Early evidence; animal data only) [[Bowen2020JCI]].
- Mouse data on celiprolol conflict, as summarised in Buso 2024. In one model celiprolol (with doxycycline) raised aortic tensile strength, but in the Bowen models "celiprolol accelerated rather than reduced death from aortic rupture" (Hypothesis; animal data) [[Buso2024]].

### vascular · Blood pressure, lifestyle and surveillance imaging
- The 2017 international vEDS consensus recommends confirming the COL3A1 variant before applying the diagnosis, adapting lifestyle to minimise injury, and building a care team [[Byers2017]].
- The same consensus says to "maintain blood pressure in the normal range and treat hypertension aggressively" [[Byers2017]].
- The 2017 consensus also says to survey the vascular tree with Doppler ultrasound, CTA (low-radiation alternatives) or MRA "if feasible on an annual basis" (expert consensus) [[Byers2017]].
- The 2022 ACC/AHA guideline describes typical protocols. Baseline MRI or CT runs from head to pelvis, with annual imaging of any dilated or dissected segment and imaging every 2 years when the first imaging is normal. It states the right frequency is uncertain [[Isselbacher2022]].
- The ACC/AHA guideline stresses that in vEDS the aorta and arterial branches may rupture or dissect "even without significant dilation" [[Isselbacher2022]].
- An Italian expert-centre review reports follow-up "every 12 to 18 months" with office and 24-hour blood-pressure monitoring, echocardiography and whole-body CT or MR angiography. Celiprolol is titrated to 400 mg/day as tolerated, and an ARB is added if blood pressure is not controlled [[Buso2024]].
- A survey of European Reference Network (VASCERN) expert centres found all endorse monitoring, but screening intervals and imaging methods differ between centres. Evidence-based guidelines are lacking [[vanDeLaar2022]].
- GeneReviews advises regular blood-pressure monitoring so that hypertension is treated early. It lists things to avoid: collision sports, heavy lifting and extreme weight training [[Byers2025GR]].
- In the Montalcino Aortic Consortium (1,780 people with heritable aortic disease genes), arterial events beyond the aorta were most common with COL3A1 (20.8%), against 1.5% with FBN1. The authors stress smoking cessation and hypertension control [[CalderonMartinez2025]].
- COL3A1 variant type changes risk. Missense and splice-site variants make up more than 90% of alterations, and median survival (51 years overall) is lower in men and varies by mutation type. These population statistics are relevant to trial allocation [[Pepin2014]].
- In a French cohort of 215 people, the median age at first complication was 29 years. Glycine-substitution and splice/in-frame variants were more severe, and haploinsufficiency or terminal variants were milder [[Frank2015]].

### vascular · The emergency "passport" and what to avoid
- The 2017 consensus recommends individual emergency-care plans (a "vascular EDS passport") carrying the diagnosis and management plan, for use when travelling. Care should be centralised at experienced centres where feasible [[Byers2017]].
- GeneReviews advises carrying documentation of the genetic diagnosis: a MedicAlert bracelet or necklace, an emergency contact letter, or a vEDS passport [[Byers2025GR]].
- GeneReviews also advises seeking immediate medical attention for sudden, unexplained pain, coordinated through the primary-care doctor when possible [[Byers2025GR]].
- GeneReviews advises discouraging arteriography, using it only to find life-threatening bleeding before surgery. It also advises avoiding routine colonoscopy without concerning symptoms or a strong colon-cancer family history, and avoiding elective surgery unless the benefit is substantial [[Byers2025GR]].
- A 2026 review calls emergency care in vEDS "deliberately counter-intuitive" and harm-avoidant. It advises avoiding catheter angiography, elective endoscopy and unnecessary surgery, and preferring endovascular and tissue-sparing techniques [[Corrao2026]].
- In the multi-institutional US cohort (86 people with COL3A1 variants), 37.4% of 139 aortic or arterial pathologies presented as emergencies. The mesenteric arteries were most often affected (31.7%), followed by cerebrovascular (16.5%), iliac (16.5%) and renal (12.2%) arteries [[Shalhub2019JVS]].

### vascular · Interventions: arteriography, open versus endovascular repair
- A 1987 review of 36 people with EDS type IV (now vEDS: 31 from the literature plus a 5-member family) reported that arteriography had a 67% complication rate. 44% died before surgery and 19% during the operative period. Historical case series; ascertainment bias likely [[Cikrit1987]].
- The same 1987 review advised managing bleeding non-operatively when possible, and repairing bleeding vessels with buttressed, tension-free sutures or ligating them [[Cikrit1987]].
- In the Mayo Clinic 30-year series (31 patients, 1971–2001), 15 patients had 30 operations for vascular complications. Procedure-related morbidity was 46%, including 37% postoperative bleeding and a 20% need for re-exploration [[Oderich2005]].
- In the same series, 3 hospital deaths came from exsanguinating haemorrhage. Late graft-related complications affected 40% of arterial reconstructions [[Oderich2005]].
- A 2013 systematic review of 231 published patients reported mortality of 30% after open surgery, 24% after endovascular procedures, 60% in miscellaneous cases and 39% overall. Median age at death was 31 years. The authors note likely publication bias and that only 24% of recent cases were molecularly confirmed [[Bergqvist2013]].
- Bergqvist 2013 concluded that invasive methods "should be used only when necessary, primarily to save the patients' life" [[Bergqvist2013]].
- In a single US tertiary centre (1994–2009; 40 people with classic, hypermobile or vascular EDS; 45 endovascular and 18 open elective procedures), endovascular procedures had no procedure-related deaths. Open procedures had 1 in-hospital death, in a person with vEDS [[Brooke2010]].
- In that centre, 5-year survival free of any complication was 85% after endovascular and 54% after open procedures. The authors argued elective repair need not be withheld until rupture. Only 9 of the 40 had vEDS [[Brooke2010]].
- In the 11-institution US cohort, most vEDS arterial pathology was managed medically. When intervention was needed, embolisation of medium-sized arteries (13.4%) and stenting (2.5%) were the main endovascular approaches [[Shalhub2019JVS]].
- In the same cohort, 4 people had successful open abdominal aortic aneurysm repair. The authors concluded vEDS "should not be a deterrent to offering an operation" when one is needed [[Shalhub2019JVS]].
- The 2022 ACC/AHA guideline recommends open repair over endovascular repair for intact thoracoabdominal aneurysm needing intervention in Marfan, Loeys-Dietz or vascular EDS [[Isselbacher2022]].
- The ACC/AHA guideline states that no data guide diameter thresholds for prophylactic repair in vEDS. Decisions rest with a multidisciplinary aortic team and shared decision-making, with meticulous open technique, embolisation or endovascular therapy as circumstances dictate [[Isselbacher2022]].
- In older natural-history series, more than 80% of people with vEDS had at least one complication by age 40, and bowel rupture (often sigmoid colon) accounted for about a quarter of complications. These are population statistics [[Pepin2000]].

### surgery · Anaesthesia and peri-operative care
- Wiesmann 2014 notes that EDS anaesthesia knowledge "is fragmented and consists mostly of case reports, small case series and expert opinion" [[Wiesmann2014]].
- The 2026 modified-Delphi survey (15 experts, 3 rounds) reached consensus on 7 guidelines. These include comprehensive airway assessment for all EDS types and planning around common comorbidities. It describes them as the first consensus-based anaesthesia practice guidelines for EDS [[Amaral2026]].
- Airway: mask ventilation, intubation and laryngeal mask are all possible. Care is needed during mask ventilation to avoid dislocating the temporomandibular joint [[Wiesmann2014]].
- Airway: repeated intubation attempts may cause bleeding, and smaller tubes may reduce mucosal damage. Cuff pressure should be checked often and kept as low as possible, and airway pressure minimised because of pneumothorax risk [[Wiesmann2014]].
- Difficult intubation should be expected more often than in the general population, owing to TMJ dysfunction, early spondylosis or occipito-atlanto-axial instability. Elective fibreoptic intubation should be considered when difficulty is anticipated [[Wiesmann2014]].
- Subclinical cervical-spine instability may be present even when neck and TMJ mobility seem normal [[Wiesmann2014]].
- Positioning: use padding to reduce shear and pressure. Position and mobilise carefully to avoid joint dislocation. Careless positioning risks brachial plexus injury and visual loss from pressure on the eye [[Wiesmann2014]].
- Skin: adhesive tapes and dressings should be easy to remove, or avoided, because of the risk of severe skin damage. Repeated non-invasive blood-pressure cuff inflations can cause bruising and haematoma [[Wiesmann2014]].
- Lines in vEDS: Wiesmann 2014 recommends avoiding central venous access and arterial puncture whenever possible in fragile-vessel subtypes, and avoiding invasive blood-pressure monitoring in non-emergency surgery in vEDS [[Wiesmann2014]].
- If a central line is needed (high-risk surgery or emergency), ultrasound guidance is "mandatory", including seeing the wire inside the vessel. Ultrasound may also reduce repeated vascular punctures generally [[Wiesmann2014]].
- Tourniquets: the authors report anecdotal knowledge of severe and lethal complications from tourniquets in minor limb surgery in vEDS, including massive compartment bleeding and haemorrhagic shock [[Wiesmann2014]].
- Neuraxial blocks: Wiesmann 2014 recommends avoiding spinal or epidural anaesthesia in vEDS, because benefits are unclear and risks are higher than with planned general anaesthesia. It considers neuraxial blocks feasible in other EDS types after spinal assessment [[Wiesmann2014]].
- The 2026 Delphi panel likewise advises cautious use of regional or neuraxial anaesthesia in vEDS, with individual risk-benefit analysis because of haematoma risk [[Amaral2026]].
- Obstetric anaesthesia: there is no agreement on the best delivery method, because uterine rupture and poor wound healing can complicate both vaginal and caesarean delivery. Cross-matching enough blood, cell salvage and prophylactic DDAVP should be discussed [[Wiesmann2014]].
- In vEDS, pregnancy-related deaths occurred in 30 of 565 deliveries (5.3%). Data were insufficient to show whether mode or timing of delivery changed risk. Population statistic from pedigree review [[Murray2014GenetMed]].

### surgery · Wound closure, joints and the cervical spine
- Classic EDS wound care (GeneReviews): close dermal wounds without tension, preferably in two layers. Apply deep stitches generously, leave skin stitches in place twice as long as usual, and tape the adjacent skin edges to prevent the scar stretching [[Malfait2024GR]].
- Classic EDS (GeneReviews): young children with fragile skin can wear pads or bandages over the forehead, knees and shins. Older children can wear soccer pads or ski stockings with shin padding during activities [[Malfait2024GR]].
- In the hEDS cohort of 79, 70.9% had undergone surgery, mainly on the limbs and abdomen. Only 33.9% of those operated on reported a positive outcome [[Rombaut2011F]].
- Surgical and anaesthetic risk differs by EDS type: the 2026 Delphi panel gives type-specific advice (bleeding work-up for classical, classical-like, kyphoscoliotic and vascular EDS; multimodal and regional strategies for hEDS; caution with neuraxial blocks in vEDS), so vEDS fragility precautions are not applied to every type by default [[Amaral2026]].
- The EDS consortium's neurology paper describes craniocervical instability, basilar invagination and Chiari I malformation as reported in EDS. It states that consensus criteria and practice guidelines "based upon stronger epidemiological and pathophysiological evidence, are needed" (Early evidence) [[Henderson2017]].
- Occipito-cervical fixation in hEDS with craniocervical instability is described in case series with opioid-minimising peri-operative protocols. No controlled outcome data were found in this pass (Early evidence) [[RamirezPaesano2023]].
- HRS 2015 lists surgical correction of Chiari I malformation as not recommended for routine use in POTS (Class III) [[Sheldon2015]].
- GeneReviews (hEDS) notes that chiropractic adjustment and yoga are not contraindicated. Like all physical treatments, though, they must avoid causing subluxations or dislocations [[Hakim2024GR]].

### surgery · Dental and periodontal care
- GeneReviews (hEDS) lists orthodontic, maxillofacial and ENT management for a narrow palate, crowded teeth and TMJ laxity and dysfunction. Periodontal disease gets standard treatment [[Hakim2024GR]].
- Oral and TMJ involvement varies across EDS types, and TMJ disorder interacts with wider musculoskeletal dysfunction [[Mitakides2017]].
- Periodontal EDS (pEDS) is autosomal dominant and caused by heterozygous variants in C1R or C1S, which encode subunits of complement C1. Features include rapidly progressive periodontitis starting in childhood or the teens, lack of attached gingiva, pretibial hyperpigmentation and easy bruising [[KapfererSeebacher2016]].
- In 35 adults with genetically confirmed pEDS, 94% had stage 3–4 periodontitis or were edentulous from periodontal destruction. First permanent tooth loss came at a median age of 21.5 years (range 13–43) [[Lepperdinger2022]].
- The probability of having no teeth at age 35–44 was 28–47% in pEDS, versus under 0.25% in the general population. Pockets were mostly shallow (94% under 4 mm), but clinical attachment loss averaged 8 mm [[Lepperdinger2022]].
- GeneReviews (pEDS) advises regular care by a periodontist from early childhood. Supportive periodontal care (re-evaluation, hygiene instruction, supra- and sub-gingival debridement) is recommended every 3–6 months [[KapfererSeebacher2021GR]].
- GeneReviews (pEDS) notes that prosthetic rehabilitation after tooth loss is challenging because most alveolar bone is destroyed [[KapfererSeebacher2021GR]].
- For dental local anaesthesia in EDS, survey data suggest articaine had the highest reported success, and failure was reported nearly three times as often as in people without EDS. See the local anaesthetic subsection [[Schubart2019LA]].

### Exam hooks
- BBEST: celiprolol in vEDS, 53 patients, events 20% vs 50% at a mean 47 months, HR 0.36 (95% CI 0.15–0.88). The trial was open-label and stopped early for benefit [[Ong2010]].
- Celiprolol = β1 antagonist + β2 agonist, titrated by 100 mg every 6 months to 400 mg/day [[Ong2010]].
- Celiprolol is not FDA-approved; the ACC/AHA judged the data insufficient for approval [[Isselbacher2022,openFDA-Celiprolol]].
- ARCADE: irbesartan added to celiprolol reduced the composite arterial outcome, 27.6% vs 53.6% at 2 years (HR 0.42) [[Jeunemaitre2025]].
- In vEDS, avoid diagnostic arteriography: one historical series reported a 67% complication rate [[Cikrit1987,Byers2025GR]].
- In vEDS, arteries can rupture "even without significant dilation", so there is no safe diameter threshold [[Isselbacher2022]].
- vEDS surveillance: baseline head-to-pelvis CT or MR, then annually if abnormal or every 2 years if normal (ACC/AHA typical protocol) [[Isselbacher2022]].
- Avoid neuraxial anaesthesia and invasive lines in vEDS where possible; use ultrasound guidance if a central line is unavoidable [[Wiesmann2014]].
- Lidocaine in EDS: equal effect at 5 minutes, but numbness at 30 minutes in 53% vs 80% of controls, suggesting a shorter duration [[Bourne2026RAPM]].
- Dental local anaesthetic failure was recalled by 88% of people with EDS vs 33% without (survey) [[Schubart2019LA]].
- POTS fluids and salt (HRS 2015): 2–3 L water and 10–12 g NaCl per day, Class IIb [[Sheldon2015]].
- POTS propranolol: low dose (10–20 mg) beats high dose for symptoms ("less is more") [[Raj2009Circ,Sheldon2015]].
- Midodrine boxed warning: supine hypertension; no doses after the evening meal or within 4 hours of bedtime [[FDA-Midodrine]].
- Ivabradine label warning: fetal toxicity, so effective contraception is needed [[FDA-Corlanor]].
- There are no FDA-approved medicines for POTS [[Vernino2021]].
- Classic EDS wounds: two-layer, tension-free closure; skin sutures left twice as long [[Malfait2024GR]].
- Desmopressin roughly halved bleeding time in children with EDS (11.3 to 6.0 min) [[Mast2009]].
- pEDS is caused by C1R/C1S (complement) variants; first tooth loss at a median of 21.5 years [[KapfererSeebacher2016,Lepperdinger2022]].
- MCAS confirmation needs an event-related tryptase rise of at least 20% + 2 ng/mL over baseline [[Valent2019IAAI]].

### In plain words
- No medicine has been proven in good trials to treat EDS pain. Doctors usually try one medicine at a time, at a low dose, and check whether it helps [[Castori2026EOP]].
- People with EDS are prescribed opioid painkillers much more often than others. Opioids carry risks, so many teams try other options first [[Schubart2019Opioid,RamirezPaesano2023]].
- Numbing injections often wear off faster in EDS. Tell your dentist or doctor if numbing has failed before, so they can plan [[Bourne2026RAPM,Wiesmann2014]].
- For POTS, the first steps are drinking more fluid, eating more salt if your doctor agrees, wearing compression, and slowly building up exercise, starting lying or sitting down [[Sheldon2015,Vernino2021]].
- Some heart-rate and blood-pressure medicines can help POTS, but none is officially approved for it [[Vernino2021]].
- In vascular EDS, a medicine called celiprolol lowered the number of artery tears in one trial. It is not approved in the US [[Ong2010,Isselbacher2022]].
- People with vascular EDS should carry an emergency card or letter that says they have vEDS. Sudden, unexplained pain needs urgent care [[Byers2025GR,Byers2017]].
- In vascular EDS, doctors try to avoid tests and operations that go inside the arteries, unless they are needed to save a life [[Byers2025GR,Corrao2026]].
- Before any operation, tell the team about EDS. They may plan extra padding, gentle positioning, careful breathing-tube placement and steps to reduce bleeding [[Wiesmann2014]].
- Cuts in classic EDS heal best with careful stitches that stay in longer than usual [[Malfait2024GR]].
- If you have periodontal EDS, see a gum specialist every few months from childhood to protect your teeth [[KapfererSeebacher2021GR]].
- The link between EDS and mast-cell problems is still unproven. Treatments for it are not well studied in EDS [[Kucharik2020,Hakim2024GR]].

## Open questions and controversies
- How strong is the celiprolol evidence? Position A: BBEST showed HR 0.36, and two national cohorts show low event rates on 400 mg/day, so celiprolol is the treatment of choice [[Ong2010,Frank2019JACC,Baderkhan2021]]. Position B: BBEST was open-label, small (53, only 33 COL3A1-confirmed) and stopped early, and US regulators judged the data insufficient [[Isselbacher2022,Buso2024]]. Status: DiSCOVER, a placebo-controlled phase 3 trial, is recruiting with an estimated primary completion in 2029 [[NCT05432466]].
- Animal data on celiprolol conflict: one mouse model shows stronger aortic walls, while the Bowen glycine-substitution models show faster death from rupture. The human relevance is unknown (Hypothesis) [[Buso2024,Bowen2020JCI]].
- ARBs: before 2025 there were "no studies showing a benefit" [[Isselbacher2022]]. ARCADE then showed irbesartan added to celiprolol reduced arterial events (HR 0.42, 95% CI upper bound 0.99, 57 patients) [[Jeunemaitre2025]]. Status: one small positive RCT, not yet replicated. Whether ARBs help without celiprolol is untested in an RCT.
- Open versus endovascular repair in vEDS: a systematic review shows high mortality with both (30% vs 24%) [[Bergqvist2013]]. Single-centre and multi-centre US series report good elective outcomes and say vEDS should not deter needed surgery [[Brooke2010,Shalhub2019JVS]]. The ACC/AHA favours open repair for intact thoracoabdominal aneurysm [[Isselbacher2022]]. Status: case series only, so decisions rest with a multidisciplinary team.
- Surveillance interval: annual imaging [[Byers2017]], versus 12–18 months [[Buso2024]], versus every 2 years when baseline imaging is normal [[Isselbacher2022]]. European centres vary [[vanDeLaar2022]]. Status: no evidence-based interval.
- Salt dose for POTS: HRS gives 10–12 g of NaCl/day (about 3.9–4.7 g sodium by arithmetic) [[Sheldon2015]], while NIH gives 3–10 g of sodium/day [[Vernino2021]]. Status: both are low-level recommendations; one short crossover trial supports high sodium physiologically [[Garland2021JACC]].
- Local anaesthetic resistance: survey data suggest very frequent failure [[Schubart2019LA]], while a controlled forearm trial found equal onset but shorter duration [[Bourne2026RAPM]]. Status: the mechanism is unknown, and agent-specific comparisons rest only on recalled survey data.
- hEDS, POTS and MCAS "triad": some report a strong association (MCAS 31% vs 2%) [[Wang2021AAP]], while systematic review and a 2026 Mayo study find no robust evidence [[Kucharik2020,Valdez2026]]. Status: unresolved, and treatment for mast-cell symptoms in EDS has no EDS-specific trial evidence.
- Craniocervical instability surgery in hEDS: described in case series [[RamirezPaesano2023]], with consortium calls for stronger evidence [[Henderson2017]]. Status: no controlled outcome data found.
- Opioids: higher use in EDS is documented [[Schubart2019Opioid]], and opioid use after hip replacement is associated with dislocation [[Kelly2024THA]]. Status: associations only, confounded by disease severity.
