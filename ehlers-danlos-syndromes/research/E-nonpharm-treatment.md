# E · Treatment: physiotherapy, exercise & psychological care

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: physio, pain.

This pass covers non-drug care for the Ehlers-Danlos syndromes (EDS) and hypermobility spectrum disorder (HSD). It includes exercise and physiotherapy trials with their doses, durations, dropout and effect sizes, and stabilisation, pacing and graded progression. It also covers orthoses, splints, compression garments, insoles and taping; occupational therapy; pain-management programmes, CBT and other psychological care; fatigue and sleep; kinesiophobia; school, work and participation; safety; vascular EDS (vEDS) activity limits; and the outcome measures used in trials. Every source was checked against PubMed E-utilities. All trial numbers come from the PubMed abstract, and some come from the open-access full text in Europe PMC (named in Provenance).

Access notes:
- The full text of the 2017 physical therapy consensus [[Engelbert2017]] is paywalled. Only its abstract-level statements are used. Its detailed advice (for example on end-range stretching, hydrotherapy or return to sport) could not be read, so none of it is quoted here.
- NCBI Bookshelf (GeneReviews) was blocked by a browser check. The vEDS activity advice therefore comes from the open-access Orphanet review [[Germain2007]] and the consensus abstract [[Byers2017]].
- The Cochrane review of treatment for symptomatic hypermobility in childhood [[Quinlan2026]] is still a protocol (September 2026) and has no results yet.
- No trial of hydrotherapy (aquatic therapy) in hEDS/HSD was found in PubMed. No trial of finger "ring" splints was found either. The one finger-orthosis study is a 14-person repeated-measures study [[Jensen2021Orth]].
- Most trials enrol hEDS/HSD (or older labels: joint hypermobility syndrome, JHS; benign joint hypermobility syndrome, BJHS; EDS hypermobility type, EDS-HT). Results should not be carried over to the rarer genetic EDS types without care. The 2017 classification recognises 13 types, and all except hEDS need molecular confirmation [[Malfait2017]]. HSD is the label for symptomatic joint hypermobility that does not meet any other diagnosis, including hEDS [[Castori2017]].

## Findings

### physio · The overall evidence base
- The 2017 international physical therapy consensus says the management evidence for JHS/hEDS "is limited in size and quality". Until multicentre RCTs exist, decisions should rest on theory and the limited research. It uses the ICF (functions, activities, participation, environmental and personal factors) as its framework [[Engelbert2017]].
- The Nature Reviews primer says management is a care team that watches for major and organ-specific complications plus "integrated physical medicine and rehabilitation". It states that no specific medical or genetic therapy exists for any EDS type [[Malfait2020]].
- A 2018 systematic review screened 1,709 papers and found only one cohort study that met its criteria (a combined physiotherapy and CBT programme, rated poor evidence). It concluded there was then "no Evidence Based Medicine literature" on hEDS rehabilitation (Established: evidence is thin) [[Corrado2018]].
- Reychler 2021 systematic review: 6 RCTs, each with 20–57 patients, with programmes of 4–8 weeks. Pain or proprioception improved in the intervention groups whatever the intervention type, and quality of life "was systematically improved" (Early evidence) [[Reychler2021]].
- Palmer 2021 systematic review: 11 studies (8 controlled trials, 3 cohorts), all exercise-based. Only 3 small controlled studies showed benefit over a control. Those 3 covered a single body area, recruited only women and had no long-term follow-up. Evidence for superiority over comparators was "weak", but improvement over time was consistent [[Palmer2021]].
- Attrition in the Palmer 2021 trials ranged from 0% to 44%. The highest was the children's generalised vs targeted trial (32 of 57 completed). The HTA pilot of advice vs advice plus physiotherapy lost 34% [[Palmer2021]].
- Buryk-Iggers 2022 systematic review (adults, all EDS types plus HSD): 10 studies, 330 participants (5 RCTs, 2 rated high quality). All studies showed some improvement in a physical and/or psychological outcome. Only 1 non-serious adverse event was possibly linked to an intervention (<1% of intervention participants) (Early evidence) [[BurykIggers2022]].
- Scheper 2016 meta-analysis (adolescents and adults with HMS/EDS-HT): pain (r=.64), fatigue (r=.91) and psychological distress (r=.86) were each significantly linked to disability. Physical and cognitive treatments reduced pain, but "treatment effectiveness on disability was not established" [[Scheper2016]].
- Peterson 2018 systematic review of children with lower-limb symptoms: 2 RCTs (86 children) and no clear benefit of one physical therapy over another. No physical therapy has been compared with sham or no treatment, and no mechanical therapy (for example orthoses) had been tested in an RCT [[Peterson2018]].
- hEDS is described largely in people with musculoskeletal complaints: joint hypermobility, subluxations and dislocations, and skin and soft-tissue features. Many report activity-related pain, and some go on to daily pain. This is the clinical picture physiotherapy targets [[Tinkle2017]].
- The UK NIHR HTA feasibility study (pilot RCT, 29 participants) compared a one-off advice session with advice plus six 30-minute physiotherapy sessions over 4 months. Recruitment was hard because patients did not see equipoise. Physiotherapy showed promise, the advice-only arm had more adverse events, and the authors judged a definitive RCT feasible [[Palmer2016]].
- A 2022 masterclass recommends early recognition and a biopsychosocial, patient-empowerment approach to functional restoration [[Simmonds2022]].
- In a 2011 Belgian survey of 79 people with EDS-HT, 51.9% were in a physical therapy programme. Only 63.4% of those reported a positive outcome, compared with 33.9% of those who had surgery [[Rombaut2011E]].
- The 2019 Perspective in Physical Therapy notes that many people with HSD/hEDS report pain and iatrogenic injuries "due to inappropriate interventions", and calls for a holistic, multisystem approach [[Russek2019]].
- The first Chinese EDS guidelines (GRADE method, 29 recommendations, experts from 24 fields) describe rehabilitation as "long-term participation". Personalised clinic- or home-based plans target joint instability, hypotonia, joint pain, low bone density, breathlessness, writing difficulty and POTS-related symptoms [[Xu2024EDS]].

### physio · Proprioception and stabilisation trials
- Ferrell 2004 (JHS, 18 participants): an 8-week home programme of progressive closed kinetic chain exercises improved knee proprioceptive acuity in 16 of 18 people (P<0.001). Balance (P<0.001), quadriceps and hamstring strength, pain (P=0.003) and SF-36 physical functioning (P=0.029) and mental health (P=0.008) also improved. There was no control group [[Ferrell2004]].
- Sahin 2008 (BJHS): knee proprioception was impaired compared with 30 healthy controls. Fifteen patients did 8 weeks of clinic-based proprioceptive exercise and 25 served as controls. Pain (VAS) fell and occupational activity improved only in the exercise group [[Sahin2008]].
- Daman 2019 RCT (24 patients with hypermobility syndrome): combined closed kinetic chain and proprioceptive exercise, 3 days a week for 4 weeks, improved knee proprioception, pain intensity and quality of life compared with no intervention [[Daman2019]].
- Toprak Celenay 2017 RCT (women with BJHS, exercise n=20 vs control n=18): lumbar spinal stabilisation exercise, 3 days a week for 8 weeks, improved pain, trunk muscle endurance and dynamic stability (eyes open) compared with controls [[ToprakCelenay2017]].
- A 2021 non-randomised neurocognitive programme (18 people with hEDS and chronic low back pain, 3 months) improved pain (NRS, P=0.003), fatigue (P=0.03), fear of movement (Tampa, P=0.003) and Oswestry disability (P=0.03) [[Celletti2021]].
- Weakness is a target in its own right. In 30 people with EDS, fatigue severity correlated with measured muscle weakness (hand-held dynamometry r=0.461) and self-reported weakness (r=0.603), and both weakness and pain predicted fatigue [[Voermans2011]].

### physio · Strength and load: the Danish shoulder trials
- Liaghat 2022 RCT (100 adults with HSD and shoulder pain and/or instability ≥3 months, primary care): 16 weeks of shoulder exercise 3 times a week. HEAVY (full range, high load, supervised twice a week) was compared with LIGHT (neutral to mid-range, low load, 3 supervised sessions in total) [[Liaghat2022BJSM]].
- At 16 weeks, 93% were followed up. WOSI (0–2100, lower is better; MID 252) favoured HEAVY by −174.5 points (95% CI −341.4 to −7.7). This is statistically significant but smaller than the MID [[Liaghat2022BJSM]].
- Harms in that trial: no serious adverse events. HEAVY had more transient muscle soreness (56% vs 37%) and headaches (40% vs 20%) [[Liaghat2022BJSM]].
- In HEAVY, more than two-thirds improved above the WOSI MID, and 64% reported an important improvement in physical symptoms [[Liaghat2022BJSM]].
- HEAVY dosing (full text): weeks 1–3 were familiarisation at 3×10 repetitions, at 50%, 70% and 90% of the estimated 10-repetition maximum (RM). Weeks 4–9 were 3 sets of 10 RM, weeks 10–15 were 4 sets of 8 RM, and week 16 was a taper. Pain below 5/10 on a numerical rating scale was allowed during exercise [[Liaghat2022BJSM]].
- At 1 year (86/100 followed up), the WOSI difference still favoured HEAVY (−92.9; 95% CI −257.4 to 71.5; p=0.268) but was not significant. Only the WOSI emotions subdomain favoured HEAVY (−36.3; 95% CI −65.4 to −7.3) [[Liaghat2024BJSM]].
- Pain over the 16 weeks fell by 0.89 NRS in HEAVY (95% CI 0.4 to 1.4) and 0.33 in LIGHT (95% CI −0.25 to 0.91), with no between-group difference. Exercise-induced pain stayed below 0.5 NRS throughout, "challenging previous beliefs" that heavy loading flares pain (Early evidence) [[Liaghat2025]].
- In the same trial, higher baseline self-efficacy, expecting a full recovery, lower fear of movement and shorter symptom duration were linked to better outcomes [[Dolso2023]].
- For every 10% gain in external-rotation or scaption strength, the odds of reporting an important improvement rose (adjusted OR 1.18 and 1.30). The authors judged the clinical relevance limited [[Christensen2026Shoulder]].
- The earlier 12-person feasibility study had 100% retention and 83% adherence. Four people had short-lasting soreness or pain. WOSI improved 51% (1037 to 509 points), and shoulder strength rose 28–31% [[Liaghat2020]].
- The trial authors note that many clinicians avoid high-load training in HSD because of safety worries and guidelines that advise against it. [[Liaghat2022BJSM]].
- In 100 people with HSD and shoulder complaints, 67 reported mechanical symptoms. Of those, 25% had a previous shoulder dislocation (vs 3% without mechanical symptoms) and 64% said the shoulder felt loose [[Liaghat2022APMR]].
- A 2024 systematic review of shoulder care in hEDS/HSD found only 4 eligible studies (7 interventions: exercise, kinesiology taping, elasticised compression orthoses), lasting 48 hours to 24 weeks. Effect sizes for WOSI, pain, function and strength were positive over time. Effects on kinesiophobia were small to negligible [[Higo2024]].
- Spanhove 2023 RCT (21 people with hEDS/HSD and multidirectional shoulder instability): two 6-month home programmes did not differ. Both improved WOSI by 240 points at 12 weeks (p=0.02) and 325 points at 24 weeks (p=0.001). Tampa kinesiophobia did not change (p=0.12) [[Spanhove2023]].
- Luder 2021 RCT (51 women, Beighton ≥6/9): 12 weeks of self-guided leg and trunk resistance training twice a week did not improve strength or muscle mass compared with controls. 63% did more than 80% of sessions, 4 stopped early, and 1 adverse event was not clearly linked to training [[Luder2021]].
- An ongoing RCT (HIPEr-Knee) compares high-load strength training with standard low-intensity care in young adults with knee hypermobility and knee pain [[Liaghat2024Protocol]].

### physio · Children: trials, dose and setting
- Kemp 2010 RCT (57 children aged 7–16): a 6-week generalised fitness programme (n=27) and a targeted joint-control programme (n=30) both reduced child and parent pain scores (P<0.05), with no difference between them. Parental global assessment favoured targeted physiotherapy (−21.29; 95% CI −40.03 to −2.55) [[Kemp2010]].
- Pacey 2013 RCT (26 children aged 7–16 with JHS and knee pain): 8 weeks of exercise into the hypermobile range (n=12) or only to neutral knee extension (n=14). Worst knee pain fell by a mean 14.5 mm on a VAS (95% CI 5.2–23.8) whichever group the child was in, with no adverse events [[Pacey2013]].
- In that trial, parents rated psychosocial health better with exercise into the hypermobile range (p=0.009) and physical health better with neutral-only exercise (p=0.037) [[Pacey2013]].
- Pacey 2013 dose (full text): 6 sessions over 8 weeks (weekly for 4 weeks, then every 2 weeks). Each session lasted 30–60 minutes, plus 3–5 home exercises at least 5 times a week, no more than 30 minutes each. Exercises progressed by competency (more repetitions, more resistance, more complex tasks) [[Pacey2013]].
- Bale 2019 "Bendy Study" RCT (119 children aged 5–16): an 8-week tailored programme of physiotherapy plus occupational therapy, including a school visit, was compared with standard advice. It gave no additional benefit at 12 months (105 completed). Pain, coordination and grip strength improved in both arms [[Bale2019]].
- The Bendy Study authors conclude that, for most children, education and positive messages promoting healthy exercise and self-management gave "significant benefit without the need for more complex interventions" [[Bale2019]].
- A 2021 systematic review found 6 studies (388 children) using 20 different outcome instruments. None was validated specifically for symptomatic hypermobility. It recommends core outcomes for pain, function, quality of life and fatigue [[Maarj2021]].
- Pilates in children: a single-case design (3 children aged 8–12, 8 weeks) showed less fatigue in 2 of 3 and some strength gains, with no change in pain or postural control (Early evidence) [[Hornsby2024]].
- A 10-week group running programme with home exercise (8 children aged 10–15) was feasible. Attendance was ≥90%, 62% did the home programme at least twice a week, and there was one minor adverse event. Leg stiffness and kinesiophobia improved [[Chappell2026]].

### physio · Pilates, breathing and multidisciplinary rehabilitation
- Russek 2026 pragmatic trial: an online modified Pilates programme for symptomatic hypermobility (8 weeks, ~25-minute modules, at least 3 days a week; 200 Pilates vs 220 waitlist). It improved BIoH, body awareness (BARQ) and Tampa kinesiophobia compared with waitlist (p<0.001 each), and gains held at 6 months. Changes were "modest", physical activity (IPAQ) did not change, and dropout was high (Early evidence) [[Russek2026]].
- The programme studied is the intellectual property of a co-author's organisation (The Zebra Club). This is a declared conflict of interest [[Russek2026]].
- Reychler 2019 (women with hEDS): inspiratory muscle strength was below 80% of predicted in 77% (80/104). A 6-week inspiratory muscle training RCT (20 per group) raised SNIP from 41 to 49 cmH2O, 6-minute walk from 455 to 515 m and FEV1 from 94% to 103% predicted (Early evidence) [[Reychler2019]].
- Breathlessness matters: in a 2026 GoodHope clinic cohort, 74.5% (35/47) reported moderate to severe dyspnoea (MRC ≥2). It was linked to a 16.9-point lower SF-36 physical score, and 57% of those with Fitbit data walked fewer than 5,000 steps a day [[AlKaabi2026]].
- Hakimi 2020 (retrospective, hEDS outpatient rehabilitation): 6-minute walk distance rose from 439.4 to 491.8 m and was maintained 6 weeks later (p=0.001). Kinesiophobia (p=0.033), fatigue impact (p=0.01) and vitality (p=0.001) also improved [[Hakimi2020]].
- Hakimi 2023 non-randomised controlled study: a 9-week control period with no change, then a 9-week programme (25 included, 22 completed, 19 followed up). Exercise capacity, eyes-closed balance, fatigue and quality of life improved, and exercise capacity, kinesiophobia, depression and hyperventilation were still better at 6 months (Early evidence) [[Hakimi2023]].
- Bathen 2013 pilot (12 women): 2.5 weeks inpatient, then 3 months of home exercise with weekly local physiotherapy, then a 4-day readmission. Everyone completed it. Daily activity, strength and endurance improved and kinesiophobia fell, with smaller changes in pain [[Bathen2013]].
- Van Meulenbroek 2020 (14 adolescents with G-HSD/hEDS, physical training plus exposure in vivo): functional disability improved by 67% (p<0.01). Perceived harmfulness of activity and pain intensity also fell. Pre-post design, no control (Early evidence) [[VanMeulenbroek2020]].
- The GoodHope (Toronto) GEAR model combines exercise and rehabilitation therapy, self-management education and links to community resources for people with all EDS types and G-HSD. Outcome data were still being collected when it was published [[Mittal2021]].
- A 2026 systematic review lists the main approaches for JHS as education, mindfulness and body-stability training, exercise and physical therapy, psychological counselling, occupational therapy, TENS, limb splinting and foot orthoses. It found evidence for effects on extra-articular symptoms limited [[Jari2026]].

### physio · Graded progression, pacing and POTS-friendly exercise
- The trials share one pattern: start supervised and below symptom thresholds, then progress by competency or load. Examples are 3 familiarisation weeks before full loading [[Liaghat2022BJSM]] and competency-based progression in children [[Pacey2013]].
- Programme durations in trials: 4 weeks [[Daman2019]], 6 weeks [[Kemp2010,Reychler2019]], 8 weeks [[Ferrell2004,Sahin2008,ToprakCelenay2017,Pacey2013,Bale2019]], 12 weeks [[Luder2021]], 16 weeks [[Liaghat2022BJSM]] and 24 weeks [[Spanhove2023]].
- A 2024 scoping review of exercise for POTS with joint hypermobility found 10 studies (2 RCTs; only one small EDS subset). In the wider POTS population, about 3 months of endurance training followed by resistance training, moving from horizontal to upright positions, reduced symptoms and improved quality of life (Early evidence for hypermobility) [[Peebles2024]].
- The 2017 autonomic consensus lists non-drug POTS and orthostatic care in hEDS as education, managing the environment to avoid triggers, improving cardiovascular fitness and keeping hydrated. Drugs are reserved for moderate to severe impairment that does not respond [[Hakim2017]].
- Activity pacing is recommended by patients and clinicians (training in activity pacing was a prioritised behaviour-change technique), but no trial has tested pacing alone in hEDS/HSD (Hypothesis) [[Bennett2022]].
- Occupational therapy guidance for EDS with POTS covers environmental changes, adaptive equipment and orthoses, exercise and fall prevention, energy conservation and pacing, sleep hygiene, and routines and habits. This is a descriptive paper, not a trial [[Levine2022OT]].
- In a UK survey of 946 people with JHS/EDS-HT, those who got exercise advice from a physiotherapist were more likely to report high weekly exercise (OR 1.75; 95% CI 1.30–2.36). So were those who believed exercise matters for long-term management (OR 2.76; 95% CI 1.38–5.50). Pain, fatigue and fear were common barriers [[Simmonds2019]].

### physio · Orthoses, splints, compression garments, insoles and taping
- Hand: a Swedish RCT in 169 adults with HSD/hEDS and hand pain or paraesthesia found no difference after 12 weeks between progressive wrist-stabilising exercise and wearing a hand orthosis during chosen activities. Outcomes were DASH, grip ability, grip strength, EQ-5D, pain and paraesthesia, and 116 completed [[Susanne2025]].
- Finger orthoses (14 people with JHS, hEDS or classical EDS; repeated measures, not randomised): timed hand-function tests improved with the orthoses for all but one test (p<0.05). The authors call the evidence not conclusive (Early evidence) [[Jensen2021Orth]].
- Compression garments RCT (36 people with hEDS; 12 physiotherapy sessions over 4 weeks, with or without daily garments): dynamic balance improved more with garments (sway velocity effect size 0.93). Pain fell in both groups with no difference between them (Early evidence) [[Benistan2024]].
- Compression garments cohort (67 people with non-vascular EDS, 91% hEDS, 2 years, no control group): VAS for the most painful joint went from 71.5 to 53.5 mm at 6 months and 45.7 mm at 24 months (p<0.0001). Not all secondary outcomes reached significance (Early evidence) [[Benistan2023]].
- Compression garments retrospective cohort (20 adults with hEDS/HSD, mean 4.4 months): 80% reported a response, mostly less pain. Seven of 13 with data used fewer analgesics, 40% reported better quality of life and 15% less fatigue. Responders wore the garments longer (Early evidence) [[Boulu2025]].
- Custom foot orthoses in adults with EDS (36 people, 3 months, no control group): foot pain (p=0.002), foot disability (p<0.001), foot function (p=0.001), fatigue (p<0.007) and mental-health quality of life (p=0.016) improved, but physical quality of life did not [[ReinaBueno2020]].
- Custom orthotics in children with generalised joint hypermobility and leg pain (53 children, 52 completed, no control group): pain VAS fell by a mean 27/100 (95% CI 21–33) at 1 month, and 6-minute walk rose by 27 m. Benefit held at 3 months, with no serious adverse events [[Maarj2023]].
- Off-the-shelf orthotics in 21 children with JHS immediately changed gait. Double-stance time at slow speed fell by 1.61% (95% CI 0.34–2.89). No pain outcome was measured [[McDermott2018]].
- Kinesiology taping RCT (hEDS with shoulder pain): one taping method improved shoulder rotation range immediately and at 48 hours, but neither method changed joint position sense. The tape was donated by a manufacturer (Early evidence) [[Tudini2024]].
- The 2017 pain consensus lists "durable medical equipment such as cushions, compressive garments, and braces" alongside physical therapy and medicines as pain-management strategies [[Chopra2017]].
- For upper cervical instability, the expert consensus says neck braces can help some highly irritable patients maintain alignment. It also says there are "no published guidelines regarding use of neck braces in this population" [[Russek2022UCI]].

### physio · Safety: what consensus statements advise avoiding
- Upper cervical instability consensus (international physiotherapists and a rheumatologist): for highly irritable patients, avoid moderate to large neck movements, isometrics with more than minimal force, cervical axial loading or distraction, and positions that load the neck in quadruped. "If in doubt", consensus is that touching the cervical spine should be avoided. Only clinicians with specific expertise should do cervical manual therapy [[Russek2022UCI]].
- The same consensus advises, for less irritable patients, sport-specific training "with precautions such as avoiding contact sports such as football", or changes such as no heading of the ball [[Russek2022UCI]].
- Sport and injury: in a meta-analysis of 18 studies, generalised joint hypermobility raised the risk of knee injury in contact sports (combined OR 4.69; 95% CI 1.33–16.52) but not ankle injury [[Pacey2010]].
- Athletes with joint hypermobility had higher odds of shoulder injury (OR 3.25; 95% CI 1.64–6.43; 6 studies, 2,335 athletes). Evidence quality was low [[Liaghat2021]].
- Paediatric intensive interdisciplinary pain treatment (40 hours a week for about 4–6 weeks, joint-protective methods, daily medical review): injuries occurred in 17.8% (8/45) of children with generalised hypermobility vs 9.3% of those without. The difference was not significant (p=0.108), and outcomes improved similarly in both groups [[Scheurich2025]].
- Upper cervical and craniocervical care belongs to specialist teams. Screening uses irritability grading and red flags, and patients with red flags are automatically classed as highly irritable [[Russek2022UCI]].

### physio · Vascular EDS: activity limits as published
- vEDS consensus management includes modifying lifestyle "to minimize injury, risk of vessel/organ rupture", an individual emergency plan ("vascular EDS passport"), keeping blood pressure in the normal range and treating hypertension aggressively [[Byers2017]].
- The Orphanet vEDS review states: "Intense physical activity, scuba diving and violent sports are inadvisable" [[Germain2007]].
- One case report describes a man with vEDS who developed intramuscular haematomas during passive hamstring stretching of a spastic limb after a stroke. The authors urge caution and note there are no established vEDS rehabilitation guidelines (case report; Hypothesis-level) [[Izumi2025]].
- Among 50 Norwegian adults with Loeys-Dietz syndrome or vEDS, 66% received disability benefits and the median age at stopping work was 41. Those still working were less fatigued and slept better [[Johansen2022vEDS]].
- No trial has defined a safe exercise dose for vEDS. Advice rests on consensus and expert opinion [[Byers2017,Germain2007,Izumi2025]].

### pain · Pain-management programmes and CBT
- Rahman 2014 (UK, JHS-specific cognitive behavioural pain-management programme: 8 days over 6 weeks, multidisciplinary team; 87 patients, 96% female, mean age 35): at 1 month, self-efficacy, catastrophising, depression, anxiety, frustration, pain impact and average pain intensity all improved (all P<0.001) [[Rahman2014]].
- At 5 months in that programme, outcomes had drifted back towards baseline. All except average pain intensity were still significantly better. This was an open study with no control group [[Rahman2014]].
- The Chew 2025 HOPE pilot RCT tested an online, self-paced pain-management programme (12 modules over 8 weeks; 36 intervention vs 34 usual care). 81–91% rated it feasible. At 3 months, worst pain improved (Cohen's d 0.63) and impact of hypermobility on BIoH improved (d 0.32) (Early evidence) [[Chew2025]].
- The HOPE investigators planned for attrition of up to 28%, the figure reported for RCTs in this population [[Chew2025]].
- Clark 2024 systematic review of psychological interventions: 6 studies, 628 enrolled, but only 343 analysed because of attrition. One was a stand-alone psychological intervention and 5 were within multidisciplinary programmes. The most helpful approaches were delivered alongside physiotherapy in outpatient or community settings [[Clark2024Psych]].
- Online positive psychology (132 people with EDS; 5 weeks): only the group that chose their own exercises had less fatigue and better positive affect and life satisfaction than waitlist at 6 weeks. Pain disability did not change, and 77% were satisfied [[Kalisch2022]].
- Adult multidisciplinary pain rehabilitation in the US (335 patients): people with symptomatic hypermobility attended and graduated at the same rates as others. They were less likely to say their needs were fully met [[Flegge2025]].
- In 75 adults with hEDS, 43% had severe pain and two-thirds had high mobility disability. Diagnostic delay, being in paid work and feelings of helplessness predicted severe pain [[Kalisch2020]].
- The 2017 pain consensus says nearly 90% of people with EDS report chronic pain in one study. Treatment studies "are few and insufficient to guide management", so care targets the cause (for example dislocation or a proprioceptive problem) as well as the pain itself [[Chopra2017]].

### pain · Kinesiophobia (fear of movement)
- In 42 people with JHS/EDS-HT, 93% scored above the Tampa Scale cut-off for fear of movement. Kinesiophobia correlated strongly with overall fatigue severity, not with pain intensity [[Celletti2013]].
- In 168 people with hEDS/HSD (online survey), pain catastrophising, anxiety and fear of falling predicted disability beyond age and pain severity. Pain severity and fear of falling were the strongest predictors [[Chuchin2024]].
- In women with hEDS, kinesiophobia correlated with worse PROMIS-10 physical function (rs −0.75) and mental health (rs −0.56). Half had a fracture as an adult [[Vasireddi2026]].
- In youth with chronic pain, those with hEDS (n=100) had higher fatigue than matched peers (n=100). Fatigue tracked with pain interference, avoidance and pain-related distress [[Sokol2025]].
- Home exercise alone did not change kinesiophobia in a shoulder RCT. The authors suggest a supervised multidisciplinary approach may be needed to change it [[Spanhove2023]].
- Programmes that reduced kinesiophobia include multidisciplinary physical plus cognitive-behavioural rehabilitation [[Bathen2013]], outpatient rehabilitation [[Hakimi2020,Hakimi2023]], online Pilates [[Russek2026]] and neurocognitive exercise [[Celletti2021]].
- Higher fear of movement at baseline was linked to more shoulder pain and lower quality of life after 16 weeks of exercise [[Dolso2023]].

### pain · Fatigue and sleep
- More than three-quarters of 273 people with EDS reported severe fatigue. It was linked to sleep disturbance, concentration problems, social functioning, low self-efficacy about fatigue and pain. The authors proposed these as targets for cognitive behavioural therapy for fatigue [[Voermans2010E]].
- The 2017 fatigue consensus says fatigue care in hEDS should first exclude anaemia, hypothyroidism and infection. It should then address sleep disorders, pain, deconditioning, autonomic dysfunction and nutrition, and usually needs "skilled physical therapy" to prevent deconditioning. No medicine treats fatigue itself [[Hakim2017Fatigue]].
- A meta-analysis of 7 EDS studies found a pooled obstructive sleep apnoea prevalence of 39.4% (95% CI 28.8–51.1). EDS/Marfan patients combined had about six times the odds of OSA (OR 6.28) [[Sedky2019]].
- In 68 adults with hEDS/HSD and OSA matched to 68 controls with OSA, CPAP use was similar (about 75%). Epworth sleepiness fell by 4.46 in controls (p<0.05) but by only 1.56 in hEDS/HSD (not significant). Insomnia was more common in hEDS/HSD (44.7% vs 34.3%) [[Zhang2026OSA]].
- Sleep hygiene sits within occupational therapy guidance for EDS with POTS. It has not been tested on its own in a trial [[Levine2022OT]].

### pain · School, work and participation as therapy
- In the Bendy Study, a physiotherapist and an occupational therapist visited each child's school together (week 4). They discussed mobility, handwriting, PE and attendance with teachers [[Bale2019]].
- People with hEDS/HSD scored lower on the Ghent Participation Scale than healthy controls. This was true both for activities they do themselves and for tasks they delegate [[DeBaets2022]].
- Patients and clinicians ranked occupational therapy support "to maintain independence at work and home" among the self-management priorities, alongside pacing, assertiveness and communication skills [[Bennett2022]].
- A group telemedicine education programme (102 respondents with hEDS/HSD): 93.1% said they understood their condition better and 88.3% said it gave them tools to improve. This is a satisfaction survey, not an effect on symptoms [[Knight2023Tele]].
- A 9-week integrative diet and self-management feasibility study enrolled 13 people, and 8 completed it. Pain VAS did not fall on average [[Guedry2024]].

### pain · Outcome measures used in trials
- The Bristol Impact of Hypermobility (BIoH) questionnaire was developed with people with JHS. It has 55 scored items and correlates with SF-36 physical component score (r=−0.725) [[Palmer2017BIoH]].
- BIoH test-retest reliability: ICC 0.923 (95% CI 0.900–0.940). The smallest detectable change is 42 points, about 19% of the mean baseline score of 223.6 [[Palmer2017SDC]].
- The HOPE trial used the Brief Pain Inventory-Short Form, BIoH, DASS-21 and the Pain Self-Efficacy Questionnaire as secondary outcomes [[Chew2025]].
- Shoulder trials use WOSI (0–2100, lower is better; MID 252) [[Liaghat2022BJSM]]. Other trial tools include the Tampa Scale for Kinesiophobia, DASH and the Patient-Specific Functional Scale [[Spanhove2023]].
- The Spider questionnaire measures 8 multisystem symptom domains in HSD/hEDS. It was validated in adults (11,151 participants across stages; domain correlations r=0.63–0.80) [[Ewer2024]].

### Exam hooks
- Exercise-based physiotherapy is the cornerstone of hEDS/HSD care, but controlled-trial evidence of superiority over comparators is weak [[Palmer2021]].
- No specific medical or genetic therapy exists for any EDS type, and management is a care team plus integrated rehabilitation [[Malfait2020]].
- High-load shoulder strengthening beat low-load at 16 weeks (WOSI −174.5) but not at 1 year [[Liaghat2022BJSM,Liaghat2024BJSM]].
- Exercise-induced pain stayed below 0.5/10 during high-load shoulder training in HSD [[Liaghat2025]].
- Children's knee pain fell by 14.5 mm on a VAS after 8 weeks of exercise, whether exercising into hyperextension or only to neutral [[Pacey2013]].
- A multidisciplinary programme added nothing over standard advice at 12 months in 119 hypermobile children [[Bale2019]].
- In one cohort, 93% of people with JHS/EDS-HT had high kinesiophobia scores [[Celletti2013]].
- More than three-quarters of people with EDS have severe fatigue [[Voermans2010E]].
- Inspiratory muscle training for 6 weeks raised 6-minute walk distance by 60 m in women with hEDS [[Reychler2019]].
- The UK JHS cognitive behavioural pain-management programme ran 8 days over 6 weeks, and most gains remained at 5 months (except pain intensity) [[Rahman2014]].
- BIoH smallest detectable change = 42 points [[Palmer2017SDC]].
- Hypermobile athletes have 4.69 times the odds of knee injury in contact sports [[Pacey2010]].
- vEDS: intense physical activity, scuba diving and violent sports are inadvisable [[Germain2007]]. Lifestyle is modified to minimise injury and rupture risk [[Byers2017]].
- Upper cervical instability: "if in doubt", avoid touching the cervical spine [[Russek2022UCI]].
- Compression garments plus physiotherapy improved dynamic balance more than physiotherapy alone, but pain fell equally in both groups [[Benistan2024]].
- Wrist-stabilising exercise and a hand orthosis gave similar results at 12 weeks in 169 adults [[Susanne2025]].
- OSA prevalence in EDS is about 39% [[Sedky2019]].

### In plain words
- Exercise with a physiotherapist who knows hypermobility is the main treatment. It helps many people, but the studies are small [[Palmer2021,Engelbert2017]].
- Start gently and build up step by step over weeks. In one trial, people spent 3 weeks learning the moves before using heavier weights [[Liaghat2022BJSM]].
- Feeling a little sore after exercise is common and usually passes. In one study, heavier training caused only a small rise in pain during exercise [[Liaghat2022BJSM,Liaghat2025]].
- Many people with hypermobility fear that moving will hurt them. Good programmes help people move with more confidence [[Celletti2013,Hakimi2023]].
- Tiredness is very common. Sleep problems, pain and low fitness all add to it, and doctors should check for other causes [[Voermans2010E,Hakim2017Fatigue]].
- Pain programmes that mix exercise with learning new ways to think about and handle pain can help. Benefits may fade, so keep practising [[Rahman2014,Clark2024Psych]].
- Braces, compression clothing and shoe inserts help some people. The proof is still early [[Benistan2023,Boulu2025,Maarj2023]].
- For children, simple advice and encouragement to stay active may work as well as complex programmes [[Bale2019]].
- Talking with the school about PE, handwriting and moving around can help a child take part [[Bale2019]].
- If you have vascular EDS, experts advise against very intense exercise, violent sports and scuba diving. Carry your emergency plan [[Germain2007,Byers2017]].
- Contact sports raise the chance of knee injury for people with very bendy joints [[Pacey2010]].
- The Beighton score is a teaching tool. Only a clinician can diagnose hEDS or HSD, and exercise plans should be made with one [[Castori2017,Malfait2017]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Load: low-load, mid-range exercise has been standard advice. A Danish RCT found high-load, full-range shoulder training better at 16 weeks and well tolerated, but the edge was below the MID and gone at 1 year [[Liaghat2022BJSM,Liaghat2024BJSM]]. A knee RCT is under way [[Liaghat2024Protocol]]. Status: unresolved.
- Range of motion: exercising into the hypermobile range vs only to neutral gave the same pain benefit in children, with mixed parent-rated trade-offs [[Pacey2013]]. Status: one small trial.
- Simple vs complex care in children: a structured multidisciplinary package was no better than standard advice at 12 months [[Bale2019]], while non-randomised multidisciplinary programmes report large gains [[VanMeulenbroek2020]]. Status: needs RCTs with no-treatment or sham arms [[Peterson2018]]. A Cochrane review has been registered [[Quinlan2026]].
- Durability of pain-programme gains: improvements after a CBT-based programme regressed by 5 months [[Rahman2014]]. Online programmes look feasible but effects are small to moderate [[Chew2025]]. Status: no long-term controlled trial.
- Compression garments: improved balance in one RCT but not pain beyond physiotherapy [[Benistan2024]], while uncontrolled cohorts report pain relief [[Benistan2023,Boulu2025]]. Status: early evidence, at risk of placebo and selection bias.
- Neck braces for upper cervical instability: experts describe benefit for some, but no guidelines or trials exist [[Russek2022UCI]].
- Hydrotherapy, ring splints, Pilates in adults and pacing as stand-alone treatments: no RCT evidence found. The online Pilates trial was a pragmatic waitlist-controlled trial (the abstract does not describe randomisation) with high dropout [[Russek2026]]. Status: unknown.
- Generalising to other EDS types: almost all trials enrolled hEDS/HSD. vEDS advice is consensus-based, and one case report urges caution with passive stretching [[Byers2017,Germain2007,Izumi2025]]. Status: no vEDS exercise trials.
- Sleep: CPAP did not reduce daytime sleepiness in hEDS/HSD despite treating the apnoea. Other drivers (pain, autonomic, insomnia) are proposed but untested [[Zhang2026OSA]].
- Outcome measures: no agreed core set for children [[Maarj2021]]. BIoH has good reliability but a smallest detectable change of 42 points [[Palmer2017SDC]].
