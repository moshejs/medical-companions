# H · Living, family, nursing & research toward better care

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: living, pregnancy, nursing, frontier.

This pass covers daily life with the Ehlers-Danlos syndromes (EDS): school, work, sport, mental health, the diagnostic odyssey and peer support. It also covers family matters (genetic counselling, cascade testing, reproductive options), pregnancy planning by type, practical nursing care, and the research frontier (hEDS genetics, biomarkers, vascular EDS trials and models, registries). Every source was checked against PubMed E-utilities (esummary and abstract) or the ClinicalTrials.gov v2 API on 2026-10-25/26.

Access notes:
- The NCBI Bookshelf pages for GeneReviews returned a CAPTCHA page, which was not bypassed. The GeneReviews statements below come from the structured summaries that PubMed indexes for each chapter (PMIDs 20301667, 20301422, 20301456). Those summaries contain the management, "agents/circumstances to avoid" and genetic counselling text.
- Full text was read on PubMed Central for Bowen 2023 (PMC10326011), Pezaro 2024 (PMC11095771), Vernino 2021 (PMC8455420) and Bowen 2020 JCI (PMC6994142). Numbers quoted from these come from the full text. Everything else comes from the abstracts.
- Byers 2017 (vEDS consensus) and Anderson 2015 (AJN nursing CE) are paywalled. Only their abstracts were read.
- I found no peer-reviewed source giving specific rules for blood-pressure cuffs, tourniquets, intramuscular injections, tape types or dislocation first aid in EDS. These are left out of the findings rather than written from general knowledge. See Open questions.
- The 2017 classification has 13 types [[Malfait2017]]. The 2020 Nat Rev Dis Primers review counts 14 types, with the molecular cause known for 13 [[Malfait2020]]. Builders should use "13 types (2017 classification)" as the headline.

## Findings

### living · The diagnostic odyssey and being believed
- In an Ehlers-Danlos Society Global Registry survey of 505 people with clinically confirmed hEDS, the mean time to diagnosis was 10.39 years. People had received an average of 10.45 alternative diagnoses [[Halverson2023]].
- In the same survey, the commonest earlier diagnoses were anxiety, depression and migraine. The diagnoses respondents most often endorsed were POTS, cervical instability and mast cell activation syndrome. The ones they most often rejected were functional neurological disorder, multiple sclerosis and fibromyalgia [[Halverson2023]].
- In a University of Miami hEDS registry survey, the median age at first symptoms was 10 years. There was a median gap of 4 years before the first medical evaluation and, on average, 10 years to an hEDS diagnosis [[Wang2024]].
- In that survey, 95.2% rated receiving a diagnosis as "important" or "highly important", and 81.9% said it helped them cope better. 76.8% said they could manage symptoms better and felt more in control of their long-term care [[Wang2024]].
- A UK national survey (n = 2002; 90.9% female; September 2023 to January 2024) reported a mean diagnostic delay of 19.0–21.7 years for hEDS/HSD [[Berg2026]].
- Among 152 women with EDS in Australia, more than half first noticed symptoms more than 15 years before diagnosis, and more than three quarters received other diagnoses first. Many felt they were "not listened to" and had to educate their clinicians [[Trudgian2024]].
- In a qualitative study across Europe and North America (43 surveyed, 39 interviewed), 73% of survey respondents questioned the extent or even the reality of their own pain. They linked this to repeated dismissal of symptoms in healthcare, especially in childhood [[Halverson2025]].
- That study concluded that clinicians may reinforce self-doubt without meaning to when they discuss diagnostic uncertainty. It recommends "epistemic humility" [[Halverson2025]].
- Twenty-two in-depth interviews framed the diagnostic odyssey as a "hero's journey" marked by uncertainty, multiplying symptoms and negative psychosocial consequences. The authors note that clinicians may dismiss subjective chronic pain [[Halverson2021]].
- In a US online survey of 2125 adults with hEDS/HSD, satisfaction with healthcare and health-related quality of life were both lower than norm groups. Lower satisfaction was associated with lower quality of life and lower symptom self-efficacy (p < .001) [[Estrella2024]].
- In that survey, the most common change participants wanted was more hEDS/HSD knowledge among healthcare professionals [[Estrella2024]].
- A review of illness uncertainty in hEDS identified diagnostic ambiguity, misdiagnosis, perceived dismissal and attribution of symptoms to mental health as likely drivers of distress. It calls a validated illness-uncertainty measure for hEDS a research priority [[Feldman2023]].
- A qualitative study of 17 adults with JHS/hEDS-HT identified five themes: healthcare limitations and low professional awareness, a restricted life, social stigma, fear of the unknown, and ways of coping. The coping approaches included acceptance, social networks, learning about the condition and adapting activities [[Bennett2021]].
- A scoping review found no study aimed at ageing in hEDS/HSD. It notes that older adults with multimorbidity may have undiagnosed hEDS/HSD [[Anderson2023]].

### living · Symptom burden, school and children
- In an online survey of 466 adults with clinic-diagnosed EDS hypermobility type (90% female), the most frequent symptoms were joint pain (99%), hypermobility (99%) and limb pain (91%). Respondents also reported chronic fatigue (82%), anxiety (73%) and depression (69%) [[Murray2013]].
- In the same survey, 46% reported constant pain and 53% said the diagnosis negatively affected their ability to work or attend school [[Murray2013]].
- In the UK survey of 2002 people with hEDS/HSD, 55.9% reported disrupted education [[Berg2026]].
- Among 100 parents of children aged 8–18 attending a connective-tissue clinic (48% EDS-hypermobile type/JHS), pain (73%) and fatigue (68%) were the symptoms most often said to affect daily activities [[Wesley2021]].
- Parents in that study were less often satisfied with school participation (33%) than with self-care (61%). The school difficulties reported most often were keeping up with handwriting (71%) and gross motor activities (70%) [[Wesley2021]].
- In 89 children with joint hypermobility syndrome, pain, fatigue and stress-incontinence symptoms together explained 75% of the variance in child-reported quality of life. Parents rated their child's quality of life lower than the children did (mean difference 4.44 points) [[Pacey2015]].
- The International Consortium's paediatric framework uses a Beighton score of at least 6/9 to define generalized joint hypermobility in children. hEDS is reserved for biologically mature adolescents who meet the 2017 criteria [[Tofts2023]].
- An Australian general-practice review states that diagnosis, ideally by adolescence, gives children and parents validation. It also directs management toward the educational and socioeconomic impact [[Nicholson2022]].
- GeneReviews lists occupational therapy for assistive devices, such as wide-grip writing utensils and home and work ergonomics, in hEDS [[Hakim2024GeneReviews]].
- For cEDS, GeneReviews advises that young children with skin fragility can wear pads or bandages over the forehead, knees and shins. It suggests older children can wear soccer pads or ski stockings with shin padding during activities [[Malfait2024GeneReviews]].
- Physical therapy guidance for JHS/hEDS uses the WHO ICF framework (function, activity, participation, environment). It states that the evidence for interventions is "limited in size and quality" [[Engelbert2017]].

### living · Work, benefits and disability
- In the UK national survey, 45.6% reported unemployment and 47.9% reported dependence on benefits [[Berg2026]].
- Interviews with nine people with hEDS found that work pressure, tasks and transport to work shape participation. Work also had positive effects on well-being when job demands matched ability [[DeBaets2021]].
- A meta-analysis of hypermobility-related disorders (21 studies) found that disability correlated with pain (r = .64), fatigue (r = .91) and psychological distress (r = .86). It found no established treatment effect on disability [[Scheper2016]].
- In 72 women with EDS-HT, functional impairment measured with the Sickness Impact Profile was similar to fibromyalgia and worse than rheumatoid arthritis [[Rombaut2011]].
- In a Norwegian survey of 50 adults with Loeys-Dietz syndrome or vEDS, 66% received disability benefits and 42% had a full-time disability pension. The median age at stopping work was 41 years [[Johansen2022]].

### living · Sport, activity and travel by type
- In a meta-analysis of 18 studies, people with generalized joint hypermobility had a higher risk of knee injury during contact sport (combined OR 4.69, 95% CI 1.33–16.52). There was no increased risk of ankle injury [[Pacey2010]].
- hEDS: GeneReviews says high-impact activity may increase the risk of acute subluxation or dislocation and of pain. It says chiropractic adjustment and yoga are "not contraindicated" if done in ways that avoid causing subluxations [[Hakim2024GeneReviews]].
- cEDS: GeneReviews lists "sports with heavy joint strain" to avoid, and says non-weight-bearing exercise promotes muscle strength and coordination [[Malfait2024GeneReviews]].
- vEDS: GeneReviews lists trauma to avoid, including collision sports, heavy lifting and weight training with extreme lifting [[Byers2025GeneReviews]].
- vEDS: the UK national service advises that "regular, low-intensity exercise should be maintained for health and psychological wellbeing" [[Bowen2023]].
- vEDS: the international consensus recommends lifestyle changes to reduce the risk of injury and rupture. It also recommends an individual emergency plan (a "vascular EDS passport") with the diagnosis and management plan for use when travelling [[Byers2017]].
- vEDS: a co-designed 12-week remote, exercise-based lifestyle programme (n = 40 planned; Sheffield Hallam University) is registered as not yet recruiting, with a start date of 2027-03-01 (Early evidence) [[NCT07697573]].

### living · Mental health and validation
- In a Swedish nationwide cohort (1771 people with EDS vs 17 710 matched comparators), EDS was associated with autism spectrum disorder (RR 7.4, 95% CI 5.2–10.7) and ADHD (RR 5.6, 4.2–7.4). It was also associated with depression (RR 3.4, 2.9–4.1), bipolar disorder (RR 2.7, 1.5–4.7) and attempted suicide (RR 2.1, 1.7–2.7), but not with suicide or schizophrenia (Established association; cause unknown) [[Cederlof2016]].
- Siblings without EDS also had higher risks of ADHD (RR 2.1), depression (RR 1.5) and suicide attempt (RR 1.8). The authors suggest a genetic and/or early environmental component (Hypothesis) [[Cederlof2016]].
- A meta-analysis of 14 studies (1006 with benign joint hypermobility syndrome, 2951 controls) found higher odds of anxiety (OR 4.39, 95% CI 1.92–10.40), depression (OR 4.10, 1.79–9.41) and panic disorder (OR 6.72, 2.22–20.35) [[Smith2014]].
- In a chart review of 106 people with classic or hypermobility-type EDS, 42.5% had a psychiatric disorder. Having any pain symptom was strongly associated with having one (OR 9.68), while joint hypermobility itself was not [[Hershenfeld2016]].
- A 2017 Consortium review describes a strong association between anxiety disorders and JHS/hEDS. It recommends integrated psychiatric and psychological care alongside physiotherapy [[Bulbena2017]].
- People with vEDS or Loeys-Dietz syndrome (n = 16) scored higher than healthy controls on anxiety, depression and fatigue. Yet 93.8% showed optimal psychosocial adjustment, and they scored better than matched hEDS patients on quality of life, pain and fatigue [[BaezaVelasco2023]].
- Pezaro 2024 perinatal guidance calls for "compassionate communication validating hEDS/HSD experiences" and a full mental health risk assessment [[Pezaro2024]].
- An online two-week mindfulness pilot (76 completers) improved the SF-36 mental component score by a clinically important margin. There was no control group (Early evidence) [[Lattimore2023]].
- The HOPE online pain management programme for hEDS/HSD was judged feasible, acceptable and appropriate in a pilot. In interviews, benefits seemed stronger for people newer to their diagnosis (Early evidence) [[Chew2025]].

### living · Peer support and the zebra community
- A survey of members of EDS support groups recruited through Facebook (102 respondents) mapped 448 social connections. In-person contact was the main mode, and discussions about EDS happened daily [[Callen2025]].
- Participants in a qualitative study named building social networks and learning about the condition as effective coping strategies [[Bennett2021]].

### living · Genetic counselling and family testing
- vEDS, cEDS and hEDS are autosomal dominant. Each child of an affected parent has a 50% chance of inheriting the condition [[Byers2025GeneReviews,Malfait2024GeneReviews,Hakim2024GeneReviews]].
- About 50% of people diagnosed with vEDS have an affected parent. In about 50% the COL3A1 variant arose de novo, in the person or in an apparently unaffected mosaic parent [[Byers2025GeneReviews]].
- Approximately 50% of people with cEDS have an affected parent and about 50% have a de novo variant [[Malfait2024GeneReviews]].
- Most people with hEDS have an affected parent. Recognising this often needs a detailed history and examination of the parents, because the parent's signs may be subtle [[Hakim2024GeneReviews]].
- For vEDS, evaluating first-degree relatives is appropriate. Evaluation starts clinically and, even without clinical signs, proceeds to testing for the known familial COL3A1 variant [[Byers2025GeneReviews]].
- In the UK national vEDS service, 54% of probands had inherited the condition from a parent. 78 of 180 patients had a relative already diagnosed [[Bowen2023]].
- In a London cohort of 63 people diagnosed with vEDS before age 18, 55% were diagnosed through family genetic testing. The authors recommend offering childhood testing when there is a family history [[Wilkinson2025]].
- In that cohort, 13 children had 18 events, with first events at a median age of 11 years. In 11 of 13, genetic testing was prompted by that first event [[Wilkinson2025]].
- About 60% of people diagnosed with vEDS before age 18 are identified through a positive family history. About half of children tested without a family history present with a major complication at an average age of 11 years [[Byers2025GeneReviews]].
- In vEDS the variant type matters for counselling. Glycine substitutions and splice-site or in-frame variants were more severe than haploinsufficient or terminal variants, with median ages at first major complication ranging from 23 to 47 years across groups [[Frank2015]].
- Median survival in vEDS was 51 years (n = 1231) and was lower in men and varied by variant type. The authors say confirming the variant and its nature helps families estimate risk. These are population figures, not individual predictions [[Pepin2014]].
- Once the familial variant is known, prenatal and preimplantation genetic testing are possible for vEDS and cEDS [[Byers2025GeneReviews,Malfait2024GeneReviews]].
- For hEDS, prenatal and preimplantation genetic testing are not possible because no causal gene has been identified [[Hakim2024GeneReviews]].
- The UK Human Fertilisation and Embryology Authority has approved vEDS for pre-implantation genetic diagnosis (PGD). A published case describes PGD with surrogacy in a woman with vEDS [[Angwin2023]].
- A vEDS review lists cascade testing as one part of lifelong management, with pregnancy and psychosocial care [[Corrao2026]].

### pregnancy · Vascular EDS
- Of 81 women with vEDS who became pregnant, 12 died of complications of pregnancy [[Pepin2000]].
- From pedigree review, pregnancy-related deaths occurred in 30 of 565 deliveries (5.3%) [[Murray2014]].
- In interviews covering 76 deliveries, 46% were uncomplicated. Life-threatening complications occurred in 14.5%: arterial dissection or rupture 9.2%, uterine rupture 2.6% and surgical complications 2.6% [[Murray2014]].
- The commonest complications in that study were third- or fourth-degree lacerations (20%) and preterm delivery (19%). Kaplan–Meier survival did not differ between parous and nulliparous women [[Murray2014]].
- A systematic review (6 studies, 412 pregnancies) reported 30% infertility, 13.8% miscarriage, 8.8% preterm live births, 11.3% obstetric anal sphincter injury and 5.7% maternal mortality per pregnancy [[Haem2024]].
- That review advised scheduling caesarean birth at 37 weeks to avoid expulsive efforts [[Haem2024]].
- A Dutch multicentre cohort (121 pregnancies in 43 women with a P/LP COL3A1 variant) found no pregnancy-related deaths, arterial dissections or uterine ruptures in the perinatal period [[vandenBersselaar2026]].
- In that cohort, miscarriage was 19% (23/121) and 19.1% of live births were preterm (18/94). Miscarriage clustered in women with glycine substitutions (19/23). The authors propose shared decision-making rather than discouraging all pregnancies [[vandenBersselaar2026]].
- GeneReviews states that affected women have "a 5% mortality risk with each pregnancy". It advises follow-up in a high-risk obstetric programme, with delivery decisions stratified by the genetic variant [[Byers2025GeneReviews]].
- In the UK national service, 5 of 104 pregnancies had life-threatening or fatal events, all before diagnosis. Women diagnosed before delivery all had early planned caesarean birth under general anaesthesia in a tertiary hospital [[Bowen2023]].
- In 41 children with vEDS, preterm birth (<37 weeks) was more common than in the US population (48.8% vs 12.2%), and this was unrelated to whether the mother was affected. The authors suggest the fetal diagnosis itself confers the risk (Early evidence) [[Stephens2022]].
- In mice, blocking oxytocin signalling after birth improved postpartum survival to about 90% in a vEDS model. This is a mouse finding only (Early evidence) [[Bowen2020JCI]].

### pregnancy · Hypermobile EDS, HSD and EDS overall
- GeneReviews advises that hEDS pregnancy planning should address preconception (musculoskeletal health, medicines), antenatal (joint instability, pelvic strength, hernias, pain), intrapartum (birth choices, mobility, anaesthesia) and postpartum issues (wound healing, pelvic health, newborn care) [[Hakim2024GeneReviews]].
- A Consortium-facilitated scoping review and expert co-creation (14 primary studies, 21 case studies) produced guidance across four phases: preconception, antenatal, intrapartum and postpartum. It states there is "limited high quality evidence" [[Pezaro2024]].
- That guidance prefers physiological vaginal birth where there are no obstetric contraindications, and reserves caesarean for obstetric indications [[Pezaro2024]].
- It reports rapid or precipitous birth (≤3 hours) at a frequency of 28–36% [[Pezaro2024]].
- It recommends at least 2.5 L of fluid daily antenatally for people with POTS, and action plans with premedication or prehydration for mast cell disease [[Pezaro2024]].
- In an international survey of 947 women (1338 pregnancies) with hEDS/HSD, reported incidences of pre-eclampsia, preterm rupture of membranes, preterm birth, haemorrhage, hyperemesis, precipitate labour and postpartum PTSD were higher than typical general-population figures. This is a self-report survey (Early evidence) [[Pearce2023]].
- In a Swedish nationwide register study, 314 births to women with JHS/EDS showed no increase in preterm birth (aOR 0.6, 95% CI 0.3–1.2), caesarean section (aOR 0.9, 0.7–1.2) or stillbirth (aOR 1.1, 0.2–7.9) [[Sundelin2017]].
- In the 62 births to women with EDS in that study, induction of labour (aOR 2.6) and amniotomy (aOR 3.8) were more common [[Sundelin2017]].
- In the US Nationwide Inpatient Sample (1999–2014), EDS was recorded in 7 per 100 000 births. It was associated with preterm birth (OR 1.47), cervical incompetence (OR 3.11) and caesarean delivery (OR 1.55). It was also associated with maternal death (OR 9.04, 95% CI 1.27–64.27), a wide interval reflecting very few events [[Spiegel2022]].
- A case series of eight women with hEDS reports that most pregnancies are uncomplicated, with good outcomes under multidisciplinary care [[Karthikeyan2018]].
- Note the conflict: large Swedish register data show no excess adverse outcomes for JHS/EDS [[Sundelin2017]], while US inpatient data and a self-report survey show raised risks [[Spiegel2022,Pearce2023]]. None separate EDS types well, except the vEDS-specific studies.

### nursing · vEDS emergency recognition and cautions (as published)
- vEDS is characterised by arterial, intestinal and/or uterine fragility, thin translucent skin, easy bruising and a characteristic face. Arterial dissection or rupture, bowel perforation or organ rupture are the presenting signs in most adults [[Byers2025GeneReviews]].
- GeneReviews says affected people "are instructed to seek immediate medical attention for sudden, unexplained pain". It also says they should carry documentation of their diagnosis, such as a MedicAlert® bracelet or necklace, an emergency contact letter, or a vEDS "passport" [[Byers2025GeneReviews]].
- GeneReviews advises that "arteriography should be discouraged and used only to identify life-threatening sources of bleeding prior to surgical intervention because of the risk of vascular injury" [[Byers2025GeneReviews]].
- GeneReviews also advises avoiding routine colonoscopy unless there are concerning symptoms or a strong family history of colon cancer, and avoiding elective surgery unless the benefit is expected to be substantial [[Byers2025GeneReviews]].
- A 2026 review describes vEDS emergency care as "harm-avoidant". It advises avoiding catheter angiography, elective endoscopy and unnecessary surgery, and preferring endovascular and tissue-sparing techniques [[Corrao2026]].
- The international consensus recommends a care team and centralising care at experienced centres where feasible. It recommends keeping blood pressure in the normal range, treating hypertension aggressively, and annual vascular surveillance with Doppler ultrasound, CTA (low-radiation) or MRA [[Byers2017]].
- In the UK national service, all patients receive emergency information cards to show any clinician. The service notes that "fragile connective tissues make invasive procedures potentially dangerous, particularly in an emergency setting" [[Bowen2023]].
- In that service, 74 of 180 patients were diagnosed only after a vascular event, an organ rupture, or both. The authors state that spontaneous bowel perforation should prompt genetic testing for vEDS in all cases [[Bowen2023]].
- Pepin 2000 advises considering the diagnosis in young people presenting with uterine rupture in pregnancy, or with arterial or visceral rupture [[Pepin2000]].
- In the 2000 cohort, 25% of index patients had a first complication by age 20 and more than 80% by age 40. Bowel rupture, often of the sigmoid colon, made up about a quarter of complications but rarely caused death [[Pepin2000]].
- Across 1780 people with heritable thoracic aortic disease, arterial events beyond the aorta occurred in 20.8% of those with COL3A1 variants, against 1.5% with FBN1 (Marfan). COL3A1 events were the most frequent and earliest [[CalderonMartinez2025]].

### nursing · Skin, wounds, bruising and documentation
- cEDS wound care in GeneReviews: dermal wounds are closed without tension, preferably in two layers. Deep stitches are applied generously, cutaneous stitches are left in place twice as long as usual, and adjacent skin is carefully taped to prevent scar stretching [[Malfait2024GeneReviews]].
- GeneReviews notes that in cEDS the skin splits after relatively minor trauma, especially over the knees, elbows, shins, forehead and chin. Easy bruising is a hallmark, and vitamin C "may reduce bruising" [[Malfait2024GeneReviews]].
- Perinatal hEDS/HSD guidance recommends gentle handling of fragile tissues. It also recommends longer-lasting absorbable sutures, tension-free interrupted closure, and additional adhesives where the skin is particularly fragile [[Pezaro2024]].
- The same guidance recommends documenting early bruising in infants, and its causes, "to avoid false accusations of maltreatment" [[Pezaro2024]].
- EDS is on the differential for bruising in suspected child abuse, especially in types with marked skin and capillary involvement. Careful examination of the child and extended family is routine. EDS as an explanation for infant fractures remains speculative (Hypothesis) [[Castori2015]].
- An AJN continuing-education article notes that EDS has historically been misunderstood and underdiagnosed, and that nurses are increasingly likely to care for people with EDS. It covers symptom management and recognising complications [[Anderson2015]].

### nursing · Handling, positioning and joints
- In hEDS, subluxations and dislocations are common and may occur spontaneously or with minimal trauma. Repeated injury can lead to degenerative joint and chronic soft-tissue disorders [[Hakim2024GeneReviews]].
- GeneReviews warns that physical treatments must avoid causing subluxations or dislocations [[Hakim2024GeneReviews]].
- Perinatal guidance advises careful positioning of the head and neck, shoulders, back, hips, knees and ankles, using wedges, balls, pillows and bed adaptations. It advises anticipating joint pain or dysfunction [[Pezaro2024]].
- For infant feeding, the same guidance advises checking the latch with a mirror rather than straining the head and neck, and offering positional support to parent and infant [[Pezaro2024]].

### nursing · Local anaesthetic, venepuncture and procedures
- In a survey of 980 people with EDS who had dental local anaesthetic, 88% recalled inadequate pain control, against 33% of 249 people without EDS (p < 0.001) [[Schubart2019]].
- In a randomised crossover trial of subcutaneous lidocaine in the forearm (NCT05603741; 91 EDS, 44 healthy), fewer people with EDS were anaesthetised at 15 minutes (60% vs 84%, p = 0.006) and at 30 minutes (53% vs 80%, p = 0.003). There was no difference at 5 minutes, which suggests a shorter duration of effect [[Bourne2026,NCT05603741]].
- Perinatal guidance states that "higher doses of local anesthesia may be needed" and that epidural is preferred over spinal anaesthesia in hEDS/HSD [[Pezaro2024]].

### nursing · Pain assessment
- Chronic pain is common in EDS. One study cited in the 2017 pain review found nearly 90% report some form of it [[Chopra2017]].
- The 2017 pain review lists durable equipment (cushions, compression garments, braces) alongside physical therapy and medicines [[Chopra2017]].
- Validated tools used in EDS research include the Sickness Impact Profile and Multidimensional Pain Inventory in adults [[Rombaut2011]]. In children they include the PedsQL 4.0, the Multidimensional Fatigue Scale and the Pediatric Pain Questionnaire [[Pacey2015]].
- Parent-reported and child-reported quality of life correlated strongly (r = 0.6–0.84), so parent report can serve as a reasonable proxy [[Pacey2015]].

### nursing · Orthostatic assessment (active stand test)
- POTS criteria (2019 NIH consensus): a sustained heart-rate rise of at least 30 beats/min within 10 minutes of standing or head-up tilt, or at least 40 beats/min at ages 12–19. There must be no sustained systolic drop of 20 mmHg or more, with orthostatic symptoms for at least 3 months [[Vernino2021]].
- POTS can usually be identified by recording heart rate and blood pressure while supine and during a 10-minute stand [[Vernino2021]].
- Evaluation of cardiovascular symptoms in hEDS ranges from clinic-room observation to tilt-table testing. Non-drug measures include education, avoiding triggers, fitness and hydration [[Hakim2017]].
- In 45 young women, POTS on the active stand test was found in 47% with generalized HSD, 27% with hEDS and 13% of controls. Head-up tilt gave different figures (43%, 7% and 7%), so the test method matters [[Peebles2022]].
- In 39 women with EDS-HT, orthostatic intolerance was more common than in controls (74% vs 34%), most often as postural tachycardia [[DeWandele2014]].

### frontier · hEDS genetics and the HEDGE study
- No genetic, epigenetic or metabolomic cause for hEDS has been identified, and diagnosis rests on the 2017 clinical criteria [[Hakim2024GeneReviews,Malfait2017]].
- A critical appraisal found only 11 of 304 publications met its quality criteria, and the hEDS genetic literature was "scant, dispersed and conflicting". It names the upcoming Hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos Genetic Evaluation (HEDGE) study (Established gap) [[Scicluna2022]].
- Whole-exome sequencing of 200 people with hEDS found rare variants in 14 of 15 kallikrein (KLK) genes, including a recurrent KLK15 missense variant (p.Gly226Asp) segregating in several families (Early evidence) [[Gensemer2025]].
- A Klk15 knock-in mouse recapitulated hEDS-like features in tendons and heart valves. The variant altered how KLK15 and lysyl oxidase are distributed in the extracellular matrix, consistent with a dominant-negative effect. Replication in independent cohorts is pending (Early evidence) [[Gensemer2025]].
- Whole-exome sequencing of 174 unresolved EDS patients found pathogenic variants in known EDS and Loeys-Dietz genes plus many variants of uncertain significance. Burden analysis found candidate loci, but none reached genome-wide significance (Early evidence) [[Vandersteen2024]].
- Reviews describe hEDS as likely genetically heterogeneous, with severity shaped by age, sex and lifestyle (Hypothesis) [[Gensemer2021]].
- A Boston University study genotyped 334 people with EDS by next-generation sequencing. It completed on 2025-04-03 with results not yet posted on the registry [[NCT03093493]].

### frontier · The 2017 criteria and their revision
- The 2017 classification recognises 13 subtypes. Every subtype except hEDS needs molecular confirmation [[Malfait2017]].
- People with symptomatic joint hypermobility who do not meet a syndrome's criteria are classed as hypermobility spectrum disorders (HSD) [[Castori2017]].
- The Ehlers-Danlos Society states that revised diagnostic criteria for all EDS types and HSD, developed by systematic review, Delphi consensus and peer review, will be published on 1 December 2026 in the American Journal of Medical Genetics. It says a care-pathways publication will follow in March 2027. This is an organisational announcement, not yet a peer-reviewed record (unverified) [[EDSociety2026]].
- Fibroblast RNA-seq found the same extracellular-matrix disarray and myofibroblast pattern in hEDS and HSD. The authors argue the two are a single entity (Early evidence) [[Ritelli2022]].
- Serum proteomics in 88 hEDS, 88 HSD and 176 controls found 54 and 49 differentially expressed proteins respectively. There were no significant differences between hEDS and HSD (Early evidence) [[Cinquina2026]].

### frontier · Biomarkers and cell models
- Plasma fibronectin and type I collagen fragment patterns were shared by hEDS and HSD and differed from cEDS, vEDS, RA, PsA and OA. The authors call this a potential first blood test, but it is not validated (Early evidence) [[Ritelli2025]].
- Mass-spectrometry serum proteomics in 29 women with hEDS vs 29 controls found 35 differentially expressed proteins, 43% of them in the complement cascade. ELISA confirmed lower C1QA, C3, C8A, C8B and C9 (Early evidence) [[Griggs2025]].
- In hEDS dermal fibroblasts, inhibiting matrix metalloproteinases with doxycycline restored a control-like matrix and partly reversed myofibroblast features. This is in vitro only, not a treatment recommendation (Early evidence) [[Chiarelli2021]].
- Induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) lines have been generated from people with vEDS, for example from a COL3A1 p.Q105* nonsense variant [[Hopperger2024]]. They have also been generated from musculocontractural EDS (CHST14), where patient iPSCs showed impaired osteogenesis (Early evidence) [[Yue2023]].
- An AI facial-analysis model (PhenoScore), trained on images of 30 people with vEDS, separated vEDS from controls and from hEDS with an AUC of at least 0.97 (Early evidence) [[Murdock2025]].
- In 65 people with vEDS, a height-adjusted vertebral tortuosity index of at least 15.5 discriminated cardiovascular events (sensitivity 70%, specificity 76%). It was associated with events among under-40s with high-risk variants (IRR 4.14) (Early evidence) [[Stephens2023]].
- A French plasma biomarker study in vEDS (MEDIC, n = 211) completed in 2015, and no results are posted on the registry [[NCT02165085]].
- A Cambridge study of metabolic phenotyping in vEDS (n = 15 planned) started recruiting on 2026-03-05 [[NCT07516496]].

### frontier · Vascular EDS trials
- BBEST/celiprolol (NCT00190411): 53 people with clinical vEDS were randomised to celiprolol or no treatment (open, blinded endpoints). Arterial events occurred in 20% vs 50% (HR 0.36, 95% CI 0.15–0.88), and the trial was stopped early for benefit (Established, single trial) [[Ong2010,NCT00190411]].
- Only 33 of those 53 participants had a confirmed COL3A1 variant [[Ong2010]].
- In a French referral cohort of 144 people with molecularly confirmed vEDS, those treated with celiprolol had better survival (p = 0.0004), with the best protection at 400 mg/day. This is observational [[Frank2019]].
- ARCADE (NCT02597361): 57 adults with vEDS already on celiprolol were randomised to irbesartan or placebo for 2 years. The composite of arterial events or new or worsening lesions occurred in 27.6% vs 53.6% (HR 0.42, 95% CI 0.17–0.99) [[Jeunemaitre2025,NCT02597361]].
- In ARCADE, 11 episodes of irbesartan-related hypotension led to down-titration in 4 patients [[Jeunemaitre2025]].
- DiSCOVER (NCT05432466) is a phase 3, double-blind, decentralised trial of celiprolol (ACER-002) 200 mg twice daily vs placebo in COL3A1-positive vEDS (n = 150 estimated). The primary outcome is time to the first vEDS-related clinical event over about 40 months [[NCT05432466]].
- DiSCOVER is listed as recruiting, with primary completion estimated for 2029-03-01 (record last updated 2025-08-24) [[NCT05432466]].
- PREVEnt (NCT05463679) is a phase 3 placebo-controlled trial of the PKCβ inhibitor enzastaurin 500 mg daily for preventing arterial events in vEDS (n = 260 planned). It is SUSPENDED, "Indefinite hold by company", with the last update on 2024-03-08 [[NCT05463679]].
- In the UK national service cohort (126 patients), progression occurred in 10/111 treated vs 5/15 untreated patients. Five-year survival was 96.53% on medication vs 42.69% untreated. The study is retrospective, observational and confounded [[Bowen2023]].
- A US trial of heart-coherence training in vEDS has a primary outcome of change in systolic blood pressure (n = 40) and is listed as recruiting [[NCT05994664]].

### frontier · vEDS mouse and mechanism studies
- In two Col3a1 glycine-substitution mouse models, abnormal PLC/IP3/PKC/ERK signalling drove vascular disease. Inhibiting ERK1/2 or PKCβ prevented death from aortic rupture (Early evidence) [[Bowen2020JCI]].
- In the Col3a1 G938D mouse, survival at 45 days was 90% with cobimetinib (MEK/ERK inhibitor) and 94% with ruboxistaurin (PKCβ inhibitor), against 52% untreated. With hydralazine from birth, 97% of mice survived to 45 days (Early evidence) [[Bowen2020JCI]].
- In those mice, losartan, propranolol and atenolol had no survival effect, and celiprolol "accelerated rather than reduced death from aortic rupture" (Early evidence; mouse) [[Bowen2020JCI]].
- In those mice, puberty-related risk was reduced by blocking androgen signalling; bicalutamide gave 70% vs 50% survival at 60 days (Early evidence) [[Bowen2020JCI]].
- In the same model, combined androgen- and mineralocorticoid-receptor antagonism gave "near-complete and durable protection" in both sexes. Selective mineralocorticoid-receptor blockade was also effective (Early evidence) [[Juzwiak2026]].
- In a different vEDS mouse model, celiprolol and doxycycline, but not losartan, increased the aortic rupture force (Early evidence) [[Dubacher2020]].

### frontier · Registries and cohorts
- RaDiCo-SEDVasc (NCT05976841) is the French national vEDS cohort, with 340 enrolled. It is active and not recruiting, with completion estimated for 2028-01 [[NCT05976841]].
- The Italian Registry of EDS (RED, NCT04133272; Rizzoli) collects clinical, genetic, imaging and quality-of-life data linked to biosamples. It aims for 3000 participants and is recruiting through 2033 [[NCT04133272]].
- The Ehlers-Danlos Society Global Registry was the sampling frame for the 505-person diagnostic-odyssey survey [[Halverson2023]].
- An oxytocin trial for chronic pain in hEDS (NCT05405257) was terminated after enrolling 2 participants because of recruitment problems. This illustrates how hard EDS trials are to run [[NCT05405257]].
- Management of every EDS type remains supportive. "No specific medical or genetic therapies are available for any type of EDS" [[Malfait2020]].

### Exam hooks
- vEDS, cEDS and hEDS are autosomal dominant, so each child has a 50% chance of inheriting the condition [[Byers2025GeneReviews,Malfait2024GeneReviews,Hakim2024GeneReviews]].
- Around half of vEDS cases are de novo, so a negative family history does not exclude vEDS [[Byers2025GeneReviews]].
- In vEDS, arteriography is discouraged except to locate life-threatening bleeding before surgery [[Byers2025GeneReviews]].
- vEDS emergency card: carry a MedicAlert®, emergency letter or vEDS "passport", and seek urgent care for sudden unexplained pain [[Byers2025GeneReviews,Byers2017]].
- Spontaneous bowel perforation in a young person should prompt vEDS genetic testing [[Bowen2023]].
- Median survival in vEDS was 48 years in the 2000 cohort and 51 years in the 2014 cohort, both population figures [[Pepin2000,Pepin2014]].
- Celiprolol RCT: arterial events 20% vs 50%, HR 0.36 [[Ong2010]].
- Irbesartan added to celiprolol (ARCADE): composite outcome 27.6% vs 53.6%, HR 0.42 [[Jeunemaitre2025]].
- vEDS pregnancy-related deaths: 30 of 565 deliveries (5.3%) [[Murray2014]].
- Prenatal and preimplantation testing are possible for vEDS and cEDS once the familial variant is known, but not for hEDS [[Byers2025GeneReviews,Hakim2024GeneReviews]].
- cEDS sutures: two layers, no tension, skin stitches left twice as long as usual [[Malfait2024GeneReviews]].
- POTS on active stand: a heart-rate rise of at least 30/min within 10 minutes (at least 40/min at ages 12–19) without orthostatic hypotension [[Vernino2021]].
- Local anaesthetic in EDS: in the trial, 53% vs 80% were anaesthetised at 30 minutes, suggesting a shorter duration of effect [[Bourne2026]].
- The mean diagnostic delay in hEDS was about 10 years in US registry surveys [[Halverson2023,Wang2024]].
- Children's hypermobility threshold in the paediatric framework is Beighton ≥6/9 [[Tofts2023]].
- Hypermobile athletes have a higher risk of knee injury in contact sport (OR 4.69) but not of ankle injury [[Pacey2010]].
- The KLK15 hEDS candidate gene is Early evidence and not a diagnostic test [[Gensemer2025]].

### In plain words
- Many people with EDS wait 10 years or more for a name for their symptoms. Getting the diagnosis often helps people cope [[Halverson2023,Wang2024]].
- If doctors have doubted you, it is common to start doubting yourself. Your pain is real [[Halverson2025]].
- Most EDS types pass from parent to child. Each child has a 1 in 2 chance [[Byers2025GeneReviews,Hakim2024GeneReviews]].
- If someone in the family has vascular EDS, their parents, brothers, sisters and children can be offered a blood test for the same gene change [[Byers2025GeneReviews]].
- With vascular EDS, sudden, unexplained pain is an emergency: go to hospital and show your emergency card [[Byers2025GeneReviews]].
- People with vascular EDS should avoid collision sports and heavy lifting, but regular gentle exercise is good for the body and mind [[Byers2025GeneReviews,Bowen2023]].
- Pregnancy with vascular EDS needs planning with a high-risk pregnancy team before and during pregnancy [[Byers2025GeneReviews]].
- Many people with hypermobile EDS have uncomplicated pregnancies, but tell your midwife early so they can plan [[Karthikeyan2018,Pezaro2024]].
- Numbing injections may wear off sooner in EDS. Tell the nurse or dentist if you can still feel things [[Bourne2026]].
- Children with EDS may need help with handwriting and PE at school. A school plan can help [[Wesley2021,Hakim2024GeneReviews]].
- Anxiety and low mood are common in EDS, and help is available [[Cederlof2016,Bulbena2017]].
- Meeting other people with EDS, often called "zebras", helps many people feel less alone [[Callen2025,Bennett2021]].
- Scientists are looking for the gene or genes behind hypermobile EDS. There is no gene test for it yet [[Hakim2024GeneReviews,Gensemer2025]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Pregnancy advice in vEDS: GeneReviews cites a 5% mortality per pregnancy and high-risk care [[Byers2025GeneReviews]], and a review advises caesarean at 37 weeks [[Haem2024]]. A Dutch cohort found no deaths among 121 pregnancies and argues current discouragement "might be too strict" [[vandenBersselaar2026]]. Older series include women diagnosed only after a catastrophe, which biases risk upward [[Murray2014,Bowen2023]]. Status: no international guideline; shared decision-making is advised.
- Celiprolol in vEDS: an open RCT [[Ong2010]] and observational cohorts [[Frank2019,Bowen2023]] support it. In one mouse model it accelerated aortic rupture [[Bowen2020JCI]], while another mouse model showed benefit [[Dubacher2020]]. Status: the double-blind placebo-controlled DiSCOVER trial is recruiting [[NCT05432466]].
- Whether hEDS and HSD are one entity: fibroblast, plasma-fragment and serum-proteomic studies found no clear molecular separation [[Ritelli2022,Ritelli2025,Cinquina2026]]. The 2017 framework keeps them separate [[Malfait2017,Castori2017]]. Status: the revised criteria are announced for December 2026 (unverified) [[EDSociety2026]].
- Pregnancy risk in hEDS/HSD: a self-report survey suggests raised risks [[Pearce2023]], while a Swedish register study found none [[Sundelin2017]]. US inpatient data show raised risk but cannot separate types [[Spiegel2022]].
- KLK15 in hEDS: there is family segregation plus mouse data [[Gensemer2025]], but independent replication is not yet published. Earlier candidate genes failed to replicate [[Scicluna2022]].
- Nursing specifics not found in validated sources: blood-pressure cuff technique, tourniquet use, intramuscular injection avoidance, tape and dressing brands, and dislocation first-aid protocols. Published vEDS cautions found concern arteriography or catheter angiography, routine colonoscopy or elective endoscopy, elective surgery and collision sports [[Byers2025GeneReviews,Corrao2026]]. Builders should not add cuff, tourniquet or IM rules without a new validated source.
- Child protection: EDS belongs on the bruising differential [[Castori2015]], but EDS as an explanation for infant fractures is unproven [[Castori2015]].
- Psychiatric associations: the risk increase is clear [[Cederlof2016,Smith2014]], but mechanisms remain hypotheses. Proposed ones are shared genetics, autonomic dysfunction and interoception [[Bulbena2017]].

## Provenance
- Validation: 81 PubMed records were confirmed with E-utilities esummary (authors, title, journal, year, volume, pages, PMID, PMCID, DOI). That count includes three GeneReviews chapters, which are indexed as PubMed book records and have no DOI. Thirteen trials and registries were confirmed with the ClinicalTrials.gov v2 API (status, dates, enrolment, sponsor, interventions and primary outcomes) on 2026-10-25/26. One source is unverified (EDSociety2026).
- Abstracts were read for every PubMed source. Full text on PubMed Central was read for Bowen2023, Pezaro2024, Vernino2021 and Bowen2020JCI. The mouse survival percentages, the UK service figures and the perinatal recommendations come from those full texts, extracted with a page-reading tool. Builders who want exact wording should spot-check these against the PMC pages.
- Not accessible: the NCBI Bookshelf GeneReviews pages (CAPTCHA; not bypassed), so the GeneReviews summaries indexed in PubMed were used instead. Byers2017 full text and Anderson2015 full text were paywalled, and only their abstracts were used.
- Dropped for lack of a validated source: blood-pressure cuff and tourniquet guidance, IM-injection cautions, specific tape or dressing products, dislocation first-aid steps, driving guidance, disability-benefit rules by country, and school-plan (504/EHCP) specifics. I found no PubMed-indexed EDS evidence on these. HEDGE study design details (target size, sites) came only from organisational web pages and were not included. Its existence is cited from Scicluna2022.
- The Gensemer 2024 Research Square preprint on KLK variants (PMID 38947032) was not cited, because the peer-reviewed iScience 2025 version supersedes it.
- Evidence labels: (Established) is used for replicated or registry-scale findings, (Early evidence) for single studies, pilots, preclinical or mouse work, and (Hypothesis) for proposed mechanisms.
