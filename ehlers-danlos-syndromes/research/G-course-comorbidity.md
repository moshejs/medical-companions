# G · Course, comorbidity & outcomes

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: vascular, autonomic, gut, pregnancy.

This pass covers what happens across a lifetime in the Ehlers-Danlos syndromes (EDS): the natural history and survival of vascular EDS (vEDS), the autonomic, gastrointestinal, mast-cell, pelvic, neurological and mental-health associations of hypermobile EDS (hEDS) and hypermobility spectrum disorder (HSD), pregnancy outcomes by type, and childhood. Every number below is a population statistic from a named cohort. It describes groups, not what will happen to any one person.

Access notes:
- Numbers come from PubMed or Europe PMC abstracts. Full text was read where it was open in PubMed Central: Bowen 2023 (UK vEDS service), Demirdas 2024 (Dutch vEDS cohort), Pearce 2023 (childbearing survey), Vernino 2021 (NIH POTS consensus), Sheldon 2015 (HRS POTS consensus) and Buso 2024 (used only to locate figures, not cited).
- Pepin 2014 (survival by COL3A1 variant class) is not open access. Its abstract gives the overall median survival (51 years) and says survival differs by sex and variant type, but gives no per-class medians. I did not quote per-class survival figures from memory. The variant-class contrast below rests on Leistritz 2011 (null variants), Frank 2015 and Shalhub 2019, which state their numbers in the abstract.
- The NCBI Bookshelf (GeneReviews) answered with a browser check, so I did not open the full chapters. GeneReviews statements are quoted from their PubMed/Europe PMC summaries only.
- The brief's "Kohn & Chang 2020" MCAS review is indexed in PubMed as Kucharik AH, Chang C (PMID 31267471). It is cited here as Kucharik2020.
- Freeman 2011 (autonomic consensus definition) has no abstract and was not opened. The POTS criteria are quoted from Sheldon 2015 and Vernino 2021, which both state them in full.
- Survey and specialist-clinic cohorts dominate the hEDS literature. Wherever a prevalence comes from such a cohort, the bullet says so, because referral and self-selection inflate it.

## Findings

### vascular · Lifetime course of vascular EDS
- In 220 index patients with biochemically confirmed vEDS and 199 affected relatives, complications were rare in childhood. 25% of index patients had a first complication by age 20, and more than 80% had at least one by age 40 [[Pepin2000]].
- The calculated median survival of that whole cohort was 48 years, and most deaths resulted from arterial rupture [[Pepin2000]].
- A larger series of 1,231 people with heterozygous COL3A1 variants (630 index cases, 601 relatives) found a median survival of 51 years. Survival was lower in men and varied with the type of variant [[Pepin2014]].
- In that series, missense and splice-site variants made up more than 90% of 572 COL3A1 alterations [[Pepin2014]].
- In 215 people with molecularly proven vEDS in France, the median age at first complication was 29 years (IQR 22-39). The commonest first events were arterial (48%) and digestive (24%) ruptures [[Frank2015]].
- Glycine substitutions and splice-site or in-frame variants were more severe than haploinsufficiency or terminal non-glycine variants. Median ages at first major complication across variant groups ranged from 23 to 47 years [[Frank2015]].
- People whose variants were null (haploinsufficient) had a lifespan that was extended. Their first complication came almost 15 years later than with missense or exon-skipping variants, and their major complications were limited to vascular events [[Leistritz2011]].
- Only 28% of relatives carrying null variants had a complication. Some reached their seventies or eighties without incident [[Leistritz2011]].
- A Mayo Clinic series of 31 patients with clinically diagnosed vEDS had survival free of vascular complications of 90% at age 20, 39% at age 40 and 20% at age 60 [[Oderich2005]].
- In the same series, survival was 68% at age 50 and 35% at age 80, and 11 of 12 deaths were linked to vascular or graft-related complications [[Oderich2005]].
- Procedure-related morbidity after vascular surgery in that series was 46%, including postoperative bleeding in 37% [[Oderich2005]].
- In a Dutch national cohort of 142 people with vEDS (50% female), 52% had a major event. The median age at first major event was 41.0 years (IQR 31.0-55.0) [[Demirdas2024]].
- In that cohort, men had a major event more often than women (48 vs 26; OR 3.61, 95% CI 1.82-7.14) and at a younger age (Kaplan-Meier P=0.004) [[Demirdas2024]].
- The median age at death was 43.0 years for index patients and 65.0 years for relatives. Relatives are typically found by family testing, so they are less severely ascertained [[Demirdas2024]].
- Male sex, the type and position of the COL3A1 variant, and a physical appearance highly suggestive of vEDS were associated with earlier major events in the Dutch cohort [[Demirdas2024]].
- The estimated frequency of vEDS is 1 in 50,000 to 1 in 200,000 [[Byers2017]].
- vEDS is autosomal dominant. About 50% of people diagnosed have an affected parent, and each child of an affected person has a 50% chance of inheriting the variant [[ByersGR-vEDS]].

### vascular · Where the arteries fail
- In 86 people with genetically confirmed vEDS from 11 US institutions, 53 (61.6%) had 139 aortic or arterial pathologies, at a median age of 33 years [[Shalhub2019]].
- The most commonly affected arteries were the mesenteric arteries (31.7%), followed by cerebrovascular (16.5%), iliac (16.5%) and renal (12.2%) arteries [[Shalhub2019]].
- 37.4% of aortic or arterial events presented as emergencies [[Shalhub2019]].
- Aortic pathology was present in 17 people (32% of those with arterial disease) [[Shalhub2019]].
- Glycine substitutions with a large amino acid had an earlier median onset of arterial pathology (30 years) than other pathogenic variants (36 years); P=0.065, so this difference was not statistically significant [[Shalhub2019]].
- There were 12 deaths (22.6%) at a median age of 36 years in that cohort [[Shalhub2019]].
- In the UK national service cohort of 180 people with vEDS, there were 25 fatal vascular events, 13 of them aortic dissections [[Bowen2023vEDS]].
- Carotid-cavernous fistula occurred in 6 of 180 (3.33%), all women aged 29-55 years [[Bowen2023vEDS]].
- The youngest vascular event in the UK cohort was an axillary artery rupture in a 12-year-old boy [[Bowen2023vEDS]].
- Spontaneous carotid-cavernous sinus fistula, arterial rupture or dissection under age 40, spontaneous sigmoid colon perforation and unexplained third-trimester uterine rupture are among the major criteria proposed as indications for vEDS genetic testing [[Demirdas2024]].

### vascular · Bowel, lung and other organ ruptures
- Bowel rupture accounted for about a quarter of complications in the 2000 series. It often involved the sigmoid colon and rarely led to death [[Pepin2000]].
- A systematic review of spontaneous gut perforation in vEDS (31 case reports, 527 cases from 4 retrospective studies) found a mean age of 26 years (range 6-64) [[ElMasri2018]].
- The colon, particularly the sigmoid, was the commonest site, followed by small bowel, upper rectum and stomach [[ElMasri2018]].
- Reperforation was considerably more frequent after partial colectomy with anastomosis than after Hartmann's procedure [[ElMasri2018]].
- In the UK cohort, 25 of 180 (13.88%) had a bowel perforation, mostly under age 30 (range 9-54). Perforations peaked in the mid-teens and early twenties, and 7 people perforated again [[Bowen2023vEDS]].
- Pneumothorax occurred in 10 of 180 (5.55%), the youngest one day old. Eight of the 10 happened before vEDS was diagnosed [[Bowen2023vEDS]].
- Four people in the UK cohort had a ruptured spleen [[Bowen2023vEDS]].
- People diagnosed with vEDS by clinical criteria alone differed from those with pathogenic COL3A1 variants. They had less intestinal perforation (3.9% vs 16.3%), less thin translucent skin (17.1% vs 48.8%) and more joint hypermobility (68.4% vs 40.7%). The authors conclude that molecular confirmation is needed [[Shalhub2020]].

### vascular · Children with vEDS
- In 63 people diagnosed with vEDS in childhood in the London national service, 13 had 18 events in childhood. The first event came at a median age of 11 years (IQR 0-13) [[Wilkinson2025]].
- In 11 of those 13 children, genetic testing started because of the first event. Across the whole cohort, 55% were diagnosed through family genetic testing [[Wilkinson2025]].
- About 60% of people diagnosed with vEDS before age 18 are identified because of a positive family history [[ByersGR-vEDS]].
- About half of children tested for vEDS without a family history present with a major complication, at an average age of 11 years [[ByersGR-vEDS]].
- Neonates may present with clubfoot, hip dislocation, limb deficiency or amniotic bands [[ByersGR-vEDS]].

### vascular · Survival in the modern era
- In a French referral cohort of 144 people with molecularly confirmed vEDS (2000-2017; median age at diagnosis 34.5 years), overall survival after a median follow-up of 5.3 years was 71.6% (95% CI 50%-90%) [[Frank2019]].
- In that cohort, 90.3% were on celiprolol by the end. Those treated had better survival than others (p=0.0004), and survival was best at 400 mg/day (p=0.003 vs lower doses). This is observational, so treatment and overall specialist care cannot be separated [[Frank2019]].
- In the UK statistical cohort (126 people, median follow-up 3.8 years), survival from first clinic visit was 99.14% at 1 year, 96.10% at 3 years and 90.53% at 5 years [[Bowen2023vEDS]].
- The UK authors report that Frank et al. (2019) found 99.3% survival at 1 year and 89.9% at 5 years [[Bowen2023vEDS]].
- 5-year survival in the UK cohort was 96.53% for people on long-term cardiovascular medication and 42.69% for those not on medication (log-rank p=0.0002). The data are retrospective and the untreated group was small [[Bowen2023vEDS]].
- Eight UK patients had vEDS-related deaths during follow-up, at a median age of 26 years (IQR 19.25-30.5) [[Bowen2023vEDS]].
- In the only randomised trial (53 people with clinical vEDS, of whom 33 were COL3A1-positive), arterial rupture or dissection occurred in 20% on celiprolol vs 50% of controls (HR 0.36, 95% CI 0.15-0.88). Mean follow-up was 47 months, and the trial stopped early for benefit [[Ong2010]].
- Consensus management includes an emergency plan or "vascular EDS passport", keeping blood pressure in the normal range, and arterial surveillance, often annually [[Byers2017]].

### vascular · The emergency picture
- People with vEDS are told to seek immediate medical attention for sudden, unexplained pain [[ByersGR-vEDS]].
- Vascular dissection or rupture, gastrointestinal perforation or organ rupture are the presenting signs in most adults with vEDS. Arterial rupture may be preceded by aneurysm, arteriovenous fistula or dissection, or may happen spontaneously [[ByersGR-vEDS]].
- Arteriography should be discouraged and used only to find life-threatening bleeding before surgery, because it can injure the vessels. Routine colonoscopy is not advised without concerning symptoms or a strong family history of colon cancer [[ByersGR-vEDS]].
- Elective surgery is discouraged unless the benefit is expected to be substantial [[ByersGR-vEDS]].
- When a young person presents with uterine rupture in pregnancy, or with arterial or visceral rupture, vEDS should be considered [[Pepin2000]].

### pregnancy · Vascular EDS
- In the 2000 series, pregnancy complications led to death in 12 of the 81 women who became pregnant [[Pepin2000]].
- A pedigree review found pregnancy-related deaths in 30 of 565 deliveries (5.3%) [[Murray2014]].
- Kaplan-Meier survival was no different between parous and nulliparous women with vEDS [[Murray2014]].
- In interviews covering 76 deliveries, 46% were uncomplicated. The most common complications were third- or fourth-degree lacerations (20%) and preterm delivery (19%) [[Murray2014]].
- Life-threatening complications occurred in 14.5% of interviewed deliveries: arterial dissection or rupture 9.2%, uterine rupture 2.6% and surgical complications 2.6%. There were 5 maternal deaths in 76 deliveries (6.5%) [[Murray2014]].
- GeneReviews summarises the risk as a 5% mortality risk with each pregnancy for affected women [[ByersGR-vEDS]].
- A 2024 systematic review (6 studies, 412 pregnancies) found a maternal mortality rate of 5.7% per pregnancy. The miscarriage rate was 13.8% (57/412), 8.8% of live births were premature, and obstetric anal sphincter injury occurred in 11.3% (23/203) of deliveries [[Haem2024]].
- The authors of that review suggest a caesarean at 37 weeks to avoid expulsive efforts. This is their conclusion, not a trial result [[Haem2024]].
- In the UK cohort, 53 women had 114 pregnancies. Excluding women with group III (later-onset) variants, 10% (5/48) had a life-threatening or fatal vascular event in pregnancy or the perinatal period [[Bowen2023vEDS]].
- No woman in the UK cohort had a uterine rupture [[Bowen2023vEDS]].
- A 2026 review reports maternal mortality in vEDS ranging from 0.10% to 50% across studies. The authors attribute the width of this range to older studies that did not use genetic testing [[Almheiri2026]].

### pregnancy · Hypermobile EDS, HSD and all-type EDS
- A Swedish nationwide cohort (314 singleton births to women with joint hypermobility syndrome or EDS vs 1,247,864 births) found no increase in preterm birth (aOR 0.6, 95% CI 0.3-1.2) or preterm premature rupture of membranes (aOR 0.8, 0.3-2.2) [[Sundelin2017]].
- In that cohort, women with an EDS code (n=62) had more induction of labour (aOR 2.6, 1.4-4.6) and amniotomy (aOR 3.8, 2.0-7.1) [[Sundelin2017]].
- A US inpatient database study (1999-2014; all EDS types, including vascular) found EDS in 7 per 100,000 births, with prevalence rising over time [[Spiegel2022]].
- In that study, EDS was associated with prematurity (aOR 1.47, 1.18-1.82), cervical incompetence (3.11, 1.99-4.85), antepartum haemorrhage (1.71), placenta previa (2.26), caesarean delivery (1.55) and maternal death (9.04, 1.27-64.27). The very wide CI reflects few deaths [[Spiegel2022]].
- An international online survey of 947 women with hEDS/HSD (1,338 pregnancies) reported preterm birth before 37 weeks in 15.45% of babies (13.08% of singletons), against a quoted population figure of 7.8% (6.2% for singletons) [[Pearce2023]].
- The same survey reported preterm rupture of membranes in 7.03% (population <3%), precipitate labour under 3 hours in 34.29% of labouring pregnancies, postpartum haemorrhage in 20.60% (population 6.38%) and hyperemesis gravidarum in 25.48% (population 3%) [[Pearce2023]].
- These survey figures are self-reported by volunteers recruited online and may over-represent complicated pregnancies [[Pearce2023]].
- In 386 French women with hEDS seen in an expert centre, caesarean delivery (14.6%) and premature birth (6.2%) were similar to the general French population. Spontaneous abortion (28%) and multiple spontaneous abortion (13%) were higher [[HugonRodin2016]].
- In a Dutch patient-association study (46 women with EDS, 128 pregnancies beyond 24 weeks), pelvic pain and instability (26% vs 7%) and postpartum haemorrhage (19% vs 7%) were more frequent than in unaffected mothers [[Lind2002]].
- In that study, preterm delivery occurred in 21% of affected mothers but in 40% of unaffected mothers carrying an affected infant. This points to fetal EDS (fragile membranes) as a driver of prematurity [[Lind2002]].
- Premature rupture of membranes was strongly associated with fetal EDS in a 2026 review, and fetal EDS status predicted complications more strongly than maternal disease [[Almheiri2026]].
- A 2026 systematic review found that EDS studies of spontaneous preterm birth were too heterogeneous for a pooled estimate. Narrative synthesis consistently pointed to more preterm birth, and the evidence is weaker than for Marfan syndrome (OR 2.71, 2.23-3.30) [[Treacy2026]].
- In most women with hEDS, pregnancy is uncomplicated. Joint dislocation, pain, autonomic symptoms and POTS are the issues to plan for [[Karthikeyan2018]].
- In the French hEDS cohort, gynaecological complaints were common: menorrhagia (76%), dysmenorrhoea (72%) and dyspareunia (43%). Symptoms worsened around puberty, before menstruation and postpartum in a subset [[HugonRodin2016]].

### pregnancy · Classical EDS
- Classical EDS (heterozygous variants in COL5A1, COL5A2 or COL1A1) is autosomal dominant with fragile, hyperextensible skin and atrophic scars. Aortic root dilatation and mitral valve prolapse occur but are rarely clinically significant [[MalfaitGR-cEDS]].
- Children with classical EDS may have mild hypotonia with delayed motor development [[MalfaitGR-cEDS]].
- I found no large cohort giving classical-EDS-specific obstetric rates. The fetal-membrane mechanism above [[Lind2002,Almheiri2026]] is the best-supported point.

### autonomic · POTS definitions
- POTS is defined as a sustained heart-rate rise of at least 30 beats/min within 10 minutes of standing or head-up tilt (at least 40 beats/min at ages 12-19), without orthostatic hypotension (no sustained systolic fall of 20 mmHg or more), with frequent orthostatic symptoms that improve rapidly on lying down [[Vernino2021,Sheldon2015]].
- Symptoms that occur on standing include light-headedness, palpitations, tremor, generalised weakness, blurred vision, exercise intolerance and fatigue [[Sheldon2015]].
- POTS prevalence has not been properly studied. Estimates based largely on clinical experience are 0.2% to 1% of the US population, about 1 to 3 million people [[Vernino2021]].
- A typical patient with POTS is female, aged 12 to 50 (female-to-male ratio over 4:1), and symptoms often begin in adolescence [[Vernino2021]].
- There are no FDA-approved medicines for POTS, and evidence for many of the medicines used is not robust [[Vernino2021]].

### autonomic · How often orthostatic problems occur in hEDS (and the bias)
- In 27 people with joint hypermobility syndrome and 21 controls tested, orthostatic hypotension, POTS or uncategorised orthostatic intolerance was found in 78% vs 10% [[Gazit2003]].
- In 39 women with EDS hypermobility type vs 35 matched controls, orthostatic intolerance was more prevalent (74% vs 34%), most often as postural tachycardia [[DeWandele2014Mech]].
- In a head-up tilt subsample, 41.0% of the EDS hypermobility group had postural orthostatic tachycardia, and tilt increased fatigue by 3.1 vs 0.5 points in controls [[DeWandele2016]].
- In 102 adults with hEDS/HSD, abnormal autonomic testing included POTS in 48%, orthostatic intolerance in 25.5% and hypotension in 3.9% [[Celletti2020]].
- Total autonomic symptom burden was higher in EDS hypermobility type (57.9) than in controls (11.3), classical EDS (32.3) and vascular EDS (29.1), and similar to fibromyalgia (53.8) [[DeWandele2014ASP]].
- Among 91 people with POTS assessed with the 2017 checklist, 31% met hEDS criteria and a further 24% had generalised joint hypermobility without meeting hEDS criteria [[Miller2020POTS]].
- The NIH consensus states that about 20-30% of POTS patients meet hEDS criteria, and that orthostatic intolerance and tachycardia occur in up to 40% of people with HSD or hEDS. These estimates come from small samples or uncontrolled surveys [[Vernino2021]].
- A review of 10 studies found that orthostatic intolerance was consistently more prevalent in joint hypermobility or EDS than in controls, with methodological variability between studies [[Roma2018]].
- An expert review states that the incidence, prevalence and natural history of autonomic conditions in hEDS remain unquantified. The figures come from specialist clinics [[Hakim2017]].
- Selection bias: most cohorts above were recruited in autonomic or hypermobility clinics, so their percentages are likely upper estimates and not population prevalence (Established) [[Hakim2017,Roma2018,Vernino2021]].
- In 708 children and adolescents seen in a paediatric POTS programme, 57.3% had joint hypermobility, 22.4% had hEDS and 34.9% had HSD [[Boris2018]].
- In that programme, symptoms started earlier in those with hypermobility (median 12.6 vs 13.7 years) and lasted longer before diagnosis (3.3 vs 1.5 years) [[Boris2018]].
- All 12 adolescents in an early series with both chronic fatigue syndrome and EDS (6 classical, 6 hypermobile) had postural tachycardia or neurally mediated hypotension [[Rowe1999]].
- Orthostatic intolerance, pain, affective distress, lower physical activity and sedative use together explained 47.7% of the variance in fatigue severity in EDS hypermobility type [[DeWandele2016]].

### gut · Functional gastrointestinal disorders
- Of 687 people with EDS seen at Mayo Clinic over 20 years, 56% had GI manifestations: abdominal pain (56.1%), nausea (42.3%), constipation (38.6%), heartburn (37.6%) and IBS-like symptoms (27.5%) [[Nelson2015Mayo]].
- In that cohort, gastric emptying was abnormal in 22.3% of those tested (11.8% delayed, 10.5% accelerated) and colonic transit was abnormal in 28.3% [[Nelson2015Mayo]].
- Rectal evacuation disorder was confirmed in 18 of 30 who had anorectal manometry [[Nelson2015Mayo]].
- In 552 consecutive new GI clinic patients, 33% had previously undiagnosed joint hypermobility syndrome. Heartburn (OR 1.66), water brash (OR 2.02) and postprandial fullness (OR 1.74) were more common in them [[Fikree2014]].
- Joint hypermobility syndrome was more prevalent in functional GI disorders than in organic GI disease (39.0% vs 27.5%; adjusted OR 1.51, 95% CI 1.07-2.12), especially in postprandial distress syndrome (51%) [[Fikree2015]].
- Of 129 tertiary neurogastroenterology referrals, 49% had generalised joint hypermobility. In 12 of 17 hypermobility-syndrome patients with upper GI symptoms, gastric emptying was delayed [[Zarate2010]].
- Joint hypermobility was found in 32% of 200 patients with rectal evacuatory dysfunction vs 14% of controls. On proctography, 86% of the hypermobile patients had morphological abnormalities, compared with 64% of those without hypermobility [[Mohammed2010]].
- In 435 children and young adults with hEDS or joint hypermobility syndrome, constipation was present in 61%, dysphagia 32%, dyspepsia or gastroparesis 25%, eosinophilic oesophagitis 21% and coeliac disease 4% [[Sood2024]].
- Delayed gastric emptying is the most commonly reported GI motility abnormality in POTS [[Mehr2018]].
- In 680 people with hEDS/HSD, 62.1% had changed their diet in the past year. 31.7% of those needed nutrition support, and a positive screen for ARFID fear of eating predicted it (OR 4.97, 95% CI 2.09-11.8) [[Topan2024]].
- In 1,044 people with hEDS/HSD, cluster analysis found 466 with functional foregut disorders plus IBS, 180 with IBS alone and 337 with no DGBI. The foregut-plus-IBS cluster had more anxiety, depression, POTS and autonomic symptoms [[Choudhary2025]].
- A gastroenterology review describes JHS/hEDS as highly prevalent in functional dyspepsia and IBS, with the pathophysiology still uncertain [[Beckers2017]].
- The American Gastroenterological Association (2025) advises testing for functional defecation disorders in hEDS/HSD with lower GI symptoms, "given the high prevalence of pelvic floor dysfunction, especially rectal hyposensitivity" [[Aziz2025]].
- The AGA also advises timely gastric-function testing in hEDS/HSD with POTS and chronic upper GI symptoms, as abnormal gastric emptying may be more common than in the general population [[Aziz2025]].
- The AGA suggests considering coeliac testing earlier in hEDS/HSD. It finds insufficient research to support routine testing for disaccharidase deficiency [[Aziz2025]].

### gut · Mast cell activation: association and the strength of evidence
- A systematic literature search on the hEDS-POTS-MCAS triad found only 4 (narrow search) and 9 (broad search) original research articles. None studied all three conditions together [[Kucharik2020]].
- That review concludes that the purported association rests on overlapping vague, subjective symptoms and on biased or outdated-criteria studies, which it calls inadequate evidence that the relationship exists (critique) [[Kucharik2020]].
- Supportive reviews propose that mast-cell mediators could disrupt connective tissue and explain multisystem symptoms in hEDS/HSD (Hypothesis) [[Monaco2022,Seneviratne2017]].
- GeneReviews lists inflammation from mast cell activation disorders among common features of hEDS, which shows how clinical-review sources and critics disagree [[HakimGR-hEDS,Kucharik2020]].
- The AGA (2025) states that mechanistic evidence linking hEDS/HSD with POTS and MCAS is limited and evolving. It does not support universal testing for POTS or MCAS in hEDS/HSD [[Aziz2025]].
- The AGA reserves mast-cell testing for patients with episodic symptoms in 2 or more organ systems. It requires a tryptase rise of 20% above baseline plus 2 ng/mL to show mast cell activation [[Aziz2025]].
- The NIH POTS consensus says accurate data on the frequency of MCAS in POTS are not available [[Vernino2021]].
- Hereditary alpha-tryptasemia (extra TPSAB1 copies) was first linked to elevated basal tryptase, dysautonomia, IBS-like symptoms and joint hypermobility in 35 families. Elevated basal tryptase is present in 4-6% of the general population [[Lyons2016]].
- When people with HSD (n=132), hEDS (n=78) and axial hypermobility (n=56) were genotyped, hereditary alpha-tryptasemia was not more prevalent than in the general population (5-7% of Western populations). It was linked to dysphagia and retained primary teeth [[Vazquez2022]].
- Evidence label: the MCAS-hEDS association is (Hypothesis) to (Early evidence). No controlled study using consensus MCAS criteria has shown it [[Kucharik2020,Aziz2025,Vazquez2022]].

### gut · Bladder, pelvic floor and prolapse
- A meta-analysis of 14 studies found joint hypermobility associated with pelvic organ prolapse (pooled OR 2.37, 95% CI 1.54-3.64), with high heterogeneity (I² 77%) and six studies at high risk of bias [[VeitRubin2016]].
- Urinary incontinence affected 73.3% of 60 women with benign joint hypermobility syndrome vs 48.3% of matched controls, and anterior vaginal wall prolapse was more severe [[Mastoroudes2013]].
- In a pilot study of 21 people with EDS hypermobility type, gastrointestinal and pelvic dysfunction were characteristic additional features [[Castori2010]].

### autonomic · Chiari malformation and craniocervical instability claims
- In 2,813 patients with Chiari malformation type I, 357 (12.7%) met criteria for EDS or a related heritable connective tissue disorder. That group was more female (8:1 vs 3:1) and showed cranial settling when upright [[Milhorat2007]].
- An international consensus review states that links between EDS and migraine, idiopathic intracranial hypertension, Tarlov cysts, tethered cord and dystonia have been reported anecdotally, without epidemiological evidence so far [[Henderson2017]].
- A systematic review of craniocervical instability in EDS found 16 articles covering 78 surgical patients, evidence levels III to V and no high-quality prospective data. It recommends fusion only with clear radiographic instability and matching symptoms [[Lohkamp2022]].
- In a neurosurgical referral cohort (717 people, 460 with hEDS), 59% had Chiari I and 73% of the hEDS patients who chose surgery had craniocervical fusion. Referral to a neurosurgical centre selects for these findings, so the rates do not apply to hEDS generally [[Ruhoy2024]].
- A small study (20 EDS patients vs 18 controls) found more spinal cord motion at C1-2 on ultrasound in EDS without radiological CCI (Early evidence) [[Klinge2021]].
- Evidence label: the association of Chiari I and craniocervical instability with hEDS is (Early evidence) from referral cohorts, and surgical benefit is unproven by controlled studies [[Henderson2017,Lohkamp2022]].

### autonomic · Mental health and neurodevelopment
- In a 1993 case-control study (114 people with joint hypermobility syndrome, 59 controls), panic disorder, agoraphobia and simple phobia were strongly associated with JHS (age- and sex-adjusted OR 10.7) [[Bulbena1993]].
- A meta-analysis (14 studies, 1,006 with and 2,951 without BJHS) found higher odds of anxiety (OR 4.39, 95% CI 1.92-10.40), depression (OR 4.10, 1.79-9.41) and panic disorder (OR 6.72, 2.22-20.35). Neither was assessed in children [[Smith2014Rheum]].
- A review finds a strong association between hEDS/JHS and anxiety disorders. Evidence for depression, eating disorders, neurodevelopmental disorders and substance use is described as limited but growing [[Bulbena2017]].
- In a Swedish national register study (1,771 people with EDS vs 17,710 matched controls), EDS was associated with autism (RR 7.4, 95% CI 5.2-10.7), ADHD (RR 5.6, 4.2-7.4), depression (RR 3.4, 2.9-4.1), bipolar disorder (RR 2.7, 1.5-4.7) and attempted suicide (RR 2.1, 1.7-2.7). It was not associated with suicide or schizophrenia [[Cederlof2016]].
- Siblings without EDS also had higher risks of ADHD (RR 2.1), depression (RR 1.5) and suicide attempt (RR 1.8), which suggests shared genetic or early environmental factors [[Cederlof2016]].
- In 201 Swedish children with HSD or hEDS, 16% had ADHD and 6% had autism. In hEDS, ADHD rose to 35% at ages 15-16 and 46% at ages 17-18 [[Kindgren2021]].
- In 109 neurodivergent adults (autism, ADHD, Tourette), 51% had generalised joint hypermobility vs about 20% in the general population (OR 4.51, 95% CI 2.17-9.37) [[Csecs2021]].
- In a school sample of 207 eleven-year-olds, high Beighton scores were not associated with neurodevelopmental problems. The authors suggest the adult link may emerge around puberty [[Glans2025]].
- In 99 German adults with EDS or generalised HSD, 58.6% self-reported a lifetime mental-health diagnosis. 60.2% scored above the PHQ-9 depression cut-off and 45.4% above the DASS anxiety cut-off [[Henning2026]].

### autonomic · Childhood course of hypermobility
- In 125 children with joint hypermobility syndrome at a London tertiary clinic, symptoms began at a mean age of 6.2 years and diagnosis came at 9.0 years, a 2-3 year delay [[Adib2005]].
- In that clinic, mean age at first walking was 15.0 months. 48% were described as "clumsy", 36% had poor coordination and 41% had missed significant school time [[Adib2005]].
- Infants with joint hypermobility and motor delay at 18 months still had gross and fine motor delay at age 5. Infants with hypermobility alone did not [[Tirosh1991]].
- Beighton scores in 1,845 Swedish schoolchildren (ages 9, 12, 15) depended on age and sex. Laxity fell with age in boys, peaked at 15 in girls, and was higher in girls at every age [[Jansson2004]].
- In the ALSPAC cohort, 4.6% had a Beighton score of 6 or more at 13.8 years. This predicted moderately troublesome pain at 17.8 years in the shoulder (OR 1.68), knee (OR 1.83) and ankle/foot (OR 1.82) [[Tobias2013]].
- Among Swedish 11-year-olds, the median Beighton score was 1 (IQR 0-2), and a score of 6 or more approximated the 95th percentile [[Glans2025]].

### autonomic · The hEDS life course (phases and burden)
- A pilot study of 21 people with EDS hypermobility type outlined three phases of natural history: hypermobility, pain and stiffness [[Castori2010]].
- A follow-up review proposed clustering pain, fatigue and headache by disease stage. These stages are based on the authors' clinical experience (Hypothesis) [[Castori2013]].
- hEDS is a clinical diagnosis with no known gene. The 2017 criteria separate it from hypermobility spectrum disorder, which covers symptomatic hypermobility that does not meet hEDS criteria [[Malfait2017,Castori2017,Tinkle2017]].
- In an international survey of 3,906 people (3,360 hEDS, 546 HSD), hEDS respondents reported a mean of 24 comorbid conditions and a mean diagnostic delay of 22.1 years. HSD respondents reported 17 comorbidities and a 17.5-year delay. These are self-reported survey data [[Daylor2025]].
- A Danish register cohort of 1,427 people with any EDS type (prevalence 0.02%; 73.9% female) recorded 42 deaths over 13 years, at a mean age of 53.6 years. 31.6% received state subsidies, mostly early retirement pensions [[KulasSoborg2017]].
- No specific medical or genetic therapy exists for any type of EDS, and management is organised around surveillance and rehabilitation [[Malfait2020]].

### Exam hooks
- vEDS: first complication in about 25% by age 20 and more than 80% by age 40; median survival 48 years (2000) and 51 years (2014) [[Pepin2000,Pepin2014]].
- The classic vEDS gut event is spontaneous sigmoid colon perforation; the emergency operation favoured in the review is Hartmann's procedure [[ElMasri2018]].
- Most vEDS deaths are from arterial rupture; bowel rupture is common but rarely fatal [[Pepin2000]].
- Mesenteric arteries are the most commonly affected arterial bed in genetically confirmed vEDS (31.7%) [[Shalhub2019]].
- Null (haploinsufficient) COL3A1 variants delay the first complication by about 15 years [[Leistritz2011]].
- Men with vEDS have major events earlier and more often than women, and lower survival [[Demirdas2024,Pepin2014]].
- Pregnancy in vEDS: about 5% maternal mortality per pregnancy [[Murray2014,ByersGR-vEDS,Haem2024]].
- Sudden unexplained pain in someone with vEDS needs immediate medical attention [[ByersGR-vEDS]].
- Avoid diagnostic arteriography and routine colonoscopy in vEDS unless clearly needed [[ByersGR-vEDS]].
- Celiprolol trial: arterial events 20% vs 50%, HR 0.36 [[Ong2010]].
- POTS: HR rise of at least 30 bpm within 10 min of standing (at least 40 at ages 12-19), with no orthostatic hypotension [[Sheldon2015,Vernino2021]].
- About 31% of a POTS cohort met 2017 hEDS criteria [[Miller2020POTS]].
- MCAS testing in hEDS is targeted, not universal; activation requires a tryptase rise of 20% + 2 ng/mL over baseline [[Aziz2025]].
- Hereditary alpha-tryptasemia is not more common in hypermobility disorders than in the general population [[Vazquez2022]].
- Joint hypermobility is about twice as common in functional as in organic GI disorders in clinic (39.0% vs 27.5%) [[Fikree2015]].
- Joint hypermobility and pelvic organ prolapse: pooled OR 2.37 [[VeitRubin2016]].
- EDS is associated with ADHD (RR 5.6) and autism (RR 7.4) in Swedish registers [[Cederlof2016]].
- hEDS natural history, as described by Castori: hypermobility, then pain, then stiffness [[Castori2010]].

### In plain words
- Vascular EDS makes blood vessels, the bowel and the womb fragile. Problems are rare in childhood but become common in adult life [[Pepin2000]].
- If someone with vascular EDS gets sudden, severe pain anywhere, they should get emergency care straight away and show their EDS information [[ByersGR-vEDS,Byers2017]].
- Survival numbers for vascular EDS are averages from groups of people. Recent clinics report that about 9 in 10 people are alive 5 years after joining care [[Bowen2023vEDS]].
- Pregnancy with vascular EDS is high risk, with about 1 death in 20 pregnancies in older studies. It needs planning with a specialist team [[Murray2014,Haem2024]].
- Most pregnancies in hypermobile EDS go well, though fast labour, heavy bleeding and pelvic pain are reported more often [[Karthikeyan2018,Pearce2023,Lind2002]].
- Many people with hypermobile EDS feel dizzy, faint or have a racing heart when they stand. This can be checked with a simple standing test [[DeWandele2016,Aziz2025]].
- Tummy pain, reflux, nausea and constipation are common in hypermobile EDS, and gut doctors have specific advice for them [[Nelson2015Mayo,Aziz2025]].
- Doctors still disagree about whether mast cell problems are truly linked to EDS. Testing is only advised when symptoms fit [[Kucharik2020,Aziz2025]].
- Anxiety and low mood are more common with joint hypermobility. They are real and treatable, and having them does not mean the physical symptoms are imagined [[Smith2014Rheum,Bulbena2017]].
- ADHD and autism are diagnosed more often in people with EDS and their siblings [[Cederlof2016]].
- Many children are bendy and outgrow it. Bendiness alone, with no pain or other problems, is not a disease [[Jansson2004,Tirosh1991]].
- Neck surgery for craniocervical instability is a big decision, and the evidence for it is weak [[Lohkamp2022]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Is vEDS survival improving because of medicines or because of specialist care? Observational cohorts show high modern survival and better survival on celiprolol or other cardiovascular drugs [[Frank2019,Bowen2023vEDS]]. The only RCT was small, open-label, stopped early and included people without a COL3A1 variant [[Ong2010]]. The French authors themselves say overall care seems to have a positive influence [[Frank2019]]. Status: unresolved. Survivorship and lead-time bias (earlier diagnosis via family testing) are plausible contributors.
- How large is the vEDS maternal mortality risk today? Pedigree and older series show about 5-6.5% [[Murray2014,Pepin2000]]. A 2024 review gives 5.7% per pregnancy [[Haem2024]]. In the UK cohort, 10% of women with non-group-III variants had a life-threatening or fatal vascular event, and there were no uterine ruptures [[Bowen2023vEDS]]. Status: older data may overestimate the risk where diagnosis is now known and delivery planned. Data on mode and timing of delivery are insufficient [[Murray2014]].
- How strongly are POTS and orthostatic intolerance linked to hEDS? Clinic cohorts report 41-78% orthostatic intolerance or POTS [[Gazit2003,DeWandele2014Mech,DeWandele2016,Celletti2020]]. Reviewers note that incidence and prevalence are unquantified [[Hakim2017]] and that estimates come from small or uncontrolled samples [[Vernino2021]]. Status: the association is (Established) in clinic populations. Population prevalence is unknown.
- Is MCAS really part of a hEDS triad? Supportive reviews: [[Seneviratne2017,Monaco2022]]. A critical review finds the evidence inadequate [[Kucharik2020]]. The AGA advises targeted rather than universal testing [[Aziz2025]]. Hereditary alpha-tryptasemia is not enriched in hypermobility disorders [[Vazquez2022]]. Status: (Hypothesis)/(Early evidence).
- Are preterm birth and PROM more common in hEDS? A national register found no increase [[Sundelin2017]]. A volunteer survey reported about twice the population rate [[Pearce2023]]. All-type EDS inpatient data show modestly raised odds [[Spiegel2022]]. A systematic review could not pool EDS studies [[Treacy2026]]. Status: unresolved. Fetal EDS status may matter more than maternal status [[Lind2002,Almheiri2026]].
- Are Chiari I and craniocervical instability part of hEDS? Evidence comes from neurosurgical referral cohorts [[Milhorat2007,Ruhoy2024]] and small mechanistic studies [[Klinge2021]]. Systematic review evidence is level III-V with no consensus criteria [[Lohkamp2022]], and consensus authors call several neurological links anecdotal [[Henderson2017]]. Status: (Early evidence). Surgery is not supported by controlled data.
- Do hypermobility and neurodevelopmental conditions co-occur from childhood, or emerge in adolescence? Register and clinic data show strong associations [[Cederlof2016,Kindgren2021,Csecs2021]]. A school-based sample of 11-year-olds found none [[Glans2025]]. Status: unresolved. Ascertainment differs between clinics and schools.
- How should hEDS be distinguished from HSD in outcome studies? Many older studies used joint hypermobility syndrome or EDS-HT labels that predate the 2017 criteria [[Castori2017,Malfait2017,Kucharik2020]]. Status: comparison across eras is limited.

- All 82 sources were checked against PubMed E-utilities `esummary` (authors, title, journal, year, volume/issue/pages, PMID, PMCID and DOI copied from the record). Abstracts were read through the Europe PMC REST API (`resultType=core`) for every source.
- Full text was read from PubMed Central (via E-utilities `efetch db=pmc`) for Bowen 2023 (PMC10326011), Demirdas 2024 (PMC11188628), Pearce 2023 (PMC10606623), Vernino 2021 (PMC8455420) and Sheldon 2015 (PMC5267948). Numbers from these papers that are not in their abstracts come from that full text: UK organ-event rates, Kaplan-Meier survival points, pregnancy events, the Frank 2019 survival points as reported by Bowen, Dutch median ages at death, the Pearce outcome table and the POTS criteria.
- Not accessible: Pepin 2014, Frank 2019, Murray 2014, Ong 2010, Malfait 2020 and the other paywalled papers were used at abstract level only. Per-variant-class median survival from Pepin 2014 was not available in the abstract and is not given. GeneReviews chapters (NBK pages) returned a browser verification page, so only their PubMed/Europe PMC summaries were used. Their source lines reuse pass B's text, including B's revision dates, which this pass did not re-check.
- Freeman 2011 (consensus definition of orthostatic hypotension and POTS) has no abstract and was not opened, so it is not cited. The definition is quoted from Sheldon 2015 and Vernino 2021.
- Name correction: the MCAS critique referred to in the brief as "Kohn & Chang 2020" is Kucharik AH, Chang C, Clin Rev Allergy Immunol 2020 (PMID 31267471).
- Dropped as unverifiable from available text: per-variant vEDS survival medians, a specific population prevalence of POTS within hEDS, classical-EDS-specific obstetric rates, and the Bulbena-group anxiety prevalence percentages (only odds ratios are in the abstracts).
