# B · Genetics, collagen biology & the 13 types

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: collagen, types, genes, skin, differential.

This pass covers the molecular basis of the Ehlers-Danlos syndromes (EDS): how fibrillar collagen is built, which step each type disrupts, the genes, proteins and inheritance of the 13 types in the 2017 classification (plus classical-like EDS type 2, AEBP1), the COL3A1 variant classes in vascular EDS and their effect on outcome, genetic testing and variants of uncertain significance, the hypermobile-EDS gene search, and the genetic differential (Marfan, Loeys-Dietz, osteogenesis imperfecta, cutis laxa, Stickler).

## Findings

### collagen · What collagen is
- Collagen makes up about one-third of total protein in humans and about three-quarters of the dry weight of skin. Twenty-eight collagen types, built from at least 46 distinct polypeptide chains, are known in vertebrates [[Shoulders2009]].
- The collagen family has 28 members, each with at least one triple-helical domain. Most are deposited in the extracellular matrix, where they form supramolecular assemblies [[RicardBlum2011]].
- Type I collagen is a heterotrimer of two α1(I) chains and one α2(I) chain, with short non-helical telopeptides at both ends [[Yamauchi2012]].
- Composition and main sites of the EDS-relevant fibrillar collagens [[Shoulders2009]]:
  - type I, α1(I)2α2(I): dermis, bone, tendon, ligament
  - type III, α1(III)3: skin, blood vessels, intestine
  - type V, several chain combinations: bone, dermis, cornea, placenta
- In the triple helix, three left-handed polyproline II-type chains coil into a right-handed triple helix with a one-residue stagger. The packing is so tight that every third residue must be glycine, giving the repeat Xaa-Yaa-Gly [[Shoulders2009]].
- Proline fills the Xaa position about 28% of the time and 4-hydroxyproline fills the Yaa position about 38% of the time. Pro-Hyp-Gly is the most common triplet, at 10.5% [[Shoulders2009]].
- The accepted model has one interchain hydrogen bond per triplet, N–H(Gly)···O=C(Xaa), and a 10/3 helical pitch with a 28.6 Å axial repeat [[Shoulders2009]].

### collagen · Building the molecule, step by step (inside the cell)
- Step 1, hydroxylation of proline. Prolyl 4-hydroxylase, a non-heme iron enzyme, converts proline in the Yaa position to 4-hydroxyproline. Its activity is needed for viability in model organisms [[Shoulders2009]].
- The collagen prolyl 4-hydroxylases sit in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) as α2β2 tetramers, with protein disulfide isomerase acting as the β subunit [[Myllyharju2003]].
- Step 2, hydroxylation of lysine. Specific lysines are hydroxylated to hydroxylysine. Selected helical hydroxylysines are then glycosylated with galactose or glucose-galactose [[Yamauchi2012]].
- Hydroxylysines have two jobs: they carry the sugars, and they are precursors of the cross-links that give collagen its tensile strength [[Yeowell2000]].
- Step 3, chain selection and folding. The C-terminal propeptides direct the three chains to associate during intracellular assembly [[Hulmes2002]].
- In vivo the triple helix folds from the C-terminus toward the N-terminus. A glycine substitution stalls folding at that point, and any delay in folding leads to overmodification of the chains [[Shoulders2009]].
- The ER peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerase FKBP14 catalyses folding of type III collagen and interacts with types III, VI and X collagen [[Giunta2018]].

### collagen · Processing and fibril assembly (outside the cell)
- Step 4, propeptide removal. Fibril formation starts only after procollagen N- and C-proteinases cut off the propeptides at both ends. The C-propeptides are needed for triple-helix formation but block fibril formation [[Shoulders2009]].
- In the matrix, the C-propeptides keep procollagen soluble. If the N-propeptides persist, they change fibril shape [[Hulmes2002]].
- The C-propeptides of procollagens I, II and III are removed by procollagen C-proteinase, which is the same protein as bone morphogenetic protein-1 (BMP-1) [[Kessler1996]].
- The N-propeptides of type I and II procollagen are removed by procollagen I N-proteinase, encoded by ADAMTS2 [[Colige1999]]. ADAMTS-2 also cleaves the N-propeptide of type III procollagen [[VanDamme2016]].
- Step 5, self-assembly. A single type I triple helix is under 2 nm wide and about 300 nm long. Tendon fibrils reach up to 1 cm in length and about 500 nm in diameter [[Shoulders2009]].
- Fibrils show a repeating band, the D-period, of 67 nm. Each molecule is 4.46 D long, not an exact multiple of D, so the stagger leaves "gap" zones of 0.54 D and "overlap" zones of 0.46 D. This is the Hodge-Petruska quarter-stagger model, with five molecules offset by D [[Shoulders2009]].
- X-ray data on fibrils in tissue show the molecules packed quasi-hexagonally into supertwisted, right-handed microfibrils that interlock with their neighbours [[Orgel2006]].
- Purified collagen can assemble into fibrils on its own. In developing tissue, cellular machinery also controls where fibrils start, which way they point, and how thick they grow [[Holmes2018Fibril]].
- Step 6, cross-linking. Lysyl oxidase (LOX) oxidises specific lysines to an aldehyde (α-aminoadipic-δ-semialdehyde), which allows covalent cross-links in collagen and elastin. LOX leaves the cell as proLOX and is activated by proteolysis [[Lucero2006]].
- Telopeptide cross-links form after fibrils assemble. They give the fibril strength and stability but are not needed to build it [[Shoulders2009]].

### collagen · Regulators of fibril number and diameter
- Type V collagen is a minor partner that co-assembles with type I in mixed fibrils [[Wenstrup2004]]:
  - mouse embryos with no α1(V) chains die at about embryonic day 10 and make virtually no collagen fibrils
  - heterozygous (col5a1+/−) mice survive but have 50% fewer fibrils and 50% less dermal collagen, with a second population of large, abnormal fibrils
- In col5a1+/− mice (a model of classical EDS), half-normal collagen V gave 50% fewer dermal fibrils, lower skin tensile strength and lower aortic stiffness. Fibrils lacking collagen V formed without control and disrupted normal fibril growth [[Wenstrup2006]].
- Tenascin-X knockout mice develop progressive skin hyperextensibility and lower skin tensile strength. Their fibrils are of normal size and shape but less dense, and their fibroblasts make collagen I yet fail to deposit it into the matrix. Tenascin-X therefore regulates collagen deposition [[Mao2002]].
- Decorin carries a dermatan sulfate chain that helps organise collagen. In CHST14 deficiency this chain is lost and replaced by chondroitin sulfate. Fibril size and shape stay normal, but collagen bundles form poorly [[Miyake2010]].
- AEBP1 encodes ACLP, which binds several fibrillar collagens through its discoidin domain and enhances collagen polymerisation. Skin from people with AEBP1 variants shows less dermal collagen and ragged fibrils [[Blackburn2018]].

### collagen · Which step each type disrupts
- The 2017 classification also proposes a "pathogenetic scheme" that groups EDS types whose proteins work in the same pathway [[Malfait2017]].
- Most of the known EDS genes encode one of three things [[Malfait2020]]:
  - the fibrillar collagens I, III or V
  - enzymes that modify or process those collagens
  - enzymes that modify glycosaminoglycan chains of proteoglycans
- Mapping of each type to the pathway step (genes and mechanisms from the papers cited):
  - **Making the chains:**
    - COL5A1/COL5A2: classical EDS, mainly too little type V collagen [[Symoens2012]]
    - COL3A1: vascular EDS [[ByersGR-vEDS]]
    - COL1A2: cardiac-valvular EDS, complete absence of proα2(I) chains [[Schwarze2004]]
    - COL12A1: myopathic EDS [[Zou2014COL12A1]]
  - **Lysine hydroxylation and folding:**
    - PLOD1 (lysyl hydroxylase 1): kyphoscoliotic EDS [[Yeowell2000]]
    - FKBP14 (ER folding catalyst): kyphoscoliotic EDS [[Baumann2012]]
    - SLC39A13 (ER zinc transporter): spondylodysplastic EDS, with underhydroxylated lysine and proline [[Giunta2008]]
  - **N-propeptide removal:**
    - ADAMTS2 (the enzyme): dermatosparaxis EDS [[Colige1999]]
    - COL1A1/COL1A2 exon 6 (the cleavage site in the substrate): arthrochalasia EDS [[Byers1997]]
  - **Fibril assembly and deposition:**
    - TNXB (tenascin-X): classical-like EDS [[Schalkwijk2001,Mao2002]]
    - AEBP1 (ACLP): classical-like EDS type 2 [[Blackburn2018]]
  - **Proteoglycan glycosaminoglycan chains:**
    - B4GALT7 and B3GALT6 (linker region): spondylodysplastic EDS [[FaiyazUlHaque2004,Malfait2013B3GALT6]]
    - CHST14 and DSE (dermatan sulfate): musculocontractural EDS [[Dundar2009,Muller2013]]
  - **Transcriptional control of matrix:**
    - ZNF469 and PRDM5: brittle cornea syndrome [[BurkittWright2011]]
  - **Complement:**
    - C1R and C1S: periodontal EDS, the one type outside the collagen and proteoglycan pathways [[KapfererSeebacher2016,vanDijk2024]]

### types · The classification over time
- The 1997 Villefranche nosology set major and minor criteria for each type, aiming to classify mainly by cause [[Beighton1998]]. It recognised six types: classical, hypermobile, vascular, kyphoscoliotic, arthrochalasis and dermatosparaxis [[Brady2017]].
- The 2017 International EDS Classification recognises 13 subtypes and gives a set of clinical criteria suggestive of each [[Malfait2017]].
- For every subtype except hypermobile EDS, a definite diagnosis needs molecular confirmation, that is, identification of the causative genetic variant(s) [[Malfait2017]].
- At publication the 13 subtypes were linked to pathogenic variants in 19 genes [[Ritelli2019]]. By 2020 the primer listed 14 recognised types, with a known molecular cause for 13, involving 20 genes [[Malfait2020]].
- The extra type in 2020 is classical-like EDS type 2, caused by recessive AEBP1 variants. Since then the TNXB form has also been called classical-like EDS type 1 [[Ritelli2019,vanDijk2024]].
- The 2017 work also revised the hypermobile-EDS criteria so that hEDS could be told apart better from other joint hypermobility disorders [[Malfait2017]]. People with symptomatic joint hypermobility who do not fit any syndrome get a diagnosis of hypermobility spectrum disorder (HSD) [[Castori2017]].
- Hypermobile EDS is described as likely the most common hereditary disorder of connective tissue [[Tinkle2017]]. No genetic, epigenetic or metabolomic cause has been found, and diagnosis rests on the 2017 clinical criteria [[HakimGR-hEDS]].
- No specific medical or genetic therapy exists for any type of EDS [[Malfait2020]].

### types · Classical EDS (cEDS)
- cEDS is autosomal dominant and mostly caused by COL5A1 or COL5A2, rarely by specific COL1A1 variants. Its reported prevalence is about 1 in 20,000 [[vanDijk2024]].
- The 2017 major criteria are generalised joint hypermobility and skin hyperextensibility with atrophic scarring [[vanDijk2024]].
- About 50% of people with cEDS have an affected parent and about 50% have a new (de novo) variant. Each child of an affected person has a 50% chance of inheriting it [[MalfaitGR-cEDS]].
- In 126 people with diagnosed or suspected cEDS [[Symoens2012]]:
  - 93 had a type V collagen defect: 73 COL5A1 variants, 13 COL5A2 variants, and 7 COL5A1 null alleles whose variant was not found
  - most COL5A1 variants created a null allele, about one-third were structural, and all COL5A2 variants were structural
  - all defects were in the 102 people who met all three major Villefranche criteria (skin hyperextensibility, dystrophic scarring, joint hypermobility)
  - none of the 24 people without dystrophic scarring had one
  - over 90% of people meeting all major criteria had a type V collagen defect
- Before 2017, genetic heterogeneity in cEDS was an open question. Evidence now points to type V collagen abnormalities as the cause, and the causative variant can be found in most patients [[Bowen2017]].
- An earlier study found loss of expression of one COL5A1 allele in 8 of 28 informative probands and estimated that about one-third of people with cEDS have COL5A1 haploinsufficiency [[Wenstrup2000]].
- Heterozygous COL5A2 variants that cause exon skipping can also cause cEDS. Dermis in these patients showed a sparse fibril network with very variable fibril size and shape [[Michalickova1998]].
- In a UK cohort of 154 people with a clinical cEDS diagnosis, 7 (4.5%) had arterial complications, mostly in medium-sized vessels [[Angwin2020]].
- The COL1A1 arginine-to-cysteine substitution p.Arg312Cys has been linked to classical EDS. Three α1(I) arginine-to-cysteine substitutions were found in people with iliac or femoral artery dissection in early adulthood [[Malfait2007RtoC]].

### types · Vascular EDS (vEDS)
- vEDS is autosomal dominant and caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants in COL3A1, which encodes type III procollagen. Type III is a major protein of vessel walls and hollow organs. Estimated frequency is 1 in 50,000 to 1 in 200,000 [[Byers2017]].
- The 2017 major criteria are [[vanDijk2024]]:
  - arterial aneurysm and/or dissection
  - sigmoid colon perforation without diverticular or other colon disease
  - uterine rupture in the third trimester without a previous caesarean or severe perineal tear
  - carotid-cavernous sinus fistula without trauma
- Some rare vEDS is caused by specific arginine-to-cysteine variants in COL1A1 [[vanDijk2024,Malfait2007RtoC]].
- About 50% of people with vEDS have an affected parent. The other 50% have a de novo variant, either their own or from an unaffected mosaic parent [[ByersGR-vEDS]].
- About 60% of people diagnosed before age 18 are found through family history [[ByersGR-vEDS]].
- The consensus statement recommends confirming a causative COL3A1 variant before applying the diagnosis [[Byers2017]].
- Pepin 2000 studied 220 index patients with biochemically confirmed vEDS and 199 affected relatives [[Pepin2000]]:
  - 25% of index patients had a first complication by age 20, and over 80% by age 40
  - calculated median survival was 48 years, with most deaths from arterial rupture
  - 12 of the 81 women who became pregnant died of pregnancy complications
- Pepin 2014 studied 1,231 individuals: 630 index cases and 601 relatives [[Pepin2014]]:
  - missense and splice-site variants made up over 90% of the 572 COL3A1 alterations
  - median survival was 51 years, lower in men, and varied with variant type
- Frank 2015 sorted 126 variants in 215 molecularly proven patients into five groups [[Frank2015]]:
  - glycine substitutions: 71 variants
  - splice-site and in-frame insertion-deletions: 36
  - haploinsufficiency: 7
  - non-glycine missense inside the triple helix: 4
  - non-glycine changes in the N- or C-terminal regions: 8
- In that cohort, median age at first complication was 29 years (IQR 22–39). The most frequent complications were arterial (48%) and digestive (24%) ruptures [[Frank2015]].
- Groups 1 and 2 (glycine substitutions; splice/in-frame variants) were more severe than groups 3–5. Median ages at first major complication ranged from 23 to 47 years across groups. Groups 3–5 had a less typical appearance and no digestive events [[Frank2015]].
- Amino acids replacing glycine were strongly biased toward the residues that most destabilise collagen assembly [[Frank2015]].
- COL3A1 null alleles cause a vEDS-like phenotype through reduced protein, not through faulty molecules built into fibrils. In the first four probands the presenting event was aneurysm or arterial rupture [[Schwarze2001]].
- Leistritz 2011 reviewed 54 individuals from 19 families with COL3A1 null variants [[Leistritz2011]]:
  - compared with missense or exon-skipping variants, the first complication came almost 15 years later and life span was longer
  - only 28% of relatives had a complication, and only 30% had minor clinical features
  - complications were almost all vascular
- Demirdas 2024 studied 142 Dutch individuals (70 families) [[Demirdas2024]]:
  - median age at first major event was 41.0 years
  - by variant class, the median age at first major event was 40.5 years (IQR 31.0–53.25) for glycine substitutions, 30.0 years (IQR 21.0–45.0) for splice/delins/deletion variants, and 53.0 years (IQR 43.0–58.0) for predicted haploinsufficient variants
  - men had more major events than women (48 vs 26; OR 3.61, 95% CI 1.82–7.14)
  - median age at death was 43.0 years for index patients and 65.0 years for relatives
- In the same cohort, 43 of 56 variants (76.8%) were predicted to act through a dominant-negative effect. Biallelic COL3A1 variants are found in under 1% of people with vEDS [[Demirdas2024]].
- Glutamic acid-to-lysine substitutions in COL3A1, previously classed as variants of uncertain significance (VUS), were reclassified as pathogenic or likely pathogenic in seven families. Carriers had both cEDS-like skin hyperextensibility and vEDS-like tissue fragility [[Ghali2019GenetMed]].
- Pregnancy-related deaths occurred in 30 of 565 deliveries (5.3%) in vEDS family pedigrees. Life-threatening complications occurred in 14.5% of deliveries in interviewed women [[Murray2014]].

### types · Kyphoscoliotic EDS (kEDS): PLOD1 and FKBP14
- kEDS is autosomal recessive, caused by biallelic PLOD1 or FKBP14 variants, with reported prevalence of about 1 in 100,000 [[vanDijk2024]].
- The 2017 major criteria are congenital muscle hypotonia, congenital or early-onset kyphoscoliosis, and generalised joint hypermobility with dislocations or subluxations [[vanDijk2024]].
- PLOD1-kEDS (the former EDS VI) is caused by deficiency of lysyl hydroxylase 1. At least 20 PLOD1 variants were known by 2000. A large exon 10–16 duplication from Alu recombination and the nonsense variant Y511X recur in five or more unrelated patients [[Yeowell2000]].
- If genetic testing finds only one variant or a VUS, a markedly raised ratio of urinary deoxypyridinoline to pyridinoline cross-links (measured by HPLC) can confirm PLOD1-kEDS [[RohrbachGR-kEDS]].
- Urinary cross-link ratios (lysyl/hydroxylysyl pyridinoline) are about 6 in EDS VI, about 1 in SLC39A13 spondylodysplastic EDS, and about 0.2 in controls [[Giunta2008]].
- PLOD1-kEDS carries a risk of life-threatening rupture and dissection of medium-sized arteries. Intelligence is normal [[RohrbachGR-kEDS]].
- FKBP14-kEDS looks almost the same as PLOD1-kEDS, but urinary pyridinoline excretion is normal and sensorineural hearing loss is common. FKBP14 is an ER folding catalyst, and deficient fibroblasts have enlarged ER cisterns [[Baumann2012]].
- Most children with FKBP14-kEDS walk independently between ages 2 and 4 years [[GiuntaGR-FKBP14]]. A 17-patient cohort (23 patients with earlier reports) found vascular complications rare [[Giunta2018]].

### types · Arthrochalasia EDS (aEDS)
- aEDS is autosomal dominant. It is caused by COL1A1 or COL1A2 variants that delete all or part of exon 6, and its reported prevalence is under 1 in 1,000,000 [[vanDijk2024]].
- Exon 6 holds the cleavage site for the procollagen N-proteinase. aEDS and dermatosparaxis therefore block the same step, one at the substrate and one at the enzyme [[Byers1997]].
- COL1A1 variants produced the most severe effects. COL1A2 variants produced congenital hip dislocation and joint instability [[Byers1997]].
- The hallmark is congenital bilateral hip dislocation with severe generalised joint hypermobility and recurrent subluxations [[vanDijk2024,Ayoub2020]].
- The exon 6 variants cause the exon to be skipped, which leads to defective collagen synthesis. Hypotonia is marked in infants and lessens with age, and facial features become less distinct [[Klaassens2012]].
- About 42 people with aEDS had been published by 2020. In a UK series of 12 [[Ayoub2020]]:
  - 9 had fractures
  - 5 had hip surgery and 3 had spinal surgery
  - 4 used a wheelchair or could not walk unaided

### types · Dermatosparaxis EDS (dEDS)
- dEDS is autosomal recessive and caused by biallelic ADAMTS2 variants that abolish procollagen N-proteinase activity. Five of the first six known patients were homozygous for the same nonsense variant, Q225X [[Colige1999]].
- By 2016 only 10 patients had been reported. Five new patients, three of them milder, led to an updated set of criteria [[VanDamme2016]].
- The criteria comprise 9 major criteria. Prevalence is under 1 in 1,000,000 [[vanDijk2024]].
- Fifteen people had been reported by 2025, the oldest followed to age 19. A series of 5 adults aged 22–42 described extreme skin fragility, redundant skin folds and fractures [[Angwin2025]].

### types · Brittle cornea syndrome (BCS)
- BCS is autosomal recessive and caused by biallelic ZNF469 or PRDM5 variants, which probably encode transcription factors for matrix components [[Dhooge2021]].
- A literature review counted 85 reported patients: 53 with ZNF469 variants and 32 with PRDM5 variants [[Dhooge2021]].
- Major features are a thin cornea with or without rupture, early progressive keratoconus or keratoglobus, and blue sclerae [[vanDijk2024]].
- PRDM5 regulates expression of matrix components, especially fibrillar collagens. ZNF469 and PRDM5 act in the same regulatory pathway [[BurkittWright2011]].
- The first ZNF469 variants were a 1-bp deletion in five unrelated patients of Tunisian Jewish ancestry and a different deletion in a Palestinian family [[Abu2008]].

### types · Spondylodysplastic EDS (spEDS)
- spEDS is autosomal recessive and caused by biallelic B4GALT7, B3GALT6 or SLC39A13 variants. Prevalence is under 1 in 1,000,000 [[vanDijk2024]].
- The major criteria are short stature, muscle hypotonia and bowing of the limbs [[vanDijk2024]].
- B3GALT6 encodes galactosyltransferase II, which adds the third sugar of the tetrasaccharide linker that attaches glycosaminoglycan chains to proteoglycan core proteins [[Malfait2013B3GALT6]].
- B3GALT6 loss of function causes a spectrum from a skeletal dysplasia (SEMD-JL1) to a progeroid form of EDS [[Nakajima2013]].
- All 12 patients in a B3GALT6 series had features of both EDS and spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia. Some had aortic dilatation or aneurysm, cervical spine instability or respiratory insufficiency [[VanDamme2018]].
- A homozygous B4GALT7 missense variant (Arg270Cys) caused a progeroid-like EDS in a consanguineous family from Qatar [[FaiyazUlHaque2004]].

### types · Musculocontractural EDS (mcEDS)
- mcEDS is autosomal recessive and caused by biallelic CHST14 or DSE variants [[KoshoGR-mcEDS]].
- Loss of CHST14 (dermatan-4-sulfotransferase 1) abolished dermatan sulfate in patient fibroblasts and left an excess of chondroitin sulfate [[Dundar2009]].
- A DSE (dermatan sulfate epimerase) variant was found in a child with mcEDS. This showed that more than one gene can cause the type [[Muller2013]].
- In 66 patients from 48 families with mcEDS-CHST14 [[Minatogawa2022]]:
  - the characteristic craniofacial, skeletal, skin and eye features were present in over 90%
  - median age at the first dislocation, and at the first large subcutaneous haematoma, was 6 years
  - nine patients had died, at a median age of 12 years

### types · Myopathic EDS (mEDS)
- mEDS is caused by COL12A1 variants. Both a recessive loss-of-function variant and a de novo dominant variant were found in the first families [[Zou2014COL12A1]].
- Twenty-five individuals from 16 families had been reported by 2024, with recessive inheritance in two families. Congenital hypotonia improves with age [[vanDijk2024]].

### types · Periodontal EDS (pEDS)
- pEDS is autosomal dominant and caused by heterozygous gain-of-function variants in C1R or C1S, which encode subunits of the first complement component [[KapfererSeebacherGR-pEDS]].
- The genetic study enrolled 19 families (107 individuals) [[KapfererSeebacher2016]]:
  - variants were found in 17 families: C1R in 15 and C1S in 2
  - the variants caused the proteins to be retained inside the cell, with mild ER enlargement
- Since the 1970s, 148 people with pEDS have been reported [[KapfererSeebacherGR-pEDS]].
- A generalised lack of attached gingiva is pathognomonic. In children of affected parents, it was present in all 12 who carried the familial variant and in none of the 7 who did not. Only 2 of the 12 affected children met the full clinical criteria [[KapfererSeebacher2021]].
- Periodontitis starts young, and on average all teeth are lost by about age 20 [[vanDijk2024]].

### types · Cardiac-valvular EDS (cvEDS)
- cvEDS is autosomal recessive. Biallelic COL1A2 variants trigger nonsense-mediated mRNA decay, so no proα2(I) chains are made. Surprisingly, this causes heart valve disease without bone involvement [[Schwarze2004]].
- Five molecularly confirmed patients had been reported when two sisters were added in 2019. The disease first affects the mitral valve [[Guarnieri2019]].

### types · Classical-like EDS type 1 (TNXB)
- Tenascin-X was absent from the serum of 5 of 151 people with EDS and present in all controls. All five had hypermobile joints, hyperelastic skin and easy bruising without atrophic scars, and all had TNXB variants consistent with recessive inheritance [[Schalkwijk2001]].
- In a cross-sectional study of 17 patients from 11 families, all had hyperextensible skin without atrophic scarring, and 16 of 17 had joint hypermobility. Molecular analysis of TNXB is technically challenging [[Demirdas2017]].
- In a UK series of 20 new patients, 3 had gastrointestinal complications: small or large bowel rupture, or oesophageal rupture [[Green2020clEDS]].
- In the literature summarised by van Dijk et al. [[vanDijk2024]]:
  - foot abnormalities: 81%
  - gastrointestinal rupture: 16%
  - major vascular events: 5%
- Reported tissue fragility also includes rupture of the trachea, oesophagus and bowel. Other features are leg oedema, mild muscle weakness and axonal polyneuropathy [[vanDijkGR-clEDS]].

### types · Classical-like EDS type 2 (AEBP1), added after 2017
- AEBP1-related EDS was described in 2018 in four individuals from three families with biallelic variants. Features were joint laxity, hyperextensible skin, poor wound healing and osteoporosis [[Blackburn2018]].
- Across 11 reported individuals [[Angwin2023]]:
  - hypermobility: 11/11
  - skin hyperextensibility: 11/11
  - easy bruising: 10/11
  - atrophic scarring: 9/11
  - hair loss: 6/11
  - arterial aneurysm and/or dissection: 2/11
- A review of 15 individuals cited by van Dijk et al. reported arterial aneurysm or dissection in 3 of 15, aortic root dilatation in 2 of 15 and bowel rupture in 2 of 15. No specific diagnostic criteria have been formulated yet [[vanDijk2024]].

### types · COL1-related overlap disorder (not one of the 13)
- Morlino et al. described 21 individuals from 13 families with COL1A1/COL1A2 variants. Their EDS-dominant phenotype fit neither osteogenesis imperfecta (OI) nor any of the three COL1-related EDS types. They proposed it as a missing EDS type [[Morlino2020]].
- The proposed criteria advise testing COL1A1 and COL1A2 when a person has 3 or more major criteria, or 1 major and 4 or more minor criteria [[vanDijk2024]]. The four major criteria are:
  - blue sclerae
  - flatfoot with valgus deformity of the hindfoot
  - generalised joint hypermobility for age
  - soft, doughy and/or hyperextensible skin
- In a cohort of 34 people with COL1A1/COL1A2 variants [[Venable2023]]:
  - 15 had possible OI-EDS overlap
  - 4 had first been diagnosed with hypermobile EDS
  - 4 of the 15 had vascular or arterial fragility

### skin · Skin findings by type
- cEDS skin is soft, velvety or doughy and hyperextensible: it stretches easily and snaps back. It splits after minor trauma over pressure points and shins, leaving atrophic or haemosiderotic scars [[MalfaitGR-cEDS]].
- Clinical photographs show skin hyperextensibility measured as ≥3.0 cm at the elbow or neck, or ≥1.5 cm on the back of the hand [[vanDijk2024]].
- vEDS skin is thin and translucent with visible veins and easy bruising. Many people have a characteristic face (thin lips, small chin, narrow nose, prominent eyes) and aged-looking hands [[ByersGR-vEDS]].
- TNXB-clEDS has hyperextensible skin without atrophic scars, and piezogenic papules are seen on the feet. pEDS shows symmetrical pretibial plaques. dEDS shows extreme skin fragility with redundant, almost lax skin [[vanDijk2024]].
- In cEDS, deep dermal wounds are closed in two layers without tension, and skin stitches are left in about twice as long as usual [[MalfaitGR-cEDS]].
- In the col5a1+/− mouse model of cEDS, both normal and wounded skin had lower tensile strength [[Wenstrup2006]].

### genes · Inheritance in a family
- Autosomal dominant types (cEDS, vEDS, aEDS, pEDS): each child of an affected parent has a 50% chance of inheriting the variant [[MalfaitGR-cEDS,ByersGR-vEDS,KapfererSeebacherGR-pEDS]].
- Autosomal recessive types (kEDS, clEDS, mcEDS and others): when both parents are carriers, each sibling has a 25% chance of being affected, 50% of being a carrier and 25% of being neither [[RohrbachGR-kEDS,vanDijkGR-clEDS,KoshoGR-mcEDS]].
- For hEDS, the GeneReviews chapter describes autosomal dominant inheritance with variable expression. Because no gene is known, prenatal and preimplantation genetic testing are not possible [[HakimGR-hEDS]].
- Once a family's variant is known in cEDS or vEDS, prenatal and preimplantation testing are possible [[MalfaitGR-cEDS,ByersGR-vEDS]].

### genes · Genetic testing, yield and VUS
- Gene-panel testing by massively parallel sequencing is the current gold standard for confirming the monogenic EDS types. Careful clinical assessment remains important for deciding what to test and for interpreting the result [[vanDijk2024]].
- A 12-gene panel (collagen and aortopathy genes) was used in 177 unrelated people with EDS [[Weerakkody2016]]:
  - 28 pathogenic and 4 likely pathogenic variants were found
  - 7 were new diagnoses that changed the clinical diagnosis, including variants in aortopathy genes (TGFBR1/2, SMAD3) outside the expected EDS genes
  - 22 VUS were found, 7 of them in aortopathy genes needing clinical follow-up
- At one US connective-tissue clinic, 907 people were referred for suspected hEDS [[Forghani2025]]:
  - 178 (19.6%) met the 2017 hEDS criteria
  - 47 of those 178 (26.4%) had an alternative or additional diagnosis found by genetic testing
  - the authors note that referral and selection bias may inflate this figure
- Variant interpretation changes over time. COL3A1 Glu>Lys variants moved from VUS to pathogenic [[Ghali2019GenetMed]], and a COL1A1 arginine-to-cysteine variant had been wrongly classed as a VUS [[Venable2023]].
- Exome sequencing found de novo diagnostic variants in COL5A1, COL3A1 and COL1A1 in three people with different EDS types, supporting broad sequencing when phenotypes overlap [[Yang2022]].
- Brittle cornea genes (ZNF469, PRDM5) should be included in connective-tissue gene panels. People can present with musculoskeletal findings before any corneal rupture [[Dhooge2021]].

### genes · The search for a hypermobile-EDS gene
- The molecular basis of hEDS is unknown [[Malfait2020,Gensemer2021]].
- The HEDGE study aims to whole-genome sequence 1,000 adults who meet the 2017 hEDS criteria. Van Dijk et al. expect most people with hEDS not to have a single-gene cause and suggest hEDS may be multifactorial (Hypothesis) [[vanDijk2024]].
- Tenascin-X deficiency was found in 5 of 151 people with classic, hypermobility or vascular EDS. These people form the recessive TNXB type, not typical hEDS [[Schalkwijk2001]].
- KLK15 (Early evidence). Whole-exome sequencing of 200 people with hEDS found rare variants in 14 of the 15 kallikrein genes [[Gensemer2025KLK15]]:
  - a recurrent KLK15 missense variant (p.Gly226Asp, minor allele frequency 0.002) segregated with hEDS in two families
  - KLK15 variants were enriched compared with gnomAD controls (p = 5.5 × 10⁻⁵)
  - knock-in mice had tendon and heart-valve changes
  - this is one group's finding, not yet independently replicated, and it is not a clinical test
- The same study found no enrichment of the common MTHFR variants C677T (p = 0.9864) or A1298C (p = 0.3156) in hEDS. The authors judged these variants unlikely to cause hEDS [[Gensemer2025KLK15]].
- Plasma fragments of fibronectin and type I collagen showed a shared pattern in hEDS and HSD. This is proposed as a possible blood biomarker and as evidence that the two are one disorder (Early evidence) [[Ritelli2025]].
- An Italian reference centre re-assessed 327 patients from 213 families with prior hEDS or joint hypermobility syndrome diagnoses. They argued the 2017 criteria are too stringent and should be relaxed [[Ritelli2024]].

### differential · Genetic look-alikes
- Marfan syndrome is caused by FBN1, which encodes fibrillin, a component of extracellular microfibrils [[Dietz1991]].
- In the revised Ghent nosology, aortic root aneurysm plus ectopia lentis are enough to diagnose Marfan syndrome when there is no family history. With improved molecular techniques, the diagnosis can be confirmed in over 95% of patients [[Loeys2010]].
- In Marfan syndrome, ectopia lentis occurs in about 60% and myopia in over 50%. About 75% of affected people have an affected parent [[DietzGR-Marfan]].
- Loeys-Dietz syndrome (LDS) was first linked to heterozygous TGFBR1 or TGFBR2 variants, with increased TGF-β signalling in tissue [[Loeys2005]]. Later genes include SMAD2, SMAD3, TGFB2 and TGFB3 (dominant) and IPO8 (recessive). About 75% of probands have a de novo variant [[LoeysGR-LDS]].
- In the 2006 LDS cohort (52 families) [[Loeys2006]]:
  - the classic triad was arterial tortuosity and aneurysms, hypertelorism, and bifid uvula or cleft palate
  - 12 of 40 people with a vEDS-like presentation but no type III collagen defect had TGFBR1/2 variants
  - mean age at death was 26.0 years
  - only one of 59 vascular operations ended in death during the procedure, which sets LDS apart from vEDS
- TGFB2 loss-of-function variants and TGFB3 variants also cause syndromic aortic aneurysm in the LDS spectrum. The TGFB3 report described 43 patients from 11 families [[Lindsay2012TGFB2,BertoliAvella2015]].
- SMAD3 aneurysms-osteoarthritis syndrome combines aortic and arterial aneurysms with early-onset osteoarthritis. Almost 90% of patients had cardiovascular abnormalities [[vanDeLaar2012]].
- In 1,780 people with pathogenic variants in aortopathy genes, arterial events beyond the aorta were most common with COL3A1 and rarest with FBN1 [[CalderonMartinez2025]]:
  - COL3A1: 20.8%
  - TGFBR2: 7.7%
  - TGFBR1: 7.3%
  - TGFB2: 6.4%
  - SMAD3: 5.6%
  - FBN1: 1.5%
- Osteogenesis imperfecta is caused by dominant COL1A1 or COL1A2 variants in about 85% of people. Its hallmark is bone fragility, unlike EDS [[Marini2017]].
- Cutis laxa syndromes share loose, redundant skin caused by fragmented dermal elastic fibres. There are acquired and inherited forms [[Beyens2021]]. In cEDS, by contrast, the skin stretches and snaps back [[MalfaitGR-cEDS]].
- Stickler syndrome, a type II/XI collagenopathy, is caused by dominant COL2A1, COL11A1 or COL11A2 variants or by recessive COL9A1/2/3 variants. It causes myopia, retinal detachment, hearing loss, cleft palate and early joint disease [[MortierGR-Stickler]]. It is the most common cause of inherited rhegmatogenous retinal detachment [[Snead2011]].
- Other conditions in the differential [[vanDijk2024]]:
  - for easy bruising: clotting disorders and non-accidental injury
  - for hypotonia with hypermobility: Bethlem and Ullrich myopathies

### Exam hooks
- In collagen, every third residue is glycine (Xaa-Yaa-Gly), because only glycine fits in the crowded centre of the triple helix [[Shoulders2009]].
- Collagen fibrils have a 67 nm D-period. Each molecule is 4.46 D long, giving gap zones of 0.54 D and overlap zones of 0.46 D [[Shoulders2009]].
- The triple helix folds from the C-terminus to the N-terminus [[Shoulders2009]].
- BMP-1 is the procollagen C-proteinase [[Kessler1996]]. ADAMTS-2 is the procollagen I N-proteinase, and its loss causes dermatosparaxis EDS [[Colige1999]].
- Arthrochalasia EDS removes the substrate's N-proteinase cleavage site (exon 6 of COL1A1/COL1A2). Dermatosparaxis removes the enzyme [[Byers1997,Colige1999]].
- Classical EDS is mostly caused by COL5A1/COL5A2 (type V collagen). Over 90% of people meeting all major criteria have a type V defect [[Symoens2012]].
- Vascular EDS is caused by COL3A1. The most common variant type is a glycine substitution, and it is autosomal dominant [[Frank2015,Demirdas2024]].
- In vEDS, null (haploinsufficient) COL3A1 variants delay the first complication by almost 15 years compared with missense or exon-skipping variants [[Leistritz2011]].
- PLOD1-kEDS shows a raised urinary deoxypyridinoline:pyridinoline ratio. FKBP14-kEDS shows normal urinary pyridinolines and often hearing loss [[RohrbachGR-kEDS,Baumann2012]].
- Classical-like EDS (TNXB) is recessive, with hyperextensible skin but no atrophic scars [[Schalkwijk2001,Demirdas2017]].
- Periodontal EDS involves complement genes (C1R/C1S). Lack of attached gingiva is pathognomonic [[KapfererSeebacher2016,KapfererSeebacher2021]].
- Cardiac-valvular EDS is caused by biallelic COL1A2 null variants, giving valve disease without bone fragility [[Schwarze2004]].
- Hypermobile EDS is the only one of the 13 types without a known gene. Its diagnosis is clinical [[Malfait2017,HakimGR-hEDS]].
- Musculocontractural EDS (CHST14, DSE) is a dermatan sulfate biosynthesis defect [[Dundar2009,Muller2013]].
- Loeys-Dietz syndrome (TGFBR1/2, SMAD3, TGFB2/3) can mimic vEDS. Unlike vEDS, it tolerates vascular surgery well [[Loeys2006]].
- Marfan's cardinal features are aortic root aneurysm and ectopia lentis, and the gene is FBN1 [[Loeys2010,Dietz1991]].

### In plain words
- Collagen is the body's main "rope" protein. It holds skin, joints, blood vessels and organs together.
- Each collagen rope is three strands twisted together. A tiny building block, glycine, has to sit at every third spot or the twist does not close properly [[Shoulders2009]].
- Most types of EDS come from a change in one gene that makes collagen, or that helps the body finish, trim or bundle collagen [[Malfait2020]].
- There are 13 types of EDS. For 12 of them a genetic test can find the cause. For hypermobile EDS, no gene has been found yet, so doctors diagnose it from a careful exam [[Malfait2017,HakimGR-hEDS]].
- Vascular EDS is caused by changes in the COL3A1 gene. It mainly affects blood vessels and hollow organs, and the exact gene change can affect how early problems tend to start [[Frank2015,Demirdas2024]].
- These risk numbers describe large groups of people. They cannot say what will happen to one person [[Pepin2014]].
- If a parent has a "dominant" type, each child has a 1-in-2 chance of inheriting it. In a "recessive" type, both parents usually carry one changed copy, and each child has a 1-in-4 chance of being affected [[MalfaitGR-cEDS,RohrbachGR-kEDS]].
- A genetic test can come back with a "variant of uncertain significance." That means "we don't know yet," not "this is the cause." Some of these results are later reclassified [[Weerakkody2016,Ghali2019GenetMed]].
- Some people told they have hypermobile EDS turn out to have a different genetic condition, which is one reason a genetics review matters [[Forghani2025,Venable2023]].
- Online claims about one "hEDS gene," such as MTHFR, are not supported by current evidence [[Gensemer2025KLK15]].
- The Beighton score and the hEDS checklist are tools for a full specialist assessment. They are not a home test, and a high score alone does not mean you have EDS [[vanDijk2024,Castori2017]].
- Several other conditions, such as Marfan and Loeys-Dietz syndromes, can look like EDS. Genetic testing helps tell them apart [[Weerakkody2016,Loeys2006]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Is hEDS a single-gene disorder? One view expects most hEDS to have no monogenic cause and possibly a multifactorial basis (Hypothesis) [[vanDijk2024]]. KLK15 variants segregating in two families and enriched in a 200-person exome cohort suggest a contributing gene (Early evidence) [[Gensemer2025KLK15]]. Status: unresolved, with no clinical gene test for hEDS [[HakimGR-hEDS]].
- Are hEDS and HSD one condition? A shared plasma pattern of matrix fragments [[Ritelli2025]] and a 327-patient re-assessment [[Ritelli2024]] argue the 2017 boundary is too strict. The 2017 framework keeps them apart so that hEDS criteria stay specific [[Malfait2017,Castori2017]]. Status: debated.
- Should COL1-related overlap disorder be a new EDS type? It was proposed in 2020 [[Morlino2020]] and supported by a 34-person cohort [[Venable2023]], but it is not in the 2017 classification. Status: proposed.
- Should myopathic EDS count as EDS? Some argue its strong muscle component makes it a congenital myopathy [[vanDijk2024]]. Status: opinion.
- AEBP1-related classical-like EDS has no formal diagnostic criteria yet, and only about 11–15 people are described [[Angwin2023,vanDijk2024]].
- How should vEDS risk be counselled by variant class? Null alleles show reduced penetrance and later onset [[Leistritz2011]], and splice variants the earliest events [[Demirdas2024]]. The type of complication was not associated with specific COL3A1 variants in the 2000 cohort [[Pepin2000]]. Atypical classes such as Glu>Lys are still being characterised [[Ghali2019GenetMed]].
- How big is the "alternative diagnosis" problem in hEDS? A single-centre study found 26.4%, but the authors flag possible referral bias [[Forghani2025]]. Multicentre yields have not been reported.
- Are rare-type prevalence figures underestimates? Van Dijk et al. say historical prevalence for many rare types may be too low, as broader testing finds milder cases, and recessive types may be more common where consanguinity is common [[vanDijk2024]].

## Provenance
- Every source above was checked against its PubMed record through the E-utilities esummary endpoint on 2026-10-25: authors, title, journal, year, volume/issue/pages, PMID, PMCID and DOI in each line come from that record. Searches were run through Europe PMC (because PubMed's shared rate limit was being hit by parallel passes), and every PMID was then confirmed in PubMed.
- Abstracts of all cited journal articles were read through Europe PMC (resultType=core). Full texts were read for van Dijk 2024 (Europe PMC), Shoulders & Raines 2009 (PMC author manuscript via the NCBI BioC API), and Demirdas 2024, Forghani 2025 and Gensemer 2025 (PMC open access via BioC). Numbers from those five papers that are not in their abstracts (for example the Pro/Hyp percentages, the D-period geometry, the vEDS variant-class ages, the 907→178→47 funnel, the KLK15 MAF and burden p value, and the MTHFR p values) were read in the full text.
- GeneReviews chapters (keys ending GR-...) are cited for their PubMed summary block only. The NCBI Bookshelf pages returned a reCAPTCHA challenge, which I did not attempt to bypass. Each GeneReviews line gives the NBK accession and the last-revision date from the PubMed record.
- Not accessible: full text of Malfait 2017 (the Wiley PDF is behind a bot check, and the request was refused), Malfait 2020, Pepin 2014, Frank 2015, Symoens 2012 and Weerakkody 2016.
