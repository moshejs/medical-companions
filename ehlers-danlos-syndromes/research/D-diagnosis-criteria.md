# D · Diagnosis, criteria, differential and assessment tools

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: beighton, criteria, hsd, differential.

This pass covers how the Ehlers-Danlos syndromes (EDS) and hypermobility spectrum disorders (HSD) are diagnosed, as published. It includes the Beighton score and its reliability, the five-part questionnaire (5PQ), the 2017 hypermobile EDS (hEDS) criteria, the HSD framework and its subtypes, the paediatric framework, the diagnostic pathway for vascular EDS (vEDS) and the other genetic types, the differential diagnosis (Marfan syndrome, Loeys-Dietz syndrome, fibromyalgia, inflammatory arthritis) and the status of the announced 2026 criteria revision. The Beighton score and the hEDS checklist are teaching tools, not self-diagnosis tools.

## Findings

### beighton · The nine Beighton manoeuvres
- The Beighton score (BS) is a 9-point score made of 5 manoeuvres, 4 of them done on both sides, scoring 1 point per side. The fifth, trunk flexion, scores 1 point [[Thwaites2022,Carroll2023]].
- Item 1 (2 points): passive dorsiflexion of the fifth finger (little finger metacarpophalangeal joint) to 90° or more. Malek describes it as "beyond 90°", and the figure redrawn from Malfait 2017 gives "≥ 90°" [[Thwaites2022,Malek2021D,Malfait2017]].
- Item 2 (2 points): passive apposition of the thumb to the volar (flexor) surface of the same-side forearm [[Thwaites2022,Carroll2023]].
- Item 3 (2 points): hyperextension of the elbow by 10° or more [[Thwaites2022,Carroll2023]].
- Item 4 (2 points): hyperextension of the knee by 10° or more [[Thwaites2022,Carroll2023]].
- Item 5 (1 point): forward flexion of the trunk so that both palms rest flat on the floor, with knees locked straight and feet together [[Thwaites2022,Carroll2023]].
- Beighton and colleagues adapted the BS in 1973 from the 1964 Carter and Wilkinson score, in a study of joint mobility in an African population. They replaced hyperextension of all the finger joints with the little finger beyond 90°, and ankle dorsiflexion with trunk flexion [[Malek2021D,Beighton1973]].
- The BS was built as an epidemiological screening tool, not a diagnostic test. The original paper gives no evidence-based reason for its choice of joints or for its usual adult cut-off of 4 or more out of 9 (Established) [[Malek2021D]].
- Two-thirds of the scored joints are in the upper limbs. Major joints such as the shoulders, hips and ankles are not scored, only one plane of motion is tested, and the score shows whether hypermobility is present but not how severe it is [[Malek2021D]].
- The BS does not correlate with shoulder laxity. In 160 people aged 16–35, a positive BS (4 or more) had low sensitivity and low positive predictive value for shoulder laxity, and raising the cut-off to 6 or more did not help [[Malek2021D]].
- In a study of over 100 people strongly suspected of having EDS, 45 had a negative BS (below 4), yet their average shoulder (glenohumeral) abduction was still 20° above normal [[Malek2021D]].
- Trunk flexion can be trained (as in ballet dancers) and is limited by short hamstrings. In one study of over 200 hypermobile people (BS 5 or more), 84.2% could not do the trunk-flexion manoeuvre [[Malek2021D]].
- Malek and colleagues conclude that the BS should not be the main tool for telling localised from generalised hypermobility, and should not be used alone to rule out generalised joint hypermobility (GJH). They give more weight to the clinician's overall judgement (Established, expert review) [[Malek2021D]].

### beighton · Age, sex and ancestry
- Hypermobility is commonest in young children and declines with age, quickly through childhood and more slowly in adulthood. A review lists GJH (BS 4 or more) in 64.6% of children aged 4–7, 35.6% at age 10, 9.4% at ages 12–13 and 11.7% at ages 13–19 [[Malek2021D]].
- In one population dataset, the probability of meeting GJH (BS 4 or more) fell by 5.5% for each extra year of age. Among 200 people over 70, nobody scored above 2 [[Malek2021D]].
- In one hypermobile cohort, most diagnosed people older than 33 no longer reached a BS of 4 [[Malek2021D]].
- GJH is more common in females at all ages, and the sex gap widens around age 14. Several studies report lower GJH prevalence in white populations than in Asian, African and Arab populations (Established) [[Malek2021D,Remvig2007]].
- In 551 Dutch schoolchildren aged 6–12, measured with goniometry, more than 35% scored above 5/9. A high BS went with greater range of motion in 16 other joint movements, so the BS was a valid measure of generalised mobility at this age [[Smits-Engelsman2011]].
- In the same 551 children, 12.3% had joint pain, independent of their BS [[Smits-Engelsman2011]].
- In 207 Swedish 11-year-olds examined at school, the median BS was 1 (IQR 0–2) with no sex difference. A score of 6 or more was close to the 95th percentile in both sexes, which supports the paediatric cut-off of 6 or more [[Glans2025]].
- The two school studies above differ sharply: more than 35% of Dutch 6–12-year-olds scored above 5, against a median of 1 in Swedish 11-year-olds. Method (goniometry against structured visual examination), age and population may explain the gap. This is unresolved [[Smits-Engelsman2011,Glans2025]].

### beighton · Reliability and measurement method
- A 2017 COSMIN-based systematic review rated every included study "fair" or "poor". For the BS, it found limited positive to conflicting evidence on reliability [[Juul-Kristensen2017]].
- That review's recommendation for clinical use: in adults, BS with a cut-point of 5 of 9 "including historical information"; in children, BS of at least 6 of 9 [[Juul-Kristensen2017]].
- In a three-phase study (40 patients in the test phase), inter-examiner kappa was 0.74 for diagnosing GJH and 0.84 for benign joint hypermobility syndrome (BJHS). Item kappas were generally above 0.80, except the fifth fingers and elbows (0.60 or more) [[Juul-Kristensen2007]].
- A 2021 systematic review (24 studies, 1,333 people aged 4–71) found that 12 of 19 inter-rater kappa values (63.2%) and 5 of 8 intraclass correlation coefficients (62.5%) were substantial to almost perfect [[Bockhorn2021]].
- A narrative review reports that most BS reliability studies give kappa values of 0.4–0.8 (moderate). Kappa below 0.6 has been called inadequate for clinical or research use [[Malek2021D]].
- In 49 healthy adults measured in degrees with a structured protocol, inter-rater ICC for total scores was 0.72–0.82. Raters' readings agreed within 5° for all but one joint, and the standard error of measurement was 1.0–6.9° [[Schlager2018]].
- In 149 patients assessed for ME/CFS, the BS agreed only poorly with clinicians' judgement of systemic joint laxity (kappa 0.39 at the 3-point cut-off; 0.39 and 0.45 at the 4- and 5-point cut-offs) [[Bernhoff2022]].
- In 97 adults at a specialised EDS clinic, joint angles estimated from smartphone video correlated moderately with goniometer readings: elbow rho 0.722, knee 0.608, shoulder 0.632 and fifth finger 0.786. Ankle estimates did not correlate significantly [[Sabo2024]].
- A video-based Beighton tool tested on 225 adults referred to an EDS clinic (125 in the test set) correctly flagged 91.9% of people with GJH (sensitivity), but its specificity was only 42.4%. It screened out 32.0% of the test set as not having GJH (Early evidence) [[Sabo2026]].
- The protocol for that work states that clinicians measure hypermobility inconsistently, with high inter-rater variability. It used a clinical-grade goniometer as the reference standard [[Mittal2022,NCT05366114]].
- In 20 children aged 7–12, BS totals from telehealth and in-person assessment rarely matched exactly but mostly agreed within 2 points. Classification at a 7/9 threshold agreed excellently [[Hornsby2023]].
- Smits-Engelsman and colleagues found the BS valid in children "when goniometry is used" [[Smits-Engelsman2011]].
- Self-reported line drawings of the 9 Beighton items, checked against blinded goniometric examination (50 participants), had sensitivity 0.87 and specificity 0.99 for GJH (BS 4 or more). Participant-observer agreement was kappa 0.96 [[Cooper2018]].
- Other scales exist. The Upper Limb Hypermobility Assessment Tool (ULHAT) scores 12 upper-limb items, with an inter-rater ICC of 0.92 and a cut-point of 7 or more out of 12 (sensitivity 0.84, specificity 0.77) in 112 adults [[Nicholson2018]].
- The paediatric working group recommends the ULHAT and the Lower Limb Assessment Score (each 12 items, several planes of movement) for detailed phenotyping beyond the BS [[Tofts2023]].
- In 2,149 people with hEDS, a registry cluster analysis concluded that Beighton scores are unreliable for phenotyping people with multiple conditions [[Petrucci2024]].

### beighton · The five-part questionnaire (5PQ) for historical hypermobility
- The 5PQ asks five yes/no questions [[Carroll2023,Hakim2003]]:
  1. Do you consider yourself double-jointed?
  2. Can you now (or could you ever) place your hands flat on the floor without bending your knees?
  3. Can you now (or could you ever) bend your thumb to touch your forearm?
  4. As a child, did you amuse your friends by contorting your body into strange shapes, or could you do the splits?
  5. As a child or teenager, did your shoulder or kneecap dislocate on more than one occasion?
- In its development study, "yes" to any 2 questions gave the best combined sensitivity and specificity: 84% and 89% in the first cohort (212 cases, 57 controls), and 84% and 80% in a replication cohort (170 cases, 50 controls) [[Hakim2003]].
- Secondary sources quote different 5PQ figures: Carroll gives 84% sensitivity and 85% specificity, and Thwaites gives sensitivity 80–85% and specificity 80–90%. The original reports 84/89 and 84/80 [[Carroll2023,Thwaites2022,Hakim2003]].
- In a Swedish non-clinical sample (297 adults), the translated 5PQ had sensitivity 91%, specificity 75% and AUC 0.87 against an age-adjusted BS [[Glans2020]].
- In 615 Chinese university students, the Chinese 5PQ had 78.6% sensitivity, 65.8% specificity and AUC 0.722 against a BS of 5 or more. Test-retest kappa was 0.703 [[Wang2026SciRep]].
- In the 2017 hEDS criteria, if a person's BS is one point below the age- and sex-specific cut-off, a 5PQ score of 2 or more counts as evidence of joint hypermobility [[Thwaites2022,Malfait2017]].
- In a UK postal survey, 18.3% of 12,853 responders were classified as hypermobile by questionnaire. They were slightly more likely to report chronic widespread pain (18.5% vs 15.8%), an association the authors call modest [[Mulvey2013]].

### criteria · How the 2017 classification is built
- The 2017 International EDS Classification recognises 13 subtypes and replaced the 1997 Villefranche nosology, which had 6 [[Malfait2017,Beighton1998]].
- For every subtype except hEDS, a definite diagnosis "relies on molecular confirmation" (finding the causative genetic variant). The clinical criteria are "suggestive", not diagnostic [[Malfait2017]].
- The 2017 consortium also revised the hEDS clinical criteria "to allow for a better distinction from other joint hypermobility disorders" [[Malfait2017]].
- The 2020 Nature Reviews primer counts 14 types, 13 with a known molecular cause in 20 genes. The cause of hEDS remains unknown [[Malfait2020]].
- hEDS is inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern, but no gene is known, so no genetic test can confirm it [[Yew2021,Gensemer2021]].
- Before 2017, symptomatic hypermobility was diagnosed as joint hypermobility syndrome under the Brighton 1998 criteria (major and minor criteria, including arthralgia, dislocations, soft tissue rheumatism, marfanoid habitus and abnormal skin) or as EDS hypermobility type under Villefranche. The 2017 nosology merged these into hEDS [[Grahame2000,Remvig2007,Tinkle2017]].

### criteria · The 2017 hEDS criteria, as published
- A diagnosis of hEDS needs Criterion 1, Criterion 2 and Criterion 3 all to be met [[Fairweather2025,Malfait2017]].
- Criterion 1 is generalised joint hypermobility on the BS, using cut-offs set by age and sex [[Tofts2023,Fairweather2025,Thwaites2022]]:
  - 6 or more out of 9 for prepubertal children and adolescents
  - 5 or more for pubertal men and women up to age 50
  - 4 or more for people over 50
- Criterion 1 may also be met when the BS is one point below the cut-off and the 5PQ score is 2 or more [[Thwaites2022]].
- Criterion 2 is two or more of Features A, B and C [[Thwaites2022,Fairweather2025]].
- Feature A means systemic signs of a more generalised connective tissue disorder. At least 5 of these 12 items must be present [[Fairweather2025,Thwaites2022]]:
  1. Unusually soft or velvety skin
  2. Mild skin hyperextensibility
  3. Unexplained striae (stretch marks)
  4. Bilateral piezogenic papules of the heel
  5. Recurrent or multiple abdominal hernias
  6. Atrophic scarring at 2 or more sites
  7. Pelvic floor, rectal or uterine prolapse (in specific groups)
  8. Dental crowding with a high or narrow palate
  9. Arachnodactyly (long, slender fingers)
  10. Arm span-to-height ratio of 1.05 or more
  11. Mitral valve prolapse
  12. Aortic root dilatation with Z-score above +2
- Mild skin hyperextensibility (Feature A item 2) is defined as skin stretch over 1.5 cm at the distal forearm or back of the hand, over 3 cm at the neck, elbow or knee, or over 1 cm on the palm [[Thwaites2022]].
- Striae (Feature A item 3) count only at the back, groins, thighs, breasts or abdomen, and only in adolescents, men or prepubertal women without a history of significant weight gain or loss [[Fairweather2025]].
- Atrophic scarring (Feature A item 6) must involve at least two sites, without the truly papyraceous (paper-thin) or haemosiderotic (brown, iron-stained) scars seen in classical EDS [[Fairweather2025]].
- Prolapse (Feature A item 7) counts only in children, men or women who have never given birth (nulliparous), without morbid obesity or another predisposing condition [[Fairweather2025,Thwaites2022]].
- Arachnodactyly (Feature A item 9) means a positive wrist sign or a positive thumb sign on both sides. Mitral valve prolapse (item 11) must be "mild or greater based on strict echocardiographic criteria" [[Fairweather2025]].
- Feature B is a positive family history: at least one first-degree relative who independently meets the current hEDS criteria [[Fairweather2025,Thwaites2022]].
- Feature C means musculoskeletal complications, at least one of the following [[Fairweather2025,Thwaites2022]]:
  - Musculoskeletal pain in two or more limbs, recurring daily for at least 3 months
  - Chronic widespread pain for 3 months or more
  - Recurrent joint dislocations or frank joint instability without trauma
- "Recurrent dislocations or instability" (Feature C) means 3 or more atraumatic dislocations in the same joint, 2 or more atraumatic dislocations in two different joints at different times, or medical confirmation of instability at 2 or more sites unrelated to trauma [[Fairweather2025]].
- Criterion 3 has three parts, all required [[Fairweather2025,Thwaites2022]]:
  - No unusual skin fragility. Skin fragility should prompt consideration of other EDS types.
  - Other heritable and acquired connective tissue disorders, including autoimmune rheumatological conditions, are excluded.
  - Alternative diagnoses that cause joint hypermobility through hypotonia or connective tissue laxity are excluded.
- People with an acquired rheumatic connective tissue disorder (for example lupus or rheumatoid arthritis) can be diagnosed with hEDS only if they meet both Features A and B. Feature C (pain or instability) cannot be counted [[Fairweather2025,Thwaites2022]].
- The criteria list the alternative diagnoses to exclude: neuromuscular disorders (for example myopathic EDS and Bethlem myopathy), other heritable connective tissue disorders (other EDS types, Loeys-Dietz syndrome, Marfan syndrome) and skeletal dysplasias (for example osteogenesis imperfecta). Exclusion can rest on history, examination and molecular genetic testing "as indicated" [[Fairweather2025]].
- In the 2017 criteria, bilateral heel piezogenic papules were added to Feature A [[Aubry-Rozier2021]].

### criteria · How many people meet each criterion
- In a Swiss reference centre (November 2017 to April 2019), 97 adults with symptomatic hypermobility were classified: 61 as hEDS and 36 as HSD. The median age was 40 and 92.7% were women [[Aubry-Rozier2021]].
- In that Swiss cohort, Criterion 1 (GJH) was met by 90% of the hEDS group and 71% of the HSD group (p = 0.07). The median BS was 7 in both groups (IQR 6–9 vs 4–8) [[Aubry-Rozier2021]].
- In the same cohort, Feature A was met by 18% vs 0% (table values), Feature B by 93% vs 33%, and Feature C by 98% vs 75%. Criterion 2 was met by 100% vs 27%, and Criterion 3 by 100% vs 78% [[Aubry-Rozier2021]].
- In the HSD group, 22% had another diagnosis that could confound the picture: Fabry disease, psoriatic arthritis, multiple sclerosis or Sjögren syndrome [[Aubry-Rozier2021]].
- Bilateral heel piezogenic papules were found in 29% of the hEDS group and 22% of the HSD group (p = 0.30) [[Aubry-Rozier2021]].
- At the Mayo Clinic Florida EDS clinic (2,695 adults, November 2019 to August 2024), the 2017 criteria gave these diagnoses [[Fairweather2025]]:
  - HSD: 60.6%
  - hEDS: 18.3%
  - localised or historical HSD: 10.7%
  - none of these diagnoses (controls): 10.4%
- In 91 people with POTS (postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome), 31% met the 2017 hEDS criteria, and another 24% had GJH without meeting them (Early evidence for the size of the overlap) [[Miller2020]].
- An Italian centre re-examined 327 patients from 213 families who had been diagnosed under the Villefranche or Brighton criteria. Its authors propose making the 2017 criteria less stringent, so that more people now classed as HSD would qualify [[Ritelli2024]].
- No study reporting inter-rater agreement for the full 2017 hEDS checklist was found in this pass. Published reliability data cover the BS and 5PQ components only (see beighton) [[Juul-Kristensen2017,Bockhorn2021]].

### criteria · Criticisms of the 2017 hEDS criteria
- The 2017 criteria have been criticised as "too stringent" and as failing to capture the multisystem involvement of hEDS (Established as a published criticism) [[Ritelli2024]].
- The hEDS and HSD groups differed only in pain, movement problems and bleeding on a 16-item severity score. Bone fragility, neuropathic pain scores (DN4 above 4/10: 47% vs 49%) and suspected mast cell activation syndrome (43% vs 50%) were equally common. The authors propose treating hEDS/HSD as one entity [[Aubry-Rozier2021]].
- After coordinated physiotherapy, 54% of the 59 people followed for about 20 months reported improvement, with no difference between hEDS and HSD (56% vs 50%). Only family history predicted a good outcome [[Aubry-Rozier2021]].
- Clustering of 105 Italian adults with hEDS/HSD found two severity classes, "complex/severe" and "simplex/milder", that "do not mirror either the old or new criteria for hEDS" [[Copetti2019]].
- In the Mayo Florida cohort, people with localised or historical HSD reported more symptoms or conditions than controls for 62 of 100 items. The figure was 58/100 for HSD and 20/100 for hEDS, so localised and historical HSD look much like HSD [[Fairweather2025]].
- The same group's earlier analysis of 2,088 patients found that HSD patients reported 42 of 115 conditions more often than hEDS patients, while hEDS patients reported only 9 more often. Conditions more frequent in hEDS were "structural" (such as prolapses) and those more frequent in HSD were "functional" (such as irritable bowel syndrome) [[Fairweather2025]].
- A plasma study found a shared pattern of fibronectin and type I collagen fragments in hEDS and HSD, different from classical EDS, vEDS, rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis and osteoarthritis. The authors suggest it could become the first blood test for hEDS/HSD (Early evidence; not a validated diagnostic test) [[Ritelli2025]].
- In children aged 0–17, heel piezogenic papules (a Feature A item) had a prevalence of 16–72% across studies. In one adolescent dataset they were seen in 76% with HSD/hEDS and 60% of controls (difference 16 points, 95% CI −4 to 36; p = 0.163), so their diagnostic value is not established [[Schubert-Hjalmarsson2026]].
- A 2026 review calls the current criteria inadequate for capturing the clinical heterogeneity of hEDS/HSD and asks for standard diagnostic practice, training and multi-site registries [[Motlak2026]].

### criteria · Diagnostic delay and the experience of diagnosis
- In a global survey (September 2023 to March 2024) of 3,906 eligible respondents, people with hEDS (n = 3,360) reported an average diagnostic delay of 22.1 years. People with HSD (n = 546) reported 17.5 years (self-report) [[Daylor2025]].
- In a UK survey of 2,002 people with hEDS/HSD, the mean diagnostic delay was 19.0–21.7 years [[Berg2026]].
- In a University of Miami hEDS registry survey, the median age at first symptoms was 10 years and diagnosis took 10 years on average. Of respondents, 95.2% rated receiving a diagnosis as "important" or "highly important" [[Wang2024AJMG]].
- Wales has a diagnosed EDS/joint hypermobility syndrome prevalence of 194.2 per 100,000 (2016/2017), or about 10 people in a practice of 5,000. Women were diagnosed 8.5 years later on average than men (95% CI 7.70 to 9.22) [[Demmler2019]].
- A protocol registered in 2026 is building a computer algorithm to scan electronic health records for people who may have undiagnosed hEDS. It produces a referral signal, not a diagnosis, with a validated model expected by Q1 2027 (Hypothesis; no results yet) [[Arvan2026]].

### criteria · The announced 2026 revision
- The Ehlers-Danlos Society states that new diagnostic criteria for all EDS types and HSD "will be published on December 1, 2026" in the American Journal of Medical Genetics, and will "replace any previously published diagnostic criteria for EDS and HSD" (unverified) [[EDSSociety2026]].
- The Society says a second publication on care and management pathways will follow in March 2027 (unverified) [[EDSSociety2026]].
- The Society-sponsored hEDS/HSD Criteria Review Study (since 2022) includes 326 participants at 8 sites in 6 countries (unverified) [[EDSSociety2025]].
- The Society reports early findings that hEDS and HSD "exist on a shared biological spectrum with overlapping features", and that no single feature clearly separates them (unverified) [[EDSSociety2025]].
- As of this pass date (2026-10-25), the revised criteria have not been published. The 2017 criteria remain the published standard [[EDSSociety2026,Malfait2017]].

### hsd · The hypermobility spectrum framework
- The 2017 framework introduced "hypermobility spectrum disorders" as diagnostic labels for people with symptomatic joint hypermobility who do not fit any other syndrome with joint hypermobility, including hEDS [[Castori2017]].
- The 2017 terminology replaces the terms EDS type III, EDS hypermobility type, hypermobility syndrome, joint hypermobility syndrome and BJHS [[Carroll2023]].
- Joint hypermobility (JH) on its own is a descriptor, not a diagnosis. Asymptomatic JH, the HSDs and hEDS are placed on one spectrum, and a person may move between categories as symptoms change [[Carroll2023,Malek2021D]].
- Localised JH affects a single joint or a few joints. GJH involves 5 or more joints, across all four limbs and the axial skeleton [[Carroll2023]].
- Peripheral JH is limited to the hands and/or feet [[Carroll2023]].
- Historical JH is GJH reported by the person (via the 5PQ) with a negative BS on examination [[Carroll2023]].
- HSD requires JH plus at least one secondary musculoskeletal manifestation [[Carroll2023]]:
  - trauma (macro- or microtrauma)
  - chronic pain
  - disturbed proprioception
  - other musculoskeletal traits: pes planus (flat feet), valgus deformities, mild scoliosis, accentuated kyphosis or lordosis, or deformational plagiocephaly
- Generalised HSD (G-HSD) is GJH on an objective score such as the BS, plus one or more secondary musculoskeletal manifestations. Its main differential diagnosis is hEDS [[Carroll2023,Castori2017]].
- Peripheral HSD (P-HSD) is JH limited to the hands and feet, plus one or more secondary musculoskeletal manifestations [[Carroll2023]].
- Localised HSD (L-HSD) is JH at single joints or groups of joints, plus one or more secondary musculoskeletal manifestations in the same region [[Carroll2023]].
- Historical HSD (H-HSD) is GJH reported on the 5PQ, a negative BS, and one or more secondary musculoskeletal manifestations [[Carroll2023]].
- Peripheral JH may be a clue to vascular EDS [[Carroll2023]].
- Operational definitions vary. The Mayo Florida clinic defines L-HSD as a positive-but-below-cut-off BS (not zero) with hypermobility elsewhere, and H-HSD as a BS of zero with a positive 5PQ. The framework defines H-HSD only as a negative BS with a positive 5PQ [[Fairweather2025,Carroll2023]].
- HSD, L-HSD and H-HSD have no specific ICD-10 codes. The Mayo clinic uses M35.7 (hypermobility syndrome) for HSD and Q79.62 for hEDS [[Fairweather2025]].
- Pointers away from HSD and toward a heritable connective tissue disorder include arachnodactyly, an arm span-to-height ratio of 1.05 or more, unusually soft or velvety skin, mild skin hyperextensibility, piezogenic papules, atrophic scars, and mitral valve prolapse or aortic root dilatation on echocardiography [[Carroll2023]].
- Reported combined prevalence figures differ. Carroll cites 1 in 600 to 1 in 900 for HSD plus hEDS, while a 2025 survey paper cites an estimated 1 in 500. The Welsh records give a diagnosed prevalence of 194.2 per 100,000 [[Carroll2023,Daylor2025,Demmler2019]].
- Before 2017, BJHS was estimated at about 3–4% of the general population, affecting 3.3% of women and 0.6% of men (as cited in a 2023 review) [[Carroll2023]].

### hsd · Children and adolescents
- The International Consortium's Paediatric Working Group proposes that children should not be assessed with the 2017 criteria, or diagnosed with hEDS, until they are biologically mature [[Tofts2023]].
- The paediatric framework has 4 components: GJH, skin and tissue abnormalities, musculoskeletal complications and core comorbidities. It produces 8 subgroups: 4 paediatric GJH and 4 paediatric generalised HSD [[Tofts2023]].
- In this framework, a BS of 6 or more out of 9 defines GJH in children, which is 2 SD above average on published population data [[Tofts2023]].
- The paediatric diagnoses are meant to change over time. A child can be reclassified as typical if GJH resolves, as asymptomatic GJH as symptoms improve, or as paediatric generalised HSD if new signs appear [[Tofts2023]].
- Hernias common in infancy (congenital umbilical hernia, inguinal hernia after neonatal intensive care) should not be counted in the paediatric framework. Recurrent hernias may suggest tissue fragility [[Tofts2023]].
- In the Sydney cohort of 89 hypermobile children aged 6–16, 94% had pain in several joints: knee 63%, foot 50%, ankle 48%, hand 36% [[Tofts2023]].
- In a Spanish paediatric genetics clinic (41 patients, 2018–2024), 61% were classified hEDS/HSD, 22% classical EDS, 7.3% vascular EDS and 9.7% other types [[FozFelipe2025]].

### differential · Suspected vascular EDS: pathway and red flags
- vEDS is caused by pathogenic variants in COL3A1 (type III procollagen). It affects an estimated 1 in 50,000 to 1 in 200,000 people [[Byers2017]].
- The international consensus recommends identifying the causative COL3A1 variant before applying the diagnosis [[Byers2017]].
- After diagnosis, the consensus recommends [[Byers2017]]:
  - an individual emergency plan (a "vascular EDS passport")
  - care at experienced centres
  - keeping blood pressure in the normal range
  - yearly vascular imaging where feasible
- Red flags in the original NEJM series: diagnosis "should be considered in young people" presenting with uterine rupture in pregnancy or with arterial or visceral rupture [[Pepin2000]].
- In 220 biochemically confirmed index patients, 25% had a first complication by age 20 and more than 80% by age 40. Median survival was 48 years (a population statistic) [[Pepin2000]].
- In the same series, bowel rupture (often of the sigmoid colon) made up about a quarter of complications. Pregnancy complications caused death in 12 of 81 women who became pregnant [[Pepin2000]].
- In 1,231 people with COL3A1 variants, median survival was 51 years, lower in men and dependent on variant type (a population statistic) [[Pepin2014]].
- In 215 molecularly proven French patients, the median age at first complication was 29 years (IQR 22–39). The most frequent complications were arterial (48%) and digestive (24%) ruptures [[Frank2015]].
- Glycine substitutions and splice-site or in-frame variants were more severe than haploinsufficiency or terminal variants. Patients with the milder variant types had less typical features and no digestive events [[Frank2015]].
- People with COL3A1 null (haploinsufficiency) variants had first complications almost 15 years later, and complications were mostly vascular. Only 28% of affected relatives had a complication and only 30% had minor clinical features [[Leistritz2011]].
- Diagnosis in a 2026 Chinese cohort followed the 2017 rules: a COL3A1 (rarely COL1A1) variant, or one of four "minimal criteria" that should prompt testing [[Wang2026OJRD,Malfait2017]]:
  - family history of vEDS
  - arterial rupture or dissection before age 40
  - unexplained sigmoid colon rupture
  - spontaneous pneumothorax with other features consistent with vEDS
- The major clinical criteria used in a multi-institutional US study were arterial rupture, intestinal rupture, uterine rupture during pregnancy and family history of vEDS [[Shalhub2020]].
- The minor criteria in the same study [[Shalhub2020]]:
  - small-joint hypermobility
  - easy bruising
  - thin translucent skin
  - characteristic face (thin lips and philtrum, small chin, thin nose, large eyes)
  - skin hyperextensibility
  - early-onset varicose veins
  - tendon or muscle rupture
  - pneumothorax or haemothorax
  - clubfoot
  - carotid-cavernous sinus fistula
- Of 173 people diagnosed with vEDS at 11 institutions, 11 (9.8%) had non-pathogenic COL3A1 changes and so did not have vEDS [[Shalhub2020]].
- In the same study, 76 people were diagnosed on clinical criteria alone. Compared with 86 people with confirmed variants, they were more often female (80.3% vs 52.3%) and more often had joint hypermobility (68.4% vs 40.7%) and mitral valve prolapse (10.5% vs 1.2%) [[Shalhub2020]].
- The clinically diagnosed group also had less easy bruising (23.7% vs 64%), less thin translucent skin (17.1% vs 48.8%) and fewer intestinal perforations (3.9% vs 16.3%). The authors judged that "the diagnosis of vEDS in the absence of confirmatory testing cannot be trusted" [[Shalhub2020]].
- Diagnosis was made younger (21.3 vs 30.2 years) when there was a family history. About 50% of people with vEDS have a de novo (new) variant, so family history is often absent [[Shalhub2020]].
- Spontaneous pneumothorax tends to occur at a younger age, and more often in males, than intestinal or arterial events. It is a window for earlier diagnosis [[Shalhub2020]].
- Re-assessing 50 molecularly proven patients suggested that the 2017 vEDS criteria may be less sensitive than the Villefranche criteria. Both sets work poorly in young people without symptoms, for whom genetic testing remains crucial [[Ritelli2020ClinGenet]].
- Atypical COL3A1 glutamic acid-to-lysine substitutions, once classed as variants of uncertain significance, were reclassified as pathogenic. They cause vEDS with skin hyperextensibility resembling classical EDS, which supports multigene panel testing [[Ghali2019]].
- In the BBEST celiprolol trial, all 53 participants were diagnosed clinically, and only 33 had a pathogenic COL3A1 variant. Shalhub and colleagues give this as an example of why molecular confirmation matters for research [[Shalhub2020]].

### differential · Other genetic EDS types and the role of testing
- Classical EDS (mainly COL5A1 or COL5A2) has two major criteria: skin hyperextensibility plus atrophic scarring (major 1), and GJH (major 2) [[Ritelli2020OJRD]].
- Molecular testing for classical EDS is prompted by major criterion 1 plus either major criterion 2 or 3 minor criteria [[Ritelli2020OJRD]].
- In 75 molecularly confirmed classical EDS patients, 78.67% met the minimal criteria, 49.33% had both major criteria, and only 58.66% had GJH (BS 5 or more), so a negative BS does not exclude classical EDS [[Ritelli2020OJRD]].
- In that cohort, the commonest minor features were skin fragility (89.33%), easy bruising (86.66%) and soft, doughy skin (78.66%) [[Ritelli2020OJRD]].
- In 72 children referred to a tertiary centre for suspected EDS, 18 (25%) met clinical criteria for a subtype, and 15 of these (83%) were confirmed molecularly [[Damseh2022]].
- Of the 54 children in that series who did not fully meet criteria, 12 (22%) later had a molecular EDS diagnosis. An EDS gene panel found a variant in 9 of 32 (28%) of those not meeting criteria [[Damseh2022]].
- Poor healing, easy bruising, atrophic scars, skin hyperextensibility and hip dysplasia correlated with a positive genetic result in that series [[Damseh2022]].
- In a Spanish paediatric cohort, clinical exome testing gave a 100% diagnostic yield in the EDS subtypes with known genes. No clear candidate variant was found in the 26% of tested patients who were clinically compatible with hEDS/HSD [[FozFelipe2025]].
- In 71 consecutive people with EDS, 28% had aortic root dilation above +2 SD: 14 of 42 with classical EDS and 6 of 29 with hypermobile EDS [[Wenstrup2002]].

### differential · Marfan syndrome (revised Ghent nosology)
- The 2010 revised Ghent nosology makes aortic root aneurysm and ectopia lentis (displaced lens) the cardinal features. Without a family history, having both is enough to diagnose Marfan syndrome [[Loeys2010]].
- Without a family history, the Ghent rules accept any of four routes [[Pollock2021,Loeys2010]]:
  - aortic Z-score of 2 or more plus ectopia lentis
  - aortic Z-score of 2 or more plus an FBN1 variant
  - aortic Z-score of 2 or more plus a systemic score of 7 or more
  - ectopia lentis plus an FBN1 variant known to be associated with aortic dilation
- With a family history, any one of these is enough: ectopia lentis, a systemic score of 7 or more, or aortic dilation (Z-score 2 or more above age 20, 3 or more below age 20) [[Pollock2021]].
- These rules apply only when there are no features pointing to Shprintzen-Goldberg syndrome, Loeys-Dietz syndrome or vascular EDS [[Pollock2021]].
- Systemic score points (maximum 20) [[Pollock2021]]:
  - wrist and thumb sign 3 (one of them 1)
  - pectus carinatum 2 (pectus excavatum or chest asymmetry 1)
  - hindfoot deformity 2 (plain pes planus 1)
  - pneumothorax 2, dural ectasia 2, protrusio acetabuli 2
  - reduced upper-to-lower segment ratio with increased arm-to-height ratio 1
  - scoliosis or thoracolumbar kyphosis 1
  - reduced elbow extension 1
  - 3 of 5 facial features 1
  - skin striae 1
  - myopia above 3 diopters 1
- Thumb sign (Steinberg): the whole distal phalanx of the thumb sticks out past the edge of the clenched fist. Wrist sign (Walker-Murdoch): thumb and little finger overlap around the opposite wrist [[Pollock2021]].
- The hEDS criteria use the wrist and thumb signs too, but Fairweather's reproduced table pairs the names the other way round ("wrist sign (Steinberg sign)"; "thumb sign (Walker sign)") [[Fairweather2025,Pollock2021]].
- Marfan syndrome affects about 1 in 5,000–10,000 people. FBN1 testing has more weight in the 2010 nosology, though it is not mandatory [[Pollock2021,Loeys2010]].
- In 343 adults seen at a Dutch Marfan clinic, applying the 2010 nosology raised Marfan diagnoses from 44 to 47. Mitral valve prolapse syndrome diagnoses rose from 5 to 28. In both nosologies, 77% of those with Marfan syndrome had an FBN1 variant [[Aalberts2012]].
- A high-arched palate with dental crowding was removed from the Marfan systemic score as non-specific. It remains one of the 12 Feature A items in the hEDS criteria [[Pollock2021,Thwaites2022]].

### differential · Loeys-Dietz syndrome
- Loeys-Dietz syndrome (LDS) was first described in 2005. It is an autosomal dominant syndrome of aortic and arterial aneurysms caused by variants in TGF-beta pathway genes [[MacCarrick2026,Loeys2006]].
- The classic triad is arterial tortuosity and aneurysms, hypertelorism (wide-set eyes), and bifid uvula or cleft palate [[Loeys2006]].
- In 2006, 12 of 40 people with a vEDS-like presentation but no type III collagen defect or LDS craniofacial features carried TGFBR1 or TGFBR2 variants ("LDS type II") [[Loeys2006]].
- In that series the mean age at death was 26.0 years. One death occurred in 59 vascular operations, a low intraoperative mortality that distinguishes LDS from vEDS [[Loeys2006]].
- The 2014 primer groups variants in TGFBR1, TGFBR2, SMAD3 or TGFB2 as LDS. These show more widespread or aggressive vascular disease than Marfan syndrome [[MacCarrick2014]].
- Aortic dissection has occurred at root diameters of 3.9–4.0 cm in LDS types 1–3, compared with the 5.0 cm threshold discussed for Marfan syndrome [[MacCarrick2014]].
- Distinguishing clues for LDS include absent lens dislocation (unlike Marfan syndrome) and velvety, thin, translucent skin with easy bruising. The skin findings overlap with vEDS and EDS, and baseline head-to-pelvis vascular imaging is recommended [[MacCarrick2014]].
- A 2026 updated LDS care primer replaces the 2014 version [[MacCarrick2026]].

### differential · Fibromyalgia and chronic widespread pain
- The 2016 revision of the fibromyalgia criteria added a "generalized pain criterion" to stop regional pain syndromes being misclassified. The physician-based criteria are valid for individual diagnosis, while the self-report version is for research only [[Wolfe2016]].
- Against the 1990 and clinical criteria, the 2010/2011 fibromyalgia criteria had a median sensitivity of 86% and specificity of 90% [[Wolfe2016]].
- The 2016 revision dropped the earlier, "confusing" advice to exclude other diagnoses, so fibromyalgia can now be diagnosed alongside another condition [[Wolfe2016]].
- In 16,987 people with painful rheumatic disorders, 27.9% met the 2011 fibromyalgia criteria and 24.0% met the 2016 revision. The criteria agreed in 96.2% of cases [[Ablin2017]].
- Chronic widespread pain is itself one of the three Feature C items of the hEDS criteria. Pain alone therefore cannot separate hEDS from fibromyalgia, and the skin, systemic and family-history features carry the distinction [[Thwaites2022,Fairweather2025]].
- Fairweather and colleagues cite their earlier report that about 70% of patients seen at their EDS clinic had been diagnosed with fibromyalgia [[Fairweather2025]].

### differential · Inflammatory and autoimmune arthritis
- The 2010 ACR/EULAR criteria classify "definite rheumatoid arthritis" when there is confirmed synovitis in at least one joint, no better explanation for it, and a score of 6 or more out of 10. The score has four domains: joints, serology, acute-phase reactants and symptom duration [[Aletaha2010]].
- In HSD, overt synovitis is unusual, although small effusions can occur. Hypermobility-related symptoms can mimic a systemic autoimmune disease or inflammatory arthritis [[Carroll2023]].
- The hEDS criteria require autoimmune rheumatological conditions to be excluded. When one is present, Feature C cannot be counted [[Fairweather2025,Thwaites2022]].

### Exam hooks
- The Beighton score has 9 points from 5 manoeuvres: little finger beyond 90°, thumb to forearm, elbow and knee hyperextension of 10° or more (each scored on both sides), and palms flat on the floor with knees straight (1 point) [[Thwaites2022,Carroll2023]].
- The 2017 hEDS GJH cut-offs are 6 or more (prepubertal children and adolescents), 5 or more (after puberty up to 50) and 4 or more (over 50) [[Tofts2023,Fairweather2025]].
- A 5PQ is positive with 2 or more "yes" answers, and it can rescue Criterion 1 when the BS is one point short [[Hakim2003,Thwaites2022]].
- hEDS requires all three criteria. Criterion 2 needs 2 of 3 features (A, B, C), and Feature A needs 5 of 12 items [[Fairweather2025,Thwaites2022]].
- Feature C needs 1 of 3: daily pain in two or more limbs for 3 months or more, chronic widespread pain for 3 months or more, or atraumatic recurrent dislocations or instability [[Thwaites2022]].
- hEDS is the only one of the 13 types without a known gene. All the others need molecular confirmation [[Malfait2017,Malfait2020]].
- When an autoimmune connective tissue disease is present, hEDS needs Features A and B, and Feature C does not count [[Fairweather2025]].
- HSD has four subtypes: generalised, peripheral, localised and historical. Each needs JH plus at least one secondary musculoskeletal manifestation [[Carroll2023,Castori2017]].
- Children should not be diagnosed with hEDS before biological maturity, and paediatric GJH uses a BS of 6 or more [[Tofts2023]].
- vEDS diagnosis needs a pathogenic COL3A1 variant. In one series, nearly 10% of people with clinically suspected vEDS had non-pathogenic COL3A1 changes [[Byers2017,Shalhub2020]].
- vEDS minimal criteria are family history, arterial rupture or dissection under 40, unexplained sigmoid colon rupture, or spontaneous pneumothorax with other features [[Wang2026OJRD]].
- The Marfan cardinal features are aortic root aneurysm and ectopia lentis, and a systemic score of 7 or more out of 20 is significant [[Loeys2010,Pollock2021]].
- The Loeys-Dietz triad is arterial tortuosity and aneurysms, hypertelorism, and bifid uvula or cleft palate [[Loeys2006]].
- Thumb sign = Steinberg sign; wrist sign = Walker-Murdoch sign [[Pollock2021]].
- vEDS median survival was 48 years in Pepin 2000 and 51 years in Pepin 2014 (population statistics) [[Pepin2000,Pepin2014]].
- Reported BS inter-rater kappa ranges from about 0.4 to 0.8, while a three-phase study found kappa 0.74 for GJH [[Malek2021D,Juul-Kristensen2007]].

### In plain words
- The Beighton score is a quick 9-point check of how far some of your joints bend. A high score means you are bendy. It does not mean you have EDS [[Malek2021D]].
- Many healthy children are very flexible, and most people get stiffer with age, so the pass mark depends on your age [[Tofts2023,Malek2021D]].
- If you used to be very flexible but are not now, five simple questions about your past can help a doctor take that into account [[Hakim2003]].
- No blood or gene test can confirm hypermobile EDS yet. Doctors decide by using a checklist and examining you [[Malfait2017,Yew2021]].
- The hEDS checklist looks at joint flexibility, skin and body features, family members with hEDS and joint problems. It also checks that nothing else explains the symptoms [[Fairweather2025]].
- If you have troublesome flexible joints but do not meet every hEDS rule, the diagnosis is usually hypermobility spectrum disorder (HSD). HSD can be just as hard to live with [[Castori2017,Aubry-Rozier2021]].
- The other types of EDS, including vascular EDS, are confirmed with a genetic test [[Malfait2017,Byers2017]].
- Vascular EDS is rare but serious. A sudden tear in a blood vessel or the bowel at a young age, or a family history of this, is a reason to ask about genetic testing [[Pepin2000,Shalhub2020]].
- Many people wait years for a diagnosis. Surveys report average delays of 10 to 22 years [[Daylor2025,Wang2024AJMG,Berg2026]].
- A score you work out at home is not a diagnosis. Please bring your questions to a clinician [[Malek2021D,Cooper2018]].
- The international rules for diagnosing EDS and HSD are being rewritten, and the new version is expected at the end of 2026 [[EDSSociety2026]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Are hEDS and HSD one condition or two? Position A: they are a single entity with the same treatment needs. Aubry-Rozier found few differences; Ritelli found a shared plasma signature (Early evidence); the Society reports a "shared biological spectrum" (unverified) [[Aubry-Rozier2021,Ritelli2025,EDSSociety2025]]. Position B: structural features differ, with prolapses more frequent in hEDS and functional disorders more frequent in HSD [[Fairweather2025]]. Status: the revised criteria are due December 2026 [[EDSSociety2026]].
- Are the 2017 hEDS criteria too strict? An Italian centre proposes loosening them [[Ritelli2024]]. Severity clusters do not follow the old or new criteria [[Copetti2019]]. Status: unresolved until the revision appears.
- Should the BS be the gatekeeper for GJH? It is the standard in all criteria [[Malfait2017,Juul-Kristensen2017]]. But it misses the shoulders, hips and ankles, its cut-offs lack an evidence base, and it can be negative in suspected EDS [[Malek2021D]]. Its agreement with clinicians' judgement of systemic laxity is poor [[Bernhoff2022]].
- How reliable is the BS? Bockhorn calls it "highly reliable" (most kappas substantial to almost perfect) [[Bockhorn2021]]. Malek reports kappas of 0.4–0.8, with some below the 0.6 adequacy threshold [[Malek2021D]]. Juul-Kristensen rates the evidence limited to conflicting [[Juul-Kristensen2017]].
- Cut-off notation: open reproductions of the 2017 table print ">6, >5, >4" [[Thwaites2022,Aubry-Rozier2021]], while others state "≥ 6/9" and "≥ 5/9 ... ≥ 4/9" [[Tofts2023,Fairweather2025]]. This pass uses ≥, matching the consortium's paediatric paper and the measurement review [[Juul-Kristensen2017]]. The original table (Malfait2017, paywalled) was not read directly.
- Angle thresholds: sources describe the fifth finger as "beyond 90°" [[Malek2021D]] or "≥ 90°" [[Thwaites2022,Carroll2023]], and the elbow and knee as "≥ 10°" [[Thwaites2022]]. The brief's ">90°" and ">10°" could not be checked against the paywalled original.
- Paediatric GJH thresholds differ widely. More than 35% of Dutch 6–12-year-olds scored above 5 [[Smits-Engelsman2011]], against a median of 1 in Swedish 11-year-olds, where 6 or more was near the 95th percentile [[Glans2025]]. The paediatric framework keeps 6 or more [[Tofts2023]].
- Do piezogenic papules belong in Feature A? They are common in healthy children (16–72%), with no significant difference from HSD/hEDS in one adolescent dataset [[Schubert-Hjalmarsson2026]].
- Wrist and thumb sign names are swapped between sources: Fairweather's hEDS table and Pollock's Marfan review pair the names oppositely [[Fairweather2025,Pollock2021]].
- Do the 2017 vEDS criteria lose sensitivity? A reassessment of 50 confirmed patients suggests so, especially in presymptomatic young people [[Ritelli2020ClinGenet]]. Molecular testing is the agreed standard [[Byers2017,Shalhub2020]].
- How common are hEDS and HSD? Estimates differ: 1 in 500 [[Daylor2025]], 1 in 600–900 [[Carroll2023]], and a diagnosed prevalence of 194.2 per 100,000 in Wales [[Demmler2019]]. All are affected by under-diagnosis.
- Can apps, video or self-report tools triage referrals? Video triage reached about 92% sensitivity but only 42% specificity [[Sabo2026]], and self-report drawings did well in 50 people [[Cooper2018]]. No tool is validated for diagnosis (Early evidence).
