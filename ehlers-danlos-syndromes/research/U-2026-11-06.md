## Findings

### what · How common hEDS, HSD and generalised joint hypermobility are
- The first systematic review and meta-analysis of the prevalence of generalised joint hypermobility (GJH), HSD and EDS (PROSPERO CRD42024505862; searches to 28 May 2025; 17 studies) gave a pooled GJH prevalence of 9.5% (95% CI 5.5–15.8) in females and 5.2% (95% CI 3.0–9.0) in males. Heterogeneity was very high (I² 95.5% and 94.8%), and the authors could not make a robust estimate for people aged 51 and over (Established: systematic review, but heterogeneous) [[Carnerup2026]].
- The same review found only one study that measured HSD and hEDS in the general population (ages 18–50). That study reported HSD in 38% of females and 39% of males, and hEDS in 2% of females and 0% of males. The reviewers concluded the evidence "did not allow for robust conclusions" on population prevalence of HSD/hEDS (Established: absence of evidence) [[Carnerup2026]].
- In US electronic health records (N3C COVID-19 Enclave, about 19.8 million patients with an encounter between 2018 and 2025), 25,152 people carried an hEDS-coded or unspecified-EDS-coded diagnosis. cEDS and vEDS were excluded. That is a coded prevalence of 0.127%, "1 in 788" (95% CI 1 in 778 to 1 in 798). Counting only the specific hEDS code, the figure was 1 in 2,549 (95% CI 1 in 2,494 to 1 in 2,607). The authors note possible misclassification and say that clinical validation by chart review "was not possible" (Early evidence: coded diagnoses, not clinically confirmed) [[Pearson2026]].
- In the same cohort, compared with 25,152 propensity-matched controls without EDS, people with an hEDS code had similar rates of COVID-19 infection but higher odds of a Long COVID code: 4.0% vs 1.4%, OR 2.88 (95% CI 2.55–3.26). The excess was concentrated in people also coded for POTS, MCAS or ME/CFS. The authors call the matched findings "descriptive and hypothesis-generating rather than causal" (Early evidence) [[Pearson2026]].
- Controversy: a coded-record estimate (about 1 in 800 [[Pearson2026]]; Wales 194.2 per 100,000 for EDS plus JHS [[Demmler2019]]) measures diagnosis in health systems. The systematic review shows there is still no robust population-based prevalence of hEDS or HSD [[Carnerup2026]].

### In plain words
- Very bendy joints are fairly common: roughly 1 in 10 women and 1 in 20 men in pooled studies, though the studies vary a lot [[Carnerup2026]].
- Nobody yet knows for certain how many people in the general population have hEDS or HSD. In one large US health-record study, about 1 in 800 people had an hEDS or unspecified EDS diagnosis code [[Pearson2026,Carnerup2026]].
