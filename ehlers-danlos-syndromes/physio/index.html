Part 4 · Treatment

Exercise & physiotherapy

Exercise with a physiotherapist who understands hypermobility is the main treatment for hypermobile EDS and hypermobility spectrum disorder. Watch a published 16-week strengthening programme build week by week, compare paced and boom-bust activity, and see what the trials actually found.

A teaching picture, not a prescriptionProgrammes in trials were supervised and adjusted to each person. Almost every trial enrolled people with hEDS or HSD, so results should not be carried over to the rarer genetic types without care, and exercise plans should be made with a clinician. 78,144,291

Weeks 1–3

Learn the moves first

In the Danish high-load shoulder trial (100 adults with HSD), the first 3 weeks were familiarisation: 3 sets of 10 repetitions at 50%, then 70%, then 90% of the estimated 10-repetition maximum (10RM). Trials share this pattern: start supervised and below symptom thresholds, then progress by load or by competency, as in a children's trial that advanced exercises only when each was done well. Muscle weakness matters in its own right: in 30 people with EDS, measured weakness correlated with fatigue (r=0.461). 268,351,492

In plain wordsStart gently. People in one trial spent 3 weeks learning the exercises with light weights before using heavier ones.
Exam hookHEAVY arm: weeks 1–3 at 50/70/90% of 10RM, then 3×10RM (weeks 4–9), 4×8RM (weeks 10–15), taper (week 16). 268

Significant is not the same as meaningful

In the 100-person shoulder trial, the WOSI score (0–2100, lower is better) favoured high-load training at 16 weeks. The whole confidence interval sat below zero, so the result was statistically significant. But the minimal important difference is 252 points, and the average gap (−174.5) was smaller than that. At one year the gap had shrunk and its interval crossed zero. 268,269

Between-group differences, HEAVY minus LIGHT; negative favours HEAVY. 268,269

What the trials tested

The evidence base is small. A 2021 systematic review of 11 exercise studies found only 3 small controlled trials showing benefit over a control, all in one body area and all in women, with no long-term follow-up; improvement over time was consistent, but superiority over comparators was "weak". Attrition ran from 0% to 44%. Filter the cards by the outcome you care about. 357

"Is it safe to…?" What the published statements say

Choose a type, then open a question. Each answer quotes or summarises a consensus statement, review or trial, and says so when the sources found for this guide say nothing. Advice differs sharply by type: vascular EDS limits come from the fragility of arteries and organs, not from loose joints. 67,180

Type
Studied: supervised, progressive

In a 100-person RCT in HSD with shoulder symptoms, supervised high-load training (pain below 5/10 allowed) caused no serious adverse events; soreness (56% vs 37%) and headaches (40% vs 20%) were more common than with low load. Many clinicians have avoided high loads; the question is not settled. 268,269

Higher injury risk

Generalised joint hypermobility raised the odds of knee injury in contact sport (OR 4.69; 95% CI 1.33–16.52), not ankle injury. For upper cervical instability, consensus advises sport-specific training "with precautions such as avoiding contact sports such as football", or changes such as no heading the ball. 350,406

No statement found

The sources reviewed for this guide make no specific statement for this type. Ask the team that knows you.

Not contraindicated, with care

GeneReviews says chiropractic adjustment and yoga are "not contraindicated", but, like all physical treatments, must avoid causing subluxations or dislocations. Many people with HSD/hEDS report injuries "due to inappropriate interventions". 199,405

Specialist only

For upper cervical instability, the consensus advises highly irritable patients to avoid moderate to large neck movements, isometrics with more than minimal force, and axial loading or distraction. "If in doubt", avoid touching the cervical spine; only clinicians with specific expertise should do cervical manual therapy. 406

May raise risk

GeneReviews says high-impact activity may increase the risk of acute subluxation or dislocation and of pain. A 10-week group running programme for 8 hypermobile children was feasible, with one minor adverse event. 85,199

The cornerstone

Exercise-based physiotherapy is the cornerstone of care; improvement over time is consistent, even though superiority over comparators is weakly shown. People given exercise advice by a physiotherapist were more likely to exercise a lot each week (OR 1.75). 357,433

Guideline-supported

The heart rhythm consensus gives a structured, progressive exercise programme Class IIa and advises starting in non-upright forms (rowing machine, recumbent bike, swimming). In POTS generally, about 3 months of endurance then resistance training, moving from lying to upright, reduced symptoms; evidence in hypermobility is early. 362,431

Beyond exercise: supports and therapies

Early evidence

Splints and hand orthoses

In 169 adults with HSD or hEDS and hand symptoms, a progressive wrist-stabilising exercise programme and wearing a hand orthosis during chosen activities gave the same results at 12 weeks. A 14-person study of finger orthoses found faster timed hand tasks, but the authors call the evidence inconclusive. No trial of finger "ring" splints was found. 225,455

Early evidence

Compression garments

In a 36-person RCT, garments worn alongside 12 physiotherapy sessions improved dynamic balance more than physiotherapy alone (effect size 0.93), but pain fell equally in both groups. Uncontrolled cohorts report less pain (most painful joint 71.5 to 45.7 mm on a VAS over 2 years), open to placebo and selection bias. 37,38,51

Early evidence

Foot orthoses and insoles

Custom foot orthoses in 36 adults with EDS improved foot pain, foot function and fatigue over 3 months, with no control group. In 53 children with leg pain, custom orthotics cut pain by a mean 27/100 at 1 month. No orthosis had been tested in an RCT in children by 2018. 283,365,377

No trial found

Hydrotherapy and braces for the neck

No trial of hydrotherapy in hEDS or HSD was found. For POTS, the heart rhythm consensus advises starting exercise in non-upright forms such as rowing, a recumbent bike or swimming. For upper cervical instability, experts say a neck brace helps some highly irritable patients, while noting "no published guidelines" on neck braces. 406,431

Occupational therapy

Work, home and school

OT guidance for EDS with POTS covers equipment, orthoses, energy conservation and pacing, sleep hygiene and routines: a descriptive paper, not a trial. Patients and clinicians rank OT support "to maintain independence at work and home" as a priority. In the Bendy Study, a physiotherapist and an OT visited each child's school together. 25,40,264

Early evidence

CBT-based pain management

A UK programme (8 days over 6 weeks, 87 people) improved self-efficacy, catastrophising, mood and pain at 1 month; by 5 months scores had drifted back, though most stayed better. An online 8-week programme was feasible, with a moderate effect on worst pain (d 0.63). The most helpful psychological approaches ran alongside physiotherapy. 87,96,371

Early evidence

Breathing muscle training

Inspiratory muscle strength was below 80% of predicted in 77% of 104 women with hEDS. In a 6-week RCT (20 per group), training raised the 6-minute walk from 455 to 515 m. 379

Hypothesis

Pacing

Activity pacing is recommended by patients and clinicians and was a prioritised behaviour-change technique, but no trial has tested pacing alone in hEDS or HSD. Fear of movement is common: 93% of 42 people with JHS/EDS-HT scored above the Tampa cut-off. 40,82
