Nursing care

Four parts of nursing care for a person living with ALS: assessment, giving the medicines, teaching and communication, and documentation. Each part has an animation or a tool to try, and every figure links to its source.

ScopeThis module reports what guidelines, drug labels and published studies say. It is not a care plan and not medical advice. It does not replace local protocols, your scope of practice or the prescriber. Where the research for this guide found no ALS-specific source, the module says so instead of filling the gap.

1 · Assessment

A nursing assessment round

Seven areas, taken in turn at each review. Press Play to go round the ring; each segment lights up and its published checks tick off on the list.

Schematic. The order follows the research summary for this guide; services order the round their own way.

Assessment round · 1 of 7

Symptoms, SpO2, FVC or SNIP, cough

NICE NG42 lists the symptoms to ask about: breathlessness, orthopnoea, disturbed or non-refreshing sleep, nightmares, morning headaches, daytime sleepiness, poor concentration, fatigue and poor appetite. The signs include a raised respiratory rate, shallow breathing, a weak cough or sniff, abdominal paradox and accessory-muscle use. At baseline it records a single SpO2 at rest on room air plus FVC or VC and/or SNIP or MIP, repeated every 2 to 3 months, and notes that a weak cough "could be assessed by measuring peak cough flow". 401 Upright and supine FVC differ, so the position of each measurement matters when values are compared over time. 286,332

In plain wordsThe nurse asks how the person sleeps and whether lying flat makes breathing harder, watches the belly and neck as they breathe, and measures how hard they can blow out or sniff in.
Exam hookIn 25 people with ALS, supine FVC below 75% predicted was 100% sensitive and specific for a low transdiaphragmatic pressure, and accessory-muscle use was 84% sensitive and 100% specific. 286 Sniff nasal pressure predicted hypercapnia better than VC (sensitivity 81% vs 53%), but no test did so with marked bulbar weakness. 306

NICE NG42 thresholds

What the numbers reach

At these values, a NICE NG42 threshold for discussion and possible referral is reached.
  • Not reachedFVC or VC below 50% predicted
  • Not reachedSNIP or MIP below 40 cmH2O
  • ReachedFVC or VC below 80% predicted, with symptoms
  • ReachedSNIP or MIP below 65 cmH2O (men), with symptoms
  • Not reachedSNIP or MIP falling by more than 10 cmH2O per 3 months
  • Not reachedSpO2 94% or below: blood gases indicated

NICE adds that a PaCO2 above 6 kPa calls for urgent referral, to be seen within 1 week. If bulbar or cognitive problems are severe, SpO2 is still measured, but FVC, VC, SNIP and MIP are not done if the interfaces are not suitable for the person. Some pulse oximeters over- or underestimate near borderline values, and overestimation has been reported in people with dark skin. 401

In plain wordsThese tests show how strong the breathing muscles are. Crossing a line on them is a reason for the team to talk about breathing support, not a verdict.
Exam hookPeak cough flow above 160 L/min is needed to clear secretions, and clinicians recommend assistive devices below 270 L/min (Class III). 332

EAT-10 · swallowing

example: 9

At 8 or more: the cut-off that identified aspiration (sensitivity 86%, specificity 72%, AUC 0.88, negative predictive value 95.5%). 426

The study: 70 people with ALS completed the EAT-10 and had videofluoroscopy. Mean scores were 4.28 in safe swallowers, 7.10 in those with penetration and 20.50 in those who aspirated. 426

Exam hookEAT-10 at 8 or more had a negative predictive value of 95.5% for aspiration in ALS: a low score made aspiration unlikely in that sample. 426

How often: NICE and EAN disagree

Month 12: NICE 4 to 6 reviews so far, EAN 2 to 4.

Controversy

Every 2 to 3 months, or every 3 to 6?

NICE NG42 sets multidisciplinary assessments usually every 2 to 3 months, more or less often depending on symptoms, the rate of progression and the person's preference. 401 The EAN 2024 guideline sets reviews "usually every 3–6 months depending on disease progression", naming the ALSFRS-R as the functional assessment. 507 Neither gives a separate frequency for BMI: weight is tied to the visit in both.

In plain wordsThe UK guideline asks for check-ups a little more often than the European one. Both say the timing should follow how fast things are changing.

Falls

  • In a prospective study of 66 people with ALS, 47% of those who walked had fallen by 3 months and 59% by 6 months; among wheelchair users the figures were 18% and 10%.
  • Lower-limb strength and fatigue predicted falls. NICE places "mobility and avoiding falls" in the occupational therapy and physiotherapy assessment.

10,401

Pressure areas

  • Controversy: older small case series described few pressure sores in ALS, but a Taiwanese claims cohort (514 people with ALS, 2056 controls) found a higher risk, adjusted HR 8.82.
  • A hospital quality-improvement project measured 6.37 to 12.2 kPa on the nasal bridge under some oronasal masks tightened against leak, and reduced category 2 mask injuries over 1466 NIV days.
  • The Braden scale has six subscales (sensory perception, moisture, activity, mobility, friction and shear, nutrition); a cut-off of 16 had 100% sensitivity in two studies, but it has not been validated in ALS.
  • For acute settings: "Good skin pressure care is vital", and a person who cannot lie flat may be propped at 30 to 45 degrees or use their own NIV.

59,100,339,452

Cognition and behaviour

  • The ECAS takes 15 to 20 minutes and was designed for health care professionals working with people with physical disability. Control-based cut-offs are 77/100 (ALS-specific), 24/36 (non-specific) and 105/136 (total).
  • In its development sample, 29% of 48 people had abnormal ALS-specific scores, and 40% of carers reported behaviour change.
  • Acute-care guidance describes about 50% of people with MND as cognitively unaffected, about 35% with mild change and up to 15% developing FTD.

2,339

The carer

  • The Zarit Burden Interview comes from research on relatives of impaired older adults.
  • In one Chinese home-care study of 120 caregiver pairs, the 22-item ZBI averaged 63.63 of 88, classed by the authors as moderate to severe burden; caregiver anxiety predicted burden.
  • NICE asks the team to discuss the psychological impact with family and carers and to tell them about carer assessment and respite.

401,491,550

The ALSFRS-R

  • The revision added dyspnoea, orthopnoea and the need for ventilatory support. Scores run from 0 to 48, higher meaning better function.
  • Inter-rater reliability ICC 0.93, telephone against in person 0.97; a self-administered version ICC 0.93; online self-rating r = 0.96 against on-site.
  • None of these studies tested nurse raters specifically.

94,257,312,335,343

Where the evidence stops

  • No ALS-specific source was found on nasal-bridge pressure-injury rates under home NIV, on the Braden scale in ALS, or on nurse-led NIV set-up.
  • NICE names a specialist nurse in the core team but gives NIV decisions to the team with the respiratory ventilation service.
  • This module does not fill these gaps with numbers.

2 · Giving the medicines

Schematic. Steps as the labels and guidelines describe them, not a local procedure; the meal times shown are an example day.

Riluzole tablet

Time it around meals

US label: 50 mg twice daily, at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal. 177 The EU SmPC does not make fasting part of the dosing, and reports that a high-fat meal lowers Cmax by 44% and AUC by 17%. 163

In plain wordsRiluzole is a tablet taken twice a day, away from food on the US label, with blood tests to check the liver.
Exam hookEAN recommends lifelong riluzole from diagnosis. If adverse events occur it suggests reducing the dose, then stopping if they persist. 507

Riluzole liver tests: the US and EU labels differ

Month 15: 4 dated tests on the US schedule, 6 on the EU schedule. Only the US label's pre-treatment test is drawn; the EU wording on a baseline test was not part of this guide's research.

Controversy: the labels differ

Monthly for 3 months, then?

The US label measures aminotransferases before and during treatment, monitoring for liver injury "every month for the first 3 months of treatment, and periodically thereafter". 177 The EU SmPC asks for ALT every month for the first 3 months, every 3 months for the rest of the first year, and periodically after that, and stops riluzole if ALT reaches 5 times the upper limit of normal. 163

Exam hookOn the US label about 50% had at least one ALT above the upper limit of normal and 8% above 3 times, with the largest rise within 3 months. 177
In plain wordsRiluzole can irritate the liver, mostly early on, so blood tests are done monthly at first. Europe then asks for them every three months during the first year.

Saliva medicines

  • NICE: consider an antimuscarinic first line, glycopyrronium first line with cognitive impairment "because it has fewer central nervous system side effects", and botulinum toxin A as a specialist option.
  • EAN lists amitriptyline, atropine, glycopyrrolate, oxybutynin and scopolamine first line, and says suction devices lack evidence and "may increase the saliva production".
  • On the glycopyrrolate oral solution label (a paediatric indication; adult ALS use is off-label), dry mouth, vomiting, constipation, flushing and nasal congestion each affected 30% or more.
  • Over-drying can make saliva thick and hard to clear, and NICE asks for saliva treatments to be reviewed then.

173,339,401,507

Breathlessness and the end of life

  • NICE suggests considering opioids for breathlessness and benzodiazepines when anxiety makes it worse (both off-label in 2016), as-needed supplies at home for people on NIV, and anticipatory medicines towards the end of life.
  • In a pilot of 6 people with terminal ALS, morphine lowered respiratory rate (42 to 29 per minute) and breathlessness (7.5 to 1.8 out of 10) at 120 minutes, without significant CO2 rise or desaturation.
  • Acute-care guidance: sedatives are "used with caution, but not withheld at end of life".

115,339,401

On admission to hospital

  • Drug routes depend on swallowing and on whether an enteral tube is present.
  • For a person on riluzole with fever, a full blood count (neutropenia) and liver tests are listed.
  • An MND alert wristband with an oxygen warning is among the alert tools described.

3 · Teaching and communication

Gastrostomy care: ESPEN and ESGE move the tube differently

Schematic, not to scale. ESPEN gives the distance for the weekly inward movement; the ESGE abstract read for this guide gives the bumper gap.

ESPEN 2022

Day 1: turn

ESPEN 2022: the exit site is checked daily and kept clean and dry, with aseptic care until it heals (usually up to 5 to 7 days). After about one week the tube "should be rotated daily", and moved inwards at least once a week, by at least 2 cm and up to 10 cm. Occlusive dressings are avoided. 63

Controversy: ESPEN pairs daily turning with weekly inward movement, while ESGE asks for daily inward movement. 63,205

Exam hookBuried-bumper warning signs on the ESPEN list: the tube is hard to move, leakage around the site on flushing, frequent pump alarms, abdominal pain, a chronic site infection, or resistance when giving feed or fluids. 63
In plain wordsMoving the tube regularly helps stop the inner disc sinking into the stomach wall, which is called a buried bumper.

In hospital: oxygen with caution

Target bands as published. Not a prescription.

Hospital admission

Why the target is lower

A BTS-based oxygen policy names neuromuscular disorders among the groups in whom oxygen may raise carbon dioxide. 82,405 MND Association guidance says supplemental oxygen is used "only with extreme caution", because excess oxygen can cause acidaemic respiratory failure and death, with monitoring by arterial blood gas and the person's own NIV used for breathlessness and hypercapnia. Saline nebulisers are driven by air rather than oxygen. 339 In 8 people with neuromuscular disease and diaphragm weakness, low-flow oxygen (0.5 to 2 L/min) raised PaCO2 by a mean 28.2 mmHg. 196

Exam hookNICE NG42 itself has no explicit oxygen-caution recommendation: the 88–92% target and the warning come from BTS and the MND Association. 82,339,401
In plain wordsWhen breathing muscles are weak, extra oxygen can let carbon dioxide build up. Hospital teams aim a little lower and use the person's own breathing machine.

Common misunderstandings

Six beliefs that published sources address directly. Choose one to see what the source says.

What the source says

Twitching means ALS.

Among 121 people diagnosed with benign fasciculations, with a normal examination and normal electrophysiology apart from the twitching, none developed motor neuron disease over 2 to 32 years. 65 NICE does list muscle twitching among possible presenting features, alongside weakness and other signs. 401

If the ventilator fails

  • NICE asks for training in NIV use and interfaces, emergency procedures, help at night with removing or replacing the mask, use with wheelchairs, "what to do if the equipment fails", and secretion management.
  • The care plan covers device maintenance and 24-hour clinical and technical support; the risk assessment covers battery back-up, a humidifier, and compatibility with eye-gaze equipment.
  • No ALS-specific source on mask cleaning schedules or alarm-response teaching was found.

401

Cough: the published order

  • NICE and EAN: unassisted breath stacking and/or manually assisted cough first; assisted breath stacking with a lung-volume recruitment bag if bulbar function is poor or cough stays ineffective; then a mechanical cough assist device. EAN adds that a suction device may be considered.
  • Mechanical insufflation-exsufflation raised peak cough flow by 26% (bulbar) and 28% (non-bulbar), against 11% to 13% for manually assisted cough (Class III; Level C).

332,401,507

Swallowing safely: the chin tuck

  • In 81 people with ALS studied by videofluoromanometry, a chin tuck resolved aspiration in 13 of 24 people who aspirated, head rotation in 6, and neither in 5.
  • A scoping review of 157 MND dysphagia papers found very few that tested whether such strategies work.
  • ESPEN 2018 recommends texture changes and postures such as the chin tuck in moderate dysphagia (good-practice point). No ALS evidence on small boluses was found beyond NICE's general advice on posture, seating and texture.

84,401,478,527

Choking and laryngospasm

  • Acute-care guidance asks staff to clear the blockage (saliva, food or fluid), offer palliation for distress, and reassure the person "that death from choking is rare".
  • EAN lists laryngospasm triggers (liquid or saliva at the larynx, reflux, smoke, strong smells, emotion, alcohol, cold air, spicy food) and recommends explaining that it is self-limiting.
  • No validated ALS-specific choking first-aid protocol was found.

339,507

Yes/no codes and eye-gaze

  • Acute-care guidance asks staff to find out the person's yes/no code (any reliable movement, such as a blink), to use yes/no or single-word questions, not to finish the person's sentences, and not to use family as translators unless the person asks.
  • NICE and EAN call for low-tech boards and high-tech AAC, referral to a specialised AAC hub for eye-gaze, and ongoing training for carers. The AAN found no controlled studies of these aids (Level U).
  • No published source on adapted call bells for weak hands in ALS was found; NICE mentions environmental controls only in general.

333,339,401,507

Energy and secretions

  • In a 28-person randomised trial in MND, three weekly one-hour occupational therapy sessions on energy conservation improved fatigue and occupational performance, but not quality of life.
  • NICE suggests humidification, nebulisers and carbocisteine for thick secretions, alongside advice on posture, oral care and suction when drooling is a problem.

No single published nursing documentation checklist for ALS was found. These items are gathered from what NICE, EAN, the MND Association, NHS Lothian and the drug labels say to assess, monitor or record. Your service decides the format.

Advance care planning: what NICE lists

  • ADRT, DNACPR, Lasting Power of Attorney, preferred place of death, what the person does not want (for example hospital admission), who will speak for them, and plans for intercurrent illness.
  • How the plans will be available when needed, for example on the Summary Care Record, and a review when gastrostomy or NIV is planned.
  • EAN adds invasive ventilation, palliative sedation and treatment withdrawal.

401,507

On admission

  • "An MND specialist (nurse, neurologist or care centre coordinator) should be alerted within 24 hours."
  • Extubation can be difficult, and a high NEWS-type score may be normal for the person.
  • In Pennsylvania, 32% of 1748 gastrostomies and 67% of 373 tracheostomies in ALS were placed during emergency admissions. EAN recommends discussing invasive ventilation in advance "to avoid emergency invasive ventilation".

339,500,507

The specialist nurse and the team

  • NICE and EAN both name a specialist nurse in the core team.
  • A Cochrane review found no randomised trials of multidisciplinary care in MND, with low-quality evidence of fewer hospital admissions.
  • Controversy: in all 719 incident cases in Ireland and Northern Ireland, attending the central multidisciplinary clinic was linked to better survival (HR 0.59), and the authors judged it superior to community care supported by a specialist care worker. In New Zealand, unplanned admissions fell after a key-worker role began (before/after audit).
  • In a 2025 UK census of 64 MND nurses and therapists, 14% were at medium and 12% at high risk of burnout.
