Part 1 · The disease

Upper and lower motor neurons

Every voluntary movement needs two nerve cells in a row: an upper motor neuron in the brain and a lower motor neuron in the spinal cord or brainstem. ALS damages both, and each kind of damage gives its own set of signs. Choose which neurons fail, then press Play and watch the signs panel fill in.

In plain wordsOne nerve cell runs from the brain to the spine, and a second runs from the spine to the muscle. When the first one fails, muscles get stiff and reflexes jump. When the second one fails, muscles get weak, thin and twitchy. ALS damages both.
EstablishedLMN

Why eye movements and sphincters are spared

The motor neurons that move the eyes (cranial nerves III, IV and VI) persist throughout the disease. This is why eye-tracking devices can support communication late in ALS. 404 Onuf's nucleus in the sacral cord, which controls the bladder and bowel sphincters, was well preserved in 5 autopsy cases, which fits with sphincter function often lasting until the latest stage. 319

On examination, an eye-movement palsy or a sphincter disturbance argues against ALS and points to another diagnosis. 194

Early evidence Spared groups contain far more of the calcium-binding proteins calbindin-D28k and parvalbumin than the groups lost early. 11 Extraocular muscles have their own myosin types and several nerve contacts per fibre. In mice, 18 genes differed more than tenfold between resistant and vulnerable motor neurons; IGF-2 was protective and extended the life of SOD1 mice by 10%. 404

Communication with eye-gaze is covered in Living with ALS.

EstablishedMechanism: hypothesis

The split hand

In ALS the thumb side of the hand wastes first: abductor pollicis brevis (APB) and first dorsal interosseous (FDI). Abductor digiti minimi (ADM), on the little-finger side, is relatively spared. All three are supplied from C8-T1, and FDI and ADM share the ulnar nerve, so no single nerve or root explains the pattern. 154

Proposed reasons are cortical (smaller magnetic motor evoked potentials from thenar muscles) and peripheral (larger persistent sodium currents, so more excitable APB and FDI axons). Heavy precision-grip use may add metabolic stress. 154 Eisen and colleagues link it to the direct cortico-motoneuronal system, which is markedly enhanced in humans. 155

The sign is reported to have high specificity in early ALS, and clinicians list it among signs with high positive predictive value, though not formally validated. 154,502 Step 6 of the animation shows it.

Established

Reinnervation hides the loss

Surviving motor neurons sprout and adopt orphaned fibres, so each surviving unit grows. Motor unit counting studies show this can keep strength and CMAP amplitude near normal even when up to half the motor units are lost (a secondary report in a review). 252

That is why weakness can be a late sign, and why needle EMG, which sees the enlarged units, can show LMN loss in a muscle that still feels strong. Under the Gold Coast criteria, EMG can stand in for clinical LMN signs. 463,501

Fast-fatigable units are the most vulnerable and are lost first; slow units compensate for a while, then degenerate too. 341,404

Controversy: does it start at the top or at the bottom?

Two hypotheses compete. They rest mostly on different evidence, human brain stimulation for one and the SOD1 mouse and the neuromuscular junction for the other, and the sources do not settle which comes first. 185,341,522

HypothesisStarts in the UMN

Dying forward

Over-active cortico-motoneurons drive the death of anterior horn cells through glutamate (excitotoxicity). 521,522

  • In 23 people with MND, short-interval intracortical inhibition was 3.6 ± 0.8%, against 8.5 ± 1.0% in controls (P < 0.001), and the cortex was over-excitable even in advanced disease. 521
  • In 3 SOD1 carriers, SICI was absent or reduced before symptoms began, and all 3 later developed ALS. 522
  • Eisen and colleagues argue that TDP-43 pathology is limited to the cortex and to nuclei under direct cortical control, placing its origin in the cortex. 155
HypothesisStarts at the muscle end

Dying back

The lower motor neuron fails first at its far end: the nerve terminal lets go of the muscle, then the axon is lost, then the cell body. 185

  • In SOD1(G93A) mice, 40% of end-plates were denervated at 47 days with no loss of cell bodies, and 60% of ventral-root axons were gone at 80 days while motor neurons were still present. 185
  • One person with sporadic ALS who died unexpectedly had denervation and reinnervation in muscle but normal-looking motor neurons. 185
  • Many molecular changes occur at the neuromuscular junction before symptoms begin. 341
