Who it is labelled for, and when

US label

Adults with ALS and a SOD1 mutation

Approved under accelerated approval "based on reduction in plasma neurofilament light chain". Continued approval may depend on confirmatory trial(s). [[FDA2023Qalsody]]

FDA accelerated approval: 25 April 2023. The sponsor named the presymptomatic phase 3 ATLAS trial as the confirmatory study. [[DrugsFDA,Biogen2023]]

EUExceptional circumstances

Authorised 29 May 2024

Positive CHMP opinion 22 February 2024; authorised under exceptional circumstances, with obligations for long-term data, a study in presymptomatic carriers and a registry with yearly reports. [[EMA2024Qalsody]]

Health Canada authorised it with conditions, on 28 February 2025 according to Health Canada summaries that were not read in full. Its drug database lists it on the market from 14 May 2025. [[HC2025Qalsody,HCDPD_Qalsody]]

Published dose

100 mg intrathecal, by lumbar puncture

100 mg (15 mL) over 1 to 3 minutes, after about 10 mL of CSF is removed. Three loading doses 14 days apart, then one every 28 days. If the second loading dose is missed, it is given as soon as possible and the third 14 days later. [[FDA2023Qalsody,EMA2024Qalsody]]

Guideline

EAN 2024

"Offer tofersen as first-line treatment" for progressive SOD1-ALS (strong), discuss the serious adverse events, and weigh benefit against treatment burden carefully in people whose disease progresses slowly. [[VanDamme2024EAN]]

Pregnancy: there are no adequate human data; animal studies at about 4 to 20 times human exposure showed no embryofetal effects, and tofersen was found in mouse milk. [[FDA2023Qalsody]] Quoted from the label as published guidance, not advice for any person. Testing for SOD1 is covered in Genes: C9orf72, SOD1 and more.

Adverse events

Most common in VALOR (tofersen n = 72 vs placebo n = 36): pain 42% vs 22%, fatigue 17% vs 6%, arthralgia 14% vs 6%, raised CSF white cells 14% vs 0%, myalgia 14% vs 6%, raised CSF protein 8% vs 3%. [[FDA2023Qalsody]] Lumbar-puncture effects were about equal in both arms (80.6% each; procedural pain 56.9% vs 58.3%). [[EMA2024Qalsody]]

Slow vital capacity −13.8% vs −18.1%; ALSAQ-5 17.0 vs 22.5. 46 people completed, with follow-up up to 192 to 276 weeks. The authors report survival longer than the expected natural history of SOD1-ALS and that every serious neurological adverse event was reversible. Survival was compared with natural history, not with a concurrent placebo group. [[Miller2026]]

ATLASOngoing

Before symptoms: the confirmatory trial

158 presymptomatic carriers of SOD1 variants enrolled. Carriers are randomised to tofersen or placebo when plasma NfL rises (at least 44 pg/mL and up by at least 10 pg/mL), and the primary endpoint is the share who develop clinically manifest ALS within 12 months. [[Benatar2022a,CTgov_ATLAS]] Estimated primary completion: August 2027; no results yet. [[CTgov_ATLAS]] More in Toward a cure.

The controversy: a surrogate endpoint

The case made for approval

  • Tofersen lowered its target, CSF SOD1, and plasma NfL fell by about half or more. [[FDA2023Qalsody]]
  • NfL is released when axons are damaged, so a fall is read as less ongoing injury. [[Gaiottino2013]]
  • People who started earlier lost fewer ALSFRS-R points at 52 and at 148 weeks. [[Miller2022,Miller2026]]

The reasons for caution

  • The randomised clinical primary endpoint was missed (P = 0.97). [[Miller2022]]
  • NfL rises with nerve damage of any cause; it is not specific to ALS. [[Khalil2018]]
  • The early-vs-delayed comparisons are open-label and nominal; the label says they "should be interpreted with caution". [[FDA2023Qalsody]]
  • FDA approval is accelerated and the EU authorisation is under exceptional circumstances; ATLAS is meant to confirm. [[FDA2023Qalsody,EMA2024Qalsody,CTgov_ATLAS]]
Exam hookTofersen: intrathecal 2'-MOE gapmer antisense oligonucleotide → RNase H degrades SOD1 mRNA. VALOR missed its 28-week ALSFRS-R endpoint (difference 1.2, 95% CI −3.2 to 5.5); FDA accelerated approval (25 April 2023) rested on lowered plasma NfL. Serious neurological adverse events in the label: myelitis, radiculitis, aseptic meningitis, papilloedema and raised intracranial pressure. [[Miller2022,FDA2023Qalsody]]
In plain wordsTofersen is a short strand of genetic code that sticks to the faulty gene's message so the cell throws it away. It is only for people whose ALS is caused by a change in the SOD1 gene, and it is given as an injection into the spinal fluid.
