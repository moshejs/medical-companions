How often the first diagnosis changes

7.3%

Irish ALS Register

Of 437 referrals in 1993–1997, 32 were later rediagnosed. Multifocal motor neuropathy (7, 22%) and Kennedy disease (4, 13%) were the commonest. 84% of the mimics had met only the "suspected" or "possible" El Escorial levels, 13% "probable" and 3% "definite". [[Traynor2000mimic]]

8%

Scottish MND Register

Of 552 people registered, 46 had another diagnosis, and a potentially beneficial treatment existed for half of them. Failure to progress, atypical features or test results prompted the revision. [[Davenport1996]]

≈10%

Across population studies

Nearly 10% of people diagnosed with ALS turn out to have another condition, and a few conditions account for most of these. No register figure more recent than these two was found for this guide. [[TurnerTalbot2013,Ghasemi2016]]

Exam hookTreatable mimics to exclude: MMN (conduction block, anti-GM1, IVIG), cervical spondylotic myelopathy (neck pain, sphincters, nothing above the neck; MRI), bulbar myasthenia (fatigable, ptosis; RNS and antibodies) and Kennedy disease (low SNAPs, gynaecomastia, CAG expansion in AR). [[TurnerTalbot2013,Lawson2014,Garnier2024,LaSpada_GeneReviews]]
In plain wordsSeveral other conditions can look like ALS early on, some of them treatable, so doctors check for them first. Numbness, double vision, bladder trouble or weakness that comes and goes are clues that something else may be going on.
