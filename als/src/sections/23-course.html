ALSFRS-R calculator

This calculator shows how the published scale, the ΔFS formula and published cut-offs classify a set of item scores. It does not diagnose ALS and it is not a prognosis for any person. The ALSFRS-R is copyrighted and is scored with its own wording; the descriptions here are short paraphrases of each item's topic and levels, written for this guide. Each item is scored 0 to 4, so the total runs from 0 to 48, where 48 is normal function. [[Cedarbaum1999,Boddy2025,Brooks2022]]

Prognosis: the ENCALS model

ENCALS is a published model that sorts people into five risk groups. The figures below are medians for groups of people in the development data, as published. They describe populations, not what will happen to one person, and the model's own inputs come from a clinical assessment.

Data came from 14 ALS centres in nine European countries, for people seen between 1992 and 2016. The endpoint combined three events, counted from symptom onset: non-invasive ventilation for more than 23 hours a day, tracheostomy, or death. Of 16 candidate predictors, eight entered the model. [[Westeneng2018]]

Tested in external data, the model separated longer from shorter times fairly well (c statistic 0.78, 95% CI 0.77-0.80) and its predictions matched observed times (calibration slope 1.01). [[Westeneng2018]]

In plain wordsA model like this groups people who share similar features. Even inside one group, some people reach the endpoint much sooner and some much later than the group's middle value.

What the numbers mean, and their limits

How much change matters

In a UK and Australian clinic cohort, the minimal important difference over about 3 months was 3.8 points on the usual summed scale (2 on an interval version); other methods gave 2.02 to 5.43. [[Boddy2025]]

90% of 65 surveyed clinicians and researchers rated a 20% slowing of decline as at least somewhat meaningful, and 93% rated a 50% slowing as very meaningful. [[CastrilloViguera2010]] In a survey of 522 people with ALS, the median minimum slowing expected from a drug was 20%. [[Meyer2026]]

One total, or four domains?

In an Irish register cohort, bulbar and motor subscore slopes fitted prognostic models better than the total slope, and the respiratory subscore added nothing once the other two were known. The authors argue for reporting domains. [[Rooney2017]]

The scale is analysed as if each point were equal, although its items are categories; an interval (Rasch) transformation has been proposed. [[Boddy2025]]

Trials and clinics differ

Pooled trial data average about 1 point a month (PRO-ACT 1.02, ±2.3). [[Atassi2014]] A clinic cohort averaged 0.58, and fast progressors were more likely to drop out of follow-up. [[Boddy2025]] Survival from onset ranged from 4 months to 11.9 years in one German cohort. [[Kollewe2008]]

King's and MiToS measure different things

King's counts regions involved and then the need for gastrostomy or NIV; MiToS counts domains in which independence is lost. In LiCALS trial data they agreed only fairly (weighted kappa 0.21): King's gives more detail early, MiToS late, and the authors propose using both. [[Roche2012,Chio2015MiToS,Fang2017]]

Losing at least one MiToS domain, or an ALSFRS-R fall of 6 to 9 points, over 6 months was the best cut-off for predicting death, tracheotomy or more than 23 hours a day of NIV at 12 to 18 months. [[Tramacere2015]]

Exam hookALSFRS-R: 12 items × 0-4 = 48. ΔFS = (48 − score) / months since onset; a higher ΔFS at diagnosis predicts shorter survival. King's 1-3 count regions, 4A is gastrostomy, 4B is NIV. [[Kimura2006,Labra2016,Roche2012]]
In plain wordsA 48-point score tracks everyday abilities. How quickly it falls says more about the future than any one score, and the fall is rarely a perfectly straight line.
