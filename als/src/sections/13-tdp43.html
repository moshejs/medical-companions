Part 1 · The disease

TDP-43 and what goes wrong

In most people with ALS, a protein called TDP-43 leaves the nucleus of motor neurons and forms clumps in the cell body. Follow it from its normal job to the damage, then switch to SOD1-ALS or FUS-ALS to see the forms that work differently.

How common is TDP-43 pathology? A 2022 Lancet seminar puts it at about 97% of ALS and about 50% of frontotemporal dementia (FTD). [[Feldman2022]] A 2013 review says "over 90%" of ALS. [[Ling2013]] Both summarise the same autopsy evidence rather than one counted cohort. In one autopsy series of 111 cases, every sporadic case (59), every case with dementia (26) and every SOD1-negative familial case (11) had TDP-43-positive inclusions, and none of the 15 SOD1 cases did. [[Mackenzie2007]]

In plain wordsIn almost everyone with ALS, the same helper protein, TDP-43, ends up in the wrong part of the cell and clumps. A few inherited forms, caused by changes in the SOD1 or FUS genes, clump a different protein instead. Genes are covered in Genes: C9orf72, SOD1 and more.

How the pathology spreads: Brettschneider stages

In 76 ALS autopsy cases, phosphorylated TDP-43 (pTDP-43) appeared in four stages, each adding regions to the ones before. The 10 C9orf72-expansion cases followed the same order with more pathology in each region. Oligodendroglia carried pTDP-43 aggregates at every stage. [[Brettschneider2013]]

Other proposed mechanisms

Many processes go wrong in ALS motor neurons, and it is not settled which come first. A 2022 review groups them as impaired RNA metabolism, altered protein handling and autophagy, cytoskeleton and transport defects, and mitochondrial dysfunction. [[Feldman2022]] The labels rate how strong the evidence is that each one helps cause ALS in people: Established repeatedly shown in people with ALS, Early evidence shown in models or limited human data, Hypothesis proposed and still being tested.

Established in tissueEarly evidence as a cause

Cryptic exons from TDP-43 loss

STMN2 and UNC13A cryptic exons are found in tissue from people with ALS, and the TDP-43 repression of cryptic exons is impaired in ALS-FTD tissue. Forcing cryptic exons back out prevented cell death in models. [[Ling2015,Klim2019,Melamed2019,Ma2022,Brown2022]]

Early evidence

Glutamate excitotoxicity

High-affinity glutamate uptake was 59% lower in spinal cord and 70% lower in motor cortex from 13 people with ALS, and not lower in unaffected regions. The astrocyte transporter EAAT2 (GLT-1) was down by 71% in motor cortex. The authors note this could be a cause or a consequence. [[Rothstein1992,Rothstein1995]] Uncleared glutamate drives repeated firing and calcium entry. [[Taylor2016]]

Riluzole's rationale. It reduces glutamate release and blocks the persistent sodium current. [[Doble1996,Urbani2000]] In the first trial, 74% on riluzole and 58% on placebo were alive at 12 months. [[Bensimon1994]] The US label says its mechanism in ALS is unknown. [[FDARiluzoleLabel]] See Riluzole, edaravone and others.

Early evidence

Oxidative stress

Markers of oxidative damage are raised in ALS spinal cord and cerebrospinal fluid, but antioxidant trials have been disappointing. [[Barber2010]] Mutant SOD1 does not harm through lost enzyme activity, so SOD1-ALS is not simply a shortage of an antioxidant. [[Taylor2016]]

Edaravone's rationale. It is an antioxidant that scavenges peroxyl radicals. [[Watanabe2018]] In a selected early-stage group, ALSFRS-R fell 5.01 points with edaravone and 7.50 with placebo over 24 weeks [[MCI186Group2017]]; how far this applies more widely is contested. [[Feldman2022]] The US label says its mechanism in ALS is unknown. [[FDAEdaravoneLabel]]

Hypothesis

Mitochondria and energy

Spinal motor neurons rely heavily on mitochondria to buffer calcium and have little calcium-binding protein of their own, which is one proposed reason they are vulnerable. [[Nijssen2017,Alexianu1994]] Excess glutamate stresses mitochondria and the endoplasmic reticulum. CHCHD10, a gene for a mitochondrial protein, is a rare ALS gene. [[Taylor2016,Feldman2022]]

Early evidence

Axonal transport

Motor axons can be over a metre long. SOD1 mutants slow transport in both directions months before neurons die, and TDP-43 mutations impair transport of RNA granules in flies and in motor neurons grown from patients' cells. Evidence is strong in models and indirect in people. [[Taylor2016]] TUBA4A and PFN1 mutations disrupt the cytoskeleton. [[Feldman2022]]

Early evidence

Protein handling and autophagy

TDP-43 pathology is said to impair the clearing of damaged proteins. [[Feldman2022]] TBK1, which regulates autophagy and inflammation, is an ALS gene. [[Taylor2016]] Clumps are not the whole story: removing large SOD1 aggregates did not change disease in mice. [[Taylor2016]]

Early evidence

Transport into the nucleus

The C9orf72 repeat RNA binds RanGAP1. RanGAP was misplaced and nuclear import impaired in flies, patient-derived neurons and patient brain, and drugs aimed at the repeat corrected it. [[Zhang2015]] Hardened TDP-43 droplets trap import factors too. [[GassetRosa2019]]

Early evidence

Glia: damage from neighbouring cells

In SOD1 mice, mutant SOD1 in motor neurons set the time of onset, while lowering it in microglia slowed later progression. [[Boillee2006]] Mutant SOD1 astrocytes killed motor neurons in culture. [[Nagai2007]] Oligodendrocytes degenerated before onset, and grey-matter demyelination was seen in patients. [[Kang2013]] Inflammatory cells are present in ALS, but in mice glial activation followed axon damage, so its causal weight is debated. [[Beers2019,Fischer2004]]

Open questions

Controversy

Are stress granules the seed?

For: most TDP-43 inclusions made in cultured cells overlapped stress granules, drugs that block stress granules reduced them, and inclusions in patient tissue carried stress granule markers. [[LiuYesucevitz2010]]

Against: cytoplasmic TDP-43 formed long-lived droplets without conventional stress granules, and these droplets hardened, blocked nuclear import and killed cells. [[GassetRosa2019]]

Controversy

Misfolded SOD1 in sporadic ALS?

For: oxidised normal SOD1 shared a misfolded shape (the C4F6 epitope) with mutant SOD1, was found in spinal motor neurons in some sporadic cases, and slowed axonal transport. [[Bosco2010]]

Against: seven antibodies for misfolded SOD1 found it below detection in sporadic and non-SOD1 inherited ALS. [[DaCruz2017]] In cell models, misfolded SOD1 can seed more misfolding and pass between cells. [[Munch2011]]

Controversy

Lost function or toxic clumps?

Cells with TDP-43 clumps usually also lose nuclear TDP-43, so both are likely involved. [[Ling2013]] But some mutant TDP-43 mice lost lower motor neurons with neither clumps nor nuclear loss, only changes in splicing. [[Ling2013]]
