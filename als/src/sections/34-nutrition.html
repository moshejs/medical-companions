Weight, energy and diet

Weight

Weight loss and survival

  • In 92 people in Limoges, a 5% loss from usual weight at diagnosis carried a 30% higher risk of death (RR 1.30, 95% CI 1.08-1.56); each further 5% lost during follow-up raised it by 34%, and each BMI unit lost by 24%. [[Marin2011]]
  • ESPEN summarises cohort data: at diagnosis each BMI point lost raised the risk of death by 9-23%, more than 5% weight loss roughly doubled it (HR 1.92), and 10% loss raised it by 45%. [[Burgos2018ESPEN]]
  • In 55 people, 16.4% were malnourished, with a 7.7-fold higher risk of death; in 79 Japanese people a BMI fall of 2.0 kg/m2 a year or more predicted shorter survival. [[Desport1999,Shimizu2019]]
  • ESPEN suggests aiming for weight gain if BMI is below 25, stability at 25-35 and loss above 35 (good-practice points). [[Burgos2018ESPEN]]
Established: consistent cohort associations
Energy

Hypermetabolism

  • Measured resting energy use was above the predicted value in 47.5% of 61 people, and 80% kept the same metabolic status over time. [[Bouteloup2009]]
  • Hypermetabolism was found in 41% of 58 people with ALS against 12% of matched controls (adjusted OR 5.4), with faster ALSFRS-R decline (−0.68 against −0.39 points a month) and a higher risk of death (HR 3.2). [[Steyn2018]]
  • The survival link is disputed: Bouteloup 2009 found no correlation, and ESPEN cites a study with no association. [[Bouteloup2009,Burgos2018ESPEN]]
  • ESPEN estimates needs at about 30 kcal/kg a day without ventilation and 25-30 kcal/kg on NIV, which lowered resting energy use by 7% in one study. [[Burgos2018ESPEN]]
Early evidence: survival link disputed
ESPEN 2018

What the nutrition guideline recommends

  • Screen for malnutrition and dysphagia at diagnosis and about every 3 months; videofluoroscopy, videofluoromanometry and FEES can detect early dysphagia. [[Burgos2018ESPEN]]
  • Texture adaptation and postures such as chin tuck in moderate dysphagia (good-practice points). [[Burgos2018ESPEN]]
  • Supplements when needs are not met by an enriched diet, while stating there is insufficient evidence that oral supplements improve survival. [[Burgos2018ESPEN]]
  • Reports PEG placed under NIV: in one study 35 people with FVC below 50% had survival similar to 40 above 50%; spirometry can underestimate breathing in bulbar weakness. [[Burgos2018ESPEN]]
Evidence

Does the tube prolong life?

  • The 2023 Cochrane review found no randomised or quasi-randomised trials comparing tube feeding with continued oral feeding, and judged such trials unlikely for ethical reasons. [[Sulistyo2023]]
  • AAN 2009 graded PEG Level B to stabilise weight and prolong survival, from 2 Class II and 7 Class III studies; 4 Class III studies found no survival benefit. [[Miller2009AAN]]
  • ESPEN reports that EFNS found no convincing survival evidence. [[Burgos2018ESPEN]]
  • A 2013 meta-analysis suggested RIG or PIG might be safer than PEG; the larger, prospective ProGas found similar 30-day mortality, but its groups differed in FVC and weight loss. [[Stavroulakis2013,ProGas2015]]
Early evidence: observational only
Diet trials

High-calorie diets

Wills 2014

Phase 2, double-blind, 24 people already tube-fed (20 started). The high-carbohydrate hypercaloric group had fewer serious adverse events (0 against 9) and no deaths against 3 of 7 controls over 5 months (p=0.03); the authors called this preliminary. [[Wills2014]]

Early evidence
LIPCAL-ALS (2020)

201 people, a 405 kcal/day fatty supplement or placebo with riluzole: survival probability at 28 months 0.37 against 0.39 (HR 0.97, p=0.44), no benefit overall. [[Ludolph2020]]

Established: no overall benefit

A survival benefit in fast progressors was found only in a post hoc analysis (effect size not read). [[Ludolph2020]]

Hypothesis
LIPCAL-ALS II (ongoing)

An ultra-high-calorie fatty diet against placebo, survival as the primary outcome, in people with disease under 24 months and ALSFRS-R loss of 0.33 points a month or more; 399 enrolled, active and not recruiting, estimated completion April 2028. EAN 2024 makes no recommendation specific to high-calorie diets. [[LIPCALALSII,VanDamme2024EAN]]

Texture

Texture modification and IDDSI

  • The IDDSI framework (released November 2015) grades drinks and foods on an 8-level continuum, 0 to 7, each level with a number, text label, colour code, definition and a way to test it. [[Cichero2017]]
  • It was built from surveys of 2050 respondents in 33 countries and 3190 in 57 countries. [[Cichero2017]]
  • Videofluoroscopy may identify textures a person can handle; in a FEES cohort, 44% refused thickeners or PEG. [[Miller2009AAN,Onesti2017]]

Background: IDDSI is a shared vocabulary for textures, not a treatment, and it is used across conditions, not only in ALS.

Where the sources disagree

The FVC 50% rule. AAN (one Class III study) and EFNS link higher risk to FVC below 50%, but ESPEN, Canada and EAN cite procedures under NIV, RIG or PIG with similar outcomes below 50%, and note that spirometry is unreliable in bulbar weakness. [[Miller2009AAN,Burgos2018ESPEN,Shoesmith2020,VanDamme2024EAN]]

PEG, RIG or PIG. A 2013 meta-analysis suggested RIG and PIG might be safer; ProGas found similar 30-day mortality, but it was not randomised and its groups differed in FVC and weight loss. [[Stavroulakis2013,ProGas2015]]

Hypermetabolism and survival. Steyn 2018 linked it to shorter survival; Bouteloup 2009 did not. [[Steyn2018,Bouteloup2009,Burgos2018ESPEN]]

Multidisciplinary clinics (observational evidence). Attending a specialist clinic was linked to longer survival in Ireland (median 7.5 months longer; HR 0.59 across Ireland and Northern Ireland) and better mental quality of life in the Netherlands, but Cochrane found no RCT, so the evidence is observational. [[Traynor2003,Rooney2015,VandenBerg2005,Ng2017]]
