Unproven stem-cell clinics

What published guidance says

A JAMA Neurology viewpoint describes "stem cell tourism" as an Internet-based industry that sells unproven procedures to people with incurable neurological diseases, including ALS, with a risk of illness and death. It calls on medical societies and licensing boards to address it. [[Bowman2015]]

The International Society for Stem Cell Research (ISSCR) guide warns about treatments that have not undergone proper testing or regulatory approval and are marketed by clinics. It encourages caution and decisions taken together with treating clinicians. [[ISSCR-Guide]]

Under trial conditions, the cell therapy NurOwn did not meet its phase 3 primary endpoint. [[Cudkowicz2022]]

In plain wordsSome clinics sell stem-cell treatments for ALS that have not been properly tested. Guidance from experts is to be cautious and to talk any offer through with the treating team first.

The big picture

About 97% of all ALS has TDP-43 accumulation. For sporadic ALS, a 2026 review calls today's disease-modifying therapies "of limited utility", which is why gene-specific routes have produced the first targeted successes. [[EstevezFraga2026]]

A 2022 Lancet Seminar noted that most recent advances "have yet to translate into patient benefit". [[Feldman2022]]

Exam hookGene-specific ALS (SOD1, FUS) is where targeted therapy has worked first; most ALS is sporadic and TDP-43-driven. [[EstevezFraga2026]]
In plain wordsResearchers are trying many routes. A few work for small genetic groups, and none yet for most people with ALS.
