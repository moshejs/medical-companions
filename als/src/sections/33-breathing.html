Living with NIV, cough, and the later choices

Adherence

NIV works when it is used for hours

  • Offered BiPAP below FVC 50%, people using it more than 4 h a day had a median survival of 14.2 months from the start, against 7.0 months with less use and 4.6 months when it was declined. [[Kleopa1999]]
  • In one Italian centre, 44 of 71 people (61.9%) tolerated NIV for 4 h a day or more; mild or moderate bulbar symptoms predicted tolerance (OR 6.09). [[LoCoco2006]]
  • Non-compliance was 75% in people with ALS and frontotemporal dysfunction against 38% in classic ALS; 94% of NIV users said they would choose it again. [[Miller2009AAN]]
  • Nasal masks are better tolerated and easier to talk through but can leak through the mouth with bulbar weakness; any mask can cause facial pressure sores. [[Dorst2019]]
Early evidence: observational cohorts
Cough

Peak cough flow: 270 and 160 L/min

  • AAN 2009: peak cough flow above 160 L/min is needed to clear secretions, and clinicians recommend assistive devices below 270 L/min (Class III). The same 270 L/min mark triggered mechanically assisted coughing in Bach's single-centre series of 101 people. [[Miller2009AAN,Bach2002]]
  • Mechanical insufflation-exsufflation raised peak cough flow by 26% in bulbar and 28% in non-bulbar patients, against 11-13% for manually assisted cough (Level C for clearing secretions). [[Miller2009AAN]]
  • NICE: breath stacking and/or manually assisted cough first, assisted breath stacking next, then a mechanical cough assist device; Canada suggests MI-E twice daily below 270 L/min (expert consensus). [[NICE2016,Shoesmith2020]]
  • In a 40-person pilot RCT, breath stacking and MI-E did not differ significantly in chest infections, admissions or survival. Secretion problems are reported by 40-60% of people with ALS. [[Rafiq2015,Berlowitz2023]]
Early evidence: small trials, consensus
Harm in trials

Diaphragm pacing shortened survival

  • The NeuRx pacing system received a US Humanitarian Device Exemption before any randomised data existed. [[DiPALS2015,Dorst2019]]
  • DiPALS (UK, 74 people): median survival 11.0 months with NIV plus pacing against 22.5 months with NIV alone (adjusted HR 2.27); recruitment was stopped. [[DiPALS2015]]
  • RespiStimALS (France, 74 people, FVC 60-80%): NIV-free survival 6.0 months with active stimulation against 8.8 months with sham (HR 1.96); stopped for excess mortality. [[GonzalezBermejo2016]]
  • EAN 2024 makes a strong recommendation against diaphragm pacing in ALS. [[VanDamme2024EAN]]
Established: two RCTs found harm
Tracheostomy

Use differs widely by country

  • 84% of US neurologists said fewer than 10% of their ALS patients had tracheostomy ventilation, against 32% of Japanese neurologists; over 70% in both groups would decline it for themselves. [[Rabkin2013]]
  • 9.5% in a German register (mostly without NIV first); 31.3% (87 of 279) in one Italian centre in 2001-2010; a US review states up to 95% in the US choose not to have it. [[Spittel2021,Spataro2012,Niedermeyer2019]]
  • In a propensity-matched Japanese registry, median overall survival was 11.33 years with it against 4.61 without. [[Hayashi2020]]
  • In a German home-ventilation study, 81% of tracheostomy users had been tracheotomised without informed consent. EAN 2024 asks for it to be discussed in advance care planning so that emergency invasive ventilation can be avoided. [[KaubWittemer2003,VanDamme2024EAN]]
End of life

Stopping ventilation at the person's request

  • The APM guidance (2015) states that in UK law a person with capacity may refuse or ask to withdraw assisted ventilation, invasive or not, and that withdrawal at their request is not assisted suicide. [[APM2015]]
  • It states that the cause of death remains the disease, giving the wording "1a ventilatory failure (due to) 1b advanced motor neurone disease". [[APM2015]]
  • In 95 UK accounts (2015-2024), 94% involved NIV and 92% of people were ventilator-dependent for more than 22 h a day; median time from removal to death was 30 minutes (10 minutes to over 54 hours), and 94% had medication beforehand, usually an opioid plus midazolam, titrated. [[Bleazard2025]]
  • NICE asks for support from professionals experienced in stopping NIV, the medicines and the legal frameworks. [[NICE2016]]
Tests

What the numbers predict

  • SNIP tracked diaphragm pressure (r = 0.9) and was the test most often still possible at the last visit (96%, against 86% for FVC and 81% for MIP). [[Morgan2005]]
  • For death within 6 months, FVC below 50% had 58% sensitivity and 96% specificity; SNIP below 40 cmH2O had 97% and 79%. [[Morgan2005]]
  • A mean night-time saturation below 93% went with a mean survival of 7 months against 18 months above it (Class IV). [[Miller2009AAN]]
  • With marked bulbar weakness no test predicted high CO2 (AAN 2009). NICE still measures SpO2 but not FVC, SNIP or MIP when severe bulbar or cognitive problems make the interfaces unsuitable, and since 2022 notes that oximeters may overestimate saturation in people with dark skin. [[Miller2009AAN,NICE2016]]

Where the sources disagree

When to start. AAN 2009 uses FVC below 50%, while EFNS, NICE and Canada also count FVC below 80% with symptoms. Dorst and Ludolph argue that the EFNS criteria rest on opinion and that earlier NIV may be better; a sham-controlled pilot of early NIV (54 people) found a small FVC benefit and no survival difference, and how well people without symptoms keep using it is uncertain. [[Miller2009AAN,Dorst2019,NICE2016,Jacobs2016]]

Severe bulbar weakness. Bourke 2006 found no survival benefit in this subgroup, and NICE limits NIV there to possible benefit for sleep-related symptoms. EAN 2024 asks that every effort be made to allow NIV regardless of bulbar function, citing later retrospective data. [[Bourke2006,NICE2016,VanDamme2024EAN,Dorst2019]]

Tracheostomy. Rates differ several-fold between countries and centres, and Rabkin's surveys suggest neurologists' attitudes, not only patients' preferences, account for part of the gap. [[Rabkin2013,Spittel2021,Spataro2012,Hayashi2020]]
