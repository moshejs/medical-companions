Part 1 · The disease

What ALS is

Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), also called motor neuron disease, damages both the upper motor neurons in the brain and the lower motor neurons in the brainstem and spinal cord, so muscles weaken, waste and stiffen. The month marks are mean times from onset in people who had died at one London clinic, with all onset types together [[Roche2012]]. Other cohorts differ, and many people live much longer.

Exam hookALS = upper plus lower motor neuron signs, spreading from one region. UMN: brisk or spreading reflexes, spasticity. LMN: weakness, wasting, fasciculations. [[Feldman2022]] Sensory loss, eye-movement palsy and sphincter disturbance argue against it [[Garnier2024]]; eye-movement and sphincter motor neurons are relatively spared until late [[Kanning2010,Mannen1977]].
In plain wordsThe nerves that move muscles slowly stop working, so muscles get weak. Feeling and the muscles that move the eyes are usually spared. It usually begins in one place, such as a hand or the voice, and spreads over months to years.

The ways ALS can look

The same disease process can start in different places and involve mostly upper or mostly lower motor neurons. These patterns, called phenotypes, differ in how fast they usually progress. Choose one.
