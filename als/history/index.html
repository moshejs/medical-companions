History & people

ALS was described in Paris in the 19th century, became known to the public through a baseball player in 1939, and was explained at the level of genes and proteins mostly after 1993. Press Play to run the playhead from 1850 to today, or jump between eras. The scene redraws itself as each era arrives.

The scenes are drawings, not portraits. Every caption in them repeats a milestone listed below, with its sources in the note panel. The axis is compressed before 1935 and again from 1935 to 1990, so the crowded recent decades stay readable.

In plain wordsALS was named in France about 150 years ago. For a century doctors could describe it but knew little about its causes. Most of what is known about the genes and proteins involved was found in the last 30 years, and in 2023 a medicine aimed at one gene, for SOD1-ALS, was approved in the US.

ALS Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, made for health-science students and for people living with ALS and their families.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should change a treatment or a care decision without your own clinical team. Doses, criteria and guideline thresholds are quoted as published. Please check the original sources, which are listed in full in the Sources module.
