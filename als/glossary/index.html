Glossary

94 of 94 terms

AAC (augmentative and alternative communication)Any way of communicating that adds to or replaces speech, from alphabet and picture boards to tablets with voice output and eye-gaze systems.
Accelerated approvalA US FDA pathway that approves a drug on a surrogate endpoint thought reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit. Continued approval may depend on confirmatory trials.
Advance care planningDiscussing and recording in advance the care a person wants or does not want, such as ventilation, feeding tubes and place of death, while they can still communicate their wishes.
ALS-FTDALS together with frontotemporal dementia. About 14-15% of people with ALS meet criteria for FTD.
ALS-FTSDALS-frontotemporal spectrum disorder: the 2017 consensus framework that classifies cognitive and behavioural change in ALS, from ALS with cognitive or behavioural impairment to ALS-FTD.
ALSFRS-RThe revised ALS Functional Rating Scale: 12 everyday-function items scored 0-4, with a total from 0 to 48, where 48 is normal function.
AMX0035Sodium phenylbutyrate-taurursodiol (Relyvrio, Albrioza). It was approved in Canada and the US in 2022, then withdrawn after the phase 3 PHOENIX trial was negative.
Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)A disease in which upper and lower motor neurons degenerate. Amyotrophic means muscle wasting, and lateral sclerosis means scarring of the lateral columns of the spinal cord. It is also called motor neurone disease (MND) and Charcot's disease.
Anterior horn cellA lower motor neuron in the front (anterior) grey matter of the spinal cord. Its axon runs out to skeletal muscle.
Antisense oligonucleotide (ASO)A short synthetic strand that pairs with a specific mRNA. Tofersen is one: it binds SOD1 mRNA so that the cell destroys it.
AspirationFood, drink or saliva going into the airway instead of the gullet. It can cause choking and pneumonia.
Awaji-shima criteriaA 2008 update of the El Escorial rules in which EMG findings count the same as clinical LMN signs, and fasciculation potentials count as denervation.
Babinski signAn upward movement of the big toe when the sole of the foot is stroked; a sign of upper motor neuron damage.
Betz cellA large pyramidal neuron in layer 5 of the primary motor cortex; the classic upper motor neuron.
BMAABeta-N-methylamino-L-alanine, an amino acid from cycad seeds proposed as a cause of ALS on Guam. The hypothesis is contested.
Brain-computer interface (BCI)A device that reads brain signals to control a computer or to produce speech. In ALS it is still research, mostly in single participants.
BulbarRelating to the brainstem-controlled muscles of speech, chewing and swallowing. Bulbar-onset ALS starts with slurred speech or difficulty swallowing.
C9orf72A gene on chromosome 9 whose GGGGCC repeat expansion is the most common genetic cause of familial ALS and familial FTD in European populations.
Caregiver burdenThe physical, emotional and financial strain on family carers. Studies link it to the person's behavioural change and to physical disability.
CNS-LSCenter for Neurologic Study-Lability Scale: a 7-item self-report scale of laughing and crying lability. A score of 13 or more is the threshold used for pseudobulbar affect.
Conduction blockA failure of nerve impulses to pass a point along a motor nerve. It argues against ALS and is the key finding in multifocal motor neuropathy.
Corticospinal tractThe main pathway for voluntary movement, from the motor cortex to the spinal cord. Most of its fibres cross the midline at the pyramidal decussation.
Cryptic exonA hidden stretch of an intron that TDP-43 normally keeps out of mRNA. When TDP-43 leaves the nucleus, the cryptic exon is spliced in and can disrupt the message.
Delta-FS (ΔFS)A progression rate at diagnosis: 48 minus the ALSFRS-R score, divided by the months since onset. Below 0.5 is usually called slower and above 1.0 faster.
Diagnostic delayThe time from first symptom to diagnosis. In one US centre the median was 11.5 months.
Dipeptide repeat proteinsShort repeating protein chains, such as poly-GA, made from C9orf72 repeat RNA without a start codon. They form aggregates in neurons.
Dying-back hypothesisThe idea that motor neuron degeneration starts at the nerve terminal and neuromuscular junction and moves back toward the cell body. It rests mainly on mouse studies.
Dying-forward hypothesisThe idea that overactive cortical neurons drive the death of lower motor neurons through glutamate. It rests mainly on human brain stimulation studies.
DysphagiaDifficulty swallowing. In one study that followed people for about 2 years, it reached 85% of people with ALS.
ECASEdinburgh Cognitive and Behavioural ALS Screen: a 15-20 minute screen of thinking and behaviour designed for people with physical disability.
EdaravoneA free-radical scavenger given in cycles by IV infusion or as an oral suspension. It is approved in Japan, the US and Canada; the EU application was withdrawn.
El Escorial criteriaCriteria published in 1994 and revised in 2000 that grade ALS as possible, probable or definite by the number of regions with UMN and LMN signs. They were designed for clinical trials.
EMG (electromyography)A needle test of the electrical activity of muscle. In ALS it shows ongoing denervation and chronic reinnervation.
ENCALS modelA European prediction model that combines 8 factors, such as onset site, age, progression rate and C9orf72 status, to predict the time to long-term ventilation or death.
ExcitotoxicityDamage to neurons from overstimulation by glutamate. It is one proposed mechanism in ALS and part of the rationale for riluzole.
Familial ALSALS that runs in a family. It makes up about 5% of ALS in population registers and 10-15% in other sources, depending on the definition.
FasciculationA visible twitch of a group of muscle fibres. It is common in ALS but also occurs in healthy people; in benign fasciculation syndrome, people followed for years did not develop ALS.
Fibrillation potentialA small spontaneous EMG discharge from a single denervated muscle fibre; a sign of ongoing denervation.
Flail armAn ALS phenotype with weakness concentrated in the arms. In a London cohort it carried longer survival than typical limb onset.
Frontotemporal dementia (FTD)A dementia that changes behaviour, language or executive function. It shares TDP-43 pathology and genes such as C9orf72 with ALS.
FUSAn RNA-binding protein gene. FUS variants can cause young-onset and often rapid ALS, sometimes arising new (de novo) in the person.
FVC (forced vital capacity)The most air a person can blow out after a full breath, given as a percentage of the predicted value. A falling FVC tracks breathing muscle weakness.
GastrostomyA feeding tube placed through the abdominal wall into the stomach (PEG, RIG or PIG). It does not stop a person eating by mouth.
Genetic counsellingA discussion before and after a genetic test about inheritance, penetrance, what results can mean and their effects on the family. The 2023 consensus says it should be offered to everyone with ALS.
Gold Coast criteriaDiagnostic criteria proposed in 2020: progressive motor impairment, UMN and LMN signs in 1 region or LMN signs in 2, and exclusion of other diseases. The result is ALS or not ALS.
Guam ALS-PDCA cluster of ALS and parkinsonism-dementia complex on the island of Guam, very common from the 1950s and declining since.
HEALEY ALS Platform TrialA US platform trial: one master protocol that tests several drugs, with shared placebo participants and a combined function-and-survival endpoint.
Hoffmann signFlicking the tip of a finger makes the thumb flex; a sign of upper motor neuron damage.
HypermetabolismBurning more energy at rest than predicted. About 41-50% of people with ALS were hypermetabolic in studies.
IDDSIInternational Dysphagia Diet Standardisation Initiative: an 8-level scale (0 to 7) for the texture of food and drink.
Inclusion body myositis (IBM)A slow muscle disease of older adults that weakens the quadriceps and finger flexors. It can mimic ALS, and muscle biopsy is the key test.
IntrathecalInto the cerebrospinal fluid, usually by lumbar puncture. Tofersen is given this way.
Kennedy disease (SBMA)Spinal and bulbar muscular atrophy: an X-linked condition in men with lower motor neuron signs only, confirmed by a CAG expansion in the androgen receptor gene.
King's stagingA staging system that counts the regions involved (stages 1 to 3), then the need for gastrostomy (4A) or non-invasive ventilation (4B).
Locked-in stateBeing aware but having lost almost all voluntary movement; in a totally locked-in state eye movements are lost too. It occurs in a minority of people on long-term tracheostomy ventilation.
Lou Gehrig's diseaseA common US name for ALS, after the baseball player who ended his playing streak in 1939 and died in 1941.
Lower motor neuron (LMN)A motor neuron in the brainstem or the anterior horn of the spinal cord whose axon reaches the muscle. Its loss causes weakness, wasting and fasciculations.
MI-E (mechanical insufflation-exsufflation)A cough assist machine that blows air into the lungs and then pulls it out quickly to clear secretions.
MimicAnother condition that looks like ALS. In population registers, about 7-10% of people first registered with ALS turned out to have another condition.
MiToS stagingMilano-Torino staging: counts how many of four functional domains (movement, swallowing, communicating, breathing) have lost independence, from stage 0 to stage 5 (death).
Motor unitOne lower motor neuron and all the muscle fibres its axon supplies.
Multidisciplinary clinicA clinic where neurologists, nurses, therapists, dietitians and others see the person together. Attendance is linked to longer survival in observational studies.
Multifocal motor neuropathy (MMN)A treatable pure motor nerve disorder with conduction block and weakness greater than wasting. It responds to IVIG.
Neurofilament light (NfL)A structural nerve protein released into body fluids when axons are damaged. Levels are high in ALS but not specific to it, and tofersen lowered them.
Neuromuscular junctionThe synapse where a motor nerve terminal meets a muscle fibre.
NIV (non-invasive ventilation)Breathing support through a mask, usually started at night. NICE says it can be stopped at any time.
Onuf's nucleusThe sacral motor neurons that control the bladder and bowel sphincters. They are well preserved in ALS.
OrthopnoeaBreathlessness when lying flat; a sign of diaphragm weakness.
Palliative careCare that focuses on comfort, symptoms and quality of life. International guidelines describe a palliative approach in ALS from diagnosis.
Peak cough flowHow fast air leaves the lungs during a cough. AAN 2009 cites more than 160 L/min as needed to clear secretions.
PEG (percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy)A gastrostomy placed with the help of an endoscope passed through the mouth. In ProGas, 30-day mortality after PEG was 3%.
PenetranceThe chance that a person who carries a disease variant develops the disease. For C9orf72, family-based and population-based studies give different estimates.
PhenoconversionThe change from carrying an ALS gene variant without symptoms to having clinically manifest disease.
Primary lateral sclerosis (PLS)A rare, slowly progressive disease of the upper motor neurons only. It does not meet the Gold Coast criteria for ALS.
Progressive muscular atrophy (PMA)Motor neuron disease with only lower motor neuron signs. It counts as ALS under the Gold Coast criteria.
Project MinEAn international ALS genetics research project, part-funded by the 2014 Ice Bucket Challenge. In 2016 its investigators reported NEK1 risk variants.
Pseudobulbar affectUncontrollable laughing or crying that is excessive for the feeling behind it. It is classically linked to corticobulbar (upper motor neuron) damage.
RIG (radiologically inserted gastrostomy)A gastrostomy placed under X-ray guidance. In ProGas its 30-day mortality was similar to PEG.
RiluzoleA tablet, suspension or film taken twice daily that is thought to reduce glutamate release. Trials show it extends median survival by about 2-3 months.
SialorrhoeaDrooling. In MND it comes from poor clearance of saliva due to swallowing difficulty, not from making extra saliva.
SNIP (sniff nasal inspiratory pressure)The pressure measured in the nose during a sharp sniff, a test of diaphragm strength. Around 40 cmH2O is a common NIV trigger.
SOD1The gene for copper-zinc superoxide dismutase, the first ALS gene found (1993). Its variants act through a toxic gain of function.
SpasticityA velocity-dependent increase in muscle tone (stiffness) from upper motor neuron damage.
Split handWasting of the thumb-side (lateral) hand muscles with relative sparing of the little-finger side, typical of ALS.
Sporadic ALSALS with no known affected relative. Some people with sporadic ALS still carry a pathogenic variant in an ALS gene.
STMN2The gene for stathmin-2, a microtubule protein that axons need to grow and repair. Loss of nuclear TDP-43 truncates its mRNA.
Surrogate endpointA laboratory or imaging measure used in place of a clinical outcome. The US approval of tofersen rested on lower plasma NfL.
TDP-43An RNA-binding protein encoded by TARDBP. It is lost from the nucleus and builds up in cytoplasmic inclusions in about 97% of ALS.
TofersenAn intrathecal antisense drug (Qalsody) for adults with SOD1-ALS that lowers SOD1 protein.
Tracheostomy ventilationLong-term ventilation through a tube placed in the windpipe. Its use varies widely between countries.
UNC13AA gene for a synapse protein. Loss of nuclear TDP-43 lets a cryptic exon into its mRNA, and common ALS risk variants make this worse.
Upper motor neuron (UMN)A motor neuron whose cell body is in the motor cortex and whose axon runs in the corticospinal or corticobulbar tract.
Variant of uncertain significance (VUS)A genetic change whose link to disease is not yet known. It may or may not contribute, and its interpretation can change over time.
Voice bankingRecording one's own speech so that a synthetic version of the voice can be used later in a speech-generating device.
