Part 1 · The disease

What ALS is

Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), also called motor neuron disease, damages both the upper motor neurons in the brain and the lower motor neurons in the brainstem and spinal cord, so muscles weaken, waste and stiffen. 180 It starts in one region of the body and spreads. Onset in one region

Weakness starts in one limb, often a hand, with wasting, twitching (fasciculations) or stiffness. Limb onset made up 74.6% of people at one London clinic 451, and spinal onset is more common in men 300. Degeneration starts focally and spreads contiguously through the motor system, usually to the other side first 180,445.

In plain wordsThe nerves that move muscles slowly stop working, so muscles get weak. Feeling and the muscles that move the eyes are usually spared. It usually begins in one place, such as a hand or the voice, and spreads over months to years.

Key numbers

These are ranges from population registers and reviews. A number for a population does not describe any one person.

New cases each year
1.6–2.2per 100,000

European registers give about 2.1-2.2 new cases per 100,000 person-years; worldwide meta-analyses 1.59-1.75 110,300,322,548. Incidence rises with age up to 70-79 years 548 and falls quickly after 80 300.

Living with ALS at one time
4.4–5.4per 100,000

Prevalence was 5.2 in the US in 2016, a median 5.40 in Europe and 4.42 worldwide 110,326,548. It is low compared with the lifetime risk because survival is short 168.

Lifetime risk
1 in 350men

In UK primary care data the lifetime risk of motor neuron disease was 1 in 350 men and 1 in 472 women 14. An Irish register gives 1 in 347 men and 1 in 436 women 459.

Men to women
1.35to 1

The overall standardised male-to-female ratio is 1.35 180. It depends on age: in south-east England the adjusted ratio fell from 2.5 under age 51 to 1.4 at older ages 318. It was 1.9 in limb onset and 0.9 in bulbar onset at a London clinic 451.

Age at onset
57–64years, mean

Mean onset was 57.4 years at a London tertiary clinic, 58.7 in Uruguay and 63.9 in the Irish population register 451,459,516. Clinics tend to see younger people than registers do 318.

Median survival
2–4years from onset

Median survival is 2-4 years 180. Across studies, medians from onset to death range from 20 to 48 months 104. Older age and bulbar onset consistently predict a shorter course 104.

Alive at 5 and 10 years
23% · 12%from diagnosis

In the Lombardy register, 76.2% were alive 1 year after diagnosis, 23.4% at 5 years and 11.8% at 10 years 433. A later analysis of the same register found 7% at 10 years and 3% at 15 years 435.

Time to diagnosis
11.5months, median

Median time from first symptom to diagnosis was 11.5 months at a US centre 414, and 11-16 months at a London clinic depending on the group 451. Bulbar onset was diagnosed about 4.4 months faster 460.

Where sources disagree

How many new cases a year?

European registers give about 2.1-2.2 per 100,000 person-years 110,300. Worldwide meta-analyses give 1.6-1.75 322,548, the US Registry about 1.5-1.7 327, and the GBD model 0.78 197. The US Registry calls its count conservative 326, and GBD is a model that pools all motor neuron diseases.

Familial: 5% or 10%?

A meta-analysis found familial ALS in 5.1% of people in prospective population registers and 4.6% across all studies, "not 10% as is often stated" 86. The 1993 SOD1 paper said about 10% 456, and a 2022 seminar says 10-15% 180. Part of the gap is the definition: whether relatives with frontotemporal dementia count, and how many generations are asked about 86,180. Genes

Ancestry or ascertainment?

Standardised incidence was 1.89 in North Europe against 0.83 in East Asia and 0.73 in South Asia 322. People in Germany had older onset (66.6 against 53.2 years) and more bulbar onset (35.9% against 22.8%) than people in China 180. These gaps are put down variously to ancestry, environment or study design, and the authors of the meta-analysis call for an international consortium to test ancestry 322.

How many live past 10 years?

A review says 10-20% survive more than 10 years 104. Population data from Lombardy give 11.8% at 10 years from diagnosis 433 and, in a later analysis, 7% 435. The figures come from different settings and starting points.

The ways ALS can look

The same disease process can start in different places and involve mostly upper or mostly lower motor neurons. These patterns, called phenotypes, differ in how fast they usually progress. Choose one.

Classic limb onset

Weakness starts in an arm or a leg, with both upper and lower motor neuron signs. Limb onset was 74.6% of people at a London clinic 451; in a French incident cohort, lumbar onset was 28.2% and cervical onset 23.1% 217. In another London cohort, median survival from onset was 35 months and 20% were alive at 5 years (31 months in Melbourne) 537.
