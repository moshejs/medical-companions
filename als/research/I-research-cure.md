# I. Research toward a cure
Scope: routes that researchers are testing to slow, stop or prevent ALS, from gene silencing to cell therapy, platform trials, immune and metabolic drugs, biomarkers as trial tools, and prevention in people who carry an ALS gene but have no symptoms. Every trial status below was read from the ClinicalTrials.gov v2 API on 2026-10-20 (status and "last update posted" date are given as seen that day). Journal sources were found through Europe PMC and checked against PubMed esummary (first author, journal, year, volume, issue, pages, DOI, PMCID); a few were also checked on Crossref. Mostly abstracts were read; the ATLAS design paper was read in full text on PMC. Access limits: the Ionis, Biogen, QurAlis (Business Wire) and MND Association sites returned HTTP 403, so the FUSION, BIIB105 and QRL-201 results come from news or press-release reprints (NeurologyLive, BioSpace), and EXPERTS-ALS results come from a University of Sheffield news item. The ISRCTN record for EXPERTS-ALS (ISRCTN15525855) did not render. No peer-reviewed paper was found for the FUSION phase 3, TUDCA-ALS, BIIB105, fosigotifator (ABBV-CLS-7262) or QRL-201 results. These are marked as press or news sources. Evidence labels used: **Established** (a randomized trial or regulatory approval shows an effect on a biomarker or a clinical outcome), **Early evidence** (case series, uncontrolled or small early-phase human data, or a positive topline result that is not yet peer reviewed), **Hypothesis** (preclinical or mechanistic data only), **Tested and negative** (an adequately sized randomized trial or a registry termination showed no benefit). Nothing here is advice to any reader.

## Facts

### Reading the evidence labels
- Most ALS (about 97% of all cases) has TDP-43 accumulation, and a recent review notes that for sporadic ALS the disease-modifying therapies available today "are of limited utility". This is why gene-specific routes (SOD1, FUS) have produced the first targeted successes while routes for most people with ALS are still unproven [EstevezFraga2026].
- A 2025 Brain viewpoint says ALS has "a long history" of promoting therapies on phase 2 data that then fail in phase 3. It names 6-month studies with function-based outcomes as too short, multiplicity as a threat, and over-interpretation of exploratory analyses as a source of "false hope" [Benatar2025e].
- A 2024 review notes that antisense drugs for SOD1, FUS and C9orf72 have often shown proof of mechanism (the target protein falls) without proof of clinical benefit. Among the possible reasons it lists are on-target effects from losing the target gene's normal function and an irreversible cascade of neuron death once toxic protein has built up [Ito2024].

### 1. SOD1 silencing (tofersen, ATLAS, AAV gene therapy)
- Label: **Established** for people with SOD1-ALS (biomarker effect in a randomized trial, long-term open-label data, accelerated approval in the US and approval under exceptional circumstances in the EU). Clinical benefit at 28 weeks was not shown in the randomized phase [Miller2022; FDA2023Qalsody; EMA2024Qalsody].
- VALOR (NCT02623699; status COMPLETED, last update 2023-07-28): 108 adults with SOD1-ALS were randomized 2:1 to 8 doses of intrathecal tofersen 100 mg or placebo over 24 weeks. In the faster-progression primary group the week-28 ALSFRS-R change was -6.98 with tofersen and -8.14 with placebo (difference 1.2 points, 95% CI -3.2 to 5.5, P = 0.97). CSF SOD1 and plasma neurofilament light (NfL) fell more with tofersen [Miller2022; NCT02623699].
- In the combined VALOR plus open-label analysis at 52 weeks, ALSFRS-R change was -6.0 with early start and -9.5 with delayed start (difference 3.5 points, 95% CI 0.4 to 6.7; not adjusted for multiplicity). Neurologic serious adverse events occurred in 7% of tofersen recipients [Miller2022].
- Final long-term data (VALOR plus OLE NCT03070119; OLE status COMPLETED, last update 2025-08-29): 95 of 108 (88%) entered the OLE and 46 completed it, with up to 192-276 weeks of follow-up. Over 148 weeks, early versus delayed start was associated with "numerically" less decline in ALSFRS-R (-9.9 vs -13.5 points) and slow vital capacity (-13.8% vs -18.1%) [Miller2026; NCT03070119].
- The long-term paper reports that tofersen "prolonged survival relative to the expected natural history of SOD1-ALS" and that all serious neurological adverse events were reversible. Survival was compared with natural history, not with a concurrent placebo group [Miller2026].
- The US label states that tofersen (Qalsody) is approved under accelerated approval "based on reduction in plasma neurofilament light chain", and that continued approval may depend on a confirmatory trial (label version effective 2024-11-13 read on openFDA) [FDA2023Qalsody].
- EMA authorised Qalsody on 29 May 2024 under exceptional circumstances. Its stated basis was SOD1 lowering, long-term data and NfL reduction (NfL was supportive, not the only basis). The obligations include studying presymptomatic carriers and a patient registry [EMA2024Qalsody].
- ATLAS (NCT04856982; status ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING, last update 2026-10-20; 158 enrolled, actual; estimated completion 2028-04-30; no results posted): a phase 3 trial of tofersen in clinically presymptomatic adult carriers of high-penetrance, rapidly progressive SOD1 variants. The design paper describes it as, to the authors' knowledge, the first interventional trial in presymptomatic ALS [Benatar2022a; NCT04856982].
- ATLAS design: Part A monitors plasma NfL monthly. A carrier becomes eligible for randomization (Part B, 1:1 tofersen or placebo, about 28 participants planned) when NfL is at least 44 pg/mL and has risen by at least 10 pg/mL from baseline while the person is still presymptomatic. The primary endpoint is the proportion who develop clinically manifest ALS within 12 months. Those who phenoconvert receive open-label tofersen (Parts C/D) [Benatar2022a].
- No ATLAS efficacy results were found in any published or press source as of 2026-10-20 [NCT04856982].
- AAV-mediated SOD1 silencing, a first-in-human case report: two people with SOD1-ALS received one intrathecal infusion of an AAV encoding a microRNA against SOD1. In patient 1, spinal cord SOD1 at autopsy was lower than in untreated SOD1-ALS. CSF SOD1 fell only slightly and transiently, and meningoradiculitis occurred. Patient 2, pretreated with immunosuppression, had stable function over 12 months. Label: **Early evidence** (n = 2) [Mueller2020].
- AMT-162 (uniQure; AAVrh10 microRNA against SOD1), EPISOD1 phase 1/2 single ascending dose (NCT06100276; status ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING, last update 2025-10-22; enrollment 20; start 2024-08-01). No efficacy data were found. Label: **Hypothesis to early evidence** (safety stage only) [NCT06100276].

### 2. C9orf72 (the most common genetic cause)
- Label for C9orf72 sense-strand antisense drugs: **Tested and negative** (two programmes stopped after target engagement without clinical benefit) [vandenBerg2024; NCT04931862].
- BIIB078 phase 1 (NCT03626012; status COMPLETED, last update 2022-01-14): 106 adults with C9orf72-ALS received intrathecal BIIB078 (5-90 mg) or placebo (3:1 within each cohort). Serious adverse events occurred in 14/79 (18%) on BIIB078 and 9/27 (33%) on placebo. Secondary and exploratory findings showed "no reduction in neurofilament levels and no benefit on clinical outcomes", and development was discontinued [vandenBerg2024; NCT03626012].
- Post-mortem and CSF follow-up of BIIB078-treated cases: CSF dipeptide repeat proteins (DPRs) fell and inflammatory markers rose (for example CCL26). The drug was widely distributed in CNS tissue, but DPRs and phosphorylated TDP-43 "remained abundant". BIIB078 therefore did not significantly change key CNS pathology [McEachin2025].
- WVE-004 (stereopure ASO), FOCUS-C9 phase 1b/2a (NCT04931862; status TERMINATED, last update 2023-10-23; 35 enrolled). The registry reason reads "Despite robust, sustained reductions in poly(GP), no clinical benefit was seen at 24 weeks". Wave's 23 May 2023 release reported maximal mean poly(GP) reductions of 48-51% across dose groups [NCT04931862; Wave2023].
- A single-patient study of an optimized mixed-backbone C9orf72 ASO (later called afinersen in other sources; the name is not in this abstract) found repeated intrathecal dosing well tolerated with significant CSF poly(GP) reductions. The authors state that further trials are needed to show safety and efficacy. Label: **Early evidence** (n = 1, biomarker only) [Tran2022].
- Why C9 has been hard: the G4C2 repeat is transcribed in both sense and antisense directions, makes several dipeptide repeat proteins, and causes a mix of loss- and gain-of-function. A 2025 Lancet Neurology review suggests ASO trials may have failed because "they only target a single gain-of-function mechanism" [Mizielinska2025].
- Other C9 approaches the same review describes as "on the horizon": targeting the DNA repeat expansion itself, targeting multiple repeat-derived RNA species, or targeting downstream TDP-43 dysfunction. Label: **Hypothesis** [Mizielinska2025].
- Small molecules that stabilise the G4C2 RNA G-quadruplex reduced RNA foci and DPRs in patient iPSC-derived neurons and improved survival in repeat-expressing Drosophila. Label: **Hypothesis** (preclinical) [Simone2018].
- Human antibodies against poly-GA crossed the blood-brain barrier in C9 BAC mice, reduced GA, GP and GR proteins, and improved behaviour and survival. Label: **Hypothesis** (preclinical) [Nguyen2020].
- Apilimod (LAM-002A, a PIKFYVE inhibitor) phase 2a in C9orf72-ALS (NCT05163886; status COMPLETED, last update 2026-09-29; 15 enrolled; results posted to the registry, no peer-reviewed report found). Label: **Early evidence pending** [NCT05163886].

### 3. FUS (ulefnersen, formerly ION363 / jacifusen)
- Label: **Early evidence, moving toward established**. A positive phase 3 topline result was announced in September 2026 but is not yet peer reviewed. Earlier evidence came from mouse data and an open-label case series [NeurologyLive2026; Shneider2025].
- In FUS knock-in mice, the non-allele-specific FUS ASO ION363 silenced Fus and delayed motor neuron degeneration. In one person with a FUS P525L variant, repeated intrathecal dosing lowered FUS and markedly reduced FUS aggregate burden [Korben2022].
- An investigator-initiated expanded-access case series (12 participants, median age 26 years, 5 sites, 2019-2023) gave jacifusen intrathecally for 2.8-33.9 months, up to 120 mg monthly. CSF NfL fell by up to 82.8% after 6 months. Most participants kept declining on ALSFRS-R, but one showed "unprecedented, objective functional recovery" after 10 months and another stayed asymptomatic. Two deaths were judged unrelated [Shneider2025].
- FUSION phase 1-3 (NCT04768972; status ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING, last update 2026-07-21; 89 enrolled, actual; estimated completion 2028-03): randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled for 72 weeks (Part 1), then open-label (Part 2) [NCT04768972; NeurologyLive2026].
- FUSION topline (Ionis and Otsuka, 22 September 2026, as reported by NeurologyLive): the primary joint-rank endpoint (death or permanent ventilation, time to rescue, ALSFRS-R change through day 505) favoured ulefnersen over placebo (P = .0005). Serum NfL and a time-to-event composite also favoured treatment. Effect sizes were not disclosed, and the drug is not approved anywhere [NeurologyLive2026].
- An early access programme for ulefnersen in FUS-ALS is listed with status AVAILABLE (NCT07862959; last update 2026-10-07). It is intended "from the availability of FUSION clinical trial results until commercial availability" [NCT07862959].

### 4. Routes aimed at TDP-43 and its downstream targets (relevant to sporadic ALS)
- ATXN2 lowering, preclinical basis: reducing ataxin-2 (by knockout or by a single CNS dose of ASO) reduced TDP-43 aggregation and markedly extended survival in TDP-43 transgenic mice [Becker2017].
- BIIB105 (ION541), ALSpire phase 1/2 (NCT04494256; status TERMINATED, "Sponsor's decision", last update 2025-10-09; 99 enrolled; results posted). In the 16 May 2024 release, CSF ATXN2 fell significantly, but plasma NfL and function, breathing and strength did not improve, including in people with an ATXN2 poly-CAG expansion. Discontinuation for adverse events was 8.3% vs 3.6% on placebo. Label: **Tested and negative** [NCT04494256; Biogen2024].
- STMN2: loss of nuclear TDP-43 causes mis-splicing that lowers STMN2, a protein needed for axon outgrowth and regeneration. Restoring STMN2 rescued these deficits in human motor neurons [Klim2019].
- STMN2 correction: ASOs that block the cryptic splice-polyadenylation site restored stathmin-2 in TDP-43-deficient human motor neurons and, after injection into CSF, in mice gene-edited to carry the human cryptic sequences. Label: **Hypothesis** (preclinical) [Baughn2023].
- QRL-201 (QurAlis; STMN2-restoring ASO), ANQUR phase 1/2 (NCT05633459; status ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING, last update 2026-05-15; 69 enrolled). A February 2026 company release reported a reduction in pNfH in the low-dose group, an ALSFRS-R "trend", and a significant result only in a post hoc subgroup. No numbers were released, and the company plans a phase 3 in 2027. Label: **Early evidence** (company interim data, not peer reviewed) [NCT05633459; QurAlis2026].
- UNC13A: TDP-43 loss lets a cryptic exon into UNC13A mRNA, which lowers UNC13A protein. The ALS/FTD risk polymorphisms sit in that intron and increase cryptic exon inclusion. Both 2022 Nature papers nominate it as a therapeutic target. Label: **Hypothesis** [Ma2022; Brown2022].
- Lithium in UNC13A rs12608932 C/C homozygotes (TRICALS, NCT06008249; status TERMINATED, last update 2026-05-13; 88 enrolled). The registry reason reads "The planned interim analysis showed no survival benefit". The protocol had planned 171 participants and selected them by genotype and TRICALS risk-profile score. Label: **Tested and negative** (interim) [NCT06008249; Willemse2022].
- TDP-43-directed antibodies: ACI-5891.9 (AC Immune) neutralised seeding-competent TDP-43 from the CSF of people with sporadic ALS in a seed amplification assay (preclinical). No ClinicalTrials.gov entry for ACI-5891 in ALS was found. Label: **Hypothesis** [Audrain2023].
- VTx-002 (VectorY; AAV-delivered antibody against TDP-43, given intracisternally), PIONEER-ALS phase 1/2 open-label (NCT07287397; status RECRUITING, last update 2026-10-16; 36 estimated; start 2025-12-19). No results. Label: **Hypothesis** (first-in-human safety stage) [NCT07287397].
- PIKFYVE inhibition activated exocytosis of aggregation-prone proteins and extended survival in models of C9ORF72, TARDBP, FUS and sporadic ALS [Hung2023]. A phase 1b of the PIKFYVE inhibitor VRG50635 (NCT06215755; TERMINATED "due to lack of risk-benefit data", last update 2025-12-15; 54 enrolled). Label: **Hypothesis** (preclinical; the one human trial found was stopped) [Hung2023; NCT06215755].
- Integrated stress response (eIF2B activators): HEALEY Regimen G, DNL343 200 mg/day (NCT05842941; COMPLETED, last update 2026-01-28): DRR 1.04 (95% credible interval 0.83-1.32; probability DRR below 1, 0.37). The authors conclude it "did not show evidence of slowing disease progression". Label: **Tested and negative** [Paganoni2026; NCT05842941].

### 5. Cell therapy and the stem-cell clinic warning
- NurOwn (MSC-NTF, autologous mesenchymal cells induced to secrete neurotrophic factors), phase 3 (NCT03280056; COMPLETED, last update 2024-02-29; 196 enrolled; results posted). 33% on MSC-NTF and 28% on placebo met the responder definition at 28 weeks (OR 1.33, P = .45), so the primary endpoint was not met. In a prespecified ALSFRS-R ≥35 subgroup (n = 58), response was 35% vs 16% (OR 2.6, P = .29). Label: **Tested and negative** [Cudkowicz2022; NCT03280056].
- FDA's Cellular, Tissue, and Gene Therapies Advisory Committee voted 17 no, 1 yes and 1 abstention on 27 September 2023 on whether the data showed substantial evidence of effectiveness for mild-to-moderate ALS. Concerns reported included missed primary and key secondary endpoints, more deaths on NurOwn than on placebo, and manufacturing controls [CGTLive2023].
- A new NurOwn phase 3b in early symptomatic ALS is registered (NCT06973629; status NOT_YET_RECRUITING, last update 2025-05-22; 200 estimated) [NCT06973629].
- Neural progenitor cells secreting GDNF (CNS10-NPC-GDNF), phase 1/2a (NCT02943850; COMPLETED, last update 2020-07-15): 18 participants received unilateral lumbar spinal cord transplants. The primary safety endpoint at 1 year was met, with no harm to the treated leg compared with the untreated leg. Grafts survived and made GDNF for up to 42 months, and benign neuromas near delivery sites were common at post-mortem. Label: **Early evidence** (safety and delivery; not designed to show efficacy) [Baloh2022; NCT02943850].
- A JAMA Neurology viewpoint describes "stem cell tourism" as an Internet-based industry selling unproven procedures to people with incurable neurological diseases, including ALS, with "risk of morbidity and mortality". It calls on medical societies and licensing boards to address it [Bowman2015].
- The ISSCR's guide to stem cell treatments warns about treatments "that have not undergone proper testing or regulatory approval and are being marketed by clinics". It encourages caution and decisions taken together with treating clinicians [ISSCR-Guide].
- A briefing note from the International Alliance of ALS/MND Associations lists "stem cell clinics" among unproven treatments (read as a search-result summary only; marked unverified) [Alliance2020].

### 6. Platform trials
- HEALEY ALS Platform Trial master protocol (NCT04297683; status ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING, last update 2026-07-21; 1500 estimated). Each regimen randomizes 3:1 drug to placebo and shares placebo participants across regimens. The primary endpoint is a Bayesian joint model of ALSFRS-R and survival, reported as a disease rate ratio (DRR; below 1 favours the drug) [Paganoni2025; NCT04297683].
- Regimen A, zilucoplan (complement C5 inhibitor; NCT04436497, COMPLETED, last update 2023-07-25): 162 randomized. DRR 1.08 (95% CrI 0.87-1.31), stopped early for futility. Label: **Tested and negative** [Paganoni2025].
- Regimen B, verdiperstat (myeloperoxidase inhibitor; NCT04436510, COMPLETED, last update 2023-06-12): 167 randomized. DRR 0.98 (95% CrI 0.77-1.24). **Tested and negative** [HEALEY2025Verdiperstat].
- Regimen C, CNM-Au8 (gold nanocrystals; NCT04414345, COMPLETED, last update 2023-07-25): 161 randomized. DRR 0.97 (95% CrI 0.783-1.175), with "no benefit" at 24 weeks. **Tested and negative** on the platform's primary endpoint [HEALEY2025CNMAu8].
- CNM-Au8 controversy: the earlier phase 2 RESCUE-ALS (NCT04098406; COMPLETED, last update 2024-07-03; 45 enrolled) missed its MUNIX primary endpoint (difference 7.7%, P = 0.43). It reported an exploratory 60% reduction in all-cause mortality (HR 0.408, 95% CI 0.166-1.001) that the authors said supports further study [Vucic2023; NCT04098406].
- Regimen D, pridopidine (sigma-1 receptor agonist; NCT04615923, COMPLETED, last update 2023-08-23): 162 analysed. DRR 0.99 (95% CrI 0.80-1.21), so it "did not impact the progression of ALS" over 24 weeks [HEALEY2025Pridopidine].
- Pridopidine controversy: a sponsor-authored post hoc subgroup (72 participants with definite/probable ALS and early progression) reported slower ALSFRS-R decline (difference 2.90, nominal p = 0.03). On that basis a phase 3, PREVAiLS, was started (NCT07322003; status RECRUITING, last update 2026-10-06; 500 estimated; 48 weeks). Label: **Tested and negative** in the primary analysis; the subgroup claim is a hypothesis [Geva2026; NCT07322003].
- Regimen E, trehalose (SLS-005; NCT05136885, COMPLETED, last update 2025-05-14): 161 eligible (120 trehalose, 41 placebo, plus 164 shared placebo). DRR 0.87 (95% CrI 0.665-1.102). Serious adverse events occurred in 16% vs 7% on regimen placebo. The authors judged it "unlikely to be efficacious". **Tested and negative** [HEALEY2025Trehalose].
- Regimen F, fosigotifator (ABBV-CLS-7262, an eIF2B activator; NCT05740813, COMPLETED, last update 2025-11-14; 310 enrolled; results posted). As reported in January 2025, the primary endpoint was not met at either dose, with an exploratory signal on muscle strength at the high dose. No peer-reviewed report was found. **Tested and negative** [LongevityTech2025; NCT05740813].
- Regimen G, DNL343: see section 4. Regimen I, NUZ-001 (NCT07410806; status ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING, last update 2026-07-20; 240 enrolled; start 2026-02-25). No Regimen H record was found on ClinicalTrials.gov [Paganoni2026; NCT07410806].
- At-home vital capacity, coached by video in the first four HEALEY regimens (233 participants), correlated well with in-clinic VC (Lin's rc 0.82, no systematic bias). This supports remote measurement as a trial tool [Macklin2026].
- MND-SMART (UK; NCT04302870; status RECRUITING, last update 2026-03-04; 1150 estimated), a multi-arm, multistage trial. At interim analysis 2 (530 analysed), monthly ALSFRS-R decline was -0.650 on memantine, -0.625 on trazodone and -0.655 on placebo. Neither met the criteria to continue, and both arms were dropped. **Tested and negative** for memantine and trazodone [Pal2024].
- MND-SMART later added amantadine (April 2023) and tacrolimus (March-April 2025); these arms were recruiting as of the 2026-03-04 registry update. No amantadine result was found [NCT04302870].
- EXPERTS-ALS (UK, ISRCTN15525855; registry page not readable in this pass) is an NfL-based screening platform in which each participant takes one drug for about 18-24 weeks. The first two drugs, metformin and nifedipine, did not lower NfL in about 30 participants (Sheffield news, 11 August 2026; not peer reviewed). Ropinirole, neflamapimod and RT1999 have been named as later drugs. **Tested and negative** for metformin and nifedipine at the screening stage [Sheffield2026].
- TRICALS is a European network that used its risk-profile score to define eligibility (score -6.0 to -2.0) in the lithium/UNC13A trial. That trial is terminated (section 4) [Willemse2022; NCT06008249].

### 7. Other routes with trial results
- Masitinib (tyrosine kinase inhibitor), AB10015 phase 2/3 (NCT02588677; COMPLETED, last update 2023-09-29): in the prespecified primary population (4.5 mg/kg/day, n = 99 vs 102 placebo) ALSFRS-R decline differed by 3.4 points (95% CI 0.65-6.13; P = 0.016). The broader "normal and fast progressor" cohort and the 3.0 mg/kg/day cohorts showed no significant effect. Serious adverse events: 31% vs 18% on placebo [Mora2020; NCT02588677].
- Masitinib confirmatory trials: AB19001 (NCT03127267) still shows RECRUITING (last update 2025-09-12; 495 estimated), although a January 2025 report says it is being replaced because of slow recruitment. The replacement, AB23005 (NCT07174492), is NOT_YET_RECRUITING (last update 2025-10-03; 412 estimated). EU and Canadian conditional applications reportedly received negative opinions. Label: **Early evidence, contested** [NCT03127267; NCT07174492; ALSNewsToday2025].
- Ibudilast (MN-166), COMBAT-ALS phase 2b/3 (NCT04057898; status ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING, last update 2026-03-06; 234 enrolled): 12 months double-blind, then 6 months open-label, with CAFS as the primary endpoint. No topline efficacy result had been reported as of 2026-10-20. Label: **Hypothesis to early evidence** (pending) [Oskarsson2021; NCT04057898].
- Low-dose IL-2, MIROCALS phase 2b (NCT03039673; COMPLETED, last update 2025-12-04): 220 randomized after a riluzole run-in. The unadjusted primary analysis showed a non-significant 19% lower risk of death (HR 0.81, 95% CI 0.54-1.22, P = 0.33). The covariate-adjusted analysis was significant (HR 0.32, 0.14-0.73), and in the 70% with low CSF pNFH, HR was 0.52 (0.30-0.89). Label: **Early evidence, contested** (primary endpoint not met) [Bensimon2025].
- A 2026 MIROCALS reanalysis found that the TRICALS risk score cannot replace CSF pNFH as a stratification variable (correlation r = 0.357). Adding the pNFH-by-treatment interaction identified a treatment effect (HR 0.28) [AlKhleifat2026].
- TUDCA alone, TUDCA-ALS phase 3 (NCT03800524; registry status UNKNOWN, last update 2023-07-10; 337 enrolled): March 2024 topline results reported no separation from placebo on ALSFRS-R over 18 months and no difference in survival or NfL. Enrollment fell short of the planned 440. No peer-reviewed results paper was found. Label: **Tested and negative** [NeurologyLive2024; Albanese2022; NCT03800524].
- Ultra-high-dose methylcobalamin, JETALS phase 3 (Japan; NCT03548311; COMPLETED, last update 2025-03-26): 130 people with early-stage ALS and moderate progression were randomized. At week 16, ALSFRS-R decline was 1.97 points smaller than placebo (-2.66 vs -4.63; 95% CI 0.44-3.50; P = .01). Label: **Early evidence** (single positive trial in a selected population, short duration) [Oki2022; NCT03548311].
- Rapamycin, RAP-ALS phase 2 (NCT03359538; COMPLETED, last update 2024-09-19): 63 randomized 1:1:1. The primary Treg endpoint was not attained, and safety findings were reassuring. Label: **Tested and negative** on its biological primary endpoint [Mandrioli2023].
- RIPK1 inhibitor SAR443820, HIMALAYA phase 2 (NCT05237284; TERMINATED, "Part A did not meet the primary endpoint", last update 2025-03-21; 305 randomized). Week-24 ALSFRS-R change was -6.73 vs -6.32 (difference -0.41, 95% CI -1.71 to 0.88), with more hepatic enzyme elevations on the drug. **Tested and negative** [Cudkowicz2026; NCT05237284].
- Individualized ("N-of-1") ASO for a rare gene: in one person with CHCHD10-ALS (NCT06392126; ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING, last update 2026-07-13), plasma NfL fell about 50% and ALSFRS-R rose from 33 to 36 over 12 months. Label: **Early evidence** (n = 1) [Cousin2026; NCT06392126].

### 8. Biomarkers and trial design
- NfL as a prognostic and presymptomatic marker: in Pre-fALS, serum and CSF NfL were elevated as far back as about 12 months before the first symptoms or signs in people who phenoconverted [Benatar2018].
- NfL as a pharmacodynamic marker and surrogate: tofersen lowered plasma NfL in VALOR [Miller2022]. FDA based accelerated approval on that reduction, which makes SOD1-ALS the first ALS indication where an NfL change supported approval [FDA2023Qalsody].
- Limits of NfL as a surrogate: a 2026 scoping review (49 trials, 8 in ALS) found that NfL reductions often paralleled clinical benefit, but several studies showed "a lack of concordance". It calls for disease- and intervention-specific validation [Zheng2026]. Concrete ALS examples: BIIB105 lowered its target but not NfL [Biogen2024]. In NurOwn, the advisory committee reportedly linked greater NfL reduction to worse outcomes in the mITT population [CGTLive2023].
- Proteomics beyond NfL: 516 longitudinal plasma samples from Pre-fALS identified 92 proteins that change before phenoconversion. A 19-protein panel predicted phenoconversion over 0.5-5 years (cross-validated AUC 0.80-0.89) and outperformed NEFL alone [Ran2026].
- Stratification: the ENCALS prediction model (14 European centres; largest cohort 1936 patients) uses 8 predictors: bulbar onset, age at onset, El Escorial category, diagnostic delay, FVC, progression rate, FTD and C9orf72 expansion. It was built for counselling and "future trial design" [Westeneng2018].
- MIROCALS showed how heterogeneity can hide or reveal effects: the unadjusted and prognosis-adjusted analyses of the same primary endpoint gave different conclusions. The authors emphasise "controlling for disease heterogeneity" [Bensimon2025].
- Phase 2 reform: experts propose NfL reduction as a better go/no-go marker than short function-based outcomes, and warn against multiplicity and over-interpretation [Benatar2025e]. EXPERTS-ALS puts this into practice with NfL as its screening readout [Sheffield2026].
- Digital endpoints: wrist-worn accelerometry in 95 people with MND declined more slowly than ALSFRS-R (-0.03 to -0.07 vs -0.10 SD/month). Peak 6-minute activity predicted smaller sample sizes for trials longer than 12 months [Holdom2025]. Coached at-home vital capacity is another remote measure tested in HEALEY [Macklin2026].
- PET imaging as a trial tool: no ALS-specific PET source was read in this pass (unverified). It is left for the diagnosis or biomarker pass.

### 9. Prevention and presymptomatic carriers
- Pre-fALS (NCT00317616; status RECRUITING, last update 2026-03-20; 500 estimated; running to 2037-12) is a natural-history and biomarker study of unaffected carriers of any ALS gene variant. Its data informed ATLAS [NCT00317616; Benatar2022a].
- Mild motor impairment (MMI) as a prodromal state: among 20 Pre-fALS phenoconverters, 11 had an observable prodrome of mild motor signs. It was short (1-3.5 months) in a minority of SOD1 A4V carriers and lasted years in SOD1 I113T carriers [Benatar2022b].
- Proposed research criteria for MMI (2025): MMI was seen before diagnosis in 17 of 31 phenoconverters (55%), in 66 of 153 presymptomatic carriers (43%) and in 7 of 49 controls (14%). The 25th-percentile time to phenoconversion was 3.7 years with MMI at baseline vs 14.1 years without (HR 5.1, 95% CI 2.2-12.2). The authors note MMI is "nonspecific" [Benatar2025a].
- A 2025 workshop guidance for at-risk carriers covers genetic testing and counselling, monitoring for early signs, and use of approved drugs and gene-targeting therapies. It acknowledges that "the evidence base ... is limited" [Benatar2025c].
- C9orf72 carriers are the largest identifiable group at elevated genetic risk for both ALS and FTD. A 2024 workshop set out outcome measures, eligibility, biomarkers, digital tools and regulatory issues for C9 prevention trials [Benatar2025d].
- A 2025 review of SOD1-ALS describes NfL as a "susceptibility/risk biomarker that predicts phenoconversion" and describes the move toward prevention trials [Benatar2025b].
- Penetrance matters for prevention: in UK Biobank exomes from 470,000 people, 122 had monoallelic SOD1 coding variants, and 93.4% of them were asymptomatic. The estimated genetic prevalence of SOD1-ALS was four times the clinically reported figure [Gagliardi2026].
- What ATLAS can show: whether starting tofersen at the point of rising NfL, before symptoms, lowers the proportion who develop clinically manifest ALS within 12 months compared with placebo. Its secondary aims compare treatment before versus at clinical onset. Results are not yet available [Benatar2022a; NCT04856982].

### 10. Open questions stated in reviews
- Why some neurons are spared: oculomotor and abducens nuclei, Onuf's nucleus and dorsal column pathways stay relatively preserved. A 2026 review calls resilience mechanisms (antioxidant defence, calcium handling, glutamate receptor make-up, glial support) under-integrated and a possible therapeutic avenue [Inoue2026].
- Heterogeneity in progression and the limits of current endpoints still complicate trial design. The field is moving toward biomarker-based stratification and cryptic-exon-derived peptides as markers of TDP-43 loss of function [EstevezFraga2026].
- Whether sporadic ALS can be treated through TDP-43 itself: silencing TDP-43 is probably "not appropriate, given its critical cellular functions", which is why indirect routes (ATXN2, STMN2, UNC13A) were pursued [Becker2017]. Clinical translation of TDP-43 gain-of-function strategies "has been challenging" [EstevezFraga2026].
- Whether lowering a toxic product is enough once neurodegeneration has started: the antisense review cites irreversible downstream cascades [Ito2024], and the BIIB078 tissue study found pathology "remained abundant" despite drug distribution [McEachin2025].
- Most recent advances "have yet to translate into patient benefit" (2022 Lancet Seminar) [Feldman2022].
- Whether NfL is a valid surrogate beyond SOD1 is unresolved (see section 8) [Zheng2026; FDA2023Qalsody].

### Controversies
- Tofersen: the randomized primary endpoint was negative (P = 0.97), but approval followed on biomarker grounds, and the long-term paper calls its data a demonstration of "benefit". The clinical case rests on early-versus-delayed-start comparisons and natural-history survival comparisons rather than a placebo-controlled clinical endpoint [Miller2022; Miller2026; FDA2023Qalsody].
- CNM-Au8, pridopidine, NurOwn and MIROCALS each had a negative primary result alongside a positive exploratory, subgroup or adjusted analysis that sponsors or authors cite as grounds for further trials. Viewpoint authors warn against this pattern [Vucic2023; Geva2026; Cudkowicz2022; Bensimon2025; Benatar2025e].
- Masitinib: the phase 2/3 benefit was limited to a prespecified subgroup, regulators issued negative opinions, and the confirmatory trial was restarted under a new protocol [Mora2020; ALSNewsToday2025].
- Name discrepancy: the FUS ASO appears as ION363, jacifusen and ulefnersen, depending on date and source [Korben2022; Shneider2025; NCT07862959].

## Step sequence
Intervention points animation along gene → RNA → protein → aggregation → neuron and glia → circuit → muscle.
1. **Gene (DNA).** Silence or edit the faulty gene. AAV-delivered microRNA against SOD1 (two-patient case report; AMT-162 in a phase 1/2 safety stage): **Early evidence**. Targeting the C9orf72 DNA repeat itself: **Hypothesis** [Mueller2020; NCT06100276; Mizielinska2025].
2. **RNA (antisense).** Destroy the toxic message. Tofersen for SOD1: **Established** (biomarker; accelerated approval). Ulefnersen for FUS: **Early evidence** (positive phase 3 topline, not yet peer reviewed). C9orf72 ASOs BIIB078 and WVE-004: **Tested and negative** [Miller2022; FDA2023Qalsody; NeurologyLive2026; vandenBerg2024; NCT04931862].
3. **RNA splicing (TDP-43 targets).** Repair what TDP-43 loss breaks. STMN2 restoration (QRL-201): **Early evidence** (company interim data). UNC13A: **Hypothesis**. ATXN2 lowering (BIIB105): **Tested and negative** [QurAlis2026; Baughn2023; Ma2022; Brown2022; Biogen2024].
4. **Protein and aggregation.** Clear misfolded TDP-43 or unblock protein handling. TDP-43 antibodies (VTx-002 first-in-human; ACI-5891.9 preclinical) and PIKFYVE inhibition: **Hypothesis**. Stress-response drugs (DNL343, fosigotifator) and trehalose: **Tested and negative** [NCT07287397; Audrain2023; Hung2023; Paganoni2026; HEALEY2025Trehalose].
5. **Neuron and glia.** Protect cells and calm inflammation. Masitinib, low-dose IL-2 and methylcobalamin: **Early evidence, contested**. Zilucoplan, verdiperstat, RIPK1 inhibitor and rapamycin: **Tested and negative**. Cell therapy, NurOwn: **Tested and negative**. GDNF-secreting progenitors: **Early evidence** (safety only) [Mora2020; Bensimon2025; Oki2022; Paganoni2025; HEALEY2025Verdiperstat; Cudkowicz2026; Cudkowicz2022; Baloh2022].
6. **Circuit and muscle (measuring the effect).** Trials read out function (ALSFRS-R), breathing (vital capacity, including at home), strength and survival, together with NfL as the blood marker of nerve damage. NfL is **Established** as a prognostic marker; its role as a surrogate is accepted only for SOD1-ALS [Macklin2026; Holdom2025; Benatar2018; FDA2023Qalsody].
7. **Before symptoms (prevention).** Treat carriers when NfL starts rising. ATLAS (tofersen in presymptomatic SOD1 carriers) is ongoing with no results yet. Mild motor impairment and plasma protein panels are being developed to time treatment. Label: **Early evidence** (design and biomarkers; efficacy unknown) [Benatar2022a; NCT04856982; Benatar2025a; Ran2026].
