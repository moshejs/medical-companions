# E. Disease-modifying drugs
Scope: what drug labels, regulators, pivotal trials, guidelines and selected real-world studies report about drugs given to slow ALS. That covers riluzole, edaravone (IV and oral), sodium phenylbutyrate-taurursodiol (AMX0035), tofersen, and briefly masitinib, high-dose methylcobalamin, ropinirole, dextromethorphan-quinidine (a symptomatic drug for pseudobulbar affect) and a set of negative phase 3 trials. Sources read: PubMed and Europe PMC abstracts for every journal source; full text for EAN 2024 (PMC); US labels through openFDA (riluzole generic and Tiglutik, Radicava/Radicava ORS, Qalsody, Nuedexta); approval dates through the Drugs@FDA API; the EU Rilutek SmPC (PDF); the EMA pages for Qalsody, Rilutek, Radicava (withdrawn application), Albrioza, Alsitek and Masitinib AB Science; the full EMA Qalsody assessment report (EMA/276404/2024, PDF); the Health Canada Albrioza product monograph and the 2024 Health Canada notice; PMDA review reports for Radicut (2015) and Rozebalamin (2024); ClinicalTrials.gov records with posted results; and two Amylyx SEC 8-K press-release exhibits. Not accessible: NEJM full text for VALOR (HTTP 403), so group-level biomarker numbers come from the FDA label and the EMA assessment report; NICE pages (403), so NICE TA20 and the NICE tofersen appraisal are not covered; FDA press-release pages (404), and the Qalsody approval letter (blocked). I found no Israel Ministry of Health source for any of these drugs, so Israel is left out. The advisory-committee votes, Japan's 26 June 2015 edaravone approval date, the 24 September 2024 Rozebalamin approval date and the Health Canada masitinib refusal rest on news or secondary reports and are marked. Nothing here is advice to a reader.

## Facts

### Riluzole: trials and effect size
- Bensimon 1994 (NEJM): double-blind RCT, 155 outpatients, riluzole 100 mg/day vs placebo, stratified by bulbar or limb onset. At 12 months 74% (57/77) on riluzole were alive vs 58% (45/78) on placebo (P = 0.014). At the end of the placebo-controlled period (median follow-up 573 days) the figures were 49% vs 37% (P = 0.046) [Bensimon1994].
- Bensimon 1994, bulbar-onset subgroup: 1-year survival 73% (11/15) on riluzole vs 35% (6/17) on placebo (P = 0.014). In limb onset it was 74% vs 64% (P = 0.17). Adverse effects included asthenia, spasticity and mild aminotransferase rises; 27 people withdrew on riluzole vs 17 on placebo [Bensimon1994].
- Lacomblez 1996 (Lancet) dose-ranging trial: 959 people with probable or definite ALS of under 5 years' duration, randomised to placebo or 50, 100 or 200 mg/day. Primary outcome was tracheostomy-free survival; median follow-up 18 months [Lacomblez1996].
- Lacomblez 1996, results: 56.8% on 100 mg/day were alive without tracheostomy vs 50.4% on placebo. Unadjusted risk 0.79 (p = 0.076); adjusted for prognostic factors 0.65 (p = 0.002). Adjusted risks were 0.76 (p = 0.04) for 50 mg and 0.61 (p = 0.0004) for 200 mg. No functional scale separated the groups, and 100 mg was judged to have the best benefit-to-risk ratio [Lacomblez1996].
- Cochrane 2012: four trials, 974 people on riluzole and 503 on placebo. For riluzole 100 mg/day, the HR for tracheostomy-free survival was 0.80 (95% CI 0.64-0.99) in the two homogeneous trials, and 0.84 (95% CI 0.698-0.997; P = 0.046) when a trial of older, more advanced patients was added [Miller2012Cochrane].
- Cochrane 2012, in absolute terms: 1-year survival 49% on placebo vs 58% on riluzole (a 9-point gain). Median survival rose from 11.8 to 14.8 months; the authors conclude riluzole "probably prolongs median survival by about two to three months" [Miller2012Cochrane].
- Cochrane 2012 also found a small benefit on bulbar and limb function but none on muscle strength. A threefold ALT rise was more frequent on riluzole: the abstract reports "mean difference 2.62, 95% CI 1.59 to 4.31", which reads like a ratio measure [Miller2012Cochrane].
- The EU SmPC gives median survival of 17.7 vs 14.9 months in the 155-person trial and about 16.5 vs 13.5 months on 100 mg/day vs placebo in the 959-person trial. The Japanese 204-person trial showed no significant difference (low power), and a trial in late-stage disease (most people with vital capacity under 60%) showed no benefit [EMA_Rilutek].
- The US label describes the same two studies differently. In Study 1, the protocol-specified log-rank test was not significant (p = 0.12; Wilcoxon p = 0.05), with a difference in median time to tracheostomy or death of about 90 days. In Study 2 the log-rank p was 0.076 (Wilcoxon p = 0.05), with about 60 days. Neither study showed benefit on strength or neurological function [FDA_RiluzoleLabel].
- Andrews 2020 reviewed 15 population studies: 8 of them reported median survival 6-19 months longer in people treated with riluzole than in those not treated. That is well above the 2-3 months seen in the pivotal RCTs [Andrews2020].

### Riluzole: label, dose, monitoring, formulations, pregnancy
- US indication: "treatment of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)". Dose: 50 mg by mouth twice daily, at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal [FDA_RiluzoleLabel].
- EU indication: "to extend life or the time to mechanical ventilation" in ALS. The SmPC states there is no evidence of effect on motor function, lung function, fasciculations or muscle strength, and no shown effect in late-stage ALS. Treatment is to be started by a specialist [EMA_Rilutek].
- US monitoring: aminotransferases before and during treatment; monitor for hepatic injury every month for the first 3 months and periodically after that. Use is not recommended if transaminases exceed 5 x ULN, and riluzole is stopped if there is evidence of liver dysfunction such as raised bilirubin [FDA_RiluzoleLabel].
- EU monitoring is more specific: ALT every month for the first 3 months, every 3 months for the rest of the first year, then periodically. Riluzole is stopped if ALT reaches 5 x ULN. It is contraindicated in hepatic disease or with baseline transaminases above 3 x ULN [EMA_Rilutek].
- In the pooled US trials, about 50% of people on riluzole had at least one ALT above ULN and 8% above 3 x ULN; 2% had ALT above 5 x ULN, and the peak rise came within 3 months [FDA_RiluzoleLabel].
- Common adverse reactions in the pooled trials (riluzole n = 313 vs placebo n = 320): asthenia 19% vs 12%, nausea 16% vs 11%, decreased lung function 10% vs 9%, abdominal pain 5% vs 4%. Dizziness was 11% in women vs 4% in men [FDA_RiluzoleLabel].
- Label warnings: severe neutropenia (ANC < 500/mm3) within the first 2 months, so people are told to report febrile illness; interstitial lung disease including hypersensitivity pneumonitis (stop riluzole); acute pancreatitis, including fatal necrotizing cases, weeks to years after starting [FDA_RiluzoleLabel]. The EU SmPC adds a chest X-ray if dry cough or dyspnoea develops [EMA_Rilutek].
- Formulations approved by FDA: Rilutek tablet on 12 December 1995 (Drugs@FDA now lists the brand as discontinued); Tiglutik oral suspension (5 mg/mL; 50 mg = 10 mL twice daily, by mouth or PEG tube) on 5 September 2018; Exservan oral film on 22 November 2019 (now listed as discontinued) [DrugsFDA; FDA_TiglutikLabel].
- Pregnancy and lactation: the US label says, based on animal data, riluzole may cause fetal harm, and it is not known whether it passes into human milk [FDA_RiluzoleLabel]. The EU SmPC contraindicates Rilutek in pregnancy and breast-feeding [EMA_Rilutek].
- Guidelines: AAN 2009 says riluzole should be offered to slow progression (Level A) [Miller2009AAN]; EFNS 2012 says to start riluzole as early as possible [Andersen2012EFNS]; EAN 2024 says "Offer lifelong riluzole to all people with ALS at diagnosis" (strong), with dose reduction and then stopping considered if adverse events persist [VanDamme2024EAN].

### Edaravone: trials
- Mechanism: PMDA describes edaravone as a free-radical scavenger, discovered in 1984 and approved in Japan for acute ischaemic stroke since 2001 [PMDA2015Radicut]. The US label states its mechanism in ALS is unknown [FDA_RadicavaLabel].
- MCI186-16 (first phase 3, Japan, 24 weeks): ALSFRS-R change -5.70 on edaravone (n = 100) vs -6.35 on placebo (n = 99), difference 0.65 ± 0.78 (p = 0.411). Adverse events 89.2% vs 88.5% [Abe2014].
- MCI186-16 post hoc: the difference was 2.20 (p = 0.036) in the "efficacy-expected subpopulation" (FVC at least 80%, every ALSFRS-R item at least 2) and 3.01 (p = 0.027) in the narrower dpEESP2y subgroup (also definite or probable ALS with onset within 2 years). These became the MCI186-19 entry criteria [EdaravoneALS16Group2017].
- MCI186-18 (Japan ALS severity grade 3, n = 25): ALSFRS-R change -6.52 on edaravone vs -6.00 on placebo (p = 0.835), so no benefit in more advanced disease [EdaravoneALS18Group2017].
- MCI186-19 entry criteria: age 20-75, Japan ALS Severity grade 1-2, every ALSFRS-R item at least 2, FVC at least 80%, definite or probable ALS, duration 2 years or less, and an ALSFRS-R fall of 1-4 points over a 12-week run-in. Of 192 enrolled, 137 were randomised (69 edaravone, 68 placebo) [MCI186Group2017].
- MCI186-19 regimen: 60 mg IV or saline for six 28-day cycles; daily for 14 days in cycle 1, then 10 of 14 days in later cycles, each followed by 14 days off [MCI186Group2017].
- MCI186-19 result at 24 weeks: ALSFRS-R change -5.01 (SE 0.64) on edaravone vs -7.50 (SE 0.66) on placebo, difference 2.49 (95% CI 0.99-3.98; p = 0.0013). This is roughly a third less decline over 24 weeks, in a selected early-stage group; more than 90% of each arm were also taking riluzole [MCI186Group2017; FDA_RadicavaLabel].
- MCI186-19 safety: treatment-emergent adverse events 84% in both arms; serious adverse events 16% on edaravone vs 24% on placebo. The authors wrote there was "no indication" of efficacy in a wider ALS population outside the criteria [MCI186Group2017].
- Witzel 2022 (Germany, 12 centres, propensity-matched): disease progression did not differ between 116 people on IV edaravone plus riluzole and 116 on standard therapy (-0.91 vs -0.85 ALSFRS-R points/month; P = 0.37). Survival and time to ventilation also did not differ, including in people who met MCI186-19 criteria. Possible adverse effects occurred in 16%, mostly infusion-site infections and allergic reactions [Witzel2022].
- Oral edaravone FAB122 (ADORE; Ferrer; NCT05178810): 313 randomised 2:1 to daily oral FAB122 or placebo. ALSFRS-R change at 48 weeks was -11.2 (SD 7.9; n = 171) vs -10.8 (SD 8.4; n = 76), and the primary endpoint was not met [CTgov_ADORE].
- MT-1186-A02 (NCT04569084; n = 384): compared once-daily oral edaravone 105 mg with the approved on/off regimen. CAFS at 48 weeks was 187.2 vs 184.2 (p = 0.777), and the study was terminated because "futility was met" [CTgov_MT1186A02]. The A04 extension (up to 96 weeks in total) also found no difference in time to a 12-point ALSFRS-R fall or death (p = 0.78) [Genge2026].
- MT-1186-A03 (open-label extension of A01, up to 144 weeks in total, on/off regimen): no new safety concerns. The commonest adverse events were fall, muscular weakness, dyspnoea, constipation and dysphagia [Genge2025].

### Edaravone: label, regulators
- US approvals: Radicava IV on 5 May 2017 (NDA 209176) and Radicava ORS oral suspension on 12 May 2022 (NDA 215446, priority review). Drugs@FDA currently lists the IV Radicava product as discontinued and ORS as prescription [DrugsFDA].
- US indication: "treatment of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)", with no restriction to the MCI186-19 population [FDA_RadicavaLabel].
- US dose: IV 60 mg over 60 minutes, or ORS 105 mg (5 mL) by mouth or feeding tube in the morning after an overnight fast, with no food for 1 hour afterwards. Cycle 1 is daily for 14 days then 14 days off; later cycles are 10 of 14 days then 14 days off [FDA_RadicavaLabel].
- ORS was approved on a bioavailability bridge: 105 mg oral given fasting gave an AUC equivalent to 60 mg IV. People can switch from IV to ORS at the same dosing frequency [FDA_RadicavaLabel].
- Adverse reactions in pooled placebo-controlled trials (n = 184 each arm): contusion 15% vs 9%, gait disturbance 13% vs 9%, headache 10% vs 6%, dermatitis 8% vs 5%, eczema 7% vs 4%. Fatigue occurred in 7.6% with ORS in an open-label 6-month study (n = 185) [FDA_RadicavaLabel].
- Warnings: hypersensitivity reactions and anaphylaxis (postmarketing). Both IV and ORS contain sodium bisulfite, which may cause allergic-type reactions including anaphylaxis and asthmatic episodes in susceptible (more often asthmatic) people. Contraindicated with prior hypersensitivity to edaravone or its ingredients [FDA_RadicavaLabel].
- Pregnancy and lactation: in rats and rabbits edaravone caused adverse developmental effects at clinically relevant doses, and it passes into rat milk; there are no human data [FDA_RadicavaLabel].
- Japan: the PMDA First Committee on New Drugs backed the ALS indication on 5 June 2015 (report dated 11 June 2015) [PMDA2015Radicut]. The approval date of 26 June 2015 is from a secondary news report (unverified at regulator).
- Health Canada: Notice of Compliance for Radicava issued 3 October 2018, announced 4 October 2018 (Summary Basis of Decision and Health Canada release, read via search summary) [HC2018Radicava].
- EMA: Mitsubishi Tanabe withdrew its marketing application on 24 May 2019. CHMP's provisional view was that Radicava "could not have been approved", citing a small single main study, imbalance favouring milder disease in the edaravone arm, a 24-week duration too short to show durable benefit, and an uninterpretable registry comparison [EMA2019Radicava].
- EAN 2024 recommends against IV or oral edaravone outside a clinical trial (interim recommendation, pending the European oral edaravone phase 3) [VanDamme2024EAN].

### Sodium phenylbutyrate-taurursodiol (AMX0035; Relyvrio in the US, Albrioza in Canada)
- CENTAUR (phase 2, NCT03127514): people with definite ALS and symptom onset within 18 months, randomised 2:1 (89 active, 48 placebo). Dose: 3 g sodium phenylbutyrate + 1 g taurursodiol once daily for 3 weeks, then twice daily [Paganoni2020].
- CENTAUR primary result over 24 weeks: ALSFRS-R slope -1.24 points/month on active drug vs -1.66 on placebo, difference 0.42 points/month (95% CI 0.03-0.81; P = 0.03). Secondary outcomes (strength, plasma pNfH, slow vital capacity, time to death/tracheostomy/permanent ventilation) did not differ. Adverse events were mainly gastrointestinal [Paganoni2020].
- CENTAUR long-term survival (open-label extension, up to 35 months, vital status for 135/137): median survival 25.0 months for those first randomised to active drug vs 18.5 months for placebo (HR 0.56, 95% CI 0.34-0.92; P = 0.023). Placebo participants could switch to active drug, so this compares early with later starting [Paganoni2021].
- Health Canada: initial authorisation with conditions (NOC/c) dated 1 June 2022, conditional on a phase 3 confirmatory trial. The May 2024 monograph says not to start it in new patients and that it will leave the Canadian market no later than 31 December 2024 [HC_Albrioza].
- FDA: Relyvrio approved 29 September 2022 (NDA 216660, priority review); Drugs@FDA now lists it as discontinued [DrugsFDA]. The advisory committee voted 6-4 against on 30 March 2022 and 7-2 for on 7 September 2022 (news reports; unverified at FDA) [News_AdcommAMX0035].
- EMA: initial negative opinion 22 June 2023, confirmed on re-examination 12 October 2023; EU refusal 5 January 2024. Reasons: the main study did not convincingly show slowing, and survival data were judged unreliable because of how they were collected and analysed [EMA2023Albrioza].
- PHOENIX (phase 3, NCT05021536; 664 adults, 3:2, 48 weeks): announced 8 March 2024. It missed its primary endpoint (ALSFRS-R change at week 48, p = 0.667) and all secondary endpoints, with no new safety signals [Amylyx2024; CTgov_PHOENIX]. Per-arm numbers had not been posted on ClinicalTrials.gov when checked.
- Withdrawal: on 4 April 2024 Amylyx began voluntary withdrawal of the US and Canadian authorisations, stopped new starts that day, and moved existing users to a free-drug programme [Amylyx2024]. Health Canada's 7 June 2024 notice confirms withdrawal from Canada by 31 December 2024 [HC_Albrioza].
- EAN 2024 (written before PHOENIX) did not recommend AMX0035 outside trials until phase 3 data were available [VanDamme2024EAN].

### Tofersen (Qalsody): mechanism and label
- Molecule: a 20-mer 5-10-5 2'-O-methoxyethyl (MOE) gapmer antisense oligonucleotide, with 10 central 2'-deoxynucleosides flanked by 5 MOE residues at each end and a mixed phosphorothioate/phosphodiester backbone [FDA2023Qalsody].
- Mechanism: tofersen binds SOD1 mRNA and causes its degradation, which reduces SOD1 protein synthesis [FDA2023Qalsody]. The EMA assessment describes the degradation as RNase H-dependent [EMA2024Qalsody].
- US indication: ALS in adults with a SOD1 mutation, under accelerated approval "based on reduction in plasma neurofilament light chain". Continued approval may depend on confirmatory trial(s) [FDA2023Qalsody].
- Dose (confirmed): 100 mg (15 mL) intrathecal bolus over 1-3 minutes by lumbar puncture, after removing about 10 mL of CSF. Three loading doses 14 days apart, then one every 28 days. If the second loading dose is missed, it is given as soon as possible and the third 14 days later [FDA2023Qalsody]. EMA states the same schedule [EMA2024Qalsody].
- Pregnancy and lactation: no adequate human data. Mouse and rabbit studies at about 4-20x human exposure showed no embryofetal effects. Tofersen was found in mouse milk, and the label asks for the benefits of breastfeeding to be weighed against the mother's need for the drug [FDA2023Qalsody].

### Tofersen: VALOR trial (phase 3, NCT02623699)
- Design: adults with SOD1-ALS randomised 2:1 to 8 doses of tofersen 100 mg (n = 72) or placebo (n = 36) over 24 weeks. Primary endpoint: change in ALSFRS-R to week 28 in the predicted faster-progressing group (mITT, n = 60: 39 tofersen, 21 placebo), defined by pre-randomisation ALSFRS-R slope and SOD1 variant type [Miller2022; FDA2023Qalsody].
- Baseline: mean ALSFRS-R 36.9 (tofersen) vs 37.3 (placebo); median time from onset 11.4 vs 14.6 months. 62% were taking riluzole and 8% edaravone [FDA2023Qalsody].
- Primary result, faster-progressing group: ALSFRS-R change -6.98 on tofersen vs -8.14 on placebo, difference 1.2 points (95% CI -3.2 to 5.5; P = 0.97). Not met [Miller2022].
- Slower-progressing group (non-mITT, n = 48): -1.33 vs -2.73, difference 1.4 (95% CI -1.1 to 3.9). ITT (n = 108): -4.5 vs -5.8, difference 1.4 (95% CI -1.3 to 4.1). Neither was significant [EMA2024Qalsody].
- CSF SOD1 at week 28, as adjusted geometric mean ratio to baseline: ITT 0.65 tofersen vs 0.98 placebo (-35% vs -2%; between-group ratio 0.66, 95% CI 0.57-0.77). Faster group: 0.71 vs 1.16 (-29% vs +16%; ratio 0.62, 95% CI 0.49-0.78) [FDA2023Qalsody]. Slower group: a 26% relative reduction vs placebo (nominal p = 0.0007) [EMA2024Qalsody].
- Plasma NfL at week 28: ITT 0.45 vs 1.12 (-55% vs +12%; ratio 0.40, 95% CI 0.33-0.49). Faster group: 0.40 vs 1.20 (-60% vs +20%; ratio 0.33, 95% CI 0.25-0.45) [FDA2023Qalsody]. Slower group: a 48% relative reduction vs placebo (nominal p < 0.0001) [EMA2024Qalsody].
- Time course: plasma NfL fell until about day 113 (around week 16) and then stayed down; pNfH and CSF neurofilament fell by similar amounts [FDA2023Qalsody].
- Death or permanent ventilation by week 28 (faster group): 4/39 (10.3%) on tofersen vs 2/21 (9.5%) on placebo, too few events to interpret [EMA2024Qalsody].
- 52-week integrated analysis (95/108, 88%, entered the open-label extension): ALSFRS-R change -6.0 with early start vs -9.5 with delayed start, difference 3.5 (95% CI 0.4-6.7; nominal p = 0.027, not adjusted for multiplicity) [Miller2022; EMA2024Qalsody].
- The label notes that open-label comparisons "should be interpreted with caution" because of possible confounding. Trends for slow vital capacity, hand-held dynamometry and death or permanent ventilation also favoured early start but were not significant [FDA2023Qalsody].
- Final open-label analysis (follow-up up to 192-276 weeks; 46 people completed): over 148 weeks, early vs delayed start showed ALSFRS-R -9.9 vs -13.5, slow vital capacity -13.8% vs -18.1% and ALSAQ-5 17.0 vs 22.5. The authors report survival longer than the expected natural history of SOD1-ALS; every serious neurological adverse event was reversible [Miller2026].

### Tofersen: safety (label and trials)
- Common adverse reactions in VALOR (tofersen n = 72 vs placebo n = 36): pain 42% vs 22%, fatigue 17% vs 6%, arthralgia 14% vs 6%, CSF white-cell increase 14% vs 0%, myalgia 14% vs 6%, CSF protein increase 8% vs 3%. Pyrexia occurred with repeated dosing in the extension [FDA2023Qalsody].
- Lumbar-puncture-related events were similar in both arms of VALOR (each 80.6%), e.g. procedural pain 56.9% vs 58.3% and post-LP syndrome 18.1% vs 30.6%. In all 147 people given 100 mg, procedural pain occurred in 57.8% and post-LP syndrome in 24.5% [EMA2024Qalsody].
- Myelitis and/or radiculitis (label, 147 people, median exposure 119.4 weeks): 6 people; 2 stopped tofersen and recovered fully, and 4 recovered without stopping [FDA2023Qalsody].
- Papilloedema and/or raised intracranial pressure: 4 people, all resolved with standard care and none stopped tofersen [FDA2023Qalsody].
- Aseptic or chemical meningitis: 2 serious cases (1 stopped tofersen) [FDA2023Qalsody]. EMA counts of all events, serious or not, out of 147: myelitis 4 (2.7%), radiculitis 4 (2.7%), aseptic meningitis 6 (4.1%), papilloedema 7 (4.8%) [EMA2024Qalsody].
- Across the trials, 10 people (about 7% of those given 100 mg) had 12 serious neurological events: 4 myelitis, 2 radiculitis, 2 aseptic meningitis, 4 intracranial hypertension and/or papilloedema. None was life-threatening or fatal, 3 led to stopping tofersen, and all but one resolved [Lovett2025]. The VALOR paper reports neurological serious adverse events in 7% of tofersen recipients [Miller2022].

### Tofersen: regulators and real-world use
- FDA accelerated approval 25 April 2023 (NDA 215887, priority review) [DrugsFDA]. The sponsor named the presymptomatic phase 3 ATLAS trial (NCT04856982) as the confirmatory study [Biogen2023]. ATLAS: 158 enrolled, primary endpoint time to clinically manifest ALS, estimated primary completion August 2027 [CTgov_ATLAS].
- FDA advisory committee, 22 March 2023: 9-0 that NfL reduction is reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit, but 3 yes, 5 no and 1 abstention on whether the clinical data were substantial evidence of effectiveness (news reports; unverified at FDA) [News_AdcommTofersen].
- EMA: positive CHMP opinion 22 February 2024; EU authorisation 29 May 2024 under exceptional circumstances. Obligations include long-term data, a study in presymptomatic carriers and a registry with yearly reports [EMA2024Qalsody].
- Health Canada: NOC with conditions on 28 February 2025 (Summary Basis of Decision; read via search summary) [HC2025Qalsody].
- EAN 2024: "offer tofersen as first-line treatment" for progressive SOD1-ALS (strong), discuss serious adverse events, and weigh benefit and treatment burden carefully in slow progressors [VanDamme2024EAN].
- German early access (6 people, case series): neurofilament fell clearly during tofersen, including in slower-progressing p.(Asp91Ala) carriers [Meyer2023].
- German early access 12-month cohort (24 people, 10 centres): median serum NfL fell from 78 to 36 pg/mL (p = 0.02) and CSF pNfH from 2226 to 1151 pg/mL (p = 0.02). Median ALSFRS-R went from 38 to 35, a progression rate of 0.11 points/month [Wiesenfarth2024].
- The same cohort found CSF pleocytosis in 73% (11/15) and intrathecal immunoglobulin synthesis in 9/10. There were two drug-related serious adverse events with myeloradiculitis symptoms, which the authors read as a possible autoimmune reaction [Wiesenfarth2024].
- Single US centre (7 people): mean serum NfL -57.9% and CSF pNfH -67.6%, with mean ALSFRS-R change +1.1 [Smith2025].
- French multicentre cohort (FORSLA, 46 people): progression rate slowed from 0.53 to 0.22 points/month by 12 months. Against a propensity-matched historical cohort, mortality was lower (adjusted HR 0.34, 95% CI 0.12-0.91), but progression rate did not differ (P = 0.30); the design is retrospective [Erazo2026].

### Other agents
- Masitinib (tyrosine-kinase inhibitor) trial AB10015: 394 people on riluzole randomised to placebo or 3.0 or 4.5 mg/kg/day. In the prespecified "normal progressor" population, 4.5 mg/kg/day gave an ALSFRS-R difference of 3.4 (95% CI 0.65-6.13). There was no effect in the broader cohort including fast progressors, and serious adverse events were 31% vs 18% on placebo [Mora2020].
- Masitinib regulators: EMA (Alsitek) negative opinion 18 April 2018 and refusal 26 July 2018, citing inspection findings at two sites and an "arbitrary" patient grouping. A new application (Masitinib AB Science) got a negative opinion 27 June 2024, confirmed 17 October 2024, refusal 19 December 2024. Health Canada refused conditional approval in February 2024 (news report; unverified at regulator) [EMA_Masitinib].
- High-dose methylcobalamin (JETALS, Japan): 130 people within 1 year of onset given 50 mg IM twice weekly for 16 weeks. ALSFRS-R change -2.66 vs -4.63, difference 1.97 (95% CI 0.44-3.50; P = .01) [Oki2022].
- Methylcobalamin regulators: the Japanese MHLW committee backed Rozebalamin for Injection 25 mg on 26 August 2024 for slowing functional impairment in ALS (50 mg IM twice weekly, orphan drug) [PMDA2024Rozebalamin]. The 24 September 2024 approval date is from a news report (unverified at regulator). It is not approved by FDA or EMA (no record found).
- Ropinirole (ROPALS, phase 1/2a, 20 people with sporadic ALS, 24 weeks double-blind): ALSFRS-R decline did not differ from placebo during the blinded period. In the open-label extension it reported 27.9 more weeks of progression-free survival. The authors note small numbers and high dropout [Morimoto2023]. Ropinirole is not approved for ALS.
- Dextromethorphan-quinidine treats pseudobulbar affect and is not disease-modifying. In ALS (n = 140), the combination improved CNS-LS by 3.3 points more than dextromethorphan alone and 3.7 more than quinidine alone over 28 days [Brooks2004]. FDA approved Nuedexta for pseudobulbar affect on 29 October 2010; label dose is one capsule daily for 7 days, then every 12 hours [DrugsFDA; FDA_NuedextaLabel].

### Controversies
- Riluzole effect size: RCTs and Cochrane give about 2-3 months of median survival, while observational studies report 6-19 months. Observational comparisons are prone to confounding by indication and immortal-time bias, so the RCT figure remains the reference estimate [Miller2012Cochrane; Andrews2020]. The US label and the NEJM abstract also differ on Study 1 (log-rank p = 0.12 vs p = 0.014 at 12 months), because they use different endpoints and analyses [FDA_RiluzoleLabel; Bensimon1994].
- Edaravone approval was split by region: approved in Japan, the US and Canada on one positive 137-person trial in a population chosen by post hoc analysis; the EU application was withdrawn; EAN does not recommend it. The German propensity-matched cohort, ADORE and MT-1186-A02 found no added benefit [MCI186Group2017; EMA2019Radicava; Witzel2022; CTgov_ADORE; VanDamme2024EAN].
- AMX0035 is an example of a phase 2 result not confirmed in phase 3. CENTAUR's P = 0.03 slope difference and its open-label survival analysis supported approval in Canada and the US, while EMA judged the survival data unreliable. PHOENIX (664 people) was negative, and the drug was withdrawn about 19 months after US approval [Paganoni2020; Paganoni2021; EMA2023Albrioza; Amylyx2024].
- Tofersen rests on a surrogate endpoint. VALOR missed its clinical primary endpoint; FDA approval relied on NfL reduction as reasonably likely to predict benefit, and the EMA authorisation was under exceptional circumstances. The 52-week and 148-week early-vs-delayed comparisons are open-label and nominal, and the label urges caution about confounding [Miller2022; FDA2023Qalsody; EMA2024Qalsody; Miller2026].
- Tofersen's inflammatory adverse events (myelitis, radiculitis, aseptic meningitis, CSF pleocytosis in 73% of one cohort) have no precedent with other ASOs. Lovett 2025 notes that myelitis, radiculitis, raised intracranial pressure and papilloedema had not been reported with any ASO [Lovett2025; Wiesenfarth2024].
- Masitinib's positive result came from a prespecified but narrowed "normal progressor" population. EMA refused it twice, citing data-integrity (GCP) findings and methods [Mora2020; EMA_Masitinib].

## Step sequence
1. SOD1-ALS: a disease-causing variant in the SOD1 gene of the motor neuron is transcribed to SOD1 mRNA, which is translated into SOD1 protein. EMA's assessment says the more than 200 ALS-linked SOD1 variants are assumed to act by toxic gain of function [EMA2024Qalsody].
2. Delivery: 100 mg tofersen in 15 mL is injected into the lumbar CSF by lumbar puncture over 1-3 minutes, after about 10 mL of CSF is removed. Loading doses fall on days 1, 15 and 29, then every 28 days; VALOR gave 8 doses over 24 weeks [FDA2023Qalsody; Miller2022].
3. The strand: a 20-mer 5-10-5 MOE gapmer, with 2'-MOE "wings" around a 10-nucleotide DNA gap [FDA2023Qalsody].
4. Binding: tofersen pairs base-by-base with SOD1 mRNA, forming an RNA:DNA hybrid at the gap [FDA2023Qalsody; EMA2024Qalsody].
5. RNase H cleavage: RNase H cuts the RNA strand of the hybrid, so the SOD1 message is destroyed and less SOD1 protein is made [EMA2024Qalsody; FDA2023Qalsody].
6. CSF SOD1 protein falls: by week 28, 0.65x baseline on tofersen vs 0.98x on placebo (ITT, -35% vs -2%); faster-progressing group 0.71x vs 1.16x [FDA2023Qalsody].
7. Plasma NfL falls more steeply and plateaus: it declines until about day 113, then holds. At week 28 it is 0.45x baseline vs 1.12x (ITT); 0.40x vs 1.20x in the faster group; 48% lower than placebo in the slower group [FDA2023Qalsody; EMA2024Qalsody].
8. Clinical endpoint at week 28 (faster group): ALSFRS-R -6.98 vs -8.14, difference 1.2 (95% CI -3.2 to 5.5; P = 0.97). The primary endpoint was not met [Miller2022].
9. Open-label extension at week 52: placebo switchers' NfL also falls; ALSFRS-R is -6.0 with early start vs -9.5 with delayed start (difference 3.5, 95% CI 0.4-6.7, nominal) [Miller2022; FDA2023Qalsody].
10. Approval and beyond: FDA accelerated approval on 25 April 2023 based on NfL, with ATLAS (presymptomatic carriers) as confirmatory; EU authorisation on 29 May 2024 under exceptional circumstances. At 148 weeks, ALSFRS-R is -9.9 with early vs -13.5 with delayed start (open-label) [DrugsFDA; Biogen2023; EMA2024Qalsody; Miller2026].

### Notes on verification
- All 33 journal sources were checked in one batched NCBI esummary call (first author, title, journal, year, volume, issue, pages, DOI, PMCID) and agree with the Europe PMC records used to read the abstracts. Titles are copied from the registry.
- Shared keys used: Bensimon1994, Lacomblez1996 (the Lancet 1996 paper, PMID 8676624; there is also a Neurology 1996 supplement paper of the same trial, PMID 8959996, which is not cited), Miller2012Cochrane, MCI186Group2017, Paganoni2020, Miller2022, FDA2023Qalsody, EMA2024Qalsody, Miller2009AAN and Andersen2012EFNS. vanEs2017, Feldman2022 and NICE2016 are not cited: the van Es and Feldman abstracts contain no drug-specific numbers and their full text was not available; NICE pages returned 403.
- FDA2023Qalsody points to the current (Nov 2024) label, not the April 2023 original; the dose and warnings are unchanged, but the safety denominator (147 people, median 119.4 weeks) is from the later label.
- Seed corrections against the dossier: AMX0035 EMA was a negative opinion in June 2023 confirmed in October 2023 with EU refusal on 5 Jan 2024 (the seed said "2024"); Health Canada's Albrioza authorisation is dated 1 June 2022; the US/Canada withdrawal began 4 April 2024, with Canada exit by 31 Dec 2024. Riluzole "2-3 months", the edaravone MCI186-19 numbers (-5.01 vs -7.50, difference 2.49), CENTAUR 0.42 points/month, the tofersen dose schedule, FDA 25 Apr 2023 and EU 29 May 2024 (exceptional circumstances) were all confirmed.
- Two things that are easy to miscite: (1) the riluzole monitoring schedule differs between the US label (monthly for 3 months, then periodically) and the EU SmPC (monthly for 3 months, then every 3 months to the end of year 1); (2) VALOR's widely quoted "-29% vs +16%" CSF SOD1 and "-60% vs +20%" NfL figures belong to the faster-progressing (mITT) group, while the label's headline "35%" and "55%" figures are for the ITT population.
- The Cochrane abstract labels the ALT effect as a "mean difference" of 2.62 (1.59-4.31); its asymmetric CI suggests a ratio measure. It is reported here as written.
