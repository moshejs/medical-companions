Toward a cure

Researchers are trying to slow, stop or prevent ALS at every step from the faulty gene to the weakening muscle. Press Play to move along the pathway; each step lights the routes that act there. Gene (DNA)

Silence or edit the faulty gene

A gene therapy can deliver a microRNA that silences SOD1 from inside cells. In a first-in-human report, two people with SOD1-ALS received one intrathecal AAV infusion. In the first, spinal cord SOD1 at autopsy was lower than in untreated SOD1-ALS and meningoradiculitis occurred; the second, pretreated with immunosuppression, had stable function over 12 months 352. AMT-162 uses the same idea and is in a phase 1/2 safety stage with no efficacy data 381. Targeting the C9orf72 DNA repeat itself is a hypothesis 338.

In plain wordsThe first point of attack is the faulty gene itself: switch it off before it makes anything harmful.
Exam hookGene-level routes so far rest on a two-patient report and a safety-stage trial: early evidence at most 352,381.
Routes acting on the gene
  • AAV microRNA (SOD1): early evidence
  • AMT-162 (SOD1): hypothesis
  • C9orf72 DNA repeat: hypothesis

Reading the labels. Established a randomised trial or a regulatory approval shows an effect on a biomarker or a clinical outcome. Early evidence case series, small or uncontrolled early human data, or a positive topline result not yet peer reviewed. Hypothesis laboratory or animal data only. Tested and negative an adequately sized randomised trial, or a registry termination, showed no benefit. ALS has a long history of promising phase 2 results that then failed in phase 3. 56

Route by route

Trial status and last-update dates as read from ClinicalTrials.gov on 20 October 2026. The filter above also applies here.

SOD1: tofersen, ATLAS and gene therapy

EstablishedTofersen (Qalsody), intrathecal antisense

In VALOR, 108 adults with SOD1-ALS were randomised 2:1 to tofersen or placebo. In the faster-progression group the week-28 ALSFRS-R difference was 1.2 points (95% CI -3.2 to 5.5, P = 0.97), so the primary endpoint was not met, while CSF SOD1 and plasma NfL fell more with tofersen 335,359. Over 148 weeks, early start was associated with numerically less decline than delayed start (ALSFRS-R -9.9 vs -13.5 points); survival was compared with natural history, not with a concurrent placebo group 336,361. The US approval is accelerated, based on reduction in plasma NfL, and the EU authorisation of 29 May 2024 is under exceptional circumstances 160,172.

VALOR · NCT02623699 · Completed · last update 2023-07-28

Open-label extension · NCT03070119 · Completed · last update 2025-08-29

Early evidence: design, no resultsATLAS: tofersen before symptoms

A phase 3 trial in clinically presymptomatic adult carriers of high-penetrance, rapidly progressive SOD1 variants. Part A measures plasma NfL monthly. A carrier is randomised 1:1 to tofersen or placebo (Part B, about 28 planned) when NfL is at least 44 pg/mL and has risen by at least 10 pg/mL from baseline while still presymptomatic. The primary endpoint is the proportion who develop clinically manifest ALS within 12 months, and anyone who does receives open-label tofersen 50. 158 people are enrolled, estimated completion is 30 April 2028, and no efficacy results were found in any published or press source as of 20 October 2026 373.

ATLAS · NCT04856982 · Active, not recruiting · last update 2026-10-20

Early evidenceAAV microRNA against SOD1 (two patients)

Two people with SOD1-ALS received one intrathecal infusion of an AAV encoding a microRNA against SOD1. In patient 1, spinal cord SOD1 at autopsy was lower than in untreated SOD1-ALS, CSF SOD1 fell only slightly and briefly, and meningoradiculitis occurred. Patient 2, pretreated with immunosuppression, had stable function over 12 months 352.

Hypothesis to early evidence: safety stageAMT-162 (AAVrh10 microRNA)

EPISOD1 is a phase 1/2 single ascending dose study with 20 participants, started on 1 August 2024. No efficacy data were found 381.

EPISOD1 · NCT06100276 · Active, not recruiting · last update 2025-10-22

C9orf72: the most common genetic cause

Why it has been hard: the G4C2 repeat is read in both directions, makes several dipeptide repeat proteins and causes a mix of loss and gain of function. A 2025 review suggests ASO trials may have failed because they targeted a single gain-of-function mechanism 338.

Tested and negativeBIIB078 (sense-strand antisense)

106 adults with C9orf72-ALS received intrathecal BIIB078 (5-90 mg) or placebo. Serious adverse events occurred in 18% on BIIB078 and 33% on placebo. There was no reduction in neurofilament levels and no benefit on clinical outcomes, and development was discontinued 365,509. In tissue from treated people, CSF dipeptide repeat proteins fell, but these proteins and phosphorylated TDP-43 remained abundant in the brain 324.

BIIB078 phase 1 · NCT03626012 · Completed · last update 2022-01-14

Tested and negativeWVE-004 (FOCUS-C9)

Terminated with 35 enrolled. The registry reason reads "Despite robust, sustained reductions in poly(GP), no clinical benefit was seen at 24 weeks"; the company reported maximal mean poly(GP) reductions of 48-51% 374,531.

FOCUS-C9 · NCT04931862 · Terminated · last update 2023-10-23

Early evidence: one person, biomarker onlyAn optimised C9orf72 antisense in one person

Repeated intrathecal dosing was well tolerated with significant falls in CSF poly(GP). The authors state that further trials are needed to show safety and efficacy 496.

Early evidence, pendingApilimod (PIKFYVE inhibitor) in C9orf72-ALS

A phase 2a with 15 enrolled; results are posted to the registry, and no peer-reviewed report was found 375.

LAM-002A · NCT05163886 · Completed · last update 2026-09-29

HypothesisOther C9 approaches

Targeting the DNA repeat itself, several repeat-derived RNAs at once, or downstream TDP-43 dysfunction are described as "on the horizon" 338. Small molecules that stabilise the repeat RNA reduced RNA foci and repeat proteins in patient-derived neurons and flies 475, and antibodies against poly-GA improved behaviour and survival in mice 399.

ATXN2: lowering ataxin-2

HypothesisThe idea, in mice

Reducing ataxin-2, by knockout or a single CNS dose of ASO, reduced TDP-43 aggregation and markedly extended survival in TDP-43 transgenic mice 44.

Tested and negativeBIIB105 (ION541), ALSpire

In the companies' 16 May 2024 release, CSF ATXN2 fell significantly, but plasma NfL, function, breathing and strength did not improve, including in people with an ATXN2 poly-CAG expansion. Discontinuation for adverse events was 8.3% vs 3.6% on placebo. The trial was terminated by sponsor decision with 99 enrolled 62,371.

ALSpire · NCT04494256 · Terminated · last update 2025-10-09

FUS: ulefnersen

The same antisense drug appears as ION363, jacifusen and ulefnersen, depending on date and source 271,389,469.

Early evidenceFrom mice to a case series

In FUS knock-in mice the ASO silenced Fus and delayed motor neuron degeneration, and in one person with a FUS P525L variant repeated intrathecal dosing lowered FUS and markedly reduced aggregate burden 271. In an expanded-access series of 12 participants (median age 26), CSF NfL fell by up to 82.8% after 6 months. Most kept declining on ALSFRS-R, one showed objective functional recovery after 10 months and another stayed without symptoms; two deaths were judged unrelated 469.

Early evidence: topline from a news report, not peer reviewedFUSION phase 1-3

Randomised, double-blind and placebo-controlled for 72 weeks, then open-label, with 89 enrolled 372. On 22 September 2026, a news report of the companies' announcement said the primary joint-rank endpoint (death or permanent ventilation, time to rescue, ALSFRS-R change through day 505) favoured ulefnersen over placebo (P = .0005), and that serum NfL and a time-to-event composite also favoured treatment. Effect sizes were not disclosed, the result is not peer reviewed, and the drug is not approved anywhere 394. An early access programme is listed for the period from the trial results until commercial availability 389.

FUSION · NCT04768972 · Active, not recruiting · last update 2026-07-21

Early access · NCT07862959 · Available (expanded access) · last update 2026-10-07

STMN2: restoring a repair protein

HypothesisThe target

Loss of nuclear TDP-43 causes mis-splicing that lowers STMN2, needed for axon outgrowth and regeneration; restoring it rescued these deficits in human motor neurons 268. ASOs that block the cryptic splice site restored stathmin-2 in human motor neurons and in gene-edited mice 43.

Early evidence: interim, post hoc, not peer reviewedQRL-201 (ANQUR)

A February 2026 company release reported a reduction in pNfH in the low-dose group, an ALSFRS-R "trend", and a significant result only in a post hoc subgroup. No numbers were released. The company plans a phase 3 in 2027. 69 are enrolled 377,437.

ANQUR · NCT05633459 · Active, not recruiting · last update 2026-05-15

Other TDP-43 routes: UNC13A, antibodies, PIKFYVE

Silencing TDP-43 itself is probably not appropriate given its critical cellular functions, which is why indirect routes were pursued 44.

HypothesisUNC13A

TDP-43 loss lets a cryptic exon into UNC13A RNA and lowers the protein; the ALS and FTD risk variants sit in that intron and increase cryptic exon inclusion. Both 2022 papers name it a therapeutic target 80,307.

Tested and negative (interim analysis)Lithium in UNC13A C/C carriers (TRICALS)

The registry reason reads "The planned interim analysis showed no survival benefit", with 88 enrolled of a planned 171 selected by genotype and TRICALS risk score 380,538.

Lithium, UNC13A · NCT06008249 · Terminated · last update 2026-05-13

Hypothesis: first-in-human safety stageVTx-002 (AAV-delivered antibody against TDP-43)

An open-label phase 1/2 given intracisternally, about 36 participants, started 19 December 2025. No results 386.

PIONEER-ALS · NCT07287397 · Recruiting · last update 2026-10-16

HypothesisACI-5891.9

Neutralised seeding-competent TDP-43 from the CSF of people with sporadic ALS in a laboratory assay. No ClinicalTrials.gov entry in ALS was found 31.

HypothesisPIKFYVE inhibition

Activated the expulsion of aggregation-prone proteins and extended survival in C9ORF72, TARDBP, FUS and sporadic ALS models 239. A phase 1b of VRG50635 was terminated "due to lack of risk-benefit data" with 54 enrolled 382.

VRG50635 · NCT06215755 · Terminated · last update 2025-12-15

Tested and negativeNurOwn (MSC-NTF), phase 3

196 enrolled. 33% on NurOwn and 28% on placebo met the responder definition at 28 weeks (OR 1.33, P = .45), so the primary endpoint was not met; a prespecified subgroup showed 35% vs 16% (P = .29) 129,363. A platform tests several drugs under one protocol, sharing placebo participants to save time.

Each regimen randomises 3:1 to drug or placebo and shares placebo participants across regimens. The primary endpoint, a joint model of ALSFRS-R and survival, is reported as a disease rate ratio (DRR), where below 1 favours the drug 369,417. Regimen I (NUZ-001) started in February 2026 388.

Biomarkers and NfL as a surrogate

EstablishedNfL as a prognostic and early marker

In people carrying an ALS gene who later developed the disease, serum and CSF NfL were raised as far back as about 12 months before the first symptoms 49. A 2025 review of SOD1-ALS calls NfL a risk biomarker that predicts phenoconversion 53.

Established for SOD1-ALS onlyNfL as a surrogate endpoint

Tofersen lowered plasma NfL in VALOR, and the FDA based accelerated approval on that reduction, the first ALS indication where an NfL change supported approval 172,335.

UnresolvedLimits of NfL

A 2026 scoping review of 49 trials (8 in ALS) found that NfL reductions often paralleled clinical benefit, but several studies showed a lack of concordance, and it calls for validation specific to each disease and intervention 552. BIIB105 lowered its target but not NfL 62, and in NurOwn the advisory committee reportedly linked greater NfL reduction with worse outcomes 96.

Early evidenceBeyond NfL: a plasma protein panel

In 516 longitudinal samples, a 19-protein panel predicted conversion to clinical ALS over 0.5-5 years (cross-validated AUC 0.80-0.89) and outperformed NfL alone 444.

Early evidenceRemote and digital measures

Video-coached at-home vital capacity agreed well with clinic measurement (Lin's rc 0.82) in 233 HEALEY participants 310. Wrist-worn accelerometry in 95 people declined more slowly than ALSFRS-R and predicted smaller samples for trials longer than 12 months 236.

Trial designStratification and phase 2 reform

The ENCALS model uses 8 predictors, including bulbar onset, age, progression rate, FTD and C9orf72, and was built for counselling and future trial design 535. In MIROCALS the unadjusted and prognosis-adjusted analyses of the same endpoint gave different conclusions 58. Experts propose NfL reduction as a better phase 2 go or no-go marker than short function-based outcomes 56.

Prevention in gene carriers

Natural history studyPre-fALS

A natural-history and biomarker study of unaffected carriers of any ALS gene variant, about 500 planned and running to 2037; its data informed ATLAS 50,357.

Pre-fALS · NCT00317616 · Recruiting · last update 2026-03-20

Early evidenceMild motor impairment before diagnosis

Among 20 phenoconverters in Pre-fALS, 11 had a prodrome of mild motor signs 51. In the 2025 proposed research criteria, mild motor impairment was seen before diagnosis in 55% of phenoconverters, 43% of presymptomatic carriers and 14% of controls, and was linked with faster conversion (HR 5.1); the authors note it is nonspecific 52.

ContextPenetrance and planning

In UK Biobank exomes, 93.4% of the 122 people with SOD1 coding variants had no symptoms, and the estimated genetic prevalence of SOD1-ALS was four times the clinically reported figure 190. Workshop guidance for at-risk carriers acknowledges that the evidence base is limited 54, and a 2024 workshop set out designs for C9orf72 prevention trials 55.

About the FUS result. The FUSION phase 3 topline of 22 September 2026 comes from a news report of the companies' announcement. It is not peer reviewed. The report gives a P value for the primary endpoint but no effect sizes, and ulefnersen is not approved anywhere. 394

Open questions

Gaps that recent reviews name themselves. Each is a place where a study could change the picture.

Why are some motor neurons spared?

Eye-movement nuclei, Onuf's nucleus and dorsal column pathways stay relatively preserved. A 2026 review calls their resilience mechanisms (antioxidant defence, calcium handling, glutamate receptor make-up, glial support) under-studied and a possible therapeutic avenue 243.

Can sporadic ALS be treated through TDP-43 itself?

Silencing TDP-43 is probably not appropriate given its critical functions 44, and translating gain-of-function strategies into the clinic "has been challenging" 166.

Is lowering a toxic product enough once nerve cells are dying?

A review points to irreversible downstream cascades 245, and after BIIB078 the pathology "remained abundant" despite the drug reaching the tissue 324.

Is NfL a valid surrogate beyond SOD1-ALS?

Unresolved. It supported approval only for tofersen, and NfL changes have not always matched clinical change 172,552.

How should trials handle how differently ALS progresses?

Heterogeneity still complicates trial design, and the field is moving toward biomarker-based stratification and markers of TDP-43 loss of function 166,535.

Why do phase 2 signals so often fail in phase 3?

A 2025 viewpoint names 6-month studies with function-based outcomes, multiplicity and over-interpretation of exploratory analyses as sources of "false hope" 56.

When should a carrier start treatment, and who?

Most SOD1 variant carriers in a population sample had no symptoms 190, so prevention trials need markers of imminent onset such as rising NfL or mild motor impairment 50,52. ATLAS is designed to test this and has no results yet 373.

Unproven stem-cell clinics

What published guidance says

A JAMA Neurology viewpoint describes "stem cell tourism" as an Internet-based industry that sells unproven procedures to people with incurable neurological diseases, including ALS, with a risk of illness and death. It calls on medical societies and licensing boards to address it. 72

The International Society for Stem Cell Research (ISSCR) guide warns about treatments that have not undergone proper testing or regulatory approval and are marketed by clinics. It encourages caution and decisions taken together with treating clinicians. 244

Under trial conditions, the cell therapy NurOwn did not meet its phase 3 primary endpoint. 129

In plain wordsSome clinics sell stem-cell treatments for ALS that have not been properly tested. Guidance from experts is to be cautious and to talk any offer through with the treating team first.

The big picture

About 97% of all ALS has TDP-43 accumulation. For sporadic ALS, a 2026 review calls today's disease-modifying therapies "of limited utility", which is why gene-specific routes have produced the first targeted successes. 166

A 2022 Lancet Seminar noted that most recent advances "have yet to translate into patient benefit". 180

Exam hookGene-specific ALS (SOD1, FUS) is where targeted therapy has worked first; most ALS is sporadic and TDP-43-driven. 166
In plain wordsResearchers are trying many routes. A few work for small genetic groups, and none yet for most people with ALS.
