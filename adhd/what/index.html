Part 1 · The condition

What ADHD is

ADHD, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, is a neurodevelopmental condition: a persistent pattern of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity that interferes with daily life. 16 Here is how common it is, in whom it is found, and how often it lasts into adulthood.

How common · worldwide

About 5 in 100 children

Polanczyk and colleagues searched MEDLINE and PsycINFO from 1978 to 2005, read 303 full texts and pooled 102 studies with 171,756 participants aged 18 or under, from every world region. The pooled prevalence was 5.29% (95% CI 5.01–5.56). 352,356

In plain wordsADHD is common. About 5 in every 100 children and teenagers around the world meet the criteria for it. 352

Diagnosed and treated: the United States

Meeting criteria in a research survey is not the same as being diagnosed, and being diagnosed is not the same as being treated. Two national US surveys show the gaps, for children and for adults.

Survey data: parent report for children, self-report for adults. Children's treatment categories overlap (a child can have both), so they do not add up to 100%. 96,98,425

In 2022, 11.4% of US children aged 3–17 had ever been diagnosed with ADHD and 10.5% had current ADHD; about 1 million more children had ever been diagnosed than in 2016. Among children with current ADHD, 53.6% took ADHD medication, 44.4% had behavioural treatment in the past year and 30.1% had neither (in 2016: 62.0%, 46.7% and 23.0%). 58.1% had moderate or severe ADHD and 77.9% at least one co-occurring disorder. The figures are parent-reported. 96,98

Trend

More diagnosis, not more ADHD

US diagnosed ADHD among children aged 4–17 rose from 6.1% in 1997–1998 to 10.2% in 2015–2016, in every subgroup. 496 Yet with standardised methods, there was "no evidence to suggest an increase" over three decades in the number of children in the community who meet criteria. 354 The World Federation consensus attributes the rise to changes in clinical and administrative practice. Established 129

Debate

Over- and under-diagnosis can coexist

The youngest children in a school year are more likely to be diagnosed (RR 1.38), 164 and a scoping review of 334 studies found Early evidence of overdiagnosis at the milder end. 222 At the same time, adults, women and minority groups appear under-detected. 74,134,499 See relative age and risk.

Adults

Often untreated

In the WHO surveys only 21.8% of adults with ADHD had any mental health treatment in the past year, and 3.3% were treated specifically for ADHD. 134 Adults more often come to clinics for depression, anxiety or alcohol problems than for ADHD itself, and their ADHD symptoms are often mistaken for those conditions. 172

Three presentations

DSM-5 replaced "subtype" with "presentation", because the pattern of symptoms can change as people grow up. The presentation describes the past 6 months. 16,115 In the general population the inattentive presentation is the most common; children with the combined presentation are more likely to be referred to clinics. 487

What ADHD is not

Established

Not a lack of effort

People with ADHD of all ages show, on average, medium-sized weaknesses in executive functions such as response inhibition, vigilance, working memory and planning (effect sizes 0.46–0.69 across 83 studies), though not every person has them. 486 In girls and women, inattention is often misread as "lacking effort or motivation". 499

Established

Not caused by parenting

Twin, adoption and family studies show that ADHD runs in biological families, and shared family environment adds little. Parenting can interact with a child's behaviour, and a child's behaviour also shapes parenting, but parenting is not the main cause. 185,244,423 NICE asks clinicians to explain that a recommendation for parent training "does not imply bad parenting". 324

Established

Not new, and not only American

Physicians described attention problems in 1775 and 1798, and Still lectured on such children in 1902. 36,241,426 When studies use the same methods, prevalence does not differ significantly between North America, Europe, Asia, Africa, South America and Oceania. 129,487 See 6.1 History.

Diagnosis

Not something a quiz can decide

ADHD "can only be diagnosed by a licensed clinician", and it "cannot be diagnosed by rating scales alone, neuropsychological tests, or methods for imaging the brain". 129 It occurs at every level of intelligence. 129 Screeners are taught in 2.3 Rating scales.

ADHD Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on attention deficit hyperactivity disorder in children and adults, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people with ADHD and their families.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose ADHD, and nothing here should start, stop or change a treatment without your own clinician. Rating scales and the criteria calculator are teaching tools, not tests. Criteria, doses and guideline statements are quoted as published.
