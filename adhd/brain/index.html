The ADHD brain

What brain imaging shows about ADHD, group by group: networks that switch on and off differently during tasks, a cortex that matures about three years later, and subcortical volumes that differ only slightly on average. Scrub from age 6 to 18 and watch two group-average brains side by side.

Three networks, one task

Toggle the three networks above. In a meta-analysis of 55 task-based fMRI studies, children with ADHD showed under-activation mainly in the frontoparietal (executive) and ventral attention networks and over-activation in the default, ventral attention and somatomotor networks. The differences were present even without comorbid disorders or any stimulant history. In inhibition tasks, the right inferior frontal cortex, supplementary motor area, anterior cingulate and striato-thalamic areas were less active: the frontostriatal loop. 82,188

In plain wordsBrain networks are teams of areas that work together. On average, in ADHD the "focus" teams are a little quieter during a task and the "daydreaming" team a little louder.

What a brain scan can and cannot tell you

Not a diagnostic test

No brain scan diagnoses ADHD

The World Federation of ADHD consensus states that most brain and cognitive differences between groups with and without ADHD "are typically small", do not clearly separate ADHD from other disorders, and so are not useful for diagnosis. ADHD is diagnosed by a licensed clinician who interviews the person and their family, not by rating scales alone, neuropsychological tests or brain imaging. 129

Established

Group differences, not individual ones

All ENIGMA subcortical effects are below d = 0.2, which the Lancet seminar calls very small. Most imaging studies are cross-sectional, so they cannot show whether a difference causes ADHD or is an adaptation to it. Small samples, head motion and weak phenotyping have made many findings hard to reproduce. 356

Established

Why small studies mislead

An analysis of about 50,000 scans found that brain-behaviour associations are much smaller than assumed. At the median neuroimaging sample of about 25 people, studies are underpowered and report inflated effects; results replicate only with samples in the thousands. 272

Established

Medicines do not explain the differences

In ENIGMA, stimulant use did not change the subcortical results (all p > 0.15). The consensus statement says brain differences are not caused by drug treatment. In 43 adolescents followed from 12.5 to 16.4 years, cortex thinned faster in those not taking stimulants (0.16 vs 0.03 mm a year); the authors found no sign that stimulants slowed cortical growth. 129,206,391

Cerebellum

Not only the front of the brain

In an NIMH cohort of 152 children and adolescents with ADHD and 139 controls, total cerebral volume was 3.2% and cerebellar volume 3.5% smaller at the first scan, and the trajectories ran roughly parallel with age. The cerebellum is under-active during timing tasks, and a cross-disorder meta-analysis found less grey matter in cerebellar lobule IX, part of the attention networks. 59,187,427

Contested

The EEG theta/beta ratio

A 2006 meta-analysis reported a very large effect (3.08). A 2013 meta-analysis found 0.62 to 0.75, shrinking over the decade because the ratio rose in comparison groups; its authors concluded it "cannot be considered a reliable diagnostic measure". The FDA nonetheless authorised it as a marker. 17,381,409

Hypothesis · default-mode interference

When the daydreaming network breaks in

The default-mode interference hypothesis proposes that slow (below 0.1 Hz) spontaneous default-mode activity can intrude on task-focused processing, causing periodic lapses and the variable performance typical of ADHD. 416

Early evidence In 20 adults with ADHD and 20 controls, the normal see-saw (antiphasic) relationship between the dorsal anterior cingulate and the default-mode network was weaker. In children aged 6–17, connectivity within the default-mode network is reduced, consistent with delayed maturation. 60,356

Symptoms are worse in long, low-stimulation tasks than in engaging ones, and reaction times vary more from trial to trial (Hedges' g = 0.76 in children, 0.46 in adults). Stimulants reduced that variability (g = −0.74). 230,356

Caution A meta-analysis of 30 resting-state studies found no consistent location of over- or under-connectivity across studies. 86

In plain wordsThe brain has a "daydreaming" network that should quiet down when we focus. In ADHD it may break in more often, causing short lapses of attention. 356,416
