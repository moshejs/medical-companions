Pass date: 2026-11-27. Window: 2026-11-20 to 2026-11-27 (PubMed entry dates), plus the watch item carried from 2026-11-20 (the 2026 consensus update). Modules served: living (exercise), history (first descriptions), review (one flashcard), risk (negative-control figure).

## Findings

### living · Exercise: the consensus position has shifted
- The site said the World Federation consensus "is more cautious" about exercise, because one meta-analysis found a symptom reduction that was no longer significant after adjusting for publication bias [[Faraone2021]].
- The 2026 update states that structured physical activity "has emerged as one of the most reliably beneficial non-medication approaches": chronic moderate-to-vigorous, cognitively engaging exercise is associated with substantial improvements in executive functions and small-to-moderate improvements in ADHD symptoms. It calls this "an important shift from the earlier consensus". It adds that structured physical activity is not very effective at reducing ADHD symptoms compared with pharmacological treatments, and that the statement now treats exercise as a recommended adjunctive intervention. [[Faraone2026]]
- The same section says the certainty of evidence for non-medication interventions tends to be low. (Early evidence)

### history, review · An earlier "first description"
- The site gave Weikard's 1775 textbook as the earliest known description [[Barkley2012Weikard,Faraone2021]].
- A 2024 paper presents a description of attention deficit in a 1753 Dutch medical book by Cornelius Kloekhof as "possibly the earliest", and says it inspired later writers such as Weikard [[vanKernebeek2024]]. The 2026 consensus update records this as its only history update [[Faraone2026]].

### What the 2026 update is, and is not
- 252 statements from large cohort studies (2,000 or more participants) and meta-analyses published 2021-01-01 to 2025-07-01, approved by 118 authors from 29 countries. [[Faraone2026]]
- It does not replace the 2021 statement: statements from 2021 are not repeated, and the update "is designed to be used with the original ICS". Its discussion says the update "strongly reinforces the original consensus statement". So the site's many citations of [[Faraone2021]] for diagnosis, validity, heritability and history stand. [[Faraone2026]]
- Table 1 of the update gives a worldwide epidemiologic prevalence of 7.6% in children, 5.6% in adolescents and 5.1% in young adults, falling to 0.8% at 65 or older. Not added to the site in this pass: the underlying meta-analyses were not read.

### risk · Correction to a cited paper
- An erratum to the Hong Kong sibling study was published on 2026-11-23 (coding errors affecting the abstract, results, tables, figures and supplement; conclusions unchanged). In the corrected abstract the pre-pregnancy negative-control hazard ratio for ADHD is 1.26 (95% CI 1.22–1.30), not 1.24 (1.20–1.28), and the sibling-matched ADHD estimate is 1.01 (0.93–1.09), not (0.93–1.08). [[Luo2026JAMAIM]]

### Exam hooks
- Kloekhof 1753, Weikard 1775, Crichton 1798 and Still 1902 are all proposed as "first". [[vanKernebeek2024]]
- 2026 consensus update: exercise is a recommended add-on, with small-to-moderate symptom effects, well below medicines. [[Faraone2026]]

### In plain words
- Being active may ease ADHD symptoms a little, but much less than medicine does.

## Open questions and controversies
- Not applied, for a full pass (each needs the underlying meta-analyses read, and none cites [[Faraone2021]] on the site):
  - Supplements. The update concludes that omega-3, multivitamins, zinc, ginkgo and other antioxidant supplements are not efficacious for ADHD symptoms and "should not be promoted as evidence-based treatments". The site's therapy module rates omega-3 as Early evidence with a small blinded effect (SMD 0.16) [[SonugaBarke2013,Bloch2011]], and the glossary says "a small effect".
  - Mindfulness. Table 1 of the update says mindfulness-based interventions do not improve symptoms or executive functions against sham controls. The site's therapy module reports d −0.66 for inattention from mostly uncontrolled studies and one positive single-blind trial [[Janssen2019]].
  - Cognitive training. The update moves from "mixed and promising but inconclusive" to saying that judging cognitive training as one category is uninformative.
  - Physical comorbidity. The update extends the 2021 links (obesity, injury) to diabetes, hypertension and other cardiovascular disease, epilepsy, migraine, sleep and dental disease.
- Does TNS work for ADHD? Unchanged: the US 218-child trial is still a preprint [[Loo2026]].
