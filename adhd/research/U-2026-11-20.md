## Findings

### parenting, living · School accommodations tested directly in a randomised trial
- The site said most accommodations had no ADHD-specific benefit and that many "have few or no experimental studies" [[Lovett2021]]. A large randomised trial has now tested four common ones.
- A crossover randomised trial in summer treatment programme classrooms enrolled children with ADHD from kindergarten to sixth grade: 306 screened, 280 enrolled, 255 started the intervention (201 male, 79%) and 235 completed. Accommodations changed daily in random order, methylphenidate (0.3 mg/kg) and placebo alternated weekly, and a general classroom and an enhanced behavioural classroom alternated every 2 weeks. [[Fabiano2026]]
- The accommodations were extended time on tasks, frequent breaks during tasks, a low-distraction workspace and the ability to stand at the desk, each compared with no accommodation. There was no evidence that any of them significantly improved behaviour or academic outcomes, alone or in combination, and no interaction was significant. [[Fabiano2026]]
- Compared with placebo, methylphenidate raised seatwork productivity (rate ratio 1.22, 95% CI 1.19–1.25) and quiz scores (3.9 points, 95% CI 3.10–4.70). Medication and behaviour therapy reduced classroom rule violations (RR 0.63, 95% CI 0.60–0.67, and RR 0.39, 95% CI 0.37–0.42). [[Fabiano2026]]
- Limits: one trial, in summer-programme classrooms and not regular schools; outcomes were rule violations, seatwork and quiz scores over short periods. (Early evidence)
- The PubMed abstract gives mean age as "8.8 [1.6] months", an evident typo for years; age is not quoted on the site.

### Exam hooks
- In a 255-child crossover RCT, extended time, breaks, a low-distraction workspace and standing at the desk did not improve behaviour or academic work; methylphenidate and behaviour therapy did. [[Fabiano2026]]

### In plain words
- Extra time and quiet desks are common at school, but in a careful study they did not by themselves help children with ADHD behave or work better. Medicine and behaviour programmes did.

## Open questions and controversies
- Do accommodations help in regular classrooms over a school year, or for tests? Not answered by this trial [[Fabiano2026]]; read-aloud for younger pupils remains the exception in the earlier review [[Lovett2021]]. Status: open.
- Does TNS work for ADHD? Unchanged from U-2026-11-13: the US 218-child trial is still a preprint [[Loo2026]].
