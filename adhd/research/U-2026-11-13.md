## Findings

### frontiers · External trigeminal nerve stimulation: the pilot has not been confirmed
- The site said the 62-child pilot behind the 2019 FDA De Novo authorisation was "waiting for replication" [[McGough2019,FDA-MonarchETNS]]. Two later trials now bear on that.
- A confirmatory UK multicentre, double-blind, sham-controlled, parallel-group phase 2b trial randomised 150 children and adolescents with ADHD to real TNS (n = 75) or sham TNS (n = 75), nightly for about 9 hours for 4 weeks, with follow-up to 6 months. Sham delivered 30 seconds of stimulation per hour at lower frequency and pulse width. [[Conti2026]]
- Intention-to-treat analysis showed no significant difference on ADHD symptoms, the primary outcome: adjusted mean difference 0.83 (95% CI −2.47 to 4.13; P = 0.622; Cohen's d 0.09). No serious adverse events were reported and side effects did not differ between groups. The authors conclude that TNS "is a safe intervention but does not demonstrate clinical efficacy for pediatric ADHD". [[Conti2026]]
- A preprint from the pilot's UCLA group reports a randomised trial (N = 218) of active (120 Hz) versus active-control (2 Hz) TNS. Differences on continuous symptom scales were attenuated by "robust active-control responses" (no numbers in the abstract), but 35% versus 15% were responders on blinded clinician-rated CGI-I (OR 3.07, 95% CI 1.45–6.24; p = 0.002). (Early evidence; preprint, not peer reviewed) [[Loo2026]]
- The preprint abstract gives no registration number. Its sample size matches the registered enrolment of NCT05374187, whose registered primary outcome is change in ADHD-RS-5 and which still has no results posted on ClinicalTrials.gov (checked 2026-11-13). [[NCT05374187,Loo2026]]
- Overall label for TNS stays Early evidence: one positive pilot, one larger independent null trial, one mixed preprint.

### Exam hooks
- TNS for ADHD: FDA De Novo 2019 on a 62-child pilot (d 0.5); a 150-participant UK sham-controlled trial found no effect on ADHD symptoms (d 0.09). [[McGough2019,Conti2026]]

### In plain words
- A forehead nerve stimulator is allowed to be sold for ADHD in the US, based on one small study. A larger, careful UK study found it worked no better than a fake device.

## Open questions and controversies
- Does TNS work for ADHD? Pilot positive [[McGough2019]]; UK confirmatory trial null [[Conti2026]]; US 218-child preprint mixed (symptom scales attenuated, CGI-I responders 35% v 15%) [[Loo2026]]. Status: open; peer-reviewed publication and registry results of the US trial are awaited.
