06:54 · Morning routine

Getting out of the door

Mornings are a common pinch point. Unmedicated children with ADHD have more difficulty waking in the morning and more daytime sleepiness than controls (16 studies). 82 NICE lists reducing stigma, "because there is no need to take medication at school or in the workplace", as one reason to think about a once-daily modified-release stimulant. 326

Supports at school, university and work

Each setting has its own words and rules. The summaries below describe what guidelines and studies say, not legal advice; local law and policy decide what applies.

School in the United States

Guideline

IEP or 504 plan

AAP 2019 says educational interventions and individualised instructional supports are "a necessary part of any treatment plan" and "often include an Individualized Education Program (IEP) or a rehabilitation plan (504 plan)" (grade A, strong recommendation). 495

Population data

What plans contain

In 97 US middle-school students with ADHD, 61.9% had an IEP and 38.1% a 504 plan. Listed services often matched federal recommendations "but had little to no research supporting their effectiveness"; in 85% of IEPs the present-performance statement described behaviour problems, yet fewer than half had measurable goals for them. 424

Little evidence

Extended time

Accommodations are "by far the most common response to ADHD in educational settings". Most showed no ADHD-specific benefit; read-aloud accommodations for younger students were the exception. The review advises pairing accommodations with evidence-based interventions. 263

Early evidence

Skill-building

In a randomised trial of 64 middle-school students, organisation training, self-management and note-taking instruction beat accommodations (organisation support, extended time, a copy of the teacher's notes) for organised binders and accurate notes. 187

Driving

Relative risk against drivers without ADHD. Population statistics from a meta-analysis of 16 studies and a US claims study. 67,454

Established

A modest raised risk, not "fourfold"

After controlling for distance driven, drivers with ADHD had a crash relative risk of 1.23 (95% CI 1.04–1.46), similar to drivers with cardiovascular disease. The meta-analysis rejected the older claim of a roughly fourfold risk; risk was higher where most drivers also had oppositional defiant or conduct disorder. 454

Population data

New drivers and older drivers

Newly licensed New Jersey drivers with ADHD had an adjusted crash rate ratio of 1.62 in their first month, and 3.6 times the rate of alcohol and/or drug violations in their first year. 91 In 2,832 drivers aged 65–79, ADHD was linked to more self-reported crashes (aIRR 1.74). 257

Early evidence

Medication and crashes

Comparing the same people on and off medication, US months on ADHD medication had lower odds of a crash-related emergency visit (men OR 0.62, women OR 0.58). 67 In Sweden, starting medication was associated with fewer first transport accidents: 24.0 versus 27.5 per 1000 person-years. 507 These are observational designs. More in Course & outcomes.

Guideline

What the guidelines say

AAP 2019 asks for "special concern" to provide medication coverage while adolescents drive. 495 NICE asks clinicians to discuss that ADHD may impair driving and that medication may improve it, and notes that in the UK people with ADHD must tell the DVLA if their symptoms or medication affect their ability to drive safely. 326

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

Register studies: observational population data, not a recommendation to stop or continue a medicine. 32,213

Established

Child development: mostly reassuring

In 861,650 Swedish children, exposure in the womb to methylphenidate, amphetamines or atomoxetine was not linked to any neurodevelopmental disorder (aHR 0.95); pooled with Danish data, HR 1.00. 33 Danish children whose mothers continued medication had no higher risk than those whose mothers stopped before pregnancy (aHR 0.97). 31 A US link between methylphenidate and child ADHD disappeared with stricter control for maternal ADHD. 435

Early evidence

Malformations and pregnancy outcomes

First-trimester methylphenidate had an adjusted RR of 1.28 (1.00–1.64) for cardiac malformations, at the edge of significance; amphetamines showed no increase (0.96). 213 Atomoxetine: any major malformation 0.99 (0.74–1.34). 52 Stimulants in early pregnancy were linked to preeclampsia (aRR 1.29), with small absolute increases; the authors say women with significant ADHD "should not be counseled to suspend their ADHD treatment based on these findings". 77 Swedish data showed more neonatal intensive care admissions (aOR 1.5), with uncertainty about how much the medicine explains. 330

Label

Breastfeeding, as the US labels state

Methylphenidate (Concerta): infant doses of 0.16% to 0.7% of the mother's weight-adjusted dose in limited sampling; monitor for agitation, poor sleep, feeding changes and reduced weight gain. 141 Amphetamines (Adderall XR, Vyvanse): relative infant doses of 2% to 13.8%; "breastfeeding is not recommended". 138,160 Atomoxetine: no data in human milk. 158 Viloxazine (Qelbree): "may cause maternal harm; discontinue when pregnancy is recognized" unless benefits outweigh the risk. 153 Several labels point to the National Pregnancy Registry for ADHD Medications. 141,147,158,160

In Denmark, ADHD medication fills in pregnancy rose from 0.08 to 7.71 per 1000 pregnancies between 2005 and 2022. 267 This guide's research found no dedicated pregnancy guideline, so decisions are an individual risk–benefit discussion with the prescriber. A systematic review covers maternal ADHD, birth outcomes and early development alongside the medicines. 230

Sleep, family and moving to adult care

Established

Sleep problems are common

Unmedicated children with ADHD have more bedtime resistance, trouble falling asleep, night waking and difficulty waking in the morning (16 studies). 82 Stimulants lengthen sleep latency and shorten total sleep in trials with objective measures. 228 A two-session sleep hygiene programme cut moderate to severe sleep problems from 56% to 30% (NNT 3.9). 201

Safety

Melatonin: helps sleep onset, store it safely

Melatonin brought sleep onset 26.9 minutes earlier in unmedicated children with insomnia, with no effect on daytime behaviour. 457 In the US it is sold as a dietary supplement; content in 31 tested products ranged from −83% to +478% of the label, 117 and poison-centre reports of child ingestions rose 530% from 2012 to 2021. 248

Family

Siblings, parents and a parent with ADHD

In a UK survey, siblings of children with ADHD reported lower happiness with life and family, and sibling bullying was more common. 341 Parents of youths with ADHD were more likely to divorce, and sooner. 496 Parental ADHD symptoms explained only 2.9% of the variance in harsh parenting, similar to other parental mental health conditions. 340 NICE asks clinicians to consider the needs of a parent who also has ADHD, including help with organisation, adherence and school routines, and to encourage carers to seek an assessment of their own needs. 326

Population data

The move to adult services

In a UK surveillance study, 64% of young people on ADHD medication had a referral accepted, 22% attended a first adult appointment and 6% had an optimal transition. 115 NICE says transition "should usually be completed by the time the young person is 18 years"; AAP suggests starting to plan at about age 14. 326,495 See Nursing care.

Often missed

Girls and women

A UK expert consensus says the gap in diagnosis partly reflects under-recognition and referral bias in girls, and advises attention to "more subtle and/or internalised" presentations and to coping strategies that mask symptoms. 501 More in Girls, women & late diagnosis.

Population data

Relationships and teenage parenthood

Research on adults points to "short-lived and discordant" romantic relationships, with little work on what protects against this. 497 In Danish registers, young women with ADHD were more likely to become parents at 12–16 (IRR 3.62); the authors suggest offering sexual education and contraceptive counselling. 336

Words that help, words that hurt

Stigma is an underestimated risk factor for poor adherence and lower wellbeing; doubt about whether the diagnosis is valid is one of its main sources. 301 The World Federation consensus was written because misconceptions "stigmatize affected people, reduce credibility of providers, and prevent/delay treatment". 130
