ADHD, explained

ADHD, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, is a lasting pattern of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity that gets in the way of daily life. 16 It affects about 5 in 100 children worldwide, often continues into adulthood, and is still missed in many girls and women. 125,352,499 Here it is in moving pictures: see how brain signalling works, try the DSM-5-TR criteria as a teaching tool, watch how each medicine acts, and compare what the trials show.

ADHD at a glance

What it is
ADHD, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, is a neurodevelopmental condition: a lasting pattern of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity that gets in the way of daily life. 16 ADHD runs in biological families, and parenting is not the main cause. 185,244,423
Who gets it
About 5 in every 100 children and teenagers around the world meet the criteria. 352 Worldwide, between about 3 and 7 adults in 100 have it, depending on how it is counted. 412 Girls with ADHD are often quiet daydreamers rather than disruptive, so their ADHD can be missed. 197,362
Main signs
People with ADHD show, on average, weaknesses in skills such as stopping an action, staying alert, working memory and planning, though not every person has them. 486 In the community the mainly inattentive form is the most common. 487 Hyperactivity often eases with age, while inattention stays fairly stable. 16,115,493
How it is diagnosed
Only a licensed clinician can diagnose ADHD. Rating scales, brain tests and brain imaging cannot diagnose it alone. 129 The clinician looks at current and past symptoms and their effects, plus family and developmental history. 356
Main treatments
For children aged 6 to 12, the American Academy of Pediatrics recommends approved medication together with parent training in behaviour management and/or behavioural classroom support, preferably both. 493 For adults, NICE recommends at least a structured supportive psychological intervention focused on ADHD, with regular follow-up. 324
Outlook
Across 20 WHO surveys, 57% of people with childhood ADHD still had it as adults. 134,396 On average people with ADHD face more risks, mostly from accidents, but many finish school, work, drive safely and live long lives. 93,117,218

ADHD Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on attention deficit hyperactivity disorder in children and adults, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people with ADHD and their families.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose ADHD, and nothing here should start, stop or change a treatment without your own clinician. Rating scales and the criteria calculator are teaching tools, not tests. Criteria, doses and guideline statements are quoted as published. Please check the original sources, which are listed in full in the Sources module.
