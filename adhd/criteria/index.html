Part 2 · Recognising & diagnosing

DSM-5-TR criteria calculator

ADHD is diagnosed against a written set of criteria: count the symptoms, then check age, duration, settings, impairment and other explanations. Scrub through the editions to see how the rules reached their present form, then tick the boxes in a teaching calculator.

Schematic. Thresholds and the onset rule are drawn from DSM-IV onward; the earlier editions show only their name and how the symptoms were grouped. 16,115,241

2013 · DSM-5

Onset 12, adults 5, autism allowed

DSM-5 kept the 18 symptoms and added examples of how they look in adolescents and adults. The threshold fell from 6 to 5 symptoms per domain at age 17 and over. Onset moved from "symptoms and impairment before 7" to "several symptoms before 12". Criterion C now asks for symptoms, not impairment, in two or more settings. Autism stopped being an exclusion, "subtypes" became "presentations", and severity and partial-remission specifiers were added. 115,356 In a British birth cohort of 2,232 children, raising the onset age to 12 raised prevalence at age 12 by only 0.1%. 353

In plain wordsThe 2013 changes made it easier to recognise ADHD in teenagers and adults, and allowed ADHD and autism to be diagnosed together.
Exam hookDSM-5: onset <12 (was 7); 5 symptoms at 17+; presentations replace subtypes; autism no longer exclusionary. 115

  • Criterion A: 6 or more symptoms in at least one domain (now 0 inattentive, 0 hyperactive-impulsive)
  • Criterion A, duration: symptoms have lasted 6 months or more
  • Criterion B: several symptoms were present before age 12
  • Criterion C: several symptoms show in two or more settings (home, school or work, friends, other activities)
  • Criterion D: clear evidence they interfere with, or reduce the quality of, social, school or work functioning
  • Criterion E: not better explained by another condition (for example a mood, anxiety or psychotic disorder, or substance intoxication or withdrawal)

Only a clinician's assessment can diagnose ADHD. It rests on interviews with the person and their family, history and reports from several settings, not on a checklist. 129,356

What a checklist cannot see

Who diagnoses

A clinician, not a scale

The World Federation of ADHD consensus states that ADHD can only be diagnosed by a licensed clinician who interviews the parent or caregiver and/or the patient. Rating scales, neuropsychological tests and brain imaging cannot diagnose it alone. 129 See rating scales & assessment.

History

The whole story

The diagnosis needs a detailed evaluation of current and past symptoms and impairment, plus family, gestational and developmental history. 356 The AAP asks clinicians to confirm DSM-5 criteria, document symptoms and impairment in more than one major setting from parents, teachers and other school staff, and rule out other causes. 493

Established

A continuum with a line drawn on it

ADHD behaves like the extreme end of a continuum, differing from typical development by degree rather than kind. DSM-5 and ICD-11 still draw categorical thresholds, which the Lancet seminar calls "somewhat arbitrary". 356

Look-alikes

Criterion E matters

Symptoms must not be better explained by another condition, such as a mood, anxiety, dissociative or personality disorder, or substance intoxication or withdrawal. 16 Many conditions overlap with ADHD or occur with it: see look-alikes & co-occurring conditions.

Ability

High IQ does not rule it out

ADHD can be diagnosed in people with high intelligence. In a birth cohort of over 5,700 children, high-, average- and low-IQ children with ADHD did not differ in age at meeting criteria, co-occurring disorders or stimulant treatment. 129

Age 12

Why the onset age moved

Adults assessed late often cannot recall exactly when symptoms began, and some children (highly able, mainly inattentive, or in very structured settings) are not impaired until demands rise in late primary or middle school. 115 Girls are among those most often missed: see girls, women & late diagnosis.
