Ages 6–11 · childhood

Diagnosis, and more injuries

Most children are diagnosed at primary-school age. On average, children and adolescents with ADHD have more unintentional injuries: across 28 studies (350,938 with ADHD) the pooled odds ratio was 1.53. In Denmark, children with ADHD had more injuries at age 10 (adjusted OR 1.29) and 12 (1.30). Hyperactive-impulsive symptoms are the ones most linked to injury and peer rejection. 94,129,380

In plain wordsChildren with ADHD get hurt more often on average. Supervision and safety habits help.
Exam hookInjuries in children with ADHD: pooled OR 1.53 (28 studies). 380

On and off medication: the same people compared with themselves

Register studies can follow the same person through months on and off ADHD medication. Comparing a person with themselves removes everything that stays fixed, such as genes and upbringing. It does not remove things that change over time, and it is still observational. Pick an outcome. 69,233

US insurance claims, 2,319,450 people with ADHD (mean age 32.5). 67 Observational: within-person designs remove stable confounders but not changes over time. 233

On vs off medication · replicated

Driving crashes

Established association Months on ADHD medication had 38% lower odds of emergency visits for crashes in men (OR 0.62) and 42% lower in women (OR 0.58). The authors estimated that up to 22.1% of crashes might have been avoided with medication throughout follow-up, a model estimate. In Swedish men, medication periods went with a 58% lower risk of serious transport accidents (HR 0.42); in women there was no significant association. 65,67

In plain wordsIn big studies, the same people had fewer of these events while taking ADHD medicine. That is strong evidence, but not proof that the medicine caused the change.

Established

Mortality: about twice, mostly accidents

In Denmark the fully adjusted mortality rate ratio was 2.07, and accidents were the most common cause of death. It was 1.86 for diagnosis before 6, 1.58 at 6–17 and 4.25 at 18 or older. Excluding oppositional, conduct and substance use disorders it was 1.50, higher in girls and women (2.85) than boys and men (1.27). A meta-analysis of 8 studies found RR 2.13 for all causes (low confidence), raised for unnatural (2.81) but not clearly for natural causes. 62,93

Co-occurring conditions

Risk climbs with other diagnoses

In Sweden (2.68 million people), the mortality HR was 1.41 for ADHD alone and 25.22 with four or more co-occurring psychiatric conditions. After adjusting for later disorders, ADHD stayed linked with death from unintentional injury (HR 2.14) but not significantly with suicide (1.13). Spotting and treating co-occurring conditions matters: see Look-alikes & co-occurring conditions. 436

Debated

How much shorter is life expectancy?

UK primary-care data estimated 6.78 years less for men and 8.64 for women with diagnosed ADHD, but only about 1 in 9 adults with ADHD there had a diagnosis, so the figure may not apply to the undiagnosed majority. A modelled estimate from 131 hyperactive children gave 8.4 years (12.7 if ADHD persisted), but it projects risk factors in a small clinical sample, not observed deaths. Excess mortality is consistent; the size of the gap is not. 37,333

Established

Persistence depends on the definition

Published adult persistence rates range from 5% to 75% depending on informant and threshold. Parent reports give higher rates than self-reports. In the MTA, combining parent and self-report with a norm-based threshold, 60% had persistent symptoms and 41% had symptoms plus impairment. Across 20 WHO surveys, 57.0% of people with childhood ADHD still had it as adults. 134,396

Hypothesis

Late-onset ADHD?

In the Dunedin cohort, childhood ADHD (6%) and adult ADHD (3%) were "virtually nonoverlapping": 90% of adult cases had no childhood ADHD. In the Pelotas cohort only 12.6% of young adults with ADHD had had it as children, and the adult group was mostly female. DSM still requires several symptoms before age 12; the question is unresolved and may partly reflect measurement and co-occurring conditions. See Girls, women & late diagnosis. 63,287

Disputed

ADHD in prison: 8% or 26%?

A 2018 meta-analysis of 102 studies estimated 26.2% of adults in detention have ADHD. Restricted to 11 unselected prison samples, a 2024 meta-analysis found 8.3%, about one in 12; the original authors' reanalysis gave 22.2%. The gap is about which studies to include. 28,29,135

Randomised vs register

When trials and registers disagree

In the randomised MTA, neither the childhood treatment nor later stimulant use was linked to adolescent substance use in either direction, while US and Swedish registers found fewer substance-related events in medicated periods. At 16 years, extended medication use was not linked to lower adult symptom severity, and consistent use went with adult height 2.55 cm shorter. See The MTA study. 66,290,363,442

School & work

Medication and exams

In 657,720 Swedish students, three months of ADHD medication went with lower odds of not qualifying for upper secondary school (OR 0.80) and 9.35 more grade points out of 320. Young adults who sat the university entrance test more than once scored 4.80 points higher (out of 200) when on medication. In the MTA, people whose symptoms eased did better on education, work and income than those whose symptoms persisted. 193,217,262

In plain words. These numbers describe large groups, not what will happen to any one person. Many people with ADHD do well, and most of the extra risk comes from accidents, which can be reduced. In large studies, the same people had fewer crashes, injuries, convictions and substance problems during months on ADHD medicine; those studies are strong but not perfect. 67,93,233,253,363
