Established

Many small risks, rarely one cause

For most people, ADHD results from many genetic and environmental risks that each have a very small effect and add up. Because confounding is hard to rule out, the consensus statement calls pre- and postnatal environmental features "correlates" rather than causes. See Genes & heritability. [[Faraone2021]]

Live controversy

Acetaminophen (paracetamol) in pregnancy

Pooled observational data gave RR 1.25, and a 2025 review of 46 studies advised limiting use; a 2021 consensus of 91 scientists called for precaution. But in 2.48 million Swedish children the crude 10-year risk was 2.46% unexposed vs 2.87% exposed, and the sibling HR was 0.98 (0.94–1.02); Hong Kong sibling data gave 1.01. Long exposure was not ruled out in a small sibling sample. Talk to a clinician about any medicine in pregnancy. [[Kim2020LancetPsych,Prada2025,Bauer2021,Ahlqvist2024,Luo2026JAMAIM,Gustavson2021APAP]]

Established

Born early or small

Odds rise with immaturity: in Sweden, ADHD medication odds were 2.1 at 23–28 weeks, 1.6 at 29–32, 1.4 at 33–34, 1.3 at 35–36 and 1.1 at 37–38 weeks, against 39–41 weeks, and similar within mothers. Inattention is most affected (SMD 1.31). Follow-up of preterm children matters. [[Lindstrom2011,Franz2018]]

Alcohol

Heavy versus light exposure

Low-to-moderate prenatal alcohol (up to 70 g a week) showed no increase in ADHD symptoms (OR 0.94). Heavy exposure is different: ADHD occurs in 50% of people with fetal alcohol spectrum disorder, about 10 times the expected rate. [[SanMartinPorter2019,Weyrauch2017]]

Head injury

Severe head injury, and reverse causation

Children who had a head injury already had more ADHD before it (16.0% vs 10.8%), because ADHD raises injury risk. Concussion and mild injury did not significantly raise later ADHD; severe injury did (OR 6.25), and 35.5% of children with severe injury had ADHD more than a year later. A smaller meta-analysis found mild injury doubled the odds, so the two disagree. [[Asarnow2021,Faraone2021]]

Resilience

Deprivation and recovery

In the English and Romanian Adoptees study, a fifth (15) of the young people with more than 6 months of institutional deprivation were problem-free at every assessment. Deprivation-related ADHD was as common in females as males and mostly inattentive. [[SonugaBarke2017,Kennedy2016]]

In plain words. Parents do not cause ADHD. Smoking in pregnancy harms babies in many ways, but better studies suggest it is not why ADHD runs in families. Being born very early, severe head injury, lead and severe early neglect are linked to more ADHD. And the youngest children in a class are diagnosed more often, so a child's age in class should be part of any assessment. [[Harold2013,Obel2016,Franz2018,Asarnow2021,Goodlad2013,Layton2018]]
