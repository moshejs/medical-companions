Part 1 · The condition

What ADHD is

ADHD, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, is a neurodevelopmental condition: a persistent pattern of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity that interferes with daily life. [[APA2022DSM]] Here is how common it is, in whom it is found, and how often it lasts into adulthood.

Diagnosed and treated: the United States

Meeting criteria in a research survey is not the same as being diagnosed, and being diagnosed is not the same as being treated. Two national US surveys show the gaps, for children and for adults.

Survey data: parent report for children, self-report for adults. Children's treatment categories overlap (a child can have both), so they do not add up to 100%. [[Danielson2024,Danielson2018,Staley2024]]

Trend

More diagnosis, not more ADHD

US diagnosed ADHD among children aged 4–17 rose from 6.1% in 1997–1998 to 10.2% in 2015–2016, in every subgroup. [[Xu2018]] Yet with standardised methods, there was "no evidence to suggest an increase" over three decades in the number of children in the community who meet criteria. [[Polanczyk2014]] The World Federation consensus attributes the rise to changes in clinical and administrative practice. Established [[Faraone2021]]

Debate

Over- and under-diagnosis can coexist

The youngest children in a school year are more likely to be diagnosed (RR 1.38), [[Frisira2025]] and a scoping review of 334 studies found Early evidence of overdiagnosis at the milder end. [[Kazda2021]] At the same time, adults, women and minority groups appear under-detected. [[Chung2019,Fayyad2017,Young2020Females]] See relative age and risk.

Adults

Often untreated

In the WHO surveys only 21.8% of adults with ADHD had any mental health treatment in the past year, and 3.3% were treated specifically for ADHD. [[Fayyad2017]] Adults more often come to clinics for depression, anxiety or alcohol problems than for ADHD itself, and their ADHD symptoms are often mistaken for those conditions. [[Ginsberg2014]]

Three presentations

DSM-5 replaced "subtype" with "presentation", because the pattern of symptoms can change as people grow up. The presentation describes the past 6 months. [[Epstein2013,APA2022DSM]] In the general population the inattentive presentation is the most common; children with the combined presentation are more likely to be referred to clinics. [[Willcutt2012]]

What ADHD is not

Established

Not a lack of effort

People with ADHD of all ages show, on average, medium-sized weaknesses in executive functions such as response inhibition, vigilance, working memory and planning (effect sizes 0.46–0.69 across 83 studies), though not every person has them. [[Willcutt2005]] In girls and women, inattention is often misread as "lacking effort or motivation". [[Young2020Females]]

Established

Not caused by parenting

Twin, adoption and family studies show that ADHD runs in biological families, and shared family environment adds little. Parenting can interact with a child's behaviour, and a child's behaviour also shapes parenting, but parenting is not the main cause. [[Larsson2014,Sprich2000,Harold2013]] NICE asks clinicians to explain that a recommendation for parent training "does not imply bad parenting". [[NICE2018H]]

Established

Not new, and not only American

Physicians described attention problems in 1775 and 1798, and Still lectured on such children in 1902. [[Barkley2012Weikard,Lange2010,Still1902]] When studies use the same methods, prevalence does not differ significantly between North America, Europe, Asia, Africa, South America and Oceania. [[Faraone2021,Willcutt2012]] See 6.1 History.

Diagnosis

Not something a quiz can decide

ADHD "can only be diagnosed by a licensed clinician", and it "cannot be diagnosed by rating scales alone, neuropsychological tests, or methods for imaging the brain". [[Faraone2021]] It occurs at every level of intelligence. [[Faraone2021]] Screeners are taught in 2.3 Rating scales.
