The MTA study & long-term treatment

In the 1990s, 579 children with ADHD were randomly given one of four treatment strategies for 14 months, then followed into their mid-twenties. Scrub through the follow-ups to see the arms separate, then come back together, and why.

The design: four arms, one randomisation

Children aged 7 to 9.9 with ADHD combined type were randomised to one of four strategies for 14 months, and all outcomes were analysed by intention to treat. Play the randomisation, then pick an arm to read its protocol. The last frame colours the children who met the "success" definition (low symptoms on averaged parent and teacher ratings) at the end of treatment. [[MTA1999,Swanson2001MTA]]

Arms drawn with equal shares; the published arm sizes are not shown. Only 19.7% of participants were girls (114 of 579), which limits how far the results generalise. [[Molina2009]]

What the MTA did and did not show

Established

What it showed

  • Over 14 months, carefully managed medication, alone or combined, beat intensive behavioural treatment and community care on core ADHD symptoms. [[MTA1999]]
  • Combined treatment did not beat medication alone on core symptoms, but gave "modest advantages" on oppositional behaviour, internalising symptoms, social skills, parent-child relations and reading, and on a composite outcome (effect size 0.28 against medication management). [[MTA1999,Conners2001]]
  • The study medication strategy beat community care even though about two-thirds of community-care children were taking medication. [[MTA1999]]
  • Children with anxiety disorders did relatively well with behavioural treatment. [[MTA1999Moderators]]
Limits

What it could not show

  • Treatment was randomised only for 14 months. After that, families chose freely, so the follow-ups compare self-selected treatment, not the original arms. [[Jensen2007MTA,Molina2009]]
  • It cannot say whether years of medication improve adult outcomes: in naturalistic subgroups, longer stimulant use was not linked to lower adult symptoms, but it was linked to shorter adult height. [[Swanson2017MTA]]
  • About 1 in 5 participants were girls, and all had the combined presentation. [[Molina2009,MTA1999]]
  • Register studies find fewer substance-related emergencies during medicated months, while the MTA found no effect on adolescent substance use: different designs, different questions. [[Quinn2017,Molina2013]]

Which comes first? The sequencing trial

A sequential multiple-assignment randomised trial (146 children aged 5 to 12, one school year) started children on either low-intensity behavioural treatment (8 group parent-training sessions plus a teacher-run daily report card) or low-dose extended-release methylphenidate. Children who needed more were re-randomised to more of the same or to add the other treatment. [[Pelham2016]]

Start with

One trial of 146 children; it has not been replicated at scale. Costs are in 2013 US dollars. [[Pelham2016,Page2016]]

Does medication keep working? Breaks and discontinuation

Only the start and week-7 means are published; the lines between them are drawn straight. Doses are quoted as reported in the trial, not as advice. [[Matthijssen2019]]

Established

A randomised answer, after 2 years

94 children and adolescents who had taken methylphenidate in regular care for over 2 years either continued (36 or 54 mg/day extended-release) or were tapered to placebo over 3 weeks. Scores worsened more after withdrawal (difference −4.6, 95% CI −8.7 to −0.56), yet some could stop without deterioration, which supports periodic review of the need to continue. [[Matthijssen2019]]

NICE NG87

Planned breaks and trial periods

NICE suggests considering a planned break over school holidays to allow "catch-up" growth if medication has clearly affected a child's height, and trial periods of stopping or reducing the dose when the balance of benefit and harm suggests it. [[NICE2018F]]

Early evidence

Drug holidays in practice

A review of 22 studies found drug holidays in 25% to 70% of families, mostly in school holidays. Longer breaks were linked to better growth, and shorter breaks could reduce insomnia and improve appetite. These were mainly observational studies. [[Ibrahim2015]]

Evidence gap

Long-term evidence is thin for every treatment

An umbrella review of 221 re-estimated meta-analyses found "no high certainty, long term evidence" at 52 weeks for any ADHD intervention, and the largest network meta-analysis of ADHD medicines did not find sufficient data at 26 and 52 weeks. [[Gosling2025,Cortese2018]]
