How stimulants work

Two families, one target: the dopamine synapse. Methylphenidate jams the recycling pump; amphetamine slips in through it, empties the stores and makes the pump run backwards. Switch between no medicine, methylphenidate and amphetamine and watch the cleft.

Synapse

Schematic. Pulse shapes and heights are drawn to show the mechanisms, not measured. Transporter occupancy figures are from PET in healthy adults. [[Volkow1998,Sulzer2005]]

Not a "paradoxical" effect. In 1978, a single dose of dextroamphetamine in 14 typically developing boys reduced motor activity and reaction time and improved test performance, which "casts doubt" on the idea of a special stimulant response in ADHD. In 1980, typically developing boys, boys with hyperactivity and men all showed less motor activity, more vigilance and better learning; the men reported euphoria, the boys felt "tired" or "different". A response to a stimulant therefore cannot confirm or rule out ADHD. Established [[Rapoport1978,Rapoport1980]]

One family, several medicines

Every US stimulant label says its mode of therapeutic action in ADHD is not known, and every one carries a boxed warning for "abuse, misuse, and addiction". Stimulants are Schedule II controlled substances. [[FDA-RitalinC,FDA-AdderallXR,FDA-Concerta,FDA-Vyvanse,FDA-Strattera,FDA-Intuniv]] Doses and release curves are in Formulations & the daily curve.

Reuptake blocker

Methylphenidate

Examples
Ritalin, Concerta
Mechanism
Blocks reuptake of noradrenaline and dopamine and increases their release into the extraneuronal space (label). [[FDA-RitalinC,FDA-Concerta]]
Form
Racemic: d-threo and l-threo enantiomers; d-threo is the more active. Oral bioavailability is low (22% d, 5% l). [[FDA-RitalinC,FDA-RitalinE]]
Status
Schedule II. [[FDA-Concerta]]

Releaser + blocker

Mixed amphetamine salts

Examples
Adderall, Adderall XR
Mechanism
Non-catecholamine sympathomimetic amines: block reuptake of noradrenaline and dopamine and increase their release. [[FDA-AdderallXR]]
Form
d- and l-amphetamine in a 3:1 ratio. [[FDA-Adderall,FDA-AdderallXR]]

Schedule II. [[FDA-AdderallXR]]
Releaser + blocker

Dextroamphetamine

Examples
Zenzedi, Dexedrine Spansule
Mechanism
The d-enantiomer of amphetamine: the same release-plus-blockade actions; lisdexamfetamine is converted into it. [[FDA-AdderallXR,FDA-Vyvanse,Sulzer2005]]
Form
Zenzedi is labelled for "pediatric patients (ages 3 to 16 years)"; Dexedrine Spansule for 6 to 16. [[FDA-Zenzedi,FDA-Dexedrine]]
Status
Stimulants are Schedule II. [[FDA-Concerta,FDA-Strattera,FDA-Intuniv]]
Prodrug

Lisdexamfetamine

Examples
Vyvanse
Mechanism
Inactive itself: the parent molecule does not bind dopamine or noradrenaline reuptake sites. Red blood cells split it into dextroamphetamine and l-lysine, even at 33% of normal haematocrit, without CYP450. [[FDA-Vyvanse]]
Timing
In children 6–12, lisdexamfetamine peaks at about 1 hour and dextroamphetamine at about 3.5 hours. [[FDA-Vyvanse]]
Status
Schedule II, "a prodrug of amphetamine". [[FDA-Vyvanse]]

Exam hooks. Methylphenidate is a reuptake blocker (DAT and NET); amphetamine is a releaser plus blocker: a transporter substrate that redistributes dopamine from vesicles via VMAT2 and reverses the transporter. [[Volkow1998,Hannestad2010,Sulzer2005,Sitte2015,Faraone2018Pharm]] Lisdexamfetamine is converted to d-amphetamine and l-lysine by red blood cells, not CYP450. [[FDA-Vyvanse]] Stimulants act similarly with and without ADHD. [[Rapoport1978,Rapoport1980]] See also Non-stimulant medicines and Side effects, safety & misuse.
