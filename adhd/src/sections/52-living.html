    Supports at school, university and work

    Each setting has its own words and rules. The summaries below describe what guidelines and studies say, not legal advice; local law and policy decide what applies.

    Driving

    Relative risk against drivers without ADHD. Population statistics from a meta-analysis of 16 studies and a US claims study. [[Vaa2014,Chang2017Crash]]

    Established

    A modest raised risk, not "fourfold"

    After controlling for distance driven, drivers with ADHD had a crash relative risk of 1.23 (95% CI 1.04–1.46), similar to drivers with cardiovascular disease. The meta-analysis rejected the older claim of a roughly fourfold risk; risk was higher where most drivers also had oppositional defiant or conduct disorder. [[Vaa2014]]

    Population data

    New drivers and older drivers

    Newly licensed New Jersey drivers with ADHD had an adjusted crash rate ratio of 1.62 in their first month, and 3.6 times the rate of alcohol and/or drug violations in their first year. [[Curry2019]] In 2,832 drivers aged 65–79, ADHD was linked to more self-reported crashes (aIRR 1.74). [[Liu2023Drivers]]

    Early evidence

    Medication and crashes

    Comparing the same people on and off medication, US months on ADHD medication had lower odds of a crash-related emergency visit (men OR 0.62, women OR 0.58). [[Chang2017Crash]] In Sweden, starting medication was associated with fewer first transport accidents: 24.0 versus 27.5 per 1000 person-years. [[Zhang2025BMJ]] These are observational designs. More in Course & outcomes.

    Guideline

    What the guidelines say

    AAP 2019 asks for "special concern" to provide medication coverage while adolescents drive. [[Wolraich2019]] NICE asks clinicians to discuss that ADHD may impair driving and that medication may improve it, and notes that in the UK people with ADHD must tell the DVLA if their symptoms or medication affect their ability to drive safely. [[NICE2018F]]

    Pregnancy and breastfeeding

    Register studies: observational population data, not a recommendation to stop or continue a medicine. [[BangMadsen2024,Huybrechts2018]]

    Established

    Child development: mostly reassuring

    In 861,650 Swedish children, exposure in the womb to methylphenidate, amphetamines or atomoxetine was not linked to any neurodevelopmental disorder (aHR 0.95); pooled with Danish data, HR 1.00. [[BangMadsen2025]] Danish children whose mothers continued medication had no higher risk than those whose mothers stopped before pregnancy (aHR 0.97). [[BangMadsen2023]] A US link between methylphenidate and child ADHD disappeared with stricter control for maternal ADHD. [[Suarez2024]]

    Early evidence

    Malformations and pregnancy outcomes

    First-trimester methylphenidate had an adjusted RR of 1.28 (1.00–1.64) for cardiac malformations, at the edge of significance; amphetamines showed no increase (0.96). [[Huybrechts2018]] Atomoxetine: any major malformation 0.99 (0.74–1.34). [[Broms2023]] Stimulants in early pregnancy were linked to preeclampsia (aRR 1.29), with small absolute increases; the authors say women with significant ADHD "should not be counseled to suspend their ADHD treatment based on these findings". [[Cohen2017Placenta]] Swedish data showed more neonatal intensive care admissions (aOR 1.5), with uncertainty about how much the medicine explains. [[Norby2017]]

    Label

    Breastfeeding, as the US labels state

    Methylphenidate (Concerta): infant doses of 0.16% to 0.7% of the mother's weight-adjusted dose in limited sampling; monitor for agitation, poor sleep, feeding changes and reduced weight gain. [[FDA-Concerta]] Amphetamines (Adderall XR, Vyvanse): relative infant doses of 2% to 13.8%; "breastfeeding is not recommended". [[FDA-AdderallXR,FDA-Vyvanse]] Atomoxetine: no data in human milk. [[FDA-Strattera]] Viloxazine (Qelbree): "may cause maternal harm; discontinue when pregnancy is recognized" unless benefits outweigh the risk. [[FDA-Qelbree]] Several labels point to the National Pregnancy Registry for ADHD Medications. [[FDA-Concerta,FDA-Vyvanse,FDA-Strattera,FDA-Intuniv]]

    In Denmark, ADHD medication fills in pregnancy rose from 0.08 to 7.71 per 1000 pregnancies between 2005 and 2022. [[Madsen2025Fills]] This guide's research found no dedicated pregnancy guideline, so decisions are an individual risk–benefit discussion with the prescriber. A systematic review covers maternal ADHD, birth outcomes and early development alongside the medicines. [[KittelSchneider2021]]

    Sleep, family and moving to adult care

    Established

    Sleep problems are common

    Unmedicated children with ADHD have more bedtime resistance, trouble falling asleep, night waking and difficulty waking in the morning (16 studies). [[Cortese2009Sleep]] Stimulants lengthen sleep latency and shorten total sleep in trials with objective measures. [[Kidwell2015]] A two-session sleep hygiene programme cut moderate to severe sleep problems from 56% to 30% (NNT 3.9). [[Hiscock2015]]

    Safety

    Melatonin: helps sleep onset, store it safely

    Melatonin brought sleep onset 26.9 minutes earlier in unmedicated children with insomnia, with no effect on daytime behaviour. [[VanderHeijden2007]] In the US it is sold as a dietary supplement; content in 31 tested products ranged from −83% to +478% of the label, [[Erland2017]] and poison-centre reports of child ingestions rose 530% from 2012 to 2021. [[Lelak2022]]

    Family

    Siblings, parents and a parent with ADHD

    In a UK survey, siblings of children with ADHD reported lower happiness with life and family, and sibling bullying was more common. [[Peasgood2016]] Parents of youths with ADHD were more likely to divorce, and sooner. [[Wymbs2008]] Parental ADHD symptoms explained only 2.9% of the variance in harsh parenting, similar to other parental mental health conditions. [[Park2017Parenting]] NICE asks clinicians to consider the needs of a parent who also has ADHD, including help with organisation, adherence and school routines, and to encourage carers to seek an assessment of their own needs. [[NICE2018F]]

    Population data

    The move to adult services

    In a UK surveillance study, 64% of young people on ADHD medication had a referral accepted, 22% attended a first adult appointment and 6% had an optimal transition. [[Eke2020]] NICE says transition "should usually be completed by the time the young person is 18 years"; AAP suggests starting to plan at about age 14. [[NICE2018F,Wolraich2019]] See Nursing care.

    Often missed

    Girls and women

    A UK expert consensus says the gap in diagnosis partly reflects under-recognition and referral bias in girls, and advises attention to "more subtle and/or internalised" presentations and to coping strategies that mask symptoms. [[Young2020Females]] More in Girls, women & late diagnosis.

    Population data

    Relationships and teenage parenthood

    Research on adults points to "short-lived and discordant" romantic relationships, with little work on what protects against this. [[Wymbs2021]] In Danish registers, young women with ADHD were more likely to become parents at 12–16 (IRR 3.62); the authors suggest offering sexual education and contraceptive counselling. [[Ostergaard2017]]

    Words that help, words that hurt

    Stigma is an underestimated risk factor for poor adherence and lower wellbeing; doubt about whether the diagnosis is valid is one of its main sources. [[Mueller2012]] The World Federation consensus was written because misconceptions "stigmatize affected people, reduce credibility of providers, and prevent/delay treatment". [[Faraone2021]]
