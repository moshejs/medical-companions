Part 1 · The condition

Dopamine & noradrenaline

Two chemical messengers, the catecholamines, tune the circuits that hold attention and weigh rewards. Follow one signal across a synapse, then see why the prefrontal cortex works best with a "just right" amount, and why a reward later can feel worth less than a reward now.

Reward timing · meta-analyses

Why "later" can feel like "never"

Across 21 studies (N = 3,913), monetary delay discounting was steeper in ADHD (d = 0.43), with no difference by age, by real versus hypothetical rewards, or by conduct or oppositional problems. A second meta-analysis (37 comparisons) found small-to-medium effects favouring small immediate rewards; with real rewards in simple-choice tasks the odds ratio almost doubled. [[Jackson2016,Marx2021]]

In fMRI, the ventral striatum responds less while people with ADHD anticipate a reward (d = 0.48–0.58). Hypothesis The dopamine transfer deficit theory proposes that dopamine firing moves poorly from the reward itself to the cues that predict it, so delayed or partial rewards work less well. [[Plichta2014,Tripp2008]]

In plain wordsMany people with ADHD find waiting for a reward harder and prefer a smaller reward now to a bigger one later. [[Jackson2016,Marx2021]]

What the evidence says about catecholamines in ADHD

Hypothesis

The dopamine hypothesis and its limits

The dopamine deficit theory proposes that low dopamine signalling in frontal-striatal circuits underlies ADHD and explains why stimulants help. [[Swanson2007]] In 53 never-medicated adults, PET showed lower DAT and D2/D3 binding in the left reward pathway. [[Volkow2009]] But other scans found higher striatal DAT, a meta-analysis of 9 studies averaged 14% higher with large heterogeneity (higher in previously medicated people), and 12 months of methylphenidate raised DAT by 24%. Treatment history is a major confounder. [[Krause2000,Spencer2007DAT,FusarPoli2012,Wang2013DAT]]

Established in animal studies

Noradrenaline and alpha-2A receptors

Noradrenaline's helpful effects act at postsynaptic alpha-2A receptors on prefrontal dendritic spines, strengthening network inputs; moderate dopamine acts at D1 receptors, weakening irrelevant inputs. In monkeys, alpha-2A stimulation inhibits cAMP and closes HCN channels, strengthening working-memory firing. Blocking prefrontal alpha-2A receptors in animals produces hyperactivity, impulsivity and poor working memory. [[Arnsten2009CNS,Wang2007Cell]]

Hypothesis

Two (or three) pathways

The dual-pathway model links poor inhibitory control to the mesocortical dopamine branch (prefrontal cortex) and delay aversion to the mesolimbic branch (nucleus accumbens). [[SonugaBarke2002]] Early evidence In 71 children with ADHD, timing, inhibition and delay deficits were independent, and many children had only one. [[SonugaBarke2010]]

Open question

Dopamine, noradrenaline, or neither?

Dopamine-deficit models sit alongside prefrontal noradrenaline models, and an effective medicine also acts on serotonin receptors. Del Campo and colleagues judge that the precise mechanisms of treatment "remain to be fully specified". Catecholamine involvement is established; a single-transmitter cause is not. [[Swanson2007,Arnsten2009CNS,GarciaOlivares2026,DelCampo2011]]

Motivation

Dopamine and drive

In 45 adults with ADHD and 41 controls, trait motivation was lower in ADHD (11 ± 5 vs 14 ± 3) and tracked D2/D3 availability in the accumbens (r = 0.39) and midbrain (r = 0.41) in ADHD only. Attention ratings also correlated with D2/D3 availability. [[Volkow2011,Volkow2009]]

Executive function: how big are the differences?

Across 83 studies (3,734 with ADHD, 2,969 without), every executive function task showed a medium effect (0.46–0.69), strongest for response inhibition, vigilance, working memory and planning. The same authors concluded these weaknesses are "neither necessary nor sufficient" to cause all cases of ADHD. [[Willcutt2005]] A review of 34 meta-analyses gives the weighted effect sizes below.
