Signs across the lifespan

The 18 DSM-5-TR symptoms stay the same from preschool to old age, but the way they show changes with every new set of demands. [[APA2022DSM,Wolraich2019]] Follow one person from age 3 to 60, then switch between what other people see and what it can feel like from the inside.

Ordinary, or impairing?

Everyone loses keys and drifts off in a dull meeting. The DSM-5-TR definition turns on how often, in how many places and how much it gets in the way. Move the sliders for one everyday difficulty and see which parts of the definition it touches. [[APA2022DSM]]

Teaching toolThis shows the structure of the criteria. It is not a test and gives no verdict: ADHD can only be diagnosed by a licensed clinician. [[Faraone2021]]

Settings: home, school or work, with friends or relatives, in other activities. Symptoms that are only oppositional behaviour, defiance or failure to understand tasks do not count. [[APA2022DSM]] The full symptom count is in 2.2 DSM-5-TR criteria.

Alongside the core symptoms

Established

Emotional dysregulation

Quick frustration, irritability and mood changes are found in about 25–45% of children and 30–70% of adults with ADHD; in a population study, mood lability affected 38% of children with ADHD, a ten-fold increase. It is not part of the DSM criteria, and whether it is a core feature is still debated Hypothesis. [[Shaw2014Emotion]] George Still noted "an abnormal degree of passionateness" in 1902. [[Lange2010]]

Established

Sleep problems

Unmedicated children with ADHD show more bedtime resistance, trouble falling asleep, night waking and daytime sleepiness, [[Cortese2009Sleep]] and adults report worse sleep on 7 of 9 measures. [[DiazRoman2018]] In a trial of 244 children, two sessions of sleep-hygiene advice cut moderate to severe sleep problems from 56% to 30%. [[Hiscock2015]]

Established

Executive functions

On average, people with ADHD show medium-sized weaknesses in response inhibition, vigilance, working memory and planning (effect sizes 0.46–0.69 across 83 studies). Not every person with ADHD has them, so a normal test does not rule ADHD out. [[Willcutt2005,Faraone2021]]

Hypothesis

Strengths and "hyperfocus"

"Hyperfocus", long absorbed focus on a task of interest, is a popular term rather than one of the 18 DSM-5-TR symptoms, [[APA2022DSM]] and the studies behind this guide do not measure it. NICE asks clinicians to talk with people after diagnosis about positive effects, including building on strengths, as well as stigma. [[NICE2018H]] ADHD occurs at every level of intelligence. [[Faraone2021]]

Not a DSM term

"Rejection sensitivity"

Intense hurt at criticism or rejection is widely discussed, but it is not a DSM-5-TR criterion or diagnosis. [[APA2022DSM]] The nearest studied idea is emotional dysregulation, which is common in ADHD. [[Shaw2014Emotion]] Being rejected by peers, a different thing, is linked to hyperactive-impulsive symptoms. [[Faraone2021]] Hypothesis

Early evidence

ADHD first noticed in adulthood

In three population cohorts most adults with ADHD had not met criteria as children. [[Moffitt2015,AgnewBlais2016,Caye2016]] With repeated assessments from several informants, about 95% of apparent late-onset cases were excluded, most often because symptoms occurred only during heavy substance use. [[Sibley2018LateOnset]] DSM-5-TR still requires several symptoms before age 12. [[APA2022DSM]]
