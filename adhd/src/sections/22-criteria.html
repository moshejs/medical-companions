What a checklist cannot see

Who diagnoses

A clinician, not a scale

The World Federation of ADHD consensus states that ADHD can only be diagnosed by a licensed clinician who interviews the parent or caregiver and/or the patient. Rating scales, neuropsychological tests and brain imaging cannot diagnose it alone. [[Faraone2021]] See rating scales & assessment.

History

The whole story

The diagnosis needs a detailed evaluation of current and past symptoms and impairment, plus family, gestational and developmental history. [[Posner2020]] The AAP asks clinicians to confirm DSM-5 criteria, document symptoms and impairment in more than one major setting from parents, teachers and other school staff, and rule out other causes. [[Wolraich2019]]

Established

A continuum with a line drawn on it

ADHD behaves like the extreme end of a continuum, differing from typical development by degree rather than kind. DSM-5 and ICD-11 still draw categorical thresholds, which the Lancet seminar calls "somewhat arbitrary". [[Posner2020]]

Look-alikes

Criterion E matters

Symptoms must not be better explained by another condition, such as a mood, anxiety, dissociative or personality disorder, or substance intoxication or withdrawal. [[APA2022DSM]] Many conditions overlap with ADHD or occur with it: see look-alikes & co-occurring conditions.

Ability

High IQ does not rule it out

ADHD can be diagnosed in people with high intelligence. In a birth cohort of over 5,700 children, high-, average- and low-IQ children with ADHD did not differ in age at meeting criteria, co-occurring disorders or stimulant treatment. [[Faraone2021]]

Age 12

Why the onset age moved

Adults assessed late often cannot recall exactly when symptoms began, and some children (highly able, mainly inattentive, or in very structured settings) are not impaired until demands rise in late primary or middle school. [[Epstein2013]] Girls are among those most often missed: see girls, women & late diagnosis.
