How the thresholds changed

ManualNameWhat changed
DSM-II (1968)Hyperkinetic Reaction of ChildhoodTwo sentences focused on overactivity, noting that the behaviour "usually diminishes by adolescence". [[Lange2010,Epstein2013]]
DSM-III (1980)Attention Deficit Disorder (with or without hyperactivity)Separate symptom lists for inattention, impulsivity and hyperactivity. [[Lange2010,Epstein2013]]
DSM-III-R (1987)Attention-deficit Hyperactivity DisorderRenamed ADHD; the two subtypes were dropped. [[Lange2010]]
DSM-IV (1994)ADHD, three subtypesPredominantly inattentive, predominantly hyperactive-impulsive and combined, from a field trial of young people up to age 17. Impairing symptoms were required before age 7. [[Lange2010,Epstein2013]]
DSM-5 (2013)ADHD, three presentationsSeveral symptoms before age 12 instead of 7; 5 symptoms for older adolescents and adults; symptoms (not impairment) in two or more settings; "presentations" instead of "types"; autism no longer excludes ADHD; severity and partial-remission specifiers. [[Epstein2013]]
ICD-11 (2019; in use 2022)ADHD, 6A05Moved from disruptive to neurodevelopmental disorders and replaced "hyperkinetic disorder"; uses presentations; no precise age of onset, duration or symptom count. [[Posner2020,TebartzVanElst2025,Reed2019ICD11]]
DSM-5-TR (2022)ADHDText revision of DSM-5. Threshold: 6 or more symptoms in a domain up to age 16, 5 or more from age 17. [[APA2022DSM,Posner2020]]

Two debates, stated neutrally

Debate

Who first described ADHD?

ProposedWeikard in 1775, [[Barkley2012Weikard]] Crichton in 1798 [[Lange2010,Palmer2001]] and Still in 1902 [[Still1902]] have all been put forward.
CautionHistorians note that these descriptions only partly match modern ADHD; Still's "defect of moral control" included other kinds of behaviour. [[Lange2010,MartinezBadia2015]]
Debate

Wider criteria, more diagnoses: over-diagnosis?

ConcernA scoping review of 334 studies found evidence of over-diagnosis in milder cases; [[Kazda2021]] the youngest children in a school year are more likely to be diagnosed (RR 1.38). [[Frisira2025]]
CounterpointThe consensus statement attributes rising diagnosis to changes in administrative and clinical practice, not a true rise in prevalence; [[Faraone2021,Polanczyk2014]] the AAP notes that ADHD "goes undiagnosed in a considerable number of children and adolescents". [[Wolraich2019]]
