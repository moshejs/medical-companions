Before and around therapy

First step

Psychoeducation: understanding the diagnosis

NICE asks clinicians to discuss with people after diagnosis both the positive effects (understanding symptoms, building on strengths, better access to services) and the negative ones, "such as stigma and labelling", and to talk about work, driving and relationships. As a minimum for adults, NICE recommends a structured supportive psychological intervention focused on ADHD, with regular follow-up. [[NICE2018F]]

Early evidence

How much psychoeducation?

In a network meta-analysis of 113 adult RCTs, psychoeducation beat placebo on clinician-rated scales (SMD −0.77), but not on self-rated scales. In parent training, higher doses of psychoeducation went with worse parent outcomes. Information is a start, not a whole treatment. [[Ostinelli2025,Dekkers2022]]

NICE NG87 · adults

When non-drug treatment is offered

NICE suggests considering non-drug treatment for adults who have chosen not to take medication, have difficulty adhering, or found it ineffective or not tolerated, and combining it with medication for adults who still have significant impairment. It may include elements of, or a full course of, CBT. [[NICE2018F]]

Self-report, labelled

What adults report helps

These come from people's own accounts and uncontrolled studies, not trials. In a qualitative review of women diagnosed as adults, self-acceptance after diagnosis was a recurring theme. Adults who had coaching reported benefits, without a control group. NICE lists practical adherence supports: reminders such as apps, alarms or pill dispensers, linking doses to routines, and peer support groups. [[Attoe2023,Kubik2010,Prevatt2015,NICE2018F]]

Cost and access

Who can get it, and from whom

A family therapy for teenagers worked in a university trial, but delivered by community therapists it beat usual care only when the therapist was licensed (22% of the sample). Brief school-delivered programmes with sessions under 20 minutes did produce large gains. In a 2023 US survey, about half of adults with ADHD had used telehealth for ADHD care. [[Sibley2016STAND,Sibley2021STAND,Langberg2018,Staley2024]]

Controversy

Clinician-rated or self-rated?

The 2025 umbrella review rated adult CBT as moderate-certainty evidence on clinician ratings (SMD 0.53), similar to methylphenidate (0.50). A component network meta-analysis found CBT, mindfulness, psychoeducation and cognitive remediation beat placebo only on clinician-rated scales; only stimulants and atomoxetine improved both self- and clinician-rated core symptoms at 12 weeks. [[Gosling2025,Ostinelli2025]]

These results describe groups of people and cannot predict how one person will respond. For the medicines that CBT usually accompanies, see How well treatments work; for daily life, see Living with ADHD.
