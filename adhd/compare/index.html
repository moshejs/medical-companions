How well treatments work

Step 5 · tolerability

Who stops because of side effects?

Odds ratios above 1 mean more dropouts because of adverse effects than on placebo. Amphetamines were worse than placebo in children (OR 2.30, 1.36–3.89) and adults (3.26, 1.54–6.92). Guanfacine was worse in children only (2.64, 1.20–5.81). Atomoxetine (2.33), methylphenidate (2.39) and modafinil (4.01) were worse in adults only. 85

In plain wordsThe medicine that helps most on average is not always the easiest to stay on. The best choice balances both.
Exam hookTolerability ≠ efficacy: amphetamines top efficacy but have higher dropout odds (children OR 2.30). 85

Effect size in plain numbers

Medium: a clear average difference

At SMD 0.78, the two groups still overlap by about 70%: many people on placebo improve more than many people on the medicine. Pick a preset to see published values: Cortese 2018 gives −1.02 for amphetamines, −0.78 for methylphenidate and −0.56 for atomoxetine in children (clinician ratings); the AAP uses about 1.0 for stimulants and 0.7 for non-stimulants; parent training reached 0.35 on unblinded ADHD-symptom ratings. 85,91,493

In plain wordsEven a large effect is an average. It cannot say in advance how much one person will improve.
Exam hookLarger SMD → smaller overlap, higher probability of superiority and lower NNT; all three come from the same number.

Context: non-drug treatments and blinding

Non-drug trials usually cannot hide which treatment a family received. Effects rated by the people closest to treatment (often parents who delivered it) are "unblinded"; those from raters unlikely to know the arm are "probably blinded". Reading the evidence

Caveat

Most trials last weeks

Cortese 2018 found "insufficient data" at 26 and 52 weeks, so its ranking covers short-term treatment only. In the MTA follow-up to adulthood, extended medication use was "not associated with reduction of symptom severity". On the other side, youths switched to placebo after more than 2 years of methylphenidate worsened more than those who continued (clinician-rated worsening 40.4% vs 15.9%). Status: open; periodic review is advised. 85,279,442

Controversy

How certain is the methylphenidate evidence?

Position A: guideline groups and the World Federation treat methylphenidate as effective, and critics of the Cochrane review reported "errors in the extraction of data". Position B: the Cochrane team rates all outcomes very low certainty: 191 of 212 trials were at high risk of bias, side effects can unblind trials, and treatment lasted a mean 28.8 days; the 2025 update keeps those conclusions. Both agree on a teacher-rated SMD near −0.74 to −0.82. 30,129,428,430,431

Response

If one stimulant fails, the other often works

The AAP cites MTA titration data suggesting "more than 70%" of children are methylphenidate responders, and reports that more than 90% respond to one of the stimulants if both classes are tried, with "approximately 40% responding to both" and "about 40% responding to only 1". 493

Dose

More is not always better

In a 2026 dose-effect network meta-analysis, efficacy in children rose up to about 45 mg/day of methylphenidate, 25 mg/day of amphetamines and 4 mg/day of guanfacine, "with no evidence of additional benefit at higher doses". In adults, doses above the licensed range added little for methylphenidate (SMD −0.23) while doubling adverse-event dropouts, and nothing for amphetamines. 131,330

Adults

Self-rated vs clinician-rated

In 113 adult RCTs, stimulants (SMD −0.39 self-rated, −0.61 clinician-rated) and atomoxetine (−0.38, −0.51) were the only interventions that reduced core symptoms on both kinds of scale; CBT, mindfulness and psychoeducation improved clinician ratings only. Medicines did not improve quality of life. An umbrella review rated adult CBT (SMD 0.53) similar to methylphenidate (0.50) on clinician ratings. 178,335

Combined care

The MTA trial

In 579 children aged 7–9.9 over 14 months, carefully managed medication beat intensive behavioural treatment and community care for ADHD symptoms; combined treatment did not differ significantly from medication alone on core symptoms. See The MTA study. 295

In plain words. ADHD medicines do not cure ADHD; they lower symptoms while they are in the body. Stimulants help most people, and if one type does not help, the other type often does. 129,279,493
