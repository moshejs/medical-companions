Part 3 · Medication

Non-stimulant medicines

Four medicines treat ADHD without being stimulants. Two block the noradrenaline transporter; two switch on alpha-2 receptors. Pick one and step from the synapse to the prefrontal network to the rest of the body.

Each medicine on its label

Doses are quoted as each US label publishes them, as teaching facts, not instructions. Neither atomoxetine nor guanfacine ER is a controlled substance; the Intuniv label says guanfacine ER "has no known potential for abuse or dependence". 146,157

NET inhibitor

Atomoxetine (Strattera)

Indication
ADHD in adults and children 6 years and older, "as an integral part of a total treatment program". 157
Boxed warning
"Suicidal thoughts and behaviors in pediatric patients 6 years of age and older": suicidal ideation in 0.4% (5/1,357) of children on atomoxetine vs 0% (0/851) on placebo in 12 short-term studies. 157
Dose, as published
Under 70 kg: start 0.5 mg/kg/day, target 1.2 mg/kg/day, maximum 1.4 mg/kg/day or 100 mg/day, whichever is less. 70 kg or more and adults: start 40 mg/day, target 80 mg/day, maximum 100 mg/day. "No additional benefit has been demonstrated with STRATTERA dosages higher than 1.2 mg/kg/day". 157
Common adverse events (children vs placebo)
Headache 19% vs 15%, abdominal pain 18% vs 10%, decreased appetite 16% vs 4%, somnolence 11% vs 4%, vomiting 11% vs 6%. 157
Also
Severe liver injury warning; contraindicated with MAOIs (within 14 days), narrow-angle glaucoma, pheochromocytoma and severe cardiac or vascular disorders. Capsules taken whole, not opened. 157
NET inhibitor + 5-HT

Viloxazine ER (Qelbree)

Indication
ADHD in adults and children 6 years and older. 152
Boxed warning
"higher rates of suicidal thoughts and behavior were reported in patients with ADHD treated with Qelbree than in patients treated with placebo": 0.9% (9/1,019) vs 0.4% (2/463) in children; 1.6% (3/189) vs 0/183 in adults. 152
Dose, as published
Ages 6–11: start 100 mg once daily, maximum 400 mg. Ages 12–17: start 200 mg, maximum 400 mg. Adults: start 200 mg, maximum 600 mg. Capsules can be sprinkled on applesauce or pudding. 152
Common adverse events (adults vs placebo)
Insomnia 23% vs 7%, headache 17% vs 7%, nausea 12% vs 3%, fatigue 12% vs 3%, dry mouth 10% vs 2%, decreased appetite 10% vs 3%. 152
Also
Contraindicated with MAOIs and with sensitive or narrow-therapeutic-range CYP1A2 substrates. In children on 100 mg, 22% had a heart-rate rise of at least 20 bpm at some point, vs 9% on placebo. 152
Alpha-2A agonist

Guanfacine ER (Intuniv)

Indication
ADHD "as monotherapy and as adjunctive therapy to stimulant medications"; studied at ages 6 to 17; not established below 6. 146
Dose, as published
Begin 1 mg once daily, increase by no more than 1 mg/week; target 0.05–0.12 mg/kg/day (total 1–7 mg/day). Not with high-fat meals; do not crush or chew; not interchangeable mg for mg with immediate-release guanfacine. 146
Adverse events
Somnolence rose with dose: 11% placebo, 28% at 1 mg, 30% at 2 mg, 38% at 3 mg, 51% at 4 mg. Hypotension 7% vs 3%. 146
Stopping
Abrupt discontinuation "can lead to clinically significant and persistent rebound hypertension"; the label tapers by no more than 1 mg every 3 to 7 days. 146
Alpha-2 agonist

Clonidine ER

Indication
ADHD as monotherapy or as an add-on to stimulants; established at ages 6 to 17. 139
Dose, as published
0.1 mg at bedtime, rising by 0.1 mg/day weekly, given twice a day with an equal or larger bedtime dose. Doses above 0.4 mg/day "were not evaluated in clinical trials for ADHD and are not recommended". 139
Adverse events
Somnolence 38% at 0.2 mg/day and 31% at 0.4 mg/day vs 4% on placebo (monotherapy); 19% vs 7% as an add-on to stimulants. 139
Stopping
Taper by no more than 0.1 mg every 3 to 7 days to avoid rebound hypertension. 139

Where they fit

Guidelines

Usually after a stimulant

NICE offers methylphenidate first in children 5 and over, and lisdexamfetamine or methylphenidate first in adults; atomoxetine is offered to adults if neither is tolerated or effective after separate 6-week trials. The AAP grades the evidence for school-age children "particularly strong for stimulant medications" and "sufficient, but not as strong, for atomoxetine, extended-release guanfacine, and extended-release clonidine, in that order". 324,493

Effect

Moderate, not large

Established The World Federation consensus describes moderate improvements with atomoxetine at all ages and with extended-release guanfacine in children, against large ones with amphetamines. The AAP gives an effect size of about 0.7 for non-stimulants and 1.0 for stimulants. 129,493

Reasons to choose one

Misuse concerns, intolerance, add-on

Stimulants "are more effective than non-stimulant medications but are also more likely to be diverted, misused, and abused". Non-stimulants are not controlled substances, and both alpha-2 agonists are labelled as add-ons to a stimulant. On tics, a meta-analysis of 22 trials found new or worse tics equally common on stimulants (5.7%) and placebo (6.5%). 76,129,139,146,157

Heart and blood pressure

Both directions

Atomoxetine and viloxazine raise pulse in children (by 5.58 and 2.79 bpm vs placebo in a network meta-analysis); guanfacine lowers it (−4.06 bpm) and lowers systolic pressure (−2.83 mm Hg). NICE asks for a dose reduction or switch if sustained orthostatic hypotension or fainting occurs on guanfacine. 132,324

In plain words. Non-stimulants take longer to work than stimulants. On atomoxetine or viloxazine, families should watch for new sad, hopeless or suicidal thoughts and tell the doctor right away. Guanfacine and clonidine should not be stopped suddenly, because blood pressure can jump. 75,139,146,152,157
