Part 2 · Recognising & diagnosing

Rating scales & assessment

Questionnaires such as the ASRS, Vanderbilt, SNAP-IV and Conners collect information quickly, but none of them diagnoses ADHD. Follow one person through a full assessment, see what a screener does, and watch why a positive screen is often a false alarm.

How a screener works: a walkthrough

Illustrative questions, not a testThese six questions were written for this guide to show how a frequency-based screener is built; each one is tied to a DSM-5-TR symptom. They are not the ASRS wording, have no validated scoring, and give no result. If you are worried about ADHD, talk to a doctor; online quizzes cannot tell you whether you have it. 16,129

Why a positive screen is often a false alarm

Pick a scale and a setting. Each figure is one of 1,000 people. The test's published sensitivity and specificity split them into true and false positives and negatives; press Sort into groups to gather them. Move the prevalence down and watch the share of true positives fall.

Accuracy figures are published; PPV and NPV here are calculated from them. Real accuracy changes with the setting: ASRS-5 specificity was 96.0% in population data but 74.0% in a clinic sample. Prevalence sources: 225,385,403,451,458

Who is asked changes the answer

Definition

Hyperactive children followed to age 19–25. 35

Informants

Ask more than one person

Young adults reported far fewer symptoms than their parents did. In the MTA follow-up, persistence was also higher with parent reports than self-reports, and with interviews than with rating scales; combining parent and self-report gave 60% symptom persistence and 41% symptom-plus-impairment persistence. 35,396

In plain wordsPeople often under-rate their own symptoms, so a clinician asks someone who knows them well too.
Exam hookLate-onset adult cases need care: about 95% of people who first screened positive in adulthood were excluded on full repeated assessment, most often because symptoms occurred only with heavy substance use. 397

Norms

Scales normed on boys

Most rating-scale norms come from male or mixed samples, which may disadvantage girls. The female ADHD consensus recommends female norms where they exist and more weight on collateral information. 499 More in girls, women & late diagnosis.

Feigning

Symptoms can be over-reported

In college students most self-report questionnaires were insensitive to feigned ADHD; failing 3 or more symptom validity tests was the most useful sign. The AAP notes some adolescents may feign symptoms to obtain stimulants. 300,493

Parents vs teachers

Different views, both valid

In 6,659 children aged 4–17, parents rated symptoms as more severe than teachers did, and agreement on inattention was weak. Each informant adds valid information. 302

Established

No biomarker yet

Neuropsychological and imaging differences between groups are small and overlap with other conditions, so they are not useful for diagnosis. 129 Look-alikes to check are covered in look-alikes & co-occurring conditions.

ADHD Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on attention deficit hyperactivity disorder in children and adults, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people with ADHD and their families.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose ADHD, and nothing here should start, stop or change a treatment without your own clinician. Rating scales and the criteria calculator are teaching tools, not tests. Criteria, doses and guideline statements are quoted as published.
