Measuring disability: EDSS

The Expanded Disability Status Scale runs from 0 (normal exam) to 10 in half-steps. Climb the staircase to see what each step means, try a teaching calculator, and see when worsening counts as confirmed.

Exam findings, walking unaffected

The EDSS (Kurtzke, 1983) expanded the earlier 0–10 Disability Status Scale into half-steps. It rests on eight functional systems: pyramidal, cerebellar, brainstem, sensory, bowel and bladder, visual, cerebral and other. 0 is a normal examination; 1.0 to 3.5 reflect increasing signs or disability in the functional systems while the person is fully ambulatory. Agreement between raters is weakest in this range. 109,267,315

Try it: a teaching calculator

A teaching tool, not a clinical score. A real EDSS comes from a full neurological examination by a trained examiner, who grades each functional system and applies Kurtzke's detailed rules. This simplified version does not reproduce those rules: it uses only the published walking and care anchors, and places any examination findings with full walking in the 1.0–3.5 range. Even trained raters disagree: reported inter-rater kappa is 0.32–0.76 for the total score and 0.23–0.58 for individual functional systems. 315

When does worsening count? Confirmed disability progression

In trials, confirmed disability accumulation is typically an EDSS rise of at least 1.0 point from a baseline of 5.5 or less, or at least 0.5 point from a baseline above 5.5, that is still there at a visit 12 or 24 weeks later. Composite definitions add a 20% or greater worsening on the timed 25-foot walk or the 9-hole peg test. Watch one person's visits below: a short-lived rise and a lasting one. 237

Confirmed, or just a bad patch?

A rise counts only if it reaches +1.0 (baseline 5.5 or less) and is still there 12 weeks later. Rise 1 (week 24): confirmed at week 36. It then fell back at week 48: a "confirmed" event that did not last. Rise 2 (week 72): confirmed at week 84.

Beyond EDSS: other measures

MSFC

MS Functional Composite

Built in 1999 from three timed tests: the Timed 25-Foot Walk (legs), the 9-Hole Peg Test (arms) and the PASAT (cognition), combined as a z-score. Change over one year predicted later change in EDSS, which suggests it is more sensitive to change. Its main limitations are learning effects and the z-score scoring. 105,315

T25FW · 9HPT

Timed walk and peg test

The Timed 25-Foot Walk and the 9-Hole Peg Test are used alone too. In trial composites, a 20% or greater worsening on either counts toward confirmed disability progression. Walking speed is also how fampridine response was measured: 35% timed-walk responders vs 8% on placebo. 173,237

SDMT

Symbol Digit Modalities Test

Measures processing speed. A change of about 4 points or 10% has been proposed as clinically meaningful. It is brief, reliable and sensitive enough to monitor cognition, including during acute disease activity. 28,29

Limitations

Why EDSS is criticised

It is ordinal, not a ruler: the lower range rests on the examination, 4–6 depends heavily on walking, and above 6 it measures handicap. Variability between raters is greatest at 1.0–3.5. Across 48 progression definitions in MSBase, concordance was only 17.3%, and 11–34% of progression events regressed within 5 years. 231,315
