Part 1 · The disease

What MS is

Multiple sclerosis is a long-term disease in which the immune system damages myelin, the insulation on nerve fibres in the brain, optic nerves and spinal cord. Step through the numbers, who gets it and where it strikes, then pick a symptom to see where it comes from.

Step 1 · The numbers

About 2.8 to 3.1 million people

The Atlas of MS, 3rd edition (country survey 2019–2020, 115 countries covering 87% of the world population) estimated 2.8 million people living with MS, a prevalence of 35.9 per 100,000. MSIF's 2024 update (133 countries, 93% of the world population) estimated 3.1 million and 38.1 per 100,000. They are two estimates made with different coverage and methods, not one count that grew by 300,000. 245,490 Among countries that reported in both 2013 and 2020, prevalence by WHO region in 2020 ran from 142.8 per 100,000 in Europe to 4.8 in the Western Pacific. 490

In plain wordsRoughly three million people in the world live with MS. It is far more common in Europe and the Americas than in Africa or East Asia.

Symptoms and where they come from

MS can affect any part of the central nervous system, so what a person notices depends on where the lesions are. Pick a symptom, or hover over one, to light the part it usually comes from. These are typical links, not a way to tell what is causing anyone's symptoms.

Optic nerve

Blurred, dim or painful vision in one eye

This is optic neuritis, inflammation of the optic nerve. It was the first attack in 34.1% of the Barcelona cohort. 473 In the Optic Neuritis Treatment Trial the 15-year risk of MS after optic neuritis was 50%: 25% with no brain lesions on the first MRI and 72% with at least one. 370 At 15 years, 72% of affected eyes saw 20/20 or better. 371

Exam hookMS is a disease of the central nervous system. CNS myelin is made by oligodendrocytes, peripheral myelin by Schwann cells, and the peripheral nerves are not the MS target. 247 Typical patient: a young adult woman; mean age at diagnosis 32, female to male about 2–3 : 1. 489,490
In plain wordsMS is when the body's defence system damages the insulation on the nerve wires of the brain, eyes and spinal cord. Messages along those wires slow down or stop, and what a person feels depends on which wires are hit. It is not contagious.

Controversy

Is MS becoming more common?

Atlas estimates rose from 2.8 to 3.1 million, but the authors stress changes in method and gaps in data from lower-income countries. GBD found a 10.4% rise in age-standardised prevalence from 1990 to 2016. Longer survival and better case-finding also raise prevalence. 162,245,291,489,490
