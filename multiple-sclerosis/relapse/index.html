Days 0–3

Symptoms build

New inflammation strips myelin and blocks conduction, the main cause of negative symptoms such as weakness, numbness or loss of vision. To count as a relapse, symptoms must last at least 24 hours without fever or infection (McDonald 2010 panel); NICE NG220 adds a stable period of at least 1 month before. A new lesion can enhance with gadolinium on MRI. 354,386,440

In plain wordsOver a day or a few days, a symptom appears or an old one gets clearly worse, and it does not go away after a day.

What counts as a relapse

McDonald 2010 panel

The attack definition

Patient-reported symptoms or objectively observed signs typical of an acute inflammatory demyelinating event in the central nervous system, lasting at least 24 hours, in the absence of fever or infection. Paroxysmal symptoms count only as multiple episodes over not less than 24 hours. 386

NICE NG220 (2022)

How the guideline describes it

New or worsening symptoms lasting more than 24 hours, with no infection or other cause, after a stable period of at least 1 month. Clinicians are asked to rule out infection (particularly urinary and respiratory) and to tell a relapse apart from fluctuation or progression. A relapse should not routinely be diagnosed when symptoms have been present for more than 3 months. 354

Fits the published description

New or worse symptoms for more than 24 hours, without infection, after at least a month of stability: this matches the NICE NG220 wording and the McDonald 2010 attack definition. Only a clinician can make the diagnosis. 354,386

Controversy

Oral or IV? Dose, not route

High-dose oral methylprednisolone was no worse than IV in COPOUSEP and in the Cochrane review. The ONTT's worse result with oral prednisone used a low dose (1 mg/kg). NICE uses oral 0.5 g a day for 5 days and says lower doses should not be used. 27,59,282,354

Established

Infections and relapses

In 73 people followed weekly (6,466 patient-weeks), the risk of a relapse was 2.1 times higher from 2 weeks before to 5 weeks after a systemic infection, and those relapses more often left a sustained deficit (rate ratio 3.8). So a fever can bring a pseudo-relapse, and can also come before a true one. 56

Looking for a urinary infection

At a suspected relapse, a possible urinary tract infection was found in 30.4% of inpatients and 20.0% of clinic patients at Johns Hopkins: about 1 in 3 and 1 in 5 screened. Female sex went with infection (OR 3.90). 150

Why recovery takes weeks

Remission comes from resolution of inflammation, remyelination, or conduction restored along fibres that stay demyelinated. In CombiRx, relapse severity was the only factor linked to the speed of recovery: of those who recovered, 80% had done so by 180 days and 94% by 365 days. 252,440
