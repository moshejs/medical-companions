Glossary

A searchable glossary of multiple sclerosis terms, from κ-FLC and PIRA to S1P modulators and NMOSD. Type to filter; the arrow on each entry jumps to the module where the term matters most.

130 of 130 terms

9-Hole Peg Test (9HPT)Timed test of hand dexterity: placing and removing nine pegs. A 20% change is the commonly used threshold for meaningful worsening 138.
Accelerated eliminationWashing teriflunomide out of the body with cholestyramine or activated charcoal over 11 days, for example before a planned pregnancy 14.
Activity (course modifier)Clinical relapses and/or MRI activity (gadolinium-enhancing or new or enlarging T2 lesions), assessed at least yearly; used to label any phenotype active or not active 287.
ADEMAcute disseminated encephalomyelitis: a demyelinating illness with encephalopathy, commoner in children. In children with ADEM, the 2024 MS criteria apply only after a further typical attack or new typical lesions more than 90 days after onset 218.
AdherenceTaking a medicine as prescribed. Reported adherence to MS DMTs ranges widely, and claims data showed no difference between self-injected and oral drugs 312,338.
aHSCTAutologous haematopoietic stem cell transplantation: chemotherapy depletes the immune system, which is then rebuilt from the person's own stem cells. Studied mainly in highly active relapsing MS 340.
AlemtuzumabAnti-CD52 antibody that depletes T and B cells, NK cells and monocytes; given in two yearly courses, with long monitoring for secondary autoimmunity 280.
Annualised relapse rate (ARR)The number of relapses per person per year; the usual primary endpoint in relapsing-MS trials.
Anti-CD20 antibodiesOcrelizumab, ofatumumab, ublituximab (and off-label rituximab): antibodies that lyse pre-B and mature B cells, sparing stem cells and most plasma cells 175,364.
Anti-JCV antibody indexA blood test for antibodies to the JC virus. Positivity and a higher index raise PML risk on natalizumab 209.
AQP4-IgGAntibody against aquaporin-4, the water channel on astrocytes; with at least one core clinical characteristic it defines AQP4-positive NMOSD. Cell-based assays are recommended 500.
Area postrema syndromeIntractable hiccups or nausea and vomiting from a lesion in the medulla; an NMOSD core characteristic 500.
Assisted reproductive technology (ART)Fertility treatment such as IVF. Pooled case series showed more relapses in the 3 months after ART; the authors' own prospective cohort did not 47.
Attack (relapse)Patient-reported symptoms with objective findings typical of an inflammatory demyelinating CNS event, lasting at least 24 hours, without fever or infection 444.
B cellLymphocyte that presents antigen and makes cytokines and antibodies. Benefit from B-cell depletion appears before antibody levels fall, pointing to antigen presentation and cytokines rather than antibody 401.
BaclofenMuscle relaxant and the NICE first-line oral drug for spasticity 353. It can also be given intrathecally through a pump 406.
Benign MSA severity label for mild disability after many years. The 2013 revision says it should be used only in hindsight and with caution 287.
Blood-brain barrierThe sealed endothelium of CNS vessels. In a new lesion it leaks gadolinium, which is how active lesions are seen on MRI 401.
Braden ScaleSix-item nursing tool for pressure-ulcer risk: sensory perception, moisture, activity, mobility, friction and shear, nutrition 34.
Breakthrough diseaseDisease activity on an established DMT: in AAN 2018 terms, 1 or more relapses, 2 or more new MRI lesions or increased disability within a year, in an adherent patient 395.
BTK inhibitorOral drug blocking Bruton's tyrosine kinase in B cells and microglia; CNS-penetrant ones may reach smouldering inflammation behind the barrier 258.
CAR-T cellsA patient's T cells engineered to kill a target such as CD19 or BCMA on B or plasma cells; early reports exist in progressive MS 145,394.
CCSVIChronic cerebrospinal venous insufficiency, a proposed venous cause of MS that blinded imaging and a sham-controlled trial rejected 471,511.
CD8+ T cellCytotoxic T cell; the dominant lymphocyte in MS lesions, often clonally expanded 17,292.
Central vein sign (CVS)A thin dark line or dot through the centre of a white-matter lesion on susceptibility MRI: the vein the lesion formed around. Supportive evidence in McDonald 2024 326.
Charcot's triadNystagmus, dysarthria and ataxia with intention tremor, from Charcot's 1868 lectures 509.
Chronic active lesionA lesion with an inactive centre and an active rim of microglia and macrophages that slowly expands; also called mixed active/inactive or smouldering 264.
CladribineOral purine analogue that builds up in lymphocytes and depletes them; given as two yearly short courses 303.
ClemastineAn old antihistamine with antimuscarinic action that shortened VEP latency in ReBUILD; a TRAP-MS arm in people with PIRA was stopped for safety 174,253.
Clinically isolated syndrome (CIS)A first clinical episode suggestive of demyelination that does not yet meet dissemination in time 287.
Compartmentalised inflammationInflammation trapped inside the CNS behind a closed barrier 273, carried by tissue-resident CD8+ T cells and B cells, with activated microglia 258,292.
Conduction blockFailure of an impulse to cross a demyelinated segment; the main cause of the negative symptoms of a relapse 439.
Confirmed disability accumulationA trial endpoint: an EDSS rise (1.0, or 0.5 from above 5.5) confirmed at 12 or 24 weeks 236.
Cooling strategiesCooling garments, drinks or surroundings used before or during heat or exercise to lessen heat sensitivity 429,435.
CYP2C9 genotypingGene test required before siponimod, because the enzyme clears the drug 305.
Dalfampridine (fampridine)Prolonged-release 4-aminopyridine, a potassium-channel blocker labelled to improve walking speed 135.
Dawson's fingersPeriventricular lesions running perpendicular to the ventricles along the medullary veins 140.
DemyelinationLoss of the myelin sheath, which slows or blocks conduction along the axon 439.
Detrusor overactivityInvoluntary bladder-muscle contractions causing urgency and frequency; found in 42.9% of people with MS on urodynamics in a meta-analysis 4.
Disease-modifying therapy (DMT)A drug that reduces relapses and MRI activity and may slow disability, as opposed to a symptomatic treatment.
Dissemination in space (DIS)Evidence of lesions in at least two separate parts of the CNS 326,463.
Dissemination in time (DIT)Evidence that lesions arose at different times: a new lesion on follow-up, enhancing and non-enhancing lesions together, or a second attack 142.
DysphagiaDifficulty swallowing; common in MS and a risk for aspiration pneumonia 136,317.
EAEExperimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, the main animal model of MS, induced by immunisation with CNS antigens 93.
Early high-efficacy therapyStarting with a high-efficacy DMT rather than stepping up; favoured by observational studies, with randomised trials awaited 182,190.
EBNA1Epstein-Barr virus nuclear antigen 1. Antibodies to it cross-react with the CNS proteins GlialCAM and anoctamin-2 271,461.
EDSSExpanded Disability Status Scale: 0 (normal) to 10 (death due to MS) in half steps, built on eight Functional Systems and, from 4.0, on walking 266.
Energy conservationPlanning, pacing and prioritising activities to manage fatigue; a meta-analysis of 2 trials found short-term benefit 40, but a later trial found it no better than nurse information on fatigue 41.
Epstein-Barr virus (EBV)Herpesvirus that causes glandular fever; infection appears to precede nearly all MS 39.
EscalationStarting with a moderate-efficacy DMT and switching up when there is breakthrough disease 324.
Extended-interval dosingGiving natalizumab every 6 weeks instead of 4 to lower PML risk; NOVA tested it 151, and the US label still states every 4 weeks 474.
FatigueOverwhelming tiredness, reported by 95% of people in a European survey 250; a meta-analysis put global prevalence at 59.1%, varying with the instrument 506. Heat and biological, physical or emotional stress can bring it on 353.
First-dose bradycardiaThe fall in heart rate within hours of a first S1P modulator dose. The fingolimod label requires at least 6 hours of observation; siponimod uses titration, with first-dose monitoring for people with some heart conditions 163,305.
FLAIR (3D)Fluid-attenuated MRI sequence that dims CSF so lesions stand out; the core brain sequence for diagnosis and monitoring 492.
FumaratesDimethyl and diroximel fumarate, oral DMTs that activate the Nrf2 pathway (the label calls the therapeutic mechanism unknown); flushing and gut upset are common early 459.
Functional SystemsThe eight domains scored for the EDSS: pyramidal, cerebellar, brainstem, sensory, bowel and bladder, visual, cerebral and other 266.
Gadolinium enhancementContrast leaking through a broken blood-brain barrier, marking a lesion that is currently active; it fades within weeks 99.
GFAPGlial fibrillary acidic protein, an astrocyte protein measured in blood as a candidate marker of progression 311.
Glatiramer acetateA mixture of synthetic polypeptides injected under the skin; one of the first DMTs 95.
HLA-A*02:01A class I HLA allele that protects against MS 330.
HLA-DRB1*15:01The HLA class II allele that is the strongest single genetic risk factor for MS 330.
IgG indexRatio comparing CSF and serum IgG to albumin; not a sufficient substitute for oligoclonal bands or κ-FLC 114.
Infratentorial lesionA lesion in the brainstem, cerebellar peduncles or cerebellum 444.
Infusion reactionA reaction during or up to 24 hours after an antibody infusion; premedication and post-infusion observation are label requirements 364.
Interferon betaInjected cytokine DMTs (beta-1a, beta-1b, peginterferon); flu-like symptoms are common at the start 399.
Intermittent self-catheterisationPassing a catheter several times a day to empty a bladder that does not empty fully 152.
JC virusA common polyomavirus that can reactivate in the brain to cause PML 474.
Juxtacortical lesionA lesion touching the cortex with no white matter in between 444.
κ-FLC indexKappa free light chain index: CSF/serum κ-FLC divided by CSF/serum albumin; a value of 6.1 or more counts as positive CSF in McDonald 2024 114.
LETMLongitudinally extensive transverse myelitis: a cord lesion over 3 or more vertebral segments, typical of NMOSD 500.
Lhermitte phenomenonAn electric sensation down the spine or limbs on bending the neck; a positive symptom of demyelinated axons 439.
Live attenuated vaccineA vaccine with weakened live organisms. ECTRIMS/EAN 2023 advises avoiding them on immunosuppressive DMTs and giving them at least 4 weeks before starting (6 for ocrelizumab and alemtuzumab) 374.
LymphopeniaA low lymphocyte count, expected with cladribine and monitored with fumarates and S1P modulators 163,303,459.
McDonald criteriaThe international MS diagnostic criteria, first published in 2001 and revised in 2005, 2010, 2017 and 2024 306,326.
MFISModified Fatigue Impact Scale, with physical, cognitive and psychosocial subscales 275.
MicrogliaResident immune cells of the CNS; activated, inflamed microglia line the rims of chronic active lesions 3,513.
MisdiagnosisLabelling a person with MS who does not have it; pooled about 15%, and migraine was the commonest true diagnosis in US series 228,442,514.
MitoxantroneAn older cytotoxic drug for worsening MS, limited by heart damage and leukaemia risk 318.
MOG-IgGAntibody against myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein; its presence defines MOGAD 19.
MOGADMOG antibody-associated disease, a separate demyelinating disease with optic neuritis, myelitis or ADEM 19.
Molecular mimicryA virus protein resembling a self protein, so the immune response cross-reacts; proposed for EBNA1 and GlialCAM 271.
MS specialist nurseA nurse who supports diagnosis, treatment starts, monitoring, adherence and self-management in MS 57,353.
MSFCMS Functional Composite: timed 25-foot walk, 9-hole peg test and a cognitive test, averaged as z-scores 105.
NatalizumabAnti-α4 integrin antibody infused every 4 weeks that blocks immune-cell entry to the CNS; PML is the main risk 474.
NEDA-3No evident disease activity: no relapses, no confirmed disability progression and no new MRI activity 166.
Neurofilament light chain (NfL)A neuron-specific protein released when axons are damaged, measured in blood; not a diagnostic test 30,114.
NMOSDNeuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder, often with AQP4-IgG, which can mimic MS; several MS DMTs may worsen it 500.
No better explanationThe rule that MS is diagnosed only after other causes have been considered and excluded 444.
Node of RanvierThe short gap between myelin segments where sodium channels cluster and the impulse is regenerated 9.
OCTOptical coherence tomography: a light scan that measures retinal layer thickness; an inter-eye difference can show optic nerve involvement 417.
Oligoclonal bands (OCB)Bands of IgG made inside the CNS, seen in CSF but not serum; present in most people with MS 118.
OligodendrocyteThe CNS cell that makes myelin, wrapping internodes on many axons 247.
OPCOligodendrocyte precursor cell; activated, it migrates, multiplies and matures to remyelinate 157.
Optic neuritisInflammation of the optic nerve with loss of vision; the site of 34.1% of first attacks in the Barcelona cohort 473.
Paramagnetic rim lesion (PRL)A lesion with an iron-laden rim on susceptibility MRI; a candidate marker of a chronic active lesion and supportive evidence in McDonald 2024 2,326.
Periventricular lesionA lesion touching the lateral ventricles with no white matter in between 444.
PHQ-9Nine-item depression questionnaire, with high sensitivity for major depression in MS 301.
PIRAProgression independent of relapse activity: worsening with no relapse around it, now seen from early in the disease 236,288.
PMLProgressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy: a brain infection by JC virus, the main risk of natalizumab 474.
Post-void residualUrine left in the bladder after voiding, measured by ultrasound to guide bladder management 352.
Postpartum relapseA relapse in the months after giving birth. In PRIMS, rates rose for about 3 months before returning to baseline 89; a later cohort found no rise above pre-pregnancy rates 270.
PPMSPrimary progressive MS: gradual worsening from onset without preceding relapses 287.
PRIMSThe Pregnancy in Multiple Sclerosis study, which mapped relapse rates before, during and after pregnancy 89.
Progressive relapsing MSA 1996 phenotype removed in 2013; such people are now called primary progressive, active 287.
Pseudo-relapseTemporary worsening of old symptoms from infection, heat or stress, without new inflammation 353.
Radiologically isolated syndrome (RIS)MS-like MRI lesions found by chance in someone with no symptoms 218.
ReboundDisease activity above pre-treatment levels after stopping natalizumab or an S1P modulator 163,184.
Red flagsFeatures that point away from MS, such as hyperacute onset, severe deficits or a CSF cell count above 50/μL 316,444.
Relapse-associated worsening (RAW)In trials, a disability increase that begins within 90 days of a relapse 236.
RemyelinationFormation of new myelin on demyelinated axons; shadow plaques are its trace 157,391.
RRMSRelapsing-remitting MS: attacks followed by full or partial recovery 287.
S1P receptor modulatorsFingolimod, siponimod, ozanimod and ponesimod: oral DMTs that keep lymphocytes in lymph nodes 163,305.
Saltatory conductionThe impulse jumping from node to node along a myelinated axon, which makes it fast 247.
Schumacher criteriaThe 1965 criteria for MS therapy trials, the forerunner of later diagnostic criteria 428.
Sclérose en plaquesCharcot's name for MS in his 1868 lectures at La Salpêtrière 509.
SDMTSymbol Digit Modalities Test of processing speed; a change of about 4 points or 10% is considered meaningful 28.
Secondary autoimmunityNew autoimmune disease, mostly thyroid, after immune reconstitution following alemtuzumab 414.
Select-6The McDonald 2024 central vein sign rule: at least 6 CVS-positive lesions; with fewer than 10 lesions, CVS-positive must outnumber CVS-negative 326,444.
Shadow plaqueA lesion that has remyelinated. New lesions normally remyelinate unless new activity interrupts them 391.
Smouldering MSA hypothesis: a diffuse, compartmentalised process present from early disease that drives disability beyond relapses 167.
Smouldering-associated worsening (SAW)A 2024 consensus term covering physical and cognitive decline from smouldering processes, broader than PIRA 426.
SpasticityStiffness and spasms from upper motor neuron damage; NICE lists spasms, stiffness, pain or restriction with movement as signs to look for 353.
SPMSSecondary progressive MS: gradual worsening after an initial relapsing course, usually recognised in hindsight 287.
Th17 cellA CD4+ T-cell subset making IL-17 and IL-22, which loosen blood-brain barrier junctions 239.
Timed 25-Foot Walk (T25FW)The time to walk 25 feet as fast as safely possible; a 20% rise is often used as meaningful change 238.
Trigeminal neuralgiaBrief stabbing facial pain; about 3.4% of people with MS have it in a meta-analysis 212.
Uhthoff phenomenonBrief, reversible worsening with heat, exercise or fever from conduction failure in demyelinated axons 160.
VEPVisual evoked potential: the delayed P100 wave shows slowed conduction in a demyelinated optic nerve 177,417.
Vitamin D (25-hydroxyvitamin D)The blood marker of vitamin D status; higher levels are linked to lower MS risk 336.
VLA-4α4β1 integrin on lymphocytes that lets them stick to inflamed vessels; natalizumab's target 504.
