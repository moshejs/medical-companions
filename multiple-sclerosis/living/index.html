Living with MS

Before pregnancy

The year before

PRIMS followed 254 women through 269 pregnancies in 12 European countries. In the year before pregnancy their annualised relapse rate was 0.7. More recent cohorts started lower: 0.37 in Kaiser Permanente (2008–2016) and 0.32 in MSBase. 89,216,271 NICE NG220 asks clinicians to explain that MS should not stop people planning a family, and mentions vitamin D and folic acid before and during pregnancy. 354

In plain wordsHaving MS is not, on its own, a reason not to have children. Which medicines to stop, and when, is a separate planning question.
Medicines before and during pregnancy are not covered here: which disease-modifying therapies need stopping, and how long before conception, is a drug-label question. See Safety & monitoring. The UK consensus from the Association of British Neurologists covers pre-pregnancy counselling, delivery, anaesthesia and the postpartum period. 120

Smoking after diagnosis

In the Swedish GEMS study of 728 people who smoked when they were diagnosed, each additional year of smoking after diagnosis brought conversion to secondary progressive MS (SPMS) forward by 4.7%. Press play to run both groups through the same ages.

The ages are the published approximate ages at conversion; the 40–60 window and the walking figures are for display. 397

GEMS, Sweden

Same start, different paths

All 728 people in this analysis smoked when they were diagnosed. Some carried on and some stopped. Each additional year of smoking after diagnosis brought conversion to SPMS forward by 4.7% (acceleration factor 1.047). 397

In plain wordsStopping smoking after a diagnosis of MS is linked with a later move to the progressive phase.

Life expectancy

Median ages from two population cohorts. A standardised mortality ratio (SMR) of 1 means the same death rate as the general population. 292,298

Norway, 60 years

About 7 years shorter

In a western Norway cohort of 1388 patients followed for 60 years, median life expectancy was 74.7 years with MS vs 81.8 in the general population: 77.8 for relapsing-remitting and 71.4 for primary progressive MS. The overall SMR was 2.7, and MS itself was the cause in 56.4% of deaths. 292

In plain wordsOn average, people with MS in this cohort lived about 7 years less than others in the same region.

Everyday life

Work

Across 153 studies (188,436 people in 29 countries), 35.6% of people with MS were unemployed and 17.2% had retired early; unemployment has fallen slightly over time. In a European survey, work capacity fell from 82% at mild disability to 8% at severe disability. 251,486

Heat

An estimated 60–80% of people with MS have temporary worsening with heat. Cooling before or during exercise can reduce exercise-induced heat sensitivity, and a cooling-garment trial found modest benefits. See heat sensitivity on the symptom map. 111,430,436

Established

Exercise

NICE NG220 tells clinicians to encourage exercise and to say it has no harmful effects on MS. In the Cochrane review, relapses were similar with and without exercise. 204,354

Vaccines and infections, in brief

The AAN 2019 guideline: follow local schedules with yearly influenza vaccination, vaccinate at least 4–6 weeks before starting immune therapy, avoid live vaccines while on it, and delay vaccination during a relapse. Infections matter: relapse risk was 2.1 times higher from 2 weeks before to 5 weeks after a systemic infection. Drug-specific advice is in Safety & monitoring. 56,132

Rules vary by country

Driving

Licensing rules differ between countries. In the UK, the DVLA web page (a regulator page, listed as unverified in our sources because it has no peer-reviewed record) says drivers with MS must notify the DVLA, and that car drivers may keep driving while they can control a vehicle safely, possibly on a shorter licence or with adapted controls. 123

Controversy

Diet

No diet is proven to change MS outcomes: a Cochrane review of 30 trials found insufficient evidence, all at unknown or high risk of bias, and NICE says no specific diet is known to improve fatigue while a healthy diet helps general health. In WAVES (87 people), both the Swank low-saturated-fat diet and the Wahls modified Paleolithic diet were followed by better fatigue scores than at baseline (MFIS −9.87 and −14.41), but there was no non-diet control group. 354,378,489

Hypothesis

Alcohol

In the CLIMB cohort, more than 4 drinks a week was associated with lower EDSS than abstinence, and red wine with faster T2 lesion growth. These are cross-sectional associations that cannot show cause and effect. 116

Early evidence

Vitamin D

SOLAR (high-dose D3 on top of interferon) missed its primary endpoint (NEDA-3 36.3% vs 35.3%). D-Lay MS, in people with a first episode, reduced disease activity driven by MRI (HR 0.66), with no significant effect on relapses. Vitamin D as a risk factor. 217,467

Controversy

Menopause

In 987 Australian women with relapse-onset MS, menopause was not associated with disability progression (HR 0.95) or conversion to SPMS after adjustment; median age at menopause was 48.5. A 2026 journal debate argues both sides. 49,71,228

Getting older with MS

Older people with MS have more age-related comorbidity, the disease shifts from inflammatory to neurodegenerative, and DMTs work less well against age-related disability; stopping a DMT may become a discussion. Comorbidity estimates vary widely between studies. 300,480

Assisted reproduction

A meta-analysis of published case series (220 cycles) showed more relapses in the 3 months after assisted reproduction, while the authors' own Boston cohort (22 cycles) did not. Status: unresolved, possibly protocol-dependent. 47

Advance care planning and palliative care

The EAN guideline on severe progressive MS (EDSS above 6.0) makes 34 recommendations, 33 of them weak, including home-based palliative care, and notes a lack of evidence on advance care planning and caregiver support. NICE says to refer to palliative care for symptom control and end-of-life care when appropriate. 354,442

Exam hookPRIMS (1998): relapse rate 0.7 a year before pregnancy, 0.5, 0.6 and 0.2 by trimester, 1.2 in the first 3 months after birth, then back to baseline. The Kaiser cohort (2020) found no rebound above the pre-pregnancy rate. Pregnancy does not increase the risk of progression. 89,271,354
In plain wordsMS touches work, family plans, holidays and later life, but it does not decide them on its own. Relapses usually quieten during pregnancy, smoking is linked with faster progression, and planning ahead, for medicines and for later life, helps.
