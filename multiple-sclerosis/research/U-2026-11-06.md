## Findings

### dx · 2024 McDonald criteria and radiologically isolated syndrome
- Italian multicentre registry study (22 tertiary MS centres; 236 adults meeting the 2023 Lebrun-Frénay RIS criteria; mean age 38.9 years, 70.3% female; median follow-up 3.0 years): applying the 2024 McDonald criteria at baseline gave an MS diagnosis in 159 of 236 people with RIS (67.4%). At baseline 96.6% met DIS, 21.6% radiological DIT, and 75.6% of those with CSF data (152/201) were CSF-positive; central vein sign data existed for only 5 people (none positive) (Early evidence) [[DiFilippo2026]].
- Of the 42 people with RIS who had a clinical event during follow-up, 88.1% would have been reclassified as MS a median of 2 years earlier (IQR 1.0–5.5); a further 18 of 236 (7.6%) met the criteria during follow-up through radiological DIT (Early evidence) [[DiFilippo2026]].
- Limitations stated by the authors: retrospective, incomplete kappa free light chain data, non-standardised MRI, negligible CVS assessment [[DiFilippo2026]].

### Exam hooks
- Under the 2024 McDonald criteria, about two in three people with RIS in an Italian registry (67.4%) already met criteria for MS at baseline [[DiFilippo2026]].

### In plain words
- In an Italian study, about two out of three people whose scans showed MS-like spots without any symptoms would now be told they have MS under the 2024 rules, often years before any symptoms [[DiFilippo2026]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Whether earlier labelling of RIS as MS improves outcomes or mainly adds diagnoses (overdiagnosis concern) remains unresolved; [[DiFilippo2026]] measures reclassification, not outcomes.
