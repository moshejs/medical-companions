## Findings

### dx · 2024 McDonald criteria in a first episode of optic neuritis
- Acute Optic Neuritis Network (ACON), a prospective multicentre study (25 centres, 17 countries, six continents); cross-sectional analysis of 310 people with a first documented optic neuritis (59 idiopathic, 85 CIS by 2017 criteria, 85 MS by 2017 criteria, 22 AQP4-IgG-positive NMOSD, 59 MOGAD), enrolled 21 Aug 2020 to 31 Dec 2025: all 85 people with MS under 2017 met the 2024 criteria, and 27 of 85 (32%) with CIS under 2017 were diagnosed with MS under 2024 (Early evidence) [[Klyscz2026]].
- Sensitivity 50% (95% CI 42–58) under 2017 vs 66% (58–73) under 2024; specificity 100% (96–100) vs 89% (80–95); accuracy 66% (60–72) vs 73% (67–79). 3 of 22 (14%) people with AQP4-IgG-positive NMOSD and 6 of 59 (10%) with MOGAD met the 2024 criteria for MS (Early evidence) [[Klyscz2026]].
- The authors' interpretation: earlier diagnosis in optic-neuritis CIS, at the cost of specificity against NMOSD and MOGAD, so alternative diagnoses need careful exclusion; further real-world validation is needed. Funding: Roche [[Klyscz2026]].

### Exam hooks
- In first-episode optic neuritis, the 2024 McDonald criteria raised sensitivity (50% to 66%) but lowered specificity (100% to 89%) against AQP4-IgG-positive NMOSD and MOGAD [[Klyscz2026]].

### In plain words
- The 2024 rules pick up MS sooner after a first bout of optic neuritis, but a few people whose optic neuritis comes from a different antibody disease would also fit them, which is why the antibody tests matter [[Klyscz2026]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Sensitivity here is measured against a 2017-criteria CIS or MS label at presentation, not against long-term outcome; whether the 2024 criteria misclassify NMOSD or MOGAD in practice depends on how consistently AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG are tested [[Klyscz2026]].
