# D. Diagnosis, MRI, CSF & differential
This file covers how MS is diagnosed: the 2017 McDonald criteria, the 2024 McDonald revisions, MRI protocols and lesion features, CSF tests (oligoclonal bands and kappa free light chains), the central vein sign, paramagnetic rim lesions, OCT and VEP, how well each test performs, how often MS is misdiagnosed, and the main differential diagnoses with their red flags. The rule sections are written so that a programmer can implement them as a calculator. **Access notes:** the full texts of Thompson 2018 (McDonald 2017) and Montalban 2025 (McDonald 2024) are paywalled (the Lancet site returned HTTP 403). Only their abstracts were read directly. The 2017 table comes from an open-access reproduction (Filippi 2023, Table 2). The 2024 rules were taken from the official slide deck of the International Advisory Committee on Clinical Trials in MS (IACCTMS), hosted on ectrims.eu. That deck reproduces Figures 2 and 3 of Montalban 2025 verbatim, including their footnotes. The 2024 rules were then cross-checked against open-access companion and secondary papers (Deisenhammer 2025, Solomon 2026, Samara 2025, Brownlee 2026). Montalban 2025 is confirmed as Lancet Neurol 2025;24(10):850-865, PMID 40975101, doi:10.1016/S1474-4422(25)00270-4 (validated: PubMed). The brief says the revisions were "presented at ECTRIMS 2024". I could not confirm this from a source I read. The slide deck dates the consensus meeting to Barcelona, 29 Nov to 2 Dec 2023, and the ECTRIMS web page dates journal publication to September 2025. ## Facts

### McDonald 2017: definitions
- An attack (relapse, exacerbation) is defined as patient-reported symptoms with corroborating objective findings on examination or paraclinical testing that reflect an inflammatory demyelinating CNS event typical of MS. It develops acutely or subacutely, lasts at least 24 h, may or may not recover, and occurs in the absence of fever or infection. This wording comes from the 2024-era glossary; I could not read the 2017 full text to compare word for word [Solomon2026].
- A clinically isolated syndrome (CIS) is a first demyelinating event suggestive of MS that meets the attack definition [Solomon2026].
- "No better explanation" means the presentation is typical of MS and there are no clinical or paraclinical findings suggesting another diagnosis, or the relevant workup for those diagnoses is negative [Solomon2026].
- The 2017 criteria apply primarily to people with a typical CIS. They define what is needed to fulfil dissemination in time (DIT) and dissemination in space (DIS), and they stress the need for "no better explanation" [Thompson2018].
- 2017 DIS by MRI: ≥1 T2-hyperintense lesion in ≥2 of 4 areas (periventricular, cortical/juxtacortical, infratentorial, spinal cord) [Thompson2018; Filippi2023].
- 2017 change: symptomatic lesions can count towards DIS or DIT in people with a supratentorial, infratentorial or spinal cord syndrome. By implication, symptomatic optic nerve lesions in optic neuritis do not count [Thompson2018].
- 2017 change: cortical lesions can be used to show DIS, merged with juxtacortical lesions into one "cortical/juxtacortical" area [Thompson2018; Filippi2023].
- 2017 DIT by MRI: Gd-enhancing and non-enhancing lesions present together at any time (symptomatic lesions included), or a new T2 and/or Gd-enhancing lesion on follow-up MRI compared with a baseline scan, whatever the timing of the baseline [Filippi2023; Rocca2024].
- 2017 change: in a typical CIS with DIS (clinical or MRI) and no better explanation, CSF-specific oligoclonal bands (OCB) substitute for DIT. This allows an MS diagnosis even when clinical and MRI findings do not meet DIT [Thompson2018; Filippi2023].
- For older people or those with vascular risk factors, the 2017-era MAGNIMS recommendation was to require ≥3 periventricular lesions for periventricular DIS [Filippi2023; Solomon2026].
- "Possible MS" in practice: one 2024 validation study used it for people with suspected MS, no better explanation, and criteria not met, including CIS and radiologically isolated syndrome (RIS). This is that study's working definition; the 2017 text itself was not readable [Brownlee2026].

### McDonald 2017: clinical presentation table (relapse onset), row by row
- Row 1: ≥2 clinical attacks plus objective clinical evidence of ≥2 lesions. Additional data needed: none [Thompson2018; Filippi2023].
- Row 2: ≥2 clinical attacks plus objective clinical evidence of 1 lesion plus clear-cut historical evidence of a previous attack involving a lesion in a distinct anatomical location. Additional data needed: none [Thompson2018; Filippi2023].
- Row 3: ≥2 clinical attacks plus objective clinical evidence of 1 lesion. Additional data needed: DIS, shown by an additional clinical attack implicating a different CNS site, or by MRI (≥1 lesion in ≥2 of periventricular, cortical/juxtacortical, infratentorial, spinal cord) [Thompson2018; Filippi2023].
- Row 4: 1 clinical attack plus objective clinical evidence of ≥2 lesions. Additional data needed: DIT, shown by a second clinical attack, or MRI DIT, or CSF-specific OCB [Thompson2018; Filippi2023].
- Row 5: 1 clinical attack plus objective clinical evidence of 1 lesion. Additional data needed: both DIS (an additional attack at a different CNS site, or MRI DIS) and DIT (a second attack, or MRI DIT, or CSF-specific OCB) [Thompson2018; Filippi2023].

### McDonald 2017: primary progressive MS (PPMS)
- PPMS 2017 requires 1 year of disability progression, determined retrospectively or prospectively and independent of clinical relapse, plus ≥2 of 3: (a) ≥1 T2-hyperintense lesion in ≥1 brain area characteristic of MS (periventricular, cortical/juxtacortical or infratentorial); (b) ≥2 T2-hyperintense spinal cord lesions, symptomatic or not; (c) CSF-specific OCB [Thompson2018; Filippi2023].
- **Early evidence:** in 117 people with PPMS, the 2017 and 2010 criteria for progressive-onset and relapse-onset MS performed similarly: sensitivity 89% vs 85%, specificity 100% vs 100%, accuracy 91% vs 87%. This was taken to support one set of MRI criteria for all MS [Filippi2023; Rocca2024].

### McDonald 2024: citation, process and general principles
- The 2024 revisions were published as Montalban X, Lebrun-Frénay C, Oh J, et al. "Diagnosis of multiple sclerosis: 2024 revisions of the McDonald criteria", Lancet Neurol 2025;24(10):850-865. Companion papers cover MRI (Barkhof 2025), OCT/VEP (Saidha 2025) and CSF (Deisenhammer 2025) [Montalban2025; Barkhof2025; Saidha2025; Deisenhammer2025].
- They were developed by 56 international experts from 16 countries under the IACCTMS, sponsored by ECTRIMS and the US National MS Society. The method was a modified nominal group technique: at least 90% of participants had to vote on a statement and 80% had to agree for it to be accepted. Statements reaching 70–80% could be re-voted [IACCTMS2025Slides].
- General principles: MS is a diagnosis of exclusion (no better explanation); paraclinical tests are essential; brain and spinal cord MRI are the most useful paraclinical tests; misdiagnosis and underdiagnosis both harm patients; the diagnosis should be reassessed periodically. Statements that apply to CIS may also apply to people with past relapses who have not yet been diagnosed [IACCTMS2025Slides].
- The 2024 criteria give one framework for relapsing and progressive courses and across the lifespan. The optic nerve becomes a fifth anatomical location. The central vein sign (CVS), paramagnetic rim lesions (PRL) and CSF kappa free light chains (kFLC) can provide supportive evidence when available. In certain cases, RIS or symptoms that are not a clear attack or progression can fulfil the criteria. Guidance is given for people aged ≥50 years and those with comorbidities [Montalban2025].
- If the newer paraclinical tools (CVS, PRL, kFLC, OCT) are unavailable, a pragmatic approach using clinical findings with conventional MRI and CSF is permitted. The new tools are not mandatory [Arrambide2026; Solomon2026].
- Apart from some PPMS cases without multiple spinal cord lesions, everyone who meets the 2017 criteria also meets the 2024 criteria. A diagnosis made under earlier criteria is not changed by the 2024 revision [Solomon2026].

### McDonald 2024: dissemination in space (5 topographies)
- DIS is fulfilled when typical lesions are present in 2 of 5 topographies: optic nerve, juxtacortical/intracortical, periventricular, infratentorial, spinal cord. Lesions count whether or not they are symptomatic [IACCTMS2025Slides; Saidha2025].
- A second clinical attack in a different anatomical location from the first can also be counted as DIS [Solomon2026].
- In a person with ≥12 months of disease progression from symptom onset, ≥2 spinal cord lesions count as DIS and meet the criterion for lesions in two CNS locations [Montalban2025; IACCTMS2025Slides].
- A lesion is an area of hyperintensity on T2-weighted or proton-density MRI [Solomon2026]. MAGNIMS guidance says a lesion should generally be ≥3 mm in its long axis to count towards diagnostic criteria. A lesion under 3 mm on the floor of the fourth ventricle is an exception [Filippi2019].
- Periventricular lesion: a T2-hyperintense white-matter lesion touching the lateral ventricles with no white matter in between; corpus callosum lesions are included. Lesions in deep grey matter that abut the ventricles do not count [Solomon2026; Filippi2019].
- Juxtacortical lesion: touches the cortex with no white matter in between. Cortical lesions lie within the cortex and often need DIR, PSIR or MPRAGE sequences to be seen [Solomon2026].
- Infratentorial lesion: a T2-hyperintense lesion in the brainstem (typically near the surface), cerebellar peduncles or cerebellum. Symmetric central pontine lesions suggest small-vessel disease and do not count [Solomon2026; Filippi2019].
- Spinal cord lesion: seen on T2-weighted, STIR, proton-density or another appropriate image. The 2024 criteria and MRI guidelines recommend confirming it in 2 sagittal sequences and 1 axial sequence, to tell small lesions from artefact [Solomon2026].
- Lesions in ≥4 of the 5 locations are enough to confirm MS without further evidence in a typical clinical presentation [Barkhof2025; IACCTMS2025Slides].

### McDonald 2024: showing the optic nerve as the fifth topography
- The optic nerve counts as a DIS topography only if no better explanation exists for the optic nerve pathology [IACCTMS2025Slides; Saidha2025].
- MRI: ≥1 intrinsic optic nerve lesion with no better explanation counts. Examples of better explanations are prominent chiasmal involvement, optic perineuritis, or a longitudinally extensive lesion [IACCTMS2025Slides].
- MRI technique: fat-saturated sequences are recommended for detecting symptomatic optic nerve lesions [Barkhof2025]. The recommended optic nerve protocol uses axial and coronal fat-suppressed T2 or STIR, and fat-suppressed contrast-enhanced T1, with slices ≤2–3 mm [Rocca2024].
- VEP: an abnormal peak time on full-field pattern-reversal VEP counts as optic nerve involvement. Abnormal means significant interocular asymmetry, or a P100 peak time above the upper limit of normal, with no better explanation [IACCTMS2025Slides]. A secondary summary gives the threshold as 2.5 or 3 SD above the mean for both absolute P100 latency and inter-ocular latency [Samara2025]. VEP latency depends on technical and methodological factors and on the centre and device [Saidha2025].
- OCT: if rigorous quality control is applied, an inter-eye difference of ≥6 μm in peripapillary RNFL, or ≥4 μm in macular ganglion cell–inner plexiform layer (GCIPL), supports unilateral optic nerve involvement for DIS [Saidha2025; IACCTMS2025Slides]. One summary states that OCT must meet the OSCAR-IB quality criteria [Samara2025].
- A symptomatic optic nerve lesion counts. In one worked example, optic neuritis with an enhancing optic nerve lesion plus one periventricular lesion fulfils both DIS and DIT under 2024 [Solomon2026].
- OCT and VEP performance in children is less clear, and PRLs are less studied in paediatric MS [Samara2025].

### McDonald 2024: dissemination in time and "positive CSF"
- 2024 DIT (Figure 3 footnote): "one or more new T2 lesions or one or more gadolinium-positive lesions or a clinical attack" [Montalban2025; IACCTMS2025Slides]. The companion glossary instead defines MRI DIT as new T2 lesions over time, or Gd-enhancing and non-enhancing lesions present together on one MRI [Solomon2026].
- DIS plus DIT is sufficient for diagnosis, as in 2017. DIS plus positive CSF (OCB and/or kFLC) is also sufficient [IACCTMS2025Slides].
- DIT is no longer mandatory in specific situations [IACCTMS2025Slides; Samara2025].
- Positive CSF means either ≥2 CSF-restricted oligoclonal IgG bands (type 2 or 3 pattern on isoelectric focusing with IgG immunoblot), or a κ-FLC index ≥6.1 [Deisenhammer2025; Solomon2026].
- The κ-FLC index is (CSF κ-FLC / serum κ-FLC) ÷ (CSF albumin / serum albumin) [Konen2026; Deisenhammer2025].
- kFLC is interchangeable with OCB and can substitute for OCB (cut-off 6.1) [IACCTMS2025Slides; Deisenhammer2025].
- Each laboratory should verify the 6.1 cut-off. If MS is strongly suspected but κ-FLC is not raised, the CSF should be sent for OCB, and the reverse also applies [Deisenhammer2025].
- The IgG index is not a sufficient substitute for OCB or kFLC [Samara2025; Deisenhammer2025].
- Neurofilament light should not be used for MS diagnosis [Deisenhammer2025].

### McDonald 2024: central vein sign and paramagnetic rim lesion definitions
- CVS (NAIMS definition): a thin hypointense line or small dot (<2 mm) seen in at least two planes, appearing as a thin line in at least one, running partly or wholly through the centre of the lesion. Lesions <3 mm, confluent lesions, and lesions with multiple veins or an unclear vein are excluded [Filippi2019; Sati2016].
- 2024 CVS positivity ("Select-6"): ≥6 lesions with CVS. If fewer than 10 white-matter lesions are seen on MRI, CVS-positive lesions must outnumber CVS-negative lesions [Montalban2025; IACCTMS2025Slides; Solomon2026].
- Children and adolescents (<18 years): CVS in >50% of T2 lesions strongly supports MS [IACCTMS2025Slides; Solomon2026].
- PRL positivity: ≥1 PRL in the brain [Montalban2025; Samara2025].
- PRL definition: a discrete paramagnetic rim on susceptibility-sensitive (phase) imaging at >1.5 T, continuous around >2/3 of the outer edge of the white-matter lesion on the slice where it is most visible. The rim must be visible on ≥2 consecutive slices or in 2 orthogonal planes. The core must co-localise with all or part of a T2-hyperintense lesion that does not enhance after gadolinium. Veins running alongside the lesion that mimic a rim are excluded [IACCTMS2025Slides].
- Showing CVS or PRL can increase diagnostic specificity, but neither is required for an MS diagnosis [IACCTMS2025Slides].
- MRI for diagnosis should always cover brain and spinal cord and should include susceptibility-sensitive sequences for CVS and PRL [Barkhof2025]. CVS and PRL can be seen at 1.5 T: 82% of PRLs seen at 3.0 T were also visible at 1.5 T [Solomon2026].

### McDonald 2024: algorithm for an attack or objective progression (Figure 3)
- Entry: (a) an attack with symptoms and signs characteristic of MS, or (b) objective progression. Then an initial workup (history, examination, imaging, laboratory tests, differential diagnosis). If better explanations have not been excluded, the next step is to monitor for new neurological or imaging changes, not to diagnose [Montalban2025; IACCTMS2025Slides].
- Branch A applies when there are lesions in ≥2 CNS sites, or when there are ≥12 months of progression from onset and ≥2 spinal cord lesions. MS can be diagnosed if ≥1 of these is also shown: CSF positivity; CVS positivity; DIT; lesions in 4 or 5 CNS anatomical locations [Montalban2025; IACCTMS2025Slides].
- Branch B applies when there are lesions in 1 CNS site, including people with ≥12 months of progression from onset. MS can be diagnosed if ≥1 of these pairs is shown: CSF positivity + CVS positivity; CSF positivity + PRL positivity; DIT + CVS positivity; DIT + PRL positivity [Montalban2025; IACCTMS2025Slides].
- In Branch B, the PRL or CVS-positive lesion may sit outside the 5 DIS topographies. Example: optic neuritis with an enhancing optic nerve lesion (1 topography + DIT) plus one non-enhancing deep white-matter PRL fulfils 2024 [Solomon2026].
- PRL is not a route in Branch A or in the RIS/non-specific pathway; in the attack/progression algorithm it appears only in Branch B [Montalban2025; Samara2025].
- Progressive course: PPMS still requires clinical progression over ≥12 months. Relapsing and primary progressive MS use one unified framework [IACCTMS2025Slides]. Unlike 2017, a progressive patient with 2 cord lesions and brain lesions also needs DIT, CVS or positive CSF [Solomon2026].
- Implementation summary of the cited rules: let `sites` = number of the 5 topographies with typical lesions (plus a second attack at a different site as clinical DIS). Let `prog12` = ≥12 months of objective progression. Let `sc2` = ≥2 spinal cord lesions. Then `branchA = sites>=2 || (prog12 && sc2)`, and MS if `branchA && (CSF || CVS6 || DIT || sites>=4)`. Otherwise, if `sites==1`, MS if `(CSF||DIT) && (CVS6||PRL1)` [Montalban2025; IACCTMS2025Slides].

### McDonald 2024: radiologically isolated syndrome and non-specific presentations (Figure 2)
- RIS is the incidental finding on MRI of CNS white-matter T2 hyperintensities that are highly typical of MS, in a person with no symptoms of inflammatory demyelination and no related findings on examination [IACCTMS2025Slides].
- Figure 2 entry: incidental imaging findings suggestive of demyelinating disease, or "other presentations with symptoms not specific to multiple sclerosis". These may include symptoms or signs overlapping with MS but no characteristic attack, or paroxysmal symptoms (for example trigeminal neuralgia), seizures and other symptoms [Montalban2025; IACCTMS2025Slides].
- Rule: after better explanations are excluded, lesions must be present in ≥2 CNS sites. MS can then be diagnosed if ≥1 of CSF positivity, CVS positivity (Select-6) or DIT is shown [Montalban2025; IACCTMS2025Slides; Solomon2026].
- In this pathway, lesions in 4–5 sites alone are not sufficient, PRL is not a listed route, and a single site does not qualify [Montalban2025; Samara2025].
- Non-specific presentations listed in the companion review include sensory symptoms that do not localise, and paroxysmal syndromes lasting <24 h: Uhthoff phenomenon, Lhermitte phenomenon, tonic spasms, trigeminal neuralgia and, uncommonly, seizures [Solomon2026].
- **Established:** about 51% of people with RIS develop clinical MS symptoms within 10 years [Rocca2024; IACCTMS2025Slides].
- **Established:** risk factors for a first clinical event in RIS include age <35 years, male sex, CSF-restricted OCB, infratentorial, spinal cord or Gd-enhancing lesions, abnormal VEP, and higher serum neurofilament light chain (NfL) [Rocca2024].
- **Early evidence:** in 273 people with RIS (median follow-up 5.0 years), IgG OCB (HR 5.09, 95% CI 2.36–10.97) and κ-FLC index ≥6.1 (HR 2.79, 1.37–5.67) were associated with developing MS symptoms [Fissolo2025].
- **Early evidence:** in 747 RIS subjects, the 5-year probability of a clinical event was 29.0% for those meeting only 1–2 DIS locations and 38.7% for 2009-RIS. Spinal cord lesions plus CSF-restricted OCB raised the risk in the first group to 38% [LebrunFrenay2023].

### McDonald 2024: special populations
- People aged ≥50 years, and/or with significant vascular risk factors (hypertension, smoking, diabetes, hyperlipidaemia), known vascular disease, and/or headache disorders: additional features are "strongly recommended" to confirm MS. These are a spinal cord lesion, CSF positivity (OCB or κ-FLC index), or CVS positivity [IACCTMS2025Slides; Solomon2026].
- Rationale: these features would not be expected in healthy ageing, headache disorders or vascular disease, so they raise specificity beyond DIS plus DIT [Solomon2026].
- Children: paediatric-onset and adult-onset MS use one framework [IACCTMS2025Slides].
- Children with an ADEM presentation: the MS criteria can be applied only after a second clinical attack consistent with typical MS and/or new T2 lesions in typical MS topography >90 days after ADEM onset [IACCTMS2025Slides].
- MOG-IgG testing with a cell-based assay is strongly recommended in children with incident CNS demyelination under age 12. From age 12, it is recommended for presentations with symptoms not specific to MS or suggestive of MOGAD, but not for everyone investigated for MS [IACCTMS2025Slides].

### McDonald 2024: early real-world application
- **Early evidence:** in 274 people with suspected MS after better explanations were excluded (London, 2024), the 2024 criteria diagnosed MS after the initial workup in 220 people vs 172 under 2017 (p<0.01). Median time to diagnosis was 40 vs 84 days [Brownlee2026].
- **Early evidence:** in the same cohort, 2024 criteria applied at the initial workup had sensitivity 92.6% (95% CI 88.1–95.8), specificity 57.8% (45.4–69.4) and accuracy 83.6% (78.7–87.8) against a 2017 diagnosis at follow-up (mean 15.3 months) [Brownlee2026].
- **Early evidence:** the extra 2024 diagnoses came from RIS or non-specific symptoms (n=23), DIS in ≥4 regions (n=15) and optic nerve inclusion (n=10) [Brownlee2026].
- **Early evidence:** in older adults (≥50 years), sensitivity was 100% (90.5–100) and specificity 55.0% (31.5–76.9). All older adults diagnosed had abnormal spinal cord MRI, positive CSF, or both [Brownlee2026].
- **Early evidence:** in a German prospective cohort (n=215; first event or RIS), 201 (93.5%) met the 2024 criteria vs 181 (84%) under 2017. Additional diagnoses came from optic nerve involvement (55%) and RIS reclassification (35%), and VEP identified 89% of optic nerve lesions [Konen2026].
- **Early evidence:** in 197 Egyptian patients, 2024 diagnosed 195 vs 168 under 2017, with low agreement (κ=0.112). Four topographies without DIT accounted for 74.1% of the extra diagnoses [Hamdy2025].
- **Early evidence:** in 184 Chinese patients using only conventional components, 2024 diagnosed 173 (94.0%) vs 166 (90.2%) under 2017 (κ=0.739) [Cai2026].
- **Early evidence:** in the multicentre CAVS-MS cohort, 191 of 420 participants presented with non-specific symptoms or incidental imaging. 36 of these (19%) met the 2024 criteria at baseline. Among the 51 meeting DIS, 43% were Select-6 positive, 33% OCB positive and 8% met DIT [Scharf2026].

### MRI protocol (MAGNIMS-CMSC-NAIMS 2021 and 2024)
- 2021 consensus: 3D FLAIR is the core brain sequence for diagnostic accuracy and detecting new lesions. Gadolinium is recommended judiciously for specific clinical purposes [Wattjes2021].
- Brain protocol for diagnosis (2021): field strength ≥1.5 T, 3.0 T preferred. Core sequences are 3D T2-FLAIR, axial T2-weighted, and T1-weighted after gadolinium; pre-contrast T1 is not required. 3D imaging should be 1 mm isotropic (≤1.5 mm); 2D slices ≤3 mm with no gap [Wattjes2021; Rocca2024].
- Spinal cord protocol (2021): ≥1.5 T (3.0 T adds no value over 1.5 T), covering the cervical and thoraco-lumbar cord including the conus. At least two of sagittal T2, proton-density, or STIR are needed, plus sagittal post-contrast T1. Sagittal slices ≤3 mm and axial slices ≤5 mm, with no gap [Wattjes2021; Rocca2024].
- Contrast: standard dose 0.1 mmol/kg, macrocyclic gadolinium agents only, with a minimum delay of 5–10 minutes [Wattjes2021; Rocca2024].
- 2024 MRI consensus: brain and spinal cord should always be imaged. Susceptibility-sensitive sequences should be included for CVS and PRL, and fat-saturated sequences used for symptomatic optic nerve lesions. Recommendations also cover RIS, older people, children, people with vascular comorbidity or migraine, and settings outside Europe and North America [Barkhof2025].

### Typical MS lesion features on MRI
- Periventricular MS lesions follow the deep medullary veins, so their long axis runs perpendicular to the lateral ventricles and they look ovoid on axial images ("Dawson's fingers") [Filippi2019].
- Thin, plate-like hyperintensities running parallel to the ventricles ("banding" or "capping") are not indicative of MS. Neither are lesions near, but separated from, the ventricles [Filippi2019; Solomon2026].
- Spinal cord MS lesions are typically short: under two vertebral segments in length, under half the cord's cross-section, and mostly peripheral (lateral and dorsal columns). On sagittal images they look cigar-like and on axial images wedge-shaped. The cervical cord is affected more often than the thoracic [Filippi2019].
- Multiple short-segment cord lesions are highly specific for MS. Cord lesions are not seen with normal ageing, migraine or most cerebrovascular disease [Filippi2019].
- Enhancement in a new MS lesion typically lasts 2–8 weeks (usually <4). Open-ring enhancement favours demyelination, whereas enhancement persisting more than 3 months suggests malignancy [Filippi2019].
- The central vein sign is found in about 80% of lesions at all stages of MS [Filippi2019].

### Test performance: 2017 vs 2010 McDonald criteria
- **Established:** in 229 Dutch patients with typical CIS (mean follow-up 65.3 months), 2017 vs 2010 had sensitivity 68% (95% CI 57–77) vs 36% (27–47) and specificity 61% (50–71) vs 85% (76–92) for clinically definite MS [vanderVuurstdeVries2018].
- **Established:** in the same cohort, 54% met 2017 at baseline vs 26% for 2010. Among those followed ≥5 years, 33% diagnosed under 2017 had no second attack, vs 23% under 2010 [vanderVuurstdeVries2018].
- **Established:** in 785 CIS patients from 9 European MAGNIMS centres, 2017 vs 2010 DIS plus DIT at 36 months had sensitivity 0.83 vs 0.66, specificity 0.39 vs 0.60 and AUC 0.61 vs 0.63 [Filippi2022].
- **Established:** in that cohort, median time to MS diagnosis was 3.2 months under 2017, 13.0 under 2010 and 58.5 for clinically definite MS. Requiring three periventricular lesions improved specificity in patients ≥45 years [Filippi2022].
- **Established:** without OCB, the 2017 criteria in that cohort had sensitivity 0.74, specificity 0.54 and AUC 0.64 [Filippi2023].
- **Established:** in 368 CIS patients, the 2010 and MAGNIMS 2016 DIS criteria had sensitivity 0.91/0.93 and specificity 0.33/0.32 at 36 months. Requiring three periventricular lesions gave sensitivity 0.85 and specificity 0.40, and adding the optic nerve gave sensitivity 0.92 and specificity 0.26 [Filippi2018].
- **Established:** in 110 children with non-encephalopathic acquired demyelinating syndromes, 2017 vs 2010 had sensitivity 83% vs 49% and specificity 73% vs 87%. No child presenting with ADEM fulfilled clinically definite MS [Wong2019].

### Test performance: CSF oligoclonal bands and κ-FLC
- **Established:** in a meta-analysis of 71 articles, 87.7% of 12,253 people with MS and 68.6% of 2,685 with CIS were OCB-positive. OCB-positive CIS had an odds ratio of 9.88 for conversion to MS, and latitude predicted OCB status in MS (p=0.009) [Dobson2013].
- **Established:** in a meta-analysis of 32 studies, the κ-FLC index had weighted sensitivity 88% and specificity 89%, vs 85% and 92% for OCB, with no significant difference in accuracy. The discriminatory cut-off was 6.1 [Hegen2023].
- Published κ-FLC index cut-offs range from 2.4 to 20. Among studies with >100 subjects the range is 4.6–12.5 (IQR 5.9–7.5), giving a weighted mean of 6.1 [Deisenhammer2025].
- Concordance between κ-FLC and OCB is close to 90% [Deisenhammer2025]. The IACCTMS deck gives 87.0% [IACCTMS2025Slides].
- OCB are found in ≤25% of NMOSD and about 10% of MOGAD [Deisenhammer2025].
- **Early evidence:** in children, a κ-FLC index >6.83 had 100% sensitivity and 92% specificity for paediatric MS vs children without inflammatory disease (n=16 MS) [Deisenhammer2025].
- **Established:** in 314 CIS patients, adding positive OCB to MRI DIS raised specificity for 2010 McDonald MS from 80.6% to 88.1% [Arrambide2018].
- **Early evidence:** in a 2024 clinic cohort, CSF-specific OCB were positive in 82% of people with possible MS vs 10% of those with other final diagnoses [Brownlee2026].

### Test performance: central vein sign and paramagnetic rim lesions
- **Established:** in 1,051 people (599 MS/CIS, 452 non-MS) across 14 European centres, the 40% CVS rule had sensitivity 78.7%, specificity 86.0% and accuracy 81.5%. CVS AUC was 0.89, vs 0.77 for cortical lesions [Cagol2024].
- **Early evidence:** in the CAVS-MS pilot (n=53 with CSF), baseline sensitivity was 0.71 for Select-6, 0.83 for Select-3 and 0.75 for OCB. Specificity was 0.86, 0.48 and 0.76 respectively [Toljan2024].
- **Early evidence:** in CAVS-MS pilot data (n=78), ≥6 CVS plus ≥1 PRL had sensitivity 0.57 and specificity 1.00, while ≥1 CVS plus ≥1 PRL had 0.76 and 0.93 [Toljan2026].
- **Early evidence:** requiring ≥1 CVS-positive lesion on top of brain MRI DIS raised specificity from 69% to 81% at an unchanged sensitivity of 92% (n=78) [Amin2025].
- **Established:** in 438 people, PRLs were seen in 52% with MS vs 7% without, giving 93% specificity [Maggi2020].
- **Established:** in 254 MS, 91 mimics and 217 older controls, ≥1 PRL had 99.7% specificity but 24.0% sensitivity [Meaton2022].
- A NAIMS diagnostic-presentation study found ≥1 PRL in 86% of participants with MS, with 90% specificity. This is reported in a secondary review; I did not read the primary study [Samara2025].
- CVS is seen in about 65% of people with MS, and PRL in about 50% [IACCTMS2025Slides].

### OCT and VEP
- **Established:** in a meta-analysis (5,776 MS eyes), peripapillary RNFL was thinner than in controls by 20.10 μm (95% CI 17.44–22.76) in eyes with past optic neuritis and 7.41 μm in eyes without. Macular GCIPL was thinner by 16.42 μm and 6.31 μm respectively [Petzold2017].
- **Early evidence:** in 157 CIS patients across 5 MAGNIMS centres, adding the optic nerve (by MRI, OCT or VEP) raised DIS sensitivity to 92.5% (MRI) from 88.2%. Specificity was 80.0% (GCIPL inter-eye asymmetry ≥4 μm) vs 82.2%, and optic nerve MRI was abnormal in 72.5% of those with optic neuritis [VidalJordana2024].
- **Early evidence:** adding the optic nerve by VEP in 151 Barcelona CIS patients raised sensitivity from 79.2% to 82.3% and accuracy from 75.5% to 78.1%, with specificity unchanged at 52.4% [VidalJordana2021].
- **Early evidence:** in 160 CIS patients followed about 15 years, including symptomatic optic nerve involvement in optic neuritis raised DIS sensitivity (95% vs 83%) but lowered specificity (57% vs 68%). Asymptomatic optic nerve involvement in non-optic-neuritis CIS added no patients [Brownlee2018].
- **Early evidence:** an inter-eye difference in ganglion cell layer of ≥2 μm identified past unilateral optic neuritis with AUC 0.79 (sensitivity 58.9%, specificity 85.1%) [Krajnc2025].

### Misdiagnosis
- **Established:** a 2024 meta-analysis found MS misdiagnosis in 5%–41% across studies. The pooled figure was 15% (95% CI 9–26%; 6 studies, n=1,621), and women were 2.1 times more likely to be misdiagnosed [Zurrer2024].
- **Established:** in 110 patients misdiagnosed with MS at 4 US centres, the alternative diagnoses were migraine 22%, fibromyalgia 15%, non-specific symptoms with abnormal MRI 12%, conversion/psychogenic disorders 11%, and NMOSD 6% [Solomon2016].
- **Established:** in the same series, 70% had received a disease-modifying therapy (DMT), 31% had unnecessary morbidity, and 33% had carried the misdiagnosis for ≥10 years [Solomon2016].
- Leading contributors to misdiagnosis were: treating symptoms atypical for demyelination as attacks; accepting attacks without objective evidence of a CNS lesion; and over-relying on MRI abnormalities in people with non-specific symptoms [Solomon2016].
- **Established:** at two Los Angeles academic centres, 17% and 19% of referrals carrying an MS diagnosis were misdiagnosed. The most common true diagnoses were migraine (16%), RIS (9%), spondylopathy (7%) and neuropathy (7%), and about 110 patient-years of unnecessary DMT had been given [Kaisey2019].
- **Early evidence:** 21% (73/347) of 2024 referrals for suspected MS had other disorders, most often migraine or functional neurological disorder with non-specific or vascular white-matter lesions [Brownlee2026].

### Differential diagnosis: NMOSD and MOGAD
- NMOSD with AQP4-IgG: ≥1 of 6 core clinical characteristics plus a positive AQP4-IgG. The core characteristics are optic neuritis, acute myelitis, area postrema syndrome, other brainstem, diencephalic, and cerebral syndromes [Wingerchuk2015].
- NMOSD without AQP4-IgG (or untested): ≥2 different core characteristics are needed. At least one must be optic neuritis, myelitis with a longitudinally extensive transverse myelitis (LETM) lesion, or area postrema syndrome with a medullary lesion, and the supportive MRI requirements must be met [Wingerchuk2015].
- LETM is a T2 cord lesion spanning ≥3 contiguous vertebral segments, often central and with cord swelling. It is the most specific imaging feature of NMOSD and very uncommon in adult MS [Wingerchuk2015; Cacciaguerra2022].
- Area postrema syndrome is intractable hiccups or nausea and vomiting, seen in 16%–43% of NMOSD [Wingerchuk2015].
- AQP4-IgG live or fixed cell-based assays are recommended: pooled sensitivity 76.7% and a 0.1% false-positive rate in an MS clinic cohort, vs 63%–64% sensitivity for immunofluorescence or ELISA [Wingerchuk2015].
- Features that favour NMOSD over MS: CSF pleocytosis >50 cells/μL (about 35% of NMOSD), absent OCB, and severe or bilateral optic neuritis. A gradually progressive course is very uncommon in NMOSD (1%–2%) [Wingerchuk2015].
- MOGAD criteria (2023) make MOG-IgG a core criterion. The typical phenotypes are ADEM, optic neuritis and transverse myelitis, and cell-based assays are important for accuracy. Not all people with MS need MOG-IgG screening [Banwell2023].
- MOGAD accounts for more than half of demyelinating presentations in children <12 years. Low-titre MOG-IgG occurs in 1%–2% of people with MS [Solomon2026].
- Compared with MS, MOGAD lesions are poorly defined, involve long segments of cord and optic nerve, often resolve, and rarely show subclinical activity. Optic nerve involvement is often bilateral, with disc swelling and nerve-sheath enhancement [Solomon2026].
- Several MS DMTs may worsen NMOSD. The IPND panel noted observational data that interferon-β, natalizumab and fingolimod may worsen NMO [Wingerchuk2015].
- **Early evidence:** in 5 AQP4-positive patients first treated as MS with natalizumab, 9 relapses occurred on treatment, 4 accumulated disability and 1 died [Kleiter2012].
- **Early evidence:** in one patient, AQP4 antibody titres rose sharply and 3 relapses occurred over 10 months on interferon beta-1a [Palace2010].
- **Early evidence:** a catastrophic brain relapse followed a single natalizumab dose in seronegative NMO [Kitley2014]. Interferon-β, glatiramer acetate, fingolimod, natalizumab, alemtuzumab and dimethyl fumarate have all been linked to unexpected NMOSD exacerbations [Kira2017].

### Differential diagnosis: other mimics and red flags
- The 2008 international consensus scored 79 red flags: 36 major (pointing fairly definitively to a non-MS diagnosis), 32 intermediate and 11 minor [Miller2008]. A 2023 consensus updated the red flags and the diagnostic approach to common presentations [Solomon2023].
- Clinical red flags include hyperacute onset (minutes to hours), severe deficits (complete blindness, paraplegia), symmetric CNS involvement and encephalopathy [Solomon2026].
- CSF red flags: white cells >50/μL, protein >100 mg/dL, or glucose <60% of serum glucose [Solomon2026].
- Cerebral small-vessel disease and migraine: their lesions usually sit in deep white matter, separated from the cortex by a rim of white matter, and do not abut the ventricles or lie perpendicular to them. Lacunes, microbleeds and symmetric central pontine lesions point to small-vessel disease [Filippi2019].
- Neurosarcoidosis: lung involvement, diabetes insipidus or hypothalamic disturbance, meningeal enhancement and multiple cranial neuropathies are red flags [Miller2008].
- Neurosarcoidosis cord findings: dorsal subpial enhancement over ≥2 segments, with an axial "trident sign". Enhancement persists at 2 months in >90% of cases, vs 12% of AQP4-positive NMOSD [Cacciaguerra2022].
- Vitamin B12 or copper deficiency: long, selective dorsal-column involvement that rarely enhances, with peripheral neuropathy or haematological abnormalities [Filippi2019; Cacciaguerra2022; Miller2008].
- Susac syndrome: rounded "snowball" lesions in the centre of the corpus callosum, retinopathy (branch retinal artery occlusion) and hearing loss [Filippi2019; Miller2008].
- CADASIL: white-matter lesions in the anterior temporal pole, external capsule and inferior frontal lobe, with lacunes and microbleeds [Filippi2019; Miller2008].
- Systemic lupus erythematosus: rash, renal involvement, livedo reticularis, arthritis and lesions crossing grey-white boundaries [Miller2008].
- Sjögren syndrome: sicca symptoms and myopathy [Miller2008].
- Behçet disease: mucosal ulcers, cerebral venous sinus thrombosis, and large diencephalic or brainstem lesions [Miller2008; Filippi2019].
- Infections: Lyme disease (multiple cranial neuropathies or polyradiculopathy, rash, arthritis), HTLV-1 myelopathy (gradual progression) and PML (large lesions without mass effect) are listed red-flag alternatives [Miller2008].
- No read source gave consensus wording on routine syphilis or HIV testing. Syphilis and Borrelia serology were part of one centre's standard workup [Konen2026].
- Adult leukodystrophies: confluent, symmetric white-matter abnormalities. Adrenomyeloneuropathy and metachromatic leukodystrophy can give a gradually progressive course from onset [Filippi2019; Miller2008].

## Controversies
- Dropping DIT in some routes: the 2024 framework allows diagnosis on DIS alone (≥4 of 5 sites), or with CVS, to speed diagnosis [Montalban2025; VanDerWalt2026]. Real-world studies show many more diagnoses and only moderate specificity against 2017 at follow-up (57.8%), with low 2017–2024 agreement in one cohort (κ=0.112) [Brownlee2026; Hamdy2025]. Status: no long-term prospective validation yet. The authors themselves note that the "specificity" figures count 2017-negative CIS and RIS as false positives [Brownlee2026].
- RIS diagnosed as MS: proponents point to about 51% conversion at 10 years and biomarker support [IACCTMS2025Slides; Fissolo2025]. Critics cite overdiagnosis risk, treatment and psychosocial implications, and implementation gaps [Konen2026; Samara2025; Brownlee2026]. Letters raising concerns were published in Lancet Neurol 2026 with an authors' reply (content not accessed) [Montalban2026Reply]. Status: unresolved.
- Optic nerve as fifth region: adding it raised sensitivity without losing specificity by VEP [VidalJordana2021] and with similar accuracy across modalities [VidalJordana2024]. Other cohorts found lower specificity [Filippi2018; Brownlee2018], and asymptomatic optic nerve findings in non-optic-neuritis CIS added little [Brownlee2018]. OCT and VEP thresholds depend on quality control, centre and device [Saidha2025]. Status: adopted in 2024, but the measurement standard varies by centre.
- CVS threshold: earlier work used a ≥40% lesion-proportion rule [Cagol2024; Filippi2019]. Select-3 was sensitive but non-specific [Toljan2024], and 2024 adopted Select-6 [Montalban2025]. The official slide deck is internally inconsistent: one slide applies the majority rule when there are "<6" lesions, another when "<10" (the published figure says fewer than ten) [IACCTMS2025Slides]. One commentary quotes "five or more" CVS lesions as giving 98% specificity [VanDerWalt2026]. Status: Select-6 with the <10-lesion majority rule is the published figure.
- 2024 DIT wording: the Figure 3 footnote lists "one or more gadolinium-positive lesions" [Montalban2025]. The companion glossary requires Gd-enhancing and non-enhancing lesions together on one scan, as in 2017 [Solomon2026]. Status: unresolved in the sources I could read; a calculator should state which reading it uses.
- κ-FLC cut-off: 6.1 is recommended as a weighted mean [Hegen2023; Deisenhammer2025]. Published cut-offs range from 2.4 to 20, laboratories are told to verify locally, and κ-FLC and OCB disagree in about 10%–13% of cases [Deisenhammer2025; IACCTMS2025Slides]. Some summaries write ">6.1" rather than "≥6.1". Status: ≥6.1 per the CSF companion paper.
- 2017 vs 2010 trade-off: 2017 gave earlier diagnosis and higher sensitivity [Filippi2022; vanderVuurstdeVries2018], but lower specificity: 33% of those diagnosed under 2017 had no second attack over ≥5 years [vanderVuurstdeVries2018]. Status: accepted as a trade-off. There is no gold standard, because people are now treated before a second attack [Brownlee2026].
- Misdiagnosis: one view holds that misdiagnosis mainly reflects misapplication of the criteria rather than flawed criteria [Solomon2016; VanDerWalt2026; Samara2025]. Another holds that the new biomarkers (CVS, PRL, CSF) and safeguards for age ≥50 will reduce it [Montalban2025; Solomon2026]. Status: pooled misdiagnosis is still about 15% [Zurrer2024].
- Access: CVS, PRL, OCT and kFLC need equipment and expertise. OCB testing was unavailable in 13% of 122 countries [Solomon2026]. Status: the criteria keep conventional routes so that they can be used worldwide [Arrambide2026; Cai2026].
