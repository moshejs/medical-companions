Step 1

Is it a typical attack?

An attack is patient-reported symptoms with corroborating objective findings, typical of an inflammatory demyelinating event, lasting at least 24 hours without fever or infection. A first such event is a clinically isolated syndrome (CIS) 445. In about 85% of young adults with MS the disease begins as a CIS of the optic nerve, brainstem or spinal cord 316. The 2017 criteria apply mainly to a typical CIS 464. 2024 has two entry routes: an attack or objective progression (Figure 3), and incidental MRI findings or symptoms not specific to MS (Figure 2) 327.

For this case: An attack with symptoms and signs characteristic of MS enters Figure 3 327.

In plain wordsFirst the doctor asks whether the episode looks like MS: symptoms that come on over hours to days, last at least a day, and show up when you are examined, without a fever or infection to explain them.
Exam hookNo objective findings, no attack. Treating atypical symptoms as attacks, and accepting attacks without objective evidence, were leading causes of misdiagnosis 443.

An attack means symptoms with corroborating objective findings, lasting at least 24 hours, without fever or infection 445. Accepting attacks without objective evidence was a leading cause of misdiagnosis 443.

Cerebrospinal fluid

κ-FLC index = (CSF κ-FLC ÷ serum κ-FLC) ÷ (CSF albumin ÷ serum albumin). Positive CSF in 2024 means OCB or an index ≥6.1; each laboratory should verify that cut-off 114,256. The 2017 criteria use OCB only 464.

Optic nerve evidence (2024)

With rigorous quality control, an inter-eye difference of ≥6 μm in peripapillary RNFL or ≥4 μm in GCIPL supports optic nerve involvement; an MRI optic nerve lesion also counts (place it on the map). Any of these needs no better explanation for the optic nerve finding 219,418.

Step 4 · No better explanation

"No better explanation" means the presentation is typical of MS and nothing clinical or paraclinical suggests another diagnosis, or the workup for those diagnoses is negative 445. A calculator cannot decide this. See The differential.

2024 dissemination in time follows the paper's Figure 3 footnote: "one or more new T2 lesions or one or more gadolinium-positive lesions or a clinical attack" 327. The companion glossary reads it more strictly, as new T2 lesions over time or enhancing and non-enhancing lesions together on one scan, as in 2017 445. The sources do not settle this; the verdict tells you when the stricter reading would change the answer.

274 people with suspected MS: 2024 diagnosed 220 at the first workup vs 172 under 2017, median 40 vs 84 days. Against a 2017 diagnosis at follow-up (mean 15.3 months): accuracy 83.6%. Extra diagnoses came from RIS or non-specific symptoms (23), ≥4 regions (15) and the optic nerve (10) 54.

A 2024 meta-analysis found MS misdiagnosis in 5%–41% across studies; pooled 15% (95% CI 9–26%), and women were 2.1 times more likely to be misdiagnosed 515. See The differential for the usual mimics.

Where experts disagree

Debated

Diagnosing without dissemination in time

2024 allows a diagnosis on 4–5 sites alone, or with a central vein sign, to speed diagnosis 327,479. Real-world cohorts found many more diagnoses, specificity of 57.8% against 2017 at follow-up, and low 2017–2024 agreement in one cohort (κ=0.112) 54,179. The London authors note that their "specificity" counts 2017-negative CIS and RIS as false positives. No long-term prospective validation yet.

Unresolved

Calling RIS multiple sclerosis

About 51% of people with RIS develop clinical symptoms within 10 years 219,409, and OCB (HR 5.09) and κ-FLC ≥6.1 (HR 2.79) predict it 149. Critics point to overdiagnosis, the consequences of treatment and labelling, and gaps in implementation 54,256,420. Early evidence In 236 people with RIS at 22 Italian centres, the 2024 criteria gave an MS diagnosis at baseline in 67.4%; of those who later had a clinical event, 88.1% would have been diagnosed a median of 2 years earlier 117. Letters raising concerns were published with an authors' reply 328.

Varies by centre

The optic nerve as a fifth site

Adding it raised sensitivity without losing specificity by VEP in one cohort 483 and gave similar accuracy across MRI, OCT and VEP in another 484. Other cohorts found lower specificity, and asymptomatic optic nerve findings in non-optic-neuritis CIS added no patients 53,140. OCT and VEP thresholds depend on quality control, centre and device 418.

Threshold choice

Central vein sign and κ-FLC cut-offs

Earlier work used a 40% lesion rule; Select-3 was sensitive but not specific, and 2024 adopted Select-6 63,327,469. Published κ-FLC cut-offs range from 2.4 to 20; 6.1 is a weighted mean, and κ-FLC and OCB disagree in about 10%–13% of cases 114,219.

Exam hook2017: DIS = 2 of 4 areas (PV, cortical/JC, IT, cord); DIT = enhancing + non-enhancing together, or a new lesion, or a second attack; CSF OCB can stand in for DIT; symptomatic lesions count except in the optic nerve; PPMS = 1 year of progression + 2 of 3 (brain, ≥2 cord, OCB). 2024: optic nerve is the 5th site; DIS + positive CSF (OCB or κ-FLC ≥6.1) is enough; one site needs (CSF or DIT) and (CVS or PRL); 4–5 sites alone only after an attack or progression; RIS can qualify with ≥2 sites + CSF, CVS or DIT. 327,464
In plain wordsTo diagnose MS, doctors look for damage in more than one part of the nervous system, signs that it happened at more than one time (or a spinal fluid test that stands in for that), and then make sure nothing else explains it better. The 2024 rules add new scan and eye tests that can make the answer faster, but the final check still belongs to a person.
