Symptom 1 of 12 · Whole nervous system

Fatigue

How common. About 59% in a 69-study meta-analysis (59.1%), and 95% in a 16-country European survey of 16,808 people. Estimates vary most with the questionnaire used, and have fallen each decade since 2000. 251,507

Non-drug approaches in the published sources
  • Established Exercise (aerobic, resistance, balance, including yoga and pilates): in 26 trials with a non-exercise control, exercise reduced fatigue (SMD −0.53), with no excess of relapses. 204,354
  • Early evidence Self-management using mindfulness and cognitive behavioural techniques; CBT had a short-term benefit (SMD −0.47) that waned after treatment ended. 354,477
  • Guideline For people with EDSS 4 or more, NICE suggests supervised aerobic and progressive resistance activity combined with cognitive behavioural techniques. 354
Medicines in the published sources
  • Guideline NICE NG220 lists amantadine, modafinil or an SSRI as off-label options after specialist discussion, and advises against vitamin B12 injections for fatigue. 354
  • Trial negative TRIUMPHANT-MS: amantadine, modafinil and methylphenidate were no better than placebo and caused more adverse events (39–40% vs 31%). 361
In plain wordsMS fatigue is not ordinary tiredness. The options with the most consistent trial support are exercise and talking-therapy techniques, not tablets.
Exam hookFatigue drugs are a live controversy: TRIUMPHANT-MS (2021) found all three no better than placebo, yet NICE NG220 (2022) still lists off-label options.

Listing what guidelines and trials report is not a recommendation for any one person.

Fatigue: a day, three drugs and two non-drug options

Fatigue is the symptom with the clearest controversy. A 2021 trial found three commonly used drugs no better than placebo, while the 2022 NICE guideline still lists drug options. The day curve at the top is a schematic of an idea, not measured data.

Top: schematic, no measured values. Middle: MFIS fatigue scores (0–84, higher means more fatigue) from TRIUMPHANT-MS. Bottom: standardised mean differences; left of zero means less fatigue than control. 204,361,477

Schematic

A day, two ways

The two curves are a drawing of an idea, not data. One day puts activity in one long burst; the other splits the same activity with rests. NICE NG220 suggests self-management that uses mindfulness and cognitive behavioural techniques, and notes that heat and biological, physical and emotional stress can bring MS fatigue on. 354

In plain wordsSpreading tasks out and resting before running empty is a common self-management idea. It is not a tested dose of anything.

Walking: the fampridine race

Fampridine (dalfampridine, prolonged-release 4-aminopyridine) is the only medicine in these sources licensed to improve walking. The trials counted "timed-walk responders", people whose walking speed improved consistently, then measured how much faster those responders walked.

Trial

Grids are the published percentages drawn per 100 people. The race uses the published speed gains; the finishing-time difference is arithmetic from them, and the walkers are schematic. 173,174

MS-F203

Who responds

A "timed-walk responder" walked faster on most on-treatment visits than on any off-treatment visit. In MS-F203, 35% (78/224) on fampridine 10 mg twice daily were responders, vs 8% (6/72) on placebo. 173

In plain wordsRoughly one person in three or four had a consistent improvement, against about one in ten or twelve on a dummy pill.

Rehabilitation and exercise

Established

Rehabilitation programmes

Cochrane (8 trials, 747 people): strong evidence that inpatient multidisciplinary rehabilitation improves activity and participation in the short term, without changing impairment. Low-intensity programmes over longer periods brought longer-term quality-of-life gains. 243

Established

Exercise did not raise relapses

In the Cochrane exercise review, relapses were similar with and without exercise (25 vs 26), and the authors concluded exercise can be prescribed without harm. NICE NG220 tells clinicians to encourage exercise and to say it has no harmful effects on MS. 204,354

People with MS move less

Exercise can help manage symptoms, restore function and improve quality of life, yet people with MS remain much less active than the general population, unchanged over 25 years. 329

Early evidence

Exercise as medicine?

A 2019 review proposes prescribing exercise early, alongside drugs. Its symptomatic benefits are established; possible disease-modifying and preventive effects are still being tested. 107

Falls and balance

Over 3 months, 56% fell and 37% fell repeatedly; 65% of falls were indoors. Odds peaked around EDSS 4.0 and 6.0, and primary progressive MS doubled them (OR 2.02). NICE suggests vestibular rehabilitation for limited standing balance. 354,359

Heat and cooling

Heat slows or blocks conduction in demyelinated axons, so symptoms can return briefly in warm weather, a hot bath or exercise. Cooling before or during exercise can reduce this. See why heat blocks a damaged fibre. 111,436

A wider programme

A Lancet seminar recommends a comprehensive management programme that promotes wellness, deals with aggravating factors and manages other conditions alongside MS. 465

Mood: getting help

Depression is common (about 24–31% in pooled studies) and suicide risk is higher, especially around diagnosis. 44,301,433

If you are struggling, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line now, or tell a doctor or someone you trust today.

For the bladder, the UK consensus checks for infection and measures the residual volume after voiding before treatment. 8,136,153,173,174
In plain wordsMany MS symptoms can be eased even when the disease itself cannot be undone. Exercise and rehabilitation have the most consistent evidence, and some symptoms have specific medicines.
