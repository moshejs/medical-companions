Part 5 · Living with MS

Nursing care

What drug labels, guidelines and nursing studies report on MS nursing care: assessment scales, infusion and first-dose monitoring, lab calendars, teaching and notes. Each part has an animation or a tool to try.

ScopeThis module reports what labels, guidelines and published studies say. It is not a care plan and does not replace local protocols, your scope of practice or the prescriber. Drug details come from current US labels unless stated. Where the sources name a gap, the module says so instead of filling it.

Domain 1 of 9

Walking and falls

In an individual-data meta-analysis of 537 people followed for 3 months, 56% fell at least once and 37% fell two or more times (1,721 falls). 65% of falls happened indoors, and fall risk peaked at EDSS 4.0 and 6.0, with odds ratios of about 5. 359 A handbook review puts balance and gait problems at 50–80% of people with MS. 69 On the Timed 25-Foot Walk, clinically stable people varied by less than 20% over 12 months, so the authors proposed that a rise of more than 20% may mean a significant change in gait. 239

Exam hookFalls: 56% fell at least once in 3 months, 37% twice or more. T25FW: more than 20% slower is the proposed change threshold.

The other scales, and what could not be confirmed

EDSS: who scores it

012345678910

The EDSS runs from 0 (normal) to 10 (death due to MS), in half-points from 1.0 to 9.5, built on 8 Functional Systems: pyramidal, cerebellar, brainstem, sensory, bowel and bladder, visual, cerebral and other. 267

It is traditionally scored by trained neurologists using the standardised Neurostatus method. In a Swiss randomised crossover study of 100 people, specially trained non-neurologist professionals agreed with neurologists at a concordance of 0.87. 297 In two phase 3 trials, 40.1% of assessments had internal inconsistencies at first entry; electronic checking cut this to 22.1% and changed the final score in 14.8%. 121 Reported inter-rater kappa is 0.32–0.76. 315

Exam hookTrials usually count confirmed worsening as +1.0 point from a baseline of 5.5 or less, or +0.5 above 5.5, confirmed after 12 or 24 weeks. 237
Item structure not confirmed

Modified Fatigue Impact Scale

A multidimensional scale with physical, cognitive and psychosocial subscales. Six-month reliability was ICC 0.863, against 0.751 for the FSS. 276 A four-country study found internal consistency of 0.92 overall but only 0.65 for the psychosocial subscale, and advised caution with that subscale. 257

The widely quoted structure of 21 items and a 0–84 total comes from a full text that could not be retrieved, so it is not stated here as fact. 273

Grade set not confirmed

Modified Ashworth Scale

In the original reliability study, two raters grading elbow-flexor spasticity in 30 patients agreed on 86.7% of ratings (Kendall's tau 0.847). 46 The grades usually taught (0, 1, 1+, 2, 3, 4) were not confirmed in the abstract read.

NICE lists signs that should raise suspicion of spasticity (spasms, stiffness, pain or restriction with movement, a change in mobility or arm function), recommends looking for aggravating factors such as pressure ulcers, bladder or bowel problems, infection, posture and pain, and notes that some people use their spasticity to stand, walk or transfer. 354

MSIS-29

A patient-reported impact scale with 20 physical and 9 psychological items, developed from 129 candidate items in a postal survey of 1,530 UK MS Society members. Item test-retest reliability was r=0.65–0.90 and Cronbach's alpha up to 0.91. 211

Advanced and inpatient MS

The EAN palliative-care guideline for severe progressive MS (EDSS above 6.0) made 34 recommendations, 33 of them weak, and notes a lack of evidence on advance care planning and on support for carers. 442 NICE recommends palliative care referral when appropriate and discussing carers' right to an assessment. 354

In Veneto death records, aspiration pneumonia, pressure ulcers or skin infections, sepsis and urinary tract infection were all associated with MS-related deaths. 137 NICE recommends regular assessment of contracture risk 354, but no MS-specific trial of nursing contracture-prevention methods was found in the research for this module.

Checks before the infusion

The ocrelizumab label calls for checking for active infection before every infusion and delaying the infusion if one is present. Before the first dose it requires hepatitis B screening, quantitative immunoglobulins and liver tests. The label places live or live-attenuated vaccines at least 4 weeks before starting, and non-live vaccines at least 2 weeks before where possible. 365 The label schedule is 300 mg, 300 mg two weeks later, then 600 mg every 6 months. 365

The first dose of fingolimod slows the heart. The label describes six hours of observation and when it is extended. Choose a course to see the decision at hour 6.

When the observation applies

In the label, fingolimod first-dose monitoring applies at the first dose, on restarting after a break of more than 14 days, and on paediatric dose increases. It begins with an ECG before dosing. 164 The label also requires VZV antibody testing in people without confirmed chickenpox or full vaccination; seronegative people are vaccinated and treatment starts a month later. 164

Self-injected medicines

Glatiramer acetate

Subcutaneous only. The label lists the arms, abdomen, hips and thighs for self-injection and says to let the syringe reach room temperature for about 20 minutes. The 20 mg/mL (daily) and 40 mg/mL (three times weekly, at least 48 hours apart) products are not interchangeable. 95

On 20 mg/mL, injection-site erythema occurred in 43% (placebo 10%). An immediate post-injection reaction (at least two of flushing, chest pain, palpitations, tachycardia, anxiety, dyspnoea, throat constriction or urticaria) occurred in about 16% (placebo 4%); it usually starts months into treatment, within seconds to minutes of an injection, and the label notes its symptoms may overlap with anaphylaxis. Lipoatrophy (about 2%) is probably permanent; correct technique and site rotation may reduce it. 95

Interferon beta-1a (SC)

The label titrates from 20% of the target dose over 4 weeks and suggests dosing on the same three days each week at least 48 hours apart, preferably late afternoon or evening. Analgesics or antipyretics on dosing days may ease flu-like symptoms. 400

Injection-site reactions occurred in 92% at 44 mcg (placebo 39%) and necrosis in 3%; influenza-like symptoms in 59% (51%). The label advises against injecting into an affected area until it has healed. 400

Ofatumumab

Self-injected into the abdomen, thigh or outer upper arm, avoiding moles, scars, stretch marks and tender, bruised, red, scaly or hard skin. The label states that the first injection should be done under healthcare-professional guidance. 241

Systemic injection reactions occurred in 21% (15% with teriflunomide plus placebo injections), most after the first injection (14.4%), falling to 4.4% after the second and under 3% after the third. 241

Exam hookOfatumumab systemic injection reactions: 14.4% at injection 1, 4.4% at injection 2.
Early evidence

Injection anxiety

In 101 people starting weekly IM interferon, injection self-efficacy before treatment predicted adherence at 6 months, through the ability to self-inject. 320 In a trial of 30 people unable to self-inject because of anxiety, 8 given a six-session nurse-administered programme self-injected at 6 weeks, against 3 controls (significant among completers, p=0.022; a trend by intention to treat, p=0.058). 321

In plain wordsFear of needles is common and can stop treatment. In a small trial, nurse-led practice sessions helped some people inject themselves.

The SOLU-MEDROL label reports arrhythmias and cardiac arrest after rapid IV administration of large doses, more than 0.5 g over less than 10 minutes. It also reports bradycardia unrelated to infusion speed. 446 NICE describes IV methylprednisolone 1 g daily for 3–5 days as the IV alternative. 354

The NICE regimens

Oral methylprednisolone 0.5 g daily for 5 days. IV methylprednisolone 1 g daily for 3–5 days is the alternative when oral steroids have failed or are not tolerated, or when admission is needed for a severe relapse or to monitor conditions such as diabetes or depression. Treatment is offered as early as possible and within 14 days of onset. 354

Exam hookNICE: oral MP 0.5 g × 5 days first; IV 1 g × 3–5 days as the alternative; within 14 days of onset.

What people are told

NICE asks that people be told about temporary mental-health effects (insomnia, depression, confusion, agitation) and worse glucose control in diabetes, and be given written information. It recommends against supplying steroids for people to treat future relapses themselves. 354 The label describes psychiatric effects from euphoria and insomnia to severe depression and psychosis, raised blood glucose, and rare toxic hepatitis, sometimes weeks later, after pulsed 1 g/day courses for MS relapse. 446

Controversy

Oral or IV, and what steroids do

A Cochrane review of 6 trials found methylprednisolone or ACTH protective against worsening within 5 weeks (OR 0.37), with too few data on later relapses or long-term disability. 145 Oral and IV gave similar recovery at 4 weeks in a Cochrane review of 5 trials. 59 In COPOUSEP, 81% (oral) vs 80% (IV) improved by day 28; insomnia was more frequent with oral treatment (77% vs 64%). Some services still default to IV. 282

In plain wordsSteroids can speed recovery from a relapse over the next few weeks. Tablets worked as well as a drip in the trials, but they did not change the long-term course.

5 · Teaching, and what gets misunderstood

Relapse or not? The NICE definition as a sorter

A worse day is not always a relapse. Answer the questions to see how NICE's definition sorts a change in symptoms.

2 · Infection, fever or another cause found?
3 · Stable for at least 1 month before this?
4 · Symptoms present for more than 3 months?
What the definition says
Infection comes first

Before diagnosing a relapse, NICE asks for infection, particularly urinary and respiratory, to be ruled out, and for a relapse to be told apart from fluctuation or progression. 354 Screened at a suspected relapse, 30.4% of inpatients and 20.0% of outpatients in one centre had a possible urinary infection. 150

In plain wordsA day or two of feeling worse in the heat, or with a bladder infection, is often the old symptoms flaring, not new damage. That is why infection is checked first.

Teaching topics the guidelines and labels name

Heat

In a Swiss registry survey of 760 people, 73.3% reported temperature sensitivity, most often to heat. Of those who were heat-sensitive and knew of cooling methods, only 44.3% used them. 220 NICE notes that MS fatigue may be brought on by heat. 354

Infection signs

NICE recommends explaining that symptom changes can come from another illness such as infection, a relapse or a change in disease course, and giving a plan that says whom to contact. 354 The natalizumab label describes PML symptoms progressing over days to weeks: one-sided weakness or clumsiness, visual disturbance, and changes in thinking, memory and orientation. 475 The alemtuzumab label asks for patients to be taught stroke symptoms. 281

Vaccines

The AAN guideline recommends following local vaccine standards with yearly influenza vaccine, vaccinating at least 4–6 weeks before starting immunosuppressive or immunomodulating therapy, avoiding live-attenuated vaccines during such therapy, and delaying vaccination during a relapse. 132 Anti-CD20 labels exclude live vaccines until B cells recover. 365

Pregnancy planning

The 2026 UK consensus puts family planning into treatment choice, describes antibody transfer into breast milk as generally low, and vaccines in pregnancy as generally safe. 335 Label contraception windows: 2 months after fingolimod 164, 6 months after ofatumumab 241, 6 months (women) or 14 weeks (men) after each cladribine course 304, and for teriflunomide during treatment and accelerated elimination. 14

After stopping a DMT

After stopping fingolimod, the label reports severe increases in disability with multiple new MRI lesions, usually within 12 weeks. 164 Without accelerated elimination, the label says teriflunomide plasma levels take on average 8 months, and up to 2 years, to fall below 0.02 mg/L; cholestyramine or activated charcoal for 11 days lowers levels by more than 98%. 14

Exam hookFingolimod rebound: usually within 12 weeks of stopping, up to 24.

Adherence

Reported DMT adherence ranged from 41% to 88% across 24 studies. 313 Pooled 1-year adherence to daily oral DMTs was 78.5% by MPR and 71.8% by PDC, with 25.4% discontinuing. 356 Depression was associated with lower adherence (OR 0.618). 339 The AAN guideline includes monitoring adherence and engaging patients among its 30 recommendations. 396

Exercise, smoking and fatigue

NICE recommends telling people that regular exercise may benefit MS and does not harm it, and advising against smoking because it increases disability progression. For fatigue it recommends a personalised self-management discussion and aerobic, resistance and balance exercise, and advises against vitamin B12 injections and hyperbaric oxygen. 354

Controversy Energy conservation helped in the short term in 2 pooled trials 40 but was no better than nurse-given fatigue information in an 86-person trial. 41

The team NICE describes

A single point of contact who knows MS services, and care through a coordinated multidisciplinary team that includes MS nurses, neurologists, physiotherapists, occupational therapists, speech and language therapists, psychologists, dietitians, continence specialists, MS pharmacists, rehabilitation consultants, social care and primary care. NICE recommends a face-to-face follow-up within 6 weeks of diagnosis and a comprehensive review at least once a year, which people can ask for. 354

What the role covers

An international review describes MS nurses helping people understand the disease and treatment options, supporting treatment start and management, and encouraging adherence; it predicted oral therapies would add drug-specific monitoring. 57 A proposal for MS Care Units puts neurologists and nurses at the core, working to formal protocols for starting and following DMTs. 441 MS Nurse PRO is a free, accredited European e-learning curriculum started in 2012. 129

Early evidence

Workforce

Of 163 UK MS specialist nurses, 83.4% regularly worked unpaid overtime; psychological support, physical assessments and benefits support were most often left undone. 394 The 2021 mapping survey counted 360 nurses (298 whole-time) and estimated that 79% of people with MS lived in areas above the sustainable caseload, a shortfall of 149 whole-time nurses. 334 A 2026 scoping review mapped 12 studies of nurse-led digital interventions with associations, not pooled effects, on self-management, adherence and fatigue. 16

The evidence gap

No randomised trial or pooled systematic review isolating the effect of MS specialist nurses on relapses, admissions or disability was found. The case for the role rests mainly on workforce surveys, service evaluations and descriptive reviews. 16,334,394

In plain wordsMS nurses are the people who explain, check, teach and coordinate. Nobody has yet run a trial that measures how much they change hard outcomes.
