Part 1 · The disease

The immune attack

How immune cells switched on in a lymph node leave it, cross the blood-brain barrier and strip myelin, then smoulder behind a closed barrier. Step through the attack, or turn on where each drug class acts.

Teal numbered badges mark the step each drug class targets. Mechanism only; trial results are in How the drugs work.

Step 1 · Lymph node

Activation in the lymph node

Established model Early in MS, a peripheral immune response aimed at the brain and spinal cord drives the disease; later, reactions confined within the CNS dominate. 204 In a lymph node, an antigen-presenting cell (APC) shows a peptide on MHC class II to CD4 helper T cells, which become Th1 and Th17 effector cells and multiply; CD8 T cells and B cells are activated too. Myelin-reactive T cells occur in similar proportions in people with and without MS, so either they are dysfunctional in MS or other immune factors are needed. 401 Weak regulatory T cells (CD4+CD25+FoxP3+) are proposed to let the response run unchecked. 244 MS risk genes point mainly to T-helper-cell differentiation and are enriched in every major peripheral immune cell type. 221,222

In plain wordsImmune cells that should ignore the brain are switched on in a lymph node, far away from it, and copy themselves.
Exam hook"Outside-in" model: autoreactive CD4 T cells (Th1, Th17) are activated in the periphery first. Myelin-reactive T cells are also found in healthy people.

Why removing B cells works before antibodies fall

Anti-CD20 antibodies empty the blood of B cells within two weeks, yet antibody levels and the plasma cells that make them barely change. Scrub through the first year after a first infusion to see the order of events.

Schematic curves through the published anchor points (B cells low by day 14; response as early as 8–12 weeks; IgG generally unchanged with initial courses). Heights are not measured values.

Weeks 12–48 · now week 14

Less inflammation, same antibodies

IgG levels generally do not change with initial courses, 175 and the abnormal spinal-fluid antibody pattern (oligoclonal bands) does not appear to change, consistent with a benefit that does not depend on antibody. 87 After the last ocrelizumab infusion, B cells took a median of 72 weeks to return. 186,364

In plain wordsRemoving B cells calms the attack within weeks, while the body's stock of antibodies stays the same. So B cells must be doing something other than making antibodies.
Exam hookFast effect, unchanged IgG, persistent oligoclonal bands: the case that B cells act as antigen presenters and cytokine sources.

Open questions

Controversy

Outside-in or inside-out?

The classical model has peripheral autoreactive lymphocytes crossing the barrier first. 204,504 But in people who died during or soon after a relapse, 7 of 12 showed widespread oligodendrocyte death and microglial activation with few or no lymphocytes, suggesting a process that starts inside the CNS. 21 The question is open; both views fit a CNS-compartmentalised response later on. 292

Controversy

Why does anti-CD20 work?

One view: through antigen presentation and cytokines, since the effect comes before immunoglobulin falls and oligoclonal bands persist. 87,283,401 The other: antibody and complement do take part in demyelination in at least some lesions. 48,289 Both may operate, weighted differently by lesion and person.

Controversy

One disease pattern, or four?

Lucchinetti 2000 described four lesion patterns (I: like T-cell-mediated EAE; II: like T-cell plus antibody and complement EAE; III and IV: suggesting a primary oligodendrocyte problem), different between people but uniform within one person. 289 21 of 22 people sampled at two or more time points kept the same pattern. 313 Active lesions in established MS at autopsy looked uniform instead. 48 Unresolved; biopsy and autopsy cohorts differ.

Established

EAE: useful but partial

Experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis reproduces inflammation, demyelination, axon loss and repair, and many MS drugs were developed or validated in it. 93 It is driven mainly by T cells, while MS also involves B cells, antibodies and innate immunity, and no model reproduces all of MS. 401 Critics call it a model of acute CNS inflammation rather than of MS. 452

Established

The genes point to immune cells

The 2019 genomic map (47,429 people with MS) found susceptibility genes enriched in every major peripheral immune cell type and, in the brain, in microglia but not in astrocytes or neurons. The authors read this as MS being triggered first in the peripheral immune system. 222 More in Genes & risk factors.
