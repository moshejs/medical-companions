Fatigue: a day, three drugs and two non-drug options

Fatigue is the symptom with the clearest controversy. A 2021 trial found three commonly used drugs no better than placebo, while the 2022 NICE guideline still lists drug options. The day curve at the top is a schematic of an idea, not measured data.

Top: schematic, no measured values. Middle: MFIS fatigue scores (0–84, higher means more fatigue) from TRIUMPHANT-MS. Bottom: standardised mean differences; left of zero means less fatigue than control. [[Nourbakhsh2021,Heine2015,vandenAkker2016]]

Walking: the fampridine race

Fampridine (dalfampridine, prolonged-release 4-aminopyridine) is the only medicine in these sources licensed to improve walking. The trials counted "timed-walk responders", people whose walking speed improved consistently, then measured how much faster those responders walked.

Trial

Grids are the published percentages drawn per 100 people. The race uses the published speed gains; the finishing-time difference is arithmetic from them, and the walkers are schematic. [[Goodman2009,Goodman2010]]

Rehabilitation and exercise

Exam hookFampridine is a potassium-channel blocker that improves walking speed in responders (35% and 42.9% vs 8% and 9.3% on placebo); a history of seizure is a contraindication. For the bladder, the UK consensus checks for infection and measures the residual volume after voiding before treatment. [[Goodman2009,Goodman2010,AmpyraLabel2026,FDAdalfampridine,Fowler2009]]
In plain wordsMany MS symptoms can be eased even when the disease itself cannot be undone. Exercise and rehabilitation have the most consistent evidence, and some symptoms have specific medicines. A worsening that comes with heat or an infection is not always a new relapse: see relapse versus pseudo-relapse.
