There is no genetic test for MS. No genetic test is used to diagnose MS or to predict who will develop it; genetic testing for MS is used only in research, and a person can carry many risk variants and never develop MS. [[MSIF2022]] Polygenic scores separate groups (AUC 0.73–0.80) [[Shams2023]], but a prediction review judged them "unlikely to ever" be clinically helpful for an individual, though useful for choosing people for prevention trials. Can MS be prevented?

No intervention has been proven to prevent MS in the general population. The only randomised evidence of delaying a first clinical event comes from people with radiologically isolated syndrome (RIS): MS-like lesions found by chance on MRI, with no symptoms. [[Okuda2023,LebrunFrenay2023TERIS]]
