The DMT ladder

How MS drugs are sequenced: start on a lower rung and step up after breakthrough disease, or start high from the beginning. See what the 2018 guidelines say, what the observational studies report, and why stopping later in life is still debated.

Published guidance and studies only. This module reports what the ECTRIMS/EAN and AAN guidelines, trials and labels say. The person on the ladder is an illustration, not a case. Which medicine suits a particular person, and when to change it, is a decision for that person and their MS team.

Breakthrough and NEDA-3

AAN 2018 · Switching recommendation 1 · Level B

What counts as breakthrough disease

Clinicians should discuss switching with people who take their DMT as prescribed, have been on it long enough for it to take full effect, and have one or more relapses, two or more unequivocally new MRI-detected lesions, or increased disability on examination, over a one-year period. Any one of the three is enough. [[RaeGrant2018]]

ECTRIMS/EAN 2018 recommendation 14 (strong) says: Offer a more efficacious drug to patients treated with interferon or glatiramer acetate who show evidence of disease activity. Recommendation 11 (consensus) sets the yardstick: a reference brain MRI usually within six months of treatment onset, compared with one typically 12 months after starting treatment. The 2021 MAGNIMS-CMSC-NAIMS consensus names 3D FLAIR as the core sequence for spotting new lesions. [[Montalban2018,Wattjes2021]]

Exam hookAAN breakthrough, in one year: ≥1 relapse, or ≥2 new MRI lesions, or worse on exam. A single new lesion does not meet it on its own.
Established definition

NEDA-3: no evident disease activity

  • 1 · Relapsesnone
  • 2 · MRIno new or enlarging T2 lesions and no gadolinium-enhancing lesions
  • 3 · Disabilityno sustained disability progression

The trial version first appeared in a post hoc analysis of AFFIRM: over 2 years, 37% on natalizumab and 7% on placebo had no clinical or radiological activity. In 2015 NEDA was proposed as a treat-to-target goal, with a new baseline MRI once the drug has taken effect and at least yearly MRI after that. In OPTIMUM, 25.0% on ponesimod and 16.4% on teriflunomide met NEDA-3 over 108 weeks. [[Havrdova2009,Giovannoni2015,Kappos2021]]

In plain wordsNEDA-3 is a three-part checklist: no attacks, no new spots on the scan, no lasting worsening. It is a target used in trials and clinics, not a promise that the disease has stopped.

Escalate, or start high? A live controversy

Early evidence · observational

For starting high

  • Harding 2019 (Wales, 592 people): 5-year EDSS change 0.3 with early intensive therapy versus 1.2 with escalation (adjusted β −0.85, 95% CI −1.38 to −0.32). 60% of those who escalated developed sustained disability before escalating. [[Harding2019]]
  • He 2020 (MSBase and Sweden, 213 versus 253 matched people): starting high-efficacy therapy within 2 years of onset versus at 4–6 years gave mean EDSS 2.3 versus 3.5 at year 10 (difference −0.98, −1.51 to −0.45, over years 6–10). [[He2020]]
  • Brown 2019 (1,555 matched people): initial fingolimod, alemtuzumab or natalizumab carried a lower hazard of conversion to SPMS than initial interferon or glatiramer acetate (HR 0.66, 0.44–0.99; 5-year risk 7% versus 12%). Escalating within 5 years versus later: HR 0.76 (0.66–0.88). [[Brown2019]]
  • Spelman 2021: 34.5% of Swedish and 7.6% of Danish patients started a highly effective DMT; the Swedish strategy was associated with less 24-week confirmed worsening (HR 0.71, 0.57–0.90). [[Spelman2021]]
  • An Italian Delphi of 65 neurologists (2025) reached strong consensus to support early HE-DMT initiation to prevent irreversible disability. [[Filippi2025]]
Why it is not settled

Against firm conclusions

  • All four studies are observational. People who start high may differ from those who escalate in ways the matching cannot remove (confounding by indication).
  • AAN 2018 called for research comparing early higher-potency treatment with stepped care. [[RaeGrant2018]]
  • Baseline DELIVER-MS data show milder disease among people willing to be randomised, and 67% of the observational arm chose early high-efficacy therapy. [[Tallantyre2026]]
  • Over half of the trials in the 2024 Cochrane network meta-analysis were industry-sponsored, and its authors found the evidence beyond 2 years insufficient. [[GonzalezLorenzo2024]]

The guidelines in their own words

Both main guidelines date from 2018. No 2024–2026 update of either was found; the newest consensus is the 2025 Italian Delphi above. ECTRIMS/EAN grades are strong, weak or consensus; AAN levels are A (must), B (should) and C (may). [[Montalban2018,RaeGrant2018,Filippi2025]]

Later life: de-escalation and stopping

DISCOMS asked whether stable older people could stop their DMT safely. It randomised 259 people aged 55 or older, with no relapse for 5 years and no new MRI lesion for 3 years, to stop or continue. Press Play to watch new disease activity accumulate in each arm over 2 years. [[Corboy2023]]

What the guidelines say about stopping

AAN 2018 (Level B) advocates that stable patients continue their current DMT unless the patient and physician decide a trial off therapy is warranted, and (Level C) allows advising stopping in SPMS without relapses or enhancing lesions in people who have not been ambulatory (EDSS 7 or more) for at least 2 years. ECTRIMS/EAN 2018 R18 (weak) favours continuing a stable, well-tolerated DMD, and R17 (weak) warns of resumed disease activity or even rebound when stopping treatment, particularly with natalizumab. [[RaeGrant2018,Montalban2018]]

Rebound: why some drugs are not simply stopped

After stopping natalizumab, relapses in RESTORE appeared from 4–8 weeks after the last dose. After stopping fingolimod, the label reports severe increases in disability, usually within 12 weeks. The timing and the numbers are animated in Safety & monitoring. [[Fox2014,GilenyaLabel2026]]

Beyond the ladder: aHSCT

Autologous haematopoietic stem cell transplantation is not a rung of either 2018 guideline in this module. It is a specialist treatment whose place is still debated, and its evidence is covered in Toward a cure.

In plain wordsSome people start on a gentler medicine and move up if MS stays active; others start with a stronger one. Later in life, some stable people and their doctors discuss stopping. Each path has benefits and risks that are weighed together.
