What counts as a relapse

McDonald 2010 panel

The attack definition

Patient-reported symptoms or objectively observed signs typical of an acute inflammatory demyelinating event in the central nervous system, lasting at least 24 hours, in the absence of fever or infection. Paroxysmal symptoms count only as multiple episodes over not less than 24 hours. [[Polman2011]]

NICE NG220 (2022)

How the guideline describes it

New or worsening symptoms lasting more than 24 hours, with no infection or other cause, after a stable period of at least 1 month. Clinicians are asked to rule out infection (particularly urinary and respiratory) and to tell a relapse apart from fluctuation or progression. A relapse should not routinely be diagnosed when symptoms have been present for more than 3 months. [[NICE2022]]

Controversy

Oral or IV? Dose, not route

High-dose oral methylprednisolone was no worse than IV in COPOUSEP and in the Cochrane review. The ONTT's worse result with oral prednisone used a low dose (1 mg/kg). NICE uses oral 0.5 g a day for 5 days and says lower doses should not be used. [[LePage2015,Burton2012,Beck1992,NICE2022]]

Established

Infections and relapses

In 73 people followed weekly (6,466 patient-weeks), the risk of a relapse was 2.1 times higher from 2 weeks before to 5 weeks after a systemic infection, and those relapses more often left a sustained deficit (rate ratio 3.8). So a fever can bring a pseudo-relapse, and can also come before a true one. [[Buljevac2002]]

Looking for a urinary infection

At a suspected relapse, a possible urinary tract infection was found in 30.4% of inpatients and 20.0% of clinic patients at Johns Hopkins: about 1 in 3 and 1 in 5 screened. Female sex went with infection (OR 3.90). [[Fitzgerald2019]]

Why recovery takes weeks

Remission comes from resolution of inflammation, remyelination, or conduction restored along fibres that stay demyelinated. In CombiRx, relapse severity was the only factor linked to the speed of recovery: of those who recovered, 80% had done so by 180 days and 94% by 365 days. [[Smith1999,Koch2023]]
