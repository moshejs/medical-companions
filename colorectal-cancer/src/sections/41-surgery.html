Part 4 · Treatment

Surgery

For bowel cancer that has not spread, an operation is the main curative treatment. Pick where the tumour sits, then watch the operation: which segment comes out, where the vessels are tied, which lymph nodes go with it, and how the bowel is put back together.

Total mesorectal excision and the circumferential margin

The rectum sits inside a fatty envelope, the mesorectum, which carries its lymph nodes and is wrapped in a thin fascia. Bill Heald's total mesorectal excision (TME) removes the rectum with that envelope intact, by sharp dissection along the plane outside the fascia. Phil Quirke showed that whether tumour reaches the cut surface of the specimen, the circumferential resection margin (CRM), largely decides whether cancer comes back in the pelvis. [[Heald1982,Heald1986,Quirke1986]]

Keyhole, open or robotic: what the trials found

For colon cancer, laparoscopic surgery matched open surgery on cancer outcomes and shortened recovery. For rectal cancer the picture is more mixed: two trials did not show that laparoscopic surgery gives an equally good specimen, while local recurrence in the larger trials was the same. Robotic surgery is the newest question. [[COST2004,Stevenson2015,Fleshman2015,Bonjer2015]]

Enhanced recovery (ERAS)

A meta-analysis of 16 randomised trials (2,376 people) found enhanced-recovery programmes lowered overall complications (RR 0.60), mainly non-surgical ones, and shortened hospital stay by 2.28 days without more readmissions. The ERAS Society's 2018 colorectal guideline grades each care item by GRADE. [[Greco2014,Gustafsson2019]]

Early evidence

Complete mesocolic excision

In Erlangen (1,329 R0 colon resections), 5-year local recurrence fell from 6.5% to 3.6% and cancer-related 5-year survival rose from 82.1% to 89.1% as CME was adopted. This is a single-centre before–after comparison, not a randomised trial. [[Hohenberger2009]]

Established

Low anterior resection syndrome

After a low join, bowel function can change. The LARS score adds five items (flatus incontinence, liquid-stool incontinence, frequency, clustering, urgency): 0–20 no LARS, 21–29 minor, 30–42 major. Scores were worse after radiotherapy and with tumours under 5 cm. [[Emmertsen2012]] More in Living with colorectal cancer.

Established

Stent as a bridge to surgery

In CReST (245 people, left-sided obstruction), stenting relieved obstruction in 82.4% of attempts. In the curative group, stent-then-surgery vs emergency surgery: 30-day mortality 3.6% vs 5.6% (not significant), stoma 47.5% vs 67.9%, and no difference in 3-year recurrence. [[CReST2022]]

Established

MRI before rectal surgery

In MERCURY (408 people), high-resolution MRI predicted a clear CRM with 92% specificity: of 349 cases MRI called clear, 327 (94%) were clear at surgery. This is why rectal cancer is planned around the MRI. [[MERCURY2006]] See Rectal cancer pathway.

Controversy

Robotic vs laparoscopic

ROLARR found no significant fall in conversion; REAL found lower 3-year locoregional recurrence (1.6% vs 4.0%). REAL comes from high-volume centres in one country, so how far it applies elsewhere is not yet known. [[Jayne2017,Feng2025]]

Survival and recurrence figures on this page come from groups of people in trials and series. They describe populations, and cannot predict what will happen to one person.
