Under 50: red flags and delay

Red flags present

Younger patients are more likely than older ones to present with visible blood (41% vs 26% in one comparison cited by the ACS), iron-deficiency anaemia and abdominal pain, and their time to diagnosis is about 40% longer, partly because the symptoms are taken for commoner conditions. [[Siegel2026CRC]] In a US case-control study of 5,075 people with early-onset cancer, four red flags in the 3 months to 2 years before diagnosis were linked to the cancer: abdominal pain, rectal bleeding, diarrhoea and iron-deficiency anaemia. [[Fritz2023]] In people under 50 in the NICE FIT study, 1.5% (16 of 1,103) had colorectal cancer, FIT sensitivity at 10 µg/g was 81.3%, and the NPV stayed above 99.5% at every cut-off. [[DSouza2021CD]]

Where the evidence is debated

Debate

Which FIT threshold?

UK guidance uses ≥10 µg Hb/g. Moving to 20 µg/g would miss fewer than one extra cancer per 1,000 tested at 2% prevalence. A UK service evaluation of 13,361 FITs found cancer in 0.1% below 4 µg/g, 0.6% at 4–9.9, 3.3% at 10–99.9 and 20.7% at 100 or more; at 10–19.9 µg/g the rate (1.4%) was below NICE's 3% PPV threshold for urgent referral. [[Monahan2022,PinVieito2022,Bailey2021]]

Debate

Does waiting longer worsen survival?

In a Danish primary-care cohort, 3-year mortality fell with diagnostic intervals up to 5 weeks and then rose, because the sickest people are diagnosed fastest. A review across cancers found more evidence of harm from delay for colorectal cancer than for many other sites, but the studies were heterogeneous Early evidence. [[Torring2011,Neal2015]]

Established

Emergencies cost lives

In England, 1997–2012, 24.3% of 286,591 colorectal cancer operations were emergencies, with 30-day mortality of 13.3% against 3.4% for planned surgery. After bowel screening began in 2006, the share of eligible people needing emergency surgery fell from 23.4% to 14.9%. [[Askari2017]]

Established

Who presents as an emergency?

Across six high-income countries, emergency presenters had substantially higher 12-month mortality, and emergency presentation was more likely at older age and advanced stage. Rectal cancer had the lowest share (12.1% on average). In England, female sex, age over 79, deprivation and Black African/Caribbean ethnicity predicted emergency surgery. [[McPhail2022,Askari2017]]
