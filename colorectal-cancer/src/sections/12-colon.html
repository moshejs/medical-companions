Why this matters for cancer

Established

The colon has no Paneth cells

In the small intestine, Paneth cells sit between the stem cells and feed them Wnt3, EGF and the Notch ligand Dll4; removing Paneth cells removes the stem cells. The colon has none. Instead, Reg4+ deep crypt secretory cells act as "Paneth cell equivalents", and ablating them in mice causes loss of colonic stem cells. [[Sato2011,Sasaki2016]]

Established

Normal crypts already carry drivers

Driver mutations are present in about 1% of normal-looking crypts in middle-aged people. Adenomas and carcinomas are rare outcomes of widespread change in normal lining. Some mutational processes act in everyone; others only in some people, some crypts or some periods of life. [[LeeSix2019]]

Established

A mutation must win the crypt

A new mutation becomes fixed in two steps: one of about 7 stem cells is hit, then its progeny take over the whole crypt, which takes several years. This crypt organisation underlies most theories of how adenomas begin. [[Nicholson2018,Humphries2008]]

Hypothesis

"Bad luck": stem-cell divisions

Lifetime cancer risk across tissues correlated with the total number of normal stem-cell divisions (r = 0.81), and replication errors were proposed to cause about two-thirds of mutations in cancers. The idea is contested: geographic differences in mutational signatures point to environmental mutagens too. [[Tomasetti2015,Tomasetti2017,DiazGay2025]]

Hypothesis

Field effects

A "field effect" is a territory of cellular and molecular change that predisposes to neoplasia. The broader "etiologic field effect" adds diet, lifestyle, microbes, hormones and genes acting on the whole tissue. Age-related CpG island methylation in normal colon overlaps with most loci methylated in colon cancer. [[Lochhead2015,Toyota1999]]

Right vs left

Two embryos, two kinds of tumour

The right (midgut) and left (hindgut) colon differ in developmental origin, patterning genes, mutagen exposure and gut flora. MSI and CIMP tumours lean right, CIN tumours left; Lynch syndrome tumours occur mainly in the right colon and FAP tumours mainly in the left. More in From polyp to cancer and Tumour biomarkers.
