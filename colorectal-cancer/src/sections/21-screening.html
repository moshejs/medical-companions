The FIT threshold: what the cut-off trades

A FIT measures haemoglobin in stool, in µg per gram. The programme chooses the level that counts as positive. Lower cut-offs find more cancers and more high-risk adenomas, but send more people to colonoscopy. The slider stops only at the thresholds the English FIT pilot (27,238 people, OC-Sensor) actually reported; values between them are not estimated. [[Li2021]]

Estimated sensitivity and the share of all participants referred, by threshold, from the English pilot. Brand matters: two FIT brands gave different positivity at the same nominal µg/g. [[Li2021,deKlerk2019]]

Around the world

Thresholds differ widely

Across 38 screening programmes in 28 countries, 15 used 20 µg/g. The rest used 8.5 to 120 µg/g, and nine sites had to change the threshold they first chose. England runs 2-yearly FIT at 120 µg/g. [[Young2025,Li2022]]

PPV

Higher cut-off, higher yield per scope

In England's modelling, each colonoscopy at 120 µg/g found a screen-detected cancer 7.10% of the time and an advanced adenoma 26.47%; at 20 µg/g, 2.07% and 16.44%. In a German cohort, PPV for advanced neoplasia rose steeply up to about 9 µg/g and barely changed above 25 µg/g, while sensitivity kept falling. [[Li2022,Brenner2017]]

Early evidence

"Negative" is not all the same

People with a negative FIT of 8–10 µg/g had a 33% cumulative incidence of advanced neoplasia over 8 years, against 5% at 0 µg/g. Risk-stratified use of the haemoglobin level is being proposed. [[Grobbee2017]]

ACS 2026 on blood tests. The 2026 American Cancer Society update reaffirms starting at 45, adds the next-generation mt-sDNA and the mt-sRNA tests as preferred stool options at 3-year intervals, and recommends blood-based tests only for people who decline or do not complete the preferred tests. Modelling explains why: a blood test meeting CMS minimums would match FIT's outcomes only with 1.4–1.8 times FIT's participation, and swapping blood tests in for stool tests or colonoscopy would raise CRC deaths. The ACP advises against serum tests altogether. Early evidence [[Wolf2026,Ladabaum2024Gastro,Ladabaum2024AIM,Qaseem2023]]

What the randomised trials showed

Screening trials compare people invited to screening with people who were not. Many of those invited never attend, so the headline result (intention to screen) is diluted. A per-protocol analysis looks only at people who were actually screened, which shows the effect of the test itself, but those people may differ in other ways too. gFOBT

Minnesota, Nottingham, Funen

Minnesota (46,551 people): 13-year CRC mortality 5.88 per 1,000 with annual screening, 8.33 biennial, 8.83 in controls. At 30 years the relative risk was 0.68 (annual) and 0.78 (biennial), with no change in all-cause mortality. Nottingham (biennial, 150,251) and Funen (61,933) found 15% and 18% lower CRC mortality. [[Mandel1993,Shaukat2013,Hardcastle1996,Kronborg1996]]

Sigmoidoscopy

Four trials, one pattern

Once-only sigmoidoscopy in the UK (170,432 people): number needed to screen 191 to prevent one diagnosis and 489 to prevent one death. In PLCO, distal CRC mortality fell 50% but proximal mortality was unaffected (RR 0.97). Pooled at 15 years: 0.51 fewer cases and 0.13 fewer deaths per 100 people, with less benefit in women (0.91) than men (0.73). [[Atkin2010,Schoen2012,Juul2022]]

Controversy

NordICC: invited versus screened

84,585 people aged 55–64 were randomised; only 42.0% of those invited had the colonoscopy. Invitation cut 10-year CRC risk from 1.20% to 0.98% (RR 0.82), and 455 had to be invited to prevent one cancer, but CRC death (0.28% vs 0.31%, RR 0.90) was not significantly lower. Per protocol, as the 2024 quality indicators report it, incidence fell 31% and mortality 50%. Mortality follow-up continues. [[Bretthauer2022,Rex2024,NCT00883792]]

Head to head

Colonoscopy or FIT first?

COLONPREV (57,404 people, Spain): participation 31.8% for colonoscopy vs 39.9% for biennial FIT, and 10-year CRC mortality 0.22% vs 0.24% (RR 0.92), so FIT was non-inferior. SCREESCO (Sweden): 35.1% vs 55.5% participation. CONFIRM (50,126 US veterans) reports after 2028. [[Castells2025,Forsberg2022,Robertson2023,NCT01239082]]

Real world

Organised screening at scale

When Kaiser Permanente organised screening, up-to-date screening rose from 38.9% to 82.7% (2000–2015); CRC incidence fell 25.5% and CRC mortality 52.4% over the same period. Taiwan's FIT programme was associated with 62% lower CRC mortality among those screened. These are observational. [[Levin2018,Chiu2015]]

Uptake

The test people will do

Mailed outreach raised one-year screening to 40.7% with FIT and 24.6% with colonoscopy, against 12.1% with usual care. Replacing gFOBT with FIT in England raised uptake from 59.3% to 66.4%. [[Gupta2013,Moss2017]]

Population numbers. Figures such as "18% fewer cancers" describe large groups of people over years, not what will happen to any one person. [[Bretthauer2022]]
