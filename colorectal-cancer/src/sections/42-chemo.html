Chemotherapy after surgery

After an operation for stage III colon cancer, chemotherapy lowers the chance that the cancer comes back. The big questions are which regimen, for how long, and who benefits at all. Watch FOLFOX and CAPOX cycles fill a calendar while the nerve damage they cause adds up.

Regimen Duration

Schematic. The calendar follows the label schedules (FOLFOX4 every 14 days, up to 12 cycles; capecitabine with oxaliplatin every 21 days, up to 8 cycles). The neuropathy curves are drawn through the two published IDEA points per regimen (grade ≥2, during therapy and the month after); the shape between them is not measured. IDEA: does 3 months do as well as 6?

IDEA pooled six phase 3 trials: 12,834 people with stage III colon cancer, randomised to 3 or 6 months of FOLFOX or CAPOX. Three months would count as "non-inferior" if the hazard ratio for disease-free survival stayed below 1.12. [[Grothey2018]]

Population results from pooled trials, not a prediction for any one person. [[Grothey2018,Andre2020IDEA]]

What the labels say (quoted, not instructions)

Oxaliplatin label

FOLFOX4 schedule

Every 14 days. Day 1: oxaliplatin 85 mg/m² over 120 minutes with leucovorin 200 mg/m² over 120 minutes, then fluorouracil 400 mg/m² bolus and 600 mg/m² over 22 hours. Day 2: leucovorin and fluorouracil repeated. Adjuvant treatment: up to 12 cycles. The indication is "adjuvant treatment of stage III colon cancer in patients who have undergone complete resection of the primary tumor". [[FDA-Oxaliplatin]]

Boxed warning

Oxaliplatin hypersensitivity

The label warns of serious and fatal hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, "within minutes of administration and during any cycle". Other warnings include acute and delayed peripheral sensory neuropathy ("avoid topical application of ice"), myelosuppression, PRES, pulmonary toxicity, hepatotoxicity, QT prolongation and rhabdomyolysis. [[FDA-Oxaliplatin]]

Capecitabine label

Adjuvant colon doses

Single agent: 1,250 mg/m² twice daily on days 1–14 of each 21-day cycle, maximum 8 cycles. With oxaliplatin: 1,000 mg/m² twice daily on days 1–14 plus oxaliplatin 130 mg/m² on day 1, maximum 8 cycles. [[FDA-Xeloda]]

Boxed warning

DPD deficiency and DPYD testing

Capecitabine and fluorouracil labels warn of serious adverse reactions or death with complete DPD deficiency: Test patients for genetic variants of DPYD prior to initiating, unless immediate treatment is necessary. The capecitabine label notes no FDA-authorized DPYD test is available, that "No XELODA dose has been proven safe" with complete deficiency, and warns of bleeding with warfarin-type anticoagulants. [[FDA-Xeloda,FDA-Fluorouracil]]

Open questions

Controversy

3 or 6 months?

IDEA formally missed non-inferiority for both DFS (HR 1.07) and OS (HR 1.02); SCOT met it, and the absolute 5-year OS gap in IDEA was 0.4%. Three months of CAPOX is widely accepted for lower-risk disease; for higher-risk disease and FOLFOX, 6 months keeps a small edge. [[Grothey2018,Andre2020IDEA,Iveson2018]]

Controversy

Oxaliplatin at 70 and over

In the ACCENT pool (11,953 people), adding oxaliplatin to IV fluorouracil gave little benefit at 70 or older (DFS HR 0.94; OS HR 1.04), but the age interaction was not significant, and oral fluoropyrimidines kept their effect at all ages. [[McCleary2013]]

Early evidence

Chemotherapy guided by ctDNA

In DYNAMIC (455 stage II patients), using circulating tumour DNA to decide cut chemotherapy use from 28% to 15%, with non-inferior 2-year recurrence-free survival (93.5% vs 92.4%). Stage III trials are pending. See Toward a cure. [[Tie2022]]

Early evidence

Immunotherapy before surgery for dMMR colon

In NICHE-2 (115 people, locally advanced dMMR colon cancer), nivolumab plus ipilimumab before surgery gave a pathological response in 98% and a complete response in 68%, with no recurrences at a median 26 months. It is non-randomised. [[Chalabi2024]]

Survival and recurrence figures on this page are population statistics from trials. They describe groups, and cannot say what will happen to one person.
