Quality: adenoma detection rate and withdrawal time

The adenoma detection rate (ADR) is the share of an endoscopist's screening colonoscopies that find at least one adenoma. It is the best-studied marker of a careful examination. In Kaiser Permanente (314,872 colonoscopies), physician ADRs ranged from 7.4% to 52.5%, and each 1% rise in ADR was linked to a 3% lower risk of interval cancer (HR 0.97). 2024 targets

ACG/ASGE quality indicators

Screening ADR ≥35% overall (≥40% in men, ≥30% in women), raised from 25% in 2015; ADR ≥50% after a positive stool test; sessile serrated lesion detection ≥6%; adenomas per colonoscopy ≥0.6; caecal intubation ≥95% with photo documentation; adequate prep ≥90%; average withdrawal ≥8 minutes in normal colonoscopies; cold snare for 4–9 mm lesions ≥90%. [[Rex2024]]

Europe

ESGE 2017 minimum standards

Adequate bowel prep 90%, caecal intubation 90%, ADR 25%, appropriate polypectomy technique 80%. In Poland's programme, endoscopists with an ADR below 20% had interval-cancer hazard ratios of 10.75–12.50. [[Kaminski2017ESGE,Kaminski2010]]

Improvement works

Raising ADR lowers risk

Endoscopists who raised their ADR had fewer interval cancers (HR 0.63) and interval-cancer deaths (HR 0.50). Reaching or staying in the top quintile gave HRs of 0.27 and 0.18. [[Kaminski2017Gastro]]

Misses

No colonoscopy sees everything

In tandem studies, miss rates were 26% for adenomas, 9% for advanced adenomas and 27% for serrated polyps. Cancers within 5 years of a colonoscopy were more often CIMP-high and MSI-high, suggesting fast-growing pathways. Early evidence [[Zhao2019,Nishihara2013]]

After a positive FIT: does waiting matter?

Why it works

The National Polyp Study

After polypectomy, CRC incidence was 76–90% lower than in three reference groups. With a median 15.8 years of follow-up, there were 12 CRC deaths against 25.4 expected: a 53% reduction. [[Winawer1993,Zauber2012]]

Comfort

CO2 instead of air

Using carbon dioxide rather than air to inflate the bowel cut moderate or severe pain after the procedure from 16.7% to 4.2%. [[Bretthauer2016]]

Definitions

Post-colonoscopy cancer

The World Endoscopy Organization standardised "post-colonoscopy colorectal cancer" (PCCRC). In England the 3-year PCCRC rate fell from 10.6% for colonoscopies done in 2001 to 7.3% in 2007, depending heavily on the method used. [[Rutter2018,Morris2015]]
