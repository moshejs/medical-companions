Tumour biomarkers

Two cancers at the same stage can behave differently and respond to different drugs. A handful of tumour tests, on mismatch repair, RAS, BRAF and HER2, plus where in the bowel the cancer began, decide which medicines are likely to work.

Decision tree: what each result opens or closes

A teaching map for metastatic colorectal cancer, following the published order: MSI-H/dMMR goes to checkpoint inhibitors, BRAF V600E to encorafenib–cetuximab-based therapy, left-sided RAS wild-type to chemotherapy plus anti-EGFR, others to chemotherapy plus anti-VEGF; HER2, KRAS G12C and NTRK guide later lines. Real choices are made by a multidisciplinary team with the person. [[Morris2023,Elez2025,FDA-Tukysa,FDA-Lumakras,FDA-Vitrakvi]]

Universal tumour testing: from biopsy to a Lynch answer

ACG 2015: "All newly diagnosed colorectal cancers (CRCs) should be evaluated for mismatch repair deficiency." Testing every tumour found every Lynch carrier in a 10,206-person analysis (sensitivity 100%), where family-history criteria missed some (87.8%). Follow one tumour through the lab and choose which result it gives. [[Syngal2015,Moreira2012,Sepulveda2017]]

How common is mismatch-repair deficiency?

Different cohorts and methods; read each bar on its own. [[Hutchins2011,Boland2010,Sargent2010,Koopman2009,Venderbosch2014]]

Why the drop in metastatic disease? dMMR tumours are less likely to have spread to nodes (OR 0.33) or distant organs (OR 0.49), independent of depth, so they are common in stage II and rare in stage IV. [[Gryfe2000]]

Prognosis. In stage II–III, MSI-H tumours had better survival (meta-analysis HR 0.65), and in QUASAR recurrence was half as common (11% vs 26%). In metastatic disease the picture flips: dMMR went with worse survival (HR 1.35), apparently driven by co-existing BRAF mutation. [[Popat2005,Hutchins2011,Venderbosch2014]]

Prediction. In pooled randomised data, fluorouracil alone helped pMMR but not dMMR stage II–III colon cancer, and stage II dMMR patients given it did worse than with surgery alone (HR 2.95); QUASAR did not confirm a predictive effect. In metastatic dMMR disease, first-line pembrolizumab doubled median progression-free survival to 16.5 months vs 8.2 with chemotherapy. [[Sargent2010,Ribic2003,Hutchins2011,Andre2020]]

Sidedness

Left and right are different diseases

Right-sided cancers are more often MSI-high, BRAF-mutant and mucinous; left-sided more often chromosomally unstable. Across 66 studies (1.4 million patients), a left-sided primary carried a lower risk of death (HR 0.82). Anti-EGFR plus chemotherapy helped left-sided RAS wild-type tumours (OS HR 0.75) but not right-sided (HR 1.12); this predictive signal comes from retrospective subgroup analyses Established for prognosis. [[Missiaglia2014,Petrelli2017,Arnold2017]]

RAS

Why RAS closes the anti-EGFR door

Cetuximab and panitumumab are antibodies against the EGF receptor. In the trials that defined their use, panitumumab's benefit was confined to wild-type KRAS (PFS HR 0.45 vs 0.99), and cetuximab gave 9.5 vs 4.8 months median OS only in wild-type tumours. Mutations beyond KRAS exon 2 predicted the same lack of benefit, hence extended RAS testing. [[Amado2008,Karapetis2008,Douillard2013]]

Lynch syndrome

A test for the tumour, an answer for the family

About 3% of new colorectal cancers are caused by an inherited MMR mutation. Relatives who inherit it have about a 45% risk of colorectal cancer by age 70, which is why EGAPP recommended offering testing to everyone with newly diagnosed CRC. . [[EGAPP2009]]
