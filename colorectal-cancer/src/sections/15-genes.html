Finding Lynch syndrome

Family criteria

Amsterdam II: "3-2-1-0"

At least three relatives with a Lynch-associated cancer (colorectal, endometrium, small bowel, ureter or renal pelvis), and all of: one is a first-degree relative of the other two; two successive generations affected; at least one diagnosed before 50; FAP excluded; tumours verified where possible. [[Vasen1999,Syngal2015]]

Tumour testing criteria

Revised Bethesda

Includes colorectal cancer before 50; synchronous or metachronous Lynch-related tumours at any age; MSI-H histology before 60; colorectal cancer with one or more first-degree relatives with a Lynch-related cancer, one diagnosed before 50. [[Umar2004,Syngal2015]]

Universal tumour testing. ACG 2015: "All newly diagnosed colorectal cancers (CRCs) should be evaluated for mismatch repair deficiency" by immunohistochemistry and/or MSI; tumours with MLH1 loss should have BRAF or MLH1 promoter-methylation testing. [[Syngal2015]] The European 2021 guideline says all colorectal and endometrial cancers should be tested. [[Seppala2021]] In 10,206 probands, universal testing had 100% sensitivity against 87.8% for the Bethesda guidelines, and 28% of Lynch cases in an Ohio cohort did not meet them. [[Moreira2012,Hampel2008]] Each Lynch proband had on average three relatives who also carried the variant. [[Hampel2008]]

What helps carriers

Established

Colonoscopy: 6% vs 16%

In a controlled 15-year Finnish trial in Lynch families, colorectal cancer developed in 6% of those having colonoscopy every 3 years against 16% without (a 62% reduction). There were no colorectal cancer deaths in the screened group against 9 in controls. [[Jarvinen2000]]

Established

Aspirin: CAPP2, 9% vs 13%

861 carriers took aspirin 600 mg or placebo for about 2 years. At about 10 years, colorectal cancer occurred in 9% (40/427) vs 13% (58/434): ITT HR 0.65 (0.43–0.97); per-protocol HR 0.56. No effect on other Lynch cancers. [[Burn2020]] Europe 2021 tells carriers there is a "high probability that daily aspirin will reduce their cancer risk", minimum 75–100 mg daily; the UK advises it with the best dose uncertain; ACG 2015 (before this report) did not recommend standard use. [[Seppala2021,Monahan2020,Syngal2015]]

Early evidence

Weight in CAPP2

Obese carriers had a 2.41-fold colorectal cancer risk, seen only in the placebo arm. [[Movahedi2015]]

Gynaecological risk

Hysterectomy timing

Risk-reducing hysterectomy at 25 would prevent endometrial cancer before 50 in 15% (MLH1), 18% (MSH2), 13% (MSH6) and 0% (PMS2) of carriers; little is gained before 40. [[DominguezValentin2021]]

Polyposis syndromes

FAP · APC · dominant

Hundreds of adenomas

Germline APC variants on 5q21 cause hundreds of adenomas; untreated, cancer follows at an average 35–40. [[Galiatsatos2006]] About 1 in 8,300 births, under 1% of colorectal cancer; about 70% have a family history and 15–30% of variants arise de novo. [[Half2009,Zaffaroni2024]] St Mark's: average age at cancer 39; 87% by 45, 93% by 50. [[Syngal2015]] Risk is "virtually 100% by 60 years" without prophylactic treatment. [[Zaffaroni2024]]

FAP · care

From age 12, then surgery

Europe 2024: colonoscopy from 12 in classic FAP, by 18–20 in attenuated FAP; UK 2020: from 12–14, every 1–3 years, upper GI surveillance from 25. [[Zaffaroni2024,Monahan2020]] Prophylactic colectomy is usually advised by the late teens or early twenties; in 303 surgical cases, ages predicting cancer were 27 (profuse), 31 (sparse) and 46 (attenuated). [[Half2009,Zaffaroni2024]] Registry-found patients had 3–10% cancer at presentation against 50–70% when they presented with symptoms. [[Monahan2020]]

FAP · beyond the colon

Duodenum and desmoids

Duodenal adenomas reach 90% by 70; duodenal cancer 4.5% by 57; desmoids in about 8% of men and 13% of women; thyroid cancer 1.5–12%; also CHRPE, osteomas and hepatoblastoma. After colectomy, duodenal cancer and desmoids are the two main causes of death. [[Bulow2004,Half2009,Zaffaroni2024]]

Attenuated FAP

10 to 100 adenomas

Usually 10–100 adenomas, appearing later, with a lower cancer risk. [[Half2009]]

MAP · MUTYH · recessive

Both copies needed

Biallelic MUTYH variants cause about 7% of adenomatous polyposis and 0.7% of colorectal cancer; 77.4% have fewer than 100 adenomas; lifetime CRC risk about 50.5%, mean age 47.85. [[Zaffaroni2024]] ACG: 19% by 50 and 43% by 60. [[Syngal2015]] One in 45 people carries a MUTYH variant, mostly one copy; single-copy carriers have 7.2% (men) and 5.6% (women) risk to 70 against 2.9% and 2.1% in the general population, yet UK guidance does not advise extra colonoscopy for them. [[Win2017,Win2014,Monahan2020]]

Serrated polyposis

Most common, often missed

WHO 2019: at least 5 serrated lesions proximal to the rectum, all ≥5 mm with 2 or more ≥10 mm; or more than 20 serrated lesions anywhere with at least 5 proximal, counted across colonoscopies. [[Monahan2020]] No common germline cause is known; first-degree relatives should have colonoscopic screening. [[Rex2026SPS]]

Hamartomatous

Peutz–Jeghers and juvenile polyposis

Peutz–Jeghers: autosomal dominant, usually STK11, with mucocutaneous pigmentation; 1 in 50,000–200,000 births; lifetime colorectal cancer risk 39%. [[Beggs2010,Syngal2015]] Juvenile polyposis: dominant, five or more juvenile polyps; CRC RR 34.0, lifetime risk 38.7%, mean age 43.9. [[Brosens2007,Syngal2015]]

Where the evidence disagrees

Controversy

How often to look?

ACG says every 1–2 years, Europe every 2–3 years with consensus split, the UK every 2 years; PMS2 may be 5-yearly in Europe. Colorectal cancer still occurs in about half of MLH1/MSH2 carriers by 75 under surveillance, which challenges the idea that adenomas are always the precursor. [[Syngal2015,Seppala2021,Monahan2020,DominguezValentin2020]]

Controversy

Penetrance depends on how carriers were found

Clinic-based PLSD cohorts and family-based studies differ, especially for MSH6 and PMS2: for example PMS2 to 70, 3.4% in PLSD vs 19% in men and 11% in women in PMS2 families; MSH6 to 70, 12% in French families. [[DominguezValentin2020,tenBroeke2015,Bonadona2011]]

Controversy

Aspirin dose

European and UK guidelines endorse aspirin; ACG 2015 did not. The best dose is unknown; the CAPP3 dose trial had no results in PubMed at the time of this pass. [[Seppala2021,Monahan2020,Syngal2015]]
