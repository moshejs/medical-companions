Scale

How to read it. Squares are pooled relative risks (RR) or hazard ratios from meta-analyses, cohorts or trials; lines are 95% confidence intervals; the log scale makes halving and doubling equal distances. Each row comes from a different study, with its own comparison (for example "per 50 g a day"), so rows are not directly comparable. Absolute mode multiplies one baseline, a 3.42% lifetime risk from age 50 without screening in a US microsimulation, by each RR: a rough illustration for one factor at a time, not a personal prediction. Colitis: a risk that is falling

Ulcerative colitis

HR 1.66, and dropping

In 96,447 people with ulcerative colitis in Denmark and Sweden (1969–2017), colorectal cancer occurred at 1.29 vs 0.82 per 1,000 person-years: HR 1.66 (1.57–1.76). In 2013–2017 the HR was 1.38, about 1 extra case per 1,058 patients over 5 years. Even after adjusting for stage, the risk of dying of the cancer was higher (HR 1.54). [[Olen2020UC]]

Crohn's disease

HR 1.40, concentrated in some groups

In 47,035 people with Crohn's disease, incident colorectal cancer was 0.82 vs 0.64 per 1,000 person-years (adjusted HR 1.40; 1.27–1.53). The excess was confined to Crohn's diagnosed before 40, colon-only disease, or primary sclerosing cholangitis. [[Olen2020CD]]

Older estimate

2%, 8%, 18%

A 2001 meta-analysis of 116 studies put the cumulative risk in ulcerative colitis at 2% after 10 years, 8% after 20 and 18% after 30 years of colitis. Newer population data show a much smaller excess. [[Eaden2001,Olen2020UC]]

How much could be prevented?

Established

Second only to lung cancer

In US adults aged 30 and older in 2019, 40.0% of cancers were attributable to modifiable risk factors. For colorectal cancer that meant 78,440 attributable cases and 25,800 attributable deaths, the largest number of attributable deaths after lung cancer. [[Islami2024]] In the 2014 analysis it had the second-highest number of attributable cases (76,910) and deaths (28,290). [[Islami2018]]

Established

Meat: Group 1 and Group 2A

The IARC Monograph 114 classified processed meat as carcinogenic to humans (Group 1) and red meat as probably carcinogenic (Group 2A). [[Bouvard2015]] In prospective studies, processed meat raised risk by about 18% per 50 g a day and fresh red meat by about 17% per 100 g a day. [[Chan2011]] The WCRF/AICR update found 12% per 100 g a day of red and processed meat. [[Vieira2017]]

Established

Dose matters

Alcohol: RR 1.07 at 10 g a day, 1.38 at 50 g and 1.82 at 100 g of ethanol. [[Fedirko2011]] Smoking: significant only after 30 years, stronger for rectal cancer; RR 1.51 for 60 more pack-years. [[Botteri2008,Liang2009]] Body fat: RR 1.06 per 5 kg/m² of BMI, stronger in men. [[Abar2018]]

Early evidence

Diabetes plus family history

In Sweden, diabetes diagnosed before 50 was linked to a 1.9-fold risk of colorectal cancer before 50; combined with a family history, 6.9-fold (4.1–12). [[AliKhan2020]]

The US Preventive Services Task Force gave a B recommendation in 2016 for low-dose aspirin to prevent cardiovascular disease and colorectal cancer in adults 50–59 with a 10-year cardiovascular risk of 10% or more, low bleeding risk and willingness to take it for 10 years or more. Where the evidence disagrees

Controversy

How big is the family-history risk?

Older meta-analyses give about 2.2 for one first-degree relative. Pooling only cohorts gives 1.37 (CI 0.76–2.46), and after 55 the PLCO trial found HR 1.23. Recall bias in case-control studies may inflate older figures. Guidelines differ as a result. [[Johns2001,Butterworth2006,Roos2019,Schoen2015]]

Controversy

Aspirin depends on age

Long-term trial follow-up shows benefit after 5 or more years, mostly in the proximal colon (incidence HR 0.35). In people 70 and older, ASPREE showed no benefit and more advanced cancer. [[Rothwell2010,McNeil2021]]

Controversy

Calcium and vitamin D

Observational data favour calcium (RR 0.92 per 300 mg a day), but randomised trials of vitamin D and calcium were null, apart from one modestly positive calcium trial. [[Keum2014,Baron2015,Manson2019,Baron1999]]
