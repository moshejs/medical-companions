People behind the milestones

1908

W. Ernest Miles

Described abdominoperineal excision for cancer of the rectum and the end of the pelvic colon in The Lancet. A historical review dates his first radical operation to 1907; the operation became a gold standard for many years. [[Miles1908,Lirici2016]]

1895–1913

Pauline Gross and Aldred Scott Warthin

Warthin's interest began in 1895 with the family history of Pauline Gross, a seamstress. His "Family G" (1913) is now recognised as the first reported Lynch syndrome family. By 2005 it had 929 known descendants, with colorectal (SIR 3.20) and endometrial cancer (SIR 3.51) predominating and an MSH2 splice-site mutation confirmed. [[Warthin1913,Campos2024,Douglas2005]]

1932

Cuthbert Dukes

Published the classification of cancer of the rectum behind "Dukes stage", still used in later surgical series. With Lockhart-Mummery he reported familial adenomatosis of the colon and rectum in 1939. [[Dukes1932,Heald1986,LockhartMummery1939]]

1966

Henry Lynch

With colleagues described two large Midwestern kindreds with hereditary cancer. The condition first called "cancer family syndrome" was later renamed Lynch syndrome in recognition of his work. [[Lynch1966,Campos2024]]

1975

Muto, Bussey and Morson

Muto, Bussey and Morson presented evidence that most colorectal cancers evolve through a polyp-to-cancer sequence. Malignant potential rises with adenoma size, villous histology and atypia, but most adenomas never become cancer. [[Muto1975]]

1973

W. I. Wolff and H. Shinya

Reported polypectomy through the fibreoptic colonoscope, removing growths beyond the reach of the sigmoidoscope. [[Wolff1973NEJM,Wolff1973AnnSurg]]

1982

R. J. Heald

Found tumour deposits in the mesorectum several centimetres beyond the visible tumour and proposed removing it whole: total mesorectal excision. His 1986 series reported 5-year survival of 87% and permanent colostomy in 11%. [[Heald1982,Heald1986]]

1988–1990

B. Vogelstein and E. R. Fearon

Showed genetic changes accumulating as tumours progress (ras mutations in 9% of adenomas under 1 cm vs 58% over 1 cm; chromosome 18 loss in 73% of carcinomas), then proposed the multistep genetic model in which the accumulation of changes matters more than their order. [[Vogelstein1988,Fearon1990]]

2022

A. Cercek and colleagues

Dostarlimab alone in 12 patients with mismatch-repair-deficient locally advanced rectal cancer gave a 100% clinical complete response, with no chemoradiotherapy or surgery needed during 6–25 months of follow-up Early evidence. [[Cercek2022]]
