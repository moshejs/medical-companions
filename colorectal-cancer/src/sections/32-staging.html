Staging: TNM calculator

Stage is three questions: how deep the tumour has grown through the bowel wall (T), whether it has reached the lymph nodes (N), and whether it has spread to distant organs (M). Grow a tumour letter by letter, then combine the letters into an AJCC stage.

Survival by stage: population statistics

The US figures below use SEER summary stage, a simpler system than AJCC: localized means the cancer is found only in the bowel, regional that it has reached nearby tissue or lymph nodes, distant that it has spread far. The two systems do not map one-to-one, so the calculator does not convert between them. Relative survival compares people with the cancer to people of the same age without it. These are averages for large groups diagnosed in 2015–2021; they cannot tell any one person what will happen. [[Siegel2026]]

The survival paradox

Why can stage III do better than stage II?

Stage numbers are not a strict ranking. In SEER under the 6th edition, stage IIIA colon cancer had 83.4% 5-year survival against 72.2% for IIB (P<0.001). Under the 8th edition the gap persists: after adjustment, IIIA carried a lower risk of cancer-specific death than IIB (HR 0.374). Two explanations are proposed: a deep T4 tumour may matter more than one to three positive nodes, and stage III patients receive chemotherapy while stage II patients generally did not. [[OConnell2004,Yu2024,Li2020]]

Trends

Distant-stage survival has tripled

US 5-year relative survival for distant-stage colorectal cancer rose from 5% (1975–1977) to 8% (1995–1997) to 15% (2015–2021); regional stage rose from 52% to 74%. Distant-stage rectal cancer went from 8% in the mid-1990s to 18%, one of the largest gains of any cancer. [[Siegel2026,Siegel2026CRC]]

Age

Distant stage by age

Distant-stage 5-year relative survival is 23% under 50, 17% at 50–64 and 10% at 65 or older. Distant metastases are present at diagnosis in 27% of people under 50 against 21–23% of older patients. [[Siegel2026CRC]]

Twelve nodes and high-risk stage II

Why at least 12 nodes? N can only be as accurate as the nodes the pathologist examines. In a systematic review of 17 studies (61,371 patients), 16 found that survival in stage II colon cancer rose with the number of nodes evaluated. In the INT-0089 trial (3,411 patients) survival improved with more nodes analysed even in node-negative patients, once positive nodes were accounted for. ASCO 2022 lists fewer than 12 sampled nodes as a high-risk factor in stage II. [[Chang2007,LeVoyer2003,Baxter2022]]

The animation draws 12 nodes for this reason: a cancer called N0 from 5 nodes is less certain than one called N0 from 12.

CEA: where to set the alarm

Carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) is a blood marker. It is "of little use in detecting early colorectal cancer", but a high level before surgery goes with a worse prognosis, and ASCO 2006 recommends ordering it before surgery if it would help staging and planning, then every 3 months for at least 3 years in stage II–III disease when the person could have treatment for a recurrence. Choose a threshold to see the trade-off from the Cochrane review. [[Duffy2001,Locker2006,Nicholson2015]]

The diagnostic workup, step by step

  1. Colonoscopy and biopsy

    The whole bowel is examined and a small piece of the tumour is taken to confirm cancer. About 1 in 25 people with colorectal cancer (3.9%) has a second, synchronous cancer, and in 34% of these it lies in a different surgical segment, so the whole colon should be examined, preferably before surgery. [[Mulder2011]]

  2. CT for spread and depth

    A CT scan of the chest, abdomen and pelvis looks for spread. For local staging of colon cancer, CT had pooled sensitivity 90% and specificity 69% for growth beyond the wall (T3–T4), but only 71% and 67% for nodes. [[Nerad2016,MERCURY2006]]

  3. Pelvic MRI for rectal cancer

    MERCURY (408 patients) showed high-resolution MRI predicts a clear circumferential resection margin (CRM) with 92% specificity. An MRI-involved CRM (tumour ≤1 mm from the mesorectal fascia) went with 5-year overall survival of 42.2% vs 62.2% (HR 1.97) and a 3.5-fold local-recurrence hazard. MRI is accurate for CRM and T but poor for nodes (specificity 71%). [[MERCURY2006,Taylor2014,AlSukhni2012]]

  4. CEA before surgery

    A baseline CEA helps planning and prognosis and gives a reference for later surveillance. The College of American Pathologists placed a raised preoperative CEA among proven prognostic factors. [[Locker2006,Compton2000]]

  5. Pathology after surgery

    The resected bowel gives the final pT and pN: depth, at least 12 nodes, tumour deposits, margins, grade, vessel and perineural invasion and budding. [[Baxter2022,Compton2000]]

  6. Tumour biomarkers

    Every new colorectal cancer is tested for mismatch repair; metastatic disease adds RAS, BRAF and HER2. . [[Sepulveda2017]]
