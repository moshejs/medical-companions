Not medical adviceThe Colorectal Cancer Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and patient understanding. It does not replace a doctor, nurse or screening programme. Doses, criteria, survival figures and guideline statements are reported as published, not as instructions or a prognosis for any individual. Anyone with symptoms should see a clinician, whatever their screening history.

Glossary

A searchable glossary of colorectal cancer terms, from adenoma detection rate and β-catenin to TME, N1c, dMMR and LARS. Type to filter; the arrow on each entry jumps to the module where the term matters most.

172 of 172 terms

Abdominoperineal resection (APR)Removal of the rectum and anus through the abdomen and perineum, leaving a permanent colostomy; first described by Miles in 1908 368.
Ablation (thermal)Destroying a tumour with heat. For liver metastases up to 3 cm it was non-inferior to resection for survival in COLLISION, with fewer adverse events 554.
AdenomaA benign glandular polyp, the usual precursor of colorectal cancer; most adenomas never become cancer 386.
Adenoma–carcinoma sequenceThe stepwise progression from normal mucosa to adenoma to carcinoma as mutations accumulate 181,386.
Adenoma detection rate (ADR)The share of screening colonoscopies in which at least one adenoma is found; the 2024 minimum is 35% 452.
Adjuvant therapyTreatment given after surgery to lower the risk of recurrence, such as FOLFOX or CAPOX in stage III colon cancer 12.
Advanced adenomaAn adenoma with higher-risk features (in surveillance guidance: ≥10 mm, villous histology or high-grade dysplasia 210); it turns into cancer at about 2.6–5.6% a year 72.
Amsterdam II criteriaA family-history rule for suspected Lynch syndrome: 3 relatives with a Lynch-associated cancer, 2 generations, 1 diagnosed before 50, FAP excluded 521,559.
Anastomotic leakLeakage from the join between two bowel ends; after low anterior resection a defunctioning stoma lowered symptomatic leak from 28.0% to 10.3% 353.
Anterior resectionRemoval of the upper (high) or whole (low) rectum with a join to the colon, preserving the anus 264.
Anti-EGFR antibodyCetuximab or panitumumab; effective only in RAS wild-type tumours, mainly left-sided ones 21,485.
APCThe gatekeeper tumour-suppressor gene on 5q21, mutated early in most colorectal tumours and in the germline in FAP 191,438.
Aspirin (chemoprevention)Long-term aspirin lowered colon cancer incidence after years of use 461 and colorectal cancer in Lynch carriers 84, but not in people over 70 356.
Attenuated FAPA milder form of FAP with usually 10–100 adenomas, later onset and lower cancer risk 215.
Autosomal dominantInheritance in which one variant copy is enough; each child of a carrier has a 50% chance of inheriting it (Lynch, FAP) 521.
Autosomal recessiveInheritance needing two variant copies; MUTYH-associated polyposis follows this pattern 598.
Bethesda guidelines (revised)Criteria for testing tumours for MSI, such as colorectal cancer before 50; they missed 28% of Lynch cases in one cohort 222,545.
BevacizumabAn anti-VEGF antibody; added to IFL it raised median survival from 15.6 to 20.3 months 246. Not indicated for adjuvant treatment 153.
Big Bang modelThe proposal that a colorectal tumour grows mainly as one expansion, with most subclonal changes arising early Early evidence 507.
Boston Bowel Preparation ScaleA 0–9 score (0–3 for each of 3 colon segments); adequate prep is ≥2 in each segment 303,452.
BRAF V600EAn activating BRAF mutation in about 8% of advanced CRC, linked to right-sided, MSI tumours and poor prognosis; targeted with encorafenib plus cetuximab 291,485.
β-cateninThe Wnt-pathway protein that, when APC is lost, accumulates and binds Tcf-4 to switch on growth genes 294.
CADe (computer-aided detection)Software that flags polyps during colonoscopy; it raised ADR from 36.7% to 44.7%, but a mortality benefit is not shown Early evidence 504.
Caecal intubationReaching the caecum, the start of the colon, documented by photo; the 2024 target is ≥95% 452.
CaecumThe first part of the large intestine, where the terminal ileum enters 416.
CapecitabineAn oral fluoropyrimidine; at least equivalent to bolus FU/LV as adjuvant therapy, with hand-foot syndrome as its hallmark toxicity 179,543.
CAPOXCapecitabine plus oxaliplatin; in IDEA, 3 months was non-inferior to 6 months 202.
CEA (carcinoembryonic antigen)A blood tumour marker used before surgery and for surveillance, not for early detection 141,333.
CetuximabAn anti-EGFR antibody, "not indicated" for Ras-mutant colorectal cancer or when Ras results are unknown 160.
Chromosomal instability (CIN)Widespread gain and loss of chromosomes; the commonest route to colorectal cancer 433.
CIMPCpG island methylator phenotype: widespread promoter methylation, including nearly all BRAF-mutant tumours 540,576.
Circulating tumour DNA (ctDNA)Tumour DNA in the blood. After surgery a positive result signals a high relapse risk 390,449.
Circumferential resection margin (CRM)The cut outer surface of a rectal cancer specimen; an involved margin predicts local recurrence 388,445.
Clinical complete response (cCR)No detectable tumour on examination, endoscopy and imaging after treatment; the gateway to watch-and-wait 212.
ColibactinA genotoxin made by pks+ E. coli that leaves a mutational signature (SBS88/ID18) in colorectal genomes 435.
ColonoscopyEndoscopic examination of the whole colon that can remove polyps in the same visit 599.
ColostomyA stoma made from the colon; end colostomy carries the highest parastomal hernia risk 20.
Complete mesocolic excision (CME)Colon cancer surgery in the mesocolic plane with central vascular ligation, the colon's analogue of TME 238.
Consensus molecular subtypes (CMS)Four expression-based subtypes: CMS1 MSI immune, CMS2 canonical, CMS3 metabolic, CMS4 mesenchymal 206.
Crohn's diseaseInflammatory bowel disease; colorectal cancer HR 1.40, with excess risk confined to early-onset, colonic or PSC-associated disease 414.
CryptA tube-shaped pit of the colonic lining with stem cells at its base; each holds 5–10 active stem cells 403.
Crypt fissionDivision of one crypt into two; normal crypts divide about every 30–40 years, adenomas far faster 37.
CT colonographyCT imaging of the air-distended colon; sensitivity 0.90 for lesions ≥10 mm in ACRIN 268. Screening interval 5 years 546.
Cytoreductive surgeryRemoving all visible peritoneal tumour; adding HIPEC gave no survival benefit in PRODIGE 7 443.
Deep crypt secretory cellReg4+ cells that replace Paneth cells as the colonic stem-cell niche 471.
Defunctioning stomaA temporary stoma that diverts stool from a new low join; 19% were never reversed in the Dutch TME trial 126.
Desmoid tumourA locally aggressive fibrous tumour; with duodenal cancer, a leading cause of death in FAP after colectomy 215.
Disease-free survival (DFS)Time alive without recurrence; the main end point of adjuvant trials such as MOSAIC 12.
dMMR / pMMRMismatch-repair deficient or proficient, usually judged by immunohistochemistry for MLH1, MSH2, MSH6 and PMS2 342.
DostarlimabA PD-1 inhibitor that produced clinical complete responses in all early dMMR rectal cancer patients who completed treatment Early evidence 91,92.
DPD / DPYDDihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase, which breaks down about 80% of a 5-FU dose, and its gene; variants raise toxicity 11.
Dukes classificationCuthbert Dukes's 1932 rectal cancer staging: A within the wall, B through the wall, C lymph nodes 142.
DuloxetineAn SNRI, the only agent with appropriate evidence for painful chemotherapy-induced neuropathy 338,501.
Early-onset colorectal cancerColorectal cancer diagnosed before 50; incidence has risen 1–2% a year since the mid-1990s in the US 496.
Emergency presentationDiagnosis after an emergency admission; 26% of English cases, with 1-year survival of 50% 148.
EncorafenibA BRAF inhibitor used with cetuximab, and first-line with chemotherapy, for BRAF V600E metastatic CRC 146,291.
EPCAMA gene next to MSH2; deletions of its 3′ end silence MSH2 and cause Lynch syndrome 328.
ERASEnhanced recovery after surgery: a bundle including carbohydrate drinks, early feeding and mobilisation; it cut morbidity (RR 0.60) and stay by 2.28 days 197.
Extended RAS testingTesting KRAS and NRAS exons 2, 3 and 4 before anti-EGFR therapy 485.
Extramural vascular invasion (EMVI)Tumour in veins beyond the bowel wall; MRI-EMVI predicted poorer 3-year relapse-free survival (35% vs 74%) 500.
Faecal immunochemical test (FIT)A stool test using antibodies to human haemoglobin; pooled sensitivity 0.79 and specificity 0.94 for cancer 312.
Familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP)Autosomal dominant APC syndrome with hundreds of adenomas and near-certain cancer without colectomy 191,598.
Family GWarthin's family, followed from 1895, now recognised as the first reported Lynch syndrome family 86,574.
Fear of recurrenceWorry that cancer will return; the most prominent support need at every stage 297.
fHb thresholdThe faecal haemoglobin cut-off above which FIT is called positive, in µg Hb/g; England's screening programme uses 120 323.
Field effectA field of cellular and molecular change that predisposes a whole territory of tissue to neoplasia 332.
Financial toxicityFinancial harm from cancer care; common in colorectal cancer across 65 studies 486.
First-degree relative (FDR)A parent, sibling or child; one affected FDR roughly doubles colorectal cancer risk 85.
Flexible sigmoidoscopyEndoscopy of the distal colon; it prevents distal but not proximal cancer deaths 477.
Fluorouracil (5-FU)The fluoropyrimidine backbone of colorectal chemotherapy, reported in 1957 232; its label carries a DPYD boxed warning 161.
FOLFOXInfusional fluorouracil, leucovorin and oxaliplatin; FOLFOX4 improved DFS in MOSAIC 12.
FOLFOXIRIA triplet of fluorouracil, leucovorin, oxaliplatin and irinotecan; with bevacizumab it raised PFS in TRIBE at the cost of more toxicity 339.
FruquintinibA VEGFR inhibitor for refractory metastatic CRC; FRESCO-2 OS 7.4 vs 4.8 months 120.
Fusobacterium nucleatumAn oral bacterium enriched in colorectal tumours; high load linked to worse survival Early evidence 88,295,369.
Gatekeeper geneA gene whose mutation starts the clonal expansion; APC in colorectal cancer 568.
gFOBTGuaiac faecal occult blood test; annual testing cut colorectal cancer mortality by 33% in the Minnesota trial 346.
Goblet cellA mucin-producing cell of the colonic crypt 34.
HaematocheziaVisible red blood from the rectum; the commonest presenting feature under 50 (45%) 125.
Haggitt levelsLevels of invasion in a pedunculated malignant polyp, from 0 (mucosa) to 4 (submucosa below the stalk) 214.
Hallmarks of cancerThe capabilities cancers acquire, such as sustained proliferative signalling, invasion and immune evasion 223,224.
Hand-foot syndromePalmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia: red, painful palms and soles, in 60% on adjuvant capecitabine 179.
HER2 amplificationFound in about 5% of KRAS exon 2 wild-type metastatic CRC; targeted with trastuzumab-based combinations 470,515.
High-output stomaSmall-bowel stoma output above about 1.5–2.0 L a day, causing water, sodium and magnesium loss 404.
HIPECHyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy; adding it to cytoreductive surgery did not improve survival 443.
Hyperplastic polypThe commonest serrated polyp, mostly harmless when small and distal 116,210.
IleostomyA stoma made from the small bowel; dehydration is the leading reason for readmission 363,566.
Interval cancerA cancer found between scheduled screens or after a negative colonoscopy; lower ADR means more interval cancers 276.
Iron-deficiency anaemiaLow haemoglobin from iron loss; Hb under 9.0 g/dL had a likelihood ratio for colorectal cancer of 27 in men 219.
Juvenile polyposisAn autosomal dominant syndrome with five or more juvenile polyps; lifetime colorectal cancer risk about 38.7% 79,521.
KRASAn oncogene mutated as adenomas grow; RAS-mutant tumours do not respond to anti-EGFR therapy 485,567.
KRAS G12CA KRAS mutation in about 3–4% of metastatic CRC, targeted by sotorasib or adagrasib with an anti-EGFR antibody 152,592.
Laparoscopic surgeryKeyhole surgery; oncologically equivalent to open colectomy in COST 111.
LARS (low anterior resection syndrome)Urgency, frequency, clustering and incontinence after rectal resection; major LARS affects about 41% 117.
LARS scoreA 5-item score from 0 to 42: 0–20 no LARS, 21–29 minor, 30–42 major 149.
Lgr5A Wnt target gene marking the stem cells at the crypt base 42.
Lymph-node yieldThe number of nodes the pathologist examines; at least 12 is the standard 48,97.
Lynch syndromeAutosomal dominant mismatch-repair deficiency (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2, EPCAM) causing colorectal, endometrial and other cancers 521.
M1cAJCC 8th-edition category for peritoneal metastasis, giving stage IVC 538.
Malignant polypA polyp containing invasive cancer; Haggitt level 4 invasion is an adverse factor 214.
MERCURYA study of high-resolution pelvic MRI before TME that predicted a clear circumferential margin with 92% specificity 361.
MesorectumThe fatty envelope around the rectum holding its lymph nodes, removed intact in TME 230.
Metachronous metastasisSpread found after the primary diagnosis; 5-year metachronous liver metastasis risk was 30.4% after stage III 349.
MicrosatelliteA short repeated DNA sequence, such as (A)n or (CA)n, prone to polymerase slippage 565.
Microsatellite instability (MSI)Length changes in microsatellites caused by mismatch-repair loss; about 15% of colorectal cancers 63.
Mismatch repair (MMR)The DNA repair system correcting base mismatches and insertion-deletion loops after replication 266,320.
MLH1Mismatch-repair gene; silenced by promoter methylation in most sporadic MSI cancers 236, and a high-risk Lynch gene 133.
Molecular residual disease (MRD)Cancer detectable only by ctDNA after curative treatment; not yet routine in early cancer per ESMO 422.
MSH2Mismatch-repair gene, the first Lynch gene identified, in 1993 185,310.
MSH6Lynch gene with moderate colorectal but high endometrial cancer risk 133.
Mucinous adenocarcinomaA cancer with abundant extracellular mucin, 11% of colorectal cancers; not independently prognostic after stage adjustment 407.
Multitarget stool DNA (mt-sDNA)A stool test combining DNA markers with FIT; cancer sensitivity 92.3% vs 73.8% for FIT 251.
MutLαThe MLH1–PMS2 heterodimer that recruits excision machinery in mismatch repair 565.
MutSαThe MSH2–MSH6 heterodimer that recognises single-base mismatches 565.
MUTYH-associated polyposis (MAP)Autosomal recessive polyposis from biallelic MUTYH variants, often with fewer than 100 adenomas 598.
N1cTumour deposits with no positive regional lymph nodes 10,538.
Neoadjuvant therapyTreatment given before surgery, such as chemoradiotherapy for rectal cancer 473 or chemotherapy for colon cancer in FOxTROT 382.
NeoantigenA mutant peptide the immune system can recognise; dMMR tumours carry many, making them sensitive to PD-1 blockade 309.
Nivolumab plus ipilimumabPD-1 plus CTLA-4 blockade for MSI-H/dMMR metastatic CRC; 24-month PFS 72% vs 14% with chemotherapy first-line 18.
NordICCA randomised trial of an invitation to one screening colonoscopy; incidence RR 0.82 at 10 years 77.
NTRK fusionA rare gene fusion (0.2–2.4% with ALK and ROS1) targeted by tumour-agnostic TRK inhibitors 138,431.
Number needed to screenHow many people must be screened to prevent one outcome; 489 to prevent one death with once-only sigmoidoscopy 27.
OligometastasesA limited number of metastases that may be treated locally, for example with stereotactic radiotherapy 381.
Organ preservationAvoiding TME after a complete response; 3-year TME-free survival 53% with consolidation chemotherapy in OPRA 193.
OxaliplatinA platinum drug whose dose-limiting toxicity is cumulative sensory neuropathy; acute symptoms are triggered by cold 171.
PanitumumabAn anti-EGFR antibody indicated for RAS wild-type metastatic CRC; boxed warning for skin toxicity 177.
Parastomal herniaA hernia next to a stoma, commonest after end colostomy 20,87.
PembrolizumabA PD-1 inhibitor; first-line in MSI-H/dMMR metastatic CRC after KEYNOTE-177 16.
Perineural invasionTumour growing along nerves; 5-year DFS 16% vs 65% without it in one series 327.
Peutz–Jeghers syndromeAutosomal dominant STK11 syndrome with hamartomatous polyps and mucocutaneous pigmentation 49.
PMS2The Lynch gene with the lowest colorectal cancer risk (10.4% to 75) 133.
PolypectomyRemoval of a polyp at colonoscopy, first reported in 1973 588.
Positive predictive value (PPV)The share of positive results that are true positives; rectal bleeding in primary care has a PPV of about 2.4% 218.
Post-colonoscopy colorectal cancer (PCCRC)A cancer diagnosed after a colonoscopy that found none, standardised by the WEO 465.
Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)A bile-duct disease; in Crohn's disease, PSC is one of the settings where colorectal cancer risk is raised 414.
Processed meatMeat preserved by curing or smoking; IARC Group 1 70; risk rises about 18% per 50 g/day 96.
Proximal (right-sided) colonCaecum, ascending colon, hepatic flexure and transverse colon; tumours here are more often MSI and BRAF-mutant 370,526.
R0 resectionComplete removal with no tumour at the margins; 94% vs 89% with neoadjuvant chemotherapy in FOxTROT 382.
RectumThe most distal part of the large intestine, between the sigmoid colon and the anal canal 571.
RegorafenibA multikinase inhibitor for refractory CRC; boxed warning for severe hepatotoxicity 174,201.
Relative survivalSurvival of people with cancer compared with similar people without it; 5-year relative survival is 65% overall in the US 496.
Robotic surgeryRobot-assisted rectal surgery; ROLARR found no significant fall in conversion 264.
Sensitivity / specificityThe share of people with disease who test positive, and without disease who test negative; FIT is about 0.79 and 0.94 for cancer 312.
Serrated pathwayA route to cancer through sessile serrated lesions, with BRAF mutation and CIMP; at least 15% of colorectal cancers 116,249.
Serrated polyposis syndromeMany serrated polyps (WHO 2019 criteria); the commonest polyposis syndrome, often unrecognised 375,453.
Sessile serrated lesion (SSL)A flat, mostly proximal serrated precursor, typically BRAF-mutant and CIMP-positive 58,116.
Short-course radiotherapy5 × 5 Gy before rectal surgery 557.
Shield (cfDNA test)A blood test for cell-free DNA; 83.1% sensitivity for cancer, 13.2% for advanced precancerous lesions 102,172.
SidednessWhether the primary is right- or left-sided; left-sided tumours have lower risk of death (HR 0.82) 429.
Sigmoid colonThe S-shaped hindgut segment before the rectum 226.
Signet-ring cell carcinomaA rare (under 1%) subtype with intracellular mucin; independently worse prognosis (HR 2.5) 407.
Split-dose preparationBowel prep divided between the evening before and the morning of colonoscopy; better cleansing (OR 2.51) 351.
StomaAn opening of the bowel on the abdomen, into which stool drains into a pouch 126.
Stoma site markingChoosing the best stoma position before surgery, ideally by a colorectal surgeon or certified ostomy nurse 467.
Surveillance colonoscopyRepeat colonoscopy after polyp removal at intervals set by the findings 210.
Survival paradoxStage IIIA doing better than stage IIB 413,596.
Synchronous cancerA second primary colorectal cancer present at diagnosis, in 3.9% 385.
T4a / T4bT4a penetrates the visceral peritoneum; T4b invades or adheres to other organs 10.
TisCarcinoma in situ or intramucosal carcinoma, limited to the epithelium or lamina propria 10,538.
TNMThe staging system for depth (T), nodes (N) and metastasis (M); the AJCC 8th edition covers colon and rectum together 10.
Total mesorectal excision (TME)Sharp dissection removing the rectum with its intact mesorectum; proposed by Heald in 1982 230.
Total neoadjuvant therapy (TNT)Giving radiotherapy and all systemic chemotherapy before rectal surgery, as in RAPIDO and PRODIGE 23 33,108.
TP53A tumour-suppressor gene on 17p whose loss marks the step from adenoma to carcinoma 567.
Traditional serrated adenoma (TSA)A serrated cancer precursor; surveillance at 3 years after removal 116,210.
Trifluridine/tipiracilAn oral drug for refractory CRC; with bevacizumab, median OS 10.8 vs 7.5 months 439.
Tumour buddingSingle cells or clusters of up to 4 at the invasive front, graded BD1–BD3 343.
Tumour depositA discrete tumour nodule in pericolic fat without node structure; found in 22% of patients and linked to poor outcome 389.
Ulcerative colitisInflammatory bowel disease with raised colorectal cancer risk (HR 1.66), falling over time 415.
Universal tumour testingMMR or MSI testing of every new colorectal cancer to find Lynch syndrome 484,521.
USPSTFUS Preventive Services Task Force; its 2021 recommendation grades screening A at 50–75 and B at 45–49 546.
Watch-and-waitClose surveillance instead of surgery after a clinical complete response of rectal cancer 212,556.
Withdrawal timeTime spent inspecting while withdrawing the colonoscope; the target average is ≥8 minutes 452.
Wnt signallingA pathway driving adult stem cells; deregulated in 93% of colorectal cancers 412,525.
