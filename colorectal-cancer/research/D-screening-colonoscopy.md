## Findings

### screening · Burden and the case for screening
- Colorectal cancer made up 9.6% of new cancers and 9.3% of cancer deaths worldwide in 2022. It ranked third for incidence and second for deaths [[Bray2024]].
- In the US in 2023 about 153,020 people were expected to be diagnosed with CRC and 52,550 to die of it. This included 19,550 cases and 3,750 deaths in people under 50 [[Siegel2023CRC]].
- The share of US CRC cases in people under 55 rose from 11% in 1995 to 20% in 2019. 60% of new cases were advanced-stage in 2019, against 52% in the mid-2000s [[Siegel2023CRC]].
- The USPSTF estimated that 10.5% of new CRC cases occur under age 50. CRC incidence at ages 40–49 rose by almost 15% from 2000–2002 to 2014–2016 [[USPSTF2021]].
- In 2016, 26% of eligible US adults had never been screened for CRC. In 2018, 31% were not up to date [[USPSTF2021]].
- Disease is usually symptomatic only at an advanced stage, which is the rationale for organised screening programmes (Established) [[Dekker2019]].

### screening · Guidelines as published (average risk)
- USPSTF 2021 grades: screen all adults aged 50–75 (A); screen adults aged 45–49 (B); selectively offer screening at 76–85 (C), weighing overall health, prior screening and preferences [[USPSTF2021]].
- The USPSTF has high certainty of substantial net benefit at 50–75 and moderate certainty of moderate net benefit at 45–49. At 76–85 it has moderate certainty of small net benefit in previously screened people. People never screened are more likely to benefit [[USPSTF2021]].
- USPSTF 2021 lists these strategies:
  - high-sensitivity gFOBT or FIT every year
  - stool DNA-FIT every 1–3 years
  - CT colonography every 5 years
  - flexible sigmoidoscopy every 5 years
  - flexible sigmoidoscopy every 10 years plus annual FIT
  - colonoscopy every 10 years [[USPSTF2021]]
- CISNET modelling for the USPSTF:
  - Screening strategies gave 171–381 life-years gained per 1,000 40-year-olds.
  - Lifetime colonoscopies ranged from 624 to 6,817 per 1,000, and complications from 5 to 22 per 1,000.
  - 41 of the 49 strategies that were efficient in all 3 models began at age 45 [[Knudsen2021]]
- Lowering the start age from 50 to 45 was modelled to add 22–27 life-years gained, 161–784 colonoscopies and 0.1–2 complications per 1,000 people [[Knudsen2021]].
- Compared with no screening, the modelled strategies averted 16–74 CRC cases and 15–32 CRC deaths per 1,000 [[Knudsen2021]].
- ACS 2018: average-risk adults aged ≥45 should have regular screening with a high-sensitivity stool test or a structural exam. Starting at 45 is a qualified recommendation; screening from 50 is a strong one [[Wolf2018]].
- ACS 2018 options:
  - FIT every year
  - high-sensitivity gFOBT every year
  - mt-sDNA every 3 years
  - colonoscopy every 10 years
  - CT colonography every 5 years
  - flexible sigmoidoscopy every 5 years [[Wolf2018]]
- ACS 2018 age limits: continue through age 75 if in good health with life expectancy over 10 years; individualise at 76–85; discourage screening over 85 [[Wolf2018]].
- ACS 2026 update:
  - It reaffirms starting at 45 and continuing through 75 when life expectancy is over 10 years.
  - It adds the next-generation mt-sDNA and the mt-sRNA tests as preferred stool options at 3-year intervals.
  - It recommends blood-based tests only for people who decline or do not complete the preferred tests [[Wolf2026]]
- ACS 2026 states that a positive non-colonoscopy test needs follow-up colonoscopy, preferably within 6 months [[Wolf2026]].
- USMSTF 2022 suggests average-risk screening begin at 45 and be individualised at 76–85. It does not recommend screening after 85. It notes that no study yet shows screening under 50 improves CRC incidence or mortality [[Patel2022]].
- USMSTF 2017 tiers:
  - Tier 1: colonoscopy every 10 years and annual FIT.
  - Tier 2: CT colonography every 5 years, FIT-fecal DNA every 3 years, and flexible sigmoidoscopy every 5–10 years.
  - Tier 3: capsule colonoscopy every 5 years.
  - It suggests the Septin9 serum assay not be used [[Rex2017]]
- ACP 2023 guidance statement (version 2):
  - Start at 50. Consider not screening at 45–49.
  - Stop after 75, or when life expectancy is 10 years or less.
  - Choose among FIT or high-sensitivity gFOBT every 2 years, colonoscopy every 10 years, or flexible sigmoidoscopy every 10 years plus FIT every 2 years.
  - Do not use stool DNA, CT colonography, capsule, urine or serum tests [[Qaseem2023]]
- England's NHS programme runs 2-yearly FIT screening at a positivity threshold of 120 µg Hb/g [[Li2022,Li2021]].
- Across 38 screening programmes in 28 countries, 15 used 20 µg/g. The rest used thresholds from 8.5 to 120 µg/g, and nine sites had to change their first-chosen threshold [[Young2025]].
- ESGE/ESGAR recommend CT colonography as a screening option where there is no organised FIT programme. They do not suggest colon capsule as a first-line screening test [[Spada2020]].

### screening · Family history and higher risk
- USMSTF 2017 says colonoscopy every 5 years, starting at 40 or 10 years before the youngest relative's diagnosis (whichever is earlier), for people with either:
  - a first-degree relative with CRC or a documented advanced adenoma diagnosed before age 60, or
  - two first-degree relatives with these findings at any age [[Rex2017]]
- USMSTF 2017: one first-degree relative diagnosed at ≥60 with CRC or an advanced adenoma → average-risk screening options, starting at age 40 [[Rex2017]].
- ESGE defines familial CRC risk as ≥2 first-degree relatives with CRC, or ≥1 first-degree relative with CRC before age 50, and recommends colonoscopy surveillance in those families [[vanLeerdam2019]].
- In Lynch syndrome, ESGE recommends colonoscopy surveillance from age 25 for MLH1 and MSH2 carriers and from age 35 for MSH6 and PMS2 carriers [[vanLeerdam2019]].

### screening · Stool tests: FIT and gFOBT accuracy
- FIT meta-analysis 2014 (19 studies), pooled for CRC: sensitivity 0.79 (95% CI 0.69–0.86), specificity 0.94 (0.92–0.95), positive LR 13.10, negative LR 0.23 [[Lee2014]].
- In the same meta-analysis, sensitivity for CRC was 0.89 (0.80–0.95) at cut-offs below 20 µg/g, against 0.70 (0.55–0.81) at 20–50 µg/g. One sample performed as well as several [[Lee2014]].
- FIT meta-analysis 2019 (31 studies, 120,255 people): at 10 µg/g, CRC sensitivity was 0.91 (0.84–0.95) with negative LR 0.10. At >20 µg/g, specificity was 0.95 (0.94–0.96) with positive LR 15.49 [[Imperiale2019]].
- For advanced adenomas, FIT sensitivity was only 0.40 (0.33–0.47) at 10 µg/g. Specificity was 0.95 at >20 µg/g. The authors conclude that one-time sensitivity for advanced adenomas is low at any threshold [[Imperiale2019]].
- Selby 2019 meta-analysis (46 studies, 2.4 million people):
  - At ≤10 µg/g: CRC sensitivity 80% (76–83), specificity 91% (89–93).
  - At >10 to ≤20 µg/g: CRC sensitivity 69% (63–75), specificity 94% (93–96).
  - Advanced adenoma sensitivity was 31% versus 21% at these two thresholds [[Selby2019]]
- Selby 2019 colonoscopy-verified pooled bands:
  - >20–30 µg/g: CRC sensitivity 73%, specificity 96%, AA sensitivity 18%
  - >30 µg/g: CRC sensitivity 66%, specificity 96%, AA sensitivity 19% [[Selby2019]]
- USPSTF evidence review, pooled accuracy for detecting cancer:
  - common FITs: sensitivity 0.74, specificity 0.94
  - stool DNA with FIT: sensitivity 0.93, specificity 0.85
  - high-sensitivity gFOBT: sensitivity 0.50–0.75, specificity 0.96–0.98 [[Lin2021]]
- FIT sensitivity for CRC in the Dutch programme was 90.5% at a 15 µg/g cut-off and 82.9% at 47 µg/g (age-adjusted). The 2-year interval-cancer incidence was 9.5 versus 13.8 per 10,000 [[ToesZoutendijk2020]].
- Two FIT brands (FOB-Gold, OC-Sensor) gave different positivity rates at the same nominal µg/g threshold but similar accuracy at the same positivity rate (AUC 0.675 vs 0.686) [[deKlerk2019]].
- People with a "negative" FIT of 8–10 µg/g had a 33% cumulative incidence of advanced neoplasia over 8 years, against 5% at 0 µg/g (hazard ratio up to 8.2) (Early evidence for risk-stratified FIT) [[Grobbee2017]].

### screening · FIT threshold: what the slider trades
- In the English FIT pilot (27,238 people, OC-Sensor), the estimated sensitivity and the share of all participants referred varied by threshold:

  | Threshold (µg/g) | CRC sensitivity | High-risk adenoma sensitivity | Referred |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 10 | 90.0% | 77.7% | 12.6% |
  | 20 | 82.2% | 64.3% | 7.8% |
  | 40 | 73.3% | 50.0% | 5.2% |
  | 80 | 58.9% | 32.1% | 2.9% |
  | 120 | 47.8% | 25.0% | 2.1% |
  | 150 | 44.4% | 20.8% | 1.8% |
  | 180 | 40.0% | 17.6% | 1.5% |

  [[Li2021]]
- At England's 120 µg/g threshold, the pilot estimated that FIT identifies 47.8% of CRC and 25.0% of high-risk adenomas. At 20 µg/g it would capture 82.2% of CRC but refer 7.8% of participants [[Li2021]].
- Among pilot participants with no CRC or high-risk adenoma, the share above threshold fell from 4.5% at 20 µg/g to 1.1% at 120 µg/g and 0.9% at 150 µg/g. This is a specificity-like measure ("no/other abnormality" column) [[Li2021]].
- Modelled over 15 years per 100,000 screened every 2 years:
  - 120 µg/g: 16,092 colonoscopies, 186 CRCs prevented, 1,142 CRCs detected
  - 20 µg/g: 58,812 colonoscopies, 550 prevented, 1,216 detected
  - 180 µg/g: 11,500 colonoscopies, 131 prevented [[Li2022]]
- For advanced neoplasia in a German screening-colonoscopy cohort (FOB-Gold), sensitivity and specificity by cut-off were:
  - 10 µg/g: 44.9% / 90.1%
  - 20 µg/g: 34.3% / 95.9%
  - 50 µg/g: 20.8% / 97.8%

  Positivity was 13.9%, 7.6% and 4.3% [[Brenner2017]]
- PPV for advanced neoplasia rose steeply up to about 9 µg/g (35.2%). It reached 56.9% at 25 µg/g and then barely changed at higher cut-offs, while sensitivity kept falling [[Brenner2017]].
- In England's 2014 FIT pilot at 20 µg/g, positivity was 7.8%: 5.7% in women aged 59–64 and 11.1% in men aged 70–75 [[Moss2017]].
- After the Netherlands raised its cut-off from 15 to 47 µg/g, the second round found more advanced neoplasia in people screened only at 47 µg/g (15.3 vs 10.4 per 1,000). Over two rounds, though, they had less (45.6 vs 52.6 per 1,000) [[Kooyker2020]].

### screening · Molecular stool and blood tests
- Multitarget stool DNA vs FIT (9,989 evaluable average-risk people):
  - CRC sensitivity: 92.3% vs 73.8%
  - advanced precancerous lesion sensitivity: 42.4% vs 23.8%
  - specificity: 86.6% vs 94.9% [[Imperiale2014]]
- Number needed to screen to detect one cancer was 154 with colonoscopy, 166 with mt-sDNA and 208 with FIT. The DNA test detected 42.4% of sessile serrated polyps ≥1 cm, against 5.1% for FIT [[Imperiale2014]].
- Next-generation mt-sDNA (BLUE-C, 20,176 people aged ≥40):
  - CRC sensitivity 93.9% (87.1–97.7)
  - advanced precancerous lesion sensitivity 43.4% (41.3–45.6)
  - specificity for advanced neoplasia 90.6% (90.1–91.0)
  - specificity for non-neoplastic findings or negative colonoscopy 92.7% [[Imperiale2024,NCT04144738]]
- The FIT comparator in BLUE-C had CRC sensitivity 67.3%, APL sensitivity 23.3% and advanced-neoplasia specificity 94.8% [[Imperiale2024]].
- Multitarget stool RNA test (CRC-PREVENT, 8,920 people aged ≥45): CRC sensitivity 94%, advanced adenoma sensitivity 46%, specificity for no lesions 88%. The FIT comparator found 78% of CRC and 29% of advanced adenomas [[Barnell2023]].
- cfDNA blood test (Shield, ECLIPSE; 7,861 evaluable):
  - CRC sensitivity 83.1% (72.2–90.3)
  - stage I–III sensitivity 87.5%
  - advanced precancerous lesion sensitivity 13.2% (11.3–15.3)
  - specificity for advanced neoplasia 89.6% (88.8–90.3) [[Chung2024,NCT04136002]]
- Plasma methylated SEPT9 had a standardised CRC sensitivity of 48.2% (stage I 35.0%), advanced adenoma sensitivity 11.2% and specificity 91.5% [[Church2014]].
- FDA PMA approvals (openFDA):
  - Cologuard: 11 Aug 2014, age ≥50 at approval
  - Cologuard's age range expanded to ≥45 on 20 Sep 2019 (supplement S029)
  - ColoSense: 3 May 2024, age ≥45
  - Shield: 26 Jul 2024, average risk, age ≥45
  - Cologuard Plus: 3 Oct 2024, age ≥45 [[FDA-Cologuard,FDA-ColoSense,FDA-Shield,FDA-CologuardPlus]]
- The Shield intended-use statement says patients with a positive result "should be followed by colonoscopy". It also says the test is not a replacement for diagnostic or surveillance colonoscopy in high-risk people [[FDA-Shield]].
- Modelling: compared with no screening, CRC death relative rates were:
  - colonoscopy every 10 years: 0.19
  - annual FIT: 0.25
  - triennial next-gen mt-sDNA: 0.28
  - triennial Shield cfDNA: 0.44 [[Ladabaum2024AIM]]
- A blood test meeting CMS minimums would match FIT's outcomes only at 1.4–1.8 times FIT's participation (another model puts it at 1.35 times FIT uptake for Shield). Swapping blood tests in for stool tests or colonoscopy would raise CRC deaths (Early evidence) [[Ladabaum2024Gastro,Ladabaum2024AIM]].

### screening · CT colonography and sigmoidoscopy
- ACRIN CT colonography trial (2,531 people):
  - Per-patient sensitivity for adenomas or cancers ≥10 mm was 0.90, specificity 0.86, PPV 0.23 and NPV 0.99.
  - Per-patient sensitivity for adenomas ≥6 mm was 0.78 [[Johnson2008]]
- In an earlier 1,233-person study of 3D CT colonography, sensitivity for adenomas ≥10 mm was 93.8%, against 87.5% for optical colonoscopy before unblinding [[Pickhardt2003]].
- In the USPSTF review, sensitivity for adenomas ≥6 mm was similar for CT colonography with bowel prep (0.86) and colonoscopy (0.89). It is unclear whether extracolonic findings are a net benefit or harm [[Lin2021]].
- CT colonography radiation:
  - The estimated mean effective dose was 8 mSv for women and 7 mSv for men per exam.
  - Screening every 5 years from 50 to 80 was estimated to cause 150 radiation-related cancers per 100,000 and to prevent 3,580–5,190 CRCs, a benefit:risk ratio of 24:1 to 35:1 [[BerringtondeGonzalez2011]]
- Pooled flexible sigmoidoscopy RCTs (4 trials, n=458,002): CRC mortality IRR 0.74 (0.68–0.80) with 1–2 screens versus none [[Lin2021]].
- 15-year pooled analysis of all 4 sigmoidoscopy RCTs (274,952 people):
  - incidence IRR 0.79 and mortality ratio (MRR) 0.80
  - 0.51 fewer cases and 0.13 fewer deaths per 100 people
  - less benefit in women (mortality ratio 0.91) than men (0.73) [[Juul2022]]

### screening · Randomised trial evidence (gFOBT, sigmoidoscopy, colonoscopy)
- Minnesota gFOBT trial (46,551 people aged 50–80, rehydrated Hemoccult): 13-year CRC mortality per 1,000 was 5.88 with annual screening, 8.33 with biennial and 8.83 in controls, a 33% reduction with annual screening [[Mandel1993]].
- At 18 years in Minnesota, the CRC mortality rate ratio was 0.67 (0.51–0.83) with annual and 0.79 (0.62–0.97) with biennial screening [[Mandel1999]].
- At 30 years in Minnesota, the relative risk of CRC death was 0.68 (annual) and 0.78 (biennial). All-cause mortality did not change (RR 1.00 and 0.99) [[Shaukat2013]].
- Nottingham gFOBT trial (150,251 people aged 45–74, biennial, unrehydrated):
  - 15% lower cumulative CRC mortality (OR 0.85, 0.74–0.98) at a median 7.8 years
  - 59.6% completed at least one screen [[Hardcastle1996]]
- Funen gFOBT trial (61,933 people aged 45–75, biennial for 10 years): CRC mortality ratio 0.82 (0.68–0.99). 67% completed the first round [[Kronborg1996]].
- Cochrane 2007 (4 RCTs, >320,000 people): gFOBT screening reduced CRC mortality by 16% (RR 0.84, 0.78–0.90). The reduction was 25% (RR 0.75) in people who attended at least one round [[Hewitson2007]].
- UK Flexible Sigmoidoscopy Screening Trial (once-only at 55–64, 170,432 people), 11-year results:
  - intention to treat: incidence −23% (HR 0.77), mortality −31% (HR 0.69)
  - per protocol: incidence −33%, mortality −43%
  - number needed to screen: 191 to prevent one diagnosis, 489 to prevent one death [[Atkin2010]]
- UKFSST at 17 years: intention-to-treat incidence −26% (HR 0.74) and mortality −30% (HR 0.70). Per protocol, incidence −35% and mortality −41% [[Atkin2017]].
- PLCO sigmoidoscopy trial (154,900 people aged 55–74, median 11.9 years):
  - Incidence was 11.9 vs 15.2 per 10,000 person-years (RR 0.79).
  - Mortality was 2.9 vs 3.9 per 10,000 person-years (RR 0.74).
  - Distal CRC mortality fell 50%; proximal CRC mortality was unaffected (RR 0.97) [[Schoen2012]]
- NORCCAP (Norway, 98,792 people aged 50–64, median 10.9 years): CRC mortality HR 0.73 (31.4 vs 43.1 per 100,000 person-years) and incidence HR 0.80. Adherence was 63% [[Holme2014]].
- NORCCAP at 15 years: CRC incidence fell in men (HR 0.66) but not clearly in women (HR 0.92). CRC mortality fell in men (HR 0.63) but not in women (HR 1.01) [[Holme2018]].
- NordICC (Poland, Norway and Sweden; 84,585 people aged 55–64) invited one group to a single colonoscopy; 42.0% of those invited attended.
  - 10-year CRC risk was 0.98% in the invited group vs 1.20% with usual care (RR 0.82, 0.70–0.93).
  - CRC death was 0.28% vs 0.31% (RR 0.90, 0.64–1.16), not significant.
  - 455 people had to be invited to prevent one CRC [[Bretthauer2022,NCT00883792]]
- NordICC all-cause mortality was 11.03% vs 11.04% (RR 0.99). There were 15 major post-polypectomy bleeds and no perforations [[Bretthauer2022]].
- As reported in the 2024 ACG/ASGE quality indicators, NordICC's per-protocol analysis (people who had the colonoscopy) showed a 31% reduction in CRC incidence and a 50% reduction in CRC mortality [[Rex2024]].
- NordICC baseline (12,574 screened):
  - participation ranged from 60.7% in Norway to 22.9% in the Netherlands
  - caecal intubation was 97.2%
  - 28.6% of endoscopists missed the 25% ADR benchmark [[Bretthauer2016]]
- COLONPREV (Spain, 57,404 people aged 50–69) compared an invitation to one colonoscopy with biennial FIT.
  - Participation was 31.8% vs 39.9%.
  - 10-year CRC mortality was 0.22% vs 0.24% (RR 0.92, 0.64–1.32), so FIT was non-inferior [[Castells2025]]
- At COLONPREV baseline, participation was 24.6% for colonoscopy vs 34.2% for FIT. Cancer detection was equal (0.1% each), while colonoscopy found more advanced adenomas (1.9% vs 0.9%) [[Quintero2012]].
- A post hoc per-protocol COLONPREV analysis found similar 10-year CRC incidence (0.86% colonoscopy vs 0.80% FIT). CRC mortality was 0.042% vs 0.072%, an imprecise difference [[Castells2026]].
- SCREESCO (Sweden, age 60):
  - Participation was 35.1% for colonoscopy vs 55.5% for two FIT rounds.
  - Colonoscopy detected advanced adenomas in 2.05% vs 1.61% with FIT.
  - There were 2 perforations and 15 major bleeds in 16,555 colonoscopies [[Forsberg2022]]
- SCREESCO at a median 4.8 years: CRC incidence was not yet lower in either arm (colonoscopy IRR 1.08; FIT IRR 0.92), but stage I–II diagnoses rose with colonoscopy (IRR 1.38) [[Westerberg2026]].
- CONFIRM (US Veterans Affairs) randomised 50,126 veterans aged 50–75 to annual FIT or colonoscopy, with follow-up planned through 2028. Of those who declined because they preferred a particular test, 58.5% preferred stool testing [[Robertson2023,NCT01239082]].

### screening · Observational effectiveness
- In two US cohorts followed for 22 years, CRC death fell after screening colonoscopy (HR 0.32) and screening sigmoidoscopy (HR 0.59). Only colonoscopy lowered proximal-colon CRC death (HR 0.47) [[Nishihara2013]].
- Meta-analysis of observational studies: CRC incidence and mortality appear 40–60% lower after screening colonoscopy than after sigmoidoscopy. The added benefit was significant only for proximal-colon deaths [[Brenner2014]].
- In Kaiser Permanente, completing ≥1 FIT was associated with 33% lower CRC death (aOR 0.67) and 42% lower death from left-sided or rectal cancer. There was no clear effect on right-sided cancer (aOR 0.83) [[Doubeni2024]].
- Taiwan's biennial FIT programme was associated with fewer advanced-stage CRCs (aRR 0.66) and CRC deaths (aRR 0.60). The effect was weaker in the proximal colon (aRR 0.72 for death) [[Chiu2021]].
- In Taiwan, FIT screening was associated with a 62% reduction in CRC mortality among screened people (RR 0.38). At 21.4% coverage this meant a 10% population-level reduction [[Chiu2015]].
- In Veneto, Italy, areas that started FIT screening early had 22% lower CRC mortality by 2006–2011 than late-starting areas (RR 0.78) [[Zorzi2015]].
- When Kaiser Permanente organised its screening, up-to-date screening rose from 38.9% to 82.7% between 2000 and 2015. Over the same period CRC incidence fell 25.5% and CRC mortality fell 52.4% [[Levin2018]].
- Raising US screening to 80% was modelled to avert about 277,000 cases and 203,000 CRC deaths from 2013 to 2030 [[Meester2015]].

### screening · Participation and outreach
- Mailed outreach to uninsured people raised one-year screening to 40.7% with FIT and 24.6% with colonoscopy, against 12.1% with usual care [[Gupta2013]].
- Over 3 years in a safety-net system, completion of the whole screening process was 38.4% with colonoscopy outreach, 28.0% with FIT outreach and 10.7% with usual care [[Singal2017]].
- Replacing gFOBT with FIT in England raised uptake from 59.3% to 66.4%. Among previous non-responders uptake nearly doubled, from 12.5% to 23.9% [[Moss2017]].
- Over three Dutch FIT rounds (10 µg/g), participation rose from 62.6% to 63.2% to 68.3%, and 73% attended at least once. Positivity fell from 8.4% to 6.0% to 5.7% [[Kapidzic2014]].
- Four rounds of two-sample FIT gave no more advanced neoplasia than one-sample FIT (4.7% vs 4.4% of invitees) but needed more colonoscopies. The authors favour one-sample FIT [[Schreuders2019]].
- The Dutch programme's second round reached 75.9% participation: 93.4% among previous participants and 21.0% among previous non-participants [[Kooyker2020]].

### screening · Follow-up after a positive stool test
- In 70,124 people with a positive FIT, colonoscopy at 2–9 months carried no excess risk compared with 8–30 days [[Corley2017]].
- Colonoscopy at 10–12 months was linked to more CRC (OR 1.48; 49 vs 30 per 1,000) and more stage III–IV disease (OR 1.97) [[Corley2017]].
- Colonoscopy after more than 12 months was linked to more CRC (OR 2.25; 76 per 1,000) and more advanced-stage disease (OR 3.22; 31 per 1,000) [[Corley2017]].
- In Denmark, people who had their colonoscopy more than 90 days after the screening invitation were more likely to have CRC (OR 3.49) and advanced-stage disease (OR 2.10) than those seen within 30 days [[Kaalby2019]].
- In the 2013 Texas outreach trial, 11 of 60 people with an abnormal FIT did not complete colonoscopy [[Gupta2013]].
- In Kaiser control participants, 79.4% of those with a positive FIT had a colonoscopy within 1 year [[Doubeni2024]].
- ACS 2026 recommends follow-up colonoscopy after any positive non-colonoscopy test, preferably within 6 months. The 2024 quality indicators set an ADR target of ≥50% for colonoscopies done after a positive stool test [[Wolf2026,Rex2024]].

### screening · PPV of FIT in programmes
- In England, at 120 µg/g with 2-yearly screening, modelled PPV per colonoscopy was 7.10% for screen-detected CRC and 26.47% for advanced adenoma. At 20 µg/g it was 2.07% and 16.44% [[Li2022]].
- In the Dutch population trial (10 µg/g), PPV for advanced neoplasia fell over successive rounds from 40.7% to 33.2% to 24.0%, as prevalence fell [[Kapidzic2014]].
- In the German cohort, PPV for advanced neoplasia was 36.8% at 10 µg/g, 51.4% at 20 µg/g and 54.4% at 50 µg/g [[Brenner2017]].

### colonoscopy · How colonoscopy prevents cancer
- National Polyp Study (1,418 people after polypectomy): CRC incidence was 76–90% lower than in three reference groups (5 observed vs 20.7–48.3 expected) [[Winawer1993]].
- National Polyp Study mortality follow-up (2,602 people with adenomas removed, median 15.8 years): 12 CRC deaths against 25.4 expected, a standardised mortality ratio of 0.47 (a 53% reduction) [[Zauber2012]].
- 46% of advanced proximal neoplasms occurred in people with no distal polyps, so sigmoidoscopy-only triage would miss about half [[Imperiale2000]].
- In VA screening colonoscopy (3,121 men and women aged 50–75):
  - 37.5% had one or more neoplastic lesions and 1.0% had invasive cancer.
  - 52% of those with advanced proximal neoplasia had no distal adenoma [[Lieberman2000]]
- In PLCO, CRC incidence was 20.0 per 10,000 person-years after an advanced adenoma, 9.1 after a non-advanced adenoma and 7.5 with no adenoma. The risk with non-advanced adenoma was not significantly raised (RR 1.2) [[Click2018]].

### colonoscopy · Quality: adenoma detection rate, withdrawal time, prep, caecal intubation
- In Poland's screening programme (45,026 people, 186 endoscopists), endoscopists with ADR below 20% had interval-cancer hazard ratios of 10.75–12.50 [[Kaminski2010]].
- Kaiser Permanente (314,872 colonoscopies): physician ADRs ranged from 7.4% to 52.5%, and interval-cancer rates fell from 9.8 to 4.8 per 10,000 person-years from the lowest to the highest ADR quintile [[Corley2014]].
- Each 1% rise in ADR was linked to a 3% lower risk of interval cancer (HR 0.97). The highest ADR quintile had a fatal interval-cancer HR of 0.38 compared with the lowest [[Corley2014]].
- Endoscopists who raised their ADR had fewer interval cancers (HR 0.63) and interval-cancer deaths (HR 0.50). Reaching or staying in the top quintile (ADR above about 24.6%) gave HRs of 0.27 and 0.18 [[Kaminski2017Gastro]].
- ACG/ASGE 2024 quality indicators:
  - screening ADR ≥35% overall (≥40% in men, ≥30% in women), raised from 25% in 2015
  - ADR after a positive stool test ≥50%
  - sessile serrated lesion detection rate ≥6%
  - adenomas per colonoscopy ≥0.6 [[Rex2024]]
- ACG/ASGE 2024 process targets:
  - caecal intubation ≥95% with photo documentation
  - adequate bowel prep ≥90% (Boston Bowel Preparation Scale ≥2 in each of 3 segments)
  - average withdrawal time in normal colonoscopies ≥8 minutes
  - cold snare for 4–9 mm lesions ≥90% [[Rex2024]]
- ESGE 2017 minimum standards: adequate bowel prep 90%, caecal intubation 90%, ADR 25%, appropriate polypectomy technique 80% [[Kaminski2017ESGE]].
- Endoscopists with mean withdrawal times of 6 minutes or more found any neoplasia in 28.3% of patients versus 11.8% for faster endoscopists. For advanced neoplasia the rates were 6.4% versus 2.6% [[Barclay2006]].
- Each extra minute of withdrawal raised ADR by 3.6%. Withdrawal under 6 minutes was linked to 2.3 times the interval-cancer rate (IRR 2.3, 1.5–3.4) [[Shaukat2015]].
- The Boston Bowel Preparation Scale is a 0–9 scale (0–3 for each of 3 segments). In validation, scores ≥5 were linked to a higher polyp detection rate (40% vs 24%) [[Lai2009]].
- In a randomised trial after positive FIT, split-dose prep raised ADR from 40.9% to 53.0% (RR 1.22) compared with day-before prep [[Radaelli2017]].
- In tandem colonoscopy studies, miss rates were 26% for adenomas, 9% for advanced adenomas and 27% for serrated polyps [[Zhao2019]].
- Using CO2 instead of air insufflation cut moderate or severe post-procedure pain from 16.7% to 4.2% [[Bretthauer2016]].

### colonoscopy · Harms
- In the USPSTF review, serious harms of screening colonoscopy were perforation at 3.1 per 10,000 procedures and major bleeding at 14.6 per 10,000 [[Lin2021]].
- Meta-analysis of population-based studies (per 1,000 colonoscopies):
  - perforation 0.5
  - post-colonoscopy bleeding 2.6
  - death 2.9 per 100,000 colonoscopies
  - with polypectomy: perforation 0.8 and post-polypectomy bleeding 9.8 per 1,000 [[Reumkens2016]]
- Post-colonoscopy bleeding fell from 6.4 to 1.0 per 1,000 between 2001 and 2015. Perforation and death rates stayed stable [[Reumkens2016]].
- In the CORI cohort (21,375 screening and surveillance colonoscopies), bleeding needing hospital care occurred in 1.59 per 1,000 and perforation in 0.19 per 1,000. Direct complications totalled 2.01 per 1,000 [[Ko2010]].
- In people aged ≥65, rates per 1,000 colonoscopies were perforation 1.0, GI bleeding 6.3 and cardiopulmonary events 19.1. People aged ≥80 had a higher perforation risk (IRR 1.6) [[Day2011]].
- 2024 quality indicators give perforation rates of about 0.04% (1 in 2,500) in screening exams, 0.016–0.8% in diagnostic exams and 0.02–8% in therapeutic exams [[Rex2024]].
- In CISNET modelling, lifetime screening complications ranged from 5 to 22 per 1,000 depending on strategy [[Knudsen2021]].

### colonoscopy · Post-colonoscopy colorectal cancer (PCCRC)
- The World Endoscopy Organization (WEO) standardised the term PCCRC (introduced in 2010) and its rate calculations. It also defined post-imaging CRC [[Rutter2018]].
- In England, published methods gave PCCRC rates from 2.5% to 7.7%. The 3-year PCCRC rate fell from 10.6% (colonoscopies in 2001) to 7.3% (2007), or 8.6% overall [[Morris2015]].
- Cancers within 5 years of colonoscopy were more often CIMP-high (OR 2.19) and MSI-high (OR 2.10). This suggests fast-growing serrated or MMR-deficient pathways (Early evidence) [[Nishihara2013]].

### schedule · Post-polypectomy surveillance (USMSTF 2020)
- The USMSTF 2020 intervals assume a complete, high-quality exam:

  | Normal colonoscopy | 10 y | strong / high |
  | 1–2 tubular adenomas <10 mm | 7–10 y | strong / moderate |
  | 3–4 tubular adenomas <10 mm | 3–5 y | weak / very low |
  | 5–10 tubular adenomas <10 mm | 3 y | strong / moderate |
  | Adenoma ≥10 mm | 3 y | strong / high |
  | Tubulovillous or villous adenoma | 3 y | strong / moderate |
  | Adenoma with high-grade dysplasia | 3 y | strong / moderate |
  | More than 10 adenomas | 1 y | weak / very low |
  | Piecemeal resection of adenoma ≥20 mm | 6 months | strong / moderate |

  [[Gupta2020]]
- The USMSTF 2020 serrated-polyp intervals:
  - ≤20 hyperplastic polyps <10 mm in rectum or sigmoid: 10 y
  - 1–2 sessile serrated lesions (SSL) <10 mm: 5–10 y
  - 3–4 SSL <10 mm: 3–5 y
  - 5–10 SSL <10 mm: 3 y
  - SSL ≥10 mm: 3 y
  - SSL with dysplasia: 3 y
  - traditional serrated adenoma: 3 y
  - hyperplastic polyp ≥10 mm: 3–5 y
  - piecemeal resection of SSL ≥20 mm: 6 months [[Gupta2020]]
- Patients with more than 10 adenomas, or more than 10 cumulative over a lifetime, may need genetic testing. Serrated polyposis criteria: either >20 hyperplastic polyps throughout the colon (≥5 proximal to the rectum), or ≥5 serrated polyps >5 mm proximal to the rectum with ≥2 that are ≥10 mm [[Gupta2020]].
- Key 2020 changes:
  - 1–2 small tubular adenomas moved from 5–10 to 7–10 years
  - more than 10 adenomas: 1 year instead of under 3 years
  - 3–4 small adenomas: 3–5 years allowed [[Gupta2020]]
- After a first surveillance colonoscopy is normal, the next interval is 10 years if the baseline was 1–4 small adenomas. It is 5 years if the baseline was an advanced adenoma or 5–10 adenomas [[Gupta2020]].
- Adequate ADR in the 2020 document was ≥30% in men and ≥20% in women. An adequate prep must show polyps >5 mm [[Gupta2020]].
- ESGE 2020 says people who had 1–4 adenomas <10 mm with low-grade dysplasia, or any serrated polyp <10 mm without dysplasia, completely removed need no surveillance and return to screening (10 years if no programme) [[Hassan2020]].
- ESGE 2020 recommends surveillance at 3 years after an adenoma ≥10 mm, high-grade dysplasia, ≥5 adenomas, or a serrated polyp ≥10 mm or with dysplasia. After piecemeal resection of a lesion ≥20 mm it recommends a repeat at 3–6 months, then surveillance 12 months later [[Hassan2020]].

### Exam hooks
- The USPSTF 2021 grades are B for ages 45–49, A for 50–75 and C for 76–85 [[USPSTF2021]].
- The ACP (2023) is the outlier among the guidelines covered here. It starts screening at 50, uses FIT every 2 years, and advises against stool DNA, CT colonography and blood tests [[Qaseem2023]].
- FIT: one sample works as well as several. Sensitivity for CRC is about 0.79, specificity about 0.94 [[Lee2014]].
- FIT sensitivity for advanced adenomas from a single test is only about 0.40 even at 10 µg/g, which is why it must be repeated annually or biennially [[Imperiale2019]].
- mt-sDNA versus FIT: sensitivity 92.3% vs 73.8%, but specificity 86.6% vs 94.9% [[Imperiale2014]].
- The Shield blood test found 83% of CRC but only 13% of advanced precancerous lesions [[Chung2024]].
- NordICC: 18% lower CRC incidence at 10 years among those invited, but no significant drop in deaths. Only 42% attended [[Bretthauer2022]].
- National Polyp Study: polypectomy cut CRC incidence by 76–90% and mortality by 53% [[Winawer1993,Zauber2012]].
- Each 1% increase in ADR means about 3% fewer interval cancers [[Corley2014]].
- The 2024 minimum ADR is 35% (40% in men, 30% in women). Withdrawal should average ≥8 minutes and caecal intubation should be ≥95% [[Rex2024]].
- Withdrawal under 6 minutes is linked to 2.3 times the interval-cancer rate [[Shaukat2015]].
- Waiting more than 10 months for colonoscopy after a positive FIT raises the risk of cancer and of advanced stage [[Corley2017]].
- 1–2 tubular adenomas <10 mm → 7–10 years. Adenoma ≥10 mm → 3 years. Piecemeal resection ≥20 mm → 6 months [[Gupta2020]].
- A positive sigmoidoscopy should prompt full colonoscopy. About half of advanced proximal neoplasms have no distal marker [[Imperiale2000,Lieberman2000]].
- Flexible sigmoidoscopy prevents distal but not proximal CRC deaths (PLCO proximal mortality RR 0.97) [[Schoen2012]].
- Annual gFOBT cut CRC mortality by 32% over 30 years but did not change all-cause mortality [[Shaukat2013]].
- Perforation occurs in about 3 per 10,000 screening colonoscopies [[Lin2021]].

### In plain words
- Screening looks for cancer before you have symptoms. It can also find and remove growths (polyps) before they turn into cancer [[Winawer1993]].
- Most US groups now say to start at age 45 if your risk is average. One group (the ACP) still says 50 [[USPSTF2021,Qaseem2023]].
- The best test is the one you actually do. A home stool test done on schedule is a good choice [[Wolf2026]].
- A stool test (FIT) is done at home every year or two. If it is positive, you need a colonoscopy, ideally within a few months [[Corley2017,Wolf2026]].
- A single FIT catches most cancers but misses many large polyps, which is why it has to be repeated [[Imperiale2019]].
- The blood test finds most cancers but very few polyps. Experts say to use it only if you will not do another test [[Chung2024,Wolf2026]].
- Colonoscopy looks at the whole bowel and can remove polyps in the same visit [[Zauber2012]].
- Serious problems from colonoscopy are rare: about 3 tears and 15 major bleeds for every 10,000 tests [[Lin2021]].
- A careful colonoscopy finds more polyps. Doctors who find more polyps have patients who get fewer later cancers [[Corley2014]].
- A good bowel prep, split between the evening before and the morning of the test, helps the doctor see small polyps [[Radaelli2017]].
- If you had one or two small polyps removed, the next colonoscopy is usually in 7 to 10 years [[Gupta2020]].
- If a parent, brother or sister had colon cancer young, you may need to start at 40 or earlier and use colonoscopy [[Rex2017]].
- Numbers like "18% fewer cancers" describe large groups of people, not what will happen to any one person [[Bretthauer2022]].

## Open questions and controversies
- **Start at 45 or 50?** USPSTF, ACS and USMSTF start at 45, based on rising early-onset incidence and modelling [[USPSTF2021,Wolf2026,Patel2022,Knudsen2021]]. The ACP starts at 50 and suggests considering not screening at 45–49 [[Qaseem2023]]. USMSTF concedes that no study yet shows outcome benefit under 50 [[Patel2022]]. Status: unresolved; US practice largely follows 45.
- **What does NordICC mean for colonoscopy?** Intention-to-screen showed an 18% incidence reduction and no significant mortality reduction at 10 years with 42% participation [[Bretthauer2022]]. Per-protocol estimates (31% incidence, 50% mortality reduction) and quality critiques suggest underestimation [[Rex2024]]. Mortality follow-up continues (registry primary completion listed as 2026-06) [[NCT00883792]]. Status: open.
- **FIT versus colonoscopy as first-line test:** COLONPREV found FIT invitation non-inferior for CRC death at 10 years, with higher participation [[Castells2025]]. A post hoc per-protocol analysis hints at lower mortality with colonoscopy, but the estimate is imprecise [[Castells2026]]. CONFIRM results are expected after 2028 [[NCT01239082]]. Status: open.
- **Blood-based screening:** FDA approved Shield in 2024 [[FDA-Shield]]. ACS 2026 limits blood tests to people who decline other tests [[Wolf2026]]. Models predict more deaths if blood tests replace stool tests or colonoscopy, but net benefit if they reach people who refuse other tests [[Ladabaum2024AIM,Ladabaum2024Gastro]]. The ACP advises against serum tests [[Qaseem2023]]. Status: active debate.
- **FIT threshold:** Thresholds range from 8.5 to 120 µg/g worldwide [[Young2025]]. England's 120 µg/g catches an estimated 47.8% of CRC per round, against 82.2% at 20 µg/g, but uses far fewer colonoscopies [[Li2021,Li2022]]. Lower thresholds find more but need more colonoscopy capacity [[Selby2019]]. Status: set by resource limits; risk-stratified use of f-Hb is proposed (Early evidence) [[Grobbee2017]].
- **Surveillance after small adenomas:** USMSTF recommends 7–10 years after 1–2 small tubular adenomas [[Gupta2020]]. ESGE returns 1–4 small adenomas to screening with no surveillance [[Hassan2020]]. Non-advanced adenoma carried no significant excess long-term CRC risk in PLCO [[Click2018]]. Status: transatlantic divergence.
- **Women and sigmoidoscopy:** Pooled trials show smaller benefit in women [[Juul2022]]. NORCCAP showed no clear benefit in women [[Holme2018]]. Status: unexplained; proximal-cancer distribution is one proposed reason (Hypothesis) [[Holme2018]].
- **Stool DNA interval (1 vs 3 years):** USPSTF allows 1–3 years [[USPSTF2021]]. ACS uses 3 years [[Wolf2018,Wolf2026]]. Annual mt-sDNA adds colonoscopies for modest gains in modelling [[Knudsen2021]].

## Provenance
- **PubMed:** 91 sources were checked against PubMed E-utilities (efetch XML). Authors, title, journal, year, volume, pages, PMID, PMCID and DOI were taken from those records, and every number was read from the abstract.
- **PMC full text (NCBI BioC):** read for Gupta2020 (surveillance tables, definitions), Rex2024 (performance targets, perforation ranges, NordICC per-protocol figures), Knudsen2021 (strategy outcomes table), Selby2019 (threshold-stratified pooled table), Li2021 (threshold sensitivity table), Li2022 (15-year modelled outcomes and PPV), and Brenner2017 (cut-off table).
- **openFDA:** 4 device PMA records (Cologuard, Cologuard Plus, ColoSense, Shield).
- **ClinicalTrials.gov:** 4 registry records (API v2).
- **USPSTF interval list:** read on the USPSTF recommendation web page, which reproduces the JAMA statement. The citation is to the PubMed-validated JAMA record.
- **NordICC per-protocol estimates:** quoted second-hand from Rex2024. The NEJM full text was not accessible.
- **Icon-array rows marked "(derived)":** my own arithmetic, not published figures. Each applies the cited paper's sensitivity and specificity to one hypothetical 1,000 people with 7 CRC and 76 advanced precancerous lesions, the prevalence in Imperiale2014 (0.7% and 7.6%).
  - Specificity was applied to the remaining 917 people.
  - For mt-sRNA the published specificity is for "no lesions", so its false-positive count is approximate.
  - Results are for one round of testing.
- **FIT slider gaps:** no validated source gave paired sensitivity and specificity at exactly 50, 100, 120 and 150 µg/g in one cohort.
  - Li2021 gives CRC and high-risk adenoma sensitivity and positivity at 10/20/40/80/120/150/180 µg/g. It also gives the share of people without CRC or high-risk adenoma above each threshold.
  - Brenner2017 gives advanced-neoplasia sensitivity and specificity at 50 µg/g.
  - 100 µg/g is not reported. Do not interpolate it as data.
  - Brand matters: deKlerk2019 shows that the same nominal µg/g gives different positivity on different FIT brands.
- **Dropped:**
  - Kahi 2016 USMSTF post-CRC-resection surveillance: the abstract carries no intervals.
  - ACG 2021 and USMSTF 2017 FIT consensus: the abstracts carry no extractable numbers.
  - Warren 2009: no numbers in the abstract.
  - England's age-extension timetable: no validated source.
- **Shared canonical keys** were checked and used as given: USPSTF2021 (34003218), Wolf2018 (29846947), Gupta2020 (Gastroenterology version, 32044092), Imperiale2014 (24645800), Bretthauer2022 (36214590), Siegel2023CRC (36856579), Bray2024 (38572751), Dekker2019 (31631858).
