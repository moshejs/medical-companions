# H · Living, survivorship & nursing

## Findings

### living · Stomas: types, permanence and reversal
- A defunctioning (diverting) stoma is often made at anterior resection for rectal cancer, to limit the harm if the anastomosis leaks. In the Dutch TME trial, 523 of 924 patients (57%) who had low anterior resection got a primary stoma [[denDulk2007]].
- Stomas meant to be temporary are not always closed. In the TME trial, 19% of the stomas created were never reversed. Postoperative complications and stomas created at a later operation predicted a permanent stoma [[denDulk2007]].
- In a Swedish population-based series (316 anterior resections, 2007–2013), 87% were defunctioned at the first operation and 24% had a permanent stoma at the end of follow-up. Anastomotic leakage and stage IV disease were risk factors [[Holmgren2017]].
- 9% of patients who had a stoma reversed had a major complication (return to theatre, intensive care or death) [[Holmgren2017]].
- In Swedish registry data (anterior resection 2007–2015), 13.6% (training set, n = 3512) and 15.2% (test set, n = 1136) had a stoma 2 years after surgery. Predictors included comorbidity, tumour category, metastasis, neoadjuvant therapy, defunctioning stoma use, tumour height and hospital volume (model AUROC 0.67) [[Back2021]].
- Defunctioning stomas may reduce the chance of ending up stoma-free after anterior resection (Early evidence; registry analysis) [[Back2021]].

### living · Parastomal hernia
- A review of the older literature found parastomal hernia in 1.8–28.3% of end ileostomies and 0–6.2% of loop ileostomies. After colostomy the rates were 4.0–48.1% (end) and 0–30.8% (loop). Rates depend on stoma type and length of follow-up [[Carne2003]].
- The European Hernia Society guideline states that end colostomy carries a higher parastomal hernia incidence than other stoma types [[Antoniou2018]].
- EHS guidance: clinical examination is needed for diagnosis. CT or ultrasound can be used when the diagnosis is uncertain [[Antoniou2018]].
- EHS **strongly recommends** a prophylactic synthetic non-absorbable mesh when an end colostomy is created. No such recommendation is made for other stoma types (Established for end colostomy) [[Antoniou2018]].
- EHS strongly recommends against suture repair for elective parastomal hernia repair [[Antoniou2018]].
- In a small RCT of permanent stomas (n = 54), at 5 years parastomal hernia was found in 17 of 21 surviving patients with a conventional stoma (5 needed repair). It was found in 2 of 15 with a sublay mesh, and neither needed repair (P < 0.001). There were no mesh infections, fistulas or strictures [[Janes2009]].
- In the older literature, mesh repair had lower recurrence rates (0–33.3%) than direct tissue repair (46–100%) or stoma relocation (0–76.2%) [[Carne2003]].

### living · High-output ileostomy, dehydration and kidney injury
- High-output stoma (HOS) is usually taken as small-bowel output above about 1.5–2.0 L/24 h, causing water, sodium and often magnesium depletion. It occurs in up to 31% of small-bowel stomas [[Nightingale2022]].
- In one UK centre (2002–2006), early HOS (> 2000 mL/day, within 3 weeks of surgery) occurred in 75 of 456 ileostomies or jejunostomies (16%). 49% resolved spontaneously, and 51% needed ongoing medical treatment, usually because of a short remaining small bowel [[Baker2011]].
- All 26 patients with late HOS (after discharge) in that series had hypomagnesaemia. 11 were admitted with renal impairment [[Baker2011]].
- A meta-analysis of 71 studies (82,451 patients) found that 20% (95% CI 18–23%) were readmitted for any cause within 30 days of ileostomy creation. 6% (95% CI 4–9%) were readmitted for dehydration [[Vogel2022]].
- Dehydration readmissions lasted 2.5–9 days and cost about $2750–$5924 per patient [[Vogel2022]].
- After colorectal cancer resection with diverting ileostomy, pooled dehydration prevalence was 9.00% (95% CI 5.31–13.45). The relative risk of dehydration versus no ileostomy was 3.37 (95% CI 2.30–4.95) [[Borucki2021]].
- Reported consequences of ileostomy-related dehydration include unplanned readmission, delayed or abandoned adjuvant chemotherapy, and progressive renal impairment or chronic kidney disease [[Borucki2021]].
- In 603 diverting loop ileostomies at one US centre, 16.9% were readmitted within 60 days, and dehydration was the commonest cause (43.1% of readmissions). Postoperative diuretic use was the only independent risk factor for dehydration readmission [[Messaris2012]].
- In that study, dehydration was defined as ostomy output ≥ 1500 mL over 24 h plus a BUN/creatinine ratio ≥ 20, or physical signs of dehydration. This gives nurses a concrete threshold to teach [[Messaris2012]].
- First-line HOS management (expert review): rehydrate first. Then restrict oral hypotonic fluid, have the patient sip a glucose-saline solution, and give antimotility drugs (often high-dose loperamide) and omeprazole. Subcutaneous saline with magnesium is an option before IV fluids [[Nightingale2022]].
- A meta-analysis of 32 studies (768 patients) found no significant reduction in stoma output with somatostatin analogues, loperamide or omeprazole. The evidence was weak and definitions were inconsistent (Early evidence) [[Lederhuber2023]].

### nursing · Stoma site marking and stoma education
- The WOCN Society/ASCRS position statement says marking the best stoma site before surgery makes independent stoma care, predictable pouch wear times and return to normal activities more likely. Colorectal surgeons and certified ostomy nurses are the best-placed clinicians to mark sites [[Salvadalena2015]].
- In emergency stoma surgery at one Japanese centre (n = 345), grade ≥ 2 stoma-related complications occurred in 24% of patients who were marked before surgery and 36% of those who were not (P = 0.010). Peristomal dermatitis was 10% vs 18% (Early evidence; retrospective) [[Nozawa2024]].
- In that study, not marking the site was an independent risk factor for stoma-related complications (adjusted OR 1.69) [[Nozawa2024]].
- A 2025 meta-analysis (9 studies; 507 educated vs 356 not) found that preoperative stoma education reduced peristomal skin complications (9.5% vs 19.4%; RR 0.45, 95% CI 0.29–0.72). There was no significant effect on length of stay, readmission or overall morbidity (very low-certainty evidence) [[Shi2025]].
- A 2-hour preoperative stoma class led by WOC nurses was associated with fewer peristomal complications than a historical control group (OR 0.35, 95% CI 0.18–0.67). Length of stay and 30-day readmission did not differ significantly [[Stokes2017]].
- Within an ERAS programme with dedicated stoma education, median total hospital stay was 6 days vs 9 days with standard care (P < 0.001). Readmissions and stoma complications were similar (n = 122) [[Forsmo2016]].

### living · Low anterior resection syndrome (LARS)
- The LARS score was built from questionnaires returned by 961 Danish patients after low anterior resection. It has 5 items: incontinence for flatus, incontinence for liquid stool, frequency, clustering and urgency. The total range is 0–42 [[Emmertsen2012]].
- Cut-offs: 0–20 = no LARS, 21–29 = minor LARS, 30–42 = major LARS. Sensitivity was 72.54% and specificity 82.52% for major LARS [[Emmertsen2012]].
- Item points (MANUEL reproduction of the score) [[Christensen2021]]:
  - Flatus incontinence: never 0, less than once a week 4, at least once a week 7.
  - Liquid stool leakage: never 0, less than once a week 3, at least once a week 3.
  - Bowel opening frequency: more than 7 a day 4, 4–7 a day 2, 1–3 a day 0, less than once a day 5.
  - Opening the bowels again within 1 hour (clustering): never 0, less than once a week 9, at least once a week 11.
  - Urgency (having to rush to the toilet): never 0, less than once a week 11, at least once a week 16.
- The score discriminated between patients with and without radiotherapy, with tumours above or below 5 cm, and with total vs partial mesorectal excision [[Emmertsen2012]].
- International validation in 801 patients from Sweden, Spain, Germany and Denmark showed high correlation with quality of life and good test-retest reliability. Sensitivity was 67.7–88.3% and specificity 58.1–86.3% by country [[Juul2014]].
- A meta-analysis of 11 studies (patients at least 1 year after surgery or stoma closure) estimated the prevalence of **major LARS at 41% (95% CI 34–48%)** [[Croese2018]].
- Radiotherapy and low tumour/anastomotic height were the most consistent risk factors. Defunctioning ileostomy worsened bowel function in 4 of 11 studies [[Croese2018]].
- Normative data matter for interpretation. In a Danish general-population sample aged 50–79 with no rectal surgery, 18.8% of women and 9.6% of men scored ≥ 30 ("major LARS"). Median scores were 16 (women) and 11 (men) [[Juul2019]].
- The MANUEL expert guidance describes stepwise care. Best supportive care comes first (diet, loperamide, skin care, pads), then pelvic floor rehabilitation, then transanal irrigation, which is used when supportive care has not worked. Most of its recommendations rest on expert opinion [[Christensen2021]].

### living · Sexual and urinary dysfunction after rectal surgery
- A Nature Reviews Urology review: more than half of patients have worse sexual function after rectal cancer treatment, and about one-third have urinary dysfunction. Surgical nerve damage is the main cause of urinary problems. Radiotherapy contributes to sexual but not urinary dysfunction [[Lange2011]].
- In the TME trial cohort, men reported increases in general sexual dysfunction (76.4%), erectile dysfunction (79.8%) and ejaculatory problems (72.2%). Women reported general sexual dysfunction (61.5%), dyspareunia (59.1%) and vaginal dryness (56.6%) [[Lange2009]].
- Risk factors for sexual dysfunction in men were nerve damage, blood loss, anastomotic leakage, preoperative radiotherapy and having a stoma. In women they were preoperative radiotherapy and a stoma [[Lange2009]].
- In 4334 Danish male survivors, erectile function scores were lower after rectal resection (mean IIEF 12.14) than after colon resection (15.82). After adjustment and excluding men with a stoma or radiotherapy, rectal resection still carried higher odds of erectile dysfunction (OR 1.42, 95% CI 1.20–1.67) [[Laurberg2021]].
- A meta-analysis of 13 studies (1964 patients) found better urinary (IPSS) and male sexual (IIEF-5) scores at 3, 6 and 12 months after robotic than laparoscopic rectal surgery. The differences were small, about 1–2 points (Early evidence; mostly non-randomised) [[Zhu2024]].

### chemo · Oxaliplatin neuropathy: acute and chronic
- The oxaliplatin label describes two forms of neuropathy:
  - Acute: usually reversible. It starts within hours to 2 days of a dose, resolves within 14 days and often recurs with later doses. Cold temperature or cold objects can trigger or worsen it [[FDA-OxaliplatinGland]].
  - Delayed: persists more than 14 days. It can impair proprioception and daily activities [[FDA-OxaliplatinGland]].
- Per the label, the acute pattern was seen in about 56% of patients on oxaliplatin with fluorouracil/leucovorin, and in about 30% in any single cycle [[FDA-OxaliplatinGland]].
- Per the label, delayed neuropathy can occur without prior acute neuropathy. 80% of patients with grade 3 persistent neuropathy progressed from earlier grade 1–2 [[FDA-OxaliplatinGland]].
- In the adjuvant trial (label), peripheral sensory neuropathy occurred in 92% (all grades), including 13% grade 3. Any-grade neuropathy was present in 60% at 28 days after the last cycle, 39% at 6 months and 21% at 18 months [[FDA-OxaliplatinGland]].
- The label gives the median time to grade 3 neuropathy as 9 cycles in adjuvant treatment [[FDA-OxaliplatinGland]].
- The label advises patients to avoid cold drinks, ice and exposing the skin to cold. It also says to avoid topical ice for mucositis prophylaxis, because cold worsens acute neurological symptoms [[FDA-OxaliplatinGland]].
- In N08CB (adjuvant FOLFOX, n = 346), 89% had at least one acute symptom with cycle 1:
  - sensitivity to touching cold items 71%
  - sensitivity to swallowing cold items 71%
  - throat discomfort 63%
  - muscle cramps 42% [[Pachman2015]].
- In N08CB, acute symptoms peaked on day 3 and did not always resolve between cycles. They were about twice as severe in cycles 2–12 as in cycle 1, and worse acute neuropathy in cycle 1 predicted worse chronic neuropathy [[Pachman2015]].
- Hands were affected more than feet during chemotherapy. By 18 months symptoms were worse in the feet [[Pachman2015]].
- In MOSAIC (FOLFOX4 adjuvant), grade 3 sensory neuropathy was 12.4% during treatment and fell to 1.1% at 1 year of follow-up [[Andre2004]].
- In the IDEA pooled analysis (n = 12,834), grade ≥ 2 neurotoxicity during therapy and the month after was 16.6% (FOLFOX) and 14.2% (CAPOX) with 3 months of therapy, vs 47.7% (FOLFOX) and 44.9% (CAPOX) with 6 months [[Grothey2018]].
- IDEA maximal neurotoxicity grades (full-text table):
  - FOLFOX: grade 2 in 14.1% and grade 3–4 in 2.5% at 3 months, vs 31.8% and 15.9% at 6 months.
  - CAPOX: grade 2 in 11.6% and grade 3–4 in 2.6% at 3 months, vs 36.0% and 8.9% at 6 months [[Grothey2018]].
- In IDEA France, maximal grade 2 and 3 neuropathy were 28% and 8% with 3 months vs 41% and 25% with 6 months. Residual neuropathy above grade 1 at the end of follow-up was 3% vs 7% [[Andre2018]].
- In PROFILES survivors (2–11 years after diagnosis), a cumulative oxaliplatin dose ≥ 842 mg/m² gave worse sensory scores than < 421 mg/m² (mean 19 vs 8), and more tingling toes/feet (13% vs 2%) [[Beijers2015]].
- Dose delays did not reduce long-term neuropathy in PROFILES. Lowering the cumulative dose was the only effective lever [[Beijers2015]].
- In a Dutch population-based cohort, 57% of chemotherapy-treated patients reported high sensory neuropathy at 1 year and 47% at 2 years. High motor neuropathy was 47% and 28%. High neuropathy levels went with worse global quality of life and functioning [[Bonhof2021]].

### chemo · Neuropathy prevention and treatment
- ASCO 2020: **no agent is recommended to prevent CIPN**, and acetyl-L-carnitine should be discouraged. Clinicians should consider delaying, reducing, substituting or stopping chemotherapy when neuropathy is intolerable or impairs function [[Loprinzi2020]].
- ASCO 2020: duloxetine is the only agent with appropriate evidence for established painful CIPN, but its benefit is limited [[Loprinzi2020]].
- In a crossover RCT (n = 231; painful CIPN after taxanes or oxaliplatin), 5 weeks of duloxetine (30 mg for week 1, then 60 mg daily) reduced average pain by 1.06 points vs 0.34 with placebo, on a 0–10 scale. The mean difference was 0.73 (95% CI 0.26–1.20) [[Smith2013]].
- In that trial, 59% on duloxetine vs 38% on placebo reported some pain reduction [[Smith2013]].
- IV calcium and magnesium did not reduce cumulative or acute oxaliplatin neurotoxicity in a phase III RCT (n = 353, N08CB) [[Loprinzi2014]].
- In an RCT of frozen gloves during oxaliplatin or taxane chemotherapy (n = 180), there was no difference in the main CIPN subscales. Hand tingling was reduced, and 34% stopped wearing the gloves because of discomfort (Early evidence) [[Beijers2020]].

### chemo · Hand-foot syndrome, diarrhoea, mucositis, neutropenia
- X-ACT (n = 1987, stage III colon cancer, 24 weeks): capecitabine was at least equivalent to bolus fluorouracil/leucovorin for disease-free survival and caused significantly fewer adverse events (P < 0.001) [[Twelves2005]].
- Xeloda label, X-ACT adjuvant table (capecitabine vs fluorouracil/leucovorin) [[FDA-Xeloda]]:

  | Adverse reaction | Capecitabine, all grades | Capecitabine, grade 3–4 | FU/LV, all grades | FU/LV, grade 3–4 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia (hand-foot syndrome) | 60% | 17% | 9% | <1% |
  | Diarrhoea | 47% | 12% | 65% | 14% |
  | Stomatitis | 22% | 2% | 60% | 14% |
- In the label's single-agent data (metastatic breast or colorectal cancer), median time to onset of grade 1–3 hand-foot syndrome was 2.6 months (range 11 days to 1 year) [[FDA-Xeloda]].
- In the same data, median time to the first grade 2–4 diarrhoea was 34 days and grade 3–4 diarrhoea lasted a median of 5 days [[FDA-Xeloda]].
- Patient-instruction wording in the label:
  - Stop capecitabine and call the care team for grade 2 hand-foot syndrome or worse.
  - Stop capecitabine for grade 2 diarrhoea ("an increase of 4 to 6 stools/day or nocturnal stools") or worse.
  - Stop immediately for grade 2 or higher dehydration.
  - Call promptly for fever ≥ 100.5°F [[FDA-Xeloda]].
- The label notes that persistent or severe hand-foot syndrome can lead to loss of fingerprints [[FDA-Xeloda]].
- In a network meta-analysis of HFS prevention trials, topical diclofenac had the best overall supporting evidence (grade ≥ 2 HFS OR 0.23, 95% CI 0.08–0.62). Celecoxib (OR 0.41) and 400 mg pyridoxine (OR 0.28) also lowered grade ≥ 2 HFS (Early evidence) [[Baskarane2026]].
- IDEA rates of grade 3–4 toxicity, 3 vs 6 months [[Grothey2018]]:

  | Toxicity (grade 3–4) | FOLFOX 3 mo | FOLFOX 6 mo | CAPOX 3 mo | CAPOX 6 mo |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Diarrhoea | 4.7% | 7.2% | 7.4% | 8.8% |
  | Neutropenia | 20.3% | 26.6% | 7.7% | 11.9% |
  | Mucositis | 0.7% | 1.6% | 0.3% | 0.9% |
  | Hand-foot syndrome | 0% | 0.3% | 0.7% | 2.6% |
- In MOSAIC, febrile neutropenia occurred in 1.8% of the FOLFOX4 arm [[Andre2004]].

### chemo · DPD deficiency and DPYD testing
- Fluoropyrimidines cause severe toxicity in up to 30% of patients. This is often due to reduced activity of dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD), mostly from DPYD variants [[Henricks2018]].
- CPIC: about 80% of a 5-fluorouracil dose is broken down by DPD, the rate-limiting catabolic enzyme. Only 1–3% becomes cytotoxic metabolites [[Amstutz2018]].
- CPIC: in Europeans, HapB3 (c.1129–5923C>G) is the commonest decreased-function variant (carrier frequency 4.1–4.8%). It is followed by c.1905+1G>A (DPYD*2A, 1–1.2%) and c.2846A>T (0.8–1.4%) [[Amstutz2018]].
- CPIC: about 7% of Europeans carry at least one of the four decreased-function variants. In people of African ancestry, c.557A>G has a carrier frequency of about 5% [[Amstutz2018]].
- CPIC phenotypes by DPYD activity score: normal metabolizer 2, intermediate metabolizer 1 or 1.5, poor metabolizer 0 or 0.5 [[Amstutz2018]].
- CPIC 2017 dosing, as published (not instructions) [[Amstutz2018]]:
  - Intermediate metabolizer, activity score 1: reduce the starting dose by 50% (strong).
  - Intermediate metabolizer, activity score 1.5: reduce by 25–50% (moderate).
  - Poor metabolizer: avoid 5-FU and its prodrugs. At activity score 0.5, if no alternative is suitable, a strongly reduced dose with early therapeutic drug monitoring may be considered.
- CPIC: after dose reduction, the dose should be increased in later cycles if the first two cycles are tolerated, so that efficacy is maintained [[Amstutz2018]].
- CPIC: testing the four main variants explains only 20–30% of early-onset 5-FU toxicities. A negative test does not rule out DPD deficiency [[Amstutz2018]].
- An IPD meta-analysis (8 studies, n = 7365) gave adjusted relative risks of severe toxicity of 4.40 for c.1679T>G, 1.59 for c.1236G>A/HapB3, 2.85 for DPYD*2A and 3.02 for c.2846A>T [[Meulendijks2015]].
- In a prospective Dutch study (1103 evaluable patients), 8% carried a DPYD variant and received genotype-guided starting doses (25% or 50% reduction). Severe toxicity was 39% in carriers vs 23% in wild-type patients [[Henricks2018]].
- With genotype-guided dosing, the relative risk of severe toxicity in DPYD*2A carriers fell to 1.31, compared with 2.87 in a historical full-dose cohort [[Henricks2018]].
- US labels (2025–2026) carry a **boxed warning**. They state: "Test patients for genetic variants of DPYD prior to initiating" capecitabine or fluorouracil "unless immediate treatment is necessary". They also say to avoid the drug in patients with certain homozygous or compound heterozygous variants that cause complete DPD deficiency [[FDA-Xeloda,FDA-FluorouracilFK]].
- The labels also say that no dose has been proven safe in complete DPD deficiency, and that data are insufficient to recommend a specific dose in partial deficiency [[FDA-Xeloda,FDA-FluorouracilFK]].
- The Xeloda label states that no FDA-authorized DPYD test is currently available, and that serious reactions can still occur when no variant is found [[FDA-Xeloda]].
- In 2020 the EMA recommended pre-treatment DPD testing by phenotyping (blood uracil) or DPYD genotyping. In a 2022 survey, 87% and 75% of responding countries reported more genotype and phenotype testing after the recommendation [[deWith2023]].

### colonoscopy · Bowel preparation teaching
- The 2025 USMSTF update replaces the 2014 recommendations [[Jacobson2025,Johnson2014]]. It recommends:
  - split-dose preparation, and suggests a 2 L regimen may be enough;
  - same-day preparation as an acceptable alternative for afternoon colonoscopy, but an inferior one for morning colonoscopy;
  - limiting diet restriction to the day before, using clear liquids or low-fibre/low-residue meals for breakfast and lunch;
  - adjunctive oral simethicone (suggested);
  - tracking adequate-prep rates, with a target above 90% per endoscopist and per unit [[Jacobson2025]].
- ESGE 2019 [[Hassan2019]]:
  - recommends a low-fibre diet the day before;
  - recommends enhanced instructions;
  - recommends split dosing (strong, high-quality evidence);
  - recommends starting the last dose within 5 hours of colonoscopy and finishing at least 2 hours before it.
- A meta-analysis of 47 RCTs (n = 13,487) found that split-dose regimens gave better cleansing than day-before regimens (OR 2.51, 95% CI 1.86–3.39). Patients were also more willing to repeat a split-dose prep (OR 1.90) [[Martel2015]].
- One day of low-residue diet was as effective as three days (adequate prep OR 0.89, 95% CI 0.65–1.21) and was better tolerated (OR 1.64). Constipation independently predicted inadequate prep (OR 1.98) [[Wang2024LRD]].
- Enhanced instructions (on top of standard ones) improved prep quality in 8 RCTs (n = 3795; OR 2.35, 95% CI 1.65–3.35) [[Guo2017]].
- Patient factors linked to inadequate prep (67 studies, 75,818 patients) [[Gandhi2018]]:
  - stroke or dementia (OR 2.09)
  - adjusted constipation (OR 1.97)
  - diabetes (OR 1.79)
  - opioid use (OR 1.70)
  - low education (OR 1.49)
  - adjusted cirrhosis (OR 3.41)
- Inadequate prep lowers detection of early adenomas (OR 0.53, 95% CI 0.46–0.62) more than advanced adenomas (OR 0.74) [[Sulz2016]].

### colonoscopy · Safety after the procedure
- Population-based pooled complication rates within 30 days of colonoscopy were 0.5 per 1000 for perforation, 2.6 per 1000 for bleeding and 2.9 per 100,000 for death [[Reumkens2016]].
- With polypectomy, perforation was 0.8 per 1000 and post-polypectomy bleeding 9.8 per 1000. Complications were less common after screening or surveillance than after diagnostic colonoscopy [[Reumkens2016]].

### nursing · ERAS
- ERAS replaces overnight fasting with carbohydrate drinks up to 2 hours before surgery. Its other elements:
  - minimally invasive surgery;
  - balanced rather than large-volume IV fluids;
  - avoiding or removing drains and tubes early;
  - early mobilisation;
  - drinks and food on the day of the operation [[Ljungqvist2017]].
- ERAS programmes have shortened hospital stay by 30–50%, with similar reductions in complications and no rise in readmissions (review) [[Ljungqvist2017]].
- An ERAS coordinator, often a nurse, is part of the implementation team [[Ljungqvist2017]].
- A meta-analysis of 16 RCTs (n = 2376) of colorectal ERAS found lower overall morbidity (RR 0.60, 95% CI 0.46–0.76), mainly fewer nonsurgical complications (RR 0.40). Hospital stay was 2.28 days shorter and readmissions did not rise [[Greco2014]].
- The ERAS Society 2018 colorectal guideline gives GRADE-rated recommendations for each item in the pathway [[Gustafsson2019]].

### living · Follow-up after cure-intent treatment
- ASCO's endorsement of the Cancer Care Ontario guideline [[Meyerhardt2013]]:
  - History, examination and CEA every 3–6 months for 5 years, toward the more frequent end for higher-risk patients.
  - CT chest/abdomen yearly for 3 years in most cases.
  - Colonoscopy 1 year after surgery, then every 5 years depending on findings.
  - No PET for routine surveillance.
- If colonoscopy was not done before diagnosis, it should be done after adjuvant therapy, before 1 year. Surveillance tests are not advised for people who could not have surgery or systemic therapy because of severe comorbidity [[Meyerhardt2013]].
- FACS (n = 1202, UK) [[Primrose2014]]:
  - Schedule: CEA every 3 months for 2 years then 6-monthly for 3 years; CT every 6 months for 2 years then yearly for 3 years.
  - Recurrence was found in 16.6% overall, and 5.9% were treated with curative intent.
  - Curative-intent surgery for recurrence: minimum follow-up 2.3%, CEA 6.7%, CT 8%, CEA + CT 6.6%.
- In FACS, deaths did not differ significantly between intensive monitoring (18.2%) and minimum follow-up (15.9%), and combining CEA with CT gave no advantage [[Primrose2014]].
- COLOFOL (n = 2509, stage II–III) compared CT + CEA at 6, 12, 18, 24 and 36 months with CT + CEA at 12 and 36 months [[WilleJorgensen2018]]:
  - 5-year overall mortality: 13.0% vs 14.1% (risk difference 1.1%, P = 0.43).
  - Colorectal cancer–specific mortality: 10.6% vs 11.4%.
  - Recurrence: 21.6% vs 19.4%.
- GILDA (n = 1228, Dukes B2–C): intensive imaging detected recurrence earlier (mean disease-free survival difference 5.9 months) but did not improve overall survival or quality of life [[Rosati2016]].
- More intensive follow-up finds recurrence earlier but has not been shown to lengthen life in three RCTs (Established) [[Primrose2014,WilleJorgensen2018,Rosati2016]].

### living · When recurrences happen
- Pooled data from 18 adjuvant colon trials (n = 20,898) [[Sargent2005]]:
  - Recurrence rates in years 1–5 were 12%, 14%, 8%, 5% and 3%.
  - **80% of recurrences occurred in the first 3 years.**
  - Median time from recurrence to death was 12 months (older fluorouracil-era trials).
- In 3655 Japanese trial patients with stage II–III disease [[Tashiro2026]]:
  - Recurrence hazard peaked at 10.0 months (stage II) and 12.8 months (stage III) after surgery.
  - Liver metastasis had the earliest peak (8.0 months).
  - 5-year relapse-free survival was 87.9% (stage II) and 77.0% (stage III).

### living · Exercise, diet and lifestyle after diagnosis
- CHALLENGE (phase 3, n = 889, resected colon cancer after adjuvant chemotherapy): a 3-year structured exercise programme vs health-education materials [[Courneya2025]]:
  - Disease-free survival improved: **HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.55–0.94)**; 5-year DFS 80.3% vs 73.9%.
  - Overall survival was consistent with benefit: **HR 0.63 (95% CI 0.43–0.94)**; 8-year OS 90.3% vs 83.2%.
- In CHALLENGE, musculoskeletal adverse events were more common with exercise (18.5% vs 11.5%) [[Courneya2025]].
- CHALLENGE is the first phase 3 trial showing that exercise after treatment improves cancer outcomes in colon cancer (Established for this population) [[Courneya2025]].
- In CALGB 89803 (observational, stage III, n = 832), activity of 18–26.9 MET-h/week (HR 0.51) or ≥ 27 MET-h/week (HR 0.55) about 6 months after chemotherapy was linked to better disease-free survival than < 3 MET-h/week [[Meyerhardt2006]].
- In the same cohort (n = 1009), the highest vs lowest quintile of a Western dietary pattern (meat, fat, refined grains, dessert) after diagnosis was associated with worse disease-free survival (adjusted HR 3.25, 95% CI 2.04–5.19). A prudent pattern showed no association (Early evidence; observational) [[Meyerhardt2007]].
- Following American Cancer Society lifestyle guidance (healthy weight, activity, vegetables, fruit, whole grains, less red/processed meat) was associated with lower mortality: score 5–6 vs 0–1, HR 0.58 (95% CI 0.34–0.99) [[VanBlarigan2018]].
- The ACS 2022 guideline gives cancer-survivor recommendations on body weight, physical activity, diet and alcohol, to reduce recurrence and mortality [[Rock2022]].
- ASCO lists keeping a healthy body weight and an active lifestyle as secondary prevention after colorectal cancer [[Meyerhardt2013]].

### living · Fertility and early-onset colorectal cancer
- In a single US centre, only 15.5% of 194 patients with early-onset colorectal cancer got fertility counselling. It was more likely for patients under 40 (OR 15.6) and women (OR 3.98), and less likely in lower-income areas [[Peng2025]].
- In an Italian survey of patients with early-onset CRC (n = 84), only 11.9% had discussed sexual issues with their oncologist and 45% had received fertility-preservation counselling [[Spring2025]].
- In that survey, persistent amenorrhoea after adjuvant chemotherapy was linked to age ≥ 45, and women reported more libido loss and sexual pain than men [[Spring2025]].
- Pelvic surgery can cause adhesions linked to female infertility and nerve damage causing ejaculatory problems. Counselling before treatment eases distress but is often missed in surgical clinics (review) [[Savitch2024]].

### living · Psychological distress, fear of recurrence, money, work, caregivers
- In PROFILES (n = 2625 survivors), depression symptoms were reported by 19.0% vs 12.8% of a normative sample, and anxiety by 20.9% vs 11.8%. Being single, low education and comorbidity were risk factors [[Mols2018]].
- In a review of 15 studies (93,805 patients), reported depression prevalence ranged from 1.6% to 57% and anxiety from 1.0% to 47.2%. Interviews by psychiatrists gave lower estimates [[Peng2019]].
- Across 54 studies of supportive care needs, emotional support for fear of recurrence was the most prominent need at every stage. Diet information, help with self-management and fatigue, and a trusted professional contact were other key needs [[Kotronoulas2017]].
- In 5515 long-term colorectal survivors, the Fear of Cancer Recurrence Inventory–Short Form worked as a screening tool, with an optimal cut-off of 22 for clinical fear of recurrence [[Ingeman2026]].
- A meta-analysis of 65 studies found financial toxicity is common in colorectal cancer. Pooled prevalence was 0.74 with the COST-PROM scale, 0.69 for catastrophic health expenditure and 0.31 with self-developed scales [[Shao2026]].
- Risk of financial toxicity was higher for women, people under 60, people with lower education, people with stage IV disease and, unexpectedly, insured patients (OR 1.88) [[Shao2026]].
- In 32,174 German working-age patients after rehabilitation, 31% were back at work within a month and 65% had returned to work for ≥ 6 months by 2 years [[Soff2026]].
- In that cohort, highest vs lowest income tertile gave aOR 3.06 for long-term return to work. The employment time lost between highest and lowest income was 10 months [[Soff2026]].
- In 185 Chinese family caregivers, caregiver burden rose and then fell, and was heaviest mid-chemotherapy. Almost 88% had moderate or high burden. The main burden shifted from the daily schedule to finances over time [[Wang2024CG]].

### living · Palliative care integration
- In metastatic NSCLC, early palliative care improved quality of life (FACT-L 98.0 vs 91.5) and reduced depressive symptoms (16% vs 38%). Fewer patients had aggressive end-of-life care (33% vs 54%), and median survival was 11.6 vs 8.9 months. This is the landmark trial, but it was not in GI cancer [[Temel2010]].
- In incurable lung and **non-colorectal** GI cancers (n = 350), early integrated palliative care improved quality of life at week 24 but not week 12. GI patients improved in both arms by week 12 [[Temel2017]].
- In the same trial, more intervention patients discussed their end-of-life wishes with their oncologist (30.2% vs 14.5%) [[Temel2017]].
- Caregivers in the same trial had lower total distress at week 12 (HADS adjusted difference −1.45) [[ElJawahri2017]].
- ALLAN (n = 118, advanced GI cancer, Sweden): early home-based specialised palliative care improved FACT-G quality of life by 13 points at week 24 (95% CI 5.7–20.2, P = 0.004) (Early evidence) [[Bojesson2024]].

### Exam hooks
- LARS score: 5 items, range 0–42. No LARS 0–20, minor 21–29, major 30–42 [[Emmertsen2012]].
- The heaviest LARS item is urgency (up to 16 points), then clustering (up to 11) [[Christensen2021]].
- Major LARS affects about 41% of patients after rectal cancer resection. Radiotherapy and a low anastomosis are the key risk factors [[Croese2018]].
- Parastomal hernia is commonest after end colostomy. Prophylactic mesh is strongly recommended for end colostomy [[Antoniou2018]].
- About 1 in 5 patients with a new ileostomy is readmitted within 30 days, and dehydration is the leading cause (6%) [[Vogel2022]].
- High-output stoma is roughly > 1.5–2 L/day. It drains water, sodium and magnesium [[Nightingale2022]].
- Acute oxaliplatin neuropathy is triggered by cold, starts within hours to 2 days and resolves within 14 days. Chronic neuropathy is cumulative and dose-related [[FDA-OxaliplatinGland]].
- Duloxetine is the only drug with evidence for painful CIPN. No drug is recommended for prevention [[Loprinzi2020]].
- Calcium/magnesium infusions do not prevent oxaliplatin neuropathy [[Loprinzi2014]].
- IDEA: 3 months of adjuvant oxaliplatin cut grade ≥ 2 neurotoxicity from about 45–48% to about 14–17% [[Grothey2018]].
- Capecitabine's hallmark toxicity is hand-foot syndrome: 60% any grade and 17% grade 3–4 in X-ACT [[FDA-Xeloda]].
- US fluoropyrimidine labels now carry a boxed warning to test for DPYD variants before starting unless treatment is urgent [[FDA-Xeloda,FDA-FluorouracilFK]].
- CPIC: DPYD activity score 1 → 50% starting-dose reduction. Activity score 0 → avoid fluoropyrimidines [[Amstutz2018]].
- About 7% of Europeans carry one of the four main decreased-function DPYD variants [[Amstutz2018]].
- Split-dose bowel prep is superior to day-before prep (OR 2.51) [[Martel2015]].
- The USMSTF target is more than 90% adequate bowel preps [[Jacobson2025]].
- Surveillance (ASCO): CEA every 3–6 months for 5 years, CT yearly for 3 years, colonoscopy at 1 year [[Meyerhardt2013]].
- About 80% of colon cancer recurrences happen within 3 years of surgery [[Sargent2005]].
- CHALLENGE: structured exercise after adjuvant chemotherapy improved DFS (HR 0.72) [[Courneya2025]].

### In plain words
- After rectal surgery, many people have urgent, frequent or clustered bowel movements. A short 5-question score (LARS) tells your team how bad it is, and there are treatments that help [[Emmertsen2012,Christensen2021]].
- A "temporary" stoma usually gets closed, but for some people it stays. Ask your surgeon about your own chance [[denDulk2007,Back2021]].
- With an ileostomy, watch your output. If the bag fills much more than usual (around 1.5 litres a day or more), or you feel very thirsty, dizzy or are passing little urine, call your team, because dehydration is the most common reason people come back to hospital [[Vogel2022,Messaris2012]].
- Having a stoma nurse mark the best spot on your belly before surgery, and learning bag care early, can mean fewer skin problems [[Salvadalena2015,Shi2025]].
- Oxaliplatin can make cold things feel like pins and needles in your hands, mouth or throat for a few days after each dose. Avoid cold drinks and ice and wear gloves outside [[FDA-OxaliplatinGland]].
- Numbness from oxaliplatin often gets better over months, but it does not always go away. Tell your team early if buttons, keys or walking get harder, because they can lower or stop the dose [[FDA-OxaliplatinGland,Loprinzi2020]].
- On capecitabine, stop the tablets and call your team if your hands or feet become red and sore enough to affect daily life, or if you have 4 or more extra bowel movements a day [[FDA-Xeloda]].
- Before 5-FU or capecitabine, a blood test for a gene (DPYD) can show if your body clears the drug slowly, so your dose can be adjusted [[FDA-Xeloda,Amstutz2018]].
- For a good bowel prep, split the laxative between the evening before and the morning of the test, eat light low-fibre food the day before, and follow the written instructions closely [[Jacobson2025,Martel2015]].
- After treatment, check-ups usually include blood tests every few months, scans once a year for a few years, and a colonoscopy after one year [[Meyerhardt2013]].
- Most recurrences that happen show up in the first 3 years, so that is when check-ups are closest together [[Sargent2005]].
- Regular exercise after chemotherapy helped people with colon cancer stay cancer-free longer in a large trial [[Courneya2025]].
- Worry about cancer coming back, money stress and feeling low are common. You can ask for help with each of them [[Kotronoulas2017,Mols2018,Shao2026]].
- If you are young and may want children, ask about fertility before treatment starts, because many people are never offered this talk [[Peng2025]].
- Palliative care is extra support for symptoms and planning. It can start early, alongside cancer treatment [[Temel2017,Bojesson2024]].

## Open questions and controversies
- **How intensive should follow-up be?** More intensive CEA/CT finds more recurrences that can be operated on [[Primrose2014]]. But three RCTs show no clear overall survival gain [[WilleJorgensen2018,Rosati2016,Primrose2014]], while guidelines still recommend CEA every 3–6 months and yearly CT [[Meyerhardt2013]]. Status: the schedule is a matter of resources and preference. Survival benefit, if any, is small.
- **Is LARS overestimated?** A score ≥ 30 is common even in people without rectal surgery (18.8% of women aged 50–79) [[Juul2019]], which may inflate prevalence estimates such as 41% [[Croese2018]]. Status: compare with normative data.
- **DPYD testing: genotype-only vs phenotype, and dose for HapB3/c.2846A>T.** Henricks found 25% reductions may be too small for c.2846A>T and c.1236G>A carriers [[Henricks2018]], while CPIC 2017 allows 25–50% for activity score 1.5 [[Amstutz2018]]. The US labels give no specific dose for partial deficiency and note that no FDA-authorized test is available [[FDA-Xeloda]]. EMA accepts either phenotyping or genotyping [[deWith2023]]. Status: evolving.
- **Does any intervention reduce high stoma output?** Common drugs (loperamide, omeprazole, somatostatin analogues) showed no significant pooled effect in low-quality trials [[Lederhuber2023]], yet expert practice uses them [[Nightingale2022]]. Status: unresolved.
- **Prophylactic mesh beyond end colostomy.** It is strongly recommended for end colostomy [[Antoniou2018]], with no recommendation for other stomas. Status: needs trials.
- **Early palliative care in colorectal cancer specifically.** The main trials excluded colorectal cancer [[Temel2017]] or were small GI-wide trials [[Bojesson2024]]. Status: evidence is extrapolated.
- **Diet after diagnosis.** Western-pattern associations are observational [[Meyerhardt2007]], unlike the randomised exercise evidence [[Courneya2025]]. Status: diet advice rests on cohort data.

## Provenance
- All 79 PubMed records were checked with E-utilities esummary in one batch (authors, title, journal, year, volume, pages, DOI, PMCID taken from the record). Abstracts were read with efetch for every paper a number was taken from.
- Full text was read through PMC/Europe PMC for:
  - Amstutz2018 (carrier frequencies, activity-score dosing table)
  - Grothey2018 (Table 3 adverse-event grades; the first column of that table pools grades 0–1, so only grade 2 and grade 3–4 figures are quoted)
  - Christensen2021 (the LARS questionnaire and points)
- 3 labels were validated on openFDA: Xeloda, fluorouracil (Fresenius Kabi; the same boxed warning appears on the Eugia, Sagent, Gland and Accord fluorouracil labels) and oxaliplatin (Gland Pharma). Some older generic fluorouracil labels in openFDA (for example Xiromed, effective 2026-02-19) do not yet carry the DPYD-testing boxed warning.
- Dropped, because they could not be traced to a readable source:
  - "inadequate prep in 20–25%"
  - CTCAE v5 grade tables (NCI PDF, no indexed record)
  - ASGE sedation guideline content (Early 2018, PMID 29306520, no abstract)
  - the 2021 WOCN/AUA/ASCRS statement content (PMID 34781309, no abstract)
  - the 2016 USMSTF post-resection colonoscopy intervals (Kahi 2016, PMID 26892199; abstract has no intervals)
  - the brief's ileostomy normal output volumes (no readable source)
- Stokes2017 abstract wording is ambiguous on which group had 44.7% vs 20.2% peristomal complications, so only the odds ratio (0.35) is quoted.
- Temel2017 and ElJawahri2017 excluded colorectal cancer. This is stated where they are cited.
- No facts are marked (unverified).
