# G · Metastatic disease & research toward a cure

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: metastatic, cure, biomarkers (drug-match side).

This pass covers metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC). It runs from where the cancer spreads, through surgery, ablation and liver-directed therapy, to the line-by-line drug sequence and biomarker-matched drugs. It also covers research aimed at cure: ctDNA-guided care, vaccines, newer RAS inhibitors, aspirin, exercise, early-onset CRC biology, neoadjuvant immunotherapy, blood-based screening and AI-assisted colonoscopy. Trial numbers come from PubMed or Europe PMC abstracts. Label wording comes from current US prescribing information in openFDA (effective dates 2024–2026). Approval dates come from openFDA Drugs@FDA submission records.

## Findings

### metastatic · How and where colorectal cancer spreads
- In a French population registry, 14.5% of people with CRC had liver metastases at diagnosis (synchronous). The 5-year cumulative rate of later (metachronous) liver metastases was also 14.5%: 3.7% after stage I, 13.3% after stage II and 30.4% after stage III [[Manfredi2006]].
- In the same registry, curative resection was done for 6.3% of synchronous and 16.9% of metachronous liver metastases. Five-year survival was 3.3% and 6.1%, respectively; these are population figures from an earlier treatment era [[Manfredi2006]].
- In 49,096 Swedish patients, rectal cancer spread more often to the chest organs (OR 2.4) and nervous system (OR 1.5) than colon cancer did, and less often to the peritoneum (OR 0.3) [[Riihimaki2016]].
- Mucinous and signet-ring adenocarcinomas spread more often within the peritoneum (OR 3.8 colon, 3.2 rectum) and less often to the liver (OR 0.5 and 0.6) than ordinary adenocarcinoma [[Riihimaki2016]].
- In rectal cancer diagnosed at a low stage, thoracic (lung) metastases are almost as common as liver metastases [[Riihimaki2016]].
- In the Netherlands from 1996 to 2011 (160,278 patients), the share diagnosed at stage IV rose from 19% to 23%, mainly from more detected lung metastases (1.7% to 5.0% of all CRC) [[vanderGeest2015]].
- Over the same Dutch period, median survival for all stage IV patients rose from 7 to 12 months. It rose from 9 to 16 months for liver-only disease and from 12 to 24 months for lung-only disease (population figures) [[vanderGeest2015]].
- In the US, 60% of new CRC cases in 2019 were diagnosed at an advanced stage, compared with 52% in the mid-2000s. Distant-stage incidence in people under 65 rose 0.5%–3% a year from about 2010 [[Siegel2023CRC]].

### metastatic · Resecting liver metastases: outcomes
- In a meta-analysis of 60 studies of liver resection for colorectal liver metastases, 5-year survival ranged from 16% to 74% (median 38%) and 10-year survival from 9% to 69% (median 26%). Median survival was 3.6 years [[Kanas2012]].
- Factors linked to worse survival after liver resection (meta-relative risks): positive margin 2.0; extrahepatic disease 1.9; CEA level 1.9; poor grade 1.9; node-positive primary 1.6; more than one metastasis 1.6; tumour over 3 cm 1.5 [[Kanas2012]].
- In 612 consecutive patients at one US centre followed for 10 years, 102 were actual 10-year survivors and 99 of those 102 (97%) were disease-free. About one-third of actual 5-year survivors later died of cancer [[Tomlinson2007]].
- The same series concluded there is "at least a one in six chance of cure" after hepatectomy in well-selected patients. Poor prognostic factors did not rule out cure [[Tomlinson2007]].
- EORTC 40983 randomised 364 patients with up to 4 resectable liver metastases to 6 cycles of FOLFOX4 before and 6 after surgery, or to surgery alone [[Nordlinger2008]].
- In EORTC 40983, 3-year PFS rose from 28.1% to 35.4% in all randomised patients (HR 0.79, 0.62–1.02; p=0.058). Among patients who were resected, it rose from 33.2% to 42.4% (HR 0.73, 0.55–0.97) [[Nordlinger2008]].
- Reversible postoperative complications were more common after chemotherapy (25% vs 16%; p=0.04) [[Nordlinger2008]].
- At 8.5 years' median follow-up, EORTC 40983 showed no overall survival difference. Five-year OS was 51.2% with perioperative FOLFOX4 vs 47.8% with surgery alone (HR 0.88, 0.68–1.14; p=0.34). Median OS was 61.3 vs 54.3 months [[Nordlinger2013]].
- The authors kept perioperative FOLFOX4 as the reference treatment because of the PFS benefit, despite no OS gain [[Nordlinger2013]].
- ASCO 2023: perioperative chemotherapy or surgery alone "should be offered" to people with mCRC who are candidates for potentially curative liver resection [[Morris2023]].
- In CAIRO5 (initially unresectable liver-only metastases, right-sided or RAS/BRAF-mutated), FOLFOXIRI-bevacizumab gave median PFS 10.6 vs 9.0 months with doublet-bevacizumab (HR 0.76, 0.60–0.98). Grade 3–4 neutropenia was 40% vs 13% and diarrhoea 19% vs 3% [[Bond2023]].
- In CAIRO5's left-sided RAS/BRAF wild-type group, adding panitumumab instead of bevacizumab to doublet chemotherapy gave no PFS benefit (10.4 vs 10.8 months; HR 1.11) and more skin toxicity (25% vs 1%) [[Bond2023]].
- In PARADIGM, panitumumab gave a higher curative (R0) resection rate than bevacizumab in left-sided RAS wild-type disease (18.3% vs 11.6%) [[Watanabe2023]].

### metastatic · Ablation, liver-directed therapy and liver transplant
- In COLLISION (300 patients with resectable colorectal liver metastases up to 3 cm), thermal ablation was non-inferior to surgical resection for overall survival (HR 1.05, 0.69–1.58). The trial stopped early after meeting its stopping rules [[vanderLei2025]].
- In COLLISION, ablation had fewer adverse events than surgery (19% vs 46%) and fewer serious adverse events (7% vs 20%). There were no treatment-related deaths after ablation and 3 (2%) after surgery [[vanderLei2025]].
- In the CLOCC phase 2 trial (119 patients with unresectable liver metastases), radiofrequency ablation with or without resection plus systemic therapy improved OS over systemic therapy alone (HR 0.58, 0.38–0.88) [[Ruers2017]].
- CLOCC 8-year OS was 35.9% with combined local plus systemic treatment vs 8.9% with systemic treatment alone. Median OS was 45.6 vs 40.5 months [[Ruers2017]].
- In a 1999 RCT after liver resection, hepatic arterial infusion (HAI) of floxuridine plus IV fluorouracil gave 2-year OS of 86% vs 72% with systemic therapy alone (p=0.03). Median survival was 72.2 vs 59.3 months [[Kemeny1999]].
- A 2022 meta-analysis of adjuvant HAI after liver resection (15 studies, 3584 patients) found a pooled OS HR of 0.77 (0.64–0.93). The pooled HR from RCTs alone was 0.91 (0.72–1.14), so the benefit rests mainly on non-randomised data [[Buisman2022]].
- ASCO 2023: stereotactic body radiation therapy "may be recommended" after systemic therapy for liver oligometastases not suitable for resection. Selective internal radiation therapy "is not routinely recommended" [[Morris2023]].
- In TransMet (94 patients with permanently unresectable, liver-only colorectal metastases), liver transplantation plus chemotherapy gave 5-year OS of 56.6% vs 12.6% with chemotherapy alone (HR 0.37, 0.21–0.65; intention to treat) [[Adam2024]].
- In TransMet's per-protocol population, 5-year OS was 73.3% with transplant vs 9.3% with chemotherapy alone. Serious adverse events occurred in 80% of transplanted patients [[Adam2024]].

### metastatic · Lung and peritoneal metastases
- PulMiCC randomised 65 patients with colorectal lung metastases to metastasectomy or continued active monitoring. Five-year survival was 38% vs 29%, and the HR for death was 0.82 (0.43–1.56) [[Treasure2019]].
- PulMiCC stopped early because of poor recruitment and cannot give a conclusive answer. It challenges the belief that lung metastasectomy adds about 35 percentage points of 5-year survival [[Treasure2019]].
- In PRODIGE 7 (265 patients with colorectal peritoneal metastases), adding oxaliplatin HIPEC to cytoreductive surgery did not improve OS. Median OS was 41.7 vs 41.2 months (HR 1.00) [[Quenet2021]].
- In PRODIGE 7, grade ≥3 adverse events at 60 days were more common with HIPEC (26% vs 15%; p=0.035) [[Quenet2021]].
- ASCO 2023: cytoreductive surgery plus systemic chemotherapy may be recommended for selected patients with peritoneal metastases, but adding HIPEC "is not recommended" [[Morris2023]].

### metastatic · Survival with modern therapy
- In a single-centre US cohort of 1420 people with de novo mCRC, median OS was 22.6 months for those diagnosed in 2004–2012, 28.8 months for 2013–2015 and 32.4 months for 2016–2019 [[Zeineddine2023]].
- In the same cohort, 5-year survival rose from 15.7% (diagnosed 2004–2006) to 26% (2013–2015) [[Zeineddine2023]].
- Factors linked to longer survival in that cohort: liver metastasis resection (HR 0.26), immunotherapy (HR 0.44) and third-line regorafenib or trifluridine/tipiracil (HR 0.74). Calendar year alone was not linked (HR 0.99) [[Zeineddine2023]].
- A 2019 Lancet seminar states that newer treatment options have "doubled overall survival for advanced disease to 3 years" [[Dekker2019]].
- Median OS in recent first-line RCTs is about 30 months or more. CALGB/SWOG 80405 reported 30.0 months (cetuximab) and 29.0 months (bevacizumab) [[Venook2017]]. TRIBE reported 29.8 months with FOLFOXIRI-bevacizumab [[Cremolini2015]]. PARADIGM reported 37.9 months with panitumumab in left-sided RAS wild-type disease [[Watanabe2023]].
- Trial medians come from selected, fitter patients and exceed population medians. Compare Dutch population median survival of 12 months for stage IV in 2008–2011 [[vanderGeest2015,Venook2017]].

### metastatic · First-line chemotherapy backbones and bevacizumab
- Hurwitz 2004 (813 patients): adding bevacizumab to IFL raised median OS from 15.6 to 20.3 months (HR 0.66; P<0.001) and median PFS from 6.2 to 10.6 months (HR 0.54) [[Hurwitz2004]].
- In Hurwitz 2004, response rates were 44.8% vs 34.8%, and grade 3 hypertension was 11.0% vs 2.3% [[Hurwitz2004]].
- TRIBE (508 patients): FOLFOXIRI plus bevacizumab gave median PFS of 12.1 vs 9.7 months with FOLFIRI plus bevacizumab (HR 0.75, 0.62–0.90). Response rate was 65% vs 53% [[Loupakis2014]].
- In TRIBE, grade 3–4 neurotoxicity, stomatitis, diarrhoea and neutropenia were significantly more common with the triplet [[Loupakis2014]].
- Updated TRIBE OS was 29.8 vs 25.8 months (HR 0.80, 0.65–0.98; p=0.03) [[Cremolini2015]].
- By molecular subgroup in TRIBE, median OS was 37.1 months for RAS/BRAF wild-type, 25.6 months for RAS-mutant and 13.4 months for BRAF-mutant tumours [[Cremolini2015]].
- TRIBE2 (679 patients): upfront FOLFOXIRI-bevacizumab with reintroduction after progression gave median PFS2 of 19.2 months, vs 16.4 months for mFOLFOX6-bevacizumab followed by FOLFIRI-bevacizumab (HR 0.74, 0.63–0.88) [[Cremolini2020]].
- TRIBE2 first-line grade 3–4 neutropenia was 50% vs 21% and diarrhoea 17% vs 5% (triplet vs doublet) [[Cremolini2020]].
- ASCO 2023: doublet chemotherapy "should be offered", or triplet therapy "may be offered", for previously untreated, initially unresectable mCRC [[Morris2023]].
- Avastin (bevacizumab) US label: indicated for mCRC with IV fluorouracil-based chemotherapy, first- or second-line. It is also indicated second-line with fluoropyrimidine-irinotecan or -oxaliplatin chemotherapy after progression on a first-line bevacizumab regimen. It "is not indicated for adjuvant treatment of colon cancer" [[FDA-Avastin]].
- The current Avastin label (effective 2025-01-06) carries no boxed warning [[FDA-Avastin]].

### metastatic · Anti-EGFR antibodies in RAS wild-type, left-sided disease
- CRYSTAL: in KRAS wild-type mCRC, adding cetuximab to FOLFIRI improved median OS (23.5 vs 20.0 months; HR 0.796), PFS (9.9 vs 8.4 months; HR 0.696) and response (57.3% vs 39.7%) [[VanCutsem2011]].
- In CRYSTAL, KRAS mutations were found in 37% of tumours, and BRAF mutation "was a strong indicator of poor prognosis" [[VanCutsem2011]].
- Extended RAS testing in CRYSTAL found other RAS mutations (KRAS exons 3–4, NRAS exons 2–4) in 14.7% of KRAS exon 2 wild-type tumours. These patients did not benefit from cetuximab. The authors called testing for all activating RAS mutations "essential" before anti-EGFR therapy [[VanCutsem2015]].
- FIRE-3 (592 patients, KRAS exon 2 wild-type): FOLFIRI-cetuximab and FOLFIRI-bevacizumab had similar response rates (62.0% vs 58.0%) and PFS (10.0 vs 10.3 months). Median OS was longer with cetuximab (28.7 vs 25.0 months; HR 0.77) [[Heinemann2014]].
- CALGB/SWOG 80405 (1137 patients, KRAS wild-type): no significant OS difference between cetuximab and bevacizumab with chemotherapy (30.0 vs 29.0 months; HR 0.88, 0.77–1.01) [[Venook2017]].
- PARADIGM (823 patients, RAS wild-type): in left-sided tumours, panitumumab plus mFOLFOX6 gave median OS of 37.9 vs 34.3 months with bevacizumab plus mFOLFOX6 (HR 0.82; P=0.03). Median PFS was similar (13.1 vs 11.9 months; HR 1.00) [[Watanabe2023]].
- In PARADIGM left-sided tumours, response rate was 80.2% vs 68.6%. Acneiform rash affected 74.8% of patients on panitumumab vs 3.2% on bevacizumab [[Watanabe2023]].
- ASCO 2023: for MSS/pMMR, left-sided, RAS wild-type mCRC, chemotherapy plus anti-EGFR therapy is recommended first-line. For right-sided RAS wild-type disease, chemotherapy plus anti-VEGF therapy is recommended [[Morris2023]].
- Erbitux (cetuximab) US label limitation of use: "not indicated for treatment of Ras-mutant colorectal cancer or when the results of the Ras mutation tests are unknown." The boxed warning covers serious and fatal infusion reactions and cardiopulmonary arrest [[FDA-Erbitux]].
- Vectibix (panitumumab) US label: indicated first-line with FOLFOX for adults with RAS wild-type mCRC (wild-type in both KRAS and NRAS). Before use, RAS status must be confirmed across KRAS and NRAS exons 2, 3 and 4 [[FDA-Vectibix]].
- Vectibix boxed warning: dermatologic toxicities occurred in 90% of patients and were severe (grade ≥3) in 15% on monotherapy [[FDA-Vectibix]].

### biomarkers · MSI-H/dMMR: immunotherapy
- KEYNOTE-177 (307 patients, first-line MSI-H/dMMR mCRC): pembrolizumab gave median PFS of 16.5 vs 8.2 months with chemotherapy (HR 0.60, 0.45–0.80; P=0.0002) [[Andre2020]].
- In KEYNOTE-177, grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events were 22% with pembrolizumab vs 66% with chemotherapy. Among responders, 83% vs 35% had ongoing responses at 24 months [[Andre2020]].
- At KEYNOTE-177 final analysis, median OS was not reached with pembrolizumab vs 36.7 months with chemotherapy (HR 0.74, 0.53–1.03). This missed the prespecified significance threshold; 60% of the chemotherapy group crossed over to anti-PD-1/PD-L1 therapy [[Diaz2022]].
- CheckMate 142 (single arm, 45 patients, first-line): nivolumab plus low-dose ipilimumab gave a 69% response rate with 13% complete responses. 24-month PFS was 74% and 24-month OS 79% [[Lenz2022]].
- CheckMate 8HW (first-line, centrally confirmed MSI-H/dMMR): 24-month PFS was 72% with nivolumab plus ipilimumab vs 14% with chemotherapy. Grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events were 23% vs 48% [[Andre2024]].
- CheckMate 8HW (all lines): nivolumab plus ipilimumab improved PFS vs nivolumab alone (HR 0.62, 0.48–0.81). Median PFS was not reached vs 39.3 months [[Andre2025]].
- CheckMate 8HW final analysis (median follow-up 68.0 months): nivolumab plus ipilimumab improved OS vs chemotherapy first-line (HR 0.37, 0.24–0.57) and vs nivolumab across all lines (HR 0.61, 0.46–0.82). First-line PFS HR vs chemotherapy was 0.21 [[Lonardi2026]].
- Keytruda (pembrolizumab) US label: indicated for adults with unresectable or metastatic MSI-H or dMMR CRC "as determined by an FDA-authorized test" [[FDA-Keytruda]].
- Opdivo and Yervoy US labels: nivolumab with ipilimumab is indicated for adults and children 12 years and older with unresectable or metastatic MSI-H or dMMR CRC. Nivolumab alone is indicated after progression on a fluoropyrimidine, oxaliplatin and irinotecan [[FDA-Opdivo,FDA-Yervoy]].
- Labelled CRC regimen: nivolumab 240 mg plus ipilimumab 1 mg/kg every 3 weeks for up to 4 doses, then nivolumab 240 mg every 2 weeks or 480 mg every 4 weeks (adults and children ≥40 kg). This is label wording, not dosing advice [[FDA-Opdivo,FDA-Yervoy]].
- Drugs@FDA records priority efficacy supplements for both Opdivo and Yervoy on 2025-04-08, matching the first-line CRC combination approval. It records Keytruda efficacy supplements in June 2020, including 2020-06-29 (unverified mapping) [[FDA-DrugsAtFDA]].
- ASCO 2023 (written before CheckMate 8HW) recommended first-line pembrolizumab for MSI-H/dMMR mCRC [[Morris2023]].

### biomarkers · BRAF V600E
- BEACON (665 patients, BRAF V600E mCRC after 1–2 prior regimens): the paper notes median OS of 4–6 months after failure of initial therapy historically [[Kopetz2019]].
- In BEACON, median OS was 9.0 months with encorafenib-binimetinib-cetuximab, 8.4 months with encorafenib-cetuximab and 5.4 months with control. HRs were 0.52 (triplet) and 0.60 (doublet). Confirmed response was 26% (triplet) vs 2% (control) [[Kopetz2019]].
- BREAKWATER first report (first-line BRAF V600E): response rate was 60.9% with encorafenib-cetuximab-mFOLFOX6 vs 40.0% with standard care (OR 2.443). The first interim OS HR was 0.47 [[Kopetz2025]].
- BREAKWATER's response result "led to accelerated Food and Drug Administration approval" of the combination, including first-line [[Elez2025]]. Drugs@FDA records a priority efficacy supplement for Braftovi on 2024-12-20 [[FDA-DrugsAtFDA]].
- BREAKWATER PFS and OS: encorafenib-cetuximab-mFOLFOX6 gave median PFS of 12.8 vs 7.1 months (HR 0.53, 0.41–0.68). Median OS at interim was 30.3 vs 15.1 months (HR 0.49, 0.38–0.63) [[Elez2025]].
- BREAKWATER cohort 3 (147 patients): encorafenib-cetuximab-FOLFIRI gave response rate 64.4% vs 39.2% and median PFS 15.2 vs 8.3 months (HR 0.44). The OS HR was 0.56 [[Kopetz2026]].
- Braftovi (encorafenib) US label: indicated with cetuximab and fluorouracil-based chemotherapy for adults with BRAF V600E mCRC "as detected by an FDA-authorized test". BRAF status may be confirmed in plasma or tumour tissue [[FDA-Braftovi]].
- Labelled Braftovi CRC dose: 300 mg orally once daily, with cetuximab plus mFOLFOX6 or FOLFIRI, or with weekly cetuximab (label wording) [[FDA-Braftovi]].
- Erbitux label: indicated with encorafenib for adults with BRAF V600E mCRC "after prior therapy" [[FDA-Erbitux]].

### biomarkers · HER2-positive
- HERACLES: 48 of 914 (5%) KRAS exon 2 wild-type mCRCs were HER2-positive. Trastuzumab plus lapatinib gave a 30% response rate in 27 refractory patients [[SartoreBianchi2016]].
- MOUNTAINEER (chemotherapy-refractory, HER2-positive, RAS wild-type): tucatinib plus trastuzumab gave a confirmed response rate of 38.1% (84 patients). Diarrhoea was the most common adverse event (64%) [[Strickler2023]].
- MOUNTAINEER final analysis (median follow-up 32.4 months): response rate 39.3%, median duration of response 15.2 months, median PFS 8.1 months and median OS 23.9 months [[Strickler2026]].
- Tukysa (tucatinib) US label: with trastuzumab for RAS wild-type, HER2-positive unresectable or metastatic CRC after fluoropyrimidine, oxaliplatin and irinotecan. This is an accelerated approval "based on tumor response rate and durability of response" [[FDA-Tukysa]]. Drugs@FDA records the efficacy supplement on 2023-01-19 [[FDA-DrugsAtFDA]].
- DESTINY-CRC01: trastuzumab deruxtecan gave a 45.3% response rate in HER2-positive refractory mCRC (53 patients). Two of five interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis cases were fatal [[Siena2021]].
- DESTINY-CRC02: at 5.4 mg/kg, response rate was 37.8% (31/82), and drug-related ILD or pneumonitis occurred in 8% (all grade 1–2). The authors chose 5.4 mg/kg as the optimal dose [[Raghav2024]].
- Enhertu US label: tumour-agnostic accelerated approval for unresectable or metastatic HER2-positive (IHC 3+) solid tumours after prior systemic treatment. Boxed warning covers interstitial lung disease and embryo-fetal toxicity [[FDA-Enhertu]].
- MOUNTAINEER-03 (NCT05253651) is a recruiting phase 3 trial of first-line tucatinib plus trastuzumab plus mFOLFOX6, estimated n=400 [[NCT05253651]].

### biomarkers · KRAS G12C
- In a US cohort of 1347 colorectal carcinomas, KRAS G12C (c.34G>T) occurred in 3.2% and other KRAS mutations in 35%. KRAS G12C was linked to higher CRC-specific mortality than KRAS wild-type (HR 1.82) [[Ugai2024]].
- KRYSTAL-1: adagrasib alone gave a 19% response rate, and adagrasib plus cetuximab gave 46% (28 patients), in pretreated KRAS G12C mCRC [[Yaeger2023]].
- KRYSTAL-1 expanded cohort (94 patients): adagrasib plus cetuximab gave a 34.0% response rate, median PFS 6.9 months and median OS 15.9 months [[Yaeger2024]].
- CodeBreaK 300 (160 patients, chemorefractory KRAS G12C): sotorasib 960 mg plus panitumumab gave median PFS 5.6 vs 2.2 months with trifluridine/tipiracil or regorafenib (HR 0.49, 0.30–0.80). Response was 26.4% vs 0% [[Fakih2023]].
- CodeBreaK 300 OS (not adequately powered): HR 0.70 (0.41–1.18; P=0.20) for sotorasib 960 mg plus panitumumab vs investigator's choice. Updated response was 30.2% vs 1.9% [[Pietrantonio2025]].
- Lumakras (sotorasib) US label: 960 mg once daily with panitumumab for KRAS G12C-mutated mCRC after fluoropyrimidine, oxaliplatin and irinotecan chemotherapy [[FDA-Lumakras]]. Drugs@FDA records this efficacy supplement on 2025-01-16 [[FDA-DrugsAtFDA]].
- Krazati (adagrasib) US label: with cetuximab for KRAS G12C-mutated locally advanced or metastatic CRC after fluoropyrimidine, oxaliplatin and irinotecan. This is an accelerated approval based on response rate and duration of response [[FDA-Krazati]]. Drugs@FDA records the efficacy supplement on 2024-06-21 [[FDA-DrugsAtFDA]].

### biomarkers · NTRK fusions (rare, tumour-agnostic)
- Larotrectinib across 17 TRK fusion-positive tumour types (55 patients) gave a 75% independently reviewed response rate. At 1 year, 71% of responses were ongoing [[Drilon2018]].
- Entrectinib in an integrated analysis (54 adults, 10 tumour types) gave a 57% response rate and a median duration of response of 10 months [[Doebele2020]].
- Vitrakvi (larotrectinib) US label: for solid tumours with an NTRK gene fusion that are metastatic or where surgery is likely to cause severe morbidity, with no satisfactory alternative. Labelled adult dose: 100 mg orally twice daily [[FDA-Vitrakvi]].

### metastatic · Later lines (refractory disease)
- CORRECT (760 patients): regorafenib gave median OS 6.4 vs 5.0 months with placebo (HR 0.77, 0.64–0.94). Grade ≥3 hand-foot skin reaction occurred in 17% [[Grothey2013]].
- Stivarga (regorafenib) label boxed warning: "Severe and sometimes fatal hepatotoxicity has occurred in clinical trials"; monitor liver function before and during treatment [[FDA-Stivarga]].
- RECOURSE: trifluridine/tipiracil (TAS-102) gave median OS 7.1 vs 5.3 months with placebo (HR 0.68, 0.58–0.81). Neutropenia occurred in 38% [[Mayer2015]].
- SUNLIGHT (492 patients): trifluridine/tipiracil plus bevacizumab gave median OS 10.8 vs 7.5 months with trifluridine/tipiracil alone (HR 0.61, 0.49–0.77). Median PFS was 5.6 vs 2.4 months (HR 0.44) [[Prager2023]].
- PRABITAS (970 patients, Japan): a 14-day schedule of trifluridine/tipiracil plus bevacizumab was non-inferior to the standard 28-day schedule (median OS 11.3 vs 10.3 months; HR 0.93). Grade ≥3 neutropenia fell from 39% to 13% [[Sakakida2026]].
- Lonsurf US label: alone or with bevacizumab for mCRC previously treated with fluoropyrimidine, oxaliplatin and irinotecan chemotherapy, an anti-VEGF agent and (if RAS wild-type) an anti-EGFR agent. Labelled dose: 35 mg/m² up to 80 mg per dose twice daily on days 1–5 and 8–12 of each 28-day cycle [[FDA-Lonsurf]].
- FRESCO-2 (691 patients, median 4 prior lines): fruquintinib gave median OS 7.4 vs 4.8 months with placebo (HR 0.66, 0.55–0.80). Grade ≥3 hypertension occurred in 14% [[Dasari2023]].
- Fruzaqla (fruquintinib) US label: for previously treated mCRC (same prior-therapy wording as Lonsurf, with anti-EGFR "if RAS wild-type and medically appropriate"). First FDA approval: 2023-11-08 [[FDA-Fruzaqla,FDA-DrugsAtFDA]].

### metastatic · Continuum of care
- ASCO 2023 recommends multidisciplinary team management and shared decision-making for mCRC [[Morris2023]].
- The order of first-line drugs depends on biomarkers. MSI-H/dMMR goes to checkpoint inhibitors. BRAF V600E goes to encorafenib-cetuximab-based therapy. Left-sided RAS wild-type goes to chemotherapy plus anti-EGFR. Others get chemotherapy plus anti-VEGF. HER2, KRAS G12C and NTRK guide later-line choices [[Morris2023,Elez2025,FDA-Tukysa,FDA-Lumakras,FDA-Vitrakvi]].
- Each refractory-line drug adds a median of about 1.4 to 3.3 months of OS over its comparator. Regorafenib +1.4, trifluridine/tipiracil +1.8, fruquintinib +2.6 and trifluridine/tipiracil plus bevacizumab +3.3 months (arithmetic from trial medians) [[Grothey2013,Mayer2015,Dasari2023,Prager2023]].
- Median OS rose 22.6 → 28.8 → 32.4 months across eras in one cohort. The authors tie this to more liver resection, immunotherapy and third-line drugs rather than calendar year [[Zeineddine2023]].

### cure · ctDNA and molecular residual disease (MRD)
- DYNAMIC (455 patients, stage II colon cancer): ctDNA-guided care cut adjuvant chemotherapy use to 15% vs 28% with standard care. Two-year RFS was non-inferior (93.5% vs 92.4%; difference 1.1 points, 95% CI −4.1 to 6.2) (Established) [[Tie2022]].
- In DYNAMIC, 3-year RFS was 86.4% for ctDNA-positive patients given chemotherapy and 92.5% for ctDNA-negative patients not given chemotherapy [[Tie2022]].
- DYNAMIC-III (968 evaluable stage III colon cancer patients): 72.5% were ctDNA-negative after surgery. Three-year RFS was 87% for ctDNA-negative vs 49% for ctDNA-positive patients [[Tie2025]].
- In DYNAMIC-III ctDNA-negative patients, de-escalation cut oxaliplatin use (34.8% vs 88.6%) and hospitalisation (8.5% vs 13.2%). RFS was slightly lower (85.3% vs 88.1%) and missed the non-inferiority margin (Early evidence) [[Tie2025]].
- In DYNAMIC-III ctDNA-positive patients, escalated chemotherapy did not improve 2-year RFS over standard therapy (51% vs 61%). Three-year RFS fell from 77% to 23% across ctDNA-burden quartiles [[Tie2025]].
- GALAXY / CIRCULATE-Japan (1039 patients, stage II–IV resectable CRC): ctDNA positivity 4 weeks after surgery carried an HR of 10.0 for recurrence [[Kotani2023]].
- Updated GALAXY (2240 patients): MRD-window ctDNA positivity carried HR 11.99 for DFS and 9.68 for OS. Sustained vs transient ctDNA clearance on adjuvant chemotherapy gave 24-month DFS of 89.0% vs 3.3% [[Nakamura2024]].
- In GALAXY, true spontaneous ctDNA clearance without recurrence was rare (1.9%, 2/105) [[Nakamura2024]].
- ALTAIR (243 patients, ctDNA-positive after standard therapy with no radiological disease): 6 months of trifluridine/tipiracil did not significantly improve DFS (median 9.30 vs 5.55 months; HR 0.79, 0.60–1.05; P=0.107). Grade ≥3 haematological adverse events were 73.0% vs 3.3% [[Bando2026]].
- Taken together: ctDNA is a strong prognostic marker (Established). Using it to de-escalate in stage II is supported by one RCT (Established for that setting). Escalation or early treatment for ctDNA-positive disease has not yet improved outcomes (Early evidence) [[Tie2022,Tie2025,Bando2026]].
- CIRCULATE-US (NCT05174169) is a recruiting phase 2/3 ctDNA-guided adjuvant colon cancer trial, estimated n=1912 [[NCT05174169]].

### cure · Cancer vaccines and next-generation RAS drugs
- AMPLIFY-201 (phase 1, 25 patients: 20 pancreatic, 5 colorectal, all with residual mutant-KRAS disease): the lymph-node-targeted G12D/G12R amphiphile vaccine ELI-002 2P induced mKRAS-specific T cells in 84% (Early evidence) [[Pant2024]].
- In AMPLIFY-201, biomarker clearance occurred in 24% (3 pancreatic, 3 colorectal). Median RFS was 16.33 months [[Pant2024]].
- AMPLIFY-201 final results (median follow-up 19.7 months): patients with strong T-cell responses had median RFS not reached vs 3.02 months (HR 0.12) and median OS not reached vs 15.98 months (HR 0.23). These are exploratory, non-randomised comparisons (Early evidence) [[Wainberg2025]].
- MRTX1133, a non-covalent KRAS G12D inhibitor, caused tumour regression in preclinical models. Co-targeting EGFR or PI3Kα enhanced its activity (Hypothesis for patients; preclinical) [[Hallin2022]].
- RMC-7977, a RAS(ON) multi-selective inhibitor, was active in preclinical models across RAS genotypes, including G12C-inhibitor-resistant models (preclinical) [[Holderfield2024]]. The related clinical compound RMC-6236 (daraxonrasib) is in a recruiting phase 1/2 trial in RAS-mutant solid tumours (NCT05379985; estimated n=754) (Early evidence) [[NCT05379985]].

### cure · Aspirin, exercise and other adjuvant add-ons
- ASCOLT (1550 patients, Dukes B/C colon or rectal cancer): aspirin 200 mg daily for 3 years after standard adjuvant therapy did not improve 5-year DFS (77.0% vs 74.8%; HR 0.91, 0.73–1.13) (Established null result) [[Chia2025]].
- ALASCCA (patients with PI3K-pathway-altered localised CRC): low-dose aspirin cut 3-year recurrence to 7.7% vs 14.1% with placebo among those with PIK3CA exon 9/20 hotspot mutations (HR 0.49, 0.24–0.98) (Early evidence: single positive RCT) [[Martling2025]].
- In ALASCCA, other PI3K-pathway alterations showed 3-year recurrence of 7.7% vs 16.8% (HR 0.42). Severe adverse events were 16.8% vs 11.6% [[Martling2025]].
- Add-Aspirin (NCT02804815) is a large phase 3 trial (estimated n=11,000) across several non-metastatic cancers, including colorectal. It is active, not recruiting, and no colorectal efficacy results were found [[NCT02804815]].
- CHALLENGE (889 patients after adjuvant chemotherapy for colon cancer): a 3-year structured exercise programme improved DFS (HR 0.72, 0.55–0.94). Five-year DFS was 80.3% vs 73.9% with health education (Established; see area H for survivorship detail) [[Courneya2025]].
- In CHALLENGE, 8-year OS was 90.3% vs 83.2% (HR 0.63). Musculoskeletal adverse events were 18.5% vs 11.5% [[Courneya2025]].

### cure · Neoadjuvant immunotherapy and organ preservation in dMMR disease
- NICHE-2 (locally advanced dMMR colon cancer, 111 patients in efficacy analysis): neoadjuvant nivolumab plus ipilimumab gave a major pathological response (≤10% residual viable tumour) in 95% and a pathological complete response in 68% (Early evidence: single-arm) [[Chalabi2024]].
- In NICHE-2, 98% had timely surgery and 4% had grade 3–4 immune-related adverse events. No patient had recurrence at 26 months' median follow-up [[Chalabi2024]].
- In the first 12 patients with dMMR locally advanced rectal cancer, dostarlimab gave a clinical complete response in 100%, with no chemoradiotherapy or surgery at report [[Cercek2022]].
- The expanded dostarlimab study (117 patients with early-stage dMMR solid tumours): all 49 who completed treatment in the rectal cohort had a clinical complete response. Two-year RFS across all 117 was 92% (Early evidence) [[Cercek2025]].
- OPRA (total neoadjuvant therapy for rectal adenocarcinoma, mostly pMMR): 3-year TME-free survival (organ preservation) was 53% with consolidation chemotherapy and 41% with induction chemotherapy. Three-year DFS was 76% in both arms [[GarciaAguilar2022]].

### cure · Early-onset CRC biology
- In the US, CRC incidence in people under 55 has risen 1%–2% a year since the mid-1990s. Their share of cases rose from 11% (1995) to 20% (2019) [[Siegel2023CRC]].
- Exposing human intestinal organoids to genotoxic pks+ E. coli produced a distinct colibactin mutational signature, which was also found in human cancer genomes, mostly CRC (Established mechanism in organoids) [[PleguezuelosManzano2020]].
- In 981 CRC genomes from 11 countries, colibactin signatures SBS88 and ID18 were 3.3 times more common in people diagnosed before age 40 than in those over 70. They were acquired early in tumour development (Early evidence) [[DiazGay2025]].
- ID18 accounted for about 25% of APC driver indels in colibactin-positive cases. The authors suggest early-life colibactin exposure "may contribute" to the rise in early-onset CRC (Hypothesis) [[DiazGay2025]].

### cure · Earlier detection technology (research landscape)
- ECLIPSE (7861 evaluable average-risk adults): a cfDNA blood test detected 83.1% of CRCs and 87.5% of stage I–III CRCs. It detected only 13.2% of advanced precancerous lesions, with 89.6% specificity for advanced neoplasia (Established performance; see area D for screening) [[Chung2024]].
- An early meta-analysis of 5 RCTs (4354 patients) found computer-aided detection (CADe) raised adenoma detection rate from 25.2% to 36.6% (RR 1.44, 1.27–1.62) [[Hassan2021]].
- A larger meta-analysis (44 RCTs, 36,201 cases) found CADe raised ADR from 36.7% to 44.7% (RR 1.21, 1.15–1.28). Advanced neoplasia per colonoscopy was unchanged (0.16 vs 0.15) and the advanced-neoplasia detection rate rose only slightly (12.7% vs 11.5%) [[Soleymanjahi2024]].
- CADe led to removal of almost 2 extra non-neoplastic polyps per 10 colonoscopies and added about 0.5 minutes of withdrawal time. Whether higher ADR from CADe lowers cancer deaths is not yet shown (Early evidence) [[Soleymanjahi2024]].

### Exam hooks
- RAS (KRAS and NRAS, exons 2–4) testing comes before any anti-EGFR antibody. RAS-mutant tumours do not benefit [[VanCutsem2015,FDA-Erbitux]].
- Anti-EGFR first-line is for left-sided, RAS wild-type, MSS tumours. PARADIGM OS was 37.9 vs 34.3 months [[Watanabe2023,Morris2023]].
- Bevacizumab's first pivotal trial: OS 20.3 vs 15.6 months with IFL (HR 0.66) [[Hurwitz2004]].
- MSI-H/dMMR mCRC first-line: pembrolizumab doubled median PFS (16.5 vs 8.2 months) [[Andre2020]].
- Nivolumab plus ipilimumab first-line: 24-month PFS 72% vs 14% with chemotherapy [[Andre2024]].
- BRAF V600E: encorafenib plus cetuximab (an anti-EGFR antibody is needed because BRAF inhibition alone is undone by EGFR feedback) [[Kopetz2019]].
- BREAKWATER first-line: median OS 30.3 vs 15.1 months [[Elez2025]].
- HER2-positive mCRC: tucatinib plus trastuzumab, response about 38% [[Strickler2023]].
- KRAS G12C mCRC: G12C inhibitor plus anti-EGFR antibody (sotorasib-panitumumab or adagrasib-cetuximab) [[Fakih2023,Yaeger2023]].
- Refractory mCRC options: regorafenib, trifluridine/tipiracil (± bevacizumab), fruquintinib [[Grothey2013,Prager2023,Dasari2023]].
- Regorafenib boxed warning: hepatotoxicity [[FDA-Stivarga]]. Panitumumab boxed warning: dermatologic toxicity [[FDA-Vectibix]]. Trastuzumab deruxtecan boxed warning: ILD [[FDA-Enhertu]].
- EORTC 40983: perioperative FOLFOX improved PFS but not OS for resectable liver metastases [[Nordlinger2013]].
- About one in six people undergoing hepatectomy for colorectal liver metastases are cured (actual 10-year survivors) [[Tomlinson2007]].
- PRODIGE 7: HIPEC added to cytoreductive surgery gave no OS benefit [[Quenet2021]].
- DYNAMIC: ctDNA-guided care halved adjuvant chemotherapy use in stage II colon cancer (15% vs 28%) without worse 2-year RFS [[Tie2022]].
- NICHE-2: 95% major pathological response with neoadjuvant nivolumab plus ipilimumab in dMMR colon cancer [[Chalabi2024]].
- Rectal cancer spreads to the lung more often than colon cancer does (OR 2.4) [[Riihimaki2016]].
- CHALLENGE: structured exercise after adjuvant chemotherapy, DFS HR 0.72 [[Courneya2025]].

### In plain words
- When colorectal cancer spreads, it most often goes to the liver. It can also spread to the lungs or the lining of the belly [[Riihimaki2016,Manfredi2006]].
- Spread to the liver does not always mean the cancer cannot be cured. For some people, surgery to remove the liver spots leads to long-term cure [[Tomlinson2007]].
- Today, small liver spots can sometimes be destroyed with heat (ablation) instead of surgery, with fewer side effects [[vanderLei2025]].
- Doctors test the tumour for markers such as RAS, BRAF, HER2 and MSI/MMR. The results help choose the medicines most likely to work [[Morris2023]].
- If the tumour is MSI-high or dMMR, immunotherapy often works better and has fewer serious side effects than chemotherapy [[Andre2020,Andre2024]].
- On average, people with metastatic colorectal cancer now live longer than 20 years ago. These are group averages; one person's outlook can be very different [[Zeineddine2023]].
- After the main treatments stop working, other pills and infusions can still add months of life [[Prager2023,Dasari2023]].
- A blood test for tumour DNA after surgery can show who is at high risk of the cancer coming back [[Nakamura2024]].
- In one large trial, that blood test helped some people with stage II colon cancer safely skip chemotherapy [[Tie2022]].
- For people with dMMR tumours, immunotherapy before surgery can make the tumour disappear, and some people may be able to avoid surgery [[Chalabi2024,Cercek2025]].
- Regular exercise after chemotherapy for colon cancer lowered the chance of the cancer coming back in a large trial [[Courneya2025]].
- Vaccines aimed at KRAS mutations are being tested. Results so far come from small early studies [[Pant2024]].
- A computer helper during colonoscopy helps doctors find more small polyps. Whether this saves more lives is not yet known [[Soleymanjahi2024]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Perioperative chemotherapy for resectable liver metastases: PFS benefit and kept as the reference treatment [[Nordlinger2013]]; no OS benefit, so ASCO allows "perioperative chemotherapy or surgery alone" [[Morris2023]]. Status: individualised.
- Triplet vs doublet first-line: FOLFOXIRI-bevacizumab improves PFS and PFS2 [[Loupakis2014,Cremolini2020]], with substantially more neutropenia and diarrhoea and more treatment-related deaths in CAIRO5's triplet arm [[Bond2023]]. Status: for fit, selected patients.
- Anti-EGFR vs anti-VEGF in left-sided RAS wild-type disease: PARADIGM showed an OS benefit for panitumumab [[Watanabe2023]]. CAIRO5 (liver-only, conversion intent) showed no PFS benefit and more toxicity [[Bond2023]]. CALGB 80405 showed no OS difference overall [[Venook2017]]. Status: anti-EGFR preferred for left-sided disease by ASCO; context matters.
- Ablation vs resection for small liver metastases: COLLISION supports non-inferiority with fewer complications [[vanderLei2025]]. Longer-term recurrence data are still needed.
- Lung metastasectomy: widely practised, but PulMiCC's small RCT found a smaller survival gap than assumed (38% vs 29% at 5 years) [[Treasure2019]]. Status: unresolved.
- HAI: one RCT showed a 2-year OS benefit [[Kemeny1999]], but the pooled RCT HR was not significant (0.91) [[Buisman2022]]. Status: done at expert centres.
- Liver transplant for unresectable liver-only disease: large OS gain in TransMet [[Adam2024]], but in highly selected patients and needing organs from a scarce pool. Status: emerging option.
- Pembrolizumab vs nivolumab plus ipilimumab first-line in MSI-H/dMMR: no head-to-head trial. CheckMate 8HW showed dual blockade beats nivolumab alone on PFS and OS [[Andre2025,Lonardi2026]]. KEYNOTE-177 OS did not reach significance with crossover [[Diaz2022]]. Status: both labelled.
- ctDNA escalation: being ctDNA-positive predicts recurrence, but more chemotherapy (DYNAMIC-III) or early trifluridine/tipiracil (ALTAIR) did not help [[Tie2025,Bando2026]]. Status: new strategies needed.
- ctDNA de-escalation in stage III: fewer toxic treatments, but RFS was slightly lower and missed non-inferiority [[Tie2025]]. Status: not standard.
- Aspirin after CRC: no benefit unselected [[Chia2025]]; benefit in PI3K-altered tumours in one RCT [[Martling2025]]. Status: biomarker-selected use under discussion.
- CADe: raises ADR but barely changes advanced neoplasia detection and adds non-neoplastic polypectomies [[Soleymanjahi2024]]. Status: effect on CRC deaths unknown.
- Early-onset CRC: colibactin signatures are enriched in younger patients [[DiazGay2025]]. Whether this explains the rise is a hypothesis.

- Two abstracts were read through Europe PMC because the PubMed record did not show the Results text: Soleymanjahi2024 (structured abstract) and Morris2023 (Recommendations section).
- Labels: openFDA drug label endpoint, current version by latest effective_time. Indications, boxed warnings and labelled doses are quoted as label wording, not as treatment instructions. - Approval dates: openFDA drugsfda endpoint. Supplement-to-indication mapping is firm for Braftovi (stated in Elez2025), Fruzaqla (single indication), Krazati, Tukysa and Lumakras (single CRC efficacy supplement). It is date-matched only (unverified mapping) for Keytruda (2020-06-29) and Opdivo/Yervoy (2025-04-08).
- Registries: ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 (status and enrollment as of 2026-10-25).
- Not used: ESMO 2023 mCRC guideline (no abstract; paywalled). Add-Aspirin efficacy results: none found for colorectal. - Lonardi2026 and Sakakida2026 are online-ahead-of-print records with no volume or pages yet.
- Survival figures throughout are population or trial-group statistics, not predictions for any one person.
