# F · Treatment of localised colon and rectal cancer

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: surgery, chemo, rectal.

This pass covers curative-intent treatment of non-metastatic colon and rectal cancer: the operations and their quality markers (mesocolic and mesorectal planes, circumferential margin), minimally invasive and robotic surgery, leaks, stomas and recovery pathways, malignant polyps and obstruction; adjuvant and neoadjuvant chemotherapy for colon cancer (including the IDEA duration question and the mismatch-repair story); and rectal cancer from short-course radiotherapy through total neoadjuvant therapy, watch-and-wait and PD-1 blockade for dMMR tumours. Trial numbers are taken from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstracts, and in four cases from the PMC full text (named in Provenance). Doses and label wording come from current US labels in openFDA and are quoted as published, not as instructions.

## Findings

### surgery · Principles and operations by tumour site
- Curative treatment for non-metastatic disease includes endoscopic and surgical local excision, downstaging preoperative radiotherapy and systemic therapy, and extensive surgery for locoregional disease. Survival remains best for non-metastasised disease (Established) [[Dekker2019]].
- In complete mesocolic excision (CME), the surgeon separates the mesocolic plane from the parietal plane and ties the supplying arteries and draining veins "right at their roots" (central ligation). The aim is an intact package of tumour plus its lymphatic drainage, by analogy with TME [[Hohenberger2009]].
- Erlangen series (1,329 consecutive R0 colon resections, 1978–2002): with CME, 5-year local recurrence fell from 6.5% (1978–1984) to 3.6% (1995–2002). Cancer-related 5-year survival after curative resection rose from 82.1% to 89.1% (single-centre, before–after comparison; Early evidence for CME as a distinct operation) [[Hohenberger2009]].
- The number of lymph nodes examined is itself prognostic (INT-0089 secondary analysis, 3,411 stage II–III patients). The median node harvest was 11 (range 1–87). Survival rose as more nodes were analysed, even in node-negative patients (overall survival P=.0005) [[LeVoyer2003]].
- Operation by site, from the trial descriptions:
  - Right and left colon and sigmoid cancers were the COLOR trial population for laparoscopic or open colectomy [[Veldkamp2005]].
  - For the rectum, ROLARR used high anterior resection (upper rectum), low anterior resection (total rectum) or abdominoperineal resection (rectum and perineum) [[Jayne2017]].
- In ACOSOG Z6051 (stage II–III rectal cancer within 12 cm of the anal verge), 76.7% of patients had a low anterior resection and 23.3% an abdominoperineal resection [[Fleshman2015]].
- In Heald's 7.5-year district series, using lower anterior resections kept abdominoperineal excision with permanent colostomy to 11%. Of 115 curative resections, 69 had anastomoses below 5 cm [[Heald1986]].

### surgery · Total mesorectal excision and the circumferential margin
- Heald 1982: in 5 cases, minute foci of adenocarcinoma lay in the mesorectum several centimetres distal to the visible lower edge of the tumour. This led to total excision of the mesorectum in over 100 consecutive anterior resections. Fifty curative cases followed for more than 2 years had no pelvic or staple-line recurrence [[Heald1982]].
- Heald 1986: at an average of 4.2 years after TME there were 3 pelvic recurrences and no staple-line recurrences after curative surgery. Corrected 5-year survival was 87%. Tumour-free survival was 94% for Dukes A, 87% for Dukes B and 58% for Dukes C [[Heald1986]].
- Heald 1986 concluded that mural (distal) clearance can often be limited to preserve the sphincters, provided the mesorectum is excised intact. Of the curative resections, 39 had mural margins under 2.5 cm [[Heald1986]].
- Quirke 1986 (52 rectal adenocarcinomas, whole-mount transverse slicing): tumour reached the lateral (circumferential) resection margin in 14 of 52 (27%). Twelve of those 14 developed local pelvic recurrence. Sensitivity was 95%, specificity 92% and positive predictive value 85% [[Quirke1986]].
- A review of more than 17,500 patients found that an involved circumferential resection margin (CRM) predicts local recurrence more strongly after neoadjuvant therapy than without it (HR 6.3 vs 2.0). It also predicts distant metastases (HR 2.8; 95% CI 1.9–4.3) and survival (HR 1.7; 1.3–2.3) [[Nagtegaal2008]].
- Pathologist grading of mesorectal completeness (Dutch TME trial, 180 patients without adjuvant treatment): the mesorectum was incomplete in 24%. Recurrence was 36.1% with an incomplete mesorectum vs 20.3% with a complete one (P=.02) [[Nagtegaal2002]].
- MERCURY (408 patients, 11 European units): high-resolution MRI predicted a clear CRM with 92% specificity. Of the 349 cases MRI called clear, 327 (94%) were clear at surgery. Median node harvest was 12 [[MERCURY2006]].
- In ACOSOG Z6051, an unsuccessful resection worsened disease-free survival (HR 1.87; 95% CI 1.21–2.91), as did a positive CRM (HR 2.31; 1.40–3.79) and a positive distal margin (HR 2.53; 1.30–3.77) [[Fleshman2019]].

### surgery · Laparoscopic vs open
- COST (872 colon cancer patients, 48 institutions, median follow-up 4.4 years):
  - 3-year recurrence was 16% laparoscopic vs 18% open (HR 0.86; 95% CI 0.63–1.17).
  - Wound recurrence was below 1% in both arms.
  - 3-year overall survival was 86% vs 85%.
  - Median hospital stay was 5 vs 6 days [[COST2004]].
- COLOR short-term (627 laparoscopic, 621 open, right/left/sigmoid colon): median blood loss was 100 vs 175 mL and surgery lasted 30 minutes longer. Conversion to open surgery was needed in 17%. Bowel function recovered earlier and hospital stay was shorter, with no difference in 28-day morbidity or mortality [[Veldkamp2005]].
- COLOR long-term (1,076 analysed): 3-year disease-free survival was 74.2% laparoscopic vs 76.2% open (difference 2.0%; 95% CI −3.2 to 7.2). Because the upper CI limit just exceeded the prespecified 7% margin, non-inferiority was formally not shown. The authors judged the difference clinically acceptable [[COLOR2009]].
- CLASICC short-term (794 patients, 27 UK centres, 2:1 laparoscopic): 29% of laparoscopic cases were converted to open, and converted patients had more complications. Margins were similar except after laparoscopic anterior resection for rectal cancer [[Guillou2005]].
- CLASICC 5-year: no differences in overall survival, disease-free survival or local or distant recurrence. Wound/port-site recurrence in the laparoscopic arm was 2.4%. Conversion was associated with worse overall survival [[Jayne2010]].
- COLOR II short-term (1,044 rectal cancer patients, 2:1 laparoscopic):
  - Median blood loss was 200 vs 400 mL and operating time 240 vs 188 minutes.
  - Hospital stay was 8 vs 9 days.
  - The CRM was positive (<2 mm) in 10% in both arms [[vanderPas2013]].
- COLOR II 3-year: locoregional recurrence was 5.0% in both arms (difference 0; 90% CI −2.6 to 2.6). Disease-free survival was 74.8% vs 70.8% and overall survival 86.7% vs 83.6% (laparoscopic vs open) [[Bonjer2015]].
- ALaCaRT (475 patients with T1–T3 rectal cancer): a successful resection (complete TME, CRM ≥1 mm, distal margin ≥1 mm) was achieved in 82% laparoscopic vs 89% open (risk difference −7.0%). Non-inferiority was not established. Conversion rate was 9% [[Stevenson2015]].
- ACOSOG Z6051 (486 patients with stage II–III rectal cancer after neoadjuvant therapy): successful resection was 81.7% laparoscopic vs 86.9% open (difference −5.3%), so non-inferiority was not met. Conversion was 11.3% and operating time 266 vs 221 minutes [[Fleshman2015]].
- ACOSOG Z6051 follow-up (median 47.9 months): 2-year disease-free survival was 79.5% laparoscopic vs 83.2% open. Local/regional recurrence was 4.6% vs 4.5%, a non-significant difference [[Fleshman2019]].

### surgery · Robotic
- ROLARR (471 rectal cancer patients, 40 surgeons in 10 countries): conversion to open was 8.1% robotic vs 12.2% laparoscopic (adjusted OR 0.61; 95% CI 0.31–1.21; P=.16). CRM positivity was 5.1% vs 6.3%. There was no significant advantage, with surgeons of varying robotic experience [[Jayne2017]].
- REAL short-term (1,171 analysed; mid/low rectal cancer; 11 Chinese hospitals), robotic vs laparoscopic:
  - CRM positivity 4.0% vs 7.2% (difference −3.2 points).
  - Clavien-Dindo ≥II complications within 30 days 16.2% vs 23.1%.
  - Conversion 1.7% vs 3.9%.
  - Abdominoperineal resection 16.9% vs 22.7% [[Feng2022]].
- REAL long-term (median follow-up 43 months), robotic vs laparoscopic:
  - 3-year locoregional recurrence 1.6% vs 4.0% (HR 0.45; 95% CI 0.22–0.92).
  - 3-year disease-free survival 87.2% vs 83.4% (HR 0.74).
  - 3-year overall survival 94.7% vs 93.0%, not significantly different.
  - This is a single-country trial in high-volume centres (Early evidence) [[Feng2025]].

### surgery · Anastomotic leak, stoma, recovery and function
- Swedish RCT (234 patients, low anterior resection, median anastomotic level 5 cm, 79% with preoperative radiotherapy):
  - Symptomatic leak was 10.3% with a defunctioning stoma vs 28.0% without (OR 3.4; 95% CI 1.6–6.9).
  - Urgent reoperation was 8.6% vs 25.4% [[Matthiessen2007]].
- In the same trial, 13.8% of defunctioned patients and 16.9% of non-defunctioned patients still had a stoma of some kind at median 42 months [[Matthiessen2007]].
- ERAS meta-analysis (16 RCTs, 2,376 colorectal surgery patients):
  - Overall morbidity was lower (RR 0.60; 95% CI 0.46–0.76), mainly from fewer non-surgical complications (RR 0.40).
  - Hospital stay was 2.28 days shorter, with no rise in readmissions [[Greco2014]].
- The ERAS Society's fourth colorectal guideline (2018) grades each perioperative care item by GRADE [[Gustafsson2019]].
- The LARS score (low anterior resection syndrome; 961 respondents) scores five items: flatus incontinence, liquid-stool incontinence, frequency, clustering and urgency. Totals of 0–20 mean no LARS, 21–29 minor and 30–42 major. Scores differed significantly with radiotherapy and with tumour height under 5 cm [[Emmertsen2012]].

### surgery · Malignant polyps and local excision
- Haggitt (129 carcinomas arising in adenomas, mean follow-up 81 months): none of 65 patients with carcinoma confined to the mucosa had an adverse outcome, compared with 8 of 64 with invasive carcinoma. Level 4 invasion (submucosa of the bowel wall below the stalk) and rectal location were the only significant adverse factors [[Haggitt1985]].
- Haggitt levels: 0 = mucosa; 1 = head; 2 = neck; 3 = stalk; 4 = submucosa of the underlying wall. Seven of 28 level-4 lesions had an adverse outcome [[Haggitt1985]].
- The 2022 NCCN rectal update covers malignant polyps and resectable non-metastatic rectal cancer. It redrew the stage II–III algorithms around total neoadjuvant therapy, expanded short-course radiation options and added watch-and-wait for complete responders [[Benson2022]].

### surgery · Obstruction: stent as a bridge to surgery
- CReST (245 patients with left-sided malignant colonic obstruction, 39 UK hospitals): stenting was attempted in 96.7% and relieved obstruction in 82.4% of attempts [[CReST2022]].
- In CReST patients treated with curative intent, stenting then elective surgery vs emergency surgery:
  - 30-day mortality 3.6% vs 5.6% (no significant difference).
  - Median stay 19 vs 18 days.
  - Stoma formation 47.5% vs 67.9% (P=.003).
  - No difference in 3-year recurrence [[CReST2022]].

### chemo · History and the oxaliplatin era
- Moertel 1990 (1,296 patients): one year of levamisole plus fluorouracil cut recurrence risk by 41% and overall death rate by 33% in stage C (node-positive) colon cancer. Levamisole alone had no effect. This made 5-FU-based adjuvant therapy standard for stage C [[Moertel1990]].
- MOSAIC (2,246 stage II–III patients, 6 months; FOLFOX4 vs LV5FU2):
  - 3-year disease-free survival 78.2% vs 72.9% (HR for recurrence 0.77; P=.002).
  - Grade 3 sensory neuropathy 12.4% during treatment, 1.1% at 1 year [[Andre2004]].
- MOSAIC final:
  - 5-year disease-free survival 73.3% vs 67.4% (HR 0.80).
  - 6-year overall survival 78.5% vs 76.0% overall, and 72.9% vs 68.7% in stage III (HR 0.80).
  - No overall survival difference in stage II.
  - Grade 3 neuropathy 1.3% at 12 months and 0.7% at 48 months [[Andre2009]].
- MOSAIC 10-year overall survival:
  - Stage III 67.1% FOLFOX4 vs 59.0% LV5FU2 (HR 0.80).
  - Stage II 78.4% vs 79.5% (HR 1.00).
  - dMMR tumours were 9.4% of the 1,008 tested [[Andre2015]].
- NSABP C-07 (2,407 eligible; FLOX = weekly bolus FU/LV + oxaliplatin 85 mg/m² in weeks 1, 3, 5 of each 8-week cycle):
  - Disease-free survival HR 0.80 (95% CI 0.69–0.93).
  - 3-year disease-free survival 76.1% vs 71.8%.
  - Grade 3 neurosensory toxicity 8.2% vs 0.7% [[Kuebler2007]].
- X-ACT (1,987 stage III patients, 24 weeks): oral capecitabine was at least equivalent to bolus FU/LV (Mayo) for disease-free survival. Relapse-free survival improved (HR 0.86; 95% CI 0.74–0.99) and there were significantly fewer adverse events [[Twelves2005]].
- XELOXA / NO16968 (1,886 stage III patients; oxaliplatin 130 mg/m² day 1 + capecitabine 1,000 mg/m² twice daily days 1–14, every 3 weeks for 24 weeks, vs bolus FU/FA): 3-year disease-free survival was 70.9% vs 66.5% (HR 0.80; 95% CI 0.69–0.93) [[Haller2011]].
- XELOXA final: 7-year disease-free survival was 63% vs 56% (HR 0.80) and 7-year overall survival 73% vs 67% (HR 0.83; 95% CI 0.70–0.99) [[Schmoll2015]].

### chemo · Stage II, MMR status, age and timing
- QUASAR (3,239 patients, 91% stage II): FU/folinic acid vs observation gave a relative risk of death of 0.82 (95% CI 0.70–0.95) and of recurrence of 0.78. If 5-year mortality without chemotherapy is 20%, this translates to an absolute survival gain of only 3.6% (95% CI 1.0–6.0) [[QUASAR2007]].
- Sargent 2010 (stage II–III, FU-based therapy vs surgery alone):
  - pMMR tumours benefited (disease-free survival HR 0.67; 95% CI 0.48–0.93).
  - dMMR tumours did not (HR 1.10; 0.42–2.91).
  - In the pooled stage II dMMR group (1,027 patients), FU was associated with worse overall survival (HR 2.95; 95% CI 1.02–8.54) [[Sargent2010]].
- dMMR tumours were 15% (70/457) of the Sargent trial sample [[Sargent2010]] and 9.4% of MOSAIC specimens tested [[Andre2015]].
- ACCENT pooled analysis (11,953 patients): in patients aged 70 years or older, adding oxaliplatin to IV fluorouracil gave little benefit (disease-free survival HR 0.94; overall survival HR 1.04). The age interaction was not statistically significant, and oral fluoropyrimidines kept their efficacy at all ages [[McCleary2013]].
- Timing meta-analysis (10 studies, 15,410 patients): each 4-week delay in starting adjuvant chemotherapy was associated with worse overall survival (HR 1.14; 95% CI 1.10–1.17) and disease-free survival (HR 1.14). The data are observational (Established association) [[Biagi2011]].
- DYNAMIC (455 stage II patients): ctDNA-guided management cut adjuvant chemotherapy use from 28% to 15%. 2-year recurrence-free survival was non-inferior (93.5% vs 92.4%) (Early evidence) [[Tie2022]].

### chemo · IDEA: 3 vs 6 months
- IDEA pooled six phase 3 trials (12,834 stage III patients; FOLFOX or CAPOX) with a non-inferiority margin of HR 1.12. Overall, non-inferiority of 3 months was not confirmed: disease-free survival HR 1.07 (95% CI 1.00–1.15) [[Grothey2018]].
- IDEA by regimen: 3 months was non-inferior for CAPOX (HR 0.95; 95% CI 0.85–1.06) but not for FOLFOX (HR 1.16; 1.06–1.26) [[Grothey2018]].
- IDEA by risk group:
  - Low risk (T1–3 N1): 3-year disease-free survival 83.1% at 3 months vs 83.3% at 6 months (HR 1.01).
  - High risk (T4 and/or N2): 6 months was superior, 64.4% vs 62.7% (HR 1.12; P=.01) [[Grothey2018]].
- IDEA, grade ≥2 neurotoxicity during therapy and the month after (full text):
  - FOLFOX: 16.6% at 3 months vs 47.7% at 6 months.
  - CAPOX: 14.2% vs 44.9% [[Grothey2018]].
- IDEA final overall survival (12,835 patients, median follow-up 72 months):
  - 5-year overall survival 82.4% at 3 months vs 82.8% at 6 months (HR 1.02); non-inferiority was not formally met.
  - CAPOX: 82.1% vs 81.2% (HR 0.96).
  - FOLFOX: 82.6% vs 83.8% (HR 1.07).
  - The authors concluded the results support 3 months of CAPOX for most patients [[Andre2020]].
- SCOT (6,065 analysed, high-risk stage II and stage III, 67% CAPOX): 3-year disease-free survival was 76.7% at 3 months vs 77.1% at 6 months (HR 1.006), which met non-inferiority. Grade ≥2 neuropathy was 25% vs 58% in the safety subset [[Iveson2018]].

### chemo · Neoadjuvant chemotherapy and immunotherapy for colon cancer
- FOxTROT (1,053 patients with radiologically staged T3–4 N0–2 colon cancer, 2:1): 6 weeks of preoperative oxaliplatin-fluoropyrimidine + 18 weeks postoperative was compared with 24 weeks postoperative. Results:
  - Residual or recurrent disease within 2 years 16.9% vs 21.5% (rate ratio 0.72; 95% CI 0.54–0.98).
  - Complete (R0) resection 94% vs 89%.
  - Little benefit in dMMR tumours [[Morton2023]].
- In FOxTROT, 4.3% of the neoadjuvant group developed obstructive symptoms needing expedited surgery, yet serious postoperative complications were fewer than in controls [[Morton2023]].
- NICHE-2 (115 patients with locally advanced dMMR colon cancer, neoadjuvant nivolumab + ipilimumab):
  - 98% had timely surgery.
  - Pathological response 98%, major response 95%, complete response 68%.
  - No recurrences at median 26 months (Early evidence) [[Chalabi2024]].
- ATOMIC (712 patients with resected stage III dMMR colon cancer): 3-year disease-free survival was 86.3% with atezolizumab + mFOLFOX6 vs 76.2% with mFOLFOX6 alone (HR 0.50; 95% CI 0.35–0.73). Grade 3–4 adverse events were 84.1% vs 71.9% [[Sinicrope2026]].

### chemo · Label doses and boxed warnings (quoted, not instructions)
- Oxaliplatin (FOLFOX4 schedule, every 14 days):
  - Day 1: oxaliplatin 85 mg/m² over 120 minutes, with leucovorin 200 mg/m² over 120 minutes, then fluorouracil 400 mg/m² bolus and 600 mg/m² over 22 hours.
  - Day 2: leucovorin and fluorouracil repeated.
  - Adjuvant treatment: up to 12 cycles [[FDA-Oxaliplatin]].
- Oxaliplatin label indication: "adjuvant treatment of stage III colon cancer in patients who have undergone complete resection of the primary tumor", with infusional fluorouracil and leucovorin [[FDA-Oxaliplatin]].
- Oxaliplatin boxed warning: serious and fatal hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, "within minutes of administration and during any cycle" [[FDA-Oxaliplatin]].
- Oxaliplatin warnings: acute and delayed peripheral sensory neuropathy ("avoid topical application of ice"), myelosuppression, PRES, pulmonary toxicity, hepatotoxicity, QT prolongation and rhabdomyolysis [[FDA-Oxaliplatin]].
- Capecitabine, adjuvant colon:
  - Single agent: 1,250 mg/m² twice daily on days 1–14 of each 21-day cycle, maximum 8 cycles.
  - With oxaliplatin: 1,000 mg/m² twice daily on days 1–14, plus oxaliplatin 130 mg/m² on day 1, maximum 8 cycles [[FDA-Xeloda]].
- Capecitabine, perioperative rectal cancer: 825 mg/m² twice daily with concomitant radiation; 1,250 mg/m² twice daily without radiation [[FDA-Xeloda]].
- Capecitabine boxed warning (label effective Feb 2026):
  - Serious adverse reactions or death with complete DPD deficiency. "Test patients for genetic variants of DPYD prior to initiating" unless immediate treatment is necessary.
  - Increased bleeding with vitamin K antagonists such as warfarin [[FDA-Xeloda]].
- The capecitabine label notes that no FDA-authorized DPYD test is currently available and that "No XELODA dose has been proven safe" with complete DPD deficiency [[FDA-Xeloda]].
- The fluorouracil injection label carries the same DPD-deficiency boxed warning, with DPYD testing before starting unless treatment is urgent [[FDA-Fluorouracil]].

### rectal · Radiotherapy before TME
- Swedish Rectal Cancer Trial (1,168 patients under 80 years; 25 Gy in 5 fractions, then surgery within a week, vs surgery alone):
  - 5-year local recurrence 11% vs 27%.
  - 5-year overall survival 58% vs 48% (P=.004).
  - This was before standardised TME [[SRCT1997]].
- Dutch TME trial (1,861 patients; 5×5 Gy + TME vs TME alone): 2-year local recurrence was 2.4% vs 8.2% (P<.001), with no 2-year survival difference (82.0% vs 81.8%) [[Kapiteijn2001]].
- Dutch TME trial, 12-year follow-up:
  - 10-year local recurrence 5% vs 11%, but no overall survival difference.
  - In stage III with a negative CRM, 10-year survival was 50% vs 40% (P=.032) [[vanGijn2011]].
- German CAO/ARO/AIO-94 (823 patients; 50.4 Gy + 5-FU, before vs after surgery):
  - 5-year local relapse 6% vs 13% (P=.006), with no survival difference (76% vs 74%).
  - Grade 3–4 acute toxicity 27% vs 40%; long-term toxicity 14% vs 24% [[Sauer2004]].
- CAO/ARO/AIO-94, 11-year follow-up:
  - 10-year local relapse 7.1% vs 10.1% (P=.048).
  - 10-year overall survival 59.6% vs 59.9%.
  - Distant metastases 29.8% vs 29.6% [[Sauer2012]].
- Stockholm III (840 patients):
  - Short-course RT with immediate surgery, short-course RT with surgery delayed 4–8 weeks, and long-course 25×2 Gy with delay all gave similar local recurrence. Both delayed arms were judged non-inferior.
  - In the pooled short-course comparison, delay lowered postoperative complications (OR 0.61; 95% CI 0.45–0.83) [[Erlandsson2017]].

### rectal · Total neoadjuvant therapy (TNT)
- RAPIDO (920 patients with high-risk locally advanced rectal cancer):
  - Experimental arm: 5×5 Gy, then 6 cycles of CAPOX or 9 of FOLFOX4, then TME.
  - Standard arm: 50.4 or 50 Gy with capecitabine 825 mg/m² twice daily, TME, and optional adjuvant chemotherapy.
  - 3-year disease-related treatment failure 23.7% vs 30.4% (HR 0.75; 95% CI 0.60–0.95) [[Bahadoer2021]].
- RAPIDO toxicity: grade ≥3 diarrhoea during preoperative therapy was 18% experimental vs 9% standard. Serious adverse events were 38% vs 34% [[Bahadoer2021]].
- RAPIDO 5-year:
  - Locoregional failure 12% vs 8% (P=.07).
  - Locoregional recurrence after resection 10% vs 6% (P=.027), more often with a breached mesorectum.
  - The reduction in disease-related treatment failure and distant metastases persisted [[Dijkstra2023]].
- PRODIGE 23 (461 patients with cT3–4 M0; 6 cycles of FOLFIRINOX before chemoradiotherapy 50 Gy + capecitabine, then TME and 3 months of adjuvant chemotherapy, vs CRT, TME and 6 months adjuvant):
  - 3-year disease-free survival 76% vs 69% (HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.49–0.97).
  - Less neurotoxicity during adjuvant therapy (12% vs 21%) [[Conroy2021]].
- PROSPECT (1,194 randomised; T2N+ or T3 cancers eligible for sphincter-sparing surgery): neoadjuvant FOLFOX, with chemoradiotherapy only if the tumour shrank by less than 20% or FOLFOX was stopped for side effects, was compared with chemoradiotherapy. Results:
  - Disease-free survival HR 0.92 (90.2% CI 0.74–1.14), non-inferior.
  - 5-year disease-free survival 80.8% vs 78.6% [[Schrag2023]].
- In PROSPECT's FOLFOX group, only 9.1% received preoperative and 1.4% postoperative chemoradiotherapy, so about 9 in 10 avoided pelvic radiation. Overall survival (HR 1.04) and local recurrence (HR 1.18) were similar [[Schrag2023]].
- In the PROSPECT FOLFOX group, 6.5% (38 patients) did not meet the 20% tumour-shrinkage threshold (full text) [[Schrag2023]].

### rectal · Organ preservation and watch-and-wait
- Habr-Gama 2004 (265 patients with distal rectal cancer; 5-FU/leucovorin + 50.4 Gy):
  - 71 (26.8%) had a complete clinical response and were observed without surgery.
  - 5-year overall and disease-free survival were 100% and 92% in the observation group, vs 88% and 83% in 22 resected pT0 patients [[HabrGama2004]].
- International Watch & Wait Database (880 patients with cCR, 47 institutes, 15 countries, median follow-up 3.3 years):
  - 2-year cumulative local regrowth 25.2% (95% CI 22.2–28.5).
  - 88% of regrowths were found in the first 2 years, and 97% were in the bowel wall [[vanderValk2018]].
- IWWD: distant metastases occurred in 8% (71/880). 5-year overall survival was 85% and disease-specific survival 94% (registry data) [[vanderValk2018]].
- OPRA (324 patients with stage II–III disease; 4 months of FOLFOX/CAPOX plus 50–56 Gy chemoradiotherapy, as induction (INCT-CRT) or consolidation (CRT-CNCT); watch-and-wait offered for clinical complete or near-complete response):
  - 3-year disease-free survival 76% in both arms.
  - 3-year TME-free survival 41% (INCT-CRT) vs 53% (CRT-CNCT) [[GarciaAguilar2022]].
- OPRA 5-year:
  - TME-free survival 39% vs 54% (P=.012); disease-free survival 71% vs 69%.
  - Of 81 regrowths, 94% occurred within 2 years and 99% within 3 years.
  - Disease-free survival was 64% both after restaging-TME and after TME for regrowth [[Verheij2024]].
- A pooled analysis of CAO/ARO/AIO-12 (mandatory TME) and OPRA (selective watch-and-wait), 628 patients, found no difference in disease-free, distant-recurrence-free, local-recurrence-free or overall survival between strategies (Early evidence; non-randomised comparison) [[Williams2025]].

### rectal · dMMR rectal cancer and PD-1 blockade
- Cercek 2022 (dMMR stage II–III rectal cancer; dostarlimab every 3 weeks for 6 months): all 12 patients who completed treatment (100%; 95% CI 74–100) had a clinical complete response. None needed chemoradiotherapy or surgery, and there were no grade ≥3 adverse events (follow-up 6–25 months) [[Cercek2022]].
- Cercek 2025 (117 patients with stage I–III dMMR tumours; dostarlimab 500 mg IV every 3 weeks for 9 cycles):
  - Rectal cohort: all 49 who completed treatment had a cCR and chose nonoperative management.
  - 37 had a sustained cCR of at least 12 months, meeting the efficacy criterion [[Cercek2025]].
- Cercek 2025 rectal cohort (n=50): 2-year recurrence-free survival was 96% (95% CI 90–100) at median follow-up of 30.2 months (full text). Across all 117 patients it was 92% [[Cercek2025]].
- Across both Cercek 2025 cohorts, 84 of 103 who completed treatment had a cCR and 82 avoided surgery. Ninety-five percent had only grade 1–2 adverse events or none [[Cercek2025]].
- Regulatory status: the JEMPERLI US label (effective 2025-09-02) is indicated for endometrial cancer and for "dMMR recurrent or advanced solid tumors" after prior treatment (accelerated approval). It has no localised rectal cancer indication. The MSKCC trial is still recruiting (estimated 212 patients) [[FDA-Jemperli,NCT04165772]].

### Exam hooks
- TME = sharp dissection removing the rectum with its intact mesorectal envelope. Heald's 1986 series reported 5-year corrected survival of 87%, with no staple-line recurrences after curative surgery [[Heald1986]].
- An involved circumferential margin was the cause of local recurrence: 12 of 14 margin-positive cases recurred in Quirke's 1986 study [[Quirke1986]].
- CME = TME's colon analogue (mesocolic plane + central vascular ligation). Local recurrence fell from 6.5% to 3.6% [[Hohenberger2009]].
- Laparoscopic colectomy is oncologically equivalent to open (COST: 3-year recurrence 16% vs 18%) [[COST2004]].
- Laparoscopic rectal resection failed pathological non-inferiority in ALaCaRT and Z6051 [[Stevenson2015,Fleshman2015]]. COLOR II showed equal 3-year locoregional recurrence (5.0% each) [[Bonjer2015]].
- ROLARR: robotic surgery did not significantly lower conversion (8.1% vs 12.2%) [[Jayne2017]].
- A defunctioning stoma after low anterior resection cut symptomatic leak from 28.0% to 10.3% [[Matthiessen2007]].
- MOSAIC: adding oxaliplatin to 5-FU/LV gave 3-year disease-free survival of 78.2% vs 72.9%, with overall survival benefit in stage III only [[Andre2004,Andre2009]].
- IDEA: overall non-inferiority of 3 months was not met (HR 1.07). 3 months of CAPOX is enough for low-risk T1–3 N1 disease [[Grothey2018]].
- Grade ≥2 neuropathy roughly triples with 6 vs 3 months: FOLFOX 47.7% vs 16.6%; CAPOX 44.9% vs 14.2% [[Grothey2018]].
- Stage II dMMR colon cancer: fluorouracil alone gives no benefit and may harm (overall survival HR 2.95) [[Sargent2010]].
- QUASAR: absolute survival gain from adjuvant 5-FU in stage II is about 3.6% [[QUASAR2007]].
- Each 4-week delay to adjuvant chemotherapy: overall survival HR 1.14 [[Biagi2011]].
- Short-course RT = 5×5 Gy. In the Dutch TME trial it cut 10-year local recurrence from 11% to 5%, without an overall survival gain [[vanGijn2011]].
- Preoperative is better than postoperative chemoradiotherapy for local control (6% vs 13%) and toxicity, with equal survival (CAO/ARO/AIO-94) [[Sauer2004]].
- RAPIDO: TNT lowered disease-related treatment failure (23.7% vs 30.4%) but raised 5-year locoregional recurrence (10% vs 6%) [[Bahadoer2021,Dijkstra2023]].
- Watch-and-wait: about 1 in 4 regrow by 2 years, and most regrowth is in the bowel wall and salvageable [[vanderValk2018]].
- dMMR rectal cancer + dostarlimab: 12/12, then 49/49, clinical complete responses [[Cercek2022,Cercek2025]].
- Capecitabine and fluorouracil labels now carry a boxed warning with DPYD testing before starting [[FDA-Xeloda,FDA-Fluorouracil]].

### In plain words
- For bowel cancer that has not spread, surgery is the main treatment. The surgeon removes the tumour together with its fatty envelope and lymph nodes in one intact package [[Hohenberger2009,Heald1986]].
- In rectal cancer, the most important thing is that no cancer is left at the outer edge of what is removed (the "margin") [[Quirke1986,Nagtegaal2008]].
- Keyhole (laparoscopic) surgery for colon cancer works as well as open surgery against the cancer, and people usually go home sooner [[COST2004,Veldkamp2005]].
- After a very low join in the rectum, a temporary bag (stoma) makes a dangerous leak less likely [[Matthiessen2007]].
- Chemotherapy after surgery lowers the chance the cancer comes back, especially when lymph nodes were involved [[Moertel1990,Andre2009]].
- For many people with lower-risk stage III colon cancer, 3 months of CAPOX works about as well as 6 months and causes much less nerve damage [[Grothey2018,Andre2020]].
- Numbness and tingling in the hands and feet from oxaliplatin is common. It gets worse the longer treatment goes on and can last after treatment ends [[Grothey2018,Andre2009]].
- Before capecitabine or 5-FU, US labels now ask doctors to test for gene changes (DPYD) that can make these drugs dangerous [[FDA-Xeloda,FDA-Fluorouracil]].
- For rectal cancer, radiation and chemotherapy are often given before surgery to shrink the tumour and lower the chance it comes back in the pelvis [[Sauer2004,Bahadoer2021]].
- Some people's rectal tumours disappear completely after treatment. In specialist centres they may be able to skip surgery and be checked very closely instead ("watch and wait") [[GarciaAguilar2022,vanderValk2018]].
- With watch-and-wait, about 1 in 4 people see the tumour grow back, mostly in the first 2 years. It can usually still be removed then [[vanderValk2018,Verheij2024]].
- A small group of rectal cancers (called dMMR) have responded completely to an immunotherapy drug in a trial. This is not yet an approved standard treatment [[Cercek2025,FDA-Jemperli]].
- Survival numbers in this section come from groups of people in studies. They cannot predict what will happen to one person.

## Open questions and controversies
- Laparoscopic rectal surgery:
  - Against: pathological non-inferiority was not shown in ALaCaRT (82% vs 89% successful resection) or Z6051 (81.7% vs 86.9%) [[Stevenson2015,Fleshman2015]].
  - For: COLOR II and Z6051 follow-up show similar locoregional recurrence (5.0% each; 4.6% vs 4.5%) [[Bonjer2015,Fleshman2019]].
  - Status: oncologically acceptable in skilled hands; specimen quality remains the surrogate debate.
- Robotic vs laparoscopic rectal surgery:
  - ROLARR found no significant conversion benefit [[Jayne2017]].
  - REAL found lower 3-year locoregional recurrence (1.6% vs 4.0%) and better function [[Feng2025]].
  - Status: open. REAL is from high-volume Chinese centres, and generalisability is unproven.
- CME for colon cancer: supported by single-centre before–after data [[Hohenberger2009]]. Randomised evidence was not reviewed in this pass (Early evidence).
- 3 vs 6 months of adjuvant therapy:
  - Formal overall non-inferiority failed for both DFS (HR 1.07) and OS (HR 1.02) [[Grothey2018,Andre2020]].
  - SCOT met non-inferiority, and the absolute OS gap was 0.4% [[Iveson2018,Andre2020]].
  - Status: CAPOX 3 months is widely accepted for low-risk disease; for high-risk disease and FOLFOX, 6 months keeps a small edge.
- Oxaliplatin in people aged 70 or over: the ACCENT point estimates suggest little benefit (OS HR 1.04), but there was no significant interaction by age [[McCleary2013]]. Status: an individual decision.
- TNT and local control: RAPIDO lowered disease-related failure but raised 5-year locoregional recurrence (10% vs 6%) [[Bahadoer2021,Dijkstra2023]]. PRODIGE 23 improved DFS with FOLFIRINOX before CRT [[Conroy2021]]. Status: which TNT sequence is best is unresolved.
- Omitting radiation: PROSPECT supports FOLFOX with selective CRT for T2N+/T3 sphincter-sparing tumours [[Schrag2023]]. It does not apply to high-risk (e.g. threatened-margin) tumours, which were the population in RAPIDO [[Bahadoer2021]].
- Watch-and-wait safety:
  - Regrowth risk is about 25% at 2 years, yet survival is high [[vanderValk2018]].
  - Pooled phase 2 data show equal survival vs mandatory TME [[Williams2025]].
  - No randomised comparison exists. Status: offered in expert centres with intensive surveillance.
- Neoadjuvant chemotherapy for colon cancer: FOxTROT improved 2-year disease control (rate ratio 0.72) but showed little benefit in dMMR [[Morton2023]]. Immunotherapy produced 68% pCR in dMMR colon cancer [[Chalabi2024]]. Status: neoadjuvant immunotherapy for dMMR colon is promising but non-randomised.
- dMMR rectal cancer and PD-1 blockade: 49/49 cCR among those completing treatment, with 2-year RFS of 96% [[Cercek2025]]. Single-centre-led phase 2; not in the FDA label [[FDA-Jemperli]]. Status: Early evidence, with long-term durability unknown.
- ctDNA-guided adjuvant therapy: DYNAMIC halved chemotherapy use without worse 2-year RFS in stage II [[Tie2022]]. Status: Early evidence; stage III trials are pending.
