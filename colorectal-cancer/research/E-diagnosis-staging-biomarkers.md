# E · Diagnosis, staging, pathology & biomarkers

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: symptoms (diagnostic workup), staging, biomarkers.

This pass covers what happens after colorectal cancer is suspected: symptom triage with FIT, colonoscopy and biopsy, CT and pelvic MRI, CEA, the AJCC 8th edition TNM system and stage groups, older Dukes-type systems, survival by stage, the pathology features that change risk (lymph-node yield, high-risk stage II features, tumour budding, tumour deposits, perineural invasion, histological subtype), and the tumour biomarkers that drive treatment (MMR/MSI, RAS, BRAF, HER2, KRAS G12C, NTRK, sidedness, ctDNA). Numbers come from PubMed abstracts unless a line says otherwise. Survival figures are population statistics and do not predict any one person's outcome.

## Findings

### symptoms · Triage of symptoms and route to diagnosis
- Red-flag symptoms named by the American Cancer Society for public and clinician awareness are rectal bleeding, abdominal pain, diarrhoea and iron-deficiency anaemia [[Siegel2026CRC]].
- Adults younger than 50 are more likely than older adults to present with haematochezia (41% vs 26% in one comparison cited by the ACS), iron-deficiency anaemia and abdominal pain [[Siegel2026CRC]].
- One study cited by the ACS found a 40% longer time to diagnosis in people under 50 than in older people, from both longer symptom duration and longer work-up, often because of misdiagnosis as more common conditions [[Siegel2026CRC]].
- The ACPGBI/BSG guideline recommends that primary-care clinicians use faecal immunochemical testing (FIT) to prioritise patients with clinical features of colorectal cancer (CRC) for urgent investigation [[Monahan2022]].
- The same guideline recommends a FIT threshold of faecal haemoglobin (fHb) ≥10 µg Hb/g faeces to select patients with lower GI symptoms for an urgent referral pathway. It also recommends that patients should not be excluded from referral on the basis of FIT alone [[Monahan2022]].
- In the systematic review summarised by the guideline (symptomatic primary-care adults), FIT at its limit of detection (≥2 to ≥7 µg Hb/g; 11 studies, n=41,388) had pooled sensitivity 93.4% (95% CI 88.0–96.4) and specificity 76.9% (67.7–84.0) for CRC [[Monahan2022]].
- At ≥10 µg Hb/g (15 studies, n=48,872) pooled sensitivity was 87.0% (81.0–91.6) and specificity 84.4% (79.4–88.3). At ≥20 µg Hb/g sensitivity was 84.1% and specificity 86.6%. At ≥150 µg Hb/g sensitivity was 64.1% and specificity 95.0% [[Monahan2022]].
- CRC prevalence among symptomatic people tested with FIT in primary care ranged from 0.8% to 1.8% across cohorts [[Monahan2022]].
- In large low-prevalence cohorts, the cancer diagnosis rate at follow-up after reassurance on very low fHb without investigation was 0.3% or less [[Monahan2022]].
- For patients with symptoms suggesting CRC, the guideline treats CT colonography as equivalent to colonoscopy for detecting CRC, with the choice set by local expertise and availability [[Monahan2022]].

### symptoms · Diagnostic workup after suspicion: colonoscopy, CT, MRI
- Synchronous CRC (two or more separate cancers at once) occurred in 534 of 13,683 patients (3.9%, about 1 in 25) in a Dutch population registry, 1995–2006 [[Mulder2011]].
- In 34% of those synchronous cases the two tumours lay in different surgical segments. The authors concluded that the whole colon should be examined, preferably before surgery [[Mulder2011]].
- Synchronous CRC was more common in men (OR 1.54) and in people over 70 (OR 1.83), and was associated with distant metastases (OR 1.69) [[Mulder2011]].
- CT for local staging of colon cancer (meta-analysis, 13 studies): pooled sensitivity 90% (95% CI 83–95) and specificity 69% (62–75) for tumour invasion beyond the bowel wall (T3–T4) [[Nerad2016]].
- CT was less accurate for nodes: sensitivity 71% (59–81) and specificity 67% (46–83) for nodal involvement across 16 studies. The authors called CT accuracy for nodal involvement low [[Nerad2016]].
- In two CT-colonography studies, sensitivity was 97% and specificity 81% for detecting T3–T4 colon tumours [[Nerad2016]].
- MERCURY (408 consecutive patients with rectal cancer, 11 European centres, 2002–2003) tested high-resolution pelvic MRI before total mesorectal excision (TME) [[MERCURY2006]].
- In MERCURY, 354 of 408 patients (87%) had a clear circumferential resection margin (CRM) at histology [[MERCURY2006]].
- MRI's specificity for predicting a clear CRM was 92% (327/354; 95% CI 90–95%). MRI predicted clear margins in 349 patients, and 327 of these (94%) had clear margins at surgery [[MERCURY2006]].
- High-resolution scans were technically satisfactory in 93% (379/408), and the median lymph-node harvest was 12 (range 0–49) [[MERCURY2006]].
- MRI measured extramural depth of tumour spread to within 0.5 mm of histology: mean 2.80 mm on MRI vs 2.81 mm on pathology, mean difference −0.05 mm (95% CI −0.49 to 0.40), in 295 patients [[MERCURY2007]].
- MERCURY 5-year follow-up (374 patients): an MRI-involved CRM was defined as tumour ≤1 mm from the mesorectal fascia [[Taylor2014]].
- 5-year overall survival (OS) was 62.2% with an MRI-clear CRM vs 42.2% with an MRI-involved CRM (HR 1.97, 95% CI 1.27–3.04) [[Taylor2014]].
- 5-year disease-free survival (DFS) was 67.2% with MRI-clear vs 47.3% with MRI-involved CRM (HR 1.65, 1.01–2.69). The hazard ratio for local recurrence was 3.50 (1.53–8.00) [[Taylor2014]].
- MRI-involved CRM was the only preoperative staging parameter that stayed significant for OS, DFS and local recurrence on multivariate analysis, outperforming AJCC TNM-based criteria [[Taylor2014]].
- A meta-analysis of 21 rectal MRI studies found specificity 94% (95% CI 88–97) for CRM involvement, 75% (68–80) for T category (T1–2 vs T3–4) and 71% (59–81) for lymph nodes [[AlSukhni2012]].
- The diagnostic odds ratio for CRM was 56.1, against 20.4 for T category and 8.3 for nodes. The authors concluded that MRI is accurate for CRM and T category but poor for nodes [[AlSukhni2012]].
- MRI-detected extramural vascular invasion (EMVI) had sensitivity 62% and specificity 88% against histology in 94 patients having primary surgery [[Smith2008]].
- 3-year relapse-free survival was 35% with an MRI-EMVI score of 3–4 vs 74% with a score of 0–2 (P<0.001), similar to histological EMVI-positive vs -negative (34% vs 73.7%) [[Smith2008]].
- Across more than 17,500 patients, an involved CRM predicted local recurrence (HR 2.0 without and 6.3 after neoadjuvant therapy), distant metastasis (HR 2.8, 95% CI 1.9–4.3) and survival (HR 1.7, 1.3–2.3) [[Nagtegaal2008]].

### symptoms · CEA as a marker
- ASCO 2006 recommends ordering carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) before surgery if it would help with staging and surgical planning [[Locker2006]].
- ASCO 2006 recommends postoperative CEA every 3 months for at least 3 years in stage II and III disease if the patient could be a candidate for surgery or chemotherapy for metastatic disease. CEA is also the marker of choice for monitoring metastatic disease on systemic therapy [[Locker2006]].
- ASCO 2006 found the data insufficient to recommend routine use of p53, ras, thymidylate synthase, DPD, thymidine phosphorylase, MSI, 18q loss of heterozygosity or DCC in CRC management. This was the 2006 position; MSI and RAS testing were later recommended by Sepulveda2017 [[Locker2006,Sepulveda2017]].
- CEA is "of little use in detecting early colorectal cancer", but a high preoperative CEA correlates with worse prognosis [[Duffy2001]].
- The College of American Pathologists placed preoperative CEA elevation in category I, the factors proven prognostic and generally used in management [[Compton2000]].
- Serial CEA detects recurrence with sensitivity of about 80%, specificity of about 70%, and a lead time of about 5 months (narrative review) [[Duffy2001]].
- Cochrane review of CEA for detecting recurrence after curative treatment (52 studies): sensitivity ranged from 41% to 97% and specificity from 52% to 100% across studies [[Nicholson2015]].
- At a CEA threshold of 2.5 µg/L (7 studies), pooled sensitivity was 82% (95% CI 78–86) and specificity 80% (59–92) [[Nicholson2015]].
- At 5 µg/L (23 studies), pooled sensitivity was 71% (64–76) and specificity 88% (84–92) [[Nicholson2015]].
- At 10 µg/L (7 studies), pooled sensitivity was 68% (53–79) and specificity 97% (90–99) [[Nicholson2015]].
- Cochrane concluded that CEA is not sensitive enough to use alone, even at a low threshold. It recommended monitoring with more than one modality and using the highest cut-off assessed (10 µg/L) [[Nicholson2015]].

### staging · AJCC 8th edition TNM: primary tumour (T)
- The AJCC 8th edition (published 2017) is the current reference TNM system for colon and rectum. Colon and rectal cancers share one TNM system [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- TX: primary tumour cannot be assessed. T0: no evidence of primary tumour [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- Tis: carcinoma in situ, intramucosal carcinoma (tumour limited to the epithelium or invading the lamina propria). AJCC adds that there is no extension through the muscularis mucosae (that phrase not printed in Tong2018) [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- T1: tumour invades the submucosa (through the muscularis mucosae but not into the muscularis propria) [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- T2: tumour invades the muscularis propria [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- T3: tumour invades through the muscularis propria into pericolorectal tissues [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- T4a: tumour penetrates the visceral peritoneum. AJCC also counts gross perforation of the bowel through tumour, and continuous invasion through inflammation to the peritoneal surface, as T4a (that detail not printed in Tong2018) [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- T4b: tumour directly invades or adheres to other organs or structures [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- The 8th edition made no change to T categories compared with the 7th [[Tong2018]].

### staging · AJCC 8th edition TNM: nodes (N) and metastasis (M)
- NX: regional nodes cannot be assessed. N0: no regional lymph-node metastasis and no tumour deposits [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- N1a: metastasis in 1 regional lymph node [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- N1b: metastasis in 2–3 regional lymph nodes [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- N1c: no regional lymph nodes are positive, but there are tumour deposits in the subserosa, mesentery, or non-peritonealised pericolic or perirectal/mesorectal tissues [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- N2a: metastasis in 4–6 regional lymph nodes. N2b: metastasis in 7 or more regional lymph nodes [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- Tumour deposits do not change the T category. Without nodal metastasis they make the case N1c; with nodal metastasis they are not added to the positive-node count [[Tong2018]].
- M0: no distant metastasis. M1a: metastasis confined to one organ or site (e.g. liver, lung, ovary, non-regional node) without peritoneal metastasis [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- M1b: metastasis to two or more organs or sites, without peritoneal metastasis [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- M1c: metastasis to the peritoneal surface, alone or with other site or organ metastases [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- The main change from the 7th to the 8th edition was the new M1c category and stage IVC for peritoneal metastasis. In the 7th edition, peritoneal disease was part of M1b [[Tong2018,Weiser2018]].
- In one cohort of 1,090 patients restaged from the 7th to the 8th edition, stage IVB fell from 2.8% to 0.8% and 2% moved into the new IVC. DFS and OS were significantly shorter for IVC than IVB (P=0.012) [[Tong2018]].
- The 8th edition kept the 7th-edition stage II and III groups unchanged [[Tong2018]].

### staging · AJCC 8th edition stage groups
- Stage 0: Tis N0 M0 [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- Stage I: T1–T2 N0 M0 [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- Stage IIA: T3 N0 M0. Stage IIB: T4a N0 M0. Stage IIC: T4b N0 M0 [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- Stage IIIA: T1–T2 N1/N1c M0, or T1 N2a M0 [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- Stage IIIB: T3–T4a N1/N1c M0, or T2–T3 N2a M0, or T1–T2 N2b M0 [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- Stage IIIC: T4a N2a M0, or T3–T4a N2b M0, or T4b N1–N2 M0 [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- Stage IVA: any T, any N, M1a. Stage IVB: any T, any N, M1b. Stage IVC: any T, any N, M1c [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- The 7th-edition substaging was based on SEER (109,953 colon cancers, 1992–2004) and NCDB (134,206) analyses. They showed that T1–2N2 tumours do better than T3–4N2, that T4bN1 behaves like T4N2, and that T4a does better than T4b within each N category [[Gunderson2010]].
- On that evidence, T1–2N2 moved from IIIC to IIIA/IIIB, T4bN1 moved from IIIB to IIIC, and T4, N1 and N2 were subdivided. Survival by TN category was "strikingly similar" for colon and rectal cancer [[Gunderson2010]].
- A newer AJCC 9th edition (Version 9) colon cancer staging has been released. A single-centre Chinese study of 1,222 stage I–III colon cancers found that 79.9% changed stage on restaging (58.4% up, 21.4% down), with slightly better overall discrimination (C-index 0.668 vs 0.665 for OS) (Early evidence) [[Song2026]].
- In that 9th-edition study, each additional tumour deposit raised the hazard of death by 20.4% (HR 1.204, 95% CI 1.067–1.359) (Early evidence) [[Song2026]].

### staging · Machine-readable T/N/M → stage group (AJCC 8th)
Rows are evaluated in order. M overrides T and N. Source: [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].

```csv
stage,T,N,M
0,Tis,N0,M0
I,T1|T2,N0,M0
IIA,T3,N0,M0
IIB,T4a,N0,M0
IIC,T4b,N0,M0
IIIA,T1|T2,N1a|N1b|N1c,M0
IIIA,T1,N2a,M0
IIIB,T3|T4a,N1a|N1b|N1c,M0
IIIB,T2|T3,N2a,M0
IIIB,T1|T2,N2b,M0
IIIC,T4a,N2a,M0
IIIC,T3|T4a,N2b,M0
IIIC,T4b,N1a|N1b|N1c|N2a|N2b,M0
IVA,any,any,M1a
IVB,any,any,M1b
IVC,any,any,M1c
```

- Every T×N combination with M0 maps to exactly one row above. For example, T4bN0 is IIC, T2N2a is IIIB and T4aN2b is IIIC [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- Prefixes: "c" is clinical, "p" is pathological, and "yp" is pathological staging after neoadjuvant therapy. The College of American Pathologists rated residual tumour after neoadjuvant therapy (ypTNM) a category IIA prognostic factor [[Compton2000]].

### staging · Older systems: Dukes and Astler–Coller
- Dukes' 1932 classification of rectal cancer: A = growth limited to the bowel wall, B = spread through the wall into extrarectal tissues without nodal metastasis, C = regional lymph-node metastasis. An informal "D" for distant metastasis was added later (unverified) [[Dukes1932]].
- Approximate mapping to TNM: Dukes A ≈ T1–T2 N0 (stage I), Dukes B ≈ T3–T4 N0 (stage II), Dukes C ≈ any T N1–N2 (stage III), "Dukes D" ≈ M1 (stage IV) (unverified) [[Dukes1932,Amin2017AJCC]].
- The Astler–Coller modification (1954) split Dukes B and C by depth of direct extension: B1/C1 within the muscularis propria, B2/C2 through it, and A limited to the mucosa. Mapping: B1 ≈ T2N0, B2 ≈ T3N0, C1 ≈ T2N+, C2 ≈ T3–4N+ (unverified) [[Astler1954]].
- TNM is preferred over Dukes because it separately records depth (T), the number of positive nodes (N) and metastatic sites (M), which the newer prognostic subgroups depend on [[Gunderson2010,Tong2018]].

### staging · Lymph-node yield: the 12-node standard
- In a systematic review (17 studies, 61,371 patients), 16 of 17 studies found that survival in stage II colon cancer rose with the number of lymph nodes evaluated. Four of six studies found the same in stage III [[Chang2007]].
- Thresholds in those studies ranged from 6 to 40 nodes. The authors proposed the node count as a quality measure for colon cancer care [[Chang2007]].
- In INT-0089 (3,411 patients with high-risk stage II and stage III colon cancer), the median number of nodes removed was 11 (range 1–87) [[LeVoyer2003]].
- In INT-0089, after controlling for the number of positive nodes, survival improved as more nodes were analysed. This held even in node-negative patients (OS P=0.0005; cause-specific survival P=0.007) [[LeVoyer2003]].
- ASCO 2022 lists sampling of fewer than 12 lymph nodes as a high-risk factor in stage II colon cancer [[Baxter2022]].
- The College of American Pathologists rated the pN category and blood or lymphatic vessel invasion as category I prognostic factors [[Compton2000]].

### staging · High-risk stage II and other pathology risk features
- ASCO 2004: routine adjuvant chemotherapy was not recommended for medically fit patients with stage II colon cancer. Groups that could be considered for it included those with inadequately sampled nodes, T4 lesions, perforation or poorly differentiated histology [[Benson2004]].
- The ASCO 2022 guideline update says adjuvant chemotherapy is not routine for stage II colon cancer outside a high-risk subgroup [[Baxter2022]].
- ASCO 2022: patients with T4 tumours are at higher risk of recurrence and should be offered adjuvant chemotherapy [[Baxter2022]].
- ASCO 2022: other high-risk factors that may lead to an offer of adjuvant chemotherapy are fewer than 12 lymph nodes sampled, perineural or lymphovascular invasion, poorly or undifferentiated grade, intestinal obstruction, tumour perforation, and grade BD3 tumour budding [[Baxter2022]].
- ASCO 2022: patients with dMMR/MSI tumours should not routinely be offered adjuvant chemotherapy. If dMMR plus high-risk features leads to an offer, oxaliplatin-containing chemotherapy is recommended [[Baxter2022]].
- The ITBCC consensus defines a tumour bud as a single tumour cell or a cluster of 4 cells or fewer [[Lugli2017]].
- ITBCC counts budding on H&E in one hotspot (a field of 0.785 mm²) at the invasive front and reports it on a three-tier system (BD1–BD3) with the count [[Lugli2017]].
- ITBCC agreed that budding independently predicts lymph-node metastasis in pT1 CRC and survival in stage II CRC, and that budding and tumour grade are not the same [[Lugli2017]].
- Tumour deposits were present in 22% of 10,106 patients with CRC in a systematic review and were invariably linked with poor outcome [[Nagtegaal2017]].
- Tumour deposits combined with nodal metastasis were the strongest predictor of liver (OR 5.5), lung (OR 4.3) and peritoneal (OR 7.0) metastases [[Nagtegaal2017]].
- The authors argued that the N1c rule (deposits counted only when nodes are negative) loses prognostic information, and proposed adding the number of deposits to the number of positive nodes (Early evidence) [[Nagtegaal2017]].
- Perineural invasion (PNI) appeared in under 0.5% of original pathology reports, but 22% of 269 tumours were PNI-positive on blinded re-review [[Liebig2009]].
- 5-year DFS was 65% for PNI-negative vs 16% for PNI-positive tumours, and 5-year OS was 72% vs 25%. PNI was an independent prognostic factor on multivariate analysis [[Liebig2009]].
- Node-negative, PNI-positive tumours had 29% 5-year DFS, worse than stage III overall (56%) in that series [[Liebig2009]].

### staging · Histology and grade
- In 3,479 consecutive resected CRCs (Germany, 1982–2012), 88% were classical adenocarcinoma, 11% mucinous adenocarcinoma and 0.9% signet-ring cell carcinoma [[Nitsche2013]].
- Mucinous (51%) and signet-ring (50%) cancers were more often right-sided than classical adenocarcinoma (28%), and were diagnosed at higher stage and grade [[Nitsche2013]].
- 5-year cause-specific survival was 67% for adenocarcinoma, 61% for mucinous and 21% for signet-ring cell carcinoma [[Nitsche2013]].
- After adjustment for stage, mucinous histology was not independently prognostic. Signet-ring histology remained independently worse (HR 2.5, 95% CI 1.6–3.8) [[Nitsche2013]].
- In 1,675 autopsied people with metastatic CRC, metastatic disease was more frequent in mucinous (33.9%) and signet-ring (61.2%) than in classical adenocarcinoma (27.6%) [[Hugen2014]].
- Classical adenocarcinoma spread mainly to the liver. Mucinous and signet-ring cancers more often spread to the peritoneum [[Hugen2014]].
- The College of American Pathologists rated tumour grade a category IIA prognostic factor [[Compton2000]].
- MSI-high tumours tend to arise in the proximal colon and to show a lymphocytic infiltrate and poorly differentiated, mucinous or signet-ring appearance [[Boland2010]].

### staging · Survival by stage (population statistics)
- US 5-year relative survival for colorectal cancer, diagnosed 2015–2021, by SEER summary stage: localized 91%, regional 74%, distant 15% [[Siegel2026]].
- Colon alone: localized 91%, regional 74%, distant 14%. Rectum alone: localized 90%, regional 74%, distant 18% [[Siegel2026]].
- For distant-stage colorectal cancer, 5-year relative survival rose from 5% (1975–1977) to 8% (1995–1997) to 15% (2015–2021). Regional-stage survival rose from 52% to 66% to 74% over the same periods [[Siegel2026]].
- Distant-stage rectal cancer had one of the largest gains of any cancer, from 8% in the mid-1990s to 18% [[Siegel2026,Siegel2026CRC]].
- Overall 5-year relative survival for CRC rose from 50% in the mid-1970s to 65% for diagnoses in 2015–2021 [[Siegel2026CRC]].
- Regional-stage 5-year relative survival ranges from 69% in people aged 65 or older to 81% in people under 50 [[Siegel2026CRC]].
- Distant-stage 5-year relative survival is 23% under age 50, 17% at ages 50–64 and 10% at 65 or older [[Siegel2026CRC]].
- Distant metastases are present at diagnosis in 27% of people under 50 vs 21–23% of older patients. Regional stage was the most common stage at diagnosis in 2018–2022 [[Siegel2026CRC]].
- 60% of new CRC cases were advanced (regional or distant) at diagnosis in 2019, vs 52% in the mid-2000s and 57% in 1995 [[Siegel2023CRC]].
- Substage survival under the AJCC 6th edition (SEER 1991–2000, 119,363 colon adenocarcinomas): 5-year survival was 93.2% for stage I, 84.7% IIA, 72.2% IIB, 83.4% IIIA, 64.1% IIIB, 44.3% IIIC and 8.1% IV [[OConnell2004]].
- The "survival paradox": in that SEER analysis, stage IIIA (83.4%) did significantly better than stage IIB (72.2%; P<0.001). The authors suggested this partly reflects stage III patients getting chemotherapy while stage II patients generally did not [[OConnell2004]].
- The paradox persists under the 8th edition. In SEER 2018–2021 (4,692 patients), 3-year cancer-specific survival was 73.1% for IIB and 70.3% for IIC vs 91% for IIIA. 3-year OS was 64.9%, 63.0% and 83.1% respectively [[Yu2024]].
- Adjusted for other factors, stage IIIA carried a lower risk of cancer-specific death than IIB (HR 0.374, 95% CI 0.296–0.473) [[Yu2024]].
- In SEER 2010–2015 (9,227 patients), stage IIIA had a 66.4% lower risk of colon cancer-specific death than IIB (HR 0.336, 95% CI 0.286–0.394). T1N2a had a 5-year OS of 74.7% vs 57.1% for T2N1 [[Li2020]].
- In a single-centre cohort of 1,090 patients staged by the 8th edition, 5-year OS was 100% for stage 0, 98.5% I, 82.6% IIA, 76.8% IIB, 67.7% IIC, 65.4% IIIA, 60% IIIB, 44.9% IIIC, 8.3% IVA, 0% IVB and 0% IVC. The IV substages were very small [[Tong2018]].

### biomarkers · Mismatch repair (MMR) and microsatellite instability (MSI)
- MSI is a hypermutable phenotype caused by loss of DNA mismatch-repair activity. It is detected in about 15% of all CRCs: about 3% are associated with Lynch syndrome and about 12% are caused by sporadic MLH1 promoter hypermethylation [[Boland2010]].
- The Revised Bethesda Guidelines state that cancers with MSI account for approximately 15% of all CRCs [[Umar2004]].
- dMMR occurs in 15–20% of all CRCs. About three-quarters of these are due to MLH1 epigenetic silencing [[Sepulveda2017]].
- About three-quarters of CRCs with MSI due to MLH1 promoter hypermethylation also carry an acquired BRAF mutation [[Sepulveda2017]].
- Fewer than one-third of people with dMMR/MSI CRC have a germline mutation in one of the four MMR genes (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2) or, rarely, an EPCAM deletion. This is Lynch syndrome [[Sepulveda2017]].
- Guideline: clinicians should order MMR status testing in patients with CRC to identify those at high risk of Lynch syndrome and/or for prognostic stratification [[Sepulveda2017]].
- Guideline: BRAF p.V600 testing should be done in dMMR tumours with loss of MLH1. A BRAF mutation strongly favours sporadic disease, but its absence does not exclude Lynch syndrome [[Sepulveda2017]].
- EGAPP (2009) found sufficient evidence to recommend offering Lynch syndrome testing to everyone with newly diagnosed CRC, to reduce illness and death in relatives. It found no single testing strategy clearly superior [[EGAPP2009]].
- EGAPP: about 3% of newly diagnosed CRCs are caused by an MMR gene mutation (Lynch syndrome). Relatives who inherit the mutation have about a 45% risk of CRC by age 70 [[EGAPP2009]].
- ESMO recommends immunohistochemistry for MLH1, MSH2, MSH6 and PMS2 as the first test for MSI/dMMR. PCR of microsatellite markers (five markers including at least BAT-25 and BAT-26) is the second method [[Luchini2019]].
- dMMR frequency by site in QUASAR (1,913 patients, mainly stage II): 26% of right-sided colon, 3% of left-sided colon and 1% of rectal tumours [[Hutchins2011]].
- Frequency by stage: about 15% in stage II–III adjuvant-trial cohorts (70 of 457, 15%; 95 of 570, 16.7%) [[Sargent2010,Ribic2003]].
- MSI-H was found in 17% of 607 population-based CRCs in people aged 50 or younger [[Gryfe2000]].
- Frequency in metastatic disease is much lower: 3.5% (18/515) in the CAIRO trial and 5.0% (153/3,063) in a pooled analysis of four first-line trials [[Koopman2009,Venderbosch2014]].
- In stage II–III colon cancer not given adjuvant therapy, MSI-H tumours had better 5-year OS than MSS/MSI-L tumours (HR for death 0.31, 95% CI 0.14–0.72) [[Ribic2003]].
- MSI-H tumours were less likely to have spread to regional nodes (OR 0.33) or distant organs (OR 0.49), independent of depth of invasion [[Gryfe2000]].
- Meta-analysis of 32 studies (7,642 cases): MSI was associated with better overall survival (HR 0.65, 95% CI 0.59–0.71) [[Popat2005]].
- Meta-analysis of 31 studies (12,782 patients): MSI was associated with better OS (OR 0.6) and DFS (OR 0.58) [[Guastadisegni2010]].
- In QUASAR, recurrence was half as common with dMMR as with pMMR tumours (11% vs 26%; RR 0.53, 95% CI 0.40–0.70) [[Hutchins2011]].
- In PETACC-3, MSI-H was strongly prognostic in stage II (RFS HR 0.26; OS HR 0.16) and less so in stage III (RFS HR 0.67; OS HR 0.70, not significant) [[Klingbiel2015]].
- Sargent 2010 (457 randomised patients, pooled to 1,027): fluorouracil (FU) adjuvant therapy improved DFS in pMMR tumours (HR 0.67, 95% CI 0.48–0.93) but not in dMMR tumours (HR 1.10, 0.42–2.91) [[Sargent2010]].
- In the pooled data set, patients with stage II dMMR tumours who received FU had worse overall survival than those who had surgery alone (HR 2.95, 95% CI 1.02–8.54) [[Sargent2010]].
- Ribic 2003: adjuvant FU improved OS in MSS/MSI-L tumours (HR 0.72, 0.53–0.99). There was no benefit in MSI-H tumours (test for interaction P=0.01) [[Ribic2003]].
- Not all data agree on prediction. In QUASAR, no marker (MMR, KRAS, BRAF) predicted benefit from FU chemotherapy [[Hutchins2011]].
- In metastatic disease, dMMR was associated with worse progression-free survival (PFS; HR 1.33) and OS (HR 1.35) in the pooled four-trial analysis. This effect appeared driven by co-existing BRAF mutation [[Venderbosch2014]].
- dMMR/MSI-H in metastatic CRC predicts benefit from PD-1 blockade. In KEYNOTE-177 (307 patients), first-line pembrolizumab gave median PFS of 16.5 vs 8.2 months with chemotherapy (HR 0.60, 95% CI 0.45–0.80) [[Andre2020]].

### biomarkers · RAS (KRAS/NRAS) and anti-EGFR antibodies
- KRAS, NRAS and BRAF mutations together have been reported in more than half of all CRCs. RAS and BRAF mutations are largely mutually exclusive [[Sepulveda2017]].
- KRAS exon 2 mutations were found in 43% of 427 tumours in the panitumumab monotherapy trial and in 42.3% in the CO.17 cetuximab trial [[Amado2008,Karapetis2008]].
- KRAS exon 2 and BRAF mutation rates were 37.0% and 7.9% in stage II–III colon cancer (PETACC-3, 1,404 tumours), and did not differ by stage [[Roth2010]].
- In PRIME, 108 of 639 patients (17%) without KRAS exon 2 mutations had other RAS mutations (KRAS exons 3–4, NRAS exons 2–4) [[Douillard2013]].
- Panitumumab vs best supportive care: the PFS benefit was confined to wild-type KRAS (HR 0.45, 95% CI 0.34–0.59) vs mutant KRAS (HR 0.99, 0.73–1.36). Response rates were 17% vs 0% [[Amado2008]].
- Cetuximab vs supportive care in wild-type KRAS: median OS 9.5 vs 4.8 months (HR 0.55, 95% CI 0.41–0.74). In mutant KRAS there was no difference (HR 0.98) [[Karapetis2008]].
- PRIME, RAS wild-type (n=512): panitumumab-FOLFOX4 vs FOLFOX4 gave median PFS 10.1 vs 7.9 months (HR 0.72) and median OS 26.0 vs 20.2 months (HR 0.78, 95% CI 0.62–0.99) [[Douillard2013]].
- Additional RAS mutations beyond KRAS exon 2 predicted lack of benefit from panitumumab-FOLFOX4, as KRAS exon 2 mutations did [[Douillard2013]].
- Guideline: patients being considered for anti-EGFR therapy must have RAS testing of KRAS and NRAS codons 12 and 13 (exon 2), 59 and 61 (exon 3), and 117 and 146 (exon 4) ("extended RAS") [[Sepulveda2017]].
- Guideline: test methods should detect mutations at 5% or more mutant allele frequency. Metastatic or recurrent tissue is preferred for predictive testing, and primary tumour is acceptable if it is not available [[Sepulveda2017]].
- US labels: cetuximab is not indicated for Ras-mutant CRC or when Ras results are unknown. Panitumumab is indicated for RAS wild-type mCRC (wild-type in both KRAS and NRAS) and is not indicated for RAS-mutant mCRC, except with sotorasib in KRAS G12C-mutated mCRC [[FDA-Erbitux,FDA-Vectibix]].
- KRAS mutation was not strongly prognostic for relapse in stage II–III colon cancer in PETACC-3. In QUASAR, KRAS-mutant tumours had a higher recurrence risk (28% vs 21%; RR 1.40) [[Roth2010,Hutchins2011]].

### biomarkers · Primary tumour sidedness
- Left-sided tumours (splenic flexure, descending colon, sigmoid, rectum) are defined against right-sided tumours (appendix, caecum, ascending colon, hepatic flexure, transverse colon) [[Tejpar2017]].
- Across 66 studies (1,437,846 patients), a left-sided primary was associated with a lower risk of death (HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.79–0.84), independent of stage [[Petrelli2017]].
- In six first-line trials (RAS wild-type mCRC, 2,159 patients), right-sided tumours had worse OS than left-sided in both the control (HR 2.03) and anti-EGFR (HR 1.38) arms [[Arnold2017]].
- Anti-EGFR plus chemotherapy benefited left-sided tumours (OS HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.67–0.84; PFS HR 0.78) but not right-sided tumours (OS HR 1.12, 0.87–1.45). Interaction P<0.001 for OS [[Arnold2017]].
- In CRYSTAL and FIRE-3 (RAS wild-type), FOLFIRI plus cetuximab clearly benefited left-sided tumours. Right-sided tumours derived limited benefit from standard treatments [[Tejpar2017]].
- These sidedness results are retrospective subgroup analyses and should be interpreted with caution (Established for prognosis; predictive effect from retrospective data) [[Arnold2017]].

### biomarkers · BRAF V600E
- BRAF activating mutations occur in about 8% of patients with advanced CRC and about 14% with localised stage II–III CRC [[Sepulveda2017]].
- In 3,063 patients with metastatic CRC, 8.2% had BRAF mutations. BRAF mutation occurred in 34.6% of dMMR vs 6.8% of pMMR tumours [[Venderbosch2014]].
- In 524 metastatic patients, 11% had BRAF-mutant tumours, which were linked with right-sided primaries and MSI, and with shorter median survival (10.4 vs 34.7 months) [[Tran2011]].
- BRAF-mutant metastatic CRC more often spread to the peritoneum (46% vs 24%) and distant nodes (53% vs 38%) and less often to the lungs (35% vs 49%) [[Tran2011]].
- In pooled metastatic trials, BRAF mutation was associated with worse OS (HR 1.91, 95% CI 1.66–2.19) [[Venderbosch2014]].
- In stage II–III colon cancer, BRAF mutation was prognostic for OS in MSI-L/MSS tumours (HR 2.2, 95% CI 1.4–3.4) but not for relapse-free survival [[Roth2010]].
- Guideline: BRAF p.V600 testing should be done in selected patients for prognostic stratification. There was insufficient evidence to use it as a predictive marker for anti-EGFR response [[Sepulveda2017]].
- BRAF V600E is now also a treatment target. In BEACON (665 previously treated patients), encorafenib-binimetinib-cetuximab gave median OS 9.0 vs 5.4 months (HR 0.52) and response 26% vs 2% [[Kopetz2019]].

### biomarkers · HER2, KRAS G12C, NTRK and other rare targets
- HER2-positive (amplified) tumours were found in 48 of 914 screened patients (5%) with KRAS exon 2 wild-type metastatic CRC [[SartoreBianchi2016]].
- HERACLES defined HER2 positivity by immunohistochemistry plus FISH using CRC-specific criteria. Trastuzumab plus lapatinib produced responses in 8 of 27 refractory patients (30%) [[SartoreBianchi2016]].
- In DESTINY-CRC01, trastuzumab deruxtecan produced responses (45.3%) only in HER2-positive tumours (IHC 3+ or IHC 2+/ISH+). There were no responses in IHC 2+/ISH− or IHC 1+ tumours [[Yoshino2023]].
- The NCCN colon guideline (2021) expanded biomarker-testing recommendations and added targeted options for HER2-amplified and BRAF V600E-mutant mCRC [[Benson2021]].
- KRAS G12C occurs in approximately 3–4% of patients with metastatic CRC [[Fakih2023]].
- In CodeBreaK 300, sotorasib 960 mg plus panitumumab gave median PFS 5.6 vs 2.2 months with standard care (HR 0.49, 95% CI 0.30–0.80) in chemorefractory KRAS G12C mCRC [[Fakih2023]].
- Adagrasib plus cetuximab gave a 46% response rate in heavily pretreated KRAS G12C mCRC, vs 19% with adagrasib alone [[Yaeger2023]].
- ALK, ROS1 and NTRK fusions together occur in 0.2–2.4% of CRCs. They cluster in older patients with right-sided, RAS wild-type, MSI-high tumours [[Pietrantonio2017]].
- People with these fusions had shorter OS (15.6 vs 33.7 months; adjusted HR 2.33). All four evaluable patients were primarily resistant to anti-EGFR agents [[Pietrantonio2017]].
- Pan-Trk immunohistochemistry screening for NTRK fusions had 100% specificity in colon carcinomas. Overall sensitivity was 87.9%, lower for NTRK3 fusions (79%) [[Solomon2020]].
- PIK3CA mutations occur in 10–18% of CRCs. The guideline gave no recommendation for PIK3CA or PTEN testing outside trials [[Sepulveda2017]].

### biomarkers · ctDNA (brief)
- ctDNA was detectable in 73% of people with localised CRC, and in more than 75% of people with advanced CRC, in a 640-patient multi-cancer study [[Bettegowda2014]].
- Before surgery, ctDNA was detected in 108 of 122 people (88.5%) with stage I–III CRC using a tumour-informed, personalised assay [[Reinert2019]].
- Blood-based screening test (ECLIPSE, 7,861 average-risk people): sensitivity was 83.1% for CRC and 13.2% for advanced precancerous lesions, and specificity 89.6% for advanced neoplasia [[Chung2024]].
- After resection of stage II colon cancer without chemotherapy, 11 of 14 ctDNA-positive patients (79%) recurred vs 16 of 164 ctDNA-negative patients (9.8%) (HR 18) [[Tie2016]].
- At postoperative day 30, ctDNA-positive patients with stage I–III CRC were 7 times more likely to relapse (HR 7.2). Serial ctDNA detected relapse up to 16.5 months (mean 8.7) ahead of imaging [[Reinert2019]].
- DYNAMIC: ctDNA-guided management of stage II colon cancer cut adjuvant chemotherapy use from 28% to 15% with non-inferior 2-year recurrence-free survival (93.5% vs 92.4%) [[Tie2022]].
- ESMO (2022): ctDNA can be used to find actionable mutations in advanced cancer. Molecular residual disease detection in early cancer has high clinical validity but cannot yet be recommended for routine practice because clinical utility is unproven [[Pascual2022]].
- In metastatic CRC, ctDNA detected clinically relevant KRAS mutations with 87.2% sensitivity and 99.2% specificity against tissue (206 patients) [[Bettegowda2014]].

### Exam hooks
- MERCURY: MRI predicts a clear CRM with 92% specificity. An MRI-involved CRM (tumour ≤1 mm from the mesorectal fascia) roughly doubles the risk of death (HR 1.97) and raises local recurrence 3.5-fold [[MERCURY2006,Taylor2014]].
- Pelvic MRI is good for CRM and T category but poor for nodes (node specificity 71%) [[AlSukhni2012]].
- About 1 in 25 people with CRC has a synchronous second CRC, so the whole colon should be examined, ideally before surgery [[Mulder2011]].
- CEA is not a screening or early-diagnosis test. It is ordered before surgery for prognosis and planning, and serially for surveillance [[Duffy2001,Locker2006]].
- CEA cut-off trade-off for recurrence: 2.5 µg/L gives 82% sensitivity/80% specificity; 10 µg/L gives 68%/97% [[Nicholson2015]].
- T1 = submucosa; T2 = muscularis propria; T3 = through the muscularis propria into pericolorectal tissue; T4a = visceral peritoneum; T4b = other organs or structures [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- N1a = 1 node; N1b = 2–3; N1c = tumour deposits with no positive nodes; N2a = 4–6; N2b = 7 or more [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- M1a = one organ; M1b = two or more organs; M1c = peritoneum (new in the 8th edition, giving stage IVC) [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]].
- Stage IIIA can have better survival than stage IIB/IIC (the "survival paradox"), because T4 depth matters more than a few positive nodes and stage III gets chemotherapy [[OConnell2004,Yu2024]].
- At least 12 lymph nodes should be examined. Fewer than 12 counts as a high-risk feature in stage II [[Baxter2022,Chang2007]].
- High-risk stage II features (ASCO 2022): T4, fewer than 12 nodes, perineural or lymphovascular invasion, poor or undifferentiated grade, obstruction, perforation, and BD3 budding [[Baxter2022]].
- A tumour bud is 1–4 tumour cells at the invasive front [[Lugli2017]].
- MSI-H/dMMR is about 15% of all CRC (3% Lynch, 12% sporadic MLH1 methylation). It is about 15% in stage II–III and only about 3.5–5% in metastatic disease [[Boland2010,Sargent2010,Koopman2009,Venderbosch2014]].
- dMMR in stage II means a good prognosis and no benefit from fluorouracil alone [[Sargent2010,Ribic2003]].
- A BRAF V600E mutation in an MLH1-deficient tumour points to sporadic disease, not Lynch syndrome [[Sepulveda2017]].
- Extended RAS testing (KRAS and NRAS exons 2, 3, 4) is required before cetuximab or panitumumab. RAS-mutant tumours do not benefit [[Sepulveda2017,Douillard2013]].
- Anti-EGFR benefit is concentrated in left-sided RAS wild-type tumours [[Arnold2017]].
- Signet-ring histology (under 1% of CRC) is independently prognostic. Mucinous histology is not, after stage adjustment [[Nitsche2013]].

### In plain words
- If bowel symptoms suggest cancer, a simple stool test (FIT) helps doctors decide who needs a camera test first [[Monahan2022]].
- A colonoscopy lets the doctor see the whole bowel and take a small piece (biopsy) to confirm cancer. About 1 in 25 people has a second cancer elsewhere in the bowel, so the whole colon is checked [[Mulder2011]].
- A CT scan of the chest, belly and pelvis looks for spread. For rectal cancer, an MRI shows how close the tumour is to the edge the surgeon must cut along [[MERCURY2006,Nerad2016]].
- CEA is a blood marker. It is not good at finding early cancer, but doctors use it to plan treatment and to watch for the cancer coming back [[Duffy2001,Locker2006]].
- Stage describes how deep the tumour has grown (T), whether lymph nodes are involved (N), and whether it has spread to distant places (M) [[Amin2017AJCC]].
- The surgeon removes lymph nodes with the tumour. The pathologist should check at least 12 to stage the cancer properly [[Baxter2022,Chang2007]].
- Survival numbers are averages for large groups. They cannot tell any one person what will happen [[Siegel2026]].
- In the US, about 91 in 100 people with cancer found only in the bowel are alive 5 years later compared with people without cancer. The figure is about 74 in 100 when it has reached nearby areas, and about 15 in 100 when it has spread far [[Siegel2026]].
- Every new colorectal cancer should be tested for mismatch repair. The result can point to an inherited condition (Lynch syndrome) that matters for the family [[Sepulveda2017,EGAPP2009]].
- For cancer that has spread, testing for RAS, BRAF, HER2 and MSI helps choose medicines that are more likely to work [[Sepulveda2017,Benson2021]].
- Where the cancer started (left or right side of the colon) can change which drugs help [[Arnold2017]].
- A blood test for tumour DNA after surgery can show who still has hidden cancer cells. It is still being studied for routine use [[Tie2022,Pascual2022]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Survival paradox (IIB/IIC vs IIIA): it persists in SEER under the 8th edition [[Yu2024,Li2020]]. The 9th edition revises substaging with better overall monotonicity in early single-centre data [[Song2026]]. Status: open; T4 biology and chemotherapy use are both proposed explanations.
- Tumour deposits: the 8th edition counts them only as N1c when nodes are negative [[Amin2017AJCC,Tong2018]]. The meta-analysis authors argue the deposit count should be added to the positive-node count [[Nagtegaal2017]], and 9th-edition data show risk rising with each deposit [[Song2026]]. Status: under revision.
- Does dMMR predict lack of fluorouracil benefit? Yes in pooled randomised data, with possible harm in stage II [[Sargent2010,Ribic2003]]. QUASAR found no predictive effect [[Hutchins2011]], and a meta-analysis found MSI data too heterogeneous to conclude [[Guastadisegni2010]]. Status: guidelines act on the prognostic value and do not offer fluorouracil alone to stage II dMMR [[Baxter2022]].
- Sidedness as a predictive marker for anti-EGFR: a strong signal in retrospective pooled data [[Arnold2017,Tejpar2017]], but the authors caution that it comes from subgroups of trials not designed for it [[Arnold2017]]. Status: used in practice; evidence is retrospective.
- CEA cut-off for surveillance: lower thresholds raise sensitivity but cause many false alarms. Cochrane favours 10 µg/L plus imaging [[Nicholson2015]], while ASCO 2006 set no specific threshold [[Locker2006]]. Status: practice varies.
- ctDNA after surgery: very high clinical validity for predicting relapse [[Tie2016,Reinert2019]] and a successful de-escalation trial in stage II [[Tie2022]]. ESMO 2022 still did not recommend routine use [[Pascual2022]]. Status: evolving.
- Perineural invasion is under-reported (under 0.5% in original reports vs 22% on review), which suggests that real-world high-risk stage II classification is incomplete [[Liebig2009]]. Status: reporting is now standardised in guidelines [[Baxter2022]].
