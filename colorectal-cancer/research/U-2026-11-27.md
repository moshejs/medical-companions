## Findings

### genes · CaPP3: aspirin dose in Lynch syndrome (catch-up, published online 2026-07-09)
- CaPP3 was a multicentre, randomised, double-blind, dose non-inferiority trial in Lynch syndrome carriers older than 18 years in the UK, Australia, Finland, Israel and Spain. 1879 people were randomised 3:3:4 to aspirin 100 mg (564), 300 mg (565) or 600 mg (750) a day; blinded for 2 years, then open label on the same dose for 3 more years (in the UK, 75 mg replaced 100 mg in the open phase). Doses as published, not as instructions. (Early evidence) [[Burn2026]]
- The intention-to-treat population was 1866; 730 (39.1%) stopped aspirin within the 2-year blinded phase and 937 (50.2%) completed 5 years. [[Burn2026]]
- Primary outcome, new mismatch repair-deficient (Lynch syndrome) cancers, at a median follow-up of 66.4 months: 216 cancers in 176 participants (57 on 100 mg, 75 on 300 mg, 84 on 600 mg). Colorectal cancer occurred in 83 of 1846 participants (21, 30 and 32). [[Burn2026]]
- 100 mg vs 600 mg, intention to treat: HR for time to first Lynch syndrome cancer 0.97 (95% CI 0.67–1.42); IRR for cancer burden 0.94 (0.65–1.38). The non-inferiority margin was 1.5. In the per-protocol analysis the cancer burden met the margin (IRR 0.90; 0.55–1.46) but time to first cancer did not (HR 1.00; 0.61–1.63). [[Burn2026]]
- 300 mg vs 600 mg: non-inferiority was not established in either analysis (ITT HR 1.28, 0.91–1.80; IRR 1.12, 0.79–1.61). [[Burn2026]]
- Adverse events of interest in the blinded phase rose with dose: 25.0% (140/561), 26.8% (151/564) and 31.2% (234/750); p = 0.03. Serious adverse events due to bleeding over 5 years: none on 100 mg, 3 (0.5%) on 300 mg and 11 (1.5%) on 600 mg; p = 0.004. [[Burn2026]]
- The authors' interpretation: non-inferiority "could not formally be concluded" for either lower dose, but there was evidence that 100 mg "had similar characteristics to 600 mg in terms of cancer risk but with fewer side-effects and less risk of bleeding". Follow-up to 10 years is planned. [[Burn2026]]
- There was no placebo arm, so CaPP3 does not re-test whether aspirin works; that evidence is CAPP2 [[Burn2020]].

### Exam hooks
- CaPP3 (1879 Lynch carriers): 100 mg aspirin looked similar to 600 mg for Lynch cancers (ITT HR 0.97) with no serious bleeds vs 1.5%, but non-inferiority was not formally concluded [[Burn2026]].

### In plain words
- A large trial compared a low, a medium and a high daily dose of aspirin in people with Lynch syndrome. The low dose seemed to protect about as well as the high dose and caused less bleeding, but the trial could not prove this formally [[Burn2026]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Best aspirin dose in Lynch syndrome: 600 mg has the placebo-controlled evidence [[Burn2020]]; 100 mg looked similar in CaPP3 without formal non-inferiority [[Burn2026]]; the guidelines the site cites predate CaPP3 [[Seppala2021,Monahan2020]]. Status: open; 10-year follow-up planned.
