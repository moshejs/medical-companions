2020 changes

What moved in 2020

1–2 small tubular adenomas moved from 5–10 to 7–10 years; more than 10 adenomas became 1 year instead of under 3 years; 3–4 small adenomas may be 3–5 years. After a normal first surveillance exam, the next is 10 years if the baseline was 1–4 small adenomas, and 5 years if it was an advanced adenoma or 5–10 adenomas. 209

Transatlantic divergence

ESGE: back to screening

ESGE 2020 says people who had 1–4 adenomas <10 mm with low-grade dysplasia, or any serrated polyp <10 mm without dysplasia, completely removed, need no surveillance and return to screening (10 years if there is no programme). It recommends 3-year surveillance after an adenoma ≥10 mm, high-grade dysplasia, ≥5 adenomas, or a serrated polyp ≥10 mm or with dysplasia. In PLCO, non-advanced adenomas carried no significant excess long-term risk (RR 1.2). 105,227

Genetics

When to think of a syndrome

People with more than 10 adenomas, or more than 10 cumulative over a lifetime, may need genetic testing. Serrated polyposis: more than 20 hyperplastic polyps throughout the colon (≥5 proximal to the rectum), or ≥5 serrated polyps larger than 5 mm proximal to the rectum with ≥2 of at least 10 mm. 209

Start at 45 or 50? USPSTF, ACS and USMSTF start at 45, citing rising early-onset incidence and modelling: CRC incidence at 40–49 rose almost 15% from 2000–2002 to 2014–2016. The ACP starts at 50, and USMSTF itself notes that no study yet shows screening under 50 improves CRC incidence or mortality. The question is unresolved; US practice largely follows 45. 422,440,545,585
