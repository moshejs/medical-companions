# C. Genetics
Scope: the genetic architecture of Parkinson's disease (PD) for the "Genes and inheritance" module. It covers monogenic and risk-variant shares, heritability, GWAS and polygenic risk, LRRK2, GBA1, SNCA, the recessive early-onset genes and VPS35, Israeli data, and published statements on genetic testing. Numbers come from PubMed/Europe PMC abstracts. Open-access full texts were read for Healy 2008, Kalia 2015, Nalls 2019, Blauwendraat 2020 and PD GENEration (Cook 2024). Access gaps:
- The NEJM letters by Ozelius 2006 and Lesage 2006 have no abstract and their full text returned HTTP 403. Their figures come from contemporary secondary reports, marked as such.
- Only the abstract of the Israeli ROPAD paper (Anis 2025) was available; the full text was paywalled. It was not BGU-led: the first and senior authors are at Sheba Medical Center, and the BGU research portal lists it because of BGU-affiliated co-authors.
- The MDS Task Force page on clinical genetic testing returned 404. No final MDS consensus recommendation on genetic testing was found.
- GeneReviews (NBK1223) was blocked by a CAPTCHA.
- The brief named "Cook 2024 Neurology" and "Lee 2017 meta-analysis". PD GENEration was published in Brain 2024, and Lee 2017 is a kin-cohort study, not a meta-analysis.
- The 50% (dominant) and 25% (recessive sibling) transmission ratios are standard Mendelian arithmetic. No specific source for them was read in this pass.
Penetrance points are given as (age, %, source) for the animation.

## Facts
### Overview: how much of PD is genetic
- About 15% of people with PD have a family history, and 5-10% have a monogenic (Mendelian) form, according to a 2018 review [Deng2018].
- Prospective panel-testing studies that count GBA1 risk variants find higher yields than the "5-10% monogenic" figure. ROPAD found a PD-relevant positive result in 14.8% of 12,580 patients from 16 countries (14.5% adjusted to world population). PD GENEration found a reportable variant in 13% of 8,301 North American participants with results [Westenberger2024; Cook2024]. The controversy is that the "monogenic" figure excludes most GBA1 carriers, while the testing yields include GBA1 risk variants.
- By ROPAD gene, the positive results were GBA1 risk variants 10.4%, LRRK2 2.9%, PRKN 0.9%, SNCA 0.2% and PINK1 0.1%, with about 0.2% carrying findings in two genes [Westenberger2024].
- By PD GENEration gene, reportable variants were GBA1 7.7%, LRRK2 2.4%, PRKN 2.1%, SNCA 0.1% and PINK1/PARK7/VPS35 combined 0.2%, with 0.4% carrying variants in more than one gene [Cook2024].
- Yield by age at onset and family history in ROPAD: onset ≤50 years 19.9%; positive family history 19.5%; both 26.9%. The probability of a positive test fell by 3% for each additional year of onset age, and women were 22% more likely than men to test positive [Westenberger2024].
- Yield in PD GENEration: 18.4% (236/1,276) with onset <50 years versus 11.9% (809/6,773) with onset ≥50. It was 18.0% with an affected first-degree relative and 23.9% with "high-risk ancestry" (Ashkenazi Jewish, Basque or North African Berber). It was 9.1% among people with none of these risk factors [Cook2024].
- A pooled analysis of 25,063 patients (6,295 carriers) found that an onset threshold of ≤50 years captured only 32% (ROPAD), 23% (PD GENEration), 63% (MDSGene literature) and 30% (GP2) of carriers. Adding family history barely improved discrimination (AUC about 0.58-0.60 in the population cohorts) [Balck2025].
- Rare variants in more than 20 genes have been reported to cause PD, and most are highly penetrant and often cause early-onset or atypical disease [Blauwendraat2020].
- Heritability: common genetic variation is estimated to account for about 22% of PD liability [Blauwendraat2020]. The 2019 meta-GWAS estimated SNP-based liability-scale heritability at 0.22 (95% CI 0.18-0.26), slightly below an earlier GCTA estimate of 0.27 (0.17-0.38) [Nalls2019].
- Family history: in a meta-analysis, having a first-degree relative with PD gave an OR of 3.23 (95% CI 2.65-3.93), and any relative with PD an OR of 4.45 (3.39-5.83). These were the strongest risk factors examined [Noyce2012].
- GWAS (Nalls 2019): 7.8 million SNPs were analysed in 37,688 cases, 18,618 UK Biobank "proxy cases" (people with a first-degree relative with PD) and 1.4 million controls, all of European ancestry. The study found 90 independent genome-wide significant risk signals across 78 genomic regions, 38 of them new [Nalls2019].
- The 90 GWAS variants explain 16-36% of the heritable risk of PD, depending on the assumed prevalence (0.5-2%) [Nalls2019].
- Polygenic risk: people in the highest polygenic-risk-score quartile had an OR for PD of 3.74 (95% CI 3.35-4.18) versus the lowest quartile in one cohort, and 6.25 (4.26-9.28) in a validation cohort. Score-based prediction reached an AUC near 0.69 [Nalls2019]. The review summarises genetic risk scoring AUCs as 0.65-0.69 and the highest-versus-lowest quartile risk as about fourfold [Blauwendraat2020].
- Ancestry bias: most GWAS risk variants were found in people of European ancestry, and relatively little is known about PD genetics in other populations [Blauwendraat2020; Nalls2019].

### LRRK2
- Discovery: in 2004 two Neuron papers identified the PARK8 gene. Paisán-Ruiz et al. found missense mutations segregating in five families from England and Spain and named the protein "dardarin", from the Basque word for tremor [PaisanRuiz2004]. Zimprich et al. found six disease-segregating mutations among 46 families and named the gene LRRK2 (leucine-rich repeat kinase 2) [Zimprich2004].
- LRRK2 PD pathology is "pleomorphic". In the first LRRK2 families, six post-mortem brains showed Lewy body PD, diffuse Lewy body disease, nigral degeneration without distinctive histopathology, and PSP-like pathology [Zimprich2004].
- Six mutations meet the International LRRK2 Consortium's criteria for proven pathogenicity. The numbers of unrelated carriers were G2019S (391), R1441G (33), R1441C (9), R1441H (5), I2020T (5) and Y1699C (2) [Healy2008].
- Mechanism: G2019S and R1441C increase LRRK2 kinase activity in vitro (autophosphorylation and substrate phosphorylation), which suggests a kinase gain-of-function mechanism [West2005].
- G2019S frequency worldwide: 1% of sporadic PD, 4% of hereditary PD, and <1% of controls (11/14,886). Frequency is highest in the Middle East/North Africa and higher in southern than northern Europe [Healy2008].
- G2019S frequency by population (sporadic PD / hereditary PD / controls) [Healy2008]:
  - North African Arabs: 39% / 36% / <1% (4/739).
  - Ashkenazi Jews: 10% / 28% / 1% (4/410).
  - Portuguese: 4% / 14% / 0%.
  - Spanish: 3% / 4% / 0%.
  - British: 1% / 2% / 0%.
  - German/Austrian: <1% / 1% / 0%.
  - Asians: <0.1% (4 carriers among 4,172 patients).
- Ashkenazi Jews (Ozelius 2006): G2019S was found in 18.3% (22/120) of Ashkenazi PD patients versus 1.3% (4/317) of Ashkenazi controls. It was found in 29.7% (11/37) of familial cases and 13.3% (11/83) of apparently sporadic cases. These figures come from secondary reports of the letter; the letter itself was not read [Ozelius2006].
- North African Arabs (Lesage 2006): of 104 North African Arab PD patients (with 151 Arab controls), G2019S was found in 37% of familial and 41% of sporadic cases. These figures come from a secondary report; the control frequency was not verified [Lesage2006].
- Penetrance points for G2019S, family-based maximum-likelihood estimates: (59, 28%), (69, 51%), (79, 74%) [Healy2008].
- Penetrance points for G2019S, Kaplan-Meier estimates (index cases excluded): (59, 36%), (69, 59%), (79, 80%) [Healy2008].
- Penetrance points for the other pathogenic LRRK2 mutations combined: (59, 40%), (69, 64%), (79, 84%) [Healy2008].
- Penetrance in Ashkenazi Jews (kin-cohort of 2,270 relatives of 474 probands, New York and Tel Aviv): (80, 26%; 95% CI 18-36%). It was 22% in male and 29% in female predicted carriers. Predicted carriers had a hazard ratio of 2.89 versus non-carriers [Marder2015].
- Penetrance in non-Ashkenazi carriers (kin-cohort of 474 relatives of 69 probands): (80, 42.5%; 95% CI 26.3-65.8%). This was not significantly different from the Ashkenazi estimate of 25% (16.7-34.2%). The authors conclude G2019S penetrance is 25-42.5% at age 80 across the populations studied [Lee2017LRRK2].
- Controversy: published G2019S penetrance estimates range from 24% to 100%. The spread reflects differences in ascertainment (families with many affected members versus unselected relatives), age, sex, ethnicity and modifiers. Family-based estimates (about 74-80% by age 79) far exceed kin-cohort estimates (about 26-42.5% by age 80) [Marder2015; Healy2008; Lee2017LRRK2].
- Clinical picture: LRRK2 PD motor features (severity, rate of progression, falls, dyskinesia) and non-motor features (cognition, olfaction) were "more benign" than in pathologically proven idiopathic PD [Healy2008].
- Clinical numbers from the same comparison [Healy2008]:
  - Dyskinesia after 5 years of treatment: 11% (LRRK2) versus 25% (idiopathic).
  - Dyskinesia after 10 years: 32% versus 41%.
  - Cognitive impairment (MMSE ≤24): 23% versus 70%.
  - Abnormal smell: 51% (22/43) of G2019S patients, which the authors call unexpectedly low compared with the 80-100% reported in idiopathic PD.
- Lewy bodies: in 37 autopsied LRRK2 PD cases, 17 had Lewy bodies and 20 did not; 11 of 17 G2019S cases had Lewy bodies. Cases with Lewy bodies were more likely to have had cognitive impairment/dementia, anxiety and orthostatic hypotension, while cases without Lewy bodies had a mainly motor phenotype [Kalia2015].
- Lewy bodies may be absent in clinically typical LRRK2 PD and Parkin PD, so Lewy bodies are not required for a PD diagnosis in genetic forms [Schneider2017].

### GBA1
- The multicentre study by Sidransky 2009 covered 16 centres (two in Israel), 5,691 patients with PD (780 Ashkenazi Jews) and 4,898 controls (387 Ashkenazi Jews). In Ashkenazi Jews, L444P or N370S was found in 15% of patients versus 3% of controls. In non-Ashkenazi participants it was 3% versus <1% [Sidransky2009].
- In the same study, full GBA sequencing of 1,883 non-Ashkenazi patients found mutations in 7%, so screening only for the two common mutations missed about half of mutant alleles. The OR for any GBA mutation across centres was 5.43 [Sidransky2009].
- Patients carrying a GBA mutation presented earlier, were more likely to have affected relatives, and were more likely to have atypical clinical features [Sidransky2009].
- Gaucher link: GBA1 encodes the lysosomal enzyme glucocerebrosidase. Two disease-causing variants (biallelic) cause Gaucher disease, while one variant (heterozygous) raises PD risk with reduced penetrance [Sidransky2009; Rossi2025].
- Nomenclature: newer protein numbering adds 39 amino acids. "N409S" is the former N370S, "L483P" the former L444P, "E365K" the former E326K and "T408M" the former T369M [Rossi2025].
- Distribution: across 27,963 GBA1 carriers from 82 countries, N409S was the most frequent pathogenic variant in Jewish and White patients, and L483P in Asian and Hispanic patients. The most common coding risk variants were E365K and T408M, both common among White patients [Rossi2025].
- Variant classes: "mild" variants (e.g. N409S) cause type 1 (non-neuronopathic) Gaucher disease when biallelic, and "severe" variants (e.g. L483P, 84GG) cause neuronopathic Gaucher disease. "Risk" variants (E365K, T408M) do not cause Gaucher disease [Rossi2025].
- Risk by class in Ashkenazi PD (1,000 patients): OR 2.2 for mild and 10.3 for severe GBA mutations. Severe carriers were more than four times as frequent as expected (4.4% versus 0.9%) [GanOr2015].
- Worldwide meta-analysis of 11,453 patients and 14,565 controls: OR for mild variants 2.84-4.94 and for severe variants 9.92-21.29, depending on the model [GanOr2015].
- Earlier onset with GBA1 variants: pooled mean onset was 53.1 ± 11.2 years with severe and 58.1 ± 10.6 with mild GBA mutations [GanOr2015]. In an Israeli Ashkenazi cohort, mean onset was 55.7 (severe), 57.9 (mild) and 60.7 years (no GBA/LRRK2 mutation) [GanOr2008].
- E326K (E365K): a meta-analysis of 15 studies (5,908 patients, 5,605 controls) gave a pooled OR of 1.99 (95% CI 1.57-2.51). Minor-allele frequency was 1.67% in patients and 1.03% in controls [Huang2018].
- T369M (T408M) is classed as a risk variant [Rossi2025], but its association with PD is inconsistent across studies. No pooled OR was checked in this pass, so a specific OR is unverified.
- Disease course (Cilia 2016, 2,764 consecutive patients, 123 GBA carriers): carriers had a higher risk of dementia (HR 3.16) and death (HR 1.85) than non-carriers. Severe-variant carriers had a greater dementia risk than mild-variant carriers but similar mortality [Cilia2016].
- In the same study, imaging of mild-variant carriers overlapped with idiopathic PD, while severe-variant carriers were closer to dementia with Lewy bodies [Cilia2016].
- Penetrance points from familial PD (French, dominant model): (50, 7.6%), (60, 13.7%), (70, 21.4%), (80, 29.7%). At age 70 penetrance did not differ between N370S, L444P and rarer mutations [Anheim2012].
- Penetrance points from unselected PD patients (kin-cohort, Italy): (60, 10%), (70, 16%), (80, 19%) [Balestrino2020].
- Penetrance points from parents of Gaucher patients (obligate carriers, New York): (65, 2.2%), (85, 10.9%) [Rana2013].
- Penetrance points from Ashkenazi Gaucher families (Israel and New York) [Alcalay2014]:
  - Heterozygous parents: (60, 1.5%), (80, 7.7%).
  - Patients with Gaucher disease: (60, 4.7%), (80, 9.1%).
  - Non-carriers: (60, 0.7%), (80, 2.1%).
- Among those who developed PD, mean onset was 54.2 years in Gaucher patients versus 65.2 in heterozygotes [Alcalay2014].
- Controversy: GBA1 penetrance estimates differ about threefold by ascertainment. Gaucher-family cohorts give about 8-11% by 80-85, unselected PD relatives about 19%, and familial PD cohorts about 30% [Anheim2012; Balestrino2020; Rana2013; Alcalay2014].

### SNCA
- In 1997 the first PD gene mutation, SNCA A53T, was identified in a large Italian kindred and three unrelated Greek families with autosomal dominant PD [Polymeropoulos1997].
- A second point mutation, A30P, was reported in 1998 [Kruger1998].
- E46K was found in a Spanish family with autosomal dominant parkinsonism, dementia and visual hallucinations, with widespread cortical and subcortical Lewy bodies [Zarranz2004].
- Gene dose: triplication of the SNCA locus was shown to cause PD in 2003 [Singleton2003]. Duplication causes a phenotype close to idiopathic PD, with later onset, slow progression and no prominent dementia. This suggests that SNCA dose relates to severity [ChartierHarlin2004].
- Multiplication dose-effect points for the animation, from 59 families [Book2018]:
  - Mean onset: 46.9 ± 10.5 years with three gene copies (heterozygous duplication) versus 34.5 ± 7.4 years with four copies (triplication or homozygous duplication).
  - Dementia was noted in 50.9% of probands.
- Common SNCA variation also raises risk: in 2,692 cases and 2,652 controls, genotypes with the 263-bp allele of the REP1 promoter repeat had an OR of 1.43 (95% CI 1.22-1.69). REP1 did not change age at onset [Maraganore2006].

### Recessive early-onset genes and VPS35
- PRKN: in 1998 parkin mutations were identified as the cause of autosomal recessive juvenile parkinsonism (6q25.2-q27; a 465-amino-acid protein with a ubiquitin-like domain and RING finger) [Kitada1998].
- Lücking 2000 [Lucking2000]:
  - 49% (36/73) of early-onset families (onset ≤45 years, unaffected parents) carried parkin mutations.
  - In isolated cases, the share was 77% (10/13) with onset ≤20 years but only 3% (2/64) with onset >30 years.
- In the same study, parkin carriers had a younger mean onset (32 ± 11 versus 42 ± 11 years). They were more often symmetric, had dystonia at onset and hyperreflexia, responded well to levodopa, and developed levodopa-induced dyskinesias [Lucking2000].
- MDSGene review of more than 1,100 patients with biallelic PRKN, PINK1 or DJ1 variants: median onset was about 30 years, PD was clinically typical with an excellent treatment response, dystonia and dyskinesia were relatively common, and cognitive decline was relatively uncommon [Kasten2018].
- Of 20 parkin autopsies reviewed (17 biallelic), Lewy bodies can be absent in clinical Parkin PD [Schneider2017].
- PINK1 (PARK6): in 2004 homozygous mutations in the kinase domain of the mitochondrial kinase PINK1 were found in three consanguineous families. This was the first direct genetic link between mitochondria and PD [Valente2004].
- DJ-1 (PARK7): in 2003 DJ-1 mutations were linked to autosomal recessive early-onset parkinsonism. Loss of DJ-1, a protein implicated in the oxidative-stress response, leads to neurodegeneration [Bonifati2003].
- VPS35 D620N (dominant): exome sequencing of a Swiss kindred with tremor-predominant, levodopa-responsive PD (mean onset 50.6 ± 7.3 years) found VPS35 c.1858G>A p.Asp620Asn. The variant was found in three more families and one sporadic case and was absent from 3,309 controls [VilarinoGuell2011].
- The same D620N variant was found independently in an Austrian family with 16 affected members (mean onset 53 years), with high but incomplete penetrance. VPS35 is part of the retromer complex [Zimprich2011VPS35].
- Rarity: in the Israeli ROPAD cohort of 2,699 patients there were 19 PRKN, 4 PINK1, and one each of PARK7, SNCA and VPS35 positive results [Anis2025]. In PD GENEration, PINK1, PARK7 and VPS35 together accounted for 0.2% of participants [Cook2024].

### Israel
- Israeli ROPAD study (Anis et al., Parkinsonism Relat Disord 2025): 2,699 patients from all participating Israeli movement-disorder clinics. Mean onset was 59.2 ± 10.6 years, 63.1% were male and 26.6% had a family history. Patients were tested for 8 PD genes plus 37 genes with overlapping phenotypes [Anis2025].
- Results: 512 patients (19.0%) had a positive PD-relevant genetic test [Anis2025]:
  - LRRK2 pathogenic variants only: 187 (6.9%).
  - GBA1 risk variants only: 283 (10.5%).
  - Both genes: 15 (0.6%).
  - PRKN, PINK1, PARK7, SNCA or VPS35: 26 (1.0%).
  - One patient had dual GBA1 + SNCA findings.
- Most prevalent variants: LRRK2 p.Gly2019Ser in 191 patients (7.1%) and GBA1 p.Asn409Ser in 173 (6.4%). Excluding patients with only LRRK2 and/or GBA1 variants, the yield was 1.2% (27/2,214) [Anis2025].
- The Israeli yield (19.0%) is higher than the ROPAD international yield (14.8%) [Anis2025; Westenberger2024].
- Gene-level versus variant-level figures, part 1 (derived here from the abstract counts):
  - Gene-level "LRRK2-only 6.9%" excludes the 15 dual LRRK2+GBA1 carriers. Variant-level "G2019S 7.1%" counts every G2019S carrier, including dual carriers.
  - Total LRRK2 carriers = 187 + 15 = 202 (7.5%). G2019S accounts for 191 of them, and the remaining ~11 carry other LRRK2 pathogenic variants.
  - Total GBA1 carriers = 283 + 15 + 1 = 299 (11.1%). N409S accounts for 173 of them (6.4% of all patients), and ~126 carry other GBA1 variants (risk, severe or other).
  - Sources: [Anis2025]; the arithmetic is mine, and the full-text breakdown was not seen.
- Gene-level versus variant-level figures, part 2: older Israeli shares are higher because they counted Ashkenazi patients only. G2019S was found in 14.8% of Ashkenazi versus 2.7% of non-Ashkenazi Jewish PD patients [OrrUrtreger2007]. The Israeli ROPAD cohort includes all Israeli patients, so its population-level shares (7.1% G2019S, 6.4% N409S) are diluted. Its ancestry breakdown was not available in the abstract (unverified).
- Ashkenazi LRRK2 shares (Tel Aviv): G2019S in 26% of familial and 10.6% of apparently sporadic Ashkenazi cases. Carrier frequency was 2.4% in Ashkenazi and 0.4% in non-Ashkenazi controls. Women and patients with a family history were over-represented among carriers [OrrUrtreger2007].
- Ashkenazi GBA shares (Tel Aviv, 420 patients, 8 founder mutations): GBA carrier frequency was 17.9% in PD versus 4.2% in elderly and 6.35% in young controls. Severe mutations made up 29% of patient carriers versus 7% of young-control carriers. Severe and mild mutations raised PD risk 13.6-fold and 2.2-fold [GanOr2008].
- More than one-third of this Ashkenazi PD population carried a GBA or LRRK2 mutation [GanOr2008].

### Genetic testing and counselling
- No final MDS consensus recommendation on clinical genetic testing in PD was found in this pass (the MDS Task Force page returned 404). An MDS-published 2023 review of gaps and controversies says testing is increasingly common, practices vary widely, and guidelines are needed. Its open questions are:
  - whether testing is appropriate without medical actionability;
  - whether testing should vary by ethnicity;
  - long-term outcomes of consumer and research testing in presymptomatic people;
  - resources and cost [Pal2023].
- Why testing has not changed standard care: "there are currently no proven gene-targeted therapies, but clinical trials are underway" [Pal2023]. Physicians have hesitated to offer testing because of the "perceived low clinical utility" of PD results, but gene-specific trials and genotype-phenotype data are starting to shift the discussion [Cook2021].
- Seven genes have been formally adjudicated as causal contributors to PD: LRRK2, GBA1, PRKN, SNCA, PINK1, PARK7 and VPS35. People with PD are often unaware of their genetic status, which hampers enrolment in variant-targeted trials [Cook2024].
- In PD GENEration all participants received results in a genetic-counselling session with a neurologist or certified genetic counsellor. Participants with biallelic GBA1 variants were referred to Gaucher centres [Cook2024].
- Counting conventions matter: PD GENEration reports GBA1 "reportable" variants in 7.7% of participants but "disease-relevant" GBA1 variants (including T408M) in 10.1% (838/8,301). The commonest GBA1 variants were E365K/E326K (3.7%), N409S/N370S (1.6%) and L483P/L444P (0.8%). LRRK2 G2019S was found in 177 participants (2.1%), two of them homozygous [Cook2024].
- The ROPAD authors argue that because about 15% of patients carry potentially actionable variants, genetic testing should be broadened in the gene-targeted trial era [Westenberger2024]. A pooled analysis concludes that onset-age (≤50) and family-history criteria miss 68-77% of carriers and supports broader, data-driven testing [Balck2025].
- Early cautions remain relevant to counselling. The LRRK2 consortium said its knowledge "should be applied with caution in the diagnosis and counselling of patients" [Healy2008]. Israeli investigators said the value of screening asymptomatic people was questionable until penetrance is better defined and preventive treatments exist [OrrUrtreger2007].
- Summary for the guide's inheritance animation: the published risk to a carrier varies by gene, ancestry and how families were recruited [Healy2008; Marder2015; Lee2017LRRK2; Anheim2012; Alcalay2014; Lucking2000]:
  - G2019S is far more common in Ashkenazi Jewish and North African Arab/Berber patients.
  - PRKN accounts for most isolated cases with onset at age 20 or younger.
  - LRRK2 penetrance is incomplete, about 25-80% by age 79-80.
  - GBA1 penetrance is low to moderate, about 8-30% by age 80.
- Gene-targeted trials (brief; covered by another pass): ROPAD frames LRRK2 and GBA1 as the main targets of trials now recruiting gene carriers [Westenberger2024; Cook2024]. A clinical benefit for any gene-targeted therapy had not been shown as of the 2023 MDS review [Pal2023].
