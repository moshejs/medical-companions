# I. Research toward a cure
Scope: this section reports research routes that aim to slow, stop or reverse Parkinson's disease, or to replace lost dopamine neurons, as of 2026-10-20. Each route lists the mechanism step it targets, its key trials (NCT ID, phase, n, primary endpoint result) and a suggested evidence label: Established, Early evidence or Hypothesis, with the reasoning. None of these approaches is an approved disease-modifying treatment. They are reported neutrally as research, not as options for readers. Trial statuses come from the ClinicalTrials.gov v2 API, queried 2026-10-20 [CTgov2026I]. Journal data were checked against PubMed E-utilities (abstracts read) and Crossref. Gaps: (1) no "2025 update" of the McFarthing pipeline review exists in PubMed or Crossref. The latest reviews are the 2024 annual update and a 2015-2024 ten-year analysis (Buff 2026), so the key McFarthing2025 is not used. (2) Full results of LUMA (BIIB122) and ACTIVATE (BIA 28-6156) are not yet in peer-reviewed papers. Their figures come from a NeurologyLive report of an MDS 2026 late-breaking abstract and from a Michael J. Fox Foundation news item, and are marked as such. (3) The Roche, Biogen, AC Immune and Bial press pages, and the PMDA review PDF, partly blocked automated access. Only text actually retrieved is used. (4) PASADENA's MDS-UPDRS Part III numbers come from the results posted on ClinicalTrials.gov, because the NEJM full text could not be opened. (5) No NCT ID could be found for the Path to Prevention (P2P) platform trial. (6) The Kyoto iPSC trial is registered in Japan (jRCT2090220384), not on ClinicalTrials.gov.

## Facts
### Evidence-label key used in this section
- Labels used here: "Established" = shown in at least one adequately powered, randomised, controlled trial (positive or negative). "Early evidence" = some randomised or controlled signal, or consistent open-label and biomarker data, that has not been confirmed in a phase 3. "Hypothesis" = mechanistic, preclinical or observational rationale, or only safety and target-engagement data in humans. Under this scheme, no route has Established disease-modifying benefit as of 2026-10-20. The 2015-2024 pipeline review concludes that "there are still no treatments to modify the course of the disease" [Buff2026].

### 1. Anti-alpha-synuclein immunotherapy (target: extracellular aggregated alpha-synuclein and its cell-to-cell spread)
- PASADENA (prasinezumab, NCT03100149, phase 2): 316 people with early PD were randomised 1:1:1 to placebo (n=105), 1500 mg (n=105) or 4500 mg (n=106) IV every 4 weeks for 52 weeks [Pagano2022].
- PASADENA primary endpoint (change in MDS-UPDRS Parts I+II+III at week 52) was not met. Mean change was 9.4 with placebo, 7.4 with 1500 mg (difference -2.0, 80% CI -4.2 to 0.2, P=0.24) and 8.8 with 4500 mg (difference -0.6, P=0.72). Dopamine transporter SPECT did not differ [Pagano2022].
- PASADENA safety: serious adverse events occurred in 6.7% (1500 mg) and 7.5% (4500 mg) of participants. Infusion reactions occurred in 19.0% and 34.0% [Pagano2022].
- PASADENA secondary motor signal (results posted on ClinicalTrials.gov): MDS-UPDRS Part III worsened by 5.57 points with placebo versus 3.69 with 1500 mg (difference -1.88, 80% CI -3.49 to -0.27, p=0.135) and 4.55 with 4500 mg (difference -1.02, p=0.42) over 52 weeks [CTgov2026I].
- A post hoc analysis of PASADENA (4 prespecified and 6 exploratory subgroups) found a larger Part III effect in faster-progressing subgroups, such as "diffuse malignant" phenotype or MAO-B inhibitor use. The authors state that this needs confirmation in new randomised trials [Pagano2024].
- PASADENA open-label extension, compared with an external PPMI comparator (n=303): Part III OFF progression was 51% slower in the delayed-start group (n=94) and 65% slower in the early-start group (n=177). This exploratory analysis was not randomised [Pagano2024OLE].
- PADOVA (prasinezumab 1500 mg IV every 4 weeks, NCT04777331, phase 2b): 586 people with early PD on stable symptomatic therapy were randomised 1:1 (293 per group) across 110 centres. Treatment lasted at least 76 weeks [Nikolcheva2026].
- PADOVA primary endpoint (time to confirmed motor progression, defined as an increase of 5 or more points in MDS-UPDRS Part III OFF) was not met. HR was 0.84 (95% CI 0.69-1.01), p=0.066. Median time to progression was 61.1 weeks with prasinezumab versus 49.7 weeks with placebo [Nikolcheva2026].
- PADOVA safety: serious adverse events occurred in 12% of each arm (34/292 vs 34/290). Three deaths were judged unrelated to the drug [Nikolcheva2026].
- Roche (16 June 2025) reported a prespecified levodopa-treated subgroup, about 75% of PADOVA participants, with HR 0.79 (0.63-0.99), nominal p=0.0431. On that basis and on open-label extension data, Roche decided to start phase 3 [Roche2025].
- PARAISO (NCT07174310, phase 3, Roche) is recruiting. Estimated n=900. Start date 2025-11-24, primary completion 2029-06-30. Primary endpoint: time to confirmed motor progression on MDS-UPDRS Part III [CTgov2026I].
- Controversy: PADOVA's own authors describe the levodopa-subgroup and other positive findings as "prespecified exploratory" evidence. The primary analysis was statistically negative. The sponsor and the trial publication both frame phase 3 as the confirmatory test [Nikolcheva2026; Roche2025].
- SPARK (cinpanemab/BIIB054, NCT03318523, phase 2): 357 participants were randomised 2:1:2:2 to placebo, 250 mg, 1250 mg or 3500 mg IV every 4 weeks. The trial was stopped after the week-72 interim analysis for lack of efficacy [Lang2022].
- SPARK primary endpoint: change in MDS-UPDRS total at 52 weeks was 10.8 with placebo versus 10.5 to 11.3 with cinpanemab (differences -0.3 to 0.5 points, all P≥0.80). DaT-SPECT showed no differences. Development was discontinued (ClinicalTrials.gov status: Terminated) [Lang2022; CTgov2026I].
- UB-312 (active vaccine, NCT04075318, phase 1, total n=70 including healthy volunteers): in the PD part, 20 patients were randomised 7:3. Serum anti-alpha-synuclein antibodies developed in 12 of 13 and CSF antibodies in 5 of 13 three-dose recipients. Clinical scales did not differ. CSF alpha-synuclein seeds fell in a subset (exploratory) [Eijsvogel2024].
- ACI-7104.056 (active vaccine, VacSYn, NCT06015841, phase 2): Part 1 randomised more than 30 patients 3:1. Interim data (April 2025) showed antibodies more than 20-fold above placebo after 4 immunisations and no reported drug-related serious adverse events. The company press release did not report clinical efficacy data [ACImmune2025]. The trial is Active, not recruiting (estimated n=150, primary completion 2028-06) [CTgov2026I].
- Evidence label: Early evidence for antibodies (two phase 2 trials missed their primary endpoints but share a consistent exploratory Part III signal, and a phase 3 is ongoing). Hypothesis for active vaccines (only immunogenicity and target engagement so far). Cinpanemab specifically: Established negative in phase 2 [Pagano2022; Lang2022; Nikolcheva2026; Eijsvogel2024].

### 2. GLP-1 receptor agonists (target: insulin-signalling, neuroinflammation and mitochondrial pathways; proposed neurotrophic action)
- Exenatide phase 2 (NCT01971242): 62 people with moderate PD were randomised to exenatide 2 mg weekly (n=32) or placebo (n=30) for 48 weeks, followed by a 12-week washout [Athauda2017].
- At 60 weeks, off-medication MDS-UPDRS Part III improved by 1.0 point with exenatide and worsened by 2.1 points with placebo. The adjusted difference was -3.5 points (95% CI -6.7 to -0.3), p=0.0318. The authors could not tell disease modification apart from a long-lasting symptomatic effect [Athauda2017].
- Exenatide-PD3 (NCT04232969, phase 3, six UK sites): 194 participants were randomised 1:1 to exenatide 2 mg weekly or placebo for 96 weeks [Vijiaratnam2025].
- Exenatide-PD3 primary endpoint was negative. MDS-UPDRS III OFF worsened by 5.7 points (SD 11.2) with exenatide versus 4.5 (SD 11.4) with placebo (adjusted coefficient 0.92, 95% CI -1.56 to 3.39, p=0.47). Serious adverse events occurred in 9% versus 11% [Vijiaratnam2025].
- In June 2026 The Lancet placed an Expression of Concern on the Exenatide-PD3 paper. The notice followed a regulatory inspection at one study site (King's College Hospital) that raised trial-conduct concerns. Investigations were pending at the time of the notice. Details of the site and inspection come from news coverage (unverified against the notice text) [Vijiaratnam2025].
- LIXIPARK (lixisenatide daily, NCT03439943, phase 2): 156 people with PD diagnosed less than 3 years earlier were randomised (78 per arm) for 12 months, followed by a 2-month washout [Meissner2024].
- LIXIPARK result: at 12 months, MDS-UPDRS III (ON) changed by -0.04 with lixisenatide versus +3.04 with placebo (difference 3.08, 95% CI 0.86 to 5.30, P=0.007). OFF scores after the 14-month washout were 17.7 versus 20.6. Other secondary endpoints did not differ [Meissner2024].
- LIXIPARK adverse events: nausea in 46% and vomiting in 13% of lixisenatide recipients [Meissner2024].
- NLY01 (pegylated exenatide, NCT04154072, phase 2, 58 US sites): 255 people with early untreated PD were randomised to 2.5 mg, 5.0 mg or placebo for 36 weeks. MDS-UPDRS II+III change did not differ (difference -0.39 and +0.36 points, p=0.77 and 0.79) [McGarry2024].
- NLY01 gastrointestinal adverse events: 61% (2.5 mg) and 75% (5.0 mg) versus 36% with placebo. Nausea: 39% and 58% versus 19% [McGarry2024].
- Semaglutide: the only registered PD trial found (GIPD, NCT03659682, Oslo; semaglutide 1.0 mg weekly; estimated n=120) has status "Unknown". Its record was last updated in 2018, and no results are posted [CTgov2026I].
- Observational data: in a Scandinavian registry cohort (158,961 new GLP-1RA users vs 188,065 sulfonylurea users), PD incidence was 5.2 versus 8.0 per 10,000 person-years (adjusted HR 0.81, 95% CI 0.68-0.96). The authors call for replication. As an observational study, it cannot exclude confounding [Engstrom2026].
- Evidence label: Early evidence. Two small phase 2 trials (exenatide and lixisenatide) were positive on motor scores. A phase 3 (exenatide, now under an Expression of Concern) and a phase 2 (NLY01) were negative. It is unresolved whether the positive effects were symptomatic or disease-modifying [Athauda2017; Meissner2024; Vijiaratnam2025; McGarry2024].

### 3. LRRK2 kinase inhibition and LRRK2 lowering (target: overactive LRRK2 kinase, which affects lysosomal function)
- LUMA (BIIB122/DNL151 225 mg daily, NCT05348785, phase 2b): 648 people with early PD were randomised across 206 sites, mostly treatment-naive (about 80%), with 48 to 144 weeks of treatment. ClinicalTrials.gov status: Completed (n=650), primary completion 2026-03-09, no posted results [NeurologyLive2026; CTgov2026I].
- LUMA primary endpoint (time to confirmed worsening of 6 or more points on modified MDS-UPDRS II+III) was negative. HR was 1.04 (95% CI 0.85-1.28), P=.4538, despite more than 90% reduction of whole-blood pS935 LRRK2. Topline results were announced 21 May 2026 and full data presented as an MDS 2026 late-breaking abstract (news report, not yet peer reviewed) [NeurologyLive2026].
- LUMA safety (as reported): serious adverse events 11.5% versus 11.9%; headache 17.1% versus 9.2%; withdrawal for adverse events 11.2% versus 4.6%. Biogen and Denali discontinued BIIB122 in idiopathic PD [NeurologyLive2026].
- LIGHTHOUSE (BIIB122 in LRRK2-variant carriers, NCT05418673, phase 3) was terminated after enrolling 7 participants. The reason given was "Sponsor Decision". A separate phase 2 in LRRK2-associated PD (NCT06602193, estimated n=50, primary completion 2027-03) is active, not recruiting [CTgov2026I].
- BIIB094/ION859 (intrathecal antisense oligonucleotide, REASON, NCT03976349, phase 1): 82 participants took part (single doses in part A, n=40; four monthly doses in part B, n=42). No drug-related serious adverse events occurred. CSF LRRK2 fell by up to 59% and pRab10 by up to 50%. Clinical efficacy was not assessed [Mabrouk2026].
- Evidence label: Hypothesis (for idiopathic PD, the hypothesis has weakened). The largest kinase-inhibitor trial was negative despite target engagement. Results in LRRK2-variant carriers and for antisense lowering are still pending [NeurologyLive2026; Mabrouk2026].

### 4. GBA1 / glucocerebrosidase (GCase) pathway (target: lysosomal GCase enzyme and its glycolipid substrates)
- Ambroxol (AiM-PD, NCT02941822, open-label, no control group): 23 patients entered and 17 were analysed, at doses escalated to 1.26 g/day for 186 days. CSF ambroxol rose by 156 ng/mL, CSF GCase protein rose by 35% but GCase activity fell by 19%, and CSF alpha-synuclein rose by 13%. MDS-UPDRS III improved by 6.8 points, but without a placebo arm [Mullin2020].
- ASPro-PD (ambroxol vs placebo, NCT05778617, phase 3, UK) is recruiting. Estimated n=330. Start date 2025-02-25, primary completion 2029-02. Primary endpoint: change in MDS-UPDRS I-III at week 104 [CTgov2026I].
- MOVES-PD part 2 (venglustat 15 mg/day, a glucosylceramide synthase inhibitor, NCT02906020, phase 2): 221 GBA1-variant carriers were randomised for 52 weeks. MDS-UPDRS II+III worsened by 7.29 points with venglustat versus 4.71 with placebo (difference 2.58, 95% CI -1.10 to 6.27, p=0.17). The long-term follow-up was halted [Giladi2023; CTgov2026I].
- ACTIVATE (BIA 28-6156, a GCase activator, 10 or 60 mg daily vs placebo, NCT05819359, phase 2): 273 GBA1-PD participants at 85 sites were treated for 18 months. Progression was similar to placebo with no safety concerns, and Bial will not continue the drug for GBA1-PD (MJFF news, 10 June 2026; no peer-reviewed data yet). ClinicalTrials.gov still lists n=237 (estimated), status Active, not recruiting [MJFF2026; CTgov2026I].
- LY3884961/PR001 (AAV9 GBA1 gene therapy, NCT04127578, phase 1/2a): status Active, not recruiting, estimated n=32, primary completion 2031-08. Primary endpoints are safety only, and no efficacy results were found [CTgov2026I].
- Evidence label: Hypothesis. Two mechanistically different randomised trials (venglustat and BIA 28-6156) were negative. Ambroxol rests on open-label target-engagement data, with a phase 3 under way [Giladi2023; MJFF2026; Mullin2020].

### 5. Cell replacement (target: replacing lost nigrostriatal dopamine neurons; not expected to stop the disease process)
- Freed 2001 (sham-surgery controlled): 40 patients with severe PD (mean duration 14 years) were randomised. At 1 year the global rating was 0.0 with transplant versus -0.4 with sham, which was not significantly different. Patients aged 60 or younger improved on UPDRS (P=0.01). Dystonia and dyskinesias recurred in 15% of transplant recipients [Freed2001].
- Olanow 2003 (double-blind, placebo procedure): 34 patients were randomised to 1 donor per side, 4 donors per side or placebo for 24 months. There was no overall effect on off-state UPDRS motor score (p=0.244). In 56% of transplanted patients, dyskinesia persisted in the "off" state [Olanow2003].
- TRANSEURO (fetal ventral mesencephalic tissue, NCT01898390, open-label): because of tissue shortage, only 11 people were grafted. There was no overall clinical effect on the primary endpoint at 3 years. 18F-DOPA uptake improved in 7 people but reached near-normal in only 1. No major graft-induced dyskinesias occurred. The authors conclude that a stem-cell source is needed [Barker2025].
- Bemdaneprocel (hESC-derived dopaminergic progenitors, NCT04802733, phase 1, open-label): 12 patients received 0.9 million (n=5) or 2.7 million (n=7) cells bilaterally into the putamen, with 1 year of immunosuppression. There were no product-related adverse events and no graft-induced dyskinesias [Tabar2025].
- In bemdaneprocel phase 1, 18F-DOPA uptake increased at 18 months. MDS-UPDRS III OFF improved by an average of 23 points in the high-dose cohort, an open-label secondary or exploratory outcome [Tabar2025].
- exPDite-2 (bemdaneprocel, NCT06944522, phase 3) is recruiting. Estimated n=102, randomised 2:1 to cells or sham surgery, with triple masking. Primary endpoint: change in diary ON-time without troublesome dyskinesia. Primary completion 2027-03 [CTgov2026I].
- Kyoto trial (allogeneic iPSC-derived dopaminergic progenitors, jRCT2090220384, phase 1/2): 7 patients aged 50-69 had no serious adverse events and 73 mild to moderate events. MDS-UPDRS III OFF improved in 4 of 6 evaluable patients, by a mean of 9.5 points (20.4%). Putaminal 18F-DOPA Ki rose by 44.7%. There was no graft overgrowth on MRI [Sawamoto2025].
- Japan granted conditional, time-limited approval to the Kyoto/Sumitomo product (raguneprocel, brand AMCHEPRY) in early March 2026, on the basis of the 7-patient trial. The sponsor has 7 years to collect confirmatory data. The approval applies only within Japan [ParkinsonCanada2026].
- Controversy: conditional approval on 7 uncontrolled patients is not the same as Established efficacy. Patient organisations note open questions on long-term safety, immune rejection and graft durability [ParkinsonCanada2026; Sawamoto2025].
- Autologous iPSC case: one patient received patient-derived dopamine progenitors (two hemispheres, 6 months apart) without immunosuppression. PET suggested graft survival, and symptoms were stable or improved at 18-24 months (single case, uncontrolled) [Schweitzer2020].
- Several other pluripotent-cell trials are registered, for example an autologous iPSC trial (NCT06422208, n=12 estimated) and ASPIRO (autologous ANPD001, NCT06344026, n=9 estimated). Both are enrolling by invitation [CTgov2026I].
- Warning about unproven clinics: the ISSCR guidelines state that stem-cell-based interventions must be "proven safe and effective" before they are marketed to patients. They also caution that registration on a trials website does not mean a study has been vetted (Recommendations 3.1.1 and 3.4.3.1) [ISSCR2021].
- Evidence label: Early evidence. Grafted cells survive and make dopamine (PET), and open-label motor signals exist. However, the two older sham-controlled fetal trials missed their primary endpoints, no randomised stem-cell trial has reported, and Japan's approval is conditional [Freed2001; Olanow2003; Tabar2025; Sawamoto2025].

### 6. Gene therapy (targets: trophic support with GDNF or neurturin; local dopamine synthesis with AADC or ProSavin; modulation of circuits through the subthalamic nucleus with GAD)
- AB-1005 (AAV2-GDNF, NCT04167540, phase 1b, open-label): 11 participants (6 mild, 5 moderate PD) received bilateral putaminal infusion, with mean putaminal coverage of 63%. Six serious adverse events in 3 participants were unrelated to the vector [VanLaar2025].
- AB-1005 at 18 months: the mild cohort was numerically stable. The moderate cohort showed MDS-UPDRS III OFF -20.4 points, OFF time -1.7 h/day and levodopa-equivalent dose -257.6 mg. None of these changes had a control arm [VanLaar2025].
- REGENERATE-PD (AB-1005, NCT06285643, phase 2, randomised) is Active, not recruiting. Estimated n=127. Primary endpoint: change in PD motor diary at month 18. Primary completion 2028-08 [CTgov2026I].
- AAV2-neurturin (CERE-120, NCT00400634, phase 2, sham-controlled): 58 patients took part. The 12-month UPDRS III OFF difference was -0.31 (95% CI -5.58 to 4.97), p=0.91, which was negative. Serious adverse events occurred in 13 of 38 treated versus 4 of 20 controls [Marks2010].
- AAV2-GAD (subthalamic, NCT00643890, phase 2, sham-controlled): of 45 randomised patients, 37 were analysed. At 6 months, UPDRS III OFF improved by 8.1 points (23.1%) with GAD versus 4.7 points (12.7%) with sham (between-group p=0.04). The trial was later listed as terminated for financial reasons [LeWitt2011; CTgov2026I].
- VY-AADC01 (AADC enzyme, PD-1101, open-label, n=15): there were no vector-related serious adverse events over 3 years. PD medication needs fell by 21-30% in the two higher-dose cohorts [Christine2022]. The randomised follow-on RESTORE-1 (NCT03562494) is listed as Completed with only 14 enrolled [CTgov2026I].
- ProSavin (lentiviral delivery of three dopamine-synthesis genes, open-label, n=15): UPDRS III OFF improved from 38 to 26 at 6 months. The most common drug-related events were on-medication dyskinesias (20 events in 11 patients) [Palfi2014]. Its successor, AXO-Lenti-PD (SUNRISE-PD, NCT03720418), was terminated after 6 participants when development stopped [CTgov2026I].
- Evidence label: Early evidence for symptomatic gene therapy (one small positive sham-controlled trial, AAV2-GAD). Hypothesis for GDNF-type disease modification (open-label only; the sham-controlled neurturin trial was negative) [LeWitt2011; Marks2010; VanLaar2025].

### 7. Other disease-modification attempts (repurposed drugs, other kinase targets, exercise)
- FAIRPARK-II (deferiprone, an iron chelator, NCT02655315, phase 2): 372 people with newly diagnosed, levodopa-naive PD were randomised for 36 weeks. MDS-UPDRS total worsened by 15.6 with deferiprone versus 6.3 with placebo (difference 9.3, 95% CI 6.3 to 12.2, P<0.001), meaning deferiprone did worse than placebo [Devos2022].
- FAIRPARK-II details: dopaminergic therapy was needed by 22.0% on deferiprone versus 2.7% on placebo. Agranulocytosis occurred in 2 and neutropenia in 3 deferiprone participants, although nigrostriatal iron did fall [Devos2022].
- STEADY-PD III (isradipine 5 mg twice daily, a calcium-channel blocker, NCT02168842, phase 3): 336 people with early untreated PD were treated for 36 months. Treatment effect on UPDRS I-III was -0.27 points (95% CI -3.02 to 2.48), P=0.85. Authors note the dose may not have engaged the target [PSG2020STEADY].
- SURE-PD3 (inosine to raise urate, NCT02642393, phase 3): 298 participants were randomised. The trial stopped early for futility. Progression was 11.1 versus 9.9 MDS-UPDRS points/year (difference 1.26, P=.18), even though urate rose 44%. Kidney stones occurred at 7.0 versus 1.4 per 100 patient-years [PSG2021SURE].
- NILO-PD (nilotinib, a c-Abl inhibitor, NCT03205488, phase 2): 76 patients with moderately advanced PD were treated for 6 months. The drug was tolerable, but its CSF/serum ratio was only 0.2-0.3%. Part III ON scores were worse at 300 mg at month 1, and the authors concluded it "should not be further tested in PD" [Simuni2021].
- Risvodetinib (c-Abl inhibitor, 201 Trial, NCT05424276, phase 2a): 137 people with untreated PD received 50, 100 or 200 mg or placebo for 12 weeks. It met its safety and tolerability primary endpoints. The lead secondary endpoint, MDS-UPDRS II+III, was not significant [Werner2026].
- UDCA (UP study, NCT03840005, phase 2): 30 participants received 30 mg/kg/day versus placebo (2:1) for 48 weeks. It was safe, and 31P-MRS suggested midbrain target engagement. MDS-UPDRS III did not differ [Payne2023].
- Terazosin (target: PGK1 activation to enhance glycolysis and ATP): the rationale comes from animal models and two clinical databases showing slower progression and fewer PD diagnoses in users [Cai2019]. A pilot (TZ-PD, NCT03905811, n=13) has completed. The multi-arm EJS ACT-PD platform (NCT07207057, phase 3, n=1200 estimated) is testing terazosin 5 mg and telmisartan 40 mg against placebo for 36 months, with MDS-UPDRS I+II as the primary endpoint [CTgov2026I].
- SPARX3 (high-intensity vs moderate-intensity treadmill exercise, NCT04284436): 370 people with untreated PD (diagnosed within 3 years) have been enrolled. Status: Active, not recruiting, primary completion 2027-07. The primary outcome is change in MDS-UPDRS III at 12 months [CTgov2026I].
- Evidence label: Established negative for isradipine and inosine (phase 3) and for deferiprone (large phase 2 showing worsening). Hypothesis for terazosin (observational and preclinical data; phase 3 ongoing), UDCA and c-Abl inhibition. Exercise as disease modification: Hypothesis, awaiting SPARX3; this is separate from exercise's symptomatic benefits, which other sections cover [Devos2022; PSG2020STEADY; PSG2021SURE; Simuni2021; Werner2026].

### Trial-design issues
- Delayed-start design: in ADAGIO (1176 participants), early-start rasagiline 1 mg met all three hierarchical end points, but 2 mg did not (P=0.60 for the week-72 comparison). The authors therefore advised caution, which shows that delayed-start results can be hard to interpret [Olanow2009].
- Symptomatic confounding: PD drugs used during a trial can mask progression. Exenatide (12-week) and lixisenatide (2-month) trials added washout periods. PADOVA used a time-to-event endpoint on OFF-medication scores [Athauda2017; Meissner2024; Nikolcheva2026].
- External-comparator analyses (PASADENA open-label extension vs PPMI) are exploratory and not randomised [Pagano2024OLE].
- Biomarker-defined enrolment: the US FDA issued a Letter of Support for CSF alpha-synuclein seed amplification assay (SAA) as a susceptibility/risk biomarker. This allows trials to enrich enrolment with people who have underlying synuclein pathology [Stephenson2026].
- Path to Prevention (P2P): a phase 2A, multi-regimen platform trial nested within PPMI. It tests investigational products in early-stage neuronal alpha-synuclein disease (NSD), recruiting from the PPMI cohort and using PPMI infrastructure [Simuni2026]. No NCT ID or named therapies were found in public sources.
- Platform designs are spreading: EJS ACT-PD is a multi-arm, multi-stage phase 3 platform (UCL) [CTgov2026I].

### 8. Pipeline overview
- 2023 snapshot: 139 active trials as of 31 January 2023, of which 76 (55%) tested symptomatic and 63 (45%) disease-modifying therapies. By phase: 34% phase 1, 52% phase 2, 14% phase 3. 42% of trials tested novel agents and 35% repurposed drugs [McFarthing2023].
- 2024 snapshot: 136 active phase 1-3 PD drug trials on ClinicalTrials.gov as of 31 January 2024. Of these, 76 (56%) were symptomatic and 60 (44%) disease-modifying. By phase: 30% phase 1, 58% phase 2, 12% phase 3 [McFarthing2024].
- Of the 2024 trials, only three phase 3 studies were testing treatments to slow progression [McFarthing2024].
- Ten-year view (2015-2024): 444 trials tested 281 drug interventions. 63% were symptomatic and 37% disease-modifying. By phase: 42% phase 1, 46% phase 2, 12% phase 3. 52% of trials tested novel drugs and about a third repurposed drugs. 133 trials were still ongoing at the end of 2024 [Buff2026].
- No McFarthing "2025 update" was found in PubMed or Crossref (searched 2026-10-20). The ten-year analysis (Buff 2026, with McFarthing as senior author) is the most recent review in the series [Buff2026].
