# F. Device-aided and surgical therapy: DBS, focused ultrasound and infusions
This pass covers deep-brain stimulation (DBS) of the subthalamic nucleus (STN), internal globus pallidus (GPi) and ventral intermediate thalamus (Vim): targets, lead geometry, stimulation parameters, proposed mechanisms (beta suppression), side effects of current spread, the US regulatory history (from FDA PMA database records), adaptive DBS (ADAPT-PD), the main randomised trials and long-term cohorts, selection criteria, MR-guided focused ultrasound lesioning, and continuous infusion therapies (levodopa-carbidopa intestinal gel, foslevodopa-foscarbidopa, ND0612, apomorphine infusion, levodopa-entacapone-carbidopa gel). It feeds the "Deep-brain stimulation" module and the infusion part of "Levodopa over time". Every PMID/DOI was checked against PubMed (esummary) or Europe PMC (PubMed-sourced records). Trial registrations were checked with the ClinicalTrials.gov v2 API, device approvals in the openFDA device PMA database, and drug approvals and labels in openFDA (Drugs@FDA and drug labels). What I could not access: the full texts of the NEJM papers (EARLYSTIM, Deuschl, Krack, Follett, Krishna, Martinez-Fernandez), Bove 2021, CAPSIT-PD (Defer 1999) and Lang 2006 (paywalled). For these, numbers come from the abstracts only, except that the EARLYSTIM levodopa-dose and suicide figures come from a secondary news report (marked). The often-quoted CAPSIT-PD levodopa-test threshold of 33% and a "60-90 µs" pulse-width range could not be read in a primary source; both are marked unverified. Ongoing regulatory events, such as ND0612 in the US, are reported only as far as public records found on 2026-10-20.

## Facts
### DBS targets and hardware (for the lead/field animation)
- The first US approval (FDA PMA P960009, 31 July 1997) was for the Medtronic Activa Tremor Control System. It covered unilateral thalamic stimulation to suppress upper-extremity tremor in essential tremor or parkinsonian tremor not adequately controlled by medication. The system had a Model 3387 lead and a Model 7424 implantable pulse generator (IPG). [FDAPMA2026]
- On 14 January 2002 the FDA approved bilateral stimulation of the GPi or STN (Activa Parkinson's Control Therapy; Model 3387/3389 leads, Soletra neurostimulator). The indication was adjunctive therapy for some symptoms of advanced, levodopa-responsive PD not adequately controlled with medication. [FDAPMA2026]
- Classic Medtronic leads (3387, 3389) are quadripolar (4 ring contacts) and 1.27 mm in diameter, with contacts 1.5 mm long. Contact spacing is 1.5 mm on the 3387 and 0.5 mm on the 3389. [Paff2020]
- A ring contact in monopolar mode produces a roughly spherical field spreading radially from the active contact. Directional (segmented) leads spread several contacts around the shaft so current can be steered. Modelling suggests steering can shift the centre of the activated volume by about 1.0-1.3 mm. [Paff2020]
- The Boston Scientific Vercise Cartesia directional lead has 8 contacts in a 1-3-3-1 pattern: a distal tip, two levels of 3 radially arranged segments, and a proximal ring. Contacts are 1.5 mm long with 0.5 mm spacing. A radio-opaque marker shows segment orientation, and the linear 8-contact version spans 15.5 mm. [Paff2020]
- FDA PMA records show these other US DBS milestones: Abbott/St. Jude Brio (rechargeable) on 12 June 2015 (STN for PD; Vim for essential tremor) and the Abbott/St. Jude Infinity system on 19 September 2016. Boston Scientific Vercise (STN) was approved on 8 December 2017, the Cartesia directional lead on 18 January 2019, Medtronic Percept PC (sensing IPG) on 24 June 2020 and the Medtronic SenSight directional lead on 25 May 2021. Infinity was the first FDA-approved system with a directional lead (2016). The PMA database decision date is 19 September 2016; trade-press reports of the company announcement date from early October 2016 (not separately cited). [FDAPMA2026; Paff2020]
- Abbott Infinity IPGs: frequency range 2-240 Hz and pulse-width range 20-500 µs. The Infinity 7 (for bilateral stimulation) has a volume of 38.6 cm³ and a predicted life of 5 years. The non-rechargeable Medtronic Activa PC has an observed average battery life of 2.6-4.5 years, against roughly 5 years in marketing. An FDA supplement (26 May 2018) extended the service life of the rechargeable Activa RC from 9 to 15 years. [Paff2020; FDAPMA2026]
- Implantation precision is typically 1-2 mm from the planned target, and the brain can shift 2-4 mm during surgery. Placement errors are reported in up to 40% of DBS surgeries. [Koeglsperger2019]

### Stimulation parameters and programming (for the pulse-train animation)
- Programming usually starts with a "monopolar review": each ring contact is tested as cathode with the IPG case as anode. Pulse width and frequency are held at 60 µs and 130 Hz while amplitude is raised to find the benefit threshold and then the side-effect threshold. [Koeglsperger2019]
- In STN-DBS, the amplitude needed to abolish wrist rigidity was 0.7-1.7 mA in one study, and side effects appeared at 1.3-3.4 mA. Intraoperatively, rigidity vanished at a mean of 0.94 V (130 Hz, 100 µs). Rigidity improves within about 20 s of effective stimulation, while tremor stops almost at once and axial signs can take hours to days. [Koeglsperger2019]
- In a double-blind study of 12 patients, voltages of 3 V or more and frequencies of 130 Hz or more gave the greatest improvement in tremor, bradykinesia and rigidity. 5 Hz significantly worsened akinesia, and the highest voltage with the narrowest pulse width was most effective. [Moro2002]
- Benefit from STN-DBS appears above about 50 Hz and peaks near 130 Hz. Pulse width is usually kept at the lowest standard value of 60 µs, and pulse widths below 60 µs (newer IPGs) can widen the therapeutic window. A "typical chronic range of 60-90 µs" is widely quoted but did not appear in the primary sources read here; they support 60 µs as the standard starting and usual value, with 30-450 µs tested experimentally (60-90 µs unverified as a range). [Paff2020; Koeglsperger2019]
- No significant extra benefit was seen above 185 Hz, and 185 Hz has been proposed for GPi-DBS. A 30 µs pulse width gave a wider therapeutic window than 60 µs with non-inferior UPDRS III efficacy (CUSTOM-DBS, n=15). [Koeglsperger2019]
- For Vim-DBS (tremor), current is raised at 60 µs/130 Hz until tremor stops or side effects appear. If tremor is not controlled at 3.5 V, pulse width and then frequency are increased. [Koeglsperger2019]
- When gait freezing or other axial problems predominate after STN-DBS, low-frequency stimulation (60-80 Hz) has helped freezing, speech and swallowing in some studies but not others. [Koeglsperger2019]
- Optimising DBS parameters usually takes 3-6 months over 4-5 programming sessions. [Bronstein2011]

### Proposed mechanisms and beta oscillations (for the beta/aDBS animation)
- Prevailing theories hold that DBS disrupts pathological circuit activity. Effects occur at ionic, protein, cellular and network levels, and high-frequency trains (~100 Hz, ~0.1 ms pulses) produce network effects that differ fundamentally from low-frequency (~10 Hz) stimulation. [Lozano2019]
- Under the "information lesion" hypothesis, a constant-frequency stimulus carries essentially zero information and overrides intrinsic activity in stimulated neurons, limiting the spread of oscillations. Primate data showing partly preserved pallidal movement-related firing during DBS argue against a simple version of this idea. The thalamus may act as a low-pass filter. It passes basal-ganglia input at and below the beta band (12-30 Hz) but not the >100 Hz activity driven by DBS. [Lozano2019]
- Exaggerated beta (13-30 Hz) synchronisation is associated with bradykinesia. After STN high-frequency stimulation, local beta power in the STN stayed reduced for 12 s, and the size of the reduction correlated with movement amplitude. The 5-12 Hz band showed no such effect. [Kuhn2008]
- STN local field potential power in the beta (~13-30 Hz) and alpha (8-12 Hz) bands is reduced by both dopaminergic medication and DBS, in proportion to motor improvement. [BronteStewart2025]
- STN high-frequency oscillations (~200-500 Hz) are a pro-kinetic signal reduced in untreated PD that shifts to about 350 Hz with dopaminergic therapy. [Koeglsperger2026]

### Adaptive (closed-loop) DBS
- First proof of principle (8 patients): beta-triggered adaptive STN-DBS improved motor scores by 66% (unblinded) and 50% (blinded). That was 29% and 27% better than continuous DBS, with stimulation time cut by 56%. [Little2013]
- ADAPT-PD (NCT04547712) was an international, open-label pivotal trial with a single-blind randomised crossover of two aDBS modes, 30 days each, followed by optional 10-month home follow-up. It enrolled 68 people (mean age 62.2 years; 70.6% male) with stable STN or GPi continuous DBS on a Medtronic Percept PC. [BronteStewart2025; CTgov2026]
- ADAPT-PD control signal: alpha-beta (8-30 Hz) local field potential power. The peak frequency chosen spanned 9-29 Hz, with medians of 17.6 Hz (left) and 18.6 Hz (right). Peaks were detected in 44/54 legacy leads (81.5%) and 13/14 SenSight leads (92.9%). [BronteStewart2025]
- ADAPT-PD primary endpoint (revised post hoc to no more than 2 h/day loss of on-time without troublesome dyskinesia versus continuous DBS): met by 91% with dual-threshold aDBS (n=40) and 79% with single-threshold aDBS (n=35). [BronteStewart2025]
- In ADAPT-PD, total electrical energy delivered fell 15% with single-threshold aDBS versus continuous DBS (148.1 to 125.0 µW; nominal P=.01) and did not differ with dual-threshold aDBS. All but one stimulation-related adverse event resolved during set-up, and there were no serious device adverse events in long-term follow-up. [BronteStewart2025]
- Controversy: the primary endpoint was revised post hoc, so the authors say confirmatory hypothesis testing is limited. The registry lists 85 enrolled against 68 in the paper, and a 2026 review reports different subgroup counts (n=30 crossover, n=40 follow-up). [BronteStewart2025; CTgov2026; Koeglsperger2026]
- Single-threshold control rapidly suppresses brief beta bursts (>500 ms), while dual-threshold control adjusts more slowly (every 2.5-5 min) to track beta changes linked to levodopa cycles. [Koeglsperger2026]
- Beta biomarkers are not universal. ADAPT-PD found bilaterally elevated alpha/beta activity in 65.2% of patients; other studies report 78-95% off medication, and one intraoperative series found bilateral beta in only 47.25% of 91 patients. [Koeglsperger2026]
- A meta-analysis of 19 small studies (all n<15) suggested aDBS improves motor symptoms by about 33.9% while cutting stimulation energy by about 55% versus continuous DBS. The reviewers caution that the findings generalise poorly. [Koeglsperger2026]
- Medtronic BrainSense Adaptive DBS received CE mark on 13 January 2025 (EU/UK) and FDA approval as PMA supplement P960009/S478, decided 20 February 2025 and announced 24 February 2025. It was the first commercial closed-loop DBS and can be added to implanted Percept neurostimulators. The FDA aDBS indication repeats the 2015 PD wording: bilateral GPi or STN stimulation for levodopa-responsive PD of at least 4 years' duration not adequately controlled with medication, "including motor complications of recent onset (from 4 months to 3 years) or motor complications of longer-standing duration". [Medtronic2025; FDAPMA2026]

### Regulatory note on "early" DBS (2015/2016)
- PMA supplement P960009/S229, decided 17 November 2015, changed the Medtronic PD indication to "levodopa-responsive Parkinson's disease of at least 4 years' duration that are not adequately controlled with medication". This expansion is often dated to 2016 in secondary sources. The "recent onset (4 months to 3 years)" wording appears in current labelling. [FDAPMA2026]

### Randomised trials and effect sizes
- Deuschl 2006 (n=156, under 75 years, randomised pairs, 6 months): STN-DBS improved PDQ-39 more than medication (50/78 pairs; mean improvement 9.5 points) and off-medication UPDRS III (55/78 pairs; 19.6 points). PDQ-39 mobility, ADL, emotional well-being, stigma and bodily discomfort improved 24-38%. Deuschl 2006 safety: serious adverse events were 13% with neurostimulation versus 4% with medication, including one fatal intracerebral haemorrhage. Overall adverse events were higher with medication (64% vs 50%). [Deuschl2006]
- Weaver 2009 (VA CSP 468, n=255, 25% aged 70 or over, 6 months): bilateral STN or GPi DBS gave a mean 4.6 h/day more on-time without troubling dyskinesia, versus 0 h/day with best medical therapy (difference 4.5 h/day, 95% CI 3.7-5.4). 71% vs 32% gained a clinically meaningful motor improvement (5 points or more). Weaver 2009 safety: 49 DBS patients vs 15 on best medical therapy had at least one serious adverse event, including 39 surgery-related events and 1 death from cerebral haemorrhage. There were small decrements in some information-processing tests. [Weaver2009]
- PD SURG (Williams 2010, n=366, UK, 1 year): PDQ-39 summary index improved 5.0 points with surgery vs 0.3 with best medical therapy (difference -4.7, 95% CI -7.6 to -1.8). 19% had serious surgery-related adverse events, with one procedure-related death and no suicides. [Williams2010]
- EARLYSTIM (Schuepbach 2013, n=251, mean age 52, mean disease duration 7.5 years, early motor complications, 2 years): PDQ-39 improved 7.8 points with STN-DBS and worsened 0.2 with medical therapy (difference 8.0; P=0.002). DBS was also superior on UPDRS III, II and IV and on time with good mobility without dyskinesia. [Schuepbach2013]
- EARLYSTIM safety: serious adverse events occurred in 54.8% (DBS) vs 44.1% (medical), and 17.7% were related to surgery or the device. [Schuepbach2013]
- EARLYSTIM secondary report (news summary of the NEJM paper, not read in full text): the off-medication UPDRS III between-group difference was 16.4 points. Levodopa-equivalent dose fell 39% with DBS and rose about 21% with medication; there were 2 suicides with DBS vs 1 with medication. [Otto2013; Schuepbach2013] (full text unverified)
- Follett 2010 (n=299, GPi 152 vs STN 147, 24 months): blinded off-medication UPDRS III change did not differ (P=0.50). STN needed less dopaminergic medication (P=0.02) but had greater visuomotor processing-speed decline (P=0.03), and depression worsened after STN but improved after GPi (P=0.02). [Follett2010]
- NSTAPS (Odekerken 2013, n=128): no difference in the primary disability or cognitive/mood/behavioural outcomes. Off-drug, STN gave larger improvements in UPDRS motor score (20.3 vs 11.4 points; P=0.03) and larger levodopa-equivalent reductions (546 vs 208 mg; P=0.01). The authors suggest STN may be preferred. [Odekerken2013]
- Controversy: the US trial supports picking a target on non-motor grounds (GPi gentler on mood and processing speed), while NSTAPS favours STN for off-phase motor benefit and drug reduction. The MDS review rates both bilateral STN and GPi DBS "clinically useful". [Follett2010; Odekerken2013; Fox2018]

### Long-term outcomes
- Krack 2003 (first 49 Grenoble STN patients, 5 years): off-medication motor scores improved 54% and ADL 49%. Speech was the only motor item not improved off medication, and dopaminergic dose and dyskinesia were reduced. Krack 2003: on-medication akinesia, speech, postural stability and freezing of gait worsened between year 1 and year 5. Three patients developed dementia after year 3, one had a large intracerebral haemorrhage and one died by suicide. [Krack2003]
- Bove 2021 (51 patients, 17.06 ± 2.18 years after STN-DBS): versus baseline, time with dyskinesia fell 75%, off-time 58.7% and dopaminergic drugs 50.6%. PDQ-39 total improved 13.8%. These two cohorts give the long-term "about 50%" levodopa-equivalent reduction after STN-DBS: 50.6% at 15+ years. They are uncontrolled (Class IV evidence). [Bove2021; Krack2003]
- Bronstein consensus: "With the exception of tremor, DBS treats primarily LD-responsive symptoms". Over time patients often develop freezing of gait, postural instability and cognitive decline, and STN-DBS can worsen speech and gait in some patients. [Bronstein2011]

### Risks and side effects
- Stimulation side effects reflect current spread to neighbouring structures. The most frequent are spastic contractions ("facial pulling") from current reaching the internal capsule. Others are dysarthria (internal capsule or cerebello-thalamic fibres), diplopia or gaze deviation (oculomotor fibres), paraesthesia (medial lemniscus), and dyskinesia from stimulation of the STN itself. [Koeglsperger2019]
- Directional stimulation widens the therapeutic window. In a randomised double-blind crossover of 234 patients, the window was wider in 183/202 (90.6%), with a mean of 2.98 vs 2.11 mA (+41%). 52.8% of blinded patients preferred the directional period vs 25.9% the omnidirectional. [Schnitzler2022]
- In a systematic review of 63 studies (13,056 patients), intracranial haemorrhage occurred in 2.9% per patient and 1.6% per lead, and 49.6% of these were symptomatic. Microelectrode recording, systolic BP over 140 mm Hg and sulcal trajectories were risk factors. All-cause mortality was 0.4%, with haemorrhage accounting for 22% of deaths. [Cheyuo2024]
- In a meta-analysis of 58 studies (11,289 patients), surgical-site infection was 4.9% (95% CI 4.1-6.1%) of DBS procedures, and 61.2% were at the IPG pocket. Most centres remove the IPG, give antibiotics and reimplant. [Spindler2023]
- Consensus ranges: infection 0-15% (the most commonly reported complication), intracranial haemorrhage 0-10% (symptomatic probably under 2% at most centres), lead erosion 1-2.5%, lead fracture 0-15% and lead migration 0-19%. [Bronstein2011]
- In a multicentre survey of 5311 STN-DBS patients, completed suicide was 0.45% and attempted suicide 0.90%. The first-year suicide rate (263/100,000/year) was 12.6-15.6 times the expected rate. Postoperative depression, being single and prior impulse-control disorders were associated factors. [Voon2008]
- In a meta-analysis (28 studies, 612 patients), the only moderate cognitive declines after STN-DBS were in semantic (Cohen's d 0.73) and phonemic (d 0.51) verbal fluency. Executive function and verbal memory declined slightly. Consensus statements add that STN-DBS may bring more depression, apathy, impulsivity, worse verbal fluency and executive dysfunction in a subset of patients. [Parsons2006; Bronstein2011]
- Three months after STN-DBS, men gained a mean 3.4 kg (fat and fat-free mass) and women 2.6 kg (fat only). Daily energy expenditure fell 7-13%. [Montaurier2007]

### Selection criteria
- The Bronstein 2011 consensus describes good candidates as people with PD who do not have significant active cognitive or psychiatric problems and who have medically intractable motor fluctuations, intractable tremor, or intolerance of medication side effects. [Bronstein2011]
- Bronstein 2011 treats a 30% improvement in UPDRS III on levodopa challenge as a marker of levodopa response, "not an absolute criterion". It finds no data for a clear age cut-off, and levodopa-resistant tremor is an accepted exception. [Bronstein2011]
- CAPSIT-PD (1999) set a common minimum assessment protocol for PD surgery. The 33% levodopa-test improvement threshold and 5-year minimum disease duration often attributed to it could not be checked in the full text (unverified). [Defer1999]
- The CNS/MDS consensus (Lang 2006) lists preoperative factors: age, comorbidity, neuropsychological and neuropsychiatric status, which motor signs are being targeted, prior response to medical therapy, and exclusion of atypical parkinsonism. [Lang2006]
- In the trials, inclusion boundaries were under 75 years (Deuschl), Hoehn and Yahr stage 2 or more off medication with 25% aged 70 or over (Weaver), and early motor complications at a mean age of 52 and mean 7.5 years' duration (EARLYSTIM; the exact age cap was not verified from the abstract). [Deuschl2006; Weaver2009; Schuepbach2013]
- MDS evidence-based review (to end-2016): bilateral STN and GPi DBS are "clinically useful" for motor fluctuations and for dyskinesia, as is levodopa intestinal infusion for motor fluctuations. [Fox2018]
- NICE NG71: offer best medical therapy, which may include intermittent apomorphine injection and/or continuous subcutaneous apomorphine infusion, for advanced PD (1.8.1). Do not offer DBS if symptoms are adequately controlled by best medical therapy (1.8.2), and consider DBS if they are not (1.8.3). [NICE2017]
- NICE NG71 1.8.4 cross-refers to TA934 (2023): foslevodopa-foscarbidopa for advanced levodopa-responsive PD with severe fluctuations and dyskinesia when apomorphine or DBS cannot be used or no longer control symptoms. Intestinal gels are left to an NHS England commissioning policy. [NICE2017]

### MR-guided focused ultrasound (MRgFUS) lesioning
- Bond 2017 (n=27, randomised 2:1 to unilateral Vim thalamotomy or sham, tremor-dominant PD): median on-medication tremor score improved 62% with FUS vs 22% with sham (P=.04) at 3 months. 26/27 participants were male. [Bond2017]
- Bond 2017 adverse events: 2 early cases (8%) of mild hemiparesis from internal-capsule heating, after which an extra thermometry axis was monitored. Persistent events were orofacial paraesthesia in 20%, finger paraesthesia in 5% and ataxia in 5%. [Bond2017]
- FDA approved Exablate Neuro for unilateral Vim thalamotomy in tremor-dominant PD with medication-refractory tremor (age 30 or over) on 16 December 2018. The original essential-tremor approval was 11 July 2016. [FDAPMA2026]
- Krishna 2023 (n=94, 3:1 unilateral GPi ablation vs sham): 69% vs 32% responded at 3 months (difference 37 points; P=0.003), with response defined as at least 3 points better on treated-side off-med MDS-UPDRS III or on UDysRS. 30 of 39 assessed responders still responded at 12 months. [Krishna2023]
- Pallidotomy adverse events in Krishna 2023 included dysarthria, gait disturbance, loss of taste, visual disturbance and facial weakness. FDA approved unilateral FUS pallidotomy (age 30 or over, medication-refractory moderate-to-severe motor complications) on 29 October 2021. [Krishna2023; FDAPMA2026]
- Martinez-Fernandez 2020 (n=40, 2:1 unilateral STN ablation vs sham, markedly asymmetric PD): more-affected-side off-med MDS-UPDRS III fell from 19.9 to 9.9 with FUS vs 18.7 to 17.1 with sham, a between-group difference of 8.1 points (95% CI 6.0-10.3) at 4 months. [MartinezFernandez2020]
- Martinez-Fernandez 2020 adverse events (active group, n=27): speech disturbance in 15 (persisting in 3 at 4 months), gait disturbance in 13 (2), off-medication dyskinesia in 6 (3), weakness in 5 (2) and facial weakness in 3 (1). Some deficits persisted at 12 months in 6 patients. [MartinezFernandez2020]
- Staged bilateral FUS subthalamotomy (6 patients; median 3.2 years between sides): off-med MDS-UPDRS III improved 52.6% (37.5 to 20.5) at 6 months after the second procedure, and the second side improved 64.3%. Speech disturbance occurred in 4, remaining mild at 6 months in 2. [MartinezFernandez2024]
- FDA (3 July 2025) approved Exablate unilateral pallidothalamic tractotomy for advanced PD with motor complications. A staged second-side tractotomy is allowed at least 6 months after the first. [FDAPMA2026]
- Why lesions have traditionally been one-sided: DBS remains the procedure of choice for bilateral treatment because adjustable stimulation is safer, which lesional surgery does not offer. In the pivotal trials, FUS lesions were unilateral and opposite the most affected side. [Lozano2019; Bond2017; Krishna2023; MartinezFernandez2020]

### Infusion therapies (for "Levodopa over time")
- Olanow 2014 (12-week double-blind double-dummy trial; all participants had a PEG-J): levodopa-carbidopa intestinal gel (LCIG) cut off-time by 4.04 h vs 2.14 h with oral immediate-release levodopa-carbidopa (difference -1.91 h; P=0.0015). On-time without troublesome dyskinesia rose 4.11 vs 2.24 h (difference +1.86 h). [Olanow2014]
- Olanow 2014 safety: adverse events in 95% (LCIG) and 100% (oral), mostly linked to the gastrojejunostomy tube. Serious adverse events were 14% vs 21%. The trial registrations are NCT00660387 and NCT00357994; the abstract prints the latter as "NCT0357994". [Olanow2014; CTgov2026]
- Duopa (LCIG; NDA 203952) received FDA approval on 9 January 2015. It is infused into the jejunum through a PEG-J over 16 hours (morning dose, continuous dose and extra doses), with a maximum of 2000 mg levodopa a day (one cassette). [DrugsFDA2026F]
- Duopa label: gastrointestinal and procedure-related risks include bezoar, intussusception, intestinal obstruction or perforation, pancreatitis, peritonitis and sepsis. 19/412 (5%) patients in trials developed a generalised (mostly axonal, sensory or sensorimotor) polyneuropathy. [DrugsFDA2026F]
- A systematic review (38 articles) found peripheral neuropathy in 30.2% of patients on oral levodopa vs 42.1% on LCIG. Onset was acute or subacute in 35.7% of LCIG cases, and neuropathy was associated with high levodopa doses, raised homocysteine and methylmalonic acid, and low B12/B6. [Romagnolo2019]
- Foslevodopa-foscarbidopa phase 3 (Soileau 2022, NCT04380142, n=141, 24 h/day subcutaneous infusion, 12 weeks): on-time without troublesome dyskinesia rose 2.72 h vs 0.97 h with oral levodopa-carbidopa (difference 1.75 h; P=0.0083). Off-time fell 2.75 vs 0.96 h (difference -1.79 h). [Soileau2022]
- Soileau 2022 safety: infusion-site erythema 27%, pain 26%, cellulitis 19% and oedema 12%. Adverse events led to discontinuation in 22% vs 1% on oral treatment. [Soileau2022]
- Vyalev (foscarbidopa/foslevodopa; NDA 216962) carries an FDA approval date of 16 October 2024 in Drugs@FDA; AbbVie announced it on 17 October 2024. The label sets a maximum of 3,525 mg foslevodopa a day (about 2,500 mg levodopa), given subcutaneously through the VYAFUSER pump. [DrugsFDA2026F]
- Vyalev label: the most common adverse reactions (10% or more and above oral) are infusion/catheter-site reactions, infusion/catheter-site infections, hallucinations and dyskinesia. Vitamin B6 is assessed before starting. [DrugsFDA2026F]
- In the EU, Produodopa received marketing authorisation by decentralised procedure in Q3 2022, the VYAFUSER pump got its CE mark in November 2023, and EU launch was announced on 9 January 2024. A "2023" date for Produodopa matches the pump's CE mark and the launch timing rather than the authorisation. [AbbVie2024]
- BouNDless (Espay 2024, NCT04006210, n=259 randomised, ND0612 24 h/day subcutaneous levodopa-carbidopa 60/7.5 mg/mL): ND0612 gave 1.72 h more on-time without troublesome dyskinesia than oral levodopa-carbidopa (95% CI 1.08-2.36) and 1.40 h less off-time. [Espay2024]
- In BouNDless, infusion-site reactions affected 83% during the open-label run-in, and 57% vs 43% in the double-blind phase (ND0612 vs oral with placebo infusion). The design is unusual: both arms started optimised on ND0612, so the "gain" reflects loss of benefit after switching to oral treatment. [Espay2024]
- ND0612 US status: the FDA issued a complete response letter in June 2024, accepted a resubmission in May 2025 and issued another complete response letter (manufacturing-site and nonclinical observations) on 23 October 2025. No ND0612 approval appeared in Drugs@FDA via openFDA on 2026-10-20. [PharmaLetter2025; DrugsFDA2026F]
- TOLEDO (Katzenschlager 2018, NCT02006121, n=107): waking-day subcutaneous apomorphine infusion (3-8 mg/h for about 16 h; mean final dose 4.68 mg/h) cut off-time by 2.47 h/day vs 0.58 h with placebo (difference -1.89 h; P=0.0025). [Katzenschlager2018]
- Onapgo (apomorphine continuous subcutaneous infusion; NDA 214056) received FDA approval on 3 February 2025 for motor fluctuations in advanced PD. Common reactions (10% or more and at least twice placebo) were infusion-site nodules, nausea, somnolence, infusion-site erythema, dyskinesia, headache and insomnia. [DrugsFDA2026F]
- Levodopa-entacapone-carbidopa intestinal gel (LECIG, Lecigon): in an 11-patient randomised crossover, a 20% lower levodopa dose gave the same systemic levodopa exposure as standard LCIG (ratio 1.10). [Senek2017]
- LECIG reached the Swedish market in 2019. In the Swedish registry (150 patients, median age 73, median 17 years since motor onset), complications were mainly PEG-J/stoma related (30%), 13.3% discontinued and 7.3% died on treatment. [Othman2025]
