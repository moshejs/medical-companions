# E. Levodopa and the other medicines
This pass covers levodopa pharmacology (absorption, peripheral metabolism, decarboxylase and COMT inhibition, half-life and Tmax), the short- and long-duration responses and why wearing-off appears, definitions of motor complications and how often they occur, the landmark trials (ELLDOPA, LEAP, STRIDE-PD, PD MED, CALM-PD, 056, ADAGIO, DATATOP), and the other medicine classes as they appear in trials, US FDA labels and NICE NG71 / MDS 2018 guidance. It is built to supply numbers for three animations: the journey of the pill, plasma level against a therapeutic window, and a treatment ladder. Trial numbers come from PubMed or Europe PMC abstracts, plus full text for the open-access reviews by Freitas 2017 and Jenner 2026. Label numbers come from openFDA or DailyMed SPL text and approval dates from Drugs@FDA (openFDA drugsfda endpoint), all opened 2026-10-20. Gaps and caveats: (1) The full texts of ELLDOPA, CALM-PD, PD MED and the Aquino & Fox review are behind paywalls, so ELLDOPA's per-arm dyskinesia percentages are marked unverified. (2) The commonly quoted figure that about 1% of levodopa reaches the brain without a decarboxylase inhibitor is traceable here only to a secondary review. (3) No source read names the brain transporter "LAT1" explicitly; labels say "large neutral amino acid carrier". (4) The Israeli Ministry of Health drug registry (israeldrugs.health.gov.il) refused automated requests (HTTP 403), so Israeli registration status is unverified except for rasagiline's 2005 approval as reported by Teva. (5) No numeric, citable estimate of "therapeutic-window narrowing" in mg or ng/mL was found; the concept is supported qualitatively and is disputed (see below). The brief asked for "Contin & Martin"; the review found is Contin & Martinelli 2010. Benserazide has no US application in Drugs@FDA, so no US label exists for it. FDA labels are grouped into four class-level source entries (levodopa products, COMT/MAO-B inhibitors, dopamine agonists, amantadine); each entry lists every product's DailyMed set ID, and each fact names the product it draws on.

## Facts
### Journey of the pill: absorption and transport
- Dopamine itself does not cross the blood-brain barrier. Levodopa, its metabolic precursor, does cross and is presumed to be converted to dopamine in the brain; this is the label's stated mechanism of symptom relief [LevodopaLabels2026]. Oral levodopa is rapidly decarboxylated to dopamine in extracerebral tissues, so "only a small portion" of a dose reaches the CNS unchanged. Large doses were therefore needed before decarboxylase inhibitors, with nausea and other effects partly due to dopamine formed outside the brain [LevodopaLabels2026].
- Levodopa is absorbed rapidly in the proximal small bowel by a saturable facilitated transport system shared with other large neutral amino acids (LNAAs). Transport from plasma into the brain uses the same carriers [Contin2010]. A 2024 label states that levodopa enters the brain "by the carrier mechanism for large neutral amino acids" [LevodopaLabels2026]. The specific name LAT1 does not appear in any source read (unverified here).
- Absorption occurs only in the upper gastrointestinal tract (duodenum/jejunum). Slowing gastric emptying, which delivers oral levodopa to that site, is recognised in chronic PD and correlates strongly with delayed time-to-ON [Jenner2026].
- In 9 patients with fluctuations, meals cut peak plasma levodopa by 29% and delayed absorption by 34 minutes. Constant IV infusion produced a stable clinical state lasting 12 hours in all 6 patients tested, and up to 36 hours in some [Nutt1984]. High-protein meals, or oral phenylalanine, leucine or isoleucine (100 mg/kg), reversed the benefit of IV levodopa without lowering plasma levodopa. Glycine and lysine at the same doses had no effect. This places the protein competition at the blood-brain barrier as well as the gut [Nutt1984].
- Immediate-release (IR) carbidopa-levodopa (Dhivy) taken fasting reached peak concentration at 1 hour for levodopa and 3 hours for carbidopa. A high-fat, high-calorie meal reduced levodopa Cmax by about 25% (AUC unchanged) and delayed the peak by about 30 minutes [LevodopaLabels2026]. Oral levodopa usually takes "at least 20 to 30 minutes to take effect", depending on protein intake, gastric emptying rate and constipation [Freitas2017]. Levodopa is 10-30% bound to plasma proteins; carbidopa is about 36% bound [LevodopaLabels2026].

### Journey of the pill: peripheral metabolism, carbidopa and the brain fraction
- Carbidopa inhibits peripheral decarboxylation of levodopa and does not cross the blood-brain barrier, so it does not affect levodopa metabolism in the brain [LevodopaLabels2026]. Carbidopa reduces the amount of levodopa needed for a given response by about 75%. It raises plasma levodopa levels and half-life and lowers plasma and urinary dopamine and homovanillic acid [LevodopaLabels2026].
- Plasma half-life of levodopa per the Sinemet label: about 50 minutes without carbidopa, about 1.5 hours with it [LevodopaLabels2026]. Other labels report about 1.5 h with carbidopa (subcutaneous foslevodopa) [LevodopaLabels2026], about 2 h (Dhivy, fasted) [LevodopaLabels2026], about 2 h terminal (Rytary) [LevodopaLabels2026] and 2.3 h after inhaled levodopa [LevodopaLabels2026]. Labels differ in method. The entacapone label gives 1.3 h with carbidopa alone, rising to 2.4 h when entacapone is added [EnzymeInhibitorLabels2026].
- Studies show peripheral dopa decarboxylase is saturated by carbidopa at about 70-100 mg/day. Patients receiving less carbidopa are more likely to have nausea and vomiting [LevodopaLabels2026]. In the Rytary label, 70-100 mg/day of carbidopa from other products is equivalent in exposure to 140-200 mg/day from Rytary [LevodopaLabels2026]. Levodopa-induced nausea and vomiting are less frequent with carbidopa-levodopa than with levodopa alone. Carbidopa does not reduce central effects: because more levodopa reaches the brain, dyskinesias "may occur at lower dosages and sooner" than with levodopa alone [LevodopaLabels2026].
- When decarboxylation is blocked, levodopa metabolism shifts to COMT, which methylates it to 3-O-methyldopa (3-OMD). Only 5-10% of levodopa given with a decarboxylase inhibitor reaches the brain. 3-OMD has a half-life of more than 10 hours, accumulates, and may compete with levodopa for transport into the brain [Jenner2026].
- Without a decarboxylase inhibitor, a 2021 review states "only 1% of levodopa reaches the brain" [Silva2021]. This is a secondary source; the primary measurement was not traced (treat as approximate).
- Pyridoxine (vitamin B6) at 10-25 mg can reverse the effect of levodopa given alone by speeding peripheral decarboxylation. Carbidopa blocks this interaction [LevodopaLabels2026]. Current carbidopa-levodopa labels carry a "Vitamin B6 Deficiency and Seizures" warning. Postmarketing seizures were refractory to anti-seizure drugs and resolved only with B6. The labels state B6 levels should be evaluated before starting and periodically [LevodopaLabels2026].
- Label interactions: iron salts chelate levodopa and carbidopa and reduce bioavailability. D2 antagonists (phenothiazines, butyrophenones, risperidone) and isoniazid may reduce the effect. Metoclopramide speeds gastric emptying but antagonises dopamine receptors [LevodopaLabels2026]. Age: levodopa AUC was 55% higher in healthy elderly than in young subjects. In PD patients, AUC was 28% higher and Cmax 24% higher at age 65 or above versus under 65 [LevodopaLabels2026].
- Benserazide, the other decarboxylase inhibitor used outside the US, has no application in Drugs@FDA [DrugsFDA2026]. It appears as an alternative to carbidopa in international trials, e.g. the SETTLE safinamide trial [Schapira2017].

### COMT inhibitors: effect on the plasma curve
- With entacapone 200 mg plus carbidopa-levodopa, levodopa AUC rises about 35% and elimination half-life lengthens from 1.3 to 2.4 hours. Peak levodopa concentration and its timing (Tmax about 1 hour) are generally unchanged. A single 200 mg dose inhibits erythrocyte COMT by about 65% on average, returning to baseline within 8 hours [EnzymeInhibitorLabels2026]. Opicapone 50 mg once daily inhibited erythrocyte COMT by up to 84%, with more than 65% inhibition maintained over 24 hours; more than 35% inhibition remained 5 days after the last dose. Levodopa Cmax rose by 43-44% and AUC by 62-94% [EnzymeInhibitorLabels2026].

### Formulations (plasma-curve shapes for the animation)
- Rytary (IPX066) extended-release capsules: levodopa bioavailability is about 70% of IR and Cmax about 30% of IR at comparable doses. Concentrations peak at about 1 hour and are maintained for about 4-5 hours. The peak-to-trough fluctuation index ((Cmax-Cmin)/Cavg) is about 1.5 versus about 3.2 for IR [LevodopaLabels2026]. ADVANCE-PD (n=393 randomised): off-time was 23.82% of waking hours on IPX066 versus 29.79% on IR. That equals an extra 1.17 h/day less off-time, with 3.6 versus 5.0 doses per day [Hauser2013].
- Crexont (IPX203): levodopa Cmax 38% of IR. After an initial peak at about 1 hour, concentrations are maintained for about 6-7 hours; fluctuation index about 1.7 versus 2.7 for IR. In the pivotal trial (506 randomised), week-20 off-time was 4.18 h versus 4.75 h on IR, and "good on" time was 11.35 h versus 10.77 h; mean levodopa dose was 1487 mg/day [LevodopaLabels2026].
- Inbrija (inhaled levodopa): median Tmax about 0.5 hours (range 0.17-2.00); bioavailability about 70% of oral IR; dose-normalised Cmax about 50% of oral IR. The label dose is 84 mg (two 42 mg capsules) as needed for OFF symptoms, up to 5 times daily (maximum 420 mg/day), and it is effective only alongside oral carbidopa-levodopa [LevodopaLabels2026]. SPAN-PD (n=351): 30 minutes after inhaled levodopa 84 mg, UPDRS III improved by 9.83 points versus 5.91 on placebo (difference -3.92; p=0.0088) [LeWitt2019]. In the same trial, 58% versus 36% returned to ON and stayed ON through 60 minutes [LevodopaLabels2026].
- Vyalev (foscarbidopa/foslevodopa, 24-hour subcutaneous infusion) bypasses the gut, so food does not change absorption. In a 12-week trial versus oral IR (n=141), "good on" time rose 2.72 h versus 0.97 h (difference 1.75 h) and off-time fell 2.75 h versus 0.96 h (difference -1.79 h) [LevodopaLabels2026].

### Practical levodopa dosing as published
- Sinemet starting dose: one 25/100 tablet three times daily, which provides 75 mg carbidopa per day. The dose may be increased by one tablet every day or every other day up to eight 25/100 tablets per day. With the 25/250 strength, the maximum is eight tablets per day (200 mg carbidopa / 2000 mg levodopa by arithmetic). Experience above 200 mg carbidopa per day is limited. The label notes blepharospasm "may be a useful early sign of excess dosage" [LevodopaLabels2026]. Switching from plain levodopa: levodopa must be stopped at least 12 hours before Sinemet, which is dosed at about 25% of the previous levodopa dose. The label advises that maintenance should provide at least 70-100 mg carbidopa per day [LevodopaLabels2026].
- Contraindications: non-selective MAO inhibitors (stopped at least two weeks beforehand) and narrow-angle glaucoma. Selegiline with carbidopa-levodopa may cause severe orthostatic hypotension [LevodopaLabels2026].
- Withdrawal: sporadic cases resembling neuroleptic malignant syndrome have followed dose reduction or withdrawal. Features include hyperthermia, rigidity, altered consciousness, autonomic instability, raised CPK, leukocytosis and myoglobinuria, with particular risk in patients receiving neuroleptics [LevodopaLabels2026]. The Dhivy label states sudden discontinuation should be avoided and the dose tapered [LevodopaLabels2026]. NICE recommends that antiparkinsonian medicines "should not be withdrawn abruptly or allowed to fail suddenly due to poor absorption" (1.3.2). It advises against "drug holidays" because of the risk of neuroleptic malignant syndrome (1.3.3), and calls for medicines to be given at the appropriate times in hospitals and care homes (1.3.4) [NICE2017].
- Sudden sleep onset without warning has been reported with carbidopa-levodopa, including while driving, as late as one year after starting treatment [LevodopaLabels2026].
- Pregnancy and lactation (Sinemet label): visceral and skeletal malformations occurred in rabbits at all doses tested, and there are no adequate human studies. Levodopa crosses the human placenta; fetal carbidopa concentrations appeared minimal. Levodopa has been detected in human milk [LevodopaLabels2026].

### Short- and long-duration response; why wearing-off appears
- Early in treatment patients notice little dose-to-dose fluctuation; this sustained benefit is the "long-duration response" (LDR). On average after 2-5 years of levodopa, the "short-duration response" (SDR) becomes more critical, with benefit oscillating around each dose [Freitas2017].
- In 18 patients studied before levodopa and at 6, 12, 24 and 48 months, mean SDR and LDR magnitudes after overnight withdrawal did not change. After 3 days off levodopa, however, SDR magnitude was progressively increasing (p<0.0001) and the LDR was decaying faster (p=0.0004). SDR duration did not change over 4 years, and dyskinesia severity increased (p=0.013) [Nutt2002].
- In a continuous IV infusion study (23 patients), the wearing-off phenomenon was interpreted as degeneration of dopamine terminals leaving an "inability to buffer variations in levodopa availability". The on-off phenomenon was interpreted as needing additional postsynaptic changes, which gradually remitted during continuous infusion [Mouradian1987]. Peripheral levodopa pharmacokinetics (clearance, plasma half-life, volume of distribution) did not differ between patients with on-off, wearing-off, stable response or no prior levodopa. The "efficacy half-time" differed markedly, pointing to central mechanisms [Fabbrini1987].
- Loss of nigrostriatal terminals and of normal dopamine recycling gives rise to pulsatile dopamine-receptor stimulation. PET shows sharp rises in striatal dopamine after levodopa in advanced PD, correlating with dyskinesia severity [Freitas2017].
- Because levodopa's plasma half-life is very short, plasma swings come to be matched by swings in clinical response as disease progresses. In advanced disease, low (subtherapeutic) concentrations can worsen parkinsonism and cause dyskinesia, while dyskinesia can also follow excessive peak concentrations [Contin2010].
- Controversy, therapeutic window: the idea of a progressively narrowing window (dyskinesia threshold falling towards the ON threshold) is widely taught. However, in 20 de novo patients followed over 4 years, dyskinesia and the antiparkinsonian response began and ended together, which the authors judged "not consistent with the postulated progressive decrease in threshold for dyskinesia" [Nutt2010]. No source read gave numeric window widths (mg or ng/mL); any such values in the animation would be illustrative only.

### Definitions of motor complications
- ON is improvement after each dose; OFF is recurrence of symptoms or lack of levodopa effect. Predictable wearing-off, the regular return of symptoms at the end of a dose, "is the most frequent type" [Freitas2017]. Unpredictable wearing-off ("sudden OFFs") occurs regardless of dose timing, is more common in advanced PD, and can develop "within a few seconds" [Freitas2017]. On-off fluctuation ("yo-yoing") is rapid, abrupt switching between ON and OFF. It is described as now rare owing to lower levodopa doses [Freitas2017].
- Delayed-on, partial response and dose failure (no-on) are attributed mainly to erratic levodopa absorption [Freitas2017], including delayed gastric emptying [Jenner2026].
- Peak-dose dyskinesia consists of involuntary movements coinciding with peak levodopa action; chorea is the most common form [Freitas2017]. Diphasic dyskinesia occurs "usually in 15% to 20% of patients", appearing as levels rise and fall at the beginning and end of a dose. It is also called the "dystonia-improvement-dystonia" (D-I-D) pattern and predominantly affects the legs [Freitas2017].
- Off-period dystonia is dystonia in OFF periods or at the ON-to-OFF transition, often painful and especially in the lower limbs [Freitas2017]. Fluctuations may also include beginning-of-dose worsening and end-of-dose rebound worse than the untreated baseline [Freitas2017].

### How often motor complications appear
- In a pooled literature analysis (74 publications, 112 treatment intervals), motor fluctuations among modern-era patients were nil in the first year of levodopa and affected about 40% by 4-6 years. Dyskinesia was "slightly less than 40%" by 4-6 years. Series that included pre-levodopa-era patients already showed median dyskinesia of 50% by 5-6 months [Ahlskog2001].
- A 2017 review cites an incidence of about 10% per year, up to 50% with some motor complication within 2-5 years, and 80-100% after 10 years of dopaminergic therapy [Freitas2017].
- Young onset: median time to dyskinesia and fluctuations was reported as 3 years in young-onset versus 4-6 years in older-onset patients. The review also describes female sex as a risk factor [Freitas2017].
- STRIDE-PD (745 analysed) grouped patients by levodopa dose: under 400 mg/day (n=157), 400 mg (n=310), 401-600 mg (n=201) and over 600 mg (n=77). Dyskinesia and wearing-off risk rose with dose (p<0.001 for both). Ranked predictors of dyskinesia were young onset, higher levodopa dose, low body weight, North American region, entacapone arm, female sex and worse UPDRS II [WarrenOlanow2013].
- Cumulative dyskinesia at 5 years was 45% with initial levodopa versus 20% with initial ropinirole [Rascol2000]. Any dopaminergic motor complication at about 6 years was 68.4% with initial levodopa versus 50.0% with initial pramipexole [PSG2009CALM].

### Landmark levodopa trials
- ELLDOPA (n=361) randomised patients to carbidopa-levodopa 37.5/150, 75/300 or 150/600 mg/day or placebo for 40 weeks, then 2 weeks' washout. UPDRS worsened from baseline to week 42 by 7.8 points on placebo versus 1.9, 1.9 and -1.4 points on the three doses (p<0.001) [Fahn2004]. In ELLDOPA's beta-CIT substudy, dopamine-transporter uptake declined more on levodopa (-6%, -4%, -7.2%) than on placebo (-1.4%; p=0.036). The 600 mg arm had significantly more dyskinesia, hypertonia, infection, headache and nausea. Per-arm dyskinesia percentages are not in the abstract (unverified) [Fahn2004].
- LEAP (n=445) was a delayed-start trial of levodopa/carbidopa 100/25 mg three times daily for 80 weeks versus placebo for 40 weeks followed by active drug. UPDRS change at week 80 was -1.0 versus -2.0 (difference 1.0; 95% CI -1.5 to 3.5; p=0.44), i.e. no disease-modifying effect. Dyskinesia and fluctuation rates did not differ [Verschuur2019].
- STRIDE-PD (n=747) tested levodopa/carbidopa/entacapone versus levodopa/carbidopa, both given 4 times daily at 3.5-hour intervals. Entacapone shortened time to dyskinesia (HR 1.29; p=0.04) and raised its frequency at week 134 (42% vs 32%; p=0.02). The entacapone group received higher levodopa-equivalent doses, and the authors suggest the regimen did not provide truly continuous delivery [Stocchi2010].
- PD MED (n=1620; open-label, pragmatic): PDQ-39 mobility was 1.8 points better with initial levodopa than with levodopa-sparing therapy (p=0.005; minimally important difference 6 points), and 1.4 points better with MAO-B inhibitors than with dopamine agonists. Discontinuation for side effects was 28% (agonists), 23% (MAO-B) and 2% (levodopa) [PDMED2014].
- CALM-PD (n=301) compared pramipexole 0.5 mg three times daily with carbidopa-levodopa 25/100 three times daily. Motor complications at 23.5 months were 28% versus 51% (HR 0.45). UPDRS improvement was 9.2 versus 4.5 points in favour of levodopa, and somnolence was 32.4% versus 17.3%. Beta-CIT decline was 20.0% versus 24.8% (p=0.15) [PSG2000CALM]. In CALM-PD's 6-year extension, Schwab & England ADL scores were similar (79.9 vs 82.5; p=0.19). Epworth sleepiness was higher with initial pramipexole (11.3 vs 8.6), and disabling dyskinesia was uncommon in both groups [PSG2009CALM].
- The 056 study (n=268, 5 years) compared ropinirole with levodopa, with open-label levodopa allowed as needed. 34% of ropinirole completers needed no levodopa. Final doses were 16.5 mg ropinirole (plus 427 mg levodopa in those supplemented) versus 753 mg levodopa [Rascol2000].
- ADAGIO (n=1176) was a rasagiline delayed-start trial (72 weeks versus 36 weeks placebo then active). At 1 mg, all three hierarchical endpoints were met: UPDRS slope weeks 12-36 was 0.09 versus 0.14 points/week, and change at week 72 was 2.82 versus 4.52 points. At 2 mg, the week-72 difference was not significant (3.47 vs 3.11; p=0.60). The authors advised caution because the two doses diverged [Olanow2009]. An FDA advisory committee voted 17-0 that the sponsor had not provided substantial evidence that rasagiline delays clinical progression of Parkinson's disease [Mechcatie2011]. Meeting date of 17 October 2011 per search snippet (unverified on fetched page).
- DATATOP (n=800, 2x2 factorial): deprenyl (selegiline) 10 mg/day delayed disability needing levodopa (HR 0.50), a median difference of about 9 months, with the benefit concentrated in the first 12 months. Tocopherol 2000 IU/day had no effect. Ratings improved in the first 3 months on deprenyl and worsened after withdrawal, so a symptomatic effect could not be excluded [PSG1993DATATOP].

### Dopamine agonists
- Pramipexole IR titration (normal renal function): 0.125 mg three times daily in week 1, rising weekly to 1.5 mg three times daily (4.5 mg/day) by week 7, no faster than every 5-7 days. In a fixed-dose early-PD study, 3, 4.5 and 6 mg/day added no significant benefit over 1.5 mg/day [AgonistLabels2026]. Pramipexole IR renal dosing (start / maximum): CrCl above 50 mL/min, 0.125 mg three times daily / 1.5 mg three times daily; 30-50 mL/min, 0.125 mg twice daily / 0.75 mg three times daily; 15-30 mL/min, 0.125 mg once daily / 1.5 mg once daily; below 15 mL/min or haemodialysis, no data [AgonistLabels2026].
- Pramipexole label: somnolence is "common" above 1.5 mg/day. Sudden sleep without warning while driving has been reported, as late as one year after starting [AgonistLabels2026]. The first case series described 8 patients on pramipexole and 1 on ropinirole who fell asleep while driving and had accidents, 5 of them with no warning [Frucht1999]. Pramipexole lactation: "inhibition of lactation is expected" because pramipexole inhibits prolactin secretion [AgonistLabels2026].
- Ropinirole IR: starting dose 0.25 mg three times daily, titrated to a maximum of 24 mg/day. In end-stage renal disease on haemodialysis, the maximum is 18 mg/day [AgonistLabels2026].
- Rotigotine patch: early PD starts at 2 mg/24 h, rising weekly by 2 mg/24 h (lowest effective dose 4 mg/24 h, maximum 6 mg/24 h). Advanced PD starts at 4 mg/24 h with a maximum of 8 mg/24 h. Application sites are rotated daily and not reused within 14 days [AgonistLabels2026]. In advanced-PD trial PD-4 (n=351), off-time fell by 2.7 h (8 mg/24 h) and 2.1 h (12 mg/24 h) versus 0.9 h on placebo, differences of -1.8 h and -1.2 h [AgonistLabels2026].
- Dopamine agonist withdrawal syndrome: 5 of 26 agonist tapers (19%) produced a stereotyped, levodopa-refractory syndrome of anxiety, panic, depression, dysphoria, sweating, fatigue, pain, orthostatic hypotension and drug craving. All 5 had prior agonist-related impulse-control disorders and higher agonist exposure (420 vs 230 mg LEDD) [Rabinak2010]. The rotigotine label carries a similar withdrawal-symptoms warning [AgonistLabels2026].
- DOMINION (n=3090, 46 centres): an impulse-control disorder (ICD) was present in 13.6% overall (gambling 5.0%, compulsive sexual behaviour 3.5%, buying 5.7%, binge eating 4.3%). ICDs affected 17.1% on a dopamine agonist versus 6.9% not on one (OR 2.72), with similar rates on pramipexole and ropinirole (17.7% vs 15.5%) [Weintraub2010]. DIGPD cohort (n=411; 306 ICD-free at baseline): 5-year cumulative ICD incidence was 46.1% overall, 51.5% in agonist ever-users and 12.4% in never-users. Lifetime average daily dose and treatment duration each showed a dose-effect relationship, and ICDs progressively resolved after agonist discontinuation [Corvol2018].
- Ergot agonists: moderate-to-severe valve regurgitation was found in 23.4% on pergolide and 28.6% on cabergoline, versus 0% on non-ergot agonists and 5.6% in controls [Zanettini2007]. NICE advises against ergot-derived agonists as first-line treatment (1.3.7) and favours non-ergot agonists in most adjunct cases because of the monitoring ergots require (1.3.11) [NICE2017].
- Subcutaneous apomorphine (Apokyn): starting dose 0.1-0.2 mL (1-2 mg) under medical supervision, maximum 0.6 mL (6 mg) per dose. Trimethobenzamide is recommended starting 3 days beforehand. 5-HT3 antagonists (e.g. ondansetron) are contraindicated because of profound hypotension and loss of consciousness [AgonistLabels2026].
- Sublingual apomorphine film (n=109): MDS-UPDRS III improved 11.1 versus 3.5 points at 30 minutes (difference -7.6). Oropharyngeal events occurred in 31% versus 7% and led 17% to discontinue; nausea affected 28%. One patient with cardiac risk factors had a fatal cardiac arrest [Olanow2020]. Kynmobi was approved 21 May 2020 and is listed as "Discontinued" in Drugs@FDA [DrugsFDA2026].
- Continuous subcutaneous apomorphine infusion (Onapgo, approved 3 Feb 2025): starts at 1 mg/h, up to 6 mg/h for up to 16 hours a day, maximum 98 mg/day. In a 12-week trial, off-time fell 2.55 h versus 0.90 h on placebo (difference -1.65 h; p=0.0114) [AgonistLabels2026; DrugsFDA2026].
- Tavapadon (Juvmo), a selective D1/D5 partial agonist, was approved by the FDA on 25 Sep 2026 (NDA 220415) for Parkinson's disease in adults [DrugsFDA2026; AgonistLabels2026]. The label gives a 5 mg once-daily maintenance dose, maximum 15 mg, and a half-life of about 25.5 h. ICDs were reported in 1% versus 0% (early-PD studies) and 2% versus 1% (adjunct study) [AgonistLabels2026]. TEMPO-1 (early PD, n=529): MDS-UPDRS II+III improved by 9.7 points on 5 mg and 10.2 points on 15 mg, versus worsening of 1.8 points on placebo (differences -11.5 and -12.1). Nausea affected 25.4% [Pahwa2026]. TEMPO-3 (adjunct, n=507): "good on" time rose 1.10 h more than placebo and off-time fell 0.94 h more; nausea was 14.3% and dyskinesia 10.0% [Fernandez2026].

### MAO-B inhibitors
- Selegiline oral: 10 mg/day given as 5 mg at breakfast and lunch, with no evidence of added benefit from higher doses (US labelled use is as adjunct to levodopa/carbidopa) [EnzymeInhibitorLabels2026]. First US approval was 5 June 1989 (NDA 019334) [DrugsFDA2026].
- As an adjunct to levodopa (LARGO, n=687), rasagiline 1 mg once daily cut daily off-time by 1.18 h versus 0.4 h on placebo, similar to entacapone 200 mg with each levodopa dose (1.2 h); on time without troublesome dyskinesia rose 0.85 h versus 0.03 h [Rascol2005].
- Rasagiline dosing: 1 mg once daily as monotherapy; 0.5 mg once daily as levodopa adjunct, rising to 1 mg if needed. The maximum is 0.5 mg with ciprofloxacin or other CYP1A2 inhibitors (which can double rasagiline levels) and in mild hepatic impairment; it should not be used in moderate or severe hepatic impairment. Tmax is about 1 hour and absolute bioavailability about 36% [EnzymeInhibitorLabels2026]. Rasagiline is contraindicated with meperidine, tramadol, methadone, propoxyphene and other MAO inhibitors, including other selective MAO-B inhibitors (risk of serotonin syndrome). At least 14 days must pass between stopping rasagiline and starting these. It is also contraindicated with St John's wort, cyclobenzaprine and dextromethorphan (psychosis or bizarre behaviour). Use with SSRIs, SNRIs, tricyclic, tetracyclic or triazolopyridine antidepressants "is not recommended" [EnzymeInhibitorLabels2026]. Rasagiline label: tyramine restriction is not required at recommended doses, but foods with very large amounts of tyramine (more than 150 mg) should be avoided. In one adjunct study, significant blood-pressure elevation occurred in 4% versus 3% on placebo [EnzymeInhibitorLabels2026].
- Rasagiline pharmacology: rat brain IC50 was 4.43 nM for MAO-B versus 412 nM for MAO-A. It was 3-15 times more potent than selegiline in vivo and, unlike selegiline, has no amphetamine-like metabolites [Youdim2001].
- Rasagiline is described by Teva as "a joint development of Teva and the Technion - Israel Institute of Technology". Israel's Ministry of Health licensed it on 3 Jan 2005 for early monotherapy and for adjunct use in moderate-to-advanced disease [Teva2005]. FDA approval followed on 16 May 2006 [DrugsFDA2026]. Current Israeli registry entries for this and the other drugs were not accessible (unverified).
- Safinamide (SETTLE, n=549): on time without troublesome dyskinesia rose 1.42 h versus 0.57 h on placebo (difference 0.96 h). Dyskinesia was the most frequent adverse event (14.6% vs 5.5%) [Schapira2017].
- Safinamide dosing: 50 mg once daily, increasable to 100 mg after two weeks; doses above 100 mg showed no added benefit. It is contraindicated with other MAO inhibitors (including linezolid), opioids such as meperidine, methadone, propoxyphene and tramadol, SNRIs, tricyclic, tetracyclic and triazolopyridine antidepressants, cyclobenzaprine, methylphenidate, amphetamines and St John's wort. FDA approval was 21 Mar 2017 [EnzymeInhibitorLabels2026; DrugsFDA2026].

### COMT inhibitors
- Entacapone: one 200 mg tablet with each carbidopa-levodopa dose, up to 8 times daily (1600 mg/day). It "has no antiparkinsonian effect of its own" [EnzymeInhibitorLabels2026]. Comtan was approved 19 Oct 1999 and Stalevo (carbidopa/levodopa/entacapone) 11 Jun 2003 [DrugsFDA2026].
- BIPARK-I (n=600): off-time changed by -116.8 min on opicapone 50 mg, -96.3 min on entacapone and -56.0 min on placebo. Opicapone 50 mg was superior to placebo (-60.8 min) and non-inferior to entacapone. Dyskinesia was the most common adverse event [Ferreira2016]. Opicapone dosing: 50 mg once daily at bedtime, with no food for 1 hour before and at least 1 hour after. Moderate hepatic impairment uses 25 mg; severe impairment should be avoided. FDA approval was 24 Apr 2020 [EnzymeInhibitorLabels2026; DrugsFDA2026].
- Tolcapone boxed warning: as of May 2005 there had been 3 fatal fulminant hepatic failures in more than 40,000 patient-years, possibly 10-100 times background. Patients without substantial benefit within 3 weeks should be withdrawn. ALT/AST are checked at baseline and every 2-4 weeks for the first 6 months, and the drug is stopped above 2 times the upper limit of normal. Dose is 100 mg three times daily (200 mg three times daily only if justified) [EnzymeInhibitorLabels2026]. Approved 29 Jan 1998; Tasmar brand now listed "Discontinued" [DrugsFDA2026].

### Amantadine, istradefylline, anticholinergics
- Amantadine is described as a weak, non-competitive NMDA antagonist (Ki 10 µM) with anticholinergic-like side effects. Half-life averages about 16-17 hours in healthy adults, rises 2-3-fold or more when CrCl is below 40 mL/min/1.73 m², and averages 8 days on haemodialysis. Abrupt discontinuation has caused parkinsonian crisis and NMS-like reactions [AmantadineLabels2026]. Amantadine ER (Gocovri) is labelled for dyskinesia and for OFF episodes. Dosing is 137 mg at bedtime for one week, then 274 mg. Renal dosing: CrCl 30-59 starts at 68.5 mg with a maximum of 137 mg; 15-29 is 68.5 mg; below 15 mL/min/1.73 m² is contraindicated. FDA approval was 24 Aug 2017 [AmantadineLabels2026; DrugsFDA2026].
- EASE LID (n=121 analysed): UDysRS improved 15.9 versus 8.0 points (difference -7.9), and off-time fell 0.6 h versus rising 0.3 h on placebo (difference -0.9 h). Adverse events included visual hallucinations (23.8% vs 1.7%), peripheral oedema (23.8% vs 0%) and dizziness (22.2% vs 0%), with 20.6% versus 6.9% discontinuing [Pahwa2017].
- Istradefylline (adenosine A2A antagonist; Nourianz, FDA approval 27 Aug 2019) was analysed across 8 pooled trials (n=2719). Off-time fell 0.38 h (20 mg) and 0.45 h (40 mg) versus placebo; in the 4 trials underpinning FDA approval, the reductions were 0.75 h and 0.82 h. Dyskinesia occurred in 16.1-17.7% versus 9.6% [Hauser2021; DrugsFDA2026].
- NICE: "Do not offer anticholinergics to people with Parkinson's disease who have developed dyskinesia and/or motor fluctuations" (1.3.14) [NICE2017].

### Treatment ladder as written in guidelines
- NICE NG71 first line: offer levodopa when motor symptoms affect quality of life (1.3.5). Consider a choice of dopamine agonist, levodopa or MAO-B inhibitor when they do not (1.3.6) [NICE2017]. NICE Table 1 contrasts the classes. Levodopa gives more improvement in motor symptoms and ADL and more motor complications. Agonists give less motor improvement, fewer complications and more excessive sleepiness, hallucinations and ICDs. MAO-B inhibitors give less motor improvement, fewer complications and fewer of those adverse events [NICE2017].
- NICE adjuncts for dyskinesia or fluctuations despite optimal levodopa: offer a choice of dopamine agonist, MAO-B inhibitor or COMT inhibitor (1.3.10). Consider amantadine if dyskinesia is not managed by modifying existing therapy (1.3.13). Ergots are only for those not controlled by a non-ergot agonist (1.3.12) [NICE2017]. NICE's adjunct evidence table: agonists give "more off-time reduction" with "more risk of hallucinations"; MAO-B inhibitors have "fewer adverse events"; COMT inhibitors have "more adverse events"; amantadine has no evidence of motor-symptom improvement [NICE2017].
- NICE requires oral and written information on ICD risk with all dopaminergic therapy, and increased risk with agonists, when treatment starts. History of impulsive behaviour, alcohol use and smoking are named risk factors (1.3.8, 1.4.2) [NICE2017].
- MDS 2018 EBM review, early PD monotherapy: non-ergot agonists, oral levodopa, selegiline and rasagiline are "clinically useful". For early/stable adjunct use: non-ergot agonists, rasagiline and zonisamide. No intervention is clinically useful to prevent or delay disease progression [Fox2018]. MDS 2018 review, motor fluctuations: most non-ergot agonists, pergolide, levodopa ER, levodopa intestinal infusion, entacapone, opicapone, rasagiline, zonisamide, safinamide and bilateral STN/GPi DBS are clinically useful. For dyskinesia: amantadine, clozapine and bilateral STN/GPi DBS. MDS does not rank an order within these lists [Fox2018].
- Disagreement: PD MED found small persistent mobility and quality-of-life benefits from starting with levodopa [PDMED2014]. Agonist-first trials found fewer motor complications but less motor benefit and more somnolence [PSG2000CALM; Rascol2000]. NICE frames the initial choice around whether symptoms affect quality of life rather than around age [NICE2017].
