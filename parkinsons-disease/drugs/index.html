Part 3 · Treatment

The other medicines

Besides levodopa, other medicines copy dopamine, slow its breakdown, or act on other signals in the movement circuit. Press play to climb the treatment ladder as NICE NG71 and the 2018 MDS evidence review describe it, and watch where each class acts at the synapse. This page reports guidelines, labels and trials. It is not advice for any person.

Click a rung to read it. Ladder and synapse are schematic, not to scale.

Rung 1 · Starting

Levodopa when symptoms affect quality of life

As first-line treatment, NICE NG71 (1.3.5) offers levodopa when motor symptoms affect quality of life. Its comparison table sets out the trade-off: levodopa gives more improvement in motor symptoms and daily activities, and more motor complications. In the PD MED trial (1,620 people), starting with levodopa gave slightly better mobility on the PDQ-39 than starting with a levodopa-sparing drug: 1.8 points, against a minimally important difference of 6. How levodopa itself works is in Levodopa and carbidopa. 242,282

In plain wordsWhen symptoms are getting in the way of daily life, NICE starts with levodopa, the medicine with the biggest effect on movement.
Exam hookNICE 1.3.5: motor symptoms affect quality of life → offer levodopa. NICE 1.3.6: they do not → consider a choice of agonist, levodopa or MAO-B inhibitor. 242

Off-time

Hours of off-time saved per day

Off-time is the part of the waking day when medicines are not working well, recorded in home diaries. Placebo also cut off-time, by up to 0.93 hours, so the difference from placebo is what counts. Rasagiline about 0.8 hours (arithmetic from 1.18 and 0.4), opicapone 60.8 minutes, rotigotine 8 mg 1.8 hours, amantadine ER 0.9 hours, istradefylline 0.38 to 0.45 hours in pooled trials (0.75 to 0.82 in the 4 trials behind US approval), tavapadon 0.94 hours, apomorphine infusion 1.65 hours. The foslevodopa trial compared the pump with oral levodopa, not with placebo (difference 1.79 hours). 7,108,109,139,190,270,306

In plain wordsMost add-ons gave back between about half an hour and two hours a day of better movement than a dummy pill.
Exam hookRead the difference from placebo, not the medicine bar alone.

Dopamine precursorhallucinationssleepiness

Levodopa (for comparison)

How it acts. Crosses into the brain and is converted to dopamine; carbidopa blocks that conversion outside the brain. Full story in Levodopa and carbidopa. 190

What trials found. The largest motor benefit of any class, with more motor complications (NICE Table 1). 242

Key harms. Sudden sleep onset without warning, including while driving, has been reported as late as a year after starting. A higher baseline levodopa-equivalent dose was an independent risk factor for new psychosis in a 12-year cohort. 111,190

First US approval: Larodopa 4 Jun 1970 · Sinemet 2 May 1975

Dopamine agonists, oralimpulse controlhallucinationssleepiness

Pramipexole and ropinirole

How it acts. Non-ergot dopamine agonists: they stimulate dopamine receptors directly, without needing conversion. 7

What trials found. CALM-PD: motor complications 28% against 51% on levodopa at 23.5 months, with less UPDRS improvement (4.5 vs 9.2 points). In the 056 study, 34% of ropinirole completers needed no levodopa at 5 years. 296,305

Key harms. Impulse-control disorders 17.7% on pramipexole and 15.5% on ropinirole (DOMINION). Somnolence 32.4% against 17.3% on levodopa (CALM-PD). The first case series of falling asleep at the wheel described 8 people on pramipexole and 1 on ropinirole, 5 with no warning. Withdrawal syndrome in 19% of tapers. NICE: more hallucinations than the other classes. 116,242,296,302,372

First US approval: Mirapex 1 Jul 1997 · Requip 19 Sep 1997

Dopamine agonist, skin patchimpulse controlhallucinationssleepiness

Rotigotine

How it acts. A non-ergot agonist delivered through the skin over 24 hours; the site is rotated daily and not reused within 14 days. 7

What trials found. Trial PD-4 (351 people with advanced Parkinson's): off-time fell 2.7 hours on 8 mg/24 h against 0.9 on placebo. 7

Key harms. As an agonist it falls under NICE's class statement of more sleepiness, hallucinations and impulse-control disorders. Large doses can cause confusion, hallucinations or delirium. The label carries a withdrawal-symptoms warning. 7,242,300

First US approval: Neupro 9 May 2007

Dopamine agonist: injection, film or pumporthostatic hypotension

Apomorphine

How it acts. A dopamine agonist given under the skin, as a single injection or a continuous infusion, or under the tongue as a film. 7,87

What trials found. Sublingual film: MDS-UPDRS III improved 11.1 against 3.5 points at 30 minutes. Continuous infusion: off-time down 2.55 against 0.90 hours. 7,256

Key harms. 5-HT3 antagonists such as ondansetron are contraindicated: profound hypotension and loss of consciousness. With the film, mouth and throat effects affected 31% against 7% and led 17% to stop; nausea 28%; one person with cardiac risk factors had a fatal cardiac arrest. 7,256

First US approval: Injection 20 Apr 2004 · film 21 May 2020 (discontinued) · pump 3 Feb 2025

D1/D5 partial agonistimpulse control

Tavapadon (Juvmo)

How it acts. A selective partial agonist at D1 and D5 dopamine receptors, taken once a day; half-life about 25.5 hours. 7

What trials found. TEMPO-1 (early Parkinson's, 529 people): MDS-UPDRS II+III 11.5 and 12.1 points better than placebo. TEMPO-3 (adjunct, 507 people): good on time 1.10 hours more, off-time 0.94 hours less. 108,271

Key harms. Impulse-control disorders 1% against 0% in the early studies and 2% against 1% in the adjunct study. Nausea 25.4% (TEMPO-1) and 14.3% (TEMPO-3); dyskinesia 10.0% in TEMPO-3. 7,108,271

First US approval: 25 Sep 2026 (NDA 220415)

Ergot-derived agonists

Pergolide and cabergoline

How it acts. Older dopamine agonists derived from ergot. 384

What trials found. The MDS review still lists pergolide as clinically useful for fluctuations. NICE keeps ergots for people not controlled by a non-ergot agonist, because of the monitoring they require. 112,242

Key harms. Moderate-to-severe heart-valve regurgitation in 23.4% on pergolide and 28.6% on cabergoline, against 0% on non-ergot agonists and 5.6% in controls. 384

First US approval: NICE: not first line

MAO-B inhibitororthostatic hypotension

Selegiline

How it acts. Blocks monoamine oxidase B, one of the enzymes that break down dopamine. 10 mg a day as 5 mg at breakfast and lunch; higher doses add no benefit. 94

What trials found. DATATOP: delayed the need for levodopa (HR 0.50, a median of about 9 months), but a symptomatic effect could not be excluded. 294

Key harms. Taken with carbidopa-levodopa it may cause severe orthostatic hypotension. 190

First US approval: 5 Jun 1989 (NDA 019334)

MAO-B inhibitor

Rasagiline

How it acts. Selective for MAO-B (rat brain IC50 4.43 nM against 412 nM for MAO-A), 3 to 15 times more potent than selegiline in vivo, with no amphetamine-like metabolites. 383

What trials found. LARGO: off-time down 1.18 against 0.4 hours. ADAGIO: 1 mg met its delayed-start endpoints, 2 mg did not, and no progression claim was accepted. 226,254,306

Key harms. Significant blood-pressure rise in 4% against 3% in one adjunct study. Serious interactions with pethidine, tramadol and other drugs (see the table below). 94

Origin. Origin, as each institution tells it: Teva calls rasagiline "a joint development of Teva and the Technion"; the Technion credits its professors Moussa Youdim and John Finberg with developing it in the 1970s. Both are institutional accounts. 352,353

First US approval: 16 May 2006 · Israel 3 Jan 2005

MAO-B inhibitor

Safinamide

How it acts. An MAO-B inhibitor used as a levodopa add-on: 50 mg once daily, increasable to 100 mg after two weeks; higher doses added nothing. 94

What trials found. SETTLE (549 people): good on time up 1.42 against 0.57 hours (difference 0.96). 317

Key harms. Dyskinesia was the most frequent adverse event (14.6% vs 5.5%). A long list of contraindicated drugs, including linezolid and several opioids (see the table below). 94,317

First US approval: 21 Mar 2017

COMT inhibitor

Entacapone

How it acts. Blocks COMT, which takes over levodopa breakdown when carbidopa blocks decarboxylation. Levodopa AUC rises about 35% and its half-life lengthens from 1.3 to 2.4 hours. 94,165

What trials found. LARGO: off-time down 1.2 hours. BIPARK-I: −96.3 minutes, against −56.0 on placebo. 109,306

Key harms. When levodopa was started with entacapone in STRIDE-PD, dyskinesia came sooner (HR 1.29) and was more frequent at week 134 (42% vs 32%), on higher levodopa-equivalent doses. 346

First US approval: Comtan 19 Oct 1999 · Stalevo 11 Jun 2003

COMT inhibitor

Opicapone

How it acts. Once-daily COMT inhibitor: up to 84% inhibition of red-cell COMT, more than 35% still present 5 days after the last dose. 94

What trials found. BIPARK-I (600 people): off-time −116.8 minutes against −56.0 on placebo; non-inferior to entacapone. 109

Key harms. Dyskinesia was the most common adverse event. 109

First US approval: 24 Apr 2020

COMT inhibitor

Tolcapone

How it acts. A COMT inhibitor taken as 100 mg three times daily (200 mg three times daily only if justified). 94

What trials found. The label says people without substantial benefit within 3 weeks should be withdrawn. 94

Key harms. Boxed warning: 3 fatal fulminant liver failures in more than 40,000 patient-years by May 2005, possibly 10 to 100 times background. Liver tests every 2 to 4 weeks for the first 6 months. 94

First US approval: 29 Jan 1998 (Tasmar brand discontinued)

NMDA antagonisthallucinations

Amantadine

How it acts. A weak, non-competitive NMDA antagonist (Ki 10 µM) with anticholinergic-like side effects. Gocovri is labelled for dyskinesia and for off episodes. 13

What trials found. EASE LID: dyskinesia rating down 15.9 against 8.0 points; off-time down 0.9 hours more than placebo. 270

Key harms. Visual hallucinations 23.8% vs 1.7%, peripheral oedema 23.8% vs 0%, dizziness 22.2% vs 0%; 20.6% vs 6.9% stopped. Abrupt discontinuation has caused parkinsonian crisis. Doses fall with kidney function. 13,270

First US approval: Gocovri (ER) 24 Aug 2017

Adenosine A2A antagonist

Istradefylline

How it acts. Blocks the adenosine A2A receptor on striatal neurons, outside the dopamine system. 139

What trials found. 8 pooled trials (2,719 people): off-time 0.38 h (20 mg) and 0.45 h (40 mg) less than placebo; 0.75 and 0.82 h in the 4 trials behind US approval. 139

Key harms. Dyskinesia in 16.1 to 17.7% against 9.6% on placebo. 139

First US approval: Nourianz 27 Aug 2019

Anticholinergicorthostatic hypotensionhallucinations

Anticholinergics

How it acts. Medicines that block acetylcholine; NICE treats them as a class. 242

What trials found. NICE: "Do not offer anticholinergics to people with Parkinson's disease who have developed dyskinesia and/or motor fluctuations." 242

Key harms. NICE lists them among medicines to review when orthostatic hypotension appears. The Parkinson's Foundation warns that bladder-control drugs, among others, may cause confusion, hallucinations and falls. 242,285

First US approval: NICE 1.3.14: not once fluctuations start

The trials behind the first choice

Five trials shaped how the first medicine is chosen and what can be claimed for it. Pick one to see its result.

PD MED, open-label pragmatic trial. Bars start at zero; the dashed bar is the minimally important difference for comparison.

PD MED · 2014

Levodopa first, by a small margin

PD MED randomised 1,620 people with early Parkinson's to start with levodopa or with a levodopa-sparing drug (an agonist or an MAO-B inhibitor), open-label, in routine practice. Mobility on the PDQ-39 was 1.8 points better with levodopa first (p=0.005), against a minimally important difference of 6 points, and 1.4 points better with MAO-B inhibitors than with agonists. Stopping a drug because of side effects: 28% on agonists, 23% on MAO-B inhibitors, 2% on levodopa. 282

In plain wordsStarting with levodopa gave a small, lasting edge in everyday mobility, and far fewer people stopped it because of side effects.
Exam hookPD MED: stopped for side effects 28% agonist, 23% MAO-B, 2% levodopa. 282

Kidneys, liver and interactions: label information

What the US prescribing information says, reported as published for study. It is not dosing advice for any person; doses are set with a prescriber.

Never change or stop a Parkinson's medicine without a clinician. NICE says antiparkinsonian medicines "should not be withdrawn abruptly or allowed to fail suddenly due to poor absorption", and advises against drug holidays, because of the risk of acute akinesia or neuroleptic malignant syndrome. Labels describe a neuroleptic malignant syndrome-like reaction after levodopa is cut or stopped, and parkinsonian crisis after amantadine is stopped abruptly; tapering a dopamine agonist produced a withdrawal syndrome in 19% of tapers in one series. 13,190,242,302

Reading the evidence

Established

Ask about impulse control at every visit

DOMINION found an impulse-control disorder in 13.6% of 3,090 people overall: buying 5.7%, gambling 5.0%, binge eating 4.3% and compulsive sexual behaviour 3.5%. NICE asks for oral and written information when dopaminergic treatment starts, including that affected people may hide it, and for the subject to be raised at reviews. If a problem develops, NICE says to seek specialist advice and reduce any agonist gradually first, watching for withdrawal. 242,372

In plain wordsNew urges to gamble, shop, eat or for sex can come from the medicine. They are common, often hidden, and can improve when the medicine is adjusted with the specialist.
Established

No medicine has been shown to slow Parkinson's

The 2018 MDS review found no intervention clinically useful to prevent or delay disease progression. DATATOP's delay could have been symptom relief; ADAGIO's two doses disagreed; and in LEAP, levodopa started 40 weeks earlier made no difference at 80 weeks (UPDRS difference 1.0, p=0.44). 112,254,294,362

Debated

Which medicine first?

Agonist-first trials (CALM-PD, 056) found fewer motor complications but less motor benefit and more sleepiness. PD MED, a larger pragmatic trial, found small persistent mobility and quality-of-life benefits from starting with levodopa, and far fewer people stopping it. NICE frames the choice around whether symptoms affect quality of life rather than around age, and MDS does not rank the options. 112,242,282,296,305

Established

Sleep attacks and driving

The first case series described 9 people who fell asleep at the wheel on pramipexole or ropinirole and had accidents, 5 of them with no warning. The labels now carry the warning, as late as a year after starting, and levodopa's label does too. NICE: people with daytime sleepiness or sudden onset of sleep should not drive. 7,116,190,242

Established

Why ergot agonists fell out of use

Moderate-to-severe heart-valve regurgitation in 23.4% on pergolide and 28.6% on cabergoline, against 0% on non-ergot agonists. NICE advises against ergot-derived agonists first line and keeps them for people not controlled by a non-ergot agonist, because of the monitoring they need. 242,384

New in 2026

Tavapadon (Juvmo)

Approved by the FDA on 25 Sep 2026 for Parkinson's disease in adults. It is a selective D1/D5 partial agonist; the label's maintenance dose is 5 mg once daily, maximum 15 mg. Its impulse-control rates in trials were low (1% vs 0% early, 2% vs 1% adjunct). With the older agonists, DIGPD found that risk rose with lifetime dose and treatment duration. 7,69,87

Rasagiline and Israel

Israel's Ministry of Health licensed rasagiline on 3 Jan 2005 for early monotherapy and for adjunct use in moderate-to-advanced disease, according to Teva's press release; the FDA approved it on 16 May 2006. Teva calls it a joint development with the Technion, and the Technion credits Youdim and Finberg's work in the 1970s. Current Israeli registry entries could not be checked for this guide. 87,352,353
