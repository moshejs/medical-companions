Glossary

136 of 136 terms

3-O-methyldopa (3-OMD)The product of levodopa methylation by COMT, which becomes the main route once decarboxylation is blocked. Its half-life is more than 10 hours, it accumulates, and it may compete with levodopa for transport into the brain.
Absolute exclusion criteriaThe 9 items in the MDS 2015 criteria that rule Parkinson's disease out, such as downward vertical supranuclear gaze palsy or normal presynaptic dopaminergic imaging. They were designed to occur in fewer than 3% of true Parkinson's disease.
Adaptive DBS (aDBS)Closed-loop deep-brain stimulation that adjusts itself to a recorded brain signal, such as alpha-beta local field power. The first commercial system received FDA approval in February 2025.
Alpha-synucleinA 140-amino-acid presynaptic protein encoded by SNCA that helps assemble SNARE complexes. Misfolded and aggregated, it is the main component of Lewy bodies and Lewy neurites.
AmantadineA weak NMDA antagonist used for dyskinesia; the extended-release form is also labelled for OFF episodes. Its half-life rises in kidney impairment, and abrupt discontinuation has caused parkinsonian crisis.
AmbroxolA drug tested on the GBA1/glucocerebrosidase pathway. An open-label study without a control group raised CSF GCase protein; a placebo-controlled phase 3 (ASPro-PD) is recruiting.
ApathyLoss of motivation, found in 39.8% of people with Parkinson's in a meta-analysis. About half of people with apathy had neither depression nor cognitive impairment.
ApomorphineA dopamine agonist given by injection, by continuous subcutaneous infusion or (formerly) as a sublingual film. Its label contraindicates ondansetron and other 5HT3 antagonists because of profound hypotension and loss of consciousness.
Aspiration pneumoniaPneumonia from food, fluid or saliva entering the airway. In a Korean national cohort it occurred about four times as often in people with Parkinson's as in controls (HR 4.21).
Basal gangliaDeep brain nuclei (striatum, GPe, GPi, STN and substantia nigra) that form loops with the cortex and thalamus. The motor loop runs through the post-commissural putamen.
BemdaneprocelDopaminergic progenitor cells grown from human embryonic stem cells. A 12-person phase 1 had no product-related adverse events; a sham-controlled phase 3 (exPDite-2) is recruiting.
BenserazideA peripheral dopa-decarboxylase inhibitor used with levodopa outside the US, as an alternative to carbidopa. It has no US application.
Beta oscillationsSynchronised brain activity in the 13-30 Hz band. Exaggerated beta has been associated with bradykinesia and is reduced by levodopa and by STN stimulation.
Body-first and brain-firstA hypothesis that Parkinson's disease can start in the gut or autonomic nerves and climb the vagus (body-first, often with prodromal RBD) or start in the brain, often on one side (brain-first).
Braak stagingA six-stage model (2003) of Lewy pathology spreading from the lower brainstem and olfactory nucleus through the midbrain to the cortex. Many brains do not follow it, and stage does not track clinical severity.
BradykinesiaIn the MDS definition, slowness of movement AND decrement in amplitude or speed, or progressive hesitations or halts, as movements are continued. It is required for parkinsonism.
CarbidopaA peripheral dopa-decarboxylase inhibitor that does not cross the blood-brain barrier. It cuts the levodopa needed by about 75% and is saturating at about 70-100 mg a day.
Cardiac MIBG scintigraphyImaging of the heart's sympathetic nerves. Cardiac sympathetic denervation on MIBG is one of the four MDS supportive criteria.
Caregiver burdenThe strain of caring. In a UK survey over 40% of caregivers said their health had suffered; burden rose with the person's disability, depression, hallucinations, confusion and falls.
Cell replacementGrafting dopamine-producing cells into the putamen. It is not expected to stop the disease process; older sham-controlled fetal-tissue trials missed their primary endpoints.
Clinically established PDThe stricter MDS 2015 level: no absolute exclusions, at least two supportive criteria and no red flags, designed so that at least 90% who meet it have Parkinson's disease.
Clinically probable PDThe MDS 2015 level allowing up to two red flags, each counterbalanced by a supportive criterion, with no absolute exclusions. It reached 94.5% sensitivity in validation.
ClozapineAn antipsychotic rated efficacious and clinically useful for Parkinson's disease psychosis by the MDS 2019 review, needing specialised monitoring. Low doses (mean 24.7 mg a day) were used in the trial.
COMT inhibitorA drug that blocks catechol-O-methyltransferase, slowing levodopa breakdown and lengthening its effect: entacapone, opicapone and tolcapone.
Corticobasal degeneration (CBD)A disease that can present as corticobasal syndrome (the commonest final diagnosis, 37.1%), a frontal behavioural-spatial syndrome, nonfluent aphasia or a PSP syndrome. A sustained levodopa response is rare.
DaT-SPECT (DaTscan)Imaging of the striatal dopamine transporter with 123I-ioflupane. It shows dopaminergic loss but was not designed to distinguish PD, MSA and PSP from one another.
DBS withdrawal syndromeRapid, possibly life-threatening worsening when deep-brain stimulation stops, for example when a battery runs out. It may not respond to high-dose levodopa.
Deep-brain stimulation (DBS)Implanted electrodes, usually in the STN or GPi, connected to a pulse generator that delivers high-frequency pulses. With the exception of tremor, it treats mainly levodopa-responsive symptoms.
DeliriumAn acute disturbance of attention and awareness. In one prospective study it was diagnosed in 66.9% of hospitalised people with Parkinson's, against 38.7% of older controls.
Dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB)A Lewy body dementia in which dementia starts before or within about a year of parkinsonism (the 1-year rule). Core features are fluctuations, visual hallucinations, RBD and parkinsonism.
Diphasic dyskinesiaInvoluntary movements as levodopa levels rise and fall at the start and end of a dose (dystonia-improvement-dystonia), usually in 15-20% of people and mostly in the legs.
Direct pathwayThe striatum-to-GPi/SNr projection of D1-receptor medium spiny neurons. Activating it releases the thalamus from inhibition and favours movement.
Directional leadA DBS lead with segmented contacts around the shaft so current can be steered. In a crossover trial it widened the therapeutic window in 90.6% of patients.
DJ-1 (PARK7)A gene whose recessive mutations cause early-onset parkinsonism. Its protein is implicated in the oxidative-stress response.
Dopa decarboxylaseThe enzyme that turns levodopa into dopamine. Outside the brain it is blocked by carbidopa or benserazide.
Dopamine agonistA drug that stimulates dopamine receptors directly, such as pramipexole, ropinirole, rotigotine and apomorphine. Agonists carry a higher risk of impulse-control disorders and sleepiness.
Dopamine agonist withdrawal syndrome (DAWS)Anxiety, panic, depression, sweating, pain and drug craving on tapering an agonist, refractory to levodopa. It occurred in 19% of agonist tapers in one series.
Dorsal motor nucleus of the vagusA brainstem nucleus that is one of the first sites of Lewy pathology in Braak stage 1. It was spared in 7% of brains in one critical series.
Drug-induced parkinsonismParkinsonism caused by dopamine blockers or depleters such as antipsychotics and metoclopramide. It is the second commonest cause of parkinsonism in older people.
DyskinesiaInvoluntary, often choreic movements that develop with dopaminergic treatment. They can occur at peak dose or in a diphasic pattern.
DysphagiaDifficulty swallowing. On objective testing 82% of people with Parkinson's have it, but only 35% report it when asked.
Early-onset Parkinson's disease (EOPD)The MDS term (2022) for onset after age 21 and before 50, preferred to 'young-onset'. Onset before 21 is called juvenile parkinsonism.
ELLDOPAA placebo-controlled trial of three levodopa doses for 40 weeks plus a 2-week washout. Placebo had worsened most after washout, while dopamine-transporter imaging declined more on levodopa.
EntacaponeA COMT inhibitor taken as 200 mg with each levodopa dose. It has no antiparkinsonian effect of its own and lengthens levodopa's half-life from 1.3 to 2.4 hours.
Essential tremorA bilateral upper-limb action tremor of at least 3 years, without parkinsonism, dystonia or ataxia. A rest tremor added to it is called 'ET plus'.
ExenatideA GLP-1 receptor agonist. A phase 2 trial was positive on motor scores, but the phase 3 Exenatide-PD3 was negative, and The Lancet placed an Expression of Concern on that paper in 2026.
Focused ultrasound (MRgFUS)MRI-guided ultrasound that makes a small lesion without opening the skull, in the Vim thalamus, GPi or STN. In the pivotal trials it was done on one side only.
Foslevodopa-foscarbidopaProdrugs of levodopa and carbidopa given by 24-hour subcutaneous infusion (Vyalev, FDA 2024). Infusion-site reactions are common.
Freezing of gaitBrief episodes in which the feet seem stuck. In a survey of 6,620 people, 47% reported regular freezing.
G2019SThe commonest pathogenic LRRK2 variant. It increases kinase activity and is especially frequent in North African Arab and Ashkenazi Jewish people with Parkinson's.
Gaucher diseaseA disease caused by two disease-causing GBA1 variants. Carrying one variant raises the risk of Parkinson's disease.
GBA1The gene for the lysosomal enzyme glucocerebrosidase (GCase). Heterozygous variants raise Parkinson's risk with reduced penetrance; it is the commonest genetic finding in panel testing.
Globus pallidus internus (GPi)An output nucleus of the basal ganglia that inhibits the thalamus. It is a DBS and focused-ultrasound target.
GLP-1 receptor agonistsDiabetes drugs, including exenatide and lixisenatide, tested for slowing Parkinson's disease. Trial results are mixed, and it is unresolved whether positive effects were symptomatic.
Hoehn and Yahr scaleA five-stage scale (1967) from unilateral involvement (1) to wheelchair or bed unless aided (5). It is weighted toward postural instability and says nothing about non-motor problems.
Hummingbird signMidbrain atrophy on MRI, with 99.5% specificity but only 51.6% sensitivity for progressive supranuclear palsy.
HyposmiaA reduced sense of smell. On age-adjusted testing 74.5% of people with Parkinson's had smell loss, and it is predicted to start more than 20 years before diagnosis.
Impulse-control disorder (ICD)Compulsive gambling, sexual behaviour, buying or eating. In DOMINION it affected 17.1% of people on a dopamine agonist versus 6.9% not on one.
Incidental Lewy body diseaseLewy pathology found at autopsy in people without clinical Parkinson's disease. Braak stages 1-2 are thought to be presymptomatic.
Indirect pathwayThe route from D2-receptor medium spiny neurons through GPe and the STN to GPi/SNr. Activating it increases inhibition of the thalamus.
Juvenile parkinsonismParkinsonism with onset before age 21. Parkin mutations explained 77% of isolated cases with onset at 20 or younger in one series.
LevodopaThe metabolic precursor of dopamine. Unlike dopamine it crosses the blood-brain barrier, using the carrier for large neutral amino acids, and is converted to dopamine in the brain.
Levodopa-carbidopa intestinal gel (LCIG)Levodopa gel infused into the jejunum through a PEG-J tube for about 16 hours (Duopa, FDA 2015). Most adverse events relate to the tube.
Lewy bodyA neuronal inclusion containing alpha-synuclein, first described by Fritz Heinrich Lewy in 1912. Electron microscopy shows crowded membranes, vesicles and organelles.
Lewy neuriteAlpha-synuclein-positive pathology in neuronal processes. With Lewy bodies, it is required for the neuropathological diagnosis of sporadic Parkinson's disease.
Locus coeruleusA noradrenergic brainstem nucleus involved in Braak stage 2. In one study it lost more neurons than the substantia nigra.
Long-duration responseThe sustained benefit from levodopa early in treatment, with little change from dose to dose. In one study it decayed faster over the first 4 years of treatment.
LRRK2Leucine-rich repeat kinase 2, the gene behind PARK8. Pathogenic variants increase kinase activity; penetrance is incomplete, and some carriers have no Lewy bodies.
LSVT BIGA high-amplitude movement training programme of 16 one-to-one hours over 4 weeks. It improved UPDRS motor scores more than Nordic walking or home exercise in one trial.
LSVT LOUDAn intensive voice treatment for dysarthria. In PD COMM it improved the Voice Handicap Index more than no therapy or standard NHS speech therapy.
MAO-B inhibitorA drug that blocks monoamine oxidase B, slowing dopamine breakdown: selegiline, rasagiline and safinamide.
MDS clinical diagnostic criteriaThe 2015 Movement Disorder Society criteria: parkinsonism, then 9 absolute exclusions, then 10 red flags counterbalanced by 4 supportive criteria. Built for research, they may guide clinical practice.
MDS-UPDRSThe Movement Disorder Society revision of the Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale: 65 items in four parts, each item rated 0 to 4.
Mild cognitive impairment (PD-MCI)Cognitive decline in Parkinson's disease shown on testing but without significant loss of functional independence. Mean prevalence in non-demented people with PD was 27%.
MoCAThe Montreal Cognitive Assessment. In Parkinson's disease, a score under 26/30 detected PD-MCI with 90% sensitivity, and under 21/30 detected dementia with 81% sensitivity.
Motor fluctuationsSwings between ON and OFF periods on treatment, including wearing-off, delayed-on and sudden OFFs. About 40% have them after 4-6 years of levodopa.
MPTPA by-product of illicit pethidine (meperidine)-analogue synthesis that caused parkinsonism in people who injected it (1983). It selectively damages nigral cells and became an animal model.
Multiple system atrophy (MSA)An alpha-synuclein disease with autonomic failure plus poorly levodopa-responsive parkinsonism and/or cerebellar ataxia. Stridor is distinctive.
Neuroleptic malignant-like syndromeRigidity, fever, altered consciousness and autonomic instability after Parkinson's medicines are reduced or withdrawn, also called parkinsonism-hyperpyrexia syndrome. Reported mortality is up to 4%.
Nigrosome-1A region of the substantia nigra seen on iron-sensitive MRI as the 'swallow tail'. Its loss separated Parkinson's disease from healthy adults with 94% sensitivity and 90% specificity.
Nigrostriatal pathwayThe projection of dopamine neurons from the substantia nigra pars compacta to the striatum, which degenerates in Parkinson's disease, with the putamen most affected.
Non-motor symptomsProblems beyond movement, such as fatigue, anxiety, depression, sleep disorders, constipation, urinary urgency and pain. 98.6% of people in PRIAMO reported at least one.
Normal-pressure hydrocephalusEnlarged ventricles with gait disturbance, cognitive impairment and urinary problems. Unlike in Parkinson's disease, external cues do little for the gait.
NSD-ISSThe neuronal alpha-synuclein disease integrated staging system (2024), defining disease by synuclein (S) and dopaminergic dysfunction (D) regardless of symptoms. Its authors call clinical use premature.
ON and OFFON is the improvement after each dose; OFF is the return of symptoms or lack of levodopa effect.
OpicaponeA once-daily COMT inhibitor taken at bedtime. In BIPARK-I it reduced off-time more than placebo and was non-inferior to entacapone.
Orthostatic hypotensionA sustained fall of at least 20 mmHg systolic or 10 mmHg diastolic within 3 minutes of standing. Pooled prevalence in Parkinson's disease was 30.1%.
Palliative careCare focused on quality of life and symptoms. Consensus statements support early integration in progressive neurological disease, and NICE suggests considering referral at any stage.
Paralysis agitansAn old name for the shaking palsy. Charcot rejected it, noting that people are not markedly weak, and named the disease after Parkinson.
Parkinson's disease nurse specialistA nurse with specialist Parkinson's training. NICE says regular monitoring, medicine adjustment and a point of contact may be provided by one; trial evidence on outcomes is mixed.
ParkinsonismA syndrome defined by the MDS as bradykinesia plus rest tremor and/or rigidity. Parkinson's disease is its commonest cause, but drugs, vascular disease and other neurodegenerative diseases can cause it.
PDQ-39A 39-item quality-of-life questionnaire with eight scales: mobility, daily activities, emotional well-being, stigma, social support, cognition, communication and bodily discomfort. Higher scores mean worse function.
Peak-dose dyskinesiaInvoluntary movements that coincide with the peak of levodopa action; chorea is the commonest form.
PenetranceThe share of carriers of a variant who develop the disease by a given age. It is incomplete for LRRK2 (about 25-80% by 79-80) and low to moderate for GBA1 (about 8-30% by 80).
PIGDThe postural instability and gait difficulty motor subtype. In DATATOP it carried more impairment than the tremor-dominant subtype.
PimavanserinA drug approved by the FDA in 2016 for hallucinations and delusions in Parkinson's disease psychosis. It carries a boxed warning about increased mortality in elderly people with dementia-related psychosis.
PINK1A mitochondrial kinase gene whose recessive mutations cause early-onset Parkinson's disease. PINK1 builds up on damaged mitochondria and recruits Parkin to remove them.
Polygenic risk scoreA score combining many common risk variants. People in the highest quartile had an odds ratio of 3.74 for Parkinson's disease versus the lowest quartile in one cohort.
PrasinezumabAn antibody against aggregated alpha-synuclein. Two phase 2 trials (PASADENA, PADOVA) missed their primary endpoints with exploratory motor signals, and a phase 3 is recruiting.
PRKN (parkin)The gene behind autosomal recessive juvenile parkinsonism (1998). Parkin carriers often have dystonia at onset, respond well to levodopa and may lack Lewy bodies.
Prodromal Parkinson's diseaseThe stage when early signs of neurodegeneration are present but motor Parkinson's disease cannot yet be diagnosed. MDS research criteria estimate it with likelihood ratios.
Progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP)A parkinsonian disorder with vertical supranuclear gaze palsy or slow vertical saccades and early unprovoked falls. About a quarter may appear levodopa-responsive.
PutamenThe part of the striatum that carries the motor loop. Post-mortem putamen dopamine was down 98%, and dorsal putamen dopamine markers were virtually lost from 4 years after diagnosis.
QUIPThe Questionnaire for Impulsive-Compulsive Disorders in Parkinson's Disease, screening for ICDs, other compulsive behaviours and compulsive medication use. A positive screen leads to a full interview.
RasagilineAn MAO-B inhibitor developed at the Technion in Israel and by Teva (Azilect). It is contraindicated with pethidine, tramadol, methadone and some other drugs.
Red flagsThe 10 MDS 2015 features of lower specificity that count against Parkinson's disease, such as early severe autonomic failure, inspiratory stridor or rapid gait decline. They must be counterbalanced by supportive criteria.
REM sleep behaviour disorder (RBD)A parasomnia of REM sleep, with a pooled prevalence of 46% in Parkinson's disease. Confirmed by polysomnography it carries a likelihood ratio of 130 for prodromal Parkinson's disease.
Rest tremorIn the MDS definition, a 4-6 Hz tremor in the fully resting limb that is suppressed when movement starts. Kinetic and postural tremor do not qualify.
Reverse causationWhen an early, unrecognised stage of a disease changes behaviour, making the behaviour look protective. A Danish study read 'ease of quitting' smoking as an early sign of Parkinson's disease.
RigidityIn the MDS definition, 'lead-pipe' velocity-independent resistance to slow passive movement. Isolated cogwheeling without lead-pipe rigidity does not qualify.
RivastigmineA cholinesterase inhibitor rated efficacious and clinically useful for Parkinson's disease dementia in the MDS 2019 review, and indicated for it in the US label.
RotenoneA pesticide that inhibits mitochondrial complex I. It reproduced nigrostriatal degeneration in rats and was linked to Parkinson's disease with an odds ratio of 2.5.
RotigotineA dopamine agonist given as a 24-hour skin patch, with sites rotated daily. Parkinson's UK guidance uses it as a substitute when people cannot take oral medicines.
Seed amplification assay (SAA)A laboratory test that amplifies misfolded alpha-synuclein from a sample such as CSF. It is endorsed for trial enrolment, not approved as a diagnostic test.
SelegilineAn MAO-B inhibitor. In DATATOP it delayed the need for levodopa, but a symptomatic effect could not be excluded.
Ser129 phosphorylationPhosphorylation of alpha-synuclein at serine 129: under 4% in healthy brain but over 90% in Lewy bodies.
Shaking palsyJames Parkinson's name for the disease in his 1817 essay, defined as involuntary tremulous motion with lessened muscular power, a stooped trunk and a tendency to pass from walking to running, 'the senses and intellects being uninjured'.
Short-duration responseThe rise and fall of benefit around each levodopa dose, which becomes more important on average after 2-5 years of treatment.
SNCAThe alpha-synuclein gene on chromosome 4. Point mutations such as A53T and duplications or triplications cause dominant Parkinson's disease.
Standardised mortality ratio (SMR)Observed deaths divided by those expected in the general population. In Parkinson's disease it ranged from 1.29 at 10 years to 3.1 at 15-20 years, rising with follow-up.
Substantia nigra pars compactaThe midbrain region whose pigmented dopamine neurons project to the striatum. The lateral ventral tier is the most affected in Parkinson's disease.
Subthalamic nucleus (STN)An excitatory nucleus in the indirect pathway that becomes overactive when dopamine is lost. It is a main DBS target.
Supportive criteriaThe 4 MDS 2015 features that support Parkinson's disease: dramatic dopaminergic response, levodopa-induced dyskinesia, documented rest tremor, and olfactory loss or MIBG denervation.
SWEDDScans without evidence of dopaminergic deficit: people diagnosed with Parkinson's disease clinically but with normal DaT imaging. Under the MDS criteria a normal scan excludes Parkinson's disease.
SynNeurGeA 2024 research classification based on alpha-synuclein (S), neurodegeneration (N) and genes (G), linked to clinical features. It can classify synuclein-negative, gene-defined disease.
Therapeutic windowThe range of levodopa levels that gives benefit without dyskinesia. A progressively narrowing window is widely taught, but one 4-year study found results not consistent with a falling dyskinesia threshold.
Time-critical medicationA medicine whose dose timing matters clinically. NICE's standard is levodopa within 30 minutes of the prescribed time in hospital or care homes.
TolcaponeA COMT inhibitor with a boxed warning for fatal fulminant liver failure, requiring liver enzyme monitoring. The Tasmar brand is now discontinued in the US.
Tremor-dominant subtypeA motor subtype in which tremor predominates. In DATATOP, 441 people were tremor-dominant and 233 PIGD-dominant.
Trichloroethylene (TCE)An industrial solvent. Ever exposure gave an odds ratio of 6.1 for Parkinson's disease in a twin study, with a wide confidence interval because of the small sample.
UK Brain Bank criteriaThree-step clinical criteria (1988-1992): bradykinesia plus another sign, a list of exclusions, and three or more supportive features. NICE NG71 uses them for diagnosis.
VagotomySurgical cutting of the vagus nerve. Danish and Swedish registry studies found lower Parkinson's risk after truncal vagotomy in some analyses, but a reanalysis disagreed.
Vascular parkinsonismParkinsonism from cerebrovascular disease, often bilateral and 'lower-body', with a shuffling gait.
Ventral intermediate thalamus (Vim)A thalamic nucleus targeted by DBS or focused-ultrasound lesioning to suppress tremor.
VPS35A retromer-complex gene whose D620N variant causes dominant, levodopa-responsive Parkinson's disease with high but incomplete penetrance.
Wearing-offThe predictable return of symptoms at the end of a levodopa dose; the most frequent type of motor fluctuation.
