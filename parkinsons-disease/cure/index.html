Part 4 · Living, caring, preventing

Toward a cure

Many trials are testing ways to slow Parkinson's, and none has yet been proven to. A ten-year review of the drug pipeline concludes that "there are still no treatments to modify the course of the disease". 48 This page reports the research routes as research, with an evidence label on each, and states negative results as plainly as positive ones.

How the labels are used here. Established means shown, positive or negative, in at least one adequately powered randomised controlled trial. Early evidence means some randomised or controlled signal, or consistent open-label and biomarker data, not confirmed in phase 3. Hypothesis means a mechanistic, laboratory or observational rationale, or only safety and target-engagement data in people. By this scheme no route has established disease-modifying benefit. 48,73

Early evidence · best evidence

Two phase 2 trials missed their primary endpoints; a phase 3 is recruiting

Prasinezumab missed its primary endpoint in PASADENA (316 people) and in PADOVA (586 people; hazard ratio for motor progression 0.84, p=0.066), with a consistent exploratory signal on motor scores. 245,267 Cinpanemab showed no effect and was discontinued. 186 PADOVA's authors call the subgroup findings "prespecified exploratory" evidence; the sponsor and the publication both frame the phase 3, PARAISO, as the confirmatory test. 245,311

In plain wordsAntibodies against synuclein have not yet clearly slowed Parkinson's. One gave hints on secondary measures, and a larger trial is now testing it properly.
Exam hookPrasinezumab: PASADENA and PADOVA missed primary endpoints; PARAISO phase 3 recruiting. Cinpanemab (SPARK): negative, discontinued.

Early evidenceTarget: Synuclein spread

Anti-synuclein antibodies

Monoclonal antibodies given by infusion bind aggregated alpha-synuclein outside cells, aiming to stop its cell-to-cell spread. 186,267

  • PASADENA · prasinezumab Missed primary
    NCT03100149 · Phase 2 · n = 316
    Primary (MDS-UPDRS I–III at 52 weeks) not met: change 9.4 on placebo, 7.4 on 1500 mg, 8.8 on 4500 mg; DaT-SPECT no different. Posted results: Part III worsened 5.57 versus 3.69 points (p=0.135). 73,267
  • SPARK · cinpanemab Missed primary
    NCT03318523 · Phase 2 · n = 357
    Stopped after the week-72 interim for lack of efficacy: MDS-UPDRS total 10.8 on placebo versus 10.5–11.3 on drug (all P≥0.80). Development discontinued. 186
  • PADOVA · prasinezumab Missed primary
    NCT04777331 · Phase 2b · n = 586
    Time to confirmed motor progression: hazard ratio 0.84 (0.69–1.01), p=0.066; median 61.1 versus 49.7 weeks. In the levodopa-treated subgroup (about 75%) the sponsor reported 0.79, nominal p=0.0431. Serious adverse events 12% in each arm. 245,311
  • PARAISO · prasinezumab Running
    NCT07174310 · Phase 3 · n = 900 (est.)
    Recruiting since November 2025; primary completion estimated 30 June 2029; primary endpoint time to confirmed motor progression. 73

Active synuclein vaccines

An active vaccine trains the immune system to make its own antibodies against pathological alpha-synuclein. 91

  • UB-312 Open-label or biomarker
    NCT04075318 · Phase 1 · n = 70 (20 with PD)
    Antibodies in serum (12 of 13) and spinal fluid (5 of 13) after three doses; clinical scales no different; spinal-fluid synuclein seeds fell in a subset (exploratory). 91
  • VacSYn · ACI-7104.056 Open-label or biomarker
    NCT06015841 · Phase 2 · n = 150 (est.)
    Interim, April 2025 (company release): antibodies more than 20-fold above placebo after 4 doses; no efficacy data. 5
  • VacSYn completion Running
    NCT06015841 · Phase 2 · n = 150 (est.)
    Active, not recruiting; primary completion estimated June 2028. 73

GLP-1 receptor agonists

GLP-1 receptor agonists, used for diabetes, act on insulin signalling, neuroinflammation and mitochondrial pathways, with a proposed neurotrophic action. 20,229

  • Exenatide phase 2 Met primary
    NCT01971242 · Phase 2 · n = 62
    OFF-medication Part III at 60 weeks, after a 12-week washout: adjusted difference −3.5 points (−6.7 to −0.3), p=0.0318. The authors could not separate disease modification from a long-lasting symptomatic effect. 20
  • LIXIPARK · lixisenatide Met primary
    NCT03439943 · Phase 2 · n = 156
    Part III (ON) at 12 months: −0.04 versus +3.04 (difference 3.08, P=0.007); other secondary endpoints no different; nausea in 46%, vomiting in 13%. 229
  • NLY01 · pegylated exenatide Missed primary
    NCT04154072 · Phase 2 · n = 255
    MDS-UPDRS II+III no different at 36 weeks (p=0.77 and 0.79); gastrointestinal effects 61–75% versus 36% on placebo. 223
  • Exenatide-PD3 Missed primary
    NCT04232969 · Phase 3 · n = 194
    OFF Part III worsened 5.7 versus 4.5 points over 96 weeks (p=0.47); serious adverse events 9% versus 11%. 363

LRRK2 inhibitors and lowering

LRRK2 mutations raise the enzyme's kinase activity, which affects lysosomes. Inhibitors block the kinase; antisense drugs lower how much LRRK2 is made. 204,376

  • LUMA · BIIB122 Missed primary
    NCT05348785 · Phase 2b · n = 648
    Time to confirmed worsening: HR 1.04 (0.85–1.28), P=.4538; headache 17.1% versus 9.2%; withdrawal for adverse events 11.2% versus 4.6% (MDS 2026 late-breaking abstract, news report). 240
  • LIGHTHOUSE · BIIB122 in carriers Terminated
    NCT05418673 · Phase 3 · n = 7
    Terminated after 7 participants; reason given "Sponsor Decision". 73
  • REASON · BIIB094 antisense Open-label or biomarker
    NCT03976349 · Phase 1 · n = 82
    No drug-related serious adverse events; spinal-fluid LRRK2 down up to 59% and pRab10 up to 50%; efficacy not assessed. 204
  • LRRK2-associated PD trial Running
    NCT06602193 · Phase 2 · n = 50 (est.)
    Active, not recruiting; primary completion estimated March 2027. 73
HypothesisTarget: Lysosome clearance

GBA1 and glucocerebrosidase

GBA1 makes the lysosomal enzyme glucocerebrosidase (GCase). Drugs try to boost the enzyme (ambroxol, activators, gene therapy) or to reduce its fatty substrates (venglustat). 126,236,334

  • AiM-PD · ambroxol Open-label or biomarker
    NCT02941822 · Open-label · n = 23 (17 analysed)
    Spinal-fluid GCase protein +35% but activity −19%; alpha-synuclein +13%; Part III improved 6.8 points with no placebo arm. 236
  • MOVES-PD · venglustat Missed primary
    NCT02906020 · Phase 2 · n = 221
    MDS-UPDRS II+III worsened 7.29 versus 4.71 points over 52 weeks (difference 2.58, p=0.17); long-term follow-up halted. 73,126
  • ACTIVATE · BIA 28-6156 Missed primary
    NCT05819359 · Phase 2 · n = 273
    Progression similar to placebo over 18 months, no safety concerns; not continued for GBA1 Parkinson's (June 2026 news, no peer-reviewed data). 230
  • ASPro-PD · ambroxol Running
    NCT05778617 · Phase 3 · n = 330 (est.)
    Recruiting; primary completion estimated February 2029; primary endpoint MDS-UPDRS I–III change at week 104. 73
  • LY3884961 · AAV9-GBA1 Running
    NCT04127578 · Phase 1/2a · n = 32 (est.)
    Gene therapy; safety endpoints only; primary completion estimated August 2031. 73
Early evidenceTarget: Dopamine shortfall

Cell replacement

Dopamine-cell precursors, from fetal tissue or now from stem cells, are placed in the putamen to replace lost neurons. This aims to restore dopamine, not to stop the disease. 24,350

  • Fetal transplant, US Missed primary
    sham-controlled · Controlled · n = 40
    Global rating at 1 year 0.0 versus −0.4 with sham, not significantly different; people 60 or younger improved on UPDRS; dyskinesia recurred in 15%. 114
  • Fetal transplant, double-blind Missed primary
    placebo procedure · Controlled · n = 34
    No overall effect on OFF motor score (p=0.244); dyskinesia persisted off medication in 56% of transplanted patients. 253
  • Autologous iPSC case Open-label or biomarker
    single patient · Case · n = 1
    Patient-derived cells without immunosuppression; PET suggested graft survival; symptoms stable or improved at 18–24 months (single uncontrolled case). 327
  • TRANSEURO · fetal tissue Missed primary
    NCT01898390 · Open-label · n = 11
    No overall clinical effect at 3 years; 18F-DOPA uptake improved in 7, near-normal in 1; no major graft-induced dyskinesia. The authors conclude a stem-cell source is needed. 24
  • Bemdaneprocel · hESC Open-label or biomarker
    NCT04802733 · Phase 1 · n = 12
    No product-related adverse events or graft-induced dyskinesia; 18F-DOPA uptake rose at 18 months; OFF Part III improved about 23 points in the high-dose group (open-label). 350
  • Kyoto · iPSC Open-label or biomarker
    jRCT2090220384 · Phase 1/2 · n = 7
    No serious adverse events; OFF Part III improved in 4 of 6 evaluable patients by a mean 9.5 points; putaminal 18F-DOPA uptake +44.7%; no graft overgrowth. 316
  • Japan: conditional approval Event
    raguneprocel (AMCHEPRY) · Regulatory
    Conditional, time-limited approval in early March 2026, valid in Japan only, on the 7-patient trial; 7 years to collect confirmatory data. 277
  • exPDite-2 · bemdaneprocel Running
    NCT06944522 · Phase 3 · n = 102 (est.)
    Randomised 2:1 to cells or sham surgery, triple-masked; primary endpoint ON time without troublesome dyskinesia; primary completion estimated March 2027. 73

Gene therapy

Viral vectors deliver genes for growth factors (GDNF, neurturin), for dopamine-making enzymes (AADC, ProSavin) or for GAD, which changes signalling in the subthalamic nucleus. 62,191,275,360

  • AAV2-neurturin · CERE-120 Missed primary
    NCT00400634 · Phase 2 · n = 58
    Sham-controlled: UPDRS III OFF difference −0.31 (−5.58 to 4.97), p=0.91 at 12 months; serious adverse events 13 of 38 versus 4 of 20. 211
  • AAV2-GAD Met primary
    NCT00643890 · Phase 2 · n = 45
    Sham-controlled: UPDRS III OFF improved 8.1 points (23.1%) versus 4.7 (12.7%) at 6 months (p=0.04); later listed as terminated for financial reasons. 73,191
  • ProSavin Open-label or biomarker
    open-label · Phase 1/2 · n = 15
    UPDRS III OFF 38 to 26 at 6 months; on-medication dyskinesia the commonest drug-related event; its successor was terminated after 6 participants. 73,275
  • VY-AADC01 Open-label or biomarker
    PD-1101 · Phase 1 · n = 15
    No vector-related serious adverse events over 3 years; medication needs fell 21–30% in the higher-dose cohorts; the randomised follow-on enrolled only 14. 62,73
  • AB-1005 · AAV2-GDNF Open-label or biomarker
    NCT04167540 · Phase 1b · n = 11
    Moderate cohort at 18 months: OFF Part III −20.4 points, OFF time −1.7 h a day, no control arm; mild cohort numerically stable. 360
  • REGENERATE-PD · AB-1005 Running
    NCT06285643 · Phase 2 · n = 127 (est.)
    Randomised; primary endpoint motor diary at month 18; completion estimated August 2028. 73

Repurposed drugs that failed

Drugs approved for other conditions were tested against iron build-up (deferiprone), calcium entry (isradipine), low urate (inosine), c-Abl kinase (nilotinib, risvodetinib), mitochondrial function (UDCA) and NAD+ supply (nicotinamide riboside). 43,83,281,298,326,337

  • STEADY-PD III · isradipine Missed primary
    NCT02168842 · Phase 3 · n = 336
    Effect on UPDRS I–III −0.27 points (−3.02 to 2.48), P=0.85 over 36 months; the dose may not have engaged the target. 298
  • SURE-PD3 · inosine Missed primary
    NCT02642393 · Phase 3 · n = 298
    Stopped for futility: urate rose 44% but progression was 11.1 versus 9.9 points a year (P=.18); kidney stones 7.0 versus 1.4 per 100 patient-years. 326
  • NILO-PD · nilotinib Missed primary
    NCT03205488 · Phase 2 · n = 76
    Spinal-fluid to serum ratio only 0.2–0.3%; Part III ON worse at 300 mg at month 1. 337
  • FAIRPARK-II · deferiprone Worse than placebo
    NCT02655315 · Phase 2 · n = 372
    MDS-UPDRS total worsened 15.6 versus 6.3 (difference 9.3, P<0.001); dopaminergic therapy needed by 22.0% versus 2.7%; agranulocytosis in 2. 83
  • UP study · UDCA Missed primary
    NCT03840005 · Phase 2 · n = 30
    Safe, with signs of midbrain target engagement on 31P-MRS; Part III no different. 281
  • 201 Trial · risvodetinib Open-label or biomarker
    NCT05424276 · Phase 2a · n = 137
    Met its safety and tolerability primary endpoints; the lead secondary endpoint, MDS-UPDRS II+III, was not significant. 375
  • NOPARK · nicotinamide riboside Worse than placebo
    NCT03568968 · Phase 3 · n = 410
    MDS-UPDRS I–III rose 2.45 points versus −0.27 on placebo at 52 weeks (difference 2.72, P=.02, favouring placebo); non-motor symptoms also worse (NMSS difference 4.22, P=.009); serious adverse events 8.3% versus 14.1%. 43
  • EJS ACT-PD · terazosin, telmisartan Running
    NCT07207057 · Phase 3 · n = 1,200 (est.)
    Multi-arm platform against placebo for 36 months; primary endpoint MDS-UPDRS I+II. 73

Exercise as disease modification

High-intensity aerobic exercise is being tested for whether it slows the disease, as distinct from easing symptoms. 318,342

  • SPARX · treadmill Met primary
    phase 2 · Phase 2 · n = 128
    High-intensity arm +0.3 versus +3.2 points in controls over 6 months (P=.03); only the high-intensity arm passed the non-futility threshold. 318
  • SPARX3 · treadmill Running
    NCT04284436 · Phase 3 · n = 370
    High versus moderate intensity, 4 times a week; active, not recruiting; primary outcome MDS-UPDRS III change at 12 months; completion estimated July 2027. 73,342

How trials try to tell slowing from masking

Parkinson's medicines improve symptoms, so a drug that simply adds a little symptomatic benefit can look like one that slows the disease. A delayed-start design is one answer. Switch between the two possibilities and play the trial.

If the drug

Schematic. No real trial data are drawn; the lines show the logic of the design only.

Delayed-start design · schematic

Catching up suggests a symptomatic effect

In phase 1 one group takes the drug and the other placebo; in phase 2 both take the drug. If the delayed group catches up, the benefit looks symptomatic; if a gap persists, that is consistent with slowing. In ADAGIO (1,176 people), early-start rasagiline 1 mg met all three hierarchical end points but 2 mg did not, so the authors advised caution: delayed-start results can be hard to interpret. 254

In plain wordsStarting a drug earlier should leave a lasting advantage only if it changes the course of the disease.
Exam hookDelayed start: persistent gap after both arms are on drug = consistent with disease modification. ADAGIO: rasagiline 1 mg positive, 2 mg not. 254
Design

Washout and off-medication scores

Medicines taken during a trial can mask progression. The exenatide trial added a 12-week washout and LIXIPARK a 2-month one; PADOVA used time to confirmed worsening on OFF-medication scores. 20,229,245

Caution

Subgroups and external comparisons

A post hoc PASADENA analysis found larger effects in faster-progressing subgroups, which its authors say needs new randomised trials. 268 The open-label extension compared participants with an outside cohort (PPMI), which is exploratory and not randomised. 269

Enrolment

Seed amplification assays

The US FDA issued a Letter of Support for the CSF alpha-synuclein seed amplification assay as a susceptibility and risk biomarker, so trials can enrol people who have underlying synuclein pathology. 345 See Diagnosis and the MDS criteria.

Platforms

Many drugs, one trial

Path to Prevention is a phase 2A, multi-regimen platform trial nested in the PPMI cohort, for early-stage neuronal alpha-synuclein disease; no registry number or named drugs were found. 339 EJS ACT-PD is a multi-arm phase 3 platform testing terazosin and telmisartan against placebo in an estimated 1,200 people. 73

Unproven stem-cell clinics

What the guidelines say. The International Society for Stem Cell Research says stem-cell-based interventions must be "proven safe and effective" before they are marketed to patients, and cautions that registration on a trials website does not mean a study has been vetted. 160

What Japan's approval is, and is not. In early March 2026 Japan granted conditional, time-limited approval to an iPS-cell-derived product on the basis of a 7-patient trial, with 7 years to collect confirmatory data and validity only within Japan. Patient organisations note open questions on long-term safety, rejection and how long grafts last. 277,316

No treatment has yet been shown to slow Parkinson's disease.

Two synuclein antibodies, an LRRK2 kinase inhibitor and two GBA1-pathway drugs missed their primary endpoints in randomised trials. 126,186,230,240,245,267 Nicotinamide riboside did worse than placebo in a phase 3 trial. 43 Two small GLP-1 trials were positive on motor scores, but a phase 3 trial, now under an Expression of Concern, and another phase 2 trial were negative. 20,223,229,363 A phase 3 antibody trial, a phase 3 cell trial and a phase 3 exercise trial are running. 73,342 See Genes and inheritance and Living with Parkinson's.
