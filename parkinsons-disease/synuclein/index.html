Part 1 · The disease

Alpha-synuclein and Lewy bodies

Alpha-synuclein is a small protein that normally sits at nerve endings and helps synaptic vesicles release their messenger. In Parkinson's it folds the wrong way, sticks together and ends up in Lewy bodies. Press play to follow one nerve ending, then change the genes to see what more protein, or weaker clearance, does.

Step 1 · The gene and the protein

A small protein at the synapse

SNCA, on the long arm of chromosome 4, encodes a 140-amino-acid protein found at presynaptic nerve endings. 288,356 It has three regions: an N-terminal stretch (residues 1–60) that tends to form a helix, a central NAC region (61–95) that is prone to aggregate, and a negatively charged C-terminal tail (96–140) that protects against aggregation. 354 At the synapse it binds the vesicle SNARE protein synaptobrevin-2 (VAMP2) and promotes SNARE-complex assembly. Mice lacking all three synucleins develop age-dependent neurological impairment and die early. 50

In plain wordsA small protein sits at nerve endings, resting on the tiny bubbles that carry chemical messages, and helps them work.
Exam hookSNCA is on chromosome 4. Its A53T mutation, found in 1997 in an Italian family and three Greek families, was the first gene change shown to cause Parkinson's. 288

From one molecule to a Lewy body

Each step from a single molecule to a Lewy body has its own shape. A laboratory test copies the seeding step: a seed amplification assay multiplies abnormal synuclein from spinal fluid. In PPMI it was positive in 87.7% of people with Parkinson's and in 6 of 163 controls. 333 An MDS statement cautions that a positive result does not necessarily reflect seeding or aggregation in the brain. 53 More in Diagnosis and the MDS criteria.

Does it start in the gut?

One idea is that synuclein pathology can begin in the nerves of the gut and climb the vagus nerve to the brainstem. If so, cutting the vagus, or removing the appendix, might lower the risk. Large registry studies have tested this, with mixed results. Filled dots are results whose 95% confidence interval excludes no effect.

Weak and mixed support

A ratio below 1 means fewer cases of Parkinson's after surgery. In Denmark, truncal vagotomy was linked to lower risk only with more than 20 years of follow-up, and the authors noted limited precision. 349 A reanalysis of the same population, followed to 2011, found no such effect. 198 In Sweden, any vagotomy showed no association, but truncal vagotomy at least 5 years before diagnosis did. 198

One Swedish registry linked appendectomy to a 19.3% lower risk, seen in rural but not urban residents. A meta-analysis of six studies found no effect, with large differences between studies. 172,201

In plain wordsSome studies suggest that cutting the nerve from the gut lowers the risk; others find nothing. The question is open.
Exam hookDual-hit hypothesis (Hawkes, Del Tredici and Braak 2007): a pathogen enters through the nose and through the gut, then climbs the vagus nerve to the medulla. 140 The staging that follows from it is in How it spreads.

How strong is the synuclein case?

Established

Lewy bodies are made of synuclein

Lewy bodies are immunoreactive for alpha-synuclein, and most of it is phosphorylated at Ser129, in familial and sporadic disease alike. 15,343 Skin biopsy finds phosphorylated synuclein in 92.7% of people with Parkinson's, 96.0% with dementia with Lewy bodies, 98.2% with MSA and 3.3% of controls. 125

Established

Changing the gene can cause Parkinson's

Point mutations in SNCA cause dominant disease: A53T in 1997, A30P in 1998, and E46K in a Spanish family with parkinsonism, dementia and hallucinations. 182,288,386 Extra copies cause it too. Across 59 families, onset averaged 46.9 years with three copies and 34.5 with four, and dementia was noted in 50.9% of probands. 36,340 Common variation counts: the 263-bp REP1 promoter allele had an odds ratio of 1.43. 209 More in Genes and inheritance.

Early evidence

It can spread like a seed

Injected fibrils spread pathology through connected regions in normal mice and killed dopamine neurons. 203 Lewy bodies appeared in fetal grafts placed in people with Parkinson's, after 11 to 16 years and after 14 years. 178,193 Most grafted cells still looked functional after a decade. 193 Whether ordinary Parkinson's spreads this way is the question in How it spreads: Braak stages.

Early evidence

Strains: one protein, different diseases?

Oligomers, ribbons and fibrils of alpha-synuclein injected into rat brain amplified there and produced different pathology and behaviour. Fibrils were the most toxic. Ribbons produced combined Parkinson's and MSA features. The assemblies crossed the blood-brain barrier after intravenous injection. 283

Hypothesis

Cause or marker? A real disagreement

For a causal role: SNCA mutations and extra copies cause disease, and injected fibrils cause neurodegeneration in mice. 203,288,340

Against a simple story: of 37 autopsied LRRK2 cases, 20 had no Lewy bodies, and Lewy bodies may be absent in typical LRRK2 and parkin disease. 169,319 In one series about 55% of people at Braak stages 5–6 had no dementia or parkinsonism. 279 In PPMI, people with a negative spinal-fluid seed test progressed no differently, and critics argue seeding "does not appear necessary" for the syndrome. 96

Hypothesis

Gut first?

Lewy bodies were found in the gut wall plexuses of all 7 people with Parkinson's examined, and in 8 of 24 people without it. 368 Healthy appendix tissue contained synuclein aggregates. 172 Braak's group proposes the earliest lesions in the olfactory bulb, the vagal nucleus and possibly the gut. 42 The surgical studies are in the plot above.

Early evidence

Antibodies against synuclein

Prasinezumab missed its primary endpoint in PASADENA and in PADOVA (hazard ratio 0.84, 95% CI 0.69–1.01), and a phase 3 trial is recruiting. Cinpanemab showed no effect and was stopped. 73,186,245,267 The details are in Toward a cure.

Other pieces of the puzzle

Synuclein is not the only thread. Toxins that poison mitochondria, recessive genes for mitochondrial quality control, and iron all point at the same vulnerable neurons. The genes are in Genes and inheritance and the trials that target these steps in Toward a cure.

Established

MPTP: a toxin that causes parkinsonism

Four people who injected an illicit pethidine (meperidine) analogue containing MPTP developed marked, lasting parkinsonism. The authors proposed that MPTP selectively damages substantia nigra cells. 187 The basal ganglia rate model was later built largely from MPTP-treated monkeys. 118 See The dopamine pathway.

Early evidence

Mitochondria and rotenone

Rats given rotenone, a pesticide that blocks mitochondrial complex I, developed selective nigrostriatal degeneration, slowness and rigidity. Their nigral neurons formed fibrillar inclusions containing ubiquitin and alpha-synuclein. 30 Human pesticide data are in Risk and protection.

Early evidence

PINK1 and parkin: removing damaged mitochondria

PINK1 is kept low on healthy mitochondria and builds up on damaged ones, which recruits parkin. Parkin then triggers removal of the mitochondrion by autophagy. Disease mutations block this at distinct steps. 239 Mutations in parkin and PINK1 cause recessive early-onset parkinsonism, and parkin disease may lack Lewy bodies. 174,319,357

Hypothesis

Iron

Total iron in the substantia nigra was 31–35% higher in Parkinson's than in controls, while globus pallidus iron was 29% lower. The authors suggested that excess nigral iron may promote toxic oxygen radicals. 84

Hypothesis

GBA1 and the lysosome as a drug target

The GCase loop is the rationale for GBA1-targeted drugs. Venglustat did not help GBA1 carriers (motor scores worsened 7.29 points against 4.71 on placebo, p=0.17), and a GCase activator progressed like placebo in sponsor-reported results. An ambroxol phase 3 trial is recruiting. 73,126,230
