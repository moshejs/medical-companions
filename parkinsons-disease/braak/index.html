Part 1 · The disease

How it spreads: Braak stages

In 2003 Heiko Braak's group proposed that Lewy pathology climbs through the brain in six stages: from the lower brainstem and the smell pathway, through the substantia nigra, to the cortex. 41 A newer model splits Parkinson's into a body-first type that starts in the gut and a brain-first type that starts inside the brain. 37 Step through each order and watch which symptoms tend to go with it.

Stage 1 · medulla and smell

Medulla and smell bulb

Lesions start in the dorsal motor nucleus of the glossopharyngeal and vagal nerves (IX/X) in the medulla, and in the anterior olfactory nucleus. 41,49 In the body-first model, this is where pathology arriving from the gut along the vagus first reaches the brain. 37 Lewy bodies were found in the gut wall plexuses of all 7 people with Parkinson's examined. 368

In plain wordsThe first clumps appear low in the brainstem, in the nucleus that sends the vagus nerve to the gut, and in the smell pathway.
Exam hookBraak stage 1 = dorsal motor nucleus of IX/X in the medulla + anterior olfactory nucleus. 41

Where Braak does not fit

Braak's scheme came from a selected sample: incidental cases and people with a clinical diagnosis, cut in unusually thick sections. 42 Other brain banks tested it on their own cases. Pick a study to see how many brains followed the expected bottom-to-top order.

Kalaitzakis 2008 · 71 Parkinson's brains

Almost half did not fit

In 71 Parkinson's brains the substantia nigra was affected in 100% and the nucleus basalis of Meynert in 98.5%. 53% fitted caudo-rostral spread and 47% did not. In 7% the dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus was spared despite nigral and cortical inclusions, so the vagal nucleus is not an obligatory starting point. 167

In plain wordsAlmost half of these brains did not follow the bottom-to-top order.

Beyond dopamine: the other systems hit

Lewy pathology and cell loss reach far beyond the substantia nigra: noradrenaline, serotonin and acetylcholine cells in the brain, and nerves in the heart, the gut and the skin. These help explain the symptoms that levodopa does not touch, covered in Beyond movement. Pick a system.

Locus coeruleus

In 19 Parkinson's cases, neuron loss was greatest in the locus coeruleus (effect size 6.47), ahead of the substantia nigra (2.58) and the nucleus basalis (0.85). 385 It is a stage 2 nucleus in Braak's scheme. 49

The debate in brief

Established

A common pattern, not a rule

In a series of 226 assessable brains, 83% fitted the staging systems. 279 Across studies, though, 6.3% to 43% of cases did not follow bottom-to-top spread. 164 The scheme came from a selected, retrospective sample studied in thick sections. 42

Established

Stage is not severity

Many older people with no neurological signs have stage 4–6 pathology at death, and some people at stages 3–4 had severe Parkinson's. 49 About 55% of people at stages 5–6 in one series had no dementia or parkinsonism. 279 A review found no relationship between Braak stage and clinical severity. 164

Early evidence

Body-first and brain-first

More than 94% of people with isolated RBD have markedly abnormal cardiac MIBG, yet they show only a 20–25% average fall in putaminal dopamine markers, against more than 50% in early Parkinson's. About 30% of people at Hoehn and Yahr I–II are expected to be RBD-positive. 37 An imaging study found the two predicted orders: heart and colon first in RBD-positive people, putamen first in RBD-negative ones. 152

Early evidence

Amygdala-predominant cases

In about 43–60% of late-stage Alzheimer's cases, Lewy pathology is mostly or only in the amygdala. Braak and Del Tredici say their staging does not apply to these cases. 42 In population brains, 29% were amygdala-predominant. 49

Established

A unified staging system

Beach and colleagues allowed two routes, brainstem-first and limbic-first. Their five stages classified all 417 brains and tracked nigral loss, MMSE and motor scores step by step. 26

Hypothesis

Braak's group replies

Braak and Del Tredici argue that the staging shaped research on the peripheral nervous system, defined prodromal cohorts and supported spreading models. They propose the earliest lesions in the olfactory bulb, the vagal nucleus and possibly the gut. 42 The surgical tests of the gut route are plotted in Alpha-synuclein and Lewy bodies.

Early evidence

Symptoms before the tremor

Timed back from diagnosis, smell loss is predicted to start more than 20 years before, constipation 10–16 years before, and bradykinesia about 5–6 years before. 107 More in Before the tremor.
