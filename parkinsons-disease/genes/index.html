Part 1 · The disease

Genes and inheritance

Most Parkinson's is not inherited in a simple way. About 15% of people with Parkinson's have a family history, and 5–10% have a single-gene form. 81 A few gene changes raise the chance a lot, many common ones raise it a little, and most people who carry even the strongest of them never develop the disease.

Seven genes, three patterns

Rare variants in more than 20 genes have been reported to cause Parkinson's, most of them highly penetrant and often with early or atypical onset. 32 Seven have been formally adjudicated as causal contributors. 68 In genetic forms, Lewy bodies may be absent even when the illness looks typical, so they are not required for the diagnosis. 319 See Alpha-synuclein and Lewy bodies.

DominantReduced penetrance

LRRK2 (PARK8)

Found in 2004 by two groups; one named the protein "dardarin", from the Basque word for tremor. 272,389 Six mutations meet the consortium's criteria for proven pathogenicity; G2019S is by far the commonest (391 unrelated carriers). 141 G2019S and R1441C raise the enzyme's kinase activity, which points to a gain of function. 376

The illness was "more benign" than idiopathic Parkinson's in one comparison: dyskinesia after 5 years in 11% versus 25%, cognitive impairment in 23% versus 70%. 141 Lewy bodies were found in 17 of 37 autopsied cases. 169

Risk factorTwo copies: Gaucher disease

GBA1

GBA1 makes glucocerebrosidase, a lysosomal enzyme. Two disease-causing variants cause Gaucher disease; one variant raises the risk of Parkinson's with reduced penetrance. 313,334 Newer numbering adds 39 amino acids: N409S was N370S, L483P was L444P, E365K was E326K. 313

"Severe" variants carried more risk than "mild" ones in Ashkenazi Parkinson's (odds ratio 10.3 versus 2.2), and earlier onset (mean 53.1 versus 58.1 years). 120 Carriers had a higher risk of dementia (hazard ratio 3.16) and death (1.85). 63

DominantGene dose

SNCA

The first Parkinson's gene: A53T, found in 1997 in an Italian kindred and three Greek families. 288 A30P followed in 1998, and E46K in a Spanish family with dementia and hallucinations. 182,386 Extra whole copies of the gene also cause it, and more copies mean earlier onset (see below). 36,54,340

RecessiveEarly onset

PRKN (parkin)

Identified in 1998 as the cause of autosomal recessive juvenile parkinsonism. 174 In 2000 parkin mutations were found in 49% of early-onset families with unaffected parents, in 77% of isolated cases starting at 20 or younger but only 3% starting after 30. 202 Carriers started younger (32 versus 42 years), more often had dystonia at onset, responded well to levodopa and developed dyskinesia. 202

Recessive

PINK1 (PARK6)

Homozygous mutations in this mitochondrial kinase were found in three consanguineous families in 2004: the first direct genetic link between mitochondria and Parkinson's. 357 Across more than 1,100 people with two PRKN, PINK1 or DJ-1 variants, median onset was about 30, the illness was clinically typical with an excellent treatment response, and cognitive decline was relatively uncommon. 170

Recessive

DJ-1 (PARK7)

Linked in 2003 to autosomal recessive early-onset parkinsonism. DJ-1 is implicated in the response to oxidative stress, and its loss leads to neurodegeneration. 35 It is very rare: one positive result among 2,699 Israeli patients tested. 17

DominantHigh, incomplete penetrance

VPS35

D620N was found by exome sequencing in a Swiss family with tremor-predominant, levodopa-responsive Parkinson's (mean onset 50.6), then in three more families, and not in 3,309 controls. 364 An Austrian family with 16 affected members (mean onset 53) carried the same variant. VPS35 is part of the retromer complex. 390

SNCA: the dose of one gene

Most people have two copies of SNCA, one on each chromosome 4. A duplication on one chromosome makes three copies; a triplication, or a duplication on both, makes four. Pick a dose.

Why the estimates disagree. Published G2019S penetrance ranges from 24% to 100%, because studies differ in ascertainment (families with many affected members versus unselected relatives), age, sex, ancestry and modifiers. 141,188,210 GBA1 estimates differ about threefold for the same reason. 10,16,23,304

What they agree on. Risk rises with age, and penetrance is incomplete for both genes: LRRK2 about 25–80% by 79–80, GBA1 about 8–30% by 80. 10,16,141,188,210 In the Ashkenazi kin-cohort, women carriers had a higher estimate than men (29% versus 22%). 210

Who carries what: founder populations

Odds ratio is not lifetime risk

In a 16-centre study, people carrying any GBA1 variant had about five times the odds of Parkinson's (odds ratio 5.43). 334 That sounds close to destiny, but it is a ratio. In Ashkenazi Gaucher families, Parkinson's by 80 was 2.1% in non-carriers and 7.7% in heterozygous parents. 10 Switch the carrier estimate to see the spread.

Common variants and polygenic scores

Established

GWAS: 90 signals

The 2019 meta-analysis tested 7.8 million variants in 37,688 cases, 18,618 "proxy cases" (people with an affected first-degree relative) and 1.4 million controls, all of European ancestry. It found 90 independent risk signals in 78 genomic regions, 38 of them new. 238

Established

Heritability

Common variation accounts for about 22% of Parkinson's liability (0.18–0.26), slightly below an earlier estimate of 0.27. The 90 variants explain 16–36% of the heritable risk, depending on the assumed prevalence. 32,238

Research use

Polygenic scores

People in the top quarter of a polygenic score had higher odds than those in the bottom quarter: 3.74 in one cohort and 6.25 in a validation cohort. Prediction reached an area under the curve near 0.69; reviews summarise 0.65–0.69, a modest ability to tell people apart. 32,238

Established

Family history

In a meta-analysis, a first-degree relative with Parkinson's gave an odds ratio of 3.23 (2.65–3.93) and any affected relative 4.45 (3.39–5.83), the strongest risk factors examined. 248 See Risk and protection.

Established

Common variants in known genes

A common SNCA promoter allele (REP1, 263 bp) raised the odds 1.43-fold without changing age at onset. 209 GBA1 E326K (E365K) raised them 1.99-fold in 15 studies. 157 The association of T408M is inconsistent across studies; no pooled figure was checked for this guide. 313

Gap

Mostly European data

Most risk variants were found in people of European ancestry, and relatively little is known about Parkinson's genetics in other populations. 32,238 LRRK2 G2019S, for example, was found in fewer than 0.1% of 4,172 Asian patients. 141

What a genetic test means

Large studies

What testing finds

ROPAD found a Parkinson's-relevant result in 14.8% of 12,580 patients from 16 countries; PD GENEration in 13% of 8,301 North American participants. Most positives were GBA1 risk variants. 68,377 Counting matters: PD GENEration reported GBA1 in 7.7% as "reportable" but 10.1% as "disease-relevant". 68

Large studies

Who tests positive

In ROPAD the yield was 19.9% with onset at 50 or younger, 19.5% with a family history and 26.9% with both; it fell 3% for each later year of onset, and women were 22% more likely to test positive. 377 In PD GENEration it was 23.9% with Ashkenazi Jewish, Basque or North African Berber ancestry and 9.1% with no risk factor. 68

Preprint

Criteria miss most carriers

A pooled analysis of 25,063 patients found that an onset threshold of 50 or younger captured only 23–32% of carriers in the population cohorts, and adding family history barely helped. Its authors, and the ROPAD authors, argue for broader testing in the gene-targeted trial era. 22,377

No proven therapy

Testing has not changed standard care

A 2023 MDS-published review notes "no proven gene-targeted therapies, but clinical trials are underway". 274 In 2026 the largest LRRK2 kinase-inhibitor trial and a GBA1 enzyme-activator trial reported no slowing. 230,240 See Toward a cure.

Counselling

How results were given

In PD GENEration every participant received results in a genetic-counselling session with a neurologist or certified genetic counsellor, and people with two GBA1 variants were referred to Gaucher centres. 68 Early consortium and Israeli authors urged caution in counselling and questioned screening people without symptoms until penetrance is better defined and prevention exists. 141,260

Open questions

No final guideline yet

No final MDS consensus on clinical genetic testing was found. The 2023 review lists open questions: testing without medical actionability, whether testing should vary by ethnicity, long-term outcomes of testing people without symptoms, and cost. 274 Many people with Parkinson's do not know their genetic status, which hampers trial enrolment. 67,68
