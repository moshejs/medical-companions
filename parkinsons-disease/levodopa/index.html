Published label information

What the Sinemet prescribing information says about starting and raising the dose. It is reported here as published, for study. It is not dosing advice for any person: doses are set with a prescriber, nothing should be changed without one, and Parkinson's medicines must never be stopped suddenly.

Reading the evidence

Established

Not toxic to the course

In ELLDOPA, people who had taken levodopa were still better than placebo after a 2-week washout. In LEAP, starting 40 weeks earlier made no difference at week 80 (UPDRS difference 1.0; 95% CI −1.5 to 3.5). Neither trial found that levodopa speeds the disease, and LEAP found it does not slow it either. 97,361

Disagreement

The imaging puzzle

ELLDOPA's dopamine-transporter scans fell more on levodopa (−6%, −4%, −7.2%) than on placebo (−1.4%; p=0.036), the opposite of the clinical scores. LEAP, 15 years later, again found no harm on clinical scores. 97,361

Label warning

Vitamin B6

Pyridoxine (vitamin B6) at 10 to 25 mg can reverse levodopa given alone by speeding its breakdown outside the brain; carbidopa blocks this. The current labels also warn of vitamin B6 deficiency and seizures: postmarketing seizures resisted anti-seizure drugs and stopped only with B6. The labels say B6 should be checked before starting and from time to time. 189

Food, iron and other drugs

A high-protein diet may delay and reduce absorption, and iron salts, including those in multivitamins, bind levodopa. Metoclopramide speeds stomach emptying but blocks dopamine receptors. Phenothiazines, butyrophenones, risperidone and isoniazid may reduce the effect. 102,189

Guidance

Never stopped suddenly

NICE says Parkinson's medicines should not be withdrawn abruptly or allowed to fail through poor absorption, advises against "drug holidays" because of the risk of neuroleptic malignant syndrome, and asks hospitals and care homes to give them at the right times. 241 NICE's quality standard is within 30 minutes of the prescribed time. 242 See Nursing care.

Sudden sleep

Falling asleep without warning, including while driving, has been reported on carbidopa-levodopa, as late as one year after starting. 189

A common myth

"It stops working after five years"

The Parkinson's Foundation calls this perhaps the most pervasive myth about Parkinson's treatment and answers that levodopa works for decades. What changes is how smoothly each dose works. 285 See Levodopa over time.

The short version

In plain wordsLevodopa is a building block the brain turns into dopamine. Carbidopa stops the body using it up on the way, which means less sickness and a smaller dose. Only a few percent of each tablet reaches the brain. It eases symptoms; it does not slow or speed the disease. Doses and changes belong with the prescriber.
