The numbers, and where sources disagree

Counts depend on the year, the method and the population. Each card names its source so the figures can be compared fairly.

Worldwide, now

Over 8.5 million

people had Parkinson's disease in 2019, according to the WHO fact sheet. Prevalence has doubled in the past 25 years. In 2019 it caused 5.8 million disability-adjusted life years, up 81% since 2000, and 329,000 deaths, up more than 100%. [[WHO2023PD]]

WHO fact sheet dated 9 August 2023.

Worldwide, 1990 to 2016

2.5 → 6.1 million

The Global Burden of Disease study counted 6.1 million people with Parkinson's in 2016 (95% UI 5.0 to 7.3), against 2.5 million in 1990. Age-standardised prevalence still rose by 21.7%, so the increase is not explained by ageing alone. Parkinson's caused 211,296 deaths in 2016. [[GBD2018PD]]

The authors attribute the rise mainly to more older people, with possible contributions from longer disease duration and environmental factors. [[GBD2018PD]]

Forecasts

12 or 25 million?

Dorsey and colleagues call Parkinson's "the fastest growing neurological disorder in the world" and project a rise from over 6 million in 2015 to over 12 million by 2040, or above 17 million with longer lifespans, less smoking and industrialisation. [[Dorsey2018]] A 2025 GBD 2021 model projects 25.2 million in 2050 (95% UI 21.7 to 30.1), a 112% increase from 2021: 89% of the growth from ageing, 20% from population growth and 3% from changing prevalence. [[Su2025]]

Sources differ The projections use different GBD rounds, base years and assumptions. Both name ageing as the main driver. [[Dorsey2018,Su2025]]

North America

About 680,000

people with Parkinson's in the US in 2010, from a prevalence of 572 per 100,000 among people aged 45 and over. The model projected about 930,000 in 2020 and 1,238,000 in 2030; the authors call these minimum estimates. [[Marras2018]]

Across five North American data sources in 2012, yearly incidence at 65 and over ranged from 108 to 212 per 100,000. [[Willis2022]] In Kaiser Permanente data, adjusted incidence was 16.6 per 100,000 in Hispanic, 13.6 in non-Hispanic White, 11.3 in Asian and 10.2 in Black members. [[VanDenEeden2003]]

Israel

1%, 2%, 3%

of people aged 50, 60 and 70 and over had Parkinson's in a drug-tracer study of an Israeli health maintenance organisation covering about a quarter of the population (1998 to 2008). Incidence was stable at about 33 per 100,000 a year; prevalence rose from 170 to 256 per 100,000; the mean age at the first anti-parkinsonian prescription was 69. Men aged 50 and over had higher prevalence (RR 1.38) and incidence (RR 1.45). [[ChillagTalmor2011]]

Against the trend In the Clalit registry (2002 to 2021), age-adjusted incidence fell from 57 to 20.3 per 100,000, faster in women than in men. The authors suggest better diagnosis and better health and environmental conditions. Elsewhere, incidence in men rose over 30 years in Olmsted County. [[Balash2026,Savica2016]]

Men and women

About 1.4 to 1

The GBD male to female ratio of age-standardised prevalence was 1.40 in 2016 and 1.37 in 1990. [[GBD2018PD]] It is projected to rise from 1.46 in 2021 to 1.64 in 2050. [[Su2025]]

For new cases the gap is wider. Men to women incidence rate ratios at 65 and over were 1.66 (Ontario), 1.69 (Medicare), 1.77 (Kaiser) and 2.57 (Rochester) in 2012. [[Willis2022]] A meta-analysis found higher incidence in men in every age group, significant at 60 to 79. [[Hirsch2016]]

Early onset

21 to 50

The MDS task force recommends "early-onset" rather than "young-onset" Parkinson's, partly to avoid age-related stigma, and defines it as onset after 21 and before 50. Onset before 21 is conventionally called juvenile parkinsonism. Published cutoffs have ranged from 40 to 60. [[Mehanna2022]]

Only 4% of Kaiser Permanente cases began before 50. [[VanDenEeden2003]] Genes matter more at younger onset: see Genes and inheritance.

Exam hookParkinsonism = bradykinesia plus rest tremor and/or rigidity (MDS 2015); postural instability is not part of the core definition. Parkinson's disease was 42% of new parkinsonism in Olmsted County, with drug-induced parkinsonism second at 20%. [[Postuma2015,Bower1999]]
