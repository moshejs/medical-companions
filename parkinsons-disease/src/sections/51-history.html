World Parkinson's Day and the red tulip

11 April

A birthday and a flower

World Parkinson's Day is held on 11 April, James Parkinson's birthday. The red and white "Dr James Parkinson" tulip was bred in 1980 by J.W.S. Van der Wereld, a Dutch horticulturist who had Parkinson's. The red tulip was adopted as the symbol of Parkinson's at the 9th World Parkinson's Disease Day Conference in Luxembourg on 11 April 2005. [[ParkinsonsNSW2026]]

Sources differ Some secondary pages give 1981 for the tulip. [[ParkinsonsNSW2026]]

What the history leaves open

Contradicted

"The senses and intellects being uninjured"

Parkinson's own definition said the intellect is spared. Long cohorts disagree: in the Sydney study, 83% of people alive 20 years after diagnosis had dementia. [[Parkinson1817,Hely2008]] He also placed the cause in the upper cervical spinal cord, extending to the medulla. [[Parkinson1817,Goetz2011]]

Sources disagree

1919 or 1921?

A centenary history dates Tretiakoff's Paris thesis on the substantia nigra to 1919; an earlier history review gives 1921. [[Holdorff2019,Goetz2011]]

Hedged

An Israeli medicine

According to the Technion, professors Moussa Youdim and John Finberg developed rasagiline there in the 1970s, and Teva then developed it as Azilect. [[Technion2016]] The FDA approved Azilect on 16 May 2006. [[FDA-DrugsFDA]] A European approval in 2005 is reported only in secondary sources and could not be checked (unverified).
