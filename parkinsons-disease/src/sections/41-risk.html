Part 4 · Living, caring, preventing

Risk and protection

Some chemicals, head injuries and family history are linked to a higher chance of Parkinson's; being physically active is linked to a lower one. Each row below is an association from observational studies, not proof of cause. Play the plot to bring the evidence in, then pick any row for the details and the caveats.

Age comes first. The strongest risk factor is age: yearly incidence rises from 0.1 per 100,000 under 40 to 133.9 at 80 and over, and men are affected more often than women. [[Savica2016,GBD2018PD]] See What Parkinson's is.

Dots are odds, hazard or risk ratios; lines are 95% confidence intervals, on a log scale. An arrow means the interval runs off the scale. A line that crosses 1 is compatible with no association. Click a row or use the buttons below.

What to take from it

Early evidence

Exercise: the one prevention message

A 2016 review concluded that promoting physical activity is the only intervention that currently seems justifiable for primary prevention of Parkinson's. [[Ascherio2016]] Exercise trials so far measured motor scores over 6 months; SPARX3 is testing intensity in a phase 3 trial that started on 30 August 2021. [[Schenkman2018,vanderKolk2019,SPARX3]] Exercise in daily life is covered in Living with Parkinson's.

Not advice

Smoking and coffee

The lower rates in smokers and coffee drinkers are associations. A Danish study found that former smokers who found quitting harder were less likely to develop Parkinson's, and the authors read "ease of quitting" as an early sign of the disease, not as proof that tobacco protects. [[Ritz2014]] These findings are not advice to smoke or to drink coffee.

Early signs, not causes?

Constipation, anxiety, depression

In a meta-analysis, constipation was linked to later Parkinson's (RR 2.34, 1.55 to 3.53), as were anxiety and depression, but these may be early features of the disease rather than causes. [[Noyce2012]] See Before the tremor.

Established

Family history and genes

A first-degree relative with Parkinson's gave OR 3.23 (2.65 to 3.93), and any relative OR 4.45 (3.39 to 5.83), the strongest factors in that meta-analysis. [[Noyce2012]] How genes contribute is in Genes and inheritance.
