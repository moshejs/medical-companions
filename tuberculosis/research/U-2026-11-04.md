## Findings

### diagnose · WHO Module 3: diagnosis, second edition (issued 2 Nov 2026)
- The second edition introduces three new recommendations (Table A, marked NEW): a new diagnostic class, near point-of-care NAATs (NPOC-NAATs); tongue swabs as a sample for people who cannot produce sputum; and pooled sputum testing (Established) [[WHO-Mod3-2026]].
- Recommendation 1: in adults and adolescents with signs and symptoms of pulmonary TB or who screen positive, "NPOC-NAATs on sputum should be used as initial diagnostic tests for TB rather than smear microscopy" (strong, moderate certainty); "When sputum cannot be obtained, NPOC-NAATs on tongue swabs should be used" (strong, moderate certainty). First in class: MTB Nucleic Acid Test Card [[WHO-Mod3-2026]].
- NPOC-NAAT accuracy on sputum (5 studies, 9 countries, 3291 participants): sensitivity 85% (95% CI 82–88%), specificity 98% (97–98%); versus smear +22.0 points sensitivity; versus Xpert Ultra −7.5 (−11.6 to −3.5). In people with HIV: 79% (69–87%) and 97% (95–98%). Tongue swabs (6 studies, 10 countries, 3335 participants): 76% (71–81%) and 99% (99–100%) [[WHO-Mod3-2026]].
- "NPOC-NAATs do not yet provide rifampicin-resistance results" [[WHO-Mod3-2026]].
- Recommendation 2 (conditional, low certainty): LC-aNAATs on tongue swabs "may be used" when respiratory samples cannot be obtained [[WHO-Mod3-2026]].
- Recommendation 6 (conditional, high certainty): when resources do not allow individual testing, LC-aNAATs on up to four pooled sputa "may be used". Seven studies, 14 741 participants, Xpert Ultra only: sensitivity 85% (81–88%), specificity 98% (98–99%); −3.1% (−7.0 to +0.8%) versus individual testing at 1:4, with larger losses in community settings [[WHO-Mod3-2026]].
- Recommendation 7 (strong, moderate certainty): for adults and adolescents with HIV who have TB signs or symptoms, screen positive, are seriously ill or have advanced HIV disease, concurrent LC-aNAAT on respiratory samples plus urine LF-LAM, rather than the NAAT alone; this "supersedes prior guidance on using LF-LAM". It corrects the site's LF-LAM row, which gave the 2024 inpatient/outpatient wording [[WHO-Mod3-2026]].

### Exam hooks
- WHO 2026: near point-of-care NAATs replace smear microscopy as an initial test (strong); tongue swab when no sputum; no rifampicin result [[WHO-Mod3-2026]].
- WHO 2026: people with HIV and TB symptoms get urine LF-LAM and a respiratory molecular test together [[WHO-Mod3-2026]].

### In plain words
- WHO now recommends a simpler molecular TB test that clinics without a lab can use, on spit or a tongue swab. It does not show drug resistance, so a positive result needs a second test [[WHO-Mod3-2026]].
