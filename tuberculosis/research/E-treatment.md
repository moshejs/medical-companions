# E · Treatment of drug-susceptible TB disease and TB infection

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: drugs, regimen, tpt, safety, drtb.

This pass covers the first-line drugs (isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide, ethambutol, rifapentine) and moxifloxacin: how they work, how fast they kill, how they are absorbed, the doses as WHO, ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA, NTCA/CDC and the US labels publish them, and the rifamycin drug interactions. It then covers the regimens for drug-susceptible TB disease (2HRZE/4HR, the 4-month rifapentine-moxifloxacin regimen, the 4-month regimen for children with non-severe TB, the TRUNCATE-TB strategy, adjunct steroids for meningitis and pericarditis), adherence support (DOT, video, self-administered), adverse effects with rates, and TB preventive treatment (TPT), including levofloxacin for contacts of people with MDR/RR-TB. Drug data for bedaquiline, pretomanid and linezolid are given here; pass F covers the drug-resistant trials in depth.

Doses and guideline statements are quoted as published, for teaching. They are not instructions. Treatment is decided by a clinician and the national TB programme, and in most programmes TB treatment is free. Outcome and risk figures are population statistics from trials and cohorts, not predictions for one person.

## Findings

### drugs · How the first-line drugs work
- Isoniazid is a prodrug. A single *M. tuberculosis* gene, katG, which encodes catalase-peroxidase, restored isoniazid sensitivity in a resistant mutant, and deleting katG went with isoniazid resistance in 2 patient isolates (Established) [[Zhang1992katG]].
- A missense mutation in inhA conferred resistance to both isoniazid and ethionamide. InhA is involved in mycolic acid biosynthesis and is likely a primary target of isoniazid and ethionamide (Established) [[Banerjee1994]].
- The US isoniazid label states that isoniazid inhibits mycolic acid synthesis, is bactericidal against actively growing organisms, and that resistance arises from mutations in katG, inhA, kasA and ahpC [[FDA-Isoniazid]].
- Rifampicin binds a pocket of the RNA polymerase β subunit more than 12 Å from the active site. It blocks the path of the growing RNA once the transcript is 2 to 3 nucleotides long (Established) [[Campbell2001Rif]].
- Pyrazinamide enters the bacillus by passive diffusion and is converted to pyrazinoic acid by pyrazinamidase (pncA). Protonated pyrazinoic acid builds up under acid conditions because efflux is weak. The drug is more active against old, slow-metabolising bacilli than against growing cultures (Established) [[Zhang2003PZA]].
- Pyrazinamide resistance arises from highly diverse pncA mutations that remove pyrazinamidase activity, and the drug acts synergistically with rifampicin as a sterilising agent [[Zhang2003PZA]].
- Ethambutol inhibits arabinan polymerisation in the cell wall. Resistance involves the embCAB gene cluster (overexpression of Emb proteins, EmbB structural mutations, or both) and was described in up to 4% of clinical isolates at the time (Established) [[Telenti1997]].
- Rifapentine is a cyclopentyl derivative of rifampin. Its longer half-life lengthens rifamycin exposure while keeping once-daily dosing [[Dorman2021]].
- Moxifloxacin is a fluoroquinolone. Adding it to first-line drugs speeds sputum-culture conversion early in treatment, but on its own that was not enough to shorten treatment to 4 months [[Dorman2021,Gillespie2014]].
- Bedaquiline is a diarylquinoline that inhibits mycobacterial ATP synthase [[Diacon2014]]. Pretomanid is a nitroimidazooxazine that kills replicating *M. tuberculosis* by inhibiting mycolic acid biosynthesis [[FDA-Pretomanid]].

### drugs · Early bactericidal versus sterilising activity
- In 100 patients given 22 regimens, the fast killing in the first 2 days was driven mainly by isoniazid (p<0.001) and rifampin (p=0.027). Sterilising activity on days 2 to 14 came from rifampin (p=0.007) and streptomycin (p=0.006), not from isoniazid or pyrazinamide (Established) [[Jindani2003]].
- In combination regimens, ethambutol's effect on days 2 to 14 ran opposite to rifampin's, which suggests it may antagonise the sterilising activity of the other drugs (Early evidence) [[Jindani2003]].
- In a 14-day dose-ranging study, the daily fall in sputum bacterial load was 0.176 log10 CFU/mL at rifampin 10 mg/kg, against 0.265 at 30 mg/kg and 0.261 at 35 mg/kg. Exposure rose more than proportionally with dose, and 35 mg/kg was safe and well tolerated over 2 weeks (Early evidence) [[Boeree2015]].

### drugs · Pharmacokinetics and food
- Rifampin 600 mg fasting gave a Cmax of 10.54 µg/mL at 2.42 h. A high-fat meal cut Cmax by 36% (to 7.27 µg/mL), nearly doubled Tmax (4.43 h) and cut AUC by only 6%. Antacids did not change these values [[Peloquin1999]].
- The US rifampin label advises taking it once daily, 1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal, with a full glass of water [[FDA-Rifampin]].
- The rifampin label gives a mean serum half-life of 3.35 ± 0.66 h after 600 mg in healthy adults. The half-life shortens to about 2 to 3 h with repeated dosing [[FDA-Rifampin]].
- The Priftin label gives a rifapentine half-life of 13.19 ± 1.38 h after a single 900 mg dose. A high-fat meal raised rifapentine Cmax and AUC by 40% to 50% compared with fasting, and the label says to take it with meals [[FDA-Priftin]].
- In Study 31, rifapentine was given within 1 hour after food and rifampin on an empty stomach, because food affects their absorption in opposite directions [[Dorman2021]].
- WHO explains the 1,200 mg flat rifapentine dose: body weight does not significantly affect rifapentine clearance, and food raises absorption. Modelling predicted that 1,200 mg with modest food would reach the target AUC of about 500 to 600 mcg·h/L [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- Isoniazid is cleared mainly by acetylation, which is genetically determined. The label states that about 50% of Black and white people are slow inactivators, and that slow acetylation can raise blood levels and toxic reactions without changing efficacy [[FDA-Isoniazid]].
- In a Japanese RCT of NAT2-genotype-guided isoniazid dosing (172 patients), 78% of slow acetylators on standard dosing (about 5 mg/kg) had isoniazid liver injury, against none on 2.5 mg/kg. Early treatment failure in rapid acetylators fell from 38% to 15.0% with about 7.5 mg/kg (Early evidence) [[Azuma2013]].
- Pyrazinamide reaches peak plasma levels within 2 hours. Doses of 20 to 25 mg/kg give 30 to 50 mcg/mL, and CSF levels roughly equal plasma levels when the meninges are inflamed [[FDA-Pyrazinamide]].

### drugs · Doses as published
- US labels, adults. Isoniazid: 5 mg/kg up to 300 mg daily [[FDA-Isoniazid]]. Rifampin: 10 mg/kg once daily, not to exceed 600 mg/day [[FDA-Rifampin]]. Pyrazinamide: 15 to 30 mg/kg once daily, not exceeding 3 g/day, with the label noting that CDC recommendations do not exceed 2 g/day daily [[FDA-Pyrazinamide]]. Ethambutol: 15 mg/kg once every 24 hours for people not previously treated, and 25 mg/kg for retreatment [[FDA-Ethambutol]].
- US labels, children. Isoniazid: 10 to 15 mg/kg up to 300 mg daily [[FDA-Isoniazid]]. Rifampin: 10 to 20 mg/kg, not to exceed 600 mg/day [[FDA-Rifampin]].
- WHO daily doses for children (WHO 2014 children's guidance, carried into Module 4): isoniazid 10 mg/kg (range 7 to 15), maximum 300 mg/day; rifampicin 15 mg/kg (10 to 20), maximum 600 mg/day; pyrazinamide 35 mg/kg (30 to 40); ethambutol 20 mg/kg (15 to 25). Strong recommendation, moderate certainty [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- 4-month rifapentine-moxifloxacin regimen as tabulated by ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA, adults: isoniazid 300 mg, rifapentine 1,200 mg and moxifloxacin 400 mg daily for 17 weeks. Pyrazinamide is daily for 8 weeks by weight: 1,000 mg at 40 to <55 kg, 1,500 mg at ≥55 to 75 kg, 2,000 mg above 75 kg. Doses use actual body weight, 7 days a week with food, avoiding milk, antacids and other cations [[Saukkonen2025]].
- The same table lists pyridoxine (vitamin B6) 25 to 50 mg/day, to be given with isoniazid to all patients on these regimens [[Saukkonen2025]].
- 4-month regimen for children with non-severe TB (ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA Table 1): isoniazid 10 to 15 mg/kg and rifampin 10 to 20 mg/kg for 16 weeks; pyrazinamide 35 (30 to 40) mg/kg and ethambutol 20 (15 to 25) mg/kg for the first 8 weeks, with ethambutol included or left out according to local guidelines [[Saukkonen2025]].
- Priftin (rifapentine) for TB disease is labelled for ages 12 years and older, always with other TB drugs. It must not be used once weekly in the continuation phase with isoniazid in people with HIV, because rifamycin-resistant relapse was higher [[FDA-Priftin]].

### drugs · Rifamycin drug interactions
- Rifampicin induces CYP3A4 in the liver and gut, plus P-glycoprotein. Induction is full in about 1 week and wears off over roughly 2 weeks after stopping, so levels of co-prescribed drugs rise again when rifampicin ends (Established) [[Niemi2003]].
- Oral midazolam, triazolam, simvastatin, verapamil and most dihydropyridine calcium-channel blockers become ineffective during rifampicin treatment. Combining rifampicin with indinavir, nelfinavir or saquinavir is contraindicated [[Niemi2003]].
- Rifampicin lowers methadone levels, which leads to opioid withdrawal symptoms in most patients. It also induces CYP2C, lowering (S)-warfarin and sulfonylurea levels, and it can cause acute transplant rejection in people taking ciclosporin [[Niemi2003]].
- The rifampin label warns that the reliability of oral and other systemic hormonal contraceptives may be affected and that alternative contraception should be considered [[FDA-Rifampin]]. The Priftin label gives the same warning for rifapentine, together with protease inhibitors and some reverse transcriptase inhibitors [[FDA-Priftin]].
- Interaction lists keep growing. Clinically relevant rifampin interactions reported between 2008 and 2012 included posaconazole, voriconazole, oxycodone and risperidone [[Baciewicz2013]].
- In INSPIRING, adults with HIV on rifampicin-based TB treatment took dolutegravir 50 mg twice daily during TB treatment and for 2 weeks after it. Week-48 viral response was 75% (52/69), against 82% with efavirenz, and the dolutegravir non-responses were driven by loss to follow-up [[Dooley2020CID]].
- In DOLPHIN, weekly rifapentine-isoniazid raised dolutegravir clearance by 36% and gave a trough-concentration ratio of 0.53. Viral load stayed below 40 copies/mL in all 60 participants, and the authors concluded 3HP can be given with dolutegravir without dose adjustment [[Dooley2020LancetHIV]].

### regimen · The 6-month standard (2HRZE/4HR)
- WHO recommendation 1.1: new patients with pulmonary TB should receive a regimen containing 6 months of rifampicin, 2HRZE/4HR (strong recommendation, high certainty). It also applies to extrapulmonary TB, except TB of the central nervous system, bone or joint, for which some expert groups suggest longer therapy [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- A meta-analysis of 57 RCTs (312 arms, 21,472 participants) found that regimens using rifampin only for the first 1 to 2 months had more failure, relapse and acquired resistance than 6-month rifampin regimens, especially when the strain was isoniazid-resistant at the start [[Menzies2009]].
- WHO's analysis of that dataset found that 5 to 7 months of rifampicin carried 0.43 times the failure rate and 0.32 times the relapse rate of 1 to 2 months. That works out to about 112 relapses averted per 1,000 people treated [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- Relapse with pansusceptible TB was 8.2% after 2-month rifampicin regimens and 3.1% after 6-month regimens. With isoniazid resistance at the start it was 38% and 5.5% [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- In Union Study A (1,355 adults with smear-positive TB), 12 months after treatment ended, unfavourable outcomes were 5% with 2EHRZ/4HR, against 10% with 2EHRZ/6HE and 14% with a thrice-weekly intensive phase plus 6HE [[Jindani2004]].
- WHO recommends daily dosing throughout and advises against thrice-weekly dosing in both phases (strong, high certainty; and conditional, very low certainty). In regression analysis, thrice-weekly dosing throughout carried 3.3 times the acquired-resistance rate of daily dosing [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- WHO recommends fixed-dose combination tablets over separate drugs (conditional, low certainty) [[WHO-Mod4-2025]]. In Study C (1,585 adults), favourable outcomes were 93.9% with a 4-drug FDC and 94.6% with separate drugs (per protocol; risk difference −0.7%, 90% CI −3.0 to 1.5) [[Lienhardt2011]].
- WHO recommends against extending the intensive phase when the sputum smear is still positive at the end of it (strong, high certainty) [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- ATS/CDC/IDSA 2016 recommends starting empiric multidrug treatment in almost all situations where active TB is suspected. It covers case management (including DOT), daily versus intermittent dosing, HIV co-treatment and extrapulmonary disease [[Nahid2016]].
- People living with HIV should receive at least the same duration of TB treatment as people without HIV, and ART should start as soon as possible within 2 weeks of starting TB treatment, whatever the CD4 count (WHO, strong recommendations) [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].

### regimen · Monitoring response: culture conversion at 2 months
- With daily dosing, 85% of patients (700/828) were culture-negative at 2 months, against 77% (333/433) with a thrice-weekly intensive phase (p=0.001) [[Jindani2004]].
- In Study 31, liquid-culture conversion by 8 weeks was 63.4% with the 6-month control, 78.5% with rifapentine-moxifloxacin and 74.2% with rifapentine alone [[Dorman2021]].
- A positive culture at 2 months predicts relapse poorly: pooled sensitivity 40% (95% CI 25 to 56) and specificity 85% (77 to 91). For a 2-month smear, sensitivity was 24% and specificity 85% [[Horne2010]].
- Faster culture conversion did not guarantee cure. Both rifapentine regimens in Study 31 converted cultures faster than control, but only the moxifloxacin-containing one was non-inferior [[Dorman2021]]. In REMoxTB, moxifloxacin regimens likewise cut bacterial load faster yet failed non-inferiority at 4 months [[Gillespie2014]].

### regimen · Shortening trials that failed (the background to Study 31)
- REMoxTB (1,931 patients): favourable outcomes per protocol were 85% when moxifloxacin replaced ethambutol and 80% when it replaced isoniazid, against 92% with control. Non-inferiority was not shown [[Gillespie2014]].
- OFLOTUB (1,836 patients; a 4-month gatifloxacin regimen): unfavourable outcomes were 21.0% against 17.2% with control (difference 3.5 points, 95% CI −0.7 to 7.7), and recurrence was 14.6% against 7.1% [[Merle2014]].
- RIFAQUIN (827 patients): a 4-month moxifloxacin plus twice-weekly rifapentine regimen had 18.2% unfavourable outcomes against 4.9% with control (per protocol). A 6-month weekly rifapentine 1,200 mg plus moxifloxacin regimen was non-inferior at 3.2% [[Jindani2014]].

### regimen · The 4-month rifapentine-moxifloxacin regimen (Study 31/A5349)
- Study 31 randomised 2,516 participants from 13 countries. In the microbiologically eligible population (n=2,343), unfavourable outcomes with rifapentine-moxifloxacin were 15.5% against 14.6% with the 6-month control (difference 1.0 point, 95% CI −2.6 to 4.5). The non-inferiority margin was 6.6 points [[Dorman2021]].
- Rifapentine without moxifloxacin was not non-inferior: 17.7% against 14.6% (difference 3.0 points, 95% CI −0.6 to 6.6) [[Dorman2021]].
- Grade 3 or higher adverse events on treatment were 19.3% with control, 18.8% with rifapentine-moxifloxacin and 14.3% with rifapentine [[Dorman2021]].
- The regimen is 8 weeks of daily isoniazid, rifapentine, moxifloxacin and pyrazinamide, then 9 weeks of daily isoniazid, rifapentine and moxifloxacin (2HPMZ/2HPM), with rifapentine 1,200 mg daily [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- WHO recommendation 2.1: people aged 12 years or older with drug-susceptible pulmonary TB may receive 2HPMZ/2HPM (conditional recommendation, moderate certainty). ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA adopted the same recommendation in 2025 [[WHO-Mod4-2025,Saukkonen2025]].
- WHO's evidence review found cure in 84.5% with the 4-month regimen and 85.4% with control (RR 0.99, 95% CI 0.95 to 1.03), and all-cause death within 14 days of the end of treatment in 0.4% and 0.8% [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- Groups left out of the WHO recommendation because the trial did not include them: people under 40 kg; people with TB meningitis, disseminated, osteoarticular or abdominal TB; people with HIV and CD4 below 100 cells/mm³; children under 12 years (the youngest participant was 13); and pregnant, breastfeeding or postpartum women [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- About 90% of the medicine cost of 2HPMZ/2HPM is the rifapentine. There is no fixed-dose combination, so the daily pill burden is higher than with the 6-month regimen [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA advises getting molecular, and possibly phenotypic, fluoroquinolone susceptibility testing before starting the regimen. It does not require a baseline ECG unless clinically indicated (older age, heart disease, prolonged QT or other QT-prolonging drugs) [[Saukkonen2025]].
- Grade 3 or higher bilirubin rises were more frequent on the rifapentine regimens (3.3% with rifapentine-moxifloxacin against 1.0% with control). Cases meeting Hy's law criteria were spread evenly across groups [[Dorman2021]].

### regimen · 4 months for children with non-severe TB (SHINE)
- SHINE randomised 1,204 children under 16 (median age 3.5 years; 11% with HIV) to 16 or 24 weeks of standard first-line treatment using WHO paediatric fixed-dose combinations. Primary events occurred in 16 children (3%) in the 4-month group and 18 (3%) in the 6-month group (adjusted difference −0.4 points, 95% CI −2.2 to 1.5) [[Turkova2022]].
- In SHINE, 95 children (8%) had a grade 3 or higher adverse event, including 15 adverse drug reactions (11 hepatic). All but 2 of the hepatic events happened in the first 8 weeks, when both groups were on the same treatment [[Turkova2022]].
- WHO recommendation 2.2: children and adolescents aged 3 months to 16 years with non-severe TB, without suspected or confirmed MDR/RR-TB, should receive 2HRZ(E)/2HR (strong, moderate certainty). ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA made the same strong recommendation in 2025 [[WHO-Mod4-2025,Saukkonen2025]].
- Non-severe TB means peripheral lymph node TB; intrathoracic lymph node TB without airway obstruction; uncomplicated TB pleural effusion; or paucibacillary, non-cavitary disease confined to one lobe without a miliary pattern. Children who do not meet these criteria receive 2HRZE/4HR or the regimens for severe extrapulmonary TB [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- WHO recommends ethambutol in the first 2 months in settings with high HIV prevalence or high isoniazid-resistance prevalence [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].

### regimen · TRUNCATE-TB: a treatment strategy, not a fixed regimen
- TRUNCATE-TB (674 participants, rifampin-susceptible pulmonary TB) tested starting with an 8-week regimen, extending it for persistent disease, monitoring after treatment and retreating any relapse. Primary events (death, ongoing treatment or active disease at week 96) were 3.9% (7/181) with standard treatment and 5.8% (11/189) with an initial bedaquiline-linezolid regimen. That strategy met non-inferiority (difference 0.8 points, 97.5% CI −3.4 to 5.1; margin 12) (Early evidence) [[Paton2023]].
- The strategy that started with high-dose rifampin plus linezolid did not meet non-inferiority: 11.4% (21/184), difference 7.4 points (97.5% CI 1.7 to 13.2) [[Paton2023]].
- Mean total treatment time was 180 days with standard treatment, 106 days with the rifampin-linezolid strategy and 85 days with the bedaquiline-linezolid strategy [[Paton2023]].
- Across the strategy groups, 17.0% of participants (range 12.7% to 22.8%) needed retreatment, against 3.3% with standard treatment [[Paton2023]].

### regimen · Meningitis, bone, pericardial TB and steroids
- WHO and ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA both say that TB of the central nervous system, bones or joints, miliary TB and pericardial TB were not covered by the 4-month trials. ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA adds that these would not usually be treated with a 6- or 4-month regimen [[WHO-Mod4-2025,Saukkonen2025]].
- In a Vietnamese trial in adults with TB meningitis, the standard regimen lasted 9 months. Intensifying the first 8 weeks with rifampin 15 mg/kg plus levofloxacin 20 mg/kg did not lower deaths: 113 against 114 deaths (HR 0.94, 95% CI 0.73 to 1.22) [[Heemskerk2016]].
- WHO recommendation 4.1: people with TB meningitis should receive initial adjuvant dexamethasone or prednisolone tapered over 6 to 8 weeks (strong, moderate certainty). For TB pericarditis, steroids may be used (conditional, very low certainty) [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- In 545 people over 14 with TB meningitis, dexamethasone lowered the risk of death at 9 months (RR 0.69, 95% CI 0.52 to 0.92) but not the risk of severe disability among survivors [[Thwaites2004]].
- A Cochrane review (9 trials, 1,337 participants) found that steroids cut deaths by almost a quarter at 3 to 18 months (RR 0.75, 95% CI 0.65 to 0.87; high-quality evidence). In the one trial with 5-year follow-up the effect was no longer apparent [[Prasad2016]].
- In IMPI (1,400 adults with TB pericarditis), prednisolone did not change the composite of death, tamponade or constriction (23.8% against 24.5%). It did lower constrictive pericarditis (4.4% against 7.8%) and raised cancer incidence (1.8% against 0.6%), mainly HIV-associated cancers [[Mayosi2014]].

### regimen · Supporting adherence: DOT, video and self-administered treatment
- A Cochrane review (11 trials, 5,662 participants) found that cure with self-administered treatment ranged from 41% to 67%. Direct observation did not substantially improve it (RR 1.08, 95% CI 0.91 to 1.27), nor treatment completion (RR 1.07, 95% CI 0.96 to 1.19) [[Karumbi2015]].
- A broader meta-analysis of 129 studies found self-administered treatment, compared with DOT, linked to lower treatment success (RCTs: RR 0.94, 95% CI 0.89 to 0.98; cohort studies: RR 0.81) and, in cohort studies, more acquired drug resistance (RR 4.19, 95% CI 2.34 to 7.49) [[Alipanah2018]].
- In an English trial of 226 people (58% with a history of homelessness, prison, drug or alcohol use or mental illness), 70% on smartphone video-observed treatment completed at least 80% of scheduled observations in the first 2 months, against 31% on DOT (adjusted OR 5.48, 95% CI 3.10 to 9.68) [[Story2019]].
- WHO conditionally recommends community- or home-based treatment support over facility-based or unsupported treatment. It also says video-supported treatment can replace in-person support when the technology can be run well [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA says DOT and integrated case management remain the standard of care. The 4-month rifapentine-moxifloxacin trial used in-person DOT at least 5 of 7 days a week [[Saukkonen2025,Dorman2021]].

### safety · Hepatotoxicity
- Reported rates of drug-induced liver injury during TB treatment range from 2% to 28%. Risk factors include older age, female sex, slow acetylator status, malnutrition, HIV and pre-existing liver disease [[Tostmann2008]].
- ATS hepatotoxicity statement: treatment should be interrupted, and usually a modified or alternative regimen used, when ALT is more than 3 times the upper limit of normal with symptoms or jaundice, or more than 5 times the upper limit without symptoms [[Saukkonen2006]].
- ATS advises ALT monitoring during TB infection treatment for people who drink alcohol regularly, take other liver-toxic drugs, have viral hepatitis or other liver disease, have had isoniazid hepatitis before, are pregnant or are within 3 months postpartum. During TB disease treatment, people with HIV are added, and some experts add everyone over 35 [[Saukkonen2006]].
- In a Montreal cohort of 430 patients, major adverse events per 100 person-months were 1.48 for pyrazinamide, 0.49 for isoniazid, 0.43 for rifampin and 0.07 for ethambutol. Pyrazinamide-induced hepatotoxicity and rash were higher than previously recognised [[Yee2003]].
- Any major side effect was more likely with age over 60 (adjusted HR 2.9), female sex (2.5), birth in Asia (2.5) and HIV (3.8) [[Yee2003]].
- The isoniazid label's boxed warning gives hepatitis rates by age: under 1 per 1,000 under age 20, 3 per 1,000 at 20 to 34, 12 at 35 to 49, 23 at 50 to 64 and 8 over 65. It advises monthly symptom review, baseline and periodic ALT/AST from age 35, and strongly considering stopping if enzymes exceed 3 to 5 times normal [[FDA-Isoniazid]].
- In 11,141 people starting isoniazid preventive therapy at a monitored public-health clinic, hepatotoxic reactions occurred in 0.10% of starters and 0.15% of completers. The risk rose with age [[Nolan1999]].
- After drug-induced hepatitis, reintroducing isoniazid, rifampicin and pyrazinamide all at full dose from day 1 led to recurrence at similar rates to two stepwise schemes: 19 of 175 patients overall (10.9%), P=0.69 between arms [[Sharma2010DILI]].

### safety · Other adverse effects of first-line drugs
- Ethambutol: in a meta-analysis, any visual impairment occurred in 22.5 per 1,000 people treated (95% CI 10.2 to 35) and permanent impairment in 4.3 per 1,000. At average doses of 27.5 mg/kg/day or less for 2 to 9 months, the figures were 19.2 and 2.3 per 1,000. Most episodes reversed after about 3 months [[Ezer2013]].
- The ethambutol label calls for visual acuity testing before treatment and periodically during it, monthly at doses above 15 mg/kg/day. It warns of optic neuritis, usually reversible, though irreversible blindness has been reported [[FDA-Ethambutol]].
- The US ethambutol label does not recommend use under 13 years and contraindicates it when a person cannot report visual changes. WHO and ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA nonetheless list ethambutol 20 (15 to 25) mg/kg for children [[FDA-Ethambutol,WHO-Mod4-2025,Saukkonen2025]].
- Pyrazinamide inhibits renal urate excretion and often causes hyperuricaemia, usually without symptoms. It is contraindicated in acute gout and should be stopped if hyperuricaemia comes with acute gouty arthritis [[FDA-Pyrazinamide]].
- Isoniazid can cause pyridoxine deficiency by competing with pyridoxal phosphate. Pre-existing neuropathy or conditions predisposing to it call for closer monitoring [[FDA-Isoniazid]]. ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA lists pyridoxine 25 to 50 mg/day with isoniazid [[Saukkonen2025]].
- Rifampin can turn teeth, urine, sweat, sputum and tears yellow, orange, red or brown, and can permanently stain soft contact lenses. People should be told about this in advance [[FDA-Rifampin]].
- Rifapentine can cause hypersensitivity reactions, including a flu-like syndrome, hypotension, urticaria, angioedema, bronchospasm, thrombocytopenia and neutropenia [[FDA-Priftin]].
- Moxifloxacin carries a boxed warning for tendinitis and tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy, CNS effects and worsening of myasthenia gravis. The label also lists QT prolongation and isolated torsade de pointes [[FDA-Moxifloxacin]].

### safety · Second-line drug toxicity (drug data; trials in pass F)
- Bedaquiline carries a boxed warning for QTc prolongation. The label says to monitor ECGs and stop the drug if significant ventricular arrhythmia or a QTc above 500 ms develops [[FDA-Sirturo]].
- The bedaquiline adult dose is 400 mg once daily for 2 weeks, then 200 mg three times a week (at least 48 hours apart) for 22 weeks, taken with food and given by DOT. Paediatric dosing by weight is labelled from age 2 and at least 8 kg [[FDA-Sirturo]].
- In the phase 2b C208 trial, bedaquiline shortened median time to culture conversion from 125 to 83 days and raised 120-week cure to 58% from 32%. There were 10 deaths with bedaquiline and 2 with placebo, with no causal pattern evident [[Diacon2014]].
- ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA calls for ECGs at baseline and at 2, 12 and 24 weeks on BPaL, and monthly when another QT-prolonging drug such as moxifloxacin is added [[Saukkonen2025]].
- Linezolid label: monitor blood counts weekly for myelosuppression. Peripheral and optic neuropathy have been reported, mainly with treatment longer than 28 days [[FDA-Zyvox]].
- In Nix-TB (109 patients; bedaquiline, pretomanid and linezolid 1,200 mg), 90% had a favourable outcome, but peripheral neuropathy occurred in 81% and myelosuppression in 48% [[Conradie2020]].
- In ZeNix, peripheral neuropathy with linezolid 1,200 mg for 26 weeks, 1,200 mg for 9 weeks, 600 mg for 26 weeks and 600 mg for 9 weeks was 38%, 24%, 24% and 13%. Myelosuppression was 22%, 15%, 2% and 7%, and favourable outcomes were 93%, 89%, 91% and 84% [[Conradie2022]].
- The pretomanid label now prefers linezolid 600 mg daily for 26 weeks, with 1,200 mg as the alternative. Linezolid is reduced or interrupted if myelosuppression, peripheral neuropathy or optic neuropathy develops [[FDA-Pretomanid]].
- In TB-PRACTECAL, grade 3 or higher or serious adverse events occurred in 19% on BPaLM against 59% on standard care [[Nyangwa2022]].

### tpt · Who gets TPT, and ruling out disease first
- WHO strongly recommends TPT for adults and adolescents living with HIV who are unlikely to have TB disease on clinical evaluation. That includes people on ART, pregnant women and people treated for TB before, whatever their immune status and even when testing for TB infection is not available [[WHO-Mod1-2024]].
- For HIV-negative household contacts aged 5 or over, having no TB symptoms and a normal chest X-ray may be used to rule out TB disease before TPT (conditional, very low certainty) [[WHO-Mod1-2024]].
- For close contacts under 15, screening should use a symptom screen (any of cough, fever or poor weight gain), a chest X-ray, or both (strong, moderate-to-low certainty) [[WHO-Mod1-2024]].
- Ruling out disease matters: in VQUIN, 61 of 3,948 people screened (1.5%) already had TB disease before randomisation [[Fox2024VQUIN]].

### tpt · Regimens and recommendations
- WHO 2024 recommendation 19: 6 or 9 months of daily isoniazid, 3 months of weekly rifapentine plus isoniazid (3HP), or 3 months of daily isoniazid plus rifampicin (3HR), regardless of HIV status (strong, moderate-to-high certainty) [[WHO-Mod1-2024]].
- WHO 2024 recommendation 20: 1 month of daily rifapentine plus isoniazid (1HP) or 4 months of daily rifampicin (4R) may be used as alternatives regardless of HIV status (conditional, low-to-moderate certainty) [[WHO-Mod1-2024]].
- NTCA/CDC 2020 prefers three rifamycin regimens: 3HP (strong, moderate certainty), 4R (strong, moderate certainty, HIV-negative) and 3HR (conditional, very low certainty). Daily isoniazid for 6 months (strong) or 9 months (conditional) is the alternative [[Sterling2020]].
- NTCA/CDC published doses: 3HP is 12 weekly doses. Isoniazid is 15 mg/kg (rounded up, maximum 900 mg) at age 12 and over, or 25 mg/kg (maximum 900 mg) at ages 2 to 11. Rifapentine goes by weight: 300 mg at 10 to 14 kg, 450 mg at 14.1 to 25 kg, 600 mg at 25.1 to 32 kg, 750 mg at 32.1 to 49.9 kg and 900 mg maximum at 50 kg and over [[Sterling2020]].
- NTCA/CDC published doses: 4R is 120 daily doses, at 10 mg/kg in adults and 15 to 20 mg/kg in children, maximum 600 mg. 6H is 180 daily doses at 5 mg/kg (adults) or 10 to 20 mg/kg (children), maximum 300 mg [[Sterling2020]].
- NTCA/CDC warns that rifampin and rifapentine are not interchangeable, and that pharmacists and prescribers unfamiliar with them may confuse the two [[Sterling2020]].
- The Priftin label approves 3HP from age 2 for people at high risk of progression. It is not recommended when exposure to rifamycin- or isoniazid-resistant TB is presumed [[FDA-Priftin]].

### tpt · Efficacy, completion and safety in trials
- Isoniazid against placebo (11 trials, 73,375 people without HIV) gave a relative risk of TB of 0.40 (95% CI 0.31 to 0.52) over 2 years or more. Six months (RR 0.44) and 12 months (RR 0.38) did not differ significantly. Hepatotoxicity was 0.36% with 6 months and 0.52% with 12 months [[Smieja2000]].
- In the IUAT trial (28,000 people with fibrotic lung lesions, 5-year follow-up), 12 weeks of isoniazid removed less than a third of TB risk and 24 weeks removed two-thirds. Fifty-two weeks prevented the most TB, but 24 weeks prevented more cases per case of hepatitis caused. Hepatitis occurred in 0.5% on isoniazid against 0.1% on placebo [[IUAT1982]].
- A network meta-analysis found the following odds ratios for TB against placebo: 6 months of isoniazid 0.65, 12 to 72 months of isoniazid 0.50, rifampicin alone 0.41, 3 to 4 months of rifampicin-isoniazid 0.53, and weekly rifapentine-isoniazid against no treatment 0.36 [[Zenner2017]].
- PREVENT TB (3HP by DOT against 9 months of self-administered isoniazid): TB developed in 7 of 3,986 (0.19%) against 15 of 3,745 (0.43%). Completion was 82.1% against 69.0%. Hepatotoxicity was 0.4% against 2.7%, but stopping for adverse events was 4.9% against 3.7% [[Sterling2011]].
- In the paediatric cohort of PREVENT TB (ages 2 to 17), no TB occurred among 471 children on 3HP against 3 of 434 on 9H. Completion was 88.1% against 80.9%, and no hepatotoxicity occurred in either arm [[Villarino2015]].
- Systemic drug reactions occurred in 3.5% on 3HP against 0.4% on 9H, mostly flu-like. They began after a median of 3 doses, about 4 hours after a dose, and resolved in a median of 24 hours. Severe reactions occurred in 0.3%, and there were no deaths [[Sterling2015]].
- In iAdhere, completion of 3HP was 87.2% with direct observation, 74.0% self-administered and 76.4% self-administered with text reminders. In the US subgroup, self-administration was non-inferior [[Belknap2017]].
- 4R against 9H in adults (6,859 people, 9 countries): confirmed TB rates differed by less than 0.01 cases per 100 person-years, which met non-inferiority. Completion was 15.1 points higher with 4R, and grade 3 to 5 hepatotoxic events were 1.2 points lower [[Menzies2018]].
- 4R against 9H in children (829 analysed): completion 85.3% against 76.4%. TB occurred in 0 of 422 against 2 of 407 (rate difference −0.37 per 100 person-years, 95% CI −0.88 to 0.14) [[Diallo2018]].
- Across rifamycin trials, rifampicin caused less hepatotoxicity than isoniazid (RR 0.12, 95% CI 0.05 to 0.30). Rifampicin plus pyrazinamide caused more hepatotoxicity (RR 4.59) [[Sharma2013Cochrane]].
- BRIEF TB (3,000 people with HIV; median CD4 470): TB or death was 0.65 per 100 person-years with 1HP and 0.67 with 9H. Completion was 97% against 90%, and serious adverse events were 6% against 7% [[Swindells2019]].
- In South African adults with HIV not on ART (1,148 people), rates of TB or death were 3.1 (3HP), 2.9 (3HR) and 2.7 (continuous isoniazid) per 100 person-years, against 3.6 with 6 months of isoniazid. None was superior [[Martinson2011]].
- In Botswana, 36 months of isoniazid lowered TB incidence compared with 6 months: 0.72% against 1.26% a year (HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.33 to 0.99). The benefit was seen in people with a positive tuberculin skin test (HR 0.26), not in TST-negative people [[Samandari2011]].
- In Temprano (Côte d'Ivoire; 2,056 adults with HIV, mean CD4 477), 6 months of isoniazid lowered 6-year mortality from 6.9% to 4.1% (adjusted HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.41 to 0.97) [[Badje2017]].

### tpt · Contacts of people with MDR/RR-TB
- WHO 2024 recommendation 21: contacts exposed to MDR/RR-TB should receive 6 months of daily levofloxacin (strong, moderate certainty) [[WHO-Mod1-2024]].
- VQUIN (Vietnam, 2,041 household contacts): confirmed TB occurred in 0.6% on levofloxacin against 1.1% on placebo (IRR 0.55, 95% CI 0.19 to 1.62; not significant). Adverse events of any grade were 31.9% against 13.0% [[Fox2024VQUIN]].
- TB-CHAMP (South Africa, 922 children, 91% under 5): TB by week 48 was 1.1% on levofloxacin against 2.6% on placebo (HR 0.44, 95% CI 0.15 to 1.25; not significant). Grade 3 or higher related adverse events were 4 against 8 [[Hesseling2024]].
- Pooled by WHO, 6 months of levofloxacin cut TB risk by 62% over 1 year (RR 0.38, 95% CI 0.17 to 0.86). Completion was 86% in TB-CHAMP and 70% in VQUIN, and tolerability fell with age [[WHO-Mod1-2024]].

- NNT = 1 ÷ (baseline risk × (1 − RR)). Using the isoniazid RR of 0.40 [[Smieja2000]], a person with a 5% baseline risk gives about 1 ÷ (0.05 × 0.60) ≈ 33 treated to prevent one case. At 1% baseline risk it is about 167. Calculated here, not reported by the source.
- In Botswana, the absolute difference between 6 and 36 months of isoniazid was 3.4% − 2.0% = 1.4 points over the trial, about 71 people given extended isoniazid to prevent one case. Calculated here from [[Samandari2011]].

### Exam hooks
- Isoniazid is a prodrug activated by catalase-peroxidase (KatG). Its target InhA sits in mycolic acid synthesis, so katG mutations cause high-level resistance and inhA mutations also cause ethionamide resistance [[Zhang1992katG,Banerjee1994]].
- Rifampicin blocks the RNA polymerase β subunit [[Campbell2001Rif]]. Pyrazinamide needs pyrazinamidase (pncA) and acid pH [[Zhang2003PZA]]. Ethambutol blocks arabinan synthesis via embCAB [[Telenti1997]].
- Isoniazid is the main early bactericidal drug, and rifampicin is the key sterilising drug [[Jindani2003]].
- 2HRZE/4HR means 2 months of four drugs, then 4 months of isoniazid plus rifampicin, given daily (WHO, strong, high certainty) [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- 2HPMZ/2HPM is for ages 12 and over, weight at least 40 kg, and not for CD4 below 100, pregnancy, or meningitis, bone or disseminated TB [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- SHINE: 4 months is enough for children aged 3 months to 16 years with non-severe TB (3% events in both arms) [[Turkova2022,WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- Rifampin goes on an empty stomach. Rifapentine goes with food [[FDA-Rifampin,FDA-Priftin]].
- Rifampicin is a strong CYP3A4 inducer: hormonal contraception fails, methadone withdrawal follows, and warfarin and protease inhibitor levels fall [[Niemi2003,FDA-Rifampin]].
- ATS stop rule: ALT more than 3 times normal with symptoms, or more than 5 times normal without [[Saukkonen2006]].
- Pyrazinamide causes the most hepatotoxicity and rash of the first-line drugs (1.48 major events per 100 person-months) [[Yee2003]].
- Ethambutol causes optic neuritis, so check visual acuity and colour vision [[FDA-Ethambutol,Ezer2013]].
- Isoniazid causes peripheral neuropathy, so give pyridoxine (B6) [[FDA-Isoniazid,Saukkonen2025]].
- A 2-month positive culture is specific but insensitive for relapse (sensitivity 40%) [[Horne2010]].
- 3HP is 12 weekly doses. Watch for a flu-like reaction (3.5%), and expect less liver injury than 9H [[Sterling2011,Sterling2015]].
- 4R is non-inferior to 9H with better completion and less hepatotoxicity [[Menzies2018]].
- Contacts of people with MDR/RR-TB get 6 months of levofloxacin (WHO 2024, strong) [[WHO-Mod1-2024]].
- Steroids reduce deaths in TB meningitis (RR 0.75) [[Prasad2016]].
- Bedaquiline means QT monitoring. Linezolid means myelosuppression and neuropathy [[FDA-Sirturo,FDA-Zyvox]].

### In plain words
- TB disease is treated with several medicines at once, because one medicine alone lets the germs become resistant.
- The usual treatment is 6 months: four medicines for 2 months, then two medicines for 4 months.
- Some people aged 12 and over can take a 4-month treatment instead, and many children with mild TB need only 4 months.
- You will usually start to feel better within weeks, but stopping early can bring TB back. Finishing the full course is what cures it.
- Rifampicin turns urine, sweat and tears orange. This is expected and harmless, but it can stain soft contact lenses.
- Rifampicin can stop birth-control pills from working and can change how many other medicines work. Tell every doctor and pharmacist that you take it.
- Tell your TB team straight away if you have yellow eyes or skin, dark urine, stomach pain, or lose your appetite.
- Tell your TB team about blurry vision or trouble telling red from green, or about tingling or burning in your hands or feet.
- Vitamin B6 is often given with isoniazid to protect the nerves.
- A health worker may watch you take your medicine, in person or by video. This is support, not suspicion.
- If you have TB infection but not TB disease, a short course of medicine (as little as 1 to 4 months) can stop it from turning into illness.
- Before preventive treatment, the team checks that you do not already have TB disease, because that needs full treatment.
- In most countries, TB testing and treatment are free through the national TB programme.
- TB is a notifiable disease. Anyone with a cough for more than 2 weeks, fever, night sweats or weight loss should see a clinician.

## Open questions and controversies
- Does DOT improve cure? A Cochrane review found no substantial benefit over self-administration (cure RR 1.08, 95% CI 0.91 to 1.27) [[Karumbi2015]]. A larger meta-analysis that included cohort studies linked self-administration to lower success and 4-fold more acquired resistance [[Alipanah2018]]. WHO now favours community or home-based support and accepts video support [[WHO-Mod4-2025]]. ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA keeps DOT as standard of care [[Saukkonen2025]]. Status: unresolved; practice is moving toward person-centred support.
- The 4-month adult regimen versus the 6-month standard: Study 31 showed non-inferiority [[Dorman2021]]. WHO made only a conditional recommendation because of cost (90% from rifapentine), pill burden, the need for fluoroquinolone testing and untested groups [[WHO-Mod4-2025]]. Status: an option in WHO and US guidelines, uptake limited by access.
- Treat everyone for 6 months, or use a strategy approach? TRUNCATE-TB's bedaquiline-linezolid strategy cut mean treatment to 85 days but needed retreatment in 12.7% to 22.8% of strategy participants (Early evidence) [[Paton2023]]. It also spends bedaquiline and linezolid, which are core drugs for drug-resistant TB. Status: not in WHO Module 4 (2025) recommendations; programme use unclear.
- Is the standard rifampicin dose too low? Rifampicin up to 35 mg/kg for 2 weeks was well tolerated, and exposure rose more than proportionally with dose (Early evidence) [[Boeree2015]]. In TB meningitis, 15 mg/kg plus levofloxacin did not improve survival [[Heemskerk2016]]. Status: under study.
- Should isoniazid be dosed by NAT2 genotype? One RCT in 172 Japanese patients showed less liver injury and fewer early failures (Early evidence) [[Azuma2013]]. Status: not in WHO or US guidance.
- Ethambutol in young children: the US label does not recommend it under 13 years [[FDA-Ethambutol]]. WHO and ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA dose it at 20 mg/kg and advise including it where HIV or isoniazid resistance is common [[WHO-Mod4-2025,Saukkonen2025]]. Status: guideline practice favours use, with vision monitoring where possible.
- Levofloxacin for MDR contacts: neither VQUIN nor TB-CHAMP was significant alone [[Fox2024VQUIN,Hesseling2024]]. The WHO pooled estimate was significant (RR 0.38) and led to a strong recommendation [[WHO-Mod1-2024]]. Tolerability in adults remains a concern (70% completion in VQUIN). Status: recommended by WHO since 2024.
- 1HP versus 3HP: there is no direct comparison of efficacy. BRIEF TB compared 1HP with 9H only in people with HIV [[Swindells2019]]. Status: WHO lists 1HP as an alternative (conditional) [[WHO-Mod1-2024]].
- Extended isoniazid in HIV: 36 months beat 6 months in Botswana, mainly in TST-positive people [[Samandari2011]]. WHO's 2024 edition withdrew the conditional recommendation for at least 36 months of isoniazid in people with HIV in high-transmission settings, calling it obsolete as shorter rifamycin regimens spread [[WHO-Mod1-2024]]. Status: shorter rifamycin TPT is preferred.

## Provenance
- Validation: all 59 journal sources were checked against PubMed esummary records (authors, title, journal, year, volume, pages, PMID, PMCID, DOI), and abstracts were read through Europe PMC or PubMed efetch. Both WHO guidelines were confirmed as Europe PMC/NCBI Bookshelf records and read in full from the WHO IRIS PDFs. All 9 US labels were retrieved from the openFDA drug label endpoint on 2026-10-25, and the set_ids and effective dates are given.
- Full texts read: Saukkonen2025 (Table 1 doses, monitoring), Sterling2020 (Tables 3 and 4), Dorman2021 (methods, culture conversion, hepatic events), Turkova2022 and Paton2023 (Europe PMC/PMC author versions). The rest were read at abstract level.
- Not accessible: Nahid2016 full text (CID; Oxford Academic and the PMC mirrors returned a bot check, which I did not try to get past). It is cited only for statements in its abstract, so its dose table and its durations for meningitis and bone TB are not quoted. The same bot check blocked the NCBI Bookshelf HTML of the WHO guidelines, so I used the IRIS PDFs.
- Not reviewed: WHO Module 4, 2nd edition (2026-09-21; PMID 42866503). Builders should check whether recommendations 1.1 to 4.2 changed.
- Dropped: Fox W et al. 1999 (BMRC review, IJTLD; PMID 10529902), because PubMed has no abstract and I could not read it. Jindani 1980 (EBA; PMID 6774638), for the same reason. The WHO TPT operational handbook (2024) was read but not cited; the withdrawal of the 36-month isoniazid recommendation is cited to WHO-Mod1-2024, which states it directly.
- The rifampin label used is a generic US label. The openFDA "Rifadin" brand result returned a non-US (infusion) patient leaflet, which I did not use.
- The NNT lines are my own arithmetic from the cited trial figures and are marked as calculated. They are not values reported by the sources.
- Not covered here (other passes): drug-resistant regimen trials in depth (F), global treatment outcome statistics (A/F), adherence and nursing practice detail (G), and diagnosis, including tests used to rule out disease before TPT (D).
