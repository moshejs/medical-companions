# H · Vaccines, the End TB Strategy & research

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: bcg, frontier.

## Findings

### bcg · History, strains and how BCG is given
- BCG (bacille Calmette-Guérin) is a live attenuated *Mycobacterium bovis* strain first given to a newborn baby in Paris in 1921; it has been given to more humans than any other vaccine and remains the only licensed TB vaccine [[Lange2022]].
- BCG provides long-lasting strong protection against miliary and meningeal TB in children but is less effective against pulmonary TB, especially in adults [[Lange2022]].
- Comparative genomics found 11 regions (91 open reading frames) of *M. tuberculosis* H37Rv absent from one or more virulent *M. bovis* strains, and 5 further regions (38 open reading frames) present in *M. bovis* but missing from some or all BCG strains, evidence that BCG daughter strains kept evolving after their original derivation [[Behr1999]].
- In the randomised-trial meta-regression there was no evidence that BCG efficacy was associated with the BCG strain used (Established) [[Mangtani2014,Abubakar2013]].
- The WHO February 2018 position paper replaced the 2004 BCG position paper and the 2007 guidance for infants at risk of HIV. It gives revised guidance on immunising children with HIV, re-emphasises the importance of the birth dose, and includes recommendations for leprosy prevention [[WHO2018BCG]].
- WHO's GTR 2025 states that BCG vaccination is recommended as part of national childhood immunisation programmes, in line with a country's TB epidemiology [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- In low-incidence countries such as Sweden, general BCG vaccination has ended and targeted BCG is offered to infants at higher risk [[Kaufmann2026]].

### bcg · Efficacy against severe childhood TB (meningitis, miliary)
- Trunz 2006 estimated that the 100.5 million BCG doses given to infants in 2002 would prevent 29,729 cases of TB meningitis (5th–95th centiles 24,063–36,192) in the first 5 years of life, one case per 3,435 vaccinations (2,771–4,177) [[Trunz2006]].
- The same model estimated 11,486 miliary TB cases prevented (7,304–16,280), one per 9,314 vaccinations (6,172–13,729) [[Trunz2006]].
- Of the cases prevented, 46% would be in South-East Asia, 27% in sub-Saharan Africa and 15% in the Western Pacific [[Trunz2006]].
- At US$2–3 per dose, BCG cost US$206 (150–272) per year of healthy life gained, a highly cost-effective intervention against severe childhood TB in high-incidence countries [[Trunz2006]].
- In randomised trials, protection against meningeal and miliary TB was high in infants (RR 0.1; 95% CI 0.01–0.77) and in children stringently tuberculin-tested before vaccination (RR 0.08; 0.03–0.25) [[Mangtani2014]].
- Colditz 1994: five studies reporting meningitis showed 64% protection (OR 0.36; 95% CI 0.18–0.70), and seven trials reporting TB deaths showed 71% protection (RR 0.29; 0.16–0.53) [[Colditz1994]].
- In newborns and infants (Colditz 1995), protection was 64% against TB meningitis (95% CI 30–82%) and 78% against disseminated TB (58–88%) [[Colditz1995]].

### bcg · Efficacy against pulmonary TB: age, prior sensitisation and latitude
- Colditz 1994 (14 prospective trials, 12 case-control studies): in trials the RR of TB was 0.49 (95% CI 0.34–0.70), a 51% protective effect; in case-control studies the OR was 0.50 (0.39–0.64) [[Colditz1994]].
- Geographic latitude of the study site and study validity score explained 66% of the heterogeneity among trials [[Colditz1994]].
- Colditz 1995: in four randomised trials of newborn/infant vaccination, protection against TB cases was 74% (95% CI 62–83%); in nine case-control studies, 52% (38–64%) [[Colditz1995]].
- Laboratory-confirmed TB in three infant case-control studies showed 83% protection (58–93%) [[Colditz1995]].
- Mangtani 2014 (18 trials of pulmonary TB): protection was greatest in children stringently tuberculin-tested (RR 0.26; 0.18–0.37) and infants (RR 0.41; 0.29–0.58) [[Mangtani2014]].
- Protection was weaker in children not stringently tested (RR 0.59; 0.35–1.01) and absent in older individuals, whether stringently tested (RR 0.88; 0.59–1.31) or not (RR 0.81; 0.55–1.22) [[Mangtani2014]].
- Protection was higher in trials further from the equator, where environmental mycobacteria are less common, and in trials with less risk of detection bias (Established) [[Mangtani2014]].
- Adult pulmonary TB protection ranged from strong in the UK MRC trial (rate ratio 0.22; 0.16–0.31) to no clinically important benefit in the large Chingleput trial in India (rate ratio 1.05; 0.88–1.25) [[Abubakar2013]].
- Failure to exclude people already sensitised to mycobacteria and study sites closer to the equator explained most of the variation in BCG efficacy [[Abubakar2013]].
- One leading hypothesis is that prior exposure to environmental mycobacteria either masks or blocks BCG's effect (Hypothesis) [[Fine1995,Mangtani2014]].

### bcg · Duration of protection
- BCG protects against pulmonary and extrapulmonary TB for up to 10 years; protection declines with time [[Abubakar2013]].
- Five studies (one trial, four observational) showed measurable protection at least 15 years after vaccination; data beyond 15 years are limited [[Abubakar2013]].
- Waning was faster at latitudes further from the equator and where BCG was given after stringent tuberculin testing [[Abubakar2013]].
- Most evidence from infant vaccination suggests efficacy may persist through 10 years [[Colditz1995]].

### bcg · Protection against infection (not only disease)
- Roy 2014 (14 studies, 3,855 children with recent exposure, IGRA-tested): BCG-vaccinated children had a risk ratio for infection of 0.81 (95% CI 0.71–0.92), 19% protection [[Roy2014]].
- In the six studies that also captured TB disease (n=1,745), protection against infection was 27% (RR 0.73; 0.61–0.87) and against active TB 71% (RR 0.29; 0.15–0.58) [[Roy2014]].
- Among infected children, BCG protected 58% against progression to disease (RR 0.42; 0.23–0.77) [[Roy2014]].

### bcg · Revaccination
- C-040-404 (phase 2, 990 BCG-primed, QFT-negative, HIV-negative South African adolescents): initial QFT conversion was 13.1% after BCG revaccination, 14.3% after H4:IC31 and 15.8% after placebo; neither vaccine prevented initial conversion (efficacy 20.1%, P=0.29 for BCG; 9.4%, P=0.63 for H4:IC31) [[Nemes2018]].
- In that trial, sustained QFT conversion was 6.7% with BCG vs 11.6% with placebo, a vaccine efficacy of 45.4% (P=0.03); H4:IC31 efficacy was 30.5% (P=0.16) [[Nemes2018]].
- BCG REVAX (phase 2b, NCT04152161; 1,836 QFT-negative, HIV-negative adolescents randomised): after a median 30 months, sustained QFT conversion occurred in 62/871 BCG vs 59/849 placebo recipients; HR 1.04 (0.73–1.48), vaccine efficacy −3.8% (−48.3 to 27.4) [[Schmidt2025]].
- BCG REVAX concluded that BCG revaccination in QFT-negative adolescents did not protect from sustained infection; adverse events, mostly injection-site pain, redness, swelling and ulceration, were more frequent with BCG [[Schmidt2025]].
- A phase 3 trial in India (BRiC, NCT05330884) comparing BCG revaccination with chemoprophylaxis in household contacts aged 6–18 years is listed as suspended for "changes in protocol" (Early evidence: no result) [[NCT05330884]].

### bcg · Adverse effects and disseminated BCG
- In a 12-month cohort of 2,435 children vaccinated with BCG-SSI in France, 17.8% had an adverse event: erythema 12.4%, induration 12.2%, abscess 2.5%, ulceration 0.9% and lymphadenitis 0.1% [[Dommergues2009]].
- Adverse events were less likely when BCG was given before age 1 year (OR 0.35 for age under 28 days vs over 1 year), when a visible papule formed, and with a low vaccine dose; reactions were frequent but rarely severe [[Dommergues2009]].
- In a Western Cape hospital series of 25 children with BCG disease, 22 (88%) had local disease and 8 (32%) had distant or disseminated disease; all 8 with disseminated disease were immunodeficient (6 with HIV) and 75% of them died [[Hesseling2006CID]].
- The estimated risk of disseminated BCG disease in infants with HIV was 110–417 per 100,000 vaccinees depending on assumed vertical HIV transmission, several hundred-fold higher than in infants without HIV [[Hesseling2007Vaccine]].
- Multicentre surveillance (2004–2006) gave a pooled incidence of disseminated BCG disease of 992 (95% CI 567–1,495) per 100,000 BCG-vaccinated infants with HIV, higher than earlier estimates [[Hesseling2009BWHO]].
- As a live vaccine, BCG can cause localised or disseminated infection in immunocompromised hosts [[Lange2022]].

### bcg · Non-specific effects and trained immunity
- BCG vaccination of healthy volunteers increased monocyte release of TNF and IL-1β about twofold in response to unrelated pathogens for at least 3 months, through NOD2 and histone H3K4 trimethylation ("trained immunity") (Early evidence) [[Kleinnijenhuis2012]].
- In a Guinea-Bissau trial of low-birth-weight infants, early BCG-Denmark gave a non-significant reduction in neonatal mortality (MRR 0.70; 0.47–1.04); a meta-analysis of three trials showed 38% lower neonatal mortality (MRR 0.62; 0.46–0.83) (Early evidence) [[BieringSorensen2017]].

### bcg · Intravenous BCG in macaques
- In rhesus macaques, intravenous BCG induced far more antigen-responsive CD4 and CD8 T cells in blood, spleen, airways and lung lymph nodes than intradermal or aerosol BCG [[Darrah2020]].
- After challenge with virulent *M. tuberculosis* 6 months later, 9 of 10 intravenously vaccinated macaques were highly protected, and 6 had no detectable infection (Early evidence: animal data) [[Darrah2020]].
- In SIV-infected macaques (a model of HIV), intravenous BCG protected 9 of 12 vaccinated animals and all 7 vaccinated SIV-naive animals, with no culturable bacteria in tissues (Early evidence: animal data) [[Larson2023]].

### frontier · The vaccine pipeline (status August 2025 – 2026)
- As of August 2025, 18 TB vaccine candidates were in clinical development (up from 15 in 2024): four in phase 1, eight in phase 2 and six in phase 3 [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- The six phase 3 candidates are M72/AS01E, MTBVAC, VPM1002, GamTBvac, Immuvac (MIP) and BCG revaccination trials, the most diverse and advanced pipeline since BCG was introduced in 1921 [[daCosta2027]].
- Platforms include live-attenuated mycobacteria (MTBVAC), recombinant BCG (VPM1002), protein-adjuvant subunits (M72/AS01E), viral vectors (e.g., ChAdOx1.85A) and inactivated whole-cell vaccines (DAR-901, RUTI, Immuvac) [[daCosta2027]].
- Five years after the 2021 global TB vaccine roadmap, several candidates are in phase 3, but the number in active trials is small and investment remains "risky and inadequate", hampered by unclear demand and insufficient committed procurement [[vanRiet2026]].
- MTBVAC is described as the leading candidate to replace BCG in infants, and M72/AS01E as the frontrunner for adolescents and adults (expert opinion) [[Vashishtha2026]].

### frontier · M72/AS01E
- Phase 2b (NCT01755598; 3,575 IGRA-positive, HIV-negative adults aged 18–50 in Kenya, South Africa and Zambia; two doses 1 month apart): 13/1,626 vaccine vs 26/1,663 placebo recipients developed bacteriologically confirmed pulmonary TB (0.3 vs 0.6 cases per 100 person-years) [[Tait2019]].
- Vaccine efficacy at month 36 was 49.7% (90% CI 12.1–71.2; 95% CI 2.1–74.2); an earlier analysis had reported 54.0% (Early evidence: a single phase 2b trial) [[Tait2019]].
- M72-specific antibodies and CD4 T cells rose after the first dose and stayed raised through 36 months; serious adverse events and deaths were similar between groups [[Tait2019]].
- A phase 3 trial (NCT06062238, Gates Medical Research Institute) began in March 2024 with a planned 20,080 adolescents and adults in South Africa, Kenya, Zambia, Malawi and Indonesia, in IGRA-positive, IGRA-negative and HIV cohorts; primary completion is listed for April 2028 [[NCT06062238]].
- Simulations for the phase 3 design assumed 9,000 IGRA-positive participants per arm, 0.4% TB incidence per year and a true efficacy of 55%; 110 events would rule out efficacy ≤10% within about 3.5–4 years [[Dagnew2026]].
- A standalone efficacy trial in IGRA-negative people would need about 134,800 participants and was judged infeasible, so the primary objective is confirming efficacy in IGRA-positive, HIV-negative people [[Dagnew2026]].

### frontier · MTBVAC
- MTBVAC is a live-attenuated *M. tuberculosis* vaccine. In a phase 2a infant trial (99 HIV-unexposed, BCG-naive South African infants), all doses were immunogenic; the 2.5 × 10^5 CFU dose was less reactogenic and more immunogenic than BCG and was chosen for phase 3 [[Tameris2025]].
- In that infant trial, IGRA conversion at day 56 occurred in 87.4% of MTBVAC recipients, but only 42.4% remained positive at day 365, which complicates the use of IGRA as a trial endpoint [[Tameris2025]].
- In BCG-vaccinated South African adults (phase 1b-2a, n=143), MTBVAC 5 × 10^5 CFU had safety and reactogenicity similar to BCG revaccination and greater immunogenicity; QFT-positive recipients had more injection-site reactions (96% vs 67%) [[Luabeya2025]].
- A phase 3 infant efficacy trial (MTBVACN3, NCT04975178) comparing MTBVAC with BCG in HIV-unexposed and HIV-exposed uninfected newborns in sub-Saharan Africa is recruiting (planned n=7,120; primary completion February 2029) (Early evidence: no efficacy result yet) [[NCT04975178]].
- A phase 2b placebo-controlled efficacy trial in adolescents and adults aged 14–45 years (NCT06272812, planned n=5,500) is active, not recruiting (Early evidence) [[NCT06272812]].

### frontier · VPM1002 and Immuvac
- VPM1002 is a recombinant BCG. In a phase 3 non-inferiority trial (NCT04351685; 6,940 newborns in sub-Saharan Africa), QFT conversion occurred in 5.3% with VPM1002 vs 4.3% with BCG; HR 1.23 (95% CI 0.99–1.53), so VPM1002 was not non-inferior [[Nduba2026]].
- That trial accrued only 334 of 632 planned infection events and stopped early in October 2024; the authors note that IGRA-defined infection endpoints are hard to use in infant vaccine trials [[Nduba2026]].
- PreVenTB (India; 12,717 household contacts aged ≥6 years): TB developed in 1.68% with VPM1002, 2.09% with Immuvac and 2.13% with placebo; VPM1002 efficacy was 21.4% (−8.9% to 43.2%) against all TB and 50.4% (0.8% to 75.2%) against extrapulmonary TB [[Singh2026BMJ]].
- PreVenTB concluded that neither vaccine was efficacious against all microbiologically confirmed TB or pulmonary TB; a post hoc analysis in children aged 6–14 suggested 64.6% efficacy for VPM1002 (Hypothesis-generating) [[Singh2026BMJ]].

### frontier · What new vaccines could do (modelling)
- In a modelling study across low- and middle-income countries for 2028–2050, an adolescent/adult vaccine was projected to avert US$38.9 billion in patient-incurred costs and spare 22.9 million households catastrophic costs; an infant vaccine, US$5.9 billion and 3.7 million households (Hypothesis: model projection) [[Portnoy2023BMJGH]].
- In that model, the poorest two income quintiles accounted for 56% of TB cases averted, so new vaccines would reduce income-based inequalities [[Portnoy2023BMJGH]].
- The End TB Strategy's 2035 targets can only be met with technological breakthroughs such as a new TB vaccine [[WHO-GTR2025]].

### frontier · End TB Strategy targets and 2024 progress
- The End TB Strategy was adopted by all WHO Member States in 2014. Its 2035 targets are a 90% reduction in the TB incidence rate and a 95% reduction in TB deaths compared with 2015 [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- The milestones were a 20% fall in incidence and a 35% fall in deaths by 2020, and a 50% fall in incidence and a 75% fall in deaths by 2025, all vs 2015 [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- The third high-level target is that no TB-affected households face catastrophic total costs, defined as costs above 20% of annual household income or expenditure [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Globally, the TB incidence rate fell by a net 12.3% between 2015 and 2024, far from the 50% milestone for 2025 [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- TB deaths fell by a net 29% between 2015 and 2024, far from the 75% milestone for 2025 [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- In 2024 an estimated 10.7 million people (95% UI 9.9–11.5 million) fell ill with TB, 131 per 100,000 population, and about 1.23 million died [[WHO-GTR2025,Zenner2027]].
- Between 2023 and 2024 the incidence rate fell by 1.7% and deaths by about 3% [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- The European Region had the largest fall in incidence (39%) and deaths (49%), and the African Region reduced incidence by 28% and deaths by 46% (2015–2024); incidence rose in the Americas (+13%) and the Western Pacific (+1.7%) [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- 101 countries passed the 2020 incidence milestone (≥20% reduction) and 65 countries passed the 2020 mortality milestone (≥35%); South Africa is estimated to have reached the 2025 incidence milestone [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- In 2024, 8.3 million people were newly diagnosed and notified (about 78% of estimated cases); rapid-test coverage rose from 48% to 54% [[WHO-GTR2025,Zenner2027]].
- Treatment success was 88% for drug-susceptible TB and 71% for drug-resistant TB in the latest cohorts; WHO estimates timely treatment has saved 83 million lives since 2000 (population statistics) [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- In 2024, 5.3 million people at high risk received TB preventive treatment (up from 4.7 million in 2023), including 3.5 million household contacts and 1.8 million people with HIV [[WHO-GTR2025,daCosta2027]].

### frontier · Catastrophic costs
- In national patient cost surveys (40 countries reporting), the weighted pooled share of people treated for TB and their households facing catastrophic total costs was 47% (95% CI 37–58%) [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- For drug-resistant TB, the pooled share was 82% (71–93%) across 37 countries [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Country estimates ranged from 13% (El Salvador) to 92% (Solomon Islands); by August 2025, 42 countries had completed a survey [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- A meta-regression across 135 LMICs estimated mean costs of US$1,253 per TB episode (95% UI 1,127–1,417), including US$530 indirect (lost income) and US$512 direct non-medical costs, even where treatment is free [[Portnoy2023LGH]].
- The same analysis estimated that 54.9% (47.0–63.2%) of TB-affected households face catastrophic costs overall, from 75.2% in the poorest quintile to 42.5% in the richest [[Portnoy2023LGH]].
- Global coverage by at least one social protection benefit is 52%, up from 43% in 2015; among the 30 high-burden countries it ranges from 3.1% (Uganda) to 94% (Mongolia) [[WHO-GTR2025]].

### frontier · UN high-level meeting 2023 targets and the funding gap
- The 2023 UN high-level meeting set targets for 2027: at least 90% of people with TB diagnosed and treated, 100% of people diagnosed initially tested with a WHO-recommended rapid test, 90% coverage of preventive treatment, and 100% access to a health and social benefits package [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- The preventive treatment target equals about 45 million people in 2023–2027: about 30 million household contacts and 15 million people with HIV [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Member States committed to at least US$22 billion a year for TB prevention, diagnosis and treatment by 2027 (US$35 billion by 2030) and US$5 billion a year for TB research by 2027 [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- In 2024 only US$5.9 billion was available for TB services in LMICs, about 27% of the 2027 target; funding has stagnated since 2020 [[WHO-GTR2025,Zenner2027]].
- TB research funding reached US$1.2 billion in 2023, 24% of the US$5 billion target; drugs took 35%, vaccines 19% and diagnostics 14% [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- The US National Institutes of Health provided 34% of all global TB research funding in 2023; cuts announced in 2025 put at least 20 TB trials in South Africa at risk [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Modelling cited by WHO warns that long-term cuts to international donor funding could cause up to 2 million extra deaths and 10 million extra people ill with TB in 2025–2035 (Hypothesis: model projection) [[WHO-GTR2025]].

### frontier · New drugs in the pipeline
- As of August 2025, 29 drugs for TB disease were in phase 1–3 trials, up from 8 in 2015, including 18 new chemical entities [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Ganfeborole (GSK3036656) is a first-in-class benzoxaborole that inhibits *M. tuberculosis* leucyl-tRNA synthetase. In a phase 2a trial (NCT03557281; 75 men, 14 days), 5, 15 and 30 mg doses gave numerical falls in sputum CFU, but 1 mg did not; all adverse events were grade 1–2 (Early evidence) [[Diacon2024]].
- Quabodepistat (OPC-167832) plus delamanid plus bedaquiline lowered sputum log10 CFU/mL by a mean 2.73 over 14 days, similar to standard HRZE control (−2.71) in a small phase 1/2 study (Early evidence) [[Dawson2025]].
- A phase 3 trial of 4-month quabodepistat-containing regimens for rifampicin-resistant TB (QUANTUM-TB, NCT07209761, planned n=532) began recruiting in October 2025 (Early evidence) [[NCT07209761]].
- BTZ-043 is a benzothiazinone DprE1 inhibitor. It was safe in healthy volunteers at 125–500 mg, and in people with TB the 1,000 mg dose cleared bacteria twofold faster than 250 mg over the first 2 days (Early evidence) [[Lutchmun2025,Koele2025]].
- Sutezolid, a next-generation oxazolidinone, added to bedaquiline, delamanid and moxifloxacin steepened the culture time-to-positivity slope by 16.7% (95% CI 0.7–35.0) at the 1,200 mg exposure; no neuropathy was reported (Early evidence) [[Heinrich2025]].
- Sorfequiline (TBAJ-876) is a second-generation diarylquinoline. In a first-in-human study, the placebo-corrected QTcF change ranged from −11.4 to +2.4 ms without dose dependency, suggesting less QT effect than bedaquiline (Early evidence) [[Darpo2026]].
- The NC-009 phase 2 trial (NCT06058299) tests three sorfequiline doses with pretomanid and linezolid against BPaL and HRZE in drug-susceptible TB, to pick a dose for phase 3 (Early evidence) [[Olugbosi2026]].
- Lancet Microbe Series reviewers describe the 2026 drug pipeline as more advanced than at any time since rifampicin was introduced (expert opinion) [[Marais2027Microbe]].

### frontier · Regimen platforms and treatment shortening
- TRUNCATE-TB (NCT03474198; 674 participants): a strategy starting with 8 weeks of bedaquiline-linezolid (plus isoniazid, pyrazinamide, ethambutol) was non-inferior to standard 6-month treatment for the week-96 composite outcome (5.8% vs 3.9%; adjusted difference 0.8 percentage points, 97.5% CI −3.4 to 5.1) (Established for this strategy) [[Paton2023]].
- In TRUNCATE-TB the mean total treatment duration was 85 days in the bedaquiline-linezolid strategy vs 180 days with standard treatment; the high-dose rifampicin-linezolid strategy did not meet non-inferiority (11.4%) [[Paton2023]].
- The Gates MRI PAN-TB trial (NCT05971602) of 2–4-month regimens with bedaquiline, quabodepistat and sutezolid plus delamanid or pretomanid was terminated: its data did not support a regimen treating TB in 3 months or less [[NCT05971602]].
- PARADIGM4TB (NCT06114628), run by the EU-funded UNITE4TB consortium, is a phase 2B/C platform testing 11 initial combinations including BTZ-043 and ganfeborole against standard 2HRZE/4HR (planned n=2,500; recruiting) (Early evidence) [[NCT06114628]].
- CRUSH-TB (TBTC Study 38, NCT05766267) compares two 4-month bedaquiline-moxifloxacin-pyrazinamide regimens (with rifabutin or delamanid) with 6-month HRZE in 288 people aged ≥12 years (Early evidence) [[Kurbatova2025]].
- RAD-TB (ACTG A5409, NCT06192160) adds linezolid, TBI-223 or sutezolid to a bedaquiline-pretomanid backbone for 8 weeks, comparing culture time-to-positivity slopes with HRZE (Early evidence) [[Harrison2025]].
- panTB-HM (NCT05686356) tests sutezolid-containing 4-month "pan-TB" regimens (usable without rifamycin susceptibility testing) plus the antioxidant N-acetylcysteine, with lung-function recovery as a superiority endpoint (Early evidence) [[Mudzengi2026]].

### frontier · Diagnostics in development
- In August 2025 almost 100 TB diagnostic products were in development, including 14 near-point-of-care NAATs awaiting WHO assessment; WHO updated its target product profiles (TPPs) for TB detection in August 2024 and for screening tests in August 2025 [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- WHO accuracy targets for near-point-of-care TB tests are ≥85% sensitivity on sputum, ≥75% on non-sputum samples and ≥98% specificity [[Yerlikaya2026]].
- Tongue swab Xpert Ultra in Kampala (183 adults) had 77.8% sensitivity (64.4–88.0) and 100% specificity against sputum Xpert Ultra; none of 8 sputum "trace" or "very low" results were detected on swabs [[Andama2022]].
- A systematic review (20 studies, 3,083 participants) found oral-swab sensitivity of 36–91% in adults and 5–42% in children, with most specificities above 90%; heterogeneity prevented meta-analysis [[Church2024]].
- MiniDock MTB (7 countries, 1,380 people aged ≥12, 226 with culture-confirmed TB) had sensitivity of 85.7% on sputum swabs and 79.6% on tongue swabs, specificity above 97.5%, and met WHO near-point-of-care targets (Early evidence) [[Yerlikaya2026]].
- In India, Uganda and Vietnam, tongue-swab sensitivity was 77.9% for Truenat MTB Ultima and 85.7% for MiniDock MTB, both more sensitive than sputum smear microscopy [[Steadman2025]].
- The next-generation urine LAM test FujiLAM, in people with HIV (13 studies, 5,819 participants), had pooled sensitivity of 63% (55–71%) and specificity of 90% (86–92%) [[Taye2025]].
- FujiLAM sensitivity rose to 76% (61–87%) at CD4 ≤100 cells/µL and fell to 42% (36–49%) at CD4 >200 cells/µL [[Taye2025]].
- Five commercial computer-aided detection (CAD) programs reading 23,954 chest x-rays in Dhaka all outperformed radiologists, with AUCs of 84.9–90.8%; only qXR and CAD4TB met the WHO triage TPP (≥90% sensitivity, ≥70% specificity) [[Qin2021]].
- All five CAD programs halved the number of Xpert tests needed while keeping sensitivity above 90%, but performed worse in people aged over 60 and people with previous TB [[Qin2021]].

### frontier · Active case finding
- ACT3 (cluster-randomised, Ca Mau, Vietnam): 3 years of annual community-wide sputum Xpert screening of everyone aged ≥15, regardless of symptoms, lowered pulmonary TB prevalence in year 4 to 126 per 100,000 vs 226 per 100,000 in control clusters (prevalence ratio 0.56; 95% CI 0.40–0.78) (Established in that setting) [[Marks2019]].
- In ACT3, TB infection prevalence in children born in 2012 did not differ (3.3% vs 2.6%; prevalence ratio 1.29; 0.70–2.36) [[Marks2019]].
- National prevalence surveys keep finding many people with bacteriologically confirmed but asymptomatic TB, which sustains transmission and is missed by symptom screening [[Zenner2027]].

### frontier · Nutrition (RATIONS)
- RATIONS (Jharkhand, India; 28 TB units cluster-randomised, 10,345 household contacts): contacts given monthly food rations (750 kcal, 23 g protein/day plus micronutrients) had TB incidence of 0.78 vs 1.27 per 100 person-years in control contacts [[Bhargava2023Lancet]].
- The adjusted IRR for all forms of TB was 0.61 (95% CI 0.43–0.85) and for microbiologically confirmed pulmonary TB 0.52 (0.35–0.79), a 39–48% relative reduction over about 2 years (Established in that setting) [[Bhargava2023Lancet]].
- About 30 households (111 household contacts) needed food support to prevent one TB case; 34% of contacts were undernourished [[Bhargava2023Lancet]].
- In the RATIONS patient cohort (2,800 adults with pulmonary TB given 1,200 kcal, 52 g protein/day), 82.4% were underweight at enrolment; treatment success was 93.7% and TB deaths 3.9% [[Bhargava2023LGH]].
- Gaining 5% of body weight by 2 months was associated with a lower hazard of TB death (adjusted HR 0.39; 0.18–0.86) [[Bhargava2023LGH]].
- A modelling study estimated the RATIONS approach cost US$289 per DALY averted (healthcare perspective) and US$229 (societal), averting 38% of TB episodes and 58% of TB deaths among contacts over 2 years (Hypothesis: model projection) [[Sinha2026]].
- WHO now lists food assistance for household contacts in food-insecure settings among TB preventive approaches [[WHO-GTR2025]].

### frontier · Host-directed therapies
- Host-directed therapies aim to limit inflammation-driven lung damage. None tested in humans has yet sped up sputum culture conversion, although some reduced biomarkers of lung damage (Hypothesis/Early evidence) [[Shaku2026]].
- In a phase 2 trial (200 patients, South Africa), CC-11050 and everolimus improved FEV1 recovery at day 180 by 6.30 and 6.56 percentage points vs control (p≈0.04–0.05); auranofin and vitamin D did not, and no therapy changed culture status (Early evidence) [[Wallis2021]].
- In that trial one auranofin recipient had life-threatening thrombocytopenia and another died of suspected intra-abdominal sepsis, illustrating safety risks of immune modulation [[Wallis2021]].
- Lancet Microbe reviewers describe host-directed therapies as advancing adjunctive strategies that remain investigational [[Marais2027Microbe]].

### Exam hooks
- BCG is a live attenuated *M. bovis* strain, first given to a baby in 1921, and is still the only licensed TB vaccine [[Lange2022]].
- BCG's most consistent benefit is against TB meningitis and miliary TB in young children; one meningitis case is prevented per about 3,435 infant vaccinations [[Trunz2006,Mangtani2014]].
- Pulmonary TB protection varies widely, from 78% in the UK MRC trial (RR 0.22) to none in Chingleput, India (RR 1.05) [[Abubakar2013]].
- Protection is greater at higher latitudes and in people not already sensitised to mycobacteria (prior infection or environmental mycobacteria) [[Mangtani2014,Colditz1994]].
- BCG strain was not associated with efficacy in trials [[Mangtani2014]].
- BCG protection lasts up to about 10 years, with some evidence beyond 15 years [[Abubakar2013]].
- BCG reduces infection (IGRA) by 19–27% and progression from infection to disease by 58% in exposed children [[Roy2014]].
- BCG revaccination: the 2018 signal (45.4% against sustained QFT conversion) was not confirmed in the 2025 phase 2b trial (efficacy −3.8%) [[Nemes2018,Schmidt2025]].
- Disseminated BCG disease is a risk in infants with HIV (pooled 992 per 100,000 vaccinated infants with HIV) [[Hesseling2009BWHO]].
- M72/AS01E: 49.7% efficacy over 3 years against pulmonary TB in IGRA-positive adults; phase 3 started 2024 [[Tait2019,NCT06062238]].
- End TB Strategy 2035: −90% incidence, −95% deaths vs 2015, and zero households facing catastrophic costs [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- By 2024: incidence down 12.3% and deaths down 29% vs 2015, against 2025 milestones of 50% and 75% [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Catastrophic costs = above 20% of annual household income or expenditure; about half of TB-affected households face them (47% in surveys) [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- TB funding in 2024: US$5.9 billion of a US$22 billion target (27%) [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- TRUNCATE-TB: an 8-week bedaquiline-linezolid strategy was non-inferior to 6 months of standard treatment [[Paton2023]].
- ACT3: community-wide Xpert screening cut pulmonary TB prevalence by 44% (PR 0.56) [[Marks2019]].
- RATIONS: food rations for household contacts cut TB incidence by 39–48% [[Bhargava2023Lancet]].
- WHO TPP for CAD triage: ≥90% sensitivity and ≥70% specificity [[Qin2021]].

### In plain words
- BCG is a vaccine most babies get in countries where TB is common. It is very good at preventing the most dangerous kinds of TB in young children, such as TB of the brain lining.
- BCG works less well at stopping lung TB in teenagers and adults, and how well it works differs from place to place.
- BCG protection fades over time, usually over about 10 to 15 years.
- Getting a second BCG shot as a teenager did not help in the most recent large trial.
- BCG is a live vaccine. It usually leaves a small sore and scar. In babies with weak immune systems, such as untreated HIV, it can rarely spread through the body.
- Scientists are testing about 18 new TB vaccines. One, called M72, cut TB disease by about half in an early trial, and a large final trial is now running.
- Some new vaccines tested in 2025–2026 did not work better than BCG or placebo. This is normal in vaccine research.
- The world agreed to cut TB deaths by 95% by 2035. So far deaths have fallen by about 29% since 2015, so progress is too slow.
- TB treatment is free in most programmes, but many families still lose money from travel and lost work. About half of families affected by TB face very high costs.
- Giving food to the families of people with TB lowered new TB cases by about 40% in a large study in India.
- New tests using a swab of the tongue may one day replace coughing up sputum.
- Researchers are testing new medicines that could shorten TB treatment from 6 months to 4 months or less.
- TB is a notifiable disease. Anyone with a cough for weeks, fever, night sweats or weight loss should see a clinician.

## Open questions and controversies
- Why does BCG efficacy vary so much? Prior sensitisation by environmental mycobacteria (masking or blocking) and latitude explain much of it [[Fine1995,Mangtani2014,Abubakar2013]]; BCG strain does not appear to matter in trials [[Mangtani2014]]; status: the mechanism is still a hypothesis.
- Is preventing sustained IGRA conversion a valid vaccine endpoint? The 2018 trial suggested BCG revaccination prevented sustained infection [[Nemes2018]], but the larger 2025 trial found no effect [[Schmidt2025]]. Infant trials also show IGRA conversions that revert [[Tameris2025]] and a mismatch between QFT and confirmed TB endpoints [[Nduba2026]]; status: unresolved, pushing trials toward disease endpoints [[Dagnew2026]].
- Will M72/AS01E work in IGRA-negative people and people with HIV? The phase 2b result applies only to IGRA-positive, HIV-negative adults [[Tait2019]]. The phase 3 trial enrols IGRA-negative and HIV cohorts mainly for safety and immunogenicity, because efficacy in IGRA-negative people alone would need about 134,800 participants [[Dagnew2026,NCT06062238]]; status: results expected after 2028.
- Can a new vaccine replace BCG in infants? MTBVAC is in phase 3 against BCG [[NCT04975178]], whereas VPM1002 was not non-inferior to BCG on QFT conversion [[Nduba2026]]; status: open.
- Does BCG have useful non-specific effects? Trained-immunity studies [[Kleinnijenhuis2012]] and neonatal mortality trials in low-birth-weight infants [[BieringSorensen2017]] suggest benefit; the single-trial result was not statistically significant; status: Early evidence.
- Can TB treatment be shortened to 3 months or less for everyone? TRUNCATE-TB shows a strategy approach can work [[Paton2023]], but the PAN-TB trial was stopped because its regimens could not reach 3 months [[NCT05971602]]; status: platform trials ongoing [[NCT06114628,Kurbatova2025,Harrison2025]].
- Is population-wide screening worth its cost outside Vietnam? ACT3 showed a 44% prevalence fall [[Marks2019]] but no effect on child infection prevalence; status: generalisability debated.
- Should food support be standard TB prevention? RATIONS found a 39–48% fall in incidence [[Bhargava2023Lancet]] and modelling suggests it is cost-effective [[Sinha2026]]; it was open-label and in one Indian state; status: WHO now lists food assistance among preventive approaches [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Funding: commitments of US$22 billion a year for services and US$5 billion for research by 2027 contrast with US$5.9 billion and US$1.2 billion available, and donor cuts from 2025 [[WHO-GTR2025]]; status: worsening.
