# C · Microbiology, immunology & pathogenesis

Pass date: 2026-10-25. Modules served: bacillus, granuloma, spectrum, drugs.

This pass covers the organism (the *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* complex, its lineages, genome, growth rate and cell envelope), the ESX-1/RD1 secretion system that explains both virulence and why interferon-gamma release assay antigens are specific, pathogenesis step by step from inhaled bacillus to cavity, granuloma architecture and its double role as container and niche, the modern spectrum from early clearance through TB infection, incipient and subclinical TB to TB disease, the timing of progression, and the drug targets and bacterial subpopulations that explain why treatment is long. Numbers are quoted as the cited source states them.

## Findings

### bacillus · the organism and the complex

- The *M. tuberculosis* H37Rv genome was sequenced in 1998 and comprises 4,411,529 base pairs with around 4,000 genes and a very high guanine + cytosine content that biases the amino-acid composition of its proteins [[Cole1998]]
- An unusually large share of that coding capacity is devoted to enzymes of lipogenesis and lipolysis, plus two new families of glycine-rich repetitive proteins (the PE/PPE families) that may be a source of antigenic variation [[Cole1998]]
- The causative agents of TB are a group of closely related bacteria, the *M. tuberculosis* complex (MTBC), with a clonal population structure and such low DNA sequence diversity that standard bacterial typing methods (for example multilocus sequence typing) are uninformative; whole-genome sequencing of large clinical collections is what opened up its ecology and evolution [[Gagneux2018]]
- Distribution of 20 insertion-deletion regions across 100 strains of *M. tuberculosis*, *M. africanum*, *M. canettii*, *M. microti* and *M. bovis* showed these polymorphisms arose from ancient, irreversible events in common progenitors, not independently; presence or absence of the TbD1 deletion divides *M. tuberculosis* into "ancestral" and "modern" strains, the latter including the Beijing, Haarlem and African clusters [[Brosch2002]]
- The same analysis contradicts the old hypothesis that *M. tuberculosis* evolved from *M. bovis*: the *M. africanum* → *M. microti* → *M. bovis* lineage diverged from the progenitor of present-day *M. tuberculosis* before TbD1 occurred, and the common ancestor probably resembled *M. tuberculosis* or *M. canettii* and may already have been a human pathogen [[Brosch2002]]
- Coalescent analysis of 259 whole MTBC genomes dates the emergence of the complex to about 70,000 years ago, accompanying out-of-Africa migrations of anatomically modern humans, with expansion during the Neolithic rise in human population density [[Comas2013]]
- Lineage 4, the most geographically widespread cause of human TB, contains both globally distributed "generalist" and geographically restricted "specialist" sublineages; most human T-cell epitopes were conserved across sublineages, but the proportion of variable epitopes was higher in the generalists, and the data support a European origin for the commonest generalist sublineage [[Stucki2016]]
- Lineage 8 was described in 2020 from two strains isolated in the African Great Lakes region and is a sister clade to all previously known MTBC lineages; its divergence preceded the loss of the cobF region (cobalamin/vitamin B12 synthesis) in the common ancestor of the other lineages, further supporting an East African origin [[Ngabonziza2020]]
- Phylogenomics of *M. africanum* revealed a further new lineage (L9) and a complex evolutionary history within the human-adapted MTBC [[Coscolla2021]]
- *M. orygis*, characterised from 22 isolates, was proposed as an MTBC subspecies and causes TB in animals and in people in Africa and South Asia [[vanIngen2012]]
- *M. bovis* causes zoonotic TB in people; the burden is likely underestimated, especially where bovine TB is endemic and people have direct contact with infected animals or animal products, and the authors call for accurate diagnosis and treatment of *M. bovis* disease in people [[OleaPopelka2017]]
- *M. tuberculosis* grows slowly: in carbon-limited chemostat culture a "fast-growing" population was held at a constant mean generation time of 23.1 h and a slow-growing population at 69.3 h, and the two populations differed phenotypically in their response to isoniazid [[Jeeves2015]]
- Slow growth in a chemostat model of *M. bovis* BCG induced several genes of the dormancy survival regulon and the transcriptome overlapped strikingly with that of *M. tuberculosis* inside macrophages, implying that slow growth is part of the in-vivo state [[Beste2007]]
- A genome-scale metabolic model (GSMN-TB) of 849 reactions, 739 metabolites and 726 genes predicted gene essentiality with 78% accuracy against in-vitro mutagenesis data, predicted known drug targets as essential, and pointed to a role for isocitrate lyase in slow mycobacterial growth [[Beste2007]]
- The mycobacterial cell wall is impermeable to many compounds, which is part of the organism's inherent resistance to numerous drugs, and its complexity requires specialised machinery for the cell to divide at all [[Hett2008]]
- Cell-wall architecture is a massive covalent core: peptidoglycan linked through an L-Rha-D-GlcNAc-P linker unit to a linear galactofuran, attached to several strands of a highly branched arabinofuran, attached in turn to mycolic acids oriented perpendicular to the membrane plane — "a truly special lipid barrier" responsible for much of the organism's physiology and disease-inducing capacity [[Brennan2003]]
- Intercalated in that lipid layer are phthiocerol dimycocerosate, cord factor (dimycolyltrehalose), sulfolipids and phosphatidylinositol mannosides; mycolic acids are recognised by CD1-restricted T cells, and antigen 85, one of the most protective *M. tuberculosis* antigens, is a mycolyltransferase [[Brennan2003]]
- Mycolic acids are the major, species-specific lipids of the mycobacterial envelope and are essential for survival of the genus; their biosynthesis is an established route to new antimycobacterial agents [[Marrakchi2014]]
- Synthesis of the whole core begins on a decaprenyl phosphate with the linker unit, and the galactan and arabinan chains are extended while the intermediate is transported through the cytoplasmic membrane; attachment of mycolic acids and ligation to peptidoglycan are predicted to be excellent drug targets [[Brennan2003]]
- Lipoarabinomannan from *M. tuberculosis* (ManLAM) inhibits phagosome maturation, apoptosis and interferon-gamma signalling in macrophages and IL-12 secretion by dendritic cells, whereas LAM from non-pathogenic mycobacteria (PILAM, carrying phospho-myo-inositol on the terminal arabinose) is strongly pro-inflammatory; the biosynthetic precursor lipomannan is pro-inflammatory through TLR2 whatever the species [[Briken2004]]
- The envelope is not a static shell but "a moving target" that is remodelled during growth, division and stress, which matters for both drug penetration and drug discovery [[Dulberger2020]]
- Microscopic counting of acid-fast bacilli in sputum was quantified as early as 1973: in 269 specimens from 28 people with pulmonary TB, microscopic counts agreed well with culturable counts before or very early in treatment in 27 of 28 people, then diverged systematically in five, giving a same-day measure of residual bacillary burden [[Hobby1973]]

### bacillus · ESX-1, ESAT-6/CFP-10 and RD1 (why IGRA antigens are specific)

- ESAT-6 is a 6 kDa secreted protein found in short-term culture filtrate of *M. tuberculosis* and of virulent *M. bovis*, and is absent from *M. bovis* BCG [[Harboe1996]]
- Comparison of BCG with virulent *M. bovis* identified a region of difference (RD1) deleted from BCG, the molecular basis of this antigenic gap [[Mahairas1996]]
- Whole-genome DNA microarray comparison of BCG vaccines showed that the different BCG daughter strains have lost further regions since 1921, so BCG substrains differ genetically from each other as well as from virulent *M. bovis* [[Behr1999]]
- The BCG Pasteur 1173P2 genome is 4,374,522 bp with 3,954 protein-coding genes, 58 of them duplicated in two independent tandem duplications (DU1, DU2); four forms of DU2 exist and amplification is ongoing even within vaccine preparations, which affects gene expression, immunogenicity and possibly protection [[Brosch2007]]
- Deleting RD1 from virulent *M. bovis* and from *M. tuberculosis* attenuated both in immunocompetent and immunocompromised mice, and the ΔRD1 *M. tuberculosis* mutant protected mice against aerosol challenge much as BCG does [[Hsu2003]]
- The primary attenuating mechanism of BCG is loss of ESAT-6-mediated secreted lytic activity: ΔRD1 mutants failed to lyse pneumocytes, a specific transposon mutation disrupting the *cfp-10 esat-6* (Rv3874-Rv3875) operon abolished the cytolytic phenotype in pneumocytes and macrophages, and three transcriptional units of RD1 are each needed for virulence [[Hsu2003]]
- Loss of RD1 also contributed to the attenuation of *M. microti*, the other historical live TB vaccine, and reintroducing RD1 into BCG restored part of the virulent phenotype [[Pym2002]]
- Deletion of RD1 from *M. tuberculosis* reproduces BCG-like attenuation, confirming RD1 as a major virulence determinant rather than an incidental loss [[Lewis2003]]
- ESX-1 (type VII secretion) exports effectors that help *M. tuberculosis* resist or evade host immunity; ESX systems vary across mycobacteria and selected non-mycobacterial species [[Groschel2016]]
- Because ESAT-6 and CFP-10 are encoded in RD1 and absent from BCG, immune-based diagnosis using these antigens can distinguish infection with *M. tuberculosis* from BCG vaccination — the design principle behind the interferon-gamma release assays [[Andersen2000]]
- Meta-analysis of commercial interferon-gamma release assays gives pooled specificity of 99% (95% CI 98-100) for QuantiFERON-TB Gold and Gold In-Tube in people not vaccinated with BCG and 96% (94-98) in people who were vaccinated, versus pooled sensitivity 78% (73-82) for Gold, 70% (63-78) for Gold In-Tube and 90% (86-93) for T-SPOT.TB in microbiologically confirmed TB disease; tuberculin skin test specificity in people not vaccinated with BCG was 97% (95-99) [[Pai2008]]
- The ATS/IDSA/CDC diagnosis guideline makes 23 evidence-based recommendations on testing for TB infection, pulmonary TB and extrapulmonary TB; 6 are strong and 17 conditional, and the guideline states that it is not intended to impose a standard of care [[Lewinsohn2017]]

### granuloma · from inhaled bacillus to adaptive response

- TB is primarily a pulmonary disease initiated by deposition of *M. tuberculosis* in aerosol droplets onto alveolar surfaces; what happens next is determined largely by the host immune response, with contributions from host genetics, insults to immunity, nutrition, and strain-to-strain differences in virulence [[Smith2003]]
- Review of the pathogenesis literature plus a guinea-pig airborne model concluded that the infecting dose is very low, usually 1-5 tubercle bacilli, that the first implant can occur anywhere in the lung, and that cavitary disease is characteristically apical [[Balasubramanian1994]]
- In mice, early productive infection occurs almost exclusively inside airway-resident alveolar macrophages; infected (but not uninfected) alveolar macrophages then relocate to the lung interstitium, a step that requires an intact ESX-1 secretion system and is driven by non-haematopoietic MyD88/IL-1 receptor signalling [[Cohen2018]]
- In experimental animals *M. tuberculosis* infects at least five distinct lung cell types: resident alveolar macrophages plus recruited neutrophils, monocytes, interstitial macrophages and dendritic cells; prolonged residence in lung phagocytes before dendritic cells acquire the bacteria is one mechanism of the delayed adaptive response [[Srivastava2014]]
- Classic electron microscopy showed that phagosomes containing intact, multiplying virulent bacilli rarely showed fusion with ferritin-labelled lysosomes, whereas virtually all phagosomes containing damaged bacilli did — the original evidence that intracellular survival is associated with non-fusion [[Armstrong1971]]
- Mycobacterial vacuoles fail to acidify below pH 6.3-6.5 and acquire the lysosomal membrane protein LAMP-1 but not the vesicular proton-ATPase responsible for phagosomal acidification [[SturgillKoszycki1994]]
- Phagolysosome biogenesis is blocked by a defined mechanism: viable *M. tuberculosis* interferes with phosphatidylinositol 3-phosphate-dependent membrane trafficking, so the block is an active, lipid-mediated subversion rather than a passive failure [[Vergne2005]]
- Autophagy is a cell-autonomous defence that inhibits survival of BCG and *M. tuberculosis* in infected macrophages, and its induction overcomes part of the phagosome maturation block [[Gutierrez2004]]
- Cytosolic *M. tuberculosis* DNA is sensed by cGAS, which induces type I interferons and activates autophagy — linking ESX-1-dependent membrane damage to both protective autophagy and the type I interferon response [[Watson2015]]
- Virulent *M. tuberculosis* and *M. leprae* translocate from the phagolysosome into the cytosol of human monocyte-derived dendritic cells and macrophages after about 2 days; cytosolic entry depends on secretion of CFP-10 and ESAT-6 and does not occur with BCG or heat-killed bacteria, and it causes significant cell death within a week [[vanderWel2007]]
- A single-cell FRET assay in thousands of infected THP-1 macrophages showed phagolysosomal rupture and cytosolic access 3-4 days after infection with wild-type *M. tuberculosis* but not with ΔRD1 or BCG; rupture was followed by necrotic death of the infected macrophage, while mutants that stayed enclosed did not kill the cell [[Simeone2012]]
- ESAT-6 is a potent activator of the NLRP3/ASC inflammasome, linking ESX-1 secretion to IL-1-family cytokine processing [[Mishra2010]]
- Whether the infected macrophage dies by apoptosis or necrosis shapes immunity: prostaglandin E2 is pro-apoptotic and protective, lipoxin A4 is pro-necrotic, and the balance between them determines the outcome of infection in both human and murine macrophages [[Divangahi2013]]
- Macrophage-centred reviews frame the cell as a contested fortress: *M. tuberculosis* survival depends on controlling phagosomal processing (inhibiting phagolysosome biogenesis and acidification) and then progressively accessing the cytosol, which in turn determines specific immune responses and cell-death programmes required for replication and dissemination [[Queval2017,Bussi2019]]
- Early clearance before any adaptive response is a real phenotype: a proportion of heavily exposed contacts never develop *M. tuberculosis* infection, and tuberculin-reactivity genetic loci support this; candidate mechanisms include alveolar macrophage killing plus neutrophils, NK T cells, MAIT cells and gamma-delta T cells, with HIV infection and vitamin D deficiency proposed as impairments [[Verrall2014]] (Early evidence)
- Innate clearance in humans requires robust cell-autonomous responses from several immune cell types, and *M. tuberculosis* has evolved to circumvent some of them [[Lerner2015]]
- In mice, dissemination precedes T-cell immunity: bacteria reach the pulmonary lymph nodes 9-11 days after aerosol inoculation, *M. tuberculosis*-specific T cells appear there 2-3 days later, and spread to spleen and liver occurs 11-14 days after infection, always before specific T cells appear in that organ [[Chackerian2002]]
- Initial activation of CD4 T cells happens in the lung-draining mediastinal lymph node and depends on antigen production by bacteria in the node itself, despite 100-fold more bacteria being present in the lungs [[Wolf2008]]
- Using ESAT-6-specific transgenic CD4 T cells, priming was shown to begin only after 10 days of infection, restricted at first to the mediastinal lymph nodes, with no priming in other nodes or the lungs; a larger challenge inoculum advanced priming by two full days [[Reiley2008]]
- Mechanisms of the delay include pathogen inhibition of apoptosis, delayed dendritic-cell migration from lung to node, and regulatory T cells [[Urdahl2011]]
- The human immune response has an "immunological life cycle": responses are only partially effective, drive bacteria into a contained state but rarely eliminate them, and that state is reversible — reactivation TB is the source of most transmission [[Ernst2012]]
- Interferon-gamma is essential: mice with a disrupted interferon-gamma gene still form granulomas but fail to make reactive nitrogen intermediates, cannot restrict bacillary growth, show heightened tissue necrosis and die rapidly, a course that exogenous recombinant interferon-gamma delayed but did not prevent [[Flynn1993]]
- Independently, interferon-gamma gene-disrupted mice could not contain a normally sublethal dose given intravenously or by aerosol, with widespread tissue destruction and very high numbers of acid-fast bacilli, although delayed-type hypersensitivity could still be elicited [[Cooper1993]]
- TNF is equally required: neutralising TNF by monoclonal antibody, or deleting the 55 kDa TNF receptor, abolished protection and early macrophage reactive nitrogen production in mice; granulomas formed in equal numbers in control and TNF-deficient mice, but necrosis occurred only in the TNF-deficient animals [[Flynn1995]]
- CD4 and CD8 T cells, interferon-gamma, IL-12, TNF and IL-6, and macrophages are the core components of the protective response; antigen presentation by MHC class I, class II and CD1 all contribute [[Flynn2001]]
- CD4 T cells and the cytokines IL-12, interferon-gamma and TNF are critical for control, yet which host factors decide why some people are protected and others progress remains unclear [[OGarra2013]]
- Immunology-led control strategies have to work with the fact that natural immunity contains rather than clears the organism, which is why correlates of protection remain the central problem for vaccine design [[Kaufmann2001]]

### granuloma · architecture, containment and niche

- Granulomas are organised aggregates of macrophages, often with characteristic morphological changes, plus other immune cells; they form in response to persistent particulate stimuli that a single macrophage cannot eradicate, and they unfold in a stepwise programme of macrophage activations and transformations that recruit further cells and change the structure [[Pagan2018]]
- The granuloma is the hallmark structure of TB and was historically regarded as a purely host-protective structure that walls off the bacteria; imaging plus genetic manipulation has shown that its innate immune mechanisms also drive expansion and dissemination of infection [[Ramakrishnan2012]]
- Quantitative intravital microscopy in zebrafish infected with *M. marinum* showed that intracellular mycobacteria use the ESX-1/RD1 locus to recruit new macrophages into the nascent granuloma and make them move rapidly within it; arriving macrophages phagocytose infected macrophages that are undergoing apoptosis, producing iterative expansion of infected macrophages and bacterial numbers, and the primary granuloma then seeds secondary granulomas by egress of infected macrophages [[Davis2009]]
- The granuloma therefore both limits growth and provides a survival niche from which bacteria may disseminate; inside it *M. tuberculosis* switches to a non-replicating but energy-generating state [[Ehlers2012]]
- Granulomas are compact, organised aggregates built from blood-derived and tissue-resident immune cells, and TB disease can arise either from early progression of a primary granuloma or from an established granuloma in a person with long-standing infection [[Guirado2013]]
- Macrophages are both the main host cell for the bacillus and a key effector of control; granuloma formation and maintenance depend on macrophage-T cell cross-talk, and the lung has two distinct macrophage lineages (embryonically derived alveolar and recruited monocyte-derived interstitial) that respond divergently inside the same granuloma [[Flynn2011]]
- Granuloma development, degeneration and necrosis is the hallmark of *M. tuberculosis* infection; formation is facilitated by the emerging acquired response, with cytokines and chemokines controlling integrity [[Orme2014]]
- Foamy macrophages, a granuloma-specific lipid-laden population, are a key participant both in sustaining persistent bacteria and in the tissue pathology that leads to cavitation and release of infectious bacilli [[Russell2009]]
- In a human in-vitro granuloma model, *M. tuberculosis* oxygenated mycolic acids triggered differentiation of monocyte-derived macrophages into foamy macrophages; inside them mycobacteria stopped replicating, switched to a dormant non-replicative state, and the bacterium-containing phagosomes migrated to and were engulfed by host lipid bodies [[Peyron2008]]
- Pimonidazole adduct imaging in guinea pigs, rabbits and non-human primates showed discrete hypoxia around necrotic and caseous regions of pulmonary granulomas; direct fibre-optic measurement in living infected rabbits found even small (3 mm) lesions severely hypoxic at 1.6 ± 0.7 mm Hg, and metronidazole (active in vitro only at low oxygen) markedly reduced lung bacterial burden in rabbits [[Via2008]]
- Human granulomas are spatially organised rather than uniform: laser-capture microdissection with mass spectrometry and confocal microscopy across six people with TB (and in rabbits) found a pro-inflammatory centre (antimicrobial peptides, reactive oxygen species, pro-inflammatory eicosanoids) and a comparatively anti-inflammatory rim around the caseum [[Marakalala2016]]
- Pathology and immune reactivity in human pulmonary TB are multidimensional: lesion types coexist in one lung and differ in cellular composition and immune activity [[Dorhoi2016]]
- Granulomas in the same host behave independently: in cynomolgus macaques most lung lesions appear to be founded by a single bacterium and reach similar maximum burdens, yet some lesions are sterilised by the host even while others progress, including in animals with active disease [[Lin2014]]
- Granuloma-level T-cell analysis showed that on average only about 8% of granuloma T cells produce cytokine after stimulation with *M. tuberculosis* antigens; sterile granulomas had modestly higher frequencies of T cells making IL-17, TNF and T-1 (interferon-gamma, IL-2 or TNF) or T-17 cytokines, bacterial burden correlated inversely with TNF and T-1/T-17 responses, and blood responses did not reflect granuloma responses [[Gideon2015]]
- Low-dose *M. tuberculosis* infection of cynomolgus macaques reproduces the human spectrum: all 17 animals were infected (tuberculin skin test conversion plus peripheral responses), 50-60% developed active-chronic infection progressing to advanced disease, and about 40% did not progress over 15-20 months with the clinical characteristics of human TB infection [[Capuano2003]]
- Detailed pathology of that model showed a spectrum of granuloma types differing by disease category; animals with active disease had more lung T cells and more interferon-gamma from blood mononuclear cells, lavage cells and mediastinal lymph nodes than animals with contained infection, and a spectrum of disease was seen even within the contained group [[Lin2009]]
- Heterogeneity in TB exists at the population level, between people, and within a single person at the same time, which is why intervention strategies have to account for independent local host-pathogen interactions [[Cadena2017]]
- Why the bacillus and not only the host shapes the tubercle: *M. tuberculosis* actively influences granuloma formation and maintenance and so helps ensure its own spread [[Russell2007]]

### granuloma · post-primary disease, cavitation and spread

- Classical radiological framing: TB manifests as primary disease shortly after infection — commonest in children and immunocompromised people, presenting with lymphadenopathy, pulmonary consolidation and pleural effusion — or as post-primary disease after a long period of infection, presenting with cavities, consolidation and centrilobular nodules [[Nachiappan2017]]
- Miliary TB is haematogenously disseminated disease, more common in immunocompromised people, with miliary lung nodules and multi-organ involvement; inactive sequelae of previous TB show characteristically as fibronodular opacities in the apical and upper zones, and radiographic stability over 6 months distinguishes inactive from active disease [[Nachiappan2017]]
- Miliary TB results from massive lympho-haematogenous dissemination; impaired cell-mediated immunity underlies it, clinical features are non-specific, typical chest radiograph findings may appear only late, and cryptic presentations and ARDS delay diagnosis [[Sharma2005]]
- The pre-chemotherapy paediatric literature shows strongly age-dependent natural history: primary infection before age 2 frequently progressed to serious disease within the first 12 months, often without significant prior symptoms; infection between 2 and 10 years rarely progressed to serious disease; and infection after age 10 frequently progressed to adult-type disease [[Marais2004]]
- In children over 3 years, symptoms represented a window of opportunity to diagnose before serious progression, and children with HIV behaved like immune-immature children under 2 [[Marais2004]]
- Hunter's review of 104 cases of untreated human TB, with the pre-antibiotic literature, argues that post-primary TB begins as an exudative tuberculous lipid pneumonia of foamy alveolar macrophages that undergoes caseation necrosis and fragmentation to produce cavities, and that granulomas in post-primary disease arise only in response to old caseous pneumonia and produce fibrosis rather than cavities [[Hunter2011]] (Hypothesis)
- The same review notes that post-primary TB occurs in immunocompetent adults, is restricted to the lungs, and accounts for 80% of all clinical cases and nearly 100% of transmission, and that *M. bovis* does not produce post-primary TB in any species, which it argues undermines animal-model support for the granuloma-erosion account of cavitation [[Hunter2011]] (Hypothesis)
- The "three-act play" paradigm: act one, primary TB spreading via lymphatics and bloodstream before systemic immunity contains organisms in granulomas; act two, an asymptomatic obstructive lobular pneumonia developing in people with effective systemic immunity, paucibacillary, without granulomas, accumulating mycobacterial antigens and host lipids for 1-2 years before sudden caseous necrosis; act three, caseous necrotic pneumonia that is either retained as fibrocaseous disease or coughed out to leave a cavity [[Hunter2016]] (Hypothesis)
- Cavitation is where immunity fails locally: in six people operated on for incurable TB, numerous bacilli were found only at the surface of cavities, i.e. in granulomas with a patent connection to the airways, and additional drug resistance was preferentially acquired there [[Kaplan2003]]
- Cytokine mRNA was up-regulated in all lesions tested in those lungs, so failure was not generalised immunosuppression; rather CD4 and CD8 T cells were selectively absent from the luminal surface of the cavity, while in the perinecrotic zone the two cell types co-localised and bacillary numbers were substantially lower [[Kaplan2003]]
- Matrix metalloproteinase-1 drives the tissue destruction: MMP-1 was significantly raised in sputum and lavage fluid of people with TB whether or not they had HIV, *M. tuberculosis* selectively up-regulated MMP1 in primary human monocytes, and mice transgenic for human MMP-1 developed alveolar destruction in lung granulomas with significantly greater collagen breakdown [[Elkington2011]]
- Neutrophils, not macrophages, are the predominant infected phagocyte in the airways of people with active pulmonary TB: neutrophils made up 86.6% ± 2.2% of cells in sputum and 78.8% ± 5.8% in lavage fluid versus 8.4% ± 1.3% and 11.8% ± 4.1% macrophages, and 67.6% ± 5.6% (sputum) and 65.1% ± 14.4% (lavage) of intracellular bacilli were in neutrophils [[Eum2010]]
- Inside the cavity, lymphocytes were the most abundant cell type (41.3% ± 11.2%), followed by neutrophils (38.8% ± 9.4%) and macrophages (19.5% ± 7.5%); bacillary length was shortest in cavities (1.9 ± 0.1 µm) versus sputum (2.9 ± 0.1 µm) and lavage (3.6 ± 0.2 µm), consistent with rapid replication just before transmission [[Eum2010]]
- Neutrophils are a double-edged cell in TB — contributing to control but also to immunopathology — which is why their role is framed as "friend or foe" [[Lowe2012]]
- Cavitation is associated with poor outcomes, treatment relapse, higher transmission rates and development of drug resistance, and cavities interface the host response with a distinctive mycobacterial niche; imaging findings, particularly cavitation, feed into treatment decisions such as duration of therapy [[Urbanowski2020,Nachiappan2017]]
- Apical localisation has a proposed route: the first implant can be anywhere, but progression to disease requires bacilli to reach "vulnerable" apical regions, either by bacillaemia during first infection (settings of low infection risk) or via the airway after repeated infection episodes (settings of high infection risk) [[Balasubramanian1994]] (Hypothesis)

### granuloma · TNF blockade and HIV

- TNF-alpha neutralisation in people unmasks TB: of 70 cases reported to the FDA MedWatch system after infliximab (median 12 weeks of therapy), 48 occurred after three or fewer infusions, 40 were extrapulmonary (17 disseminated, 11 lymph node, 4 peritoneal, 2 pleural, and one each meningeal, enteric, paravertebral, bone, genital and bladder), and 64 of the 70 reports came from countries with low TB incidence [[Keane2001]]
- The reported frequency of TB with infliximab was much higher than that of other opportunistic infections with the drug and higher than available background rates, which is why screening for TB infection or disease before starting the drug was recommended [[Keane2001]]
- In cynomolgus macaques, TNF neutralisation before infection produced fulminant disseminated disease by 8 weeks, and neutralisation in animals with contained infection caused reactivation in the majority; granuloma structure and composition were nonetheless similar to control animals with active disease, and the first signs of reactivation often appeared in thoracic lymph nodes [[Lin2010]]
- HIV depletes exactly the T cells TB control needs: *M. tuberculosis*-specific CD4 T cells were depleted rapidly after HIV-1 infection while cytomegalovirus-specific CD4 T cells persisted; the TB-specific cells were less mature and mostly IL-2-producing, IL-2 production was associated with CD25 expression, and HIV DNA was most abundant in IL-2-producing cells [[Geldmacher2010]]
- The risk of TB rises very soon after HIV seroconversion: in a retrospective cohort of 23,874 South African gold miners the increase in TB risk appeared unexpectedly early after HIV infection, implying that predictive models underestimate the effect of HIV where TB is endemic [[Sonnenberg2005]]
- HIV and *M. tuberculosis* act as a syndemic that worsens the morbidity and mortality of each pathogen alone, with effects on granuloma structure and function as well as on systemic T-cell numbers [[Diedrich2011]]
- Clinical phenotype tracks immune status: HIV co-infection is associated with less cavitation, while diabetes co-morbidity is associated with more cavitation and greater transmission risk [[Flynn2001]] — note this synthesis is also stated in reviews of human TB immunology [[OGarra2013]]

### spectrum · early clearance, infection, incipient and subclinical TB

- The active-versus-latent dichotomy has been replaced by a continuum: human TB exists along a continuous spectrum of metabolic bacterial activity and antagonistic immune responses, for which two further clinical states — incipient TB and subclinical TB — have been proposed between TB infection and TB disease [[Drain2018]]
- The international ICE-TB Delphi consensus (71 participants) distinguishes disease from infection by the presence of macroscopic pathology and defines two subclinical and two clinical TB states, differentiated by reported symptoms or signs and by likely infectiousness, with viable *M. tuberculosis* and a host response prerequisite for every state [[Coussens2024]]
- Across publicly available national prevalence surveys since 1990, between 36.1% and 79.7% (median 50.4%) of prevalent bacteriologically confirmed TB was subclinical, i.e. negative on symptom screening; chest radiography detected 89% (range 73-98%) of bacteriologically confirmed TB [[Frascella2021]]
- No association was found in those surveys between the prevalence of subclinical TB and overall bacteriologically confirmed TB prevalence, the patient diagnostic rate, or country-level HIV prevalence (p = 0.32, 0.4 and 0.34) [[Frascella2021]]
- Subclinical disease is demonstrable by imaging: among 35 asymptomatic, antiretroviral-naive adults with HIV-1 and TB infection, combined PET-CT identified 10 with pulmonary abnormalities suggestive of subclinical active disease, and those 10 were substantially more likely to progress to clinical disease [[Esmail2016]]
- Blood transcriptomics give a timeline: a 16-gene signature of risk predicted progression with sensitivity 66.1% (95% CI 63.2-68.9) and specificity 80.6% (79.2-82.0) in the 12 months before TB diagnosis, derived from 46 progressors and 107 matched controls within a cohort of 6,363 adolescents and validated in independent South African and Gambian cohorts (4,466 participants) [[Zak2016]]
- In the same adolescent cohort, progression was a sequential process: type I/II interferon signalling and complement were already elevated 18 months before diagnosis, while myeloid inflammation, lymphoid, monocyte and neutrophil modules changed closer to diagnosis, with early suppression of Th17 responses in progressors [[Scriba2017]]
- Immunological tests indicate infection in a large fraction of humanity: modelling of 131 infection surveys (1950-2011) plus WHO smear-positive prevalence estimates put the global burden of TB infection in 2014 at 23.0% (95% uncertainty interval 20.4-26.4), about 1.7 billion people, with the South-East Asia, Western Pacific and Africa regions holding around 80% [[Houben2016]]
- Recent infection (within 2 years) affected 0.8% (95% UI 0.7-0.9) of the global population, about 55.5 million (48.2-63.8) people at high risk of TB disease, of whom 10.9% (10.2-11.8) carried isoniazid-resistant strains [[Houben2016]]
- Existing infection alone, with no new infections after 2015, would still generate TB incidence of around 16.5 per 100,000 per year in 2035 and 8.3 per 100,000 per year in 2050 [[Houben2016]]
- "Latent TB" is better understood as a range of responses producing physiologically distinct granulomatous lesions whose microenvironments differ in their ability to support or suppress viable bacteria, which is the rational basis for drugs that target infection [[Barry2009]]
- Reactivation from contained infection has long been quoted at about 10% of infected people over a lifetime, with an estimated 2 billion people infected as the reservoir [[Tufariello2003]]
- The commonly quoted figure comes with caveats: WHO's 2018 TB resource page stated "On average, 5-10% of those who are infected will develop active TB disease over their lifetime", quoted verbatim in the analysis that challenges it [[Behr2018]]
- Modelling the lifetime risk of reactivation among people with a positive tuberculin skin test found it is 20% or more in most people with induration ≥10 mm who also have HIV or evidence of old healed TB, and 10-20% in people with recent skin-test conversion, in most people under 35 on infliximab with induration ≥15 mm, and in children aged 5 or under with induration ≥10 mm [[Horsburgh2004]]
- A model of TB in England and Wales estimated an age-weighted average lifetime risk of disease of approximately 12% over the last 50 years of the 20th century, highest after infection in early adulthood and declining with year of infection; incubation-period distributions differ depending on whether they are viewed prospectively or retrospectively [[Vynnycky2000]]
- Allowing for self-clearance raises the per-person risk while shrinking the at-risk population: inferring self-clearance from post-mortem and tuberculin-reversion studies gave 24.4% (95% UI 17.8-32.6) self-clearing within 10 years of infection and 73.1% (64.6-81.7) over a lifetime, a lifetime risk of TB disease of 17.0% (10.9-22.5) versus 12.6% (10.1-15.0) assuming lifelong infection, and a population at risk in India, China and Japan 35-80% smaller [[Emery2021]] (Early evidence)
- Immunoreactivity can outlast viable infection: in the large US isoniazid chemoprophylaxis trial (1956-1966) one year of treatment lowered TB incidence by 60-70% over the next nine years, yet treated people remained tuberculin-positive up to nine years later, showing that reactivity can persist at least nine years after the infection is eliminated [[Behr2019]]
- Pooling those epidemiological experiments, Behr and colleagues estimate that between 1% and 11% of people with TB immunoreactivity still harbour viable bacteria capable of causing disease [[Behr2019]] (Early evidence)
- Pre-antibiotic autopsy series found that 96-98% of visible calcified tuberculous lung lesions in people who died of other causes were sterile [[Behr2018]]
- Two pre-antibiotic histopathology series argued against endogenous reactivation as the usual route: Terplan found that 90% of 51 people aged over 40 died of new exogenous infection without involvement of the previous focus, and Canetti and colleagues ruled out endogenous reactivation in 67% of 69 cases [[Behr2018]]
- Two centuries of shifting definitions explain much of the confusion around the word "latent", and the term is better replaced by explicit statements about viable bacteria, pathology and symptoms [[Behr2021]]

### spectrum · timing of progression

- Poulsen, working in the Faroe Islands between 1939 and 1947, could pinpoint exposure to a two-week period and often a single day, and determined the incubation period of primary infection — new tuberculous reactivity, often with characteristic clinical features — to be under six weeks [[Behr2018,Poulsen1950]]
- In the same cohort the incubation period of TB disease was typically 3-9 months and almost always under two years [[Behr2018,Poulsen1950]]
- Wallgren's Stockholm "time-table of tuberculosis" similarly found that active pulmonary TB generally developed within 1-2 years of exposure [[Behr2018,Wallgren1948]]
- Gedde-Dahl, following people prospectively through tuberculin skin test conversion, found the same 3-9 month incubation period for TB disease and rarely beyond two years after newly documented reactivity [[Behr2018]]
- Modern low-incidence data agree: among untreated tuberculin-positive contacts in Amsterdam (2002-11), 75% of TB cases in contacts occurred within one year of the index diagnosis and 97% within two years, with the same timeline across all age groups even though children and adolescents were at greater risk [[Behr2018]]
- Molecular-fingerprinting work gave a median incubation period of 1.3 years (95% CI 1.1-1.4, range 0-12.8) with probabilities of disease within one, two and five years of 45%, 62% and 83% [[Behr2018]]
- A Canadian outbreak analysed with a time-labelled genome phylogeny found that the majority of 50 secondary cases presented within two years of infection [[Behr2018]]
- Behr and colleagues conclude there is no evidence for a bimodal curve separating primary progressive from reactivation TB, and that most TB cases occur within 18-24 months of infection [[Behr2018]] (Established for the timing; the implication that "latent TB" should be de-emphasised in strategy is contested)
- Very long intervals do occur: the most dramatic documented example is father-to-son transmission in Denmark with a 33-year interval between infection and disease, confirmed by genome sequencing [[Behr2018]]
- Isoniazid's protective effect in the Bethel (Alaska) studies persisted for more than 19 years, with the magnitude related to the amount of isoniazid taken, consistent with a lifelong reduction in risk [[Comstock1979]]
- Re-examination of the isoniazid trials shows the long "tail" of cases is partly new infection, not reactivation: in the Ferebee household-contact trial (13,176 people, one year of isoniazid or placebo then 10 years of observation) the later rates converged between arms, and the same convergence was seen in the South African gold-miner isoniazid trial [[Behr2018]]
- *M. tuberculosis* keeps replicating during chronic infection rather than sitting inert: an unstable-plasmid "replication clock" in mice showed continued replication throughout chronic infection, restrained by the immune system, with cumulative bacterial burden substantially higher than colony-forming unit counts suggest [[Gill2009]]
- Whole-genome sequencing of 33 isolates from nine macaques with active, contained or reactivated disease found a similar mutation rate during contained infection as during active disease or logarithmic culture over the same time, with a pattern suggesting oxidative DNA damage — which is why isoniazid monotherapy for TB infection is a risk factor for emergent isoniazid resistance [[Ford2011]]
- Dormancy is nonetheless a real bacterial programme in vitro and in lesions: *M. tuberculosis* can shut down central metabolism and stop replicating, becoming extremely resistant to host defence and to drugs [[Gengenbacher2012]]
- Wayne's controlled-oxygen model defines two stages of non-replicating persistence: NRP stage 1 begins abruptly as dissolved oxygen approaches 1% saturation (turbidity rises without a rise in colony counts or DNA synthesis, glycine dehydrogenase production starts, ATP stays steady), and NRP stage 2 begins below about 0.06% saturation (no further turbidity increase, glycine dehydrogenase falls); drug effects depended on which stage the bacilli occupied [[Wayne1996]]

### drugs · targets, subpopulations and why treatment is long

- Chemotherapy has a sequence of dominant drugs: isoniazid is responsible for an initial kill of about 95% of organisms during the first two days of treatment, after which rifampicin and pyrazinamide take over the bactericidal role through the intensive phase [[Mitchison2000]]
- In an isoniazid/rifampicin continuation phase, rifampicin is the only drug effective against persisters, as shown by the similar response of people with initially isoniazid-resistant and isoniazid-susceptible strains; if the continuation phase contains isoniazid but not rifampicin, outcomes are appreciably worse when the initial strain is isoniazid-resistant [[Mitchison2000]]
- Mitchison's framework of distinct bacterial subpopulations with different drug susceptibilities — actively growing, slowly or intermittently metabolising, acid-inhibited and near-dormant organisms — is the classical basis for combination, multi-phase short-course chemotherapy [[Mitchison1979,Mitchison1985,Mitchison2000]]
- Early bactericidal activity, the measured fall in viable sputum bacilli over the first days of treatment, was established as a drug-by-drug quantity in people with pulmonary TB and remains the standard early-phase readout [[Jindani1980,Mitchison2000]]
- Drugs have to cross three barriers in series — from blood into complex lung lesions, then through the mycobacterial cell wall, then to an intracellular target — and most current anti-TB drugs entered clinical use without this being considered, contributing to long, imperfect therapy [[Dartois2014]]
- MALDI mass-spectrometry imaging of human and animal lesions showed that rifampicin and pyrazinamide penetrate the sites of infection well, with rifampicin accumulating in necrotic caseum, whereas moxifloxacin — active in vitro against a persisting subpopulation and treatment-shortening in mice — does not diffuse well into caseum, concordant with its failure to shorten therapy in clinical trials [[Prideaux2015]]
- Differential lesion penetration can create temporal and spatial windows of effective monotherapy in specific niches, a proposed mechanism for the gradual emergence of multidrug-resistant TB [[Prideaux2015]] (Hypothesis)
- Bacilli in rabbit cavity caseum are largely non-replicating and extremely tolerant of many first- and second-line drugs; among drugs tested only the rifamycins fully sterilised caseum, caseum bacilli were more tolerant than non-replicating bacilli in the Wayne and Loebel models, and pyrazinamide was cidal in caseum although it has no detectable activity in those classic non-replicating assays [[Sarathy2018]]
- Treatment-shortening therefore has to reckon with host pathology as well as with the drugs: the open questions include markers of progression, markers of drug response, predictors of relapse, in-vitro synergy tools that translate clinically, and animal models that reliably predict shortening [[Dartois2022]]
- Resistance arises by chromosomal mutation, not plasmid transfer: fluctuation tests gave average mutation rates per bacterium per generation of 2.56 × 10⁻⁸ for isoniazid, 2.95 × 10⁻⁸ for streptomycin, 10⁻⁷ for ethambutol and 2.25 × 10⁻¹⁰ for rifampicin [[David1970]]
- Isoniazid activation requires the katG catalase-peroxidase: a single *M. tuberculosis* gene, katG, restored isoniazid sensitivity to a resistant *M. smegmatis* mutant, and katG deletion was associated with isoniazid resistance in two clinical isolates [[Zhang1992]]
- The isoniazid and ethionamide target is InhA, an enoyl reductase of mycolic acid synthesis: a missense mutation in inhA conferred resistance to both drugs, and the wild-type gene on a multicopy plasmid conferred resistance by overexpression [[Banerjee1994]]
- Rifampicin resistance maps to a short hot spot: sequencing an 411 bp rpoB fragment from 122 isolates found mutations at 8 conserved amino acids in 64 of 66 rifampicin-resistant isolates and in none of 56 susceptible isolates, all clustered within a 23-amino-acid region — the molecular basis of single-step high-level resistance and of rapid molecular testing for MDR-TB [[Telenti1993]]
- Pyrazinamide is a prodrug activated by pyrazinamidase: mutations in pncA cause resistance in acquired-resistant *M. tuberculosis* and account for the natural resistance of *M. bovis*, and transforming resistant strains with a functional pncA restored both enzyme activity and susceptibility [[Scorpio1996]]
- Ethambutol inhibits polymerisation of cell-wall arabinan, causing accumulation of decaprenol phosphoarabinose; resistance results from overexpression of Emb protein(s), structural mutation in EmbB, or both, in an embCAB gene cluster encoding arabinosyl transferases, with resistance described in up to 4% of clinical isolates [[Telenti1997]]
- Fluoroquinolones target DNA gyrase: gyrA mutations analogous to those in other fluoroquinolone-resistant bacteria were found in all 14 isolates with ciprofloxacin MIC > 2 µg/mL, and spontaneous resistant mutants of *M. bovis* BCG and *M. tuberculosis* arose at a frequency of 1 in 10⁷ to 10⁸ at 2 µg/mL [[Takiff1994]]
- Streptomycin resistance in *M. tuberculosis* arises from alterations in the ribosomal protein S12 gene (rpsL) and point mutations within a functional 16S rRNA pseudoknot (rrs) [[Finken1993]]
- Linezolid-resistant *M. tuberculosis* mutants can be selected in vitro, the basis for monitoring emergent resistance to the oxazolidinones now central to drug-resistant TB regimens [[Hillemann2008]]
- Bedaquiline (R207910, a diarylquinoline) inhibits mycobacterial ATP synthase: MIC 0.06 µg/mL against drug-susceptible and drug-resistant strains, bactericidal activity in mice exceeding isoniazid and rifampicin by at least 1 log, culture conversion by 2 months in some combinations when it replaced a first-line drug, and a single dose inhibiting mycobacterial growth for a week; resistant mutants selected in vitro pointed to the ATP synthase proton pump [[Andries2005]]
- The nitroimidazopyrans, including PA-824 (pretomanid), are activated by a mechanism dependent on the *M. tuberculosis* F420 cofactor and then inhibit protein and cell-wall lipid synthesis; unlike the drugs of the time they are bactericidal against both replicating and static *M. tuberculosis* [[Stover2000]]
- The activating enzyme is Ddn (Rv3547), a deazaflavin-dependent nitroreductase: it converts PA-824 into three primary metabolites, the major one being the des-nitroimidazole, whose formation correlates with anaerobic killing and generates reactive nitrogen species including nitric oxide; NO scavengers protect the bacilli, so the compound acts in part as an intracellular NO donor [[Singh2008]]
- Delamanid (OPC-67683), a nitro-dihydro-imidazooxazole, inhibits mycolic acid biosynthesis with MIC 0.006-0.024 µg/mL in vitro including against MDR strains; combined with rifampicin and pyrazinamide in mice it eradicated viable bacilli from the lung at least 2 months sooner than a rifampicin/isoniazid/ethambutol/pyrazinamide regimen, and it neither induced nor was affected by liver microsomal enzymes [[Matsumoto2006]]
- The current US guidelines for drug-susceptible TB treatment (ATS/CDC/IDSA 2016) and the 2025 ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA update for drug-susceptible and drug-resistant TB are the reference statements on which regimens are recommended; regimens and doses are quoted as published by these bodies and WHO and are not instructions for any individual [[Nahid2016,Saukkonen2025]]

### Exam hooks

- The *M. tuberculosis* H37Rv genome is 4,411,529 bp with about 4,000 genes, published in 1998 [[Cole1998]]
- MTBC is clonal with very low sequence diversity; TbD1 separates "ancestral" from "modern" *M. tuberculosis*, and *M. tuberculosis* did not evolve from *M. bovis* [[Brosch2002]]
- Lineage 8 (African Great Lakes, 2020) is a sister clade to all other MTBC lineages; *M. orygis* is an MTBC subspecies; *M. bovis* causes zoonotic TB [[Ngabonziza2020,vanIngen2012,OleaPopelka2017]]
- The cell-wall core is peptidoglycan → linker unit → galactofuran → arabinofuran → mycolic acids; ethambutol blocks arabinan polymerisation (embCAB), isoniazid blocks mycolic acid synthesis via InhA after katG activation [[Brennan2003,Telenti1997,Banerjee1994,Zhang1992]]
- ESAT-6 and CFP-10 lie in RD1, which is deleted from every BCG substrain; that is why IGRAs are specific in people vaccinated with BCG (pooled specificity 96% after BCG, 99% without) [[Harboe1996,Mahairas1996,Pai2008]]
- Deleting RD1 attenuates *M. tuberculosis* to a BCG-like phenotype, and the attenuating mechanism is loss of ESAT-6-dependent cytolytic/invasive function [[Hsu2003,Lewis2003]]
- Mycobacterial phagosomes fail to acidify below pH 6.3-6.5 and exclude the vesicular proton-ATPase while acquiring LAMP-1 [[SturgillKoszycki1994]]
- ESX-1-dependent phagolysosomal rupture lets *M. tuberculosis* reach the cytosol about 2-4 days after infection and is followed by necrotic host-cell death; BCG and ΔRD1 cannot do this [[vanderWel2007,Simeone2012]]
- T-cell priming starts in the mediastinal lymph node, only after about 10 days in mice, and dissemination precedes T-cell immunity in each organ — the reason for the window before tuberculin or IGRA conversion [[Reiley2008,Wolf2008,Chackerian2002]]
- Interferon-gamma and TNF are each essential in mice; granulomas still form without them, but necrosis and uncontrolled growth follow [[Flynn1993,Cooper1993,Flynn1995]]
- TNF blockade in people causes early, often extrapulmonary TB (40 of 70 infliximab cases extrapulmonary; 48 of 70 after ≤3 infusions) [[Keane2001]]
- Granulomas are hypoxic: 1.6 ± 0.7 mm Hg measured directly in 3 mm rabbit lesions, which is why metronidazole works in that model [[Via2008]]
- Granulomas within one host behave independently; some are sterilised while others progress, even in active disease [[Lin2014]]
- Median incubation period of TB disease is a few months to two years: 75% of contact cases within 1 year and 97% within 2 years in Amsterdam; 45%/62%/83% at 1/2/5 years by molecular fingerprinting [[Behr2018]]
- Median 50.4% (range 36.1-79.7%) of prevalent bacteriologically confirmed TB in prevalence surveys is subclinical — no symptoms on screening [[Frascella2021]]
- Global TB infection burden was 23.0% of the world population (about 1.7 billion) in 2014, with 55.5 million recently infected [[Houben2016]]
- Mutation rates to resistance per bacterium per generation: rifampicin 2.25 × 10⁻¹⁰, isoniazid 2.56 × 10⁻⁸, streptomycin 2.95 × 10⁻⁸, ethambutol 10⁻⁷ — hence combination therapy and the dangers of monotherapy [[David1970]]
- Rifampicin resistance is clustered in a 23-amino-acid region of rpoB (mutations in 64/66 resistant, 0/56 susceptible isolates), the basis of rapid molecular DST [[Telenti1993]]
- Isoniazid kills about 95% of organisms in the first two days; rifampicin is the drug that acts on persisters in the continuation phase [[Mitchison2000]]
- Bedaquiline targets ATP synthase; pretomanid and delamanid are nitroimidazole prodrugs (Ddn-activated, NO-releasing for pretomanid; mycolic-acid-synthesis-inhibiting for delamanid) [[Andries2005,Singh2008,Stover2000,Matsumoto2006]]
- Only rifamycins fully sterilised caseum ex vivo; moxifloxacin penetrates caseum poorly, matching its failure to shorten treatment in trials [[Sarathy2018,Prideaux2015]]

### In plain words

- TB is caused by a slow-growing germ. In the laboratory it takes around a day to double, while many common bacteria double in under an hour — that slowness is one reason treatment takes months, not days [[Jeeves2015]]
- The germ has an unusual waxy outer coat built from fatty molecules called mycolic acids. The coat keeps many drugs out, and it is also why the germ shows up with the special "acid-fast" stain used in laboratories [[Brennan2003,Hobby1973]]
- TB spreads through the air. Very few germs — often just one to five — are enough to start an infection in the lungs [[Balasubramanian1994]]
- The first cells to take the germ in are immune cells in the air sacs of the lung. Normally these cells digest what they swallow, but TB germs stop that digestion from finishing, so they survive inside [[SturgillKoszycki1994,Armstrong1971]]
- It takes a few weeks for the body's specific defences to learn to recognise the germ. That is the gap before a skin test or blood test turns positive, and it is why a test done straight after exposure can be negative [[Reiley2008,Wolf2008]]
- The body then builds a tubercle, or granuloma: a tight ball of immune cells around the germs. It holds the infection back, but it can also shelter the germs, so it works both ways [[Ramakrishnan2012,Ehlers2012]]
- Inside a granuloma there is very little oxygen and the centre can turn into a soft, cheese-like material. Germs there are hard for both the immune system and medicines to reach [[Via2008,Sarathy2018]]
- Different granulomas in the same person can behave differently at the same time: some are cleared completely while others grow [[Lin2014]]
- If a granuloma breaks into an airway it leaves a hole, called a cavity. Cavities hold enormous numbers of germs and are the main reason TB is passed on to other people [[Urbanowski2020,Kaplan2003]]
- Most people who breathe in the germ never become unwell. Being infected is not the same as having TB disease, and TB infection is not contagious [[Drain2018,Coussens2024]]
- When TB disease does develop, it usually happens within the first two years after infection; later disease is much less common [[Behr2018]]
- About half of people found to have TB in community surveys had no cough or other symptoms when they were tested. This is called subclinical TB, and it is one reason screening with a chest X-ray finds cases that questions alone would miss [[Frascella2021]]
- Having a positive TB test does not prove that live germs are still present. In many people the germs have died, and the test only remembers the past encounter [[Behr2019]]
- Medicines that block a signal called TNF, used for arthritis and bowel disease, weaken the walls the body builds around TB. People starting them are screened for TB first [[Keane2001]]
- HIV removes exactly the immune cells that keep TB in check, which is why TB risk goes up soon after HIV infection [[Geldmacher2010,Sonnenberg2005]]
- TB is always treated with several medicines at once. Resistance appears by chance mutation in a very small number of germs, and one drug alone would let those few take over [[David1970,Mitchison2000]]
- TB is a notifiable disease, treatment is free in most national programmes and cures most people. Anyone with a cough lasting more than two or three weeks, fever, night sweats or weight loss should see a clinician.

## Open questions and controversies

- Question: does the granuloma contain infection or feed it? Position A: it is a protective structure that walls off bacteria, the historical view [[Russell2007]]; position B: imaging and genetic work show its innate mechanisms drive macrophage recruitment, iterative expansion of infected macrophages and secondary seeding [[Ramakrishnan2012,Davis2009]]; status: both are now held together — containment and niche in the same structure, varying lesion by lesion [[Ehlers2012,Lin2014]]
- Question: does post-primary cavitary TB arise from caseating granulomas eroding into bronchi, or from a lipid pneumonia of foamy macrophages? Position A: the contemporary paradigm of caseating granulomas eroding into airways [[Hunter2011]]; position B: Hunter's re-reading of pre-antibiotic human pathology plus 104 untreated cases proposes obstructive lobular lipid pneumonia that caseates and fragments, with granulomas producing fibrosis rather than cavities [[Hunter2011,Hunter2016]]; status: unresolved, limited by the scarcity of untreated human lung tissue (Hypothesis)
- Question: how much TB comes from remote infection versus recent transmission? Position A: a vast latent reservoir of about 1.7 billion people is a central obstacle to elimination [[Houben2016]]; position B: disease occurs within a few months to two years of infection with no late spike, so most global TB is from recent transmission and the reservoir has been overstated [[Behr2018]]; status: the timing data are consistent across eras and methods; the strategic implication is actively contested
- Question: is TB infection lifelong? Position A: positive immune tests indicate persistent viable bacteria for life [[Tufariello2003]]; position B: tuberculin reactivity outlasts cure by at least nine years and only 1-11% of people with reactivity still harbour viable bacteria [[Behr2019]]; position C: modelling of reversion and post-mortem data gives 24.4% self-clearance within 10 years and 73.1% over a lifetime [[Emery2021]]; status: shifting towards substantial self-clearance (Early evidence)
- Question: what is the lifetime risk of disease after infection? Positions: 5-10% as commonly quoted by WHO [[Behr2018]]; about 12% age-weighted in a 20th-century England and Wales model [[Vynnycky2000]]; 17.0% once self-clearance is allowed for, because the denominator shrinks [[Emery2021]]; ≥20% in defined high-risk groups such as people with HIV or old healed TB [[Horsburgh2004]]; status: the number depends entirely on who is counted as infected — these are population statistics, not individual predictions
- Question: is the bacillus truly dormant during asymptomatic infection? Position A: dormancy is a definable bacterial programme with staged entry at measurable oxygen thresholds and a dormancy survival regulon [[Wayne1996,Gengenbacher2012]]; position B: replication clocks in mice and equal mutation rates during contained and active macaque infection show continued replication and ongoing DNA damage, and the epidemiology does not require a special dormant state [[Gill2009,Ford2011,Behr2018]]; status: both replicating and non-replicating subpopulations appear to coexist, differing by lesion
- Question: why do drugs that shorten treatment in mice fail in people? Position: lesion pharmacology — rifamycins and pyrazinamide reach caseum while moxifloxacin does not, and caseum bacilli are extremely drug tolerant [[Prideaux2015,Sarathy2018]]; status: an active reframing of regimen design, with lesion-centred PK-PD proposed as the prioritisation criterion [[Dartois2022]] (Early evidence)
- Question: are neutrophils protective or harmful in human TB? Position A: they are the predominant infected phagocyte in airways and may provide a permissive site for a final burst of replication before transmission [[Eum2010]]; position B: they contribute to early control; status: "friend or foe" remains unsettled, probably stage-dependent [[Lowe2012]]
- Question: how do bacilli reach the apex, where cavities form? Position A: via bacillaemia during first infection where infection risk is low; position B: via the airway after repeated infection where infection risk is high [[Balasubramanian1994]]; status: hypothesis, not settled (Hypothesis)
- Question: does BCG substrain genetic drift matter for protection? Position: DU2 variants, sigma-factor and regulator lesions and ongoing amplification within vaccine preparations change gene expression and immunogenicity, and early BCG vaccines may even be superior to the later, more widely used ones [[Brosch2007,Behr1999]]; status: plausible and partly supported, with efficacy consequences unresolved (Early evidence)

## Provenance

- Validation method: every record was checked live on 2026-10-25 against Europe PMC's REST `search` endpoint with `resultType=core` (authors, title, journal, year, volume, issue, pages, PMID, PMCID, DOI and abstract returned in one call) or, for records fetched earlier in the pass, against PubMed E-utilities `esummary`/`efetch`. Both services are named per line. No PMID or DOI in this file was written from memory.
- PubMed E-utilities rate-limited heavily during this pass (HTTP 429), so most validation was moved to Europe PMC; the records validated on PubMed first (Cole1998, Ngabonziza2020, Mahairas1996, Behr1999, Pym2002, Lewis2003, Andersen2000, Dulberger2020, Marrakchi2014) are marked as such.
- Abstracts were read for every source from which a number is quoted, with these exceptions, each of which is also flagged on its Sources line: Mitchison1979, Mitchison1985, Jindani1980, Wallgren1948, Poulsen1950, Behr2021 and Hillemann2008 have no abstract in PubMed or Europe PMC. For each, the claim attributed in Findings is one that an abstract-bearing or full-text source read in this pass states explicitly (Mitchison2000 for the drug-by-drug and early-bactericidal-activity claims; Behr2018 for the Poulsen and Wallgren cohort figures; the title-level framing only for Behr2021 and Hillemann2008). No number is taken from these records directly.
- Behr2018 (PMC6105930) and Behr2019 (PMC6812595) are BMJ analysis articles without abstracts; their full open-access text was fetched from Europe PMC and the quoted figures (incubation periods; 75%/97% at 1 and 2 years; median 1.3 years with 45%/62%/83% at 1/2/5 years; 96-98% of calcified lesions sterile; Terplan 90% of 51 and Canetti 67% of 69; the 33-year father-to-son interval; 1-11% still harbouring viable bacteria; the quoted WHO 5-10% wording; Ferebee trial size 13,176) are quoted from that text, which is the source actually read.
- The WHO tuberculosis resource page (2018) was not fetched; its "5-10%" wording is quoted only as reproduced in Behr2018, and is attributed that way. No WHO consolidated-guideline module is cited in this pass.
- Could not be used: Leung 1999 (Radiology, "Pulmonary tuberculosis: the essentials") has no abstract in Europe PMC and the full text is paywalled, so the Ghon focus / Ranke complex / Simon foci terminology is not asserted with a dedicated citation; the primary-versus-post-primary radiological pattern is instead cited to Nachiappan2017, whose abstract states it. Opie's 1917 study of bacilli in latent lesions returned no record in Europe PMC and is not cited; its substance is covered by the pre-antibiotic sterility figures reported in Behr2018. Menzies 1999 (repeated tuberculin tests) has no abstract, and no numeric claim about skin-test boosting, conversion or reversion windows is made from it; it is not listed as a source.
- Comstock's 1974 progress report (PMID 4414015) has no abstract and is not cited; the Bethel isoniazid figure (protection persisting more than 19 years) is taken from the 1979 final report, whose abstract was read. Readers of the shared key list should note this pass uses Comstock1979, not Comstock1974.
- Doubling time: no review read in this pass states a numeric generation time in its abstract, so the "18-24 h" figure from the brief is not asserted. The numbers given (23.1 h and 69.3 h mean generation time) come from Jeeves2015's controlled chemostat cultures, whose abstract states them. No comparison figure for *E. coli* is given because none was validated.
- Key choices that differ from the commonly cited year: Vergne2005 (PNAS 2005, often cited as 2004), Ehlers2012 (Front Immunol 2012, often cited as 2013), Divangahi2013 (Adv Exp Med Biol 2013). The suffix-free surname-year keys Lin2009, Lin2010 and Lin2014 all refer to different Flynn-laboratory papers and are distinguished in the Sources list; Flynn1993, Flynn1995, Flynn2001 and Flynn2011 likewise.
- All claims labelled (Hypothesis) are so labelled because the cited source itself presents them as a proposed model (Hunter's lipid-pneumonia account of post-primary disease and cavitation; Balasubramanian's apical-localisation pathways; the monotherapy-window explanation for emergent multidrug resistance in Prideaux2015).
