# D · Clinical presentation and diagnosis

Pass date: 2026-10-30. Modules served: symptoms, tests, diagnose, resistance.

This pass covers how TB disease shows itself and how it is found: symptoms and their predictive value, screening tools (the WHO four-symptom screen, chest X-ray with computer-aided detection, C-reactive protein), extrapulmonary TB and TB meningitis, TB in children, tests for TB infection (TST, IGRAs, antigen-based skin tests), tests for TB disease (smear, Xpert MTB/RIF and Ultra, Truenat, urine LF-LAM, culture), drug-susceptibility testing (phenotypic, line probe assays, Xpert MTB/XDR, targeted sequencing), the main resistance mutations, the differential diagnosis, and turnaround times for the lab-pipeline animation. Accuracy figures are pooled estimates from the populations studied. They are not the chance that any one person's result is right, and predictive values shift with how common TB is in the group tested.

## Findings

### symptoms · Pulmonary TB symptoms and how well they predict disease
- The WHO four-symptom screen for people living with HIV counts as positive if any one of these is present: current cough of any duration, fever, night sweats or weight loss. It came from an individual-participant meta-analysis of 12 studies (9,626 people with HIV, 557 [5.8%] with TB disease; 8,148 in the primary analysis) [[Getahun2011]].
- In that analysis the four-symptom screen had a sensitivity of 78.9% (95% CI 58.3–90.9) and a specificity of 49.6% (29.2–70.1). Sensitivity rose to 90.1% in clinic-recruited participants and 88.0% in people not previously screened [[Getahun2011]].
- The screen's negative predictive value was 97.7% at 5% TB prevalence and 90.0% at 20% prevalence, so a negative screen rules TB out well only where TB is uncommon [[Getahun2011]].
- Adding any chest X-ray abnormality raised sensitivity from 78.9% to 90.6% and lowered specificity from 49.6% to 38.9% [[Getahun2011]].
- The four-symptom screen is much less sensitive in people taking antiretroviral therapy (ART). A meta-analysis of 18 studies found pooled sensitivity of 51.0% (95% CI 28.4–73.2) on ART versus 89.4% (83.0–93.5) in people not yet on ART. Specificity was 70.7% versus 28.1% [[Hamada2018]].
- In people on ART (two studies, 646 people), adding any chest X-ray abnormality raised sensitivity from 52.2% to 84.6% and lowered specificity from 55.5% to 29.8% [[Hamada2018]].
- An individual-participant meta-analysis of 22 studies (15,666 ambulatory people with HIV) found a four-symptom screen sensitivity of 82% (72–89) and specificity of 42% (29–57) [[Dhana2022]].
- In that analysis, cough of 2 weeks or more, haemoglobin below 10 g/dL, BMI below 18.5 kg/m² and lymphadenopathy each had high specificity (80–90%) but low sensitivity (29–43%) [[Dhana2022]].
- In HIV-negative adults or adults of unknown HIV status screened in the community, cough for 2 weeks or more had a summary sensitivity of 42.1% (95% CI 36.6–47.7) and specificity of 94.4% (92.6–95.8) (41 studies) [[VantHoog2022]].
- In the same review, any cough had a sensitivity of 51.3% and specificity of 87.6% (21 studies). Any TB symptom had a sensitivity of 70.6% and specificity of 65.1% (29 studies) [[VantHoog2022]].
- In national TB prevalence surveys since 1990, a median of 50.4% (range 36.1–79.7%) of people with bacteriologically confirmed TB were "subclinical": they screened negative on symptoms. Chest X-ray detected 89% (range 73–98%) [[Frascella2021]].
- Cough for 2 weeks or more is therefore specific but misses about half of people with TB disease in community screening. That is why WHO-endorsed screening pairs symptoms with chest X-ray [[VantHoog2022,Frascella2021]].

### symptoms · Screening tests: chest X-ray, computer-aided detection, CRP
- Any chest X-ray abnormality had a summary sensitivity of 94.7% (95% CI 92.2–96.4) and specificity of 89.1% (85.6–91.8) for bacteriologically confirmed pulmonary TB (23 studies). Abnormalities suggestive of TB had a sensitivity of 84.8% and specificity of 95.6% (19 studies) [[VantHoog2022]].
- Combining cough for 2 weeks or more with chest X-ray (positive if either is positive) gave a summary sensitivity of 99.2% (96.8–99.8) and specificity of 84.9% (81.2–88.1) (15 studies) [[VantHoog2022]].
- Computer-aided detection (CAD) software reads a digital chest X-ray and gives a numerical abnormality score. In 23,954 people aged 15 or older screened in Dhaka, all five commercial algorithms outperformed the radiologists. Areas under the curve were 84.9–90.8% [[Qin2021]].
- In that study, only qXR (74.3% specificity) and CAD4TB (72.9%) met the WHO target product profile for a triage test (at least 90% sensitivity and 70% specificity). All five halved the number of Xpert tests needed while keeping sensitivity above 90% [[Qin2021]].
- CAD performs worse in people over 60 and in people with a history of TB, because old scars look like disease [[Qin2021,Codlin2021]].
- An independent test of 12 CAD products in Viet Nam found 6 that performed on par with an expert human reader. Performance varied with the X-ray equipment used [[Codlin2021]].
- A 2019 systematic review found median areas under the curve of 0.88 in CAD development studies but 0.75 in clinical evaluation studies. Most early evidence came from development rather than real-world testing [[Harris2019]].
- In a 2021–2022 multinational cohort of adults with 2 weeks or more of cough (1,392 people, 22% with TB), CAD4TB v7 had the highest specificity at 90% sensitivity: 70.3%, versus 65.1% for the Xpert TB Host Response blood test and 49.7% for point-of-care CRP [[Crowder2026]].
- In that cohort, two-test algorithms did better than any single test. CRP followed by CAD4TB reached 75.9% specificity at 90% sensitivity [[Crowder2026]].
- In Ugandan adults with HIV starting ART with CD4 counts of 350/µL or less, finger-prick CRP of 10 mg/L or more had a sensitivity of 89% and specificity of 72% for culture-confirmed TB. Specificity was 58 percentage points higher than the symptom screen and sensitivity 7 points lower [[Yoon2017]].
- In the individual-participant meta-analysis, CRP of 10 mg/L or more had a sensitivity of 77% and specificity of 74%. A positive symptom screen followed by CRP of 5 mg/L or more, in outpatients not on ART, kept sensitivity at 84% and raised specificity to 64% [[Dhana2022]].
- At 10% TB prevalence, these CRP strategies would need 244–272 fewer confirmatory molecular tests per 1,000 people with HIV than the symptom screen alone, at the cost of missing 1–2 more cases. The authors note that CRP has been included in the updated WHO screening guidelines [[Dhana2022]].
- In WHO's 2024 data, 15% of people newly diagnosed with TB were reported as detected through screening, in 106 countries that account for 87% of global notifications [[WHO-GTR2025]].

### symptoms · Extrapulmonary TB: share and sites
- In 2024, 8.3 million people worldwide were diagnosed with a new episode of TB and notified. Of these, 84% had pulmonary TB and 16% had extrapulmonary TB [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- In 2024, 8.3% of notified people were children aged 0–14 years. Bacteriological confirmation of pulmonary TB stayed at 62–64% globally from 2022 to 2024, so more than a third of pulmonary TB is treated on clinical grounds [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Of 253,299 US cases reported in 1993–2006, 73.6% were pulmonary and 18.7% extrapulmonary only. Cases with both, and miliary cases, were excluded from both groups [[Peto2009]].
- In that series, extrapulmonary sites were lymphatic 40.4%, pleural 19.8%, bone and/or joint 11.3%, genitourinary 6.5%, meningeal 5.4%, peritoneal 4.9% and unclassified 11.8% [[Peto2009]].
- Extrapulmonary TB was associated with female sex (OR 1.7) and birth outside the US (OR 1.5). It was negatively associated with multidrug resistance (OR 0.6). Its share of US cases rose from 15.7% in 1993 to 21.0% in 2006 [[Peto2009]].
- Peripheral TB lymphadenitis makes up about 10% of US TB cases. It typically shows as 1–2 months of painless swelling of one group of cervical lymph nodes, with a female-to-male ratio of 1.4:1 and a peak age of 30–40 years [[Fontanilla2011]].
- Excisional lymph-node biopsy has the highest diagnostic sensitivity, at 80%. Fine-needle aspiration is less invasive. Paradoxical worsening during treatment occurs in about 20% of people with TB lymphadenitis [[Fontanilla2011]].
- Pleural fluid adenosine deaminase (ADA) for TB pleurisy had a pooled sensitivity of 0.92 (95% CI 0.90–0.93) and specificity of 0.90 (0.89–0.91) across 63 studies [[Liang2008]].
- The ATS/IDSA/CDC guideline reports pleural ADA sensitivity of 89–99% and specificity of 88–97%, mostly at thresholds around 40 U/L. Free interferon-γ was 89% sensitive and 97% specific in pleural fluid, and 93% and 99% in peritoneal fluid [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- Smear sensitivity in extrapulmonary specimens is low, from under 10% up to 42% depending on site. Culture sensitivity ranges from 23% to 90% [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- Miliary TB comes from massive lymphohaematogenous spread of bacilli. Its symptoms are non-specific, and the typical chest X-ray pattern may appear only late, which delays diagnosis [[Sharma2005]].

### symptoms · TB meningitis
- TB meningitis is the most lethal and disabling form of TB, with more than 100,000 new cases estimated each year. In people with HIV, mortality approaches 50% [[Wilkinson2017]].
- In a meta-analysis of 39 studies (5,752 adults), pooled 6-month mortality was 24%: 57% (95% CI 48–67) in adults with HIV versus 16% (10–24) in adults without HIV. These are population averages [[Stadelman2020]].
- Mortality ranged from 2% to 67% in Asian studies and from 23% to 80% in sub-Saharan African studies. 32% (22–43%) of adult survivors had a physical disability [[Stadelman2020]].
- In 1,699 Vietnamese adults with TB meningitis, 9-month mortality was 23.0% without HIV and 51.3% with HIV. A higher Medical Research Council (MRC) severity grade and a lower CSF lymphocyte count predicted death in both groups [[Thao2018]].
- In children with TB meningitis (19 studies, 1,636 children), the risk of death was 19.3% (14.0–26.1). Only 36.7% survived without neurological sequelae, and 53.9% of survivors had sequelae [[Chiang2014]].
- 47% of these children were diagnosed at the most advanced stage (stage 3), which carried worse outcomes. CSF smear was positive in 8.9% and CSF culture in 35.1% [[Chiang2014]].
- A 2010 consensus case definition classifies TB meningitis as definite, probable or possible, for use in research in any age group, HIV status or resource setting [[Marais2010LID]].
- In Vietnamese adults, five features separated TB meningitis from bacterial meningitis: age, length of history, blood white-cell count, total CSF white-cell count and CSF neutrophil proportion. The resulting rule was 86% sensitive and 79% specific when tested prospectively [[Thwaites2002]].
- On CSF, Xpert Ultra had a pooled sensitivity of 89.4% and specificity of 91.2% against culture (6 studies). Xpert MTB/RIF had 71.1% and 96.9% (30 studies) [[Kohli2021]].
- In Ugandan adults with HIV and suspected meningitis, CSF Xpert Ultra detected 95% (21 of 22) of TB meningitis by a composite standard, versus 45% for both Xpert and culture. Testing 6 mL or more of CSF raised detection (26% vs 7%) [[Bahr2018]].
- A 2022 review puts CSF Xpert Ultra sensitivity at about 70%. A negative result cannot rule out TB meningitis [[Huynh2022]].

### symptoms · TB in children
- WHO estimates that children and young adolescents under 15 make up about 11% of people with TB: 1.1 million fall ill each year and more than 225,000 die [[WHO-Mod5-2022]].
- Microbiological confirmation of childhood TB is rare because specimens are hard to collect, smear sensitivity is low and culture access is poor [[Detjen2015]].
- In 4,718 children under 10 assessed for pulmonary TB in 13 studies, 1,811 (38.4%) were classified as having TB. Only 29.9% of these were bacteriologically confirmed and 70.1% were unconfirmed [[Gunasekera2023]].
- WHO treatment-decision algorithms for children score clinical features with or without chest X-ray, after triage for how sick the child is. The score with chest X-ray had a combined sensitivity of 0.86 and specificity of 0.37. The clinical-only score had 0.84 and 0.30 [[Gunasekera2023]].
- These algorithms were added to the operational handbook of the WHO 2022 guidelines on TB in children and adolescents, so health workers in primary care can start treatment without bacteriological confirmation [[Gunasekera2023,WHO-Mod5-2022]].
- In HIV-negative children in Cape Town, the combination of persistent non-remitting cough over 2 weeks, documented failure to thrive in the past 3 months, and fatigue had a sensitivity of 62.6%, specificity of 89.8% and positive predictive value of 83.6% [[Marais2006]].
- That symptom approach worked better at 3 years or older (sensitivity 82.3%) than under 3 (51.8%). It offered little diagnostic value in children with HIV, in whom the TST was positive in fewer than 20% [[Marais2006]].
- Consensus case definitions classify intrathoracic TB in children for research use, so diagnostic studies can be compared and pooled [[Graham2015]].
- Xpert Ultra summary sensitivity in children against a microbiological reference: sputum 75.3%, gastric aspirate 69.6%, stool 68.0%, nasopharyngeal aspirate 46.2%. Specificity was 91.0–98.2% [[Kay2025]].
- The ATS/IDSA/CDC guideline reports yields in children of up to 40–50% from three consecutive morning gastric aspirates (up to 90% in infants), 20–30% from nasopharyngeal aspirate or induced sputum, and 10–22% from bronchoalveolar lavage [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- Earlier Xpert MTB/RIF in children had a pooled sensitivity of 62% on expectorated or induced sputum and 66% on gastric lavage against culture, with 98% specificity. That sensitivity was 36–44% higher than microscopy [[Detjen2015]].

### tests · Tuberculin skin test (Mantoux)
- The Mantoux TST is 0.1 mL of 5 tuberculin units (TU) of PPD injected intradermally into the forearm. It is read 48–72 hours later [[ATS2000]].
- The ATS/IDSA/CDC 2017 guideline restates the cut-offs. A result of 5 mm or more is positive in close contacts of people with TB, people who are immunosuppressed (including people with HIV), people with evidence of current or past TB, and people taking TNF blockers [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- A result of 10 mm or more is positive in other people at increased risk, such as people born in high-incidence countries, people with occupational exposure, and people with medical risk factors for progression. A result of 15 mm or more is positive in everyone else [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- In someone retested after a negative result, an increase of 10 mm or more within 2 years counts as a TST conversion and suggests recent infection [[ATS2000]].
- The 2000 statement notes that BCG-induced reactivity can be boosted by repeated tuberculin testing. No method can reliably tell BCG reactions from infection, so a positive TST in a BCG-vaccinated person at increased risk is taken to show infection [[ATS2000]].
- Across 24 studies of 240,203 people given BCG in infancy, 8.5% had a TST of 10 mm or more due to BCG. Only 1% did when tested 10 or more years after BCG [[Farhat2006]].
- In people given BCG after their first birthday, 41.8% had a false-positive TST of 10 mm or more, and 21.2% still did after 10 years [[Farhat2006]].
- Cross-reaction with non-tuberculous mycobacteria caused false-positive TSTs in only 0.1–2.3% of people across regions, so it matters only where NTM sensitisation is common and TB infection is rare [[Farhat2006]].
- TST specificity is about 97% in people without BCG but about 60% in people with a BCG history. TST sensitivity is 71–82% in likely-infected people at low or intermediate risk and about 43% in people with HIV [[Lewinsohn2017]].

### tests · Interferon-γ release assays (IGRAs)
- QuantiFERON-TB Gold In-Tube is positive when the TB antigen response (minus Nil) is at least 0.35 IU/mL and at least 25% of the Nil value, with Nil at 8.0 IU/mL or less [[Mazurek2010]].
- A QFT result is indeterminate if Nil is above 8.0 IU/mL, or if the TB response is negative and the mitogen (positive control) response is below 0.5 IU/mL [[Mazurek2010]].
- T-SPOT.TB is positive at 8 spots or more, borderline at 5–7 spots and negative at 4 or fewer, provided Nil is 10 spots or fewer. A mitogen well under 20 spots with a negative TB response is indeterminate [[Mazurek2010]].
- QFT blood is incubated for 16–24 hours, and IGRA results can be available within 24 hours without a second visit [[Mazurek2010]].
- QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus adds a second antigen tube (TB2) that stimulates CD8+ as well as CD4+ T cells. In 119 contacts it agreed closely with QFT-GIT (Cohen's κ 0.8) [[Barcellini2016]].
- The earliest FDA premarket-approval record that names QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus is supplement P010033/S031, dated 7 June 2017, which approved a design change to the TB antigen tubes [[FDA-QFTPlus]].
- In a meta-analysis of 41 studies, QFT-Plus had sensitivity and specificity similar to QFT-GIT and T-SPOT.TB. Its specificity was 0.12 (95% CI 0.02–0.22) higher than the TST [[Zhang2023QFT]].
- Pooled sensitivity in microbiologically confirmed TB was 70% (63–78) for QFT-GIT and 90% (86–93) for T-SPOT.TB. Pooled QFT specificity was 99% without BCG and 96% with BCG [[Pai2008]].
- The ATS/IDSA/CDC guideline strongly recommends an IGRA over the TST for people aged 5 or older who are likely infected, at low or intermediate risk of progression, and either BCG-vaccinated or unlikely to return for a TST reading (moderate-quality evidence) [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- The same guideline suggests a TST over an IGRA for healthy children under 5 (conditional; very low-quality evidence). For people at high risk of progression, there were insufficient data to prefer either test [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- Neither the TST nor IGRAs can tell TB infection from TB disease, so TB disease must be excluded before preventive treatment [[Lewinsohn2017]].

### tests · Serial testing, conversion and reversion
- In 2,418 US healthcare workers tested every 6 months for 18 months, conversion rates were 6.1% for QFT-GIT, 8.3% for T-SPOT.TB and 0.9% for TST [[Dorman2014HCW]].
- 76.4% of QFT-GIT converters and 77.1% of T-SPOT.TB converters were negative again 6 months later. The authors concluded that most IGRA conversions in low-incidence settings are false positives and that apparent converters should be retested [[Dorman2014HCW]].
- Repeating blood draws 2 weeks apart gave discordant results in 8.8% (QFT-GIT) and 12.6% (T-SPOT.TB) of participants [[Dorman2014HCW]].
- In Indian medical and nursing students retested after 18 months, QFT conversion was 11.6% using the 0.35 IU/mL cut-off and 7.5% using 0.70 IU/mL [[Pai2006HCW]].
- QFT reversion occurred in 7% of people who were positive on both tests at baseline, versus 70% of those whose baseline TST and QFT disagreed [[Pai2006HCW]].

### tests · Predicting progression to TB disease
- In 15 cohorts (26,680 people), TB incidence in IGRA-positive people was 4–48 per 1,000 person-years. The pooled incidence rate ratio was 2.11 for IGRA-positive and 1.60 for TST-positive (10 mm) people, compared with test-negative people [[Rangaka2012]].
- A 2012 meta-analysis put the positive predictive value for progression at 2.7% for commercial IGRAs versus 1.5% for the TST. In high-risk groups these were 6.8% and 2.4%. Negative predictive values were 99.7% and 99.4% [[Diel2012]].
- In the UK PREDICT cohort (9,610 recent contacts and migrants, median follow-up 2.9 years), 1.0% developed TB disease. Incidence was 13.2 per 1,000 person-years in T-SPOT.TB-positive, 11.1 in TST-15-positive and 10.1 in QFT-GIT-positive people [[Abubakar2018]].
- TST at 5 mm identified the largest share of people who progressed (83%), at the cost of lower specificity [[Abubakar2018]].
- An individual-participant meta-analysis of 13 studies (32,034 people) found a pooled hazard ratio for incident TB of 4.15 for QFT-GIT and 2.88 for a BCG-stratified TST (15 mm if BCG-vaccinated, 5 mm if not). Much of the difference came from one study [[Hamada2023]].
- In lower-incidence countries, positive predictive values for TB disease were 2.74% (TST 15 mm) and 2.46% (QFT-GIT). Negative predictive values were 99.42% and 99.52%. Both tests did worse in high-incidence countries [[Hamada2023]].
- Fewer than 3 in 100 people with a positive test for TB infection develop TB disease over the follow-up in these cohorts, even without preventive treatment. This is the "predictive value gap" that newer tests aim to close (Established) [[Hamada2023,Diel2012]].

### tests · Antigen-based skin tests (TBSTs)
- TBSTs (Cy-Tb, C-Tb, Diaskintest, C-TST) inject the M. tuberculosis-specific antigens ESAT-6 and CFP-10 into the skin, so BCG does not affect them [[Krutikov2022,Hamada2021]].
- WHO recommends that "Mycobacterium tuberculosis antigen-based skin tests (TBSTs) may be used to test for TB infection" (conditional; very low certainty of evidence) [[WHO-Mod3-2022TBI,WHO-Mod1-2024]].
- In a meta-analysis, C-Tb had a sensitivity of 74.5% versus 71.7% for IGRA, and specificity of 97.9% versus 93.3% for TST at 15 mm. Diaskintest agreed with IGRA in 87.2% of a mixed cohort [[Krutikov2022]].
- In Spanish contact tracing, C-Tb positivity rose with exposure, from 3% in negative controls to 16% in occasional and 43% in close contacts. It agreed with QFT in 94% of 834 participants aged 5 or older [[Ruhwald2017]].
- A 2021 landscape found 20 tests for TB infection (15 in vitro, 5 skin tests). Most use ESAT-6 and CFP-10, and some use lateral-flow formats suited to decentralised testing [[Hamada2021]].

### diagnose · Smear microscopy and culture
- Three acid-fast bacilli (AFB) smears give about 70% sensitivity against culture-confirmed TB. The first specimen gives 53.8%, the second adds about 11.1% and the third only 2–5% [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- A first-morning specimen is 12% more sensitive than a spot specimen. Concentration adds about 18%, and fluorescence microscopy is about 10% more sensitive than conventional light microscopy [[Lewinsohn2017,Steingart2006Fluor]].
- Smear specificity is 90% or more, and its positive predictive value is 70–90% depending on TB prevalence. A negative smear does not exclude pulmonary TB [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- The guideline asks for at least 3 mL of sputum, with 5–10 mL optimal. It suggests both liquid and solid culture for every specimen, and at least liquid culture always [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- Liquid culture detects growth in an average of 10–14 days and solid culture in 3–4 weeks. Liquid culture sensitivity is 88–90%, versus 76% for solid [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- In South African gold miners, MGIT liquid culture gave a higher mycobacterial yield than Löwenstein–Jensen solid medium (29.7% vs 22.8%) and a shorter median time to positivity in smear-negative specimens (14 vs 25 days). Contamination was higher (16.7% vs 9.3%) [[Chihota2010]].
- In that study, 77.3% of smear-negative specimens positive only on MGIT grew non-tuberculous mycobacteria, which is why speciation is needed [[Chihota2010]].
- In a 10-study meta-analysis, BACTEC MGIT 960 had a sensitivity of 81.5% and specificity of 99.6% for mycobacteria. Adding solid media raised sensitivity to 87.7% [[Cruciani2004]].

### diagnose · Xpert MTB/RIF, Xpert Ultra and Truenat
- Xpert MTB/RIF is an automated cartridge PCR that detects M. tuberculosis and rifampicin resistance from untreated sputum in under 2 hours with minimal hands-on time [[Boehme2010,Lawn2011Xpert]].
- In 1,730 people evaluated for TB, a single Xpert test detected 98.2% of smear-positive and 72.5% of smear-negative culture-positive TB, with 99.2% specificity. Three tests raised smear-negative sensitivity to 90.2% [[Boehme2010]].
- Against phenotypic DST, Xpert correctly identified 97.6% of rifampicin-resistant and 98.1% of rifampicin-susceptible strains [[Boehme2010]].
- In district-level use (6,648 people), one Xpert test detected 90.3% of culture-confirmed TB, versus 67.1% for microscopy. Sensitivity was not significantly lower in people with HIV [[Boehme2011]].
- Xpert Ultra has a limit of detection of 15.6 CFU/mL of sputum, versus 112.6 CFU/mL for Xpert MTB/RIF (M. tuberculosis H37Rv) [[Chakravorty2017]].
- In a comparison across 8 countries, Ultra sensitivity was 63% versus 46% for Xpert in smear-negative culture-positive TB, and 90% versus 77% in people with HIV. Specificity was 96% versus 98% overall and 93% versus 98% in people with prior TB [[Dorman2018Ultra]].
- A Cochrane review found pooled sensitivity of 90.9% for Ultra versus 84.7% for Xpert MTB/RIF, and specificity of 95.6% versus 98.4% (7 studies, about 2,834 participants; high certainty) [[Zifodya2021]].
- Per 1,000 people with symptoms at 10% prevalence, Ultra would miss 9 cases and wrongly diagnose 40. Xpert MTB/RIF would miss 15 and wrongly diagnose 14 [[Zifodya2021]].
- "Trace" results, Ultra's lowest positive category, made up 3.0–30.4% of Ultra positives. Ultra gave more indeterminate rifampicin results (7.6% vs 0.8%) [[Zifodya2021]].
- For rifampicin resistance, Ultra had a sensitivity of 94.9% and specificity of 99.1%. Xpert MTB/RIF had 95.3% and 98.8% [[Zifodya2021]].
- WHO strongly recommends Xpert Ultra for the initial diagnosis of TB in adults with signs and symptoms of pulmonary TB (high certainty of evidence for test accuracy) [[WHO-Mod3-2024]].
- In Peru, India, Ethiopia and Papua New Guinea, Truenat MTB and MTB Plus had pooled sensitivities of 73% and 80% in microscopy centres, and the Truenat MTB-RIF Dx assay detected rifampicin resistance with 84% sensitivity. Performance was similar to Xpert [[PennNicholson2021]].
- WHO conditionally recommends Truenat MTB or MTB Plus as an initial diagnostic test for pulmonary TB in adults (moderate certainty) [[WHO-Mod3-2024]].
- With a positive smear, NAAT sensitivity is 96%. With a negative smear it is 66%, with 98% specificity. A negative NAAT does not exclude pulmonary TB [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- On extrapulmonary specimens, Xpert MTB/RIF sensitivity against culture was 49.5% in pleural fluid and 81.6% in lymph-node aspirate (against a composite reference). Ultra was 75.0% in pleural fluid [[Kohli2021]].
- When nurses ran Xpert at primary-care clinics, more people had a same-day diagnosis (24% vs 13%) and started treatment the same day (23% vs 15%) than with smear, but TB-related illness at 2 and 6 months did not differ [[Theron2014]].
- Pooling five trials, Xpert did not clearly lower 6-month mortality (OR 0.88, 95% CI 0.68–1.14). In people with HIV, the death rate was lower (hazard ratio 0.76, 0.60–0.97) in a secondary analysis [[DiTanna2019]].
- In 2024, a WHO-recommended rapid diagnostic test was the first test for 54% of people newly diagnosed with TB, about 4.5 of 8.3 million [[WHO-GTR2025]].

### diagnose · Urine LF-LAM in people with HIV
- The urine lateral flow lipoarabinomannan test (AlereLAM) detects a mycobacterial cell-wall glycolipid in urine at the bedside, so it does not need sputum [[Bjerrum2019]].
- In adults with HIV and TB symptoms, LF-LAM had a pooled sensitivity of 42% (31–55) and specificity of 91% (85–95). Sensitivity was 52% in inpatients versus 29% in outpatients, and it rose as CD4 count fell [[Bjerrum2019]].
- In adults with HIV not selected by symptoms, sensitivity was 35% and specificity 95% [[Bjerrum2019]].
- In a randomised trial in 10 African hospitals (2,528 adults with HIV), adding LAM-guided treatment lowered 8-week mortality from 25% to 21%: an absolute reduction of 4% and a relative reduction of 17% (risk ratio 0.83, 0.73–0.96) [[Peter2016]].
- The STAMP trial screened all HIV-positive inpatients with urine LAM and Xpert. It did not significantly lower overall 56-day mortality (18% vs 21%, adjusted risk difference −2.8%, p=0.074). Mortality was lower in people with CD4 under 100/µL, severe anaemia or clinically suspected TB [[GuptaWright2018]].
- Pooling the inpatient trials, LF-LAM-based strategies likely reduce 8-week mortality (RR 0.85, 0.76–0.94; moderate certainty) [[Nathavitharana2021LAM]].
- WHO strongly recommends AlereLAM for inpatients with HIV who have TB signs or symptoms, advanced HIV disease or serious illness. It also recommends it for inpatients with HIV and CD4 under 200 regardless of symptoms. It is an add-on test, not a replacement or triage test [[WHO-Mod3-2024]].
- In outpatients with HIV who have TB signs or symptoms, WHO conditionally recommends AlereLAM (low certainty in test accuracy) [[WHO-Mod3-2024]].

### resistance · Drug-susceptibility testing pipeline
- WHO-recommended rapid tests include low-complexity automated NAATs (Xpert MTB/RIF, Ultra, Truenat), moderate-complexity automated NAATs, line probe assays, low-complexity NAATs for isoniazid and second-line resistance (Xpert MTB/XDR), and targeted next-generation sequencing [[WHO-Mod3-2024]].
- In 2024, 83% of people with bacteriologically confirmed TB were tested for rifampicin resistance. 58% of people with rifampicin-resistant TB were tested for fluoroquinolone resistance [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Line probe assays had a pooled sensitivity of 96.7% and specificity of 98.8% for rifampicin resistance, and 90.2% and 99.2% for isoniazid resistance (74 studies) [[Nathavitharana2017LPA]].
- Xpert MTB/XDR, in people whose TB it detects, had summary sensitivity and specificity of 94.2% and 98.5% for isoniazid, 93.2% and 98.0% for fluoroquinolones, 98.0% and 99.7% for ethionamide, and 86.1% and 98.9% for amikacin [[Pillay2022]].
- WHO conditionally recommends targeted next-generation sequencing on respiratory samples from people with rifampicin-resistant pulmonary TB, to detect resistance to isoniazid, fluoroquinolones, bedaquiline, linezolid, clofazimine, pyrazinamide, ethambutol, amikacin and streptomycin instead of phenotypic DST [[WHO-Mod3-2024]].
- Across 24 accuracy studies, targeted sequencing had a combined sensitivity of 94.1% and specificity of 98.1% across all drugs. By drug, sensitivity ranged from 76.5% for capreomycin to 99.1% for rifampicin [[Schwab2024]].
- Whole-genome sequencing of 10,209 isolates predicted resistance to isoniazid, rifampicin, ethambutol and pyrazinamide with sensitivities of 97.1%, 97.5%, 94.6% and 91.3%. 97.9% of isolates predicted to be pansusceptible were correct [[AllixBeguec2018]].
- The 2021 WHO mutation catalogue drew on 38,215 isolates from 45 countries. 1,149 of 15,667 mutation–drug associations were graded as associated with resistance. For rifampicin, isoniazid, ethambutol, fluoroquinolones and streptomycin, pooled sensitivity was over 80% [[Walker2022]].
- The catalogue classified only two resistance mutations each for bedaquiline, delamanid, clofazimine and linezolid, because phenotypic resistance to these drugs was rare. Genotypic testing for the newest drugs is therefore still incomplete (Established) [[Walker2022]].
- First-line phenotypic DST takes 1–2 weeks after growth is detected. Solid-medium testing for second-line and newer drugs takes 3–4 weeks [[Lewinsohn2017]].

### resistance · Resistance mutations
- Rifampicin resistance maps to a short region of rpoB. In 64 of 66 rifampicin-resistant isolates, mutations in 8 conserved amino acids fell within a 23-amino-acid region, the rifampicin resistance-determining region (RRDR) [[Telenti1993]].
- Mutations in this single rpoB region account for more than 95% of phenotypically rifampicin-resistant isolates. That is why one PCR target such as Xpert can stand in for rifampicin DST [[Seifert2015]].
- Some "disputed" (borderline) rpoB mutations are missed by routine phenotypic DST. In Bangladesh they caused most rifampicin resistance in new patients, and 5 of 7 affected people had poor first-line treatment outcomes [[VanDeun2015]].
- Isoniazid is activated by the catalase-peroxidase KatG. Deleting katG caused isoniazid resistance in two patient isolates [[Zhang1992katG]].
- Globally, the katG315 mutation was linked to 64% of phenotypic isoniazid resistance, and inhA −15 (promoter) to 19%. Together with 10 other common inhA-promoter and ahpC-oxyR mutations, they explained 84% [[Seifert2015]].
- inhA encodes a target of both isoniazid and ethionamide, so inhA mutations can cause cross-resistance between the two [[Banerjee1994]].
- Pyrazinamide resistance comes mainly from pncA mutations that cause loss of pyrazinamidase activity [[Scorpio1996]].
- Fluoroquinolone resistance comes from mutations in the quinolone resistance region of gyrA (and gyrB). gyrA mutations were found in all 14 clinical isolates with a ciprofloxacin MIC above 2 µg/mL [[Takiff1994]].
- Bedaquiline targets ATP synthase, and resistant mutants selected in vitro carry changes in the atpE-encoded proton pump [[Andries2005]].
- Rv0678 mutations upregulate the MmpS5–MmpL5 efflux pump and cause low-level resistance to both bedaquiline and clofazimine. They have been found in paired isolates from people treated with bedaquiline [[Hartkoorn2014,Andries2014]].

### diagnose · Differential diagnosis
- Community-acquired pneumonia: empirical fluoroquinolones delayed TB diagnosis and treatment by a mean of 19 days and raised the odds of fluoroquinolone-resistant TB (pooled OR 2.70) [[Chen2011FQ]].
- Non-tuberculous mycobacteria: more than 190 species and subspecies exist, and some cause lung disease that looks like TB. Mycobacterium avium complex, M. kansasii, M. xenopi and M. abscessus are the most common [[Daley2020]].
- Non-tuberculous mycobacteria can also grow in TB cultures. 77.3% of smear-negative specimens positive only in MGIT liquid culture grew NTM [[Chihota2010]].
- Chronic pulmonary aspergillosis: diagnosis needs one or more cavities or nodules on imaging, evidence of Aspergillus (microscopy, culture or an immune response) and exclusion of other diagnoses, all present for at least 3 months. Aspergillus antibody is raised in more than 90% of people with it [[Denning2016]].
- Sarcoidosis: diagnosis rests on a compatible clinical picture, non-necrotising granulomas in tissue and exclusion of other granulomatous causes such as TB, so the diagnosis is "never fully secure" [[Crouser2020]].
- Bacterial versus TB meningitis: age, length of history, white-cell counts and CSF neutrophil proportion help separate them [[Thwaites2002]].
- HIV-associated TB: it often presents outside the lungs, sputum may be impossible to produce, and samples hold few bacilli, which limits smear and NAATs [[Peter2016]]. Urine LAM sensitivity rises as CD4 falls, and Ultra is more sensitive than Xpert MTB/RIF in people with HIV (87.6% vs 74.9%) [[Bjerrum2019,Zifodya2021]].
- Past TB: CAD software performs worse, and Xpert Ultra specificity drops to 88.2% (vs 97.4% for Xpert MTB/RIF), in people with previous TB, so a positive result needs careful reading [[Qin2021,Zifodya2021]].

### diagnose · Turnaround times for the lab-pipeline animation
- In district-level use, median time to TB detection was 0 days (IQR 0–1) for Xpert, 1 day for microscopy, 16 days (13–21) for liquid culture and 30 days (23–43) for solid culture [[Boehme2011]].
- Median time to resistance results was 20 days (10–26) by line probe assay and 106 days (30–124) by conventional DST [[Boehme2011]].
- Xpert cut the median time to treatment for smear-negative TB from 56 days (39–81) to 5 days (2–8) [[Boehme2011]].
- The ATS/IDSA/CDC guideline lists about 1 day each for NAAT detection, smear and identification. NAAT resistance markers take 1–2 days, liquid culture 10–14 days, solid culture 3–4 weeks, first-line phenotypic DST 1–2 weeks after growth, and second-line and newer drugs on solid medium 3–4 weeks [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- An IGRA result can be ready within 24 hours of the blood draw, while a TST needs a return visit 48–72 hours later [[Mazurek2010,ATS2000]].

### Exam hooks
- WHO four-symptom screen in people with HIV: any one of current cough, fever, night sweats or weight loss. Sensitivity 78.9%, specificity 49.6% [[Getahun2011]].
- The four-symptom screen loses sensitivity on ART (51% vs 89% in people not on ART) [[Hamada2018]].
- Cough for 2 weeks or more is specific (94%) but insensitive (42%) in community screening [[VantHoog2022]].
- About half of people with TB found in prevalence surveys have no symptoms on screening (subclinical TB) [[Frascella2021]].
- Any chest X-ray abnormality is about 95% sensitive for pulmonary TB in screening [[VantHoog2022]].
- TST: 5 TU PPD intradermally, read at 48–72 hours, measure induration only. Cut-offs are 5 mm (contacts, immunosuppression including HIV, TNF blockers, prior TB on X-ray), 10 mm (other risk groups) and 15 mm (no risk factors) [[ATS2000,Lewinsohn2017]].
- BCG given in infancy rarely affects a TST done 10 or more years later (1% at 10 mm or more) [[Farhat2006]].
- QFT is positive at 0.35 IU/mL or more. T-SPOT.TB is positive at 8 spots or more and borderline at 5–7 [[Mazurek2010]].
- IGRA is preferred after BCG and for people unlikely to return. TST is suggested for healthy children under 5 [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- Neither TST nor IGRA separates TB infection from TB disease, and both have positive predictive values for progression under about 3% [[Lewinsohn2017,Hamada2023]].
- Most IGRA conversions in serially tested healthcare workers in low-incidence settings revert. Retest before acting [[Dorman2014HCW]].
- Three smears give about 70% sensitivity. Fluorescence microscopy adds about 10% [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- Xpert MTB/RIF gives a result in under 2 hours and is about 98% sensitive in smear-positive and 72% in smear-negative TB [[Boehme2010]].
- Xpert Ultra trades a little specificity for sensitivity: 90.9% vs 84.7% sensitivity and 95.6% vs 98.4% specificity [[Zifodya2021]].
- More than 95% of rifampicin resistance comes from mutations in one short rpoB stretch, the RRDR [[Seifert2015,Telenti1993]].
- katG315 is the most common isoniazid-resistance mutation (64%), then the inhA −15 promoter mutation (19%). inhA mutations can also cause ethionamide cross-resistance [[Seifert2015,Banerjee1994]].
- Urine LF-LAM is most useful in very sick inpatients with HIV and low CD4. In a trial, LAM-guided treatment cut 8-week mortality by 17% (relative) [[Peter2016,Bjerrum2019]].
- Rv0678 mutations cause cross-resistance between bedaquiline and clofazimine [[Hartkoorn2014]].
- Liquid culture detects growth in about 10–14 days and solid culture in 3–4 weeks [[Lewinsohn2017]].

### In plain words
- A cough that lasts more than 2 weeks, fever, night sweats or weight loss are reasons to get checked for TB. Some people with TB feel well [[VantHoog2022,Frascella2021]].
- A chest X-ray is a good first look. It cannot prove TB, but it helps decide who needs a sputum test [[VantHoog2022]].
- The skin test and the blood test (IGRA) show whether your body has met the TB germ. They cannot tell whether you are sick now [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- After a skin test you must come back in 2 or 3 days so a nurse can measure the bump. Only the raised, firm part (called induration) is measured [[ATS2000]].
- The BCG vaccine you may have had as a baby rarely changes a skin test done many years later. The blood test is not affected by BCG at all [[Farhat2006,Pai2008]].
- Most people with a positive skin or blood test never get sick with TB. Preventive treatment lowers the risk further [[Hamada2023]].
- To check for TB disease, you are usually asked to cough up sputum. An early-morning sample works best [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- A rapid test called Xpert can find TB and an important kind of drug resistance in about 2 hours [[Boehme2010]].
- A culture grows the germ in the lab and can take 2 to 6 weeks. It also shows which medicines will work [[Lewinsohn2017]].
- For people with advanced HIV, a simple urine test can help find TB when sputum is hard to give [[Bjerrum2019]].
- Children often cannot cough up sputum. Doctors may test stool, a stomach sample or a nose sample, or start treatment based on symptoms and an X-ray [[Kay2025,Gunasekera2023]].
- TB can affect places other than the lungs, most often the lymph nodes in the neck [[Peto2009,Fontanilla2011]].
- TB is a notifiable disease. Testing and treatment are free in most public programmes, and anyone with symptoms should see a clinician.

## Open questions and controversies
- IGRA versus TST for predicting TB disease: IGRA showed a higher hazard ratio in low-incidence countries (4.15 vs 2.88) [[Hamada2023]], and a higher positive predictive value in an earlier meta-analysis (2.7% vs 1.5%) [[Diel2012]]. The individual-participant analysis says the gap is driven by one study and that absolute predictive values are almost the same [[Hamada2023]]. Status: test choice is contextual. Both perform poorly for prediction (Established).
- How to act on Xpert Ultra "trace" results: trace makes up 3.0–30.4% of Ultra positives, and data were insufficient to judge repeat testing [[Zifodya2021]]. Specificity falls in people with prior TB (93% vs 98%) [[Dorman2018Ultra]]. Status: open (Early evidence).
- Does faster molecular diagnosis save lives? Point-of-care Xpert increased same-day treatment without lowering illness at 6 months [[Theron2014]]. Pooled trials could neither confirm nor exclude lower mortality, apart from a possible benefit in people with HIV [[DiTanna2019]]. Status: unresolved. Gains depend on the whole care cascade (Early evidence).
- Urine LAM for every HIV-positive inpatient versus targeted use: one trial showed a 17% relative mortality reduction [[Peter2016]]. STAMP found no significant overall benefit, with benefit in subgroups with low CD4 or severe anaemia [[GuptaWright2018]]. The pooled estimate favours LAM strategies (RR 0.85) [[Nathavitharana2021LAM]]. Status: WHO recommends it for symptomatic, seriously ill or advanced-HIV inpatients, and for CD4 under 200 [[WHO-Mod3-2024]].
- CRP versus CXR/CAD as the screening triage test: CRP improves specificity over symptoms in people with HIV not on ART [[Dhana2022,Yoon2017]]. In a multinational cohort with cough, CAD4TB outperformed CRP and a host-response blood test [[Crowder2026]]. Status: setting-dependent. Two-test algorithms look most accurate (Early evidence).
- Screening for subclinical TB: about half of prevalent TB is symptom-negative [[Frascella2021]], which argues for X-ray-based screening [[VantHoog2022]]. CAD is less accurate in older people and people with prior TB [[Qin2021]]. Status: active research (Early evidence).
- Genotypic DST for new drugs: catalogue coverage of bedaquiline, linezolid, delamanid and clofazimine resistance is thin [[Walker2022]]. Rv0678 variants are heterogeneous [[Andries2014]]. Disputed rpoB mutations are missed by phenotypic DST [[VanDeun2015]]. Status: phenotypic testing is still needed for some drugs (Established).
- Diagnosing TB in children: about 70% of children treated for pulmonary TB are never bacteriologically confirmed [[Gunasekera2023]], and the treatment-decision scores have low specificity (0.30–0.37) [[Gunasekera2023]]. Status: prospective validation is pending (Early evidence).
