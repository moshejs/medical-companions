## Findings

### what · WHO Global tuberculosis report 2026 (released 12 Nov 2026; 2025 data)
- In 2025 an estimated 10.6 million people (95% UI 9.8–11.4 million) fell ill with TB, 129 per 100 000 (95% UI 119–138). This was 0.7% (75 000) fewer than in 2024; the rate fell 1.6% from 131 (Established) [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- 1.13 million people died (95% UI 1.05–1.22 million): 1.0 million HIV-negative people (0.92–1.08 million) and 137 000 people with HIV (109 000–168 000). The 2024 total is now given as 1.18 million [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- Against 2015 the incidence rate was down 14% (milestone for 2025: 50%) and deaths 35% (1.74 million to 1.13 million). WHO calls this reaching the first (2020) mortality milestone "albeit 5 years behind schedule"; the 2025 milestone was 75% [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- WHO re-estimated the series for 2010–2025: "direct comparisons with estimates from previous editions are not appropriate" (publication page). The 2025 report's 10.7 million and 1.23 million for 2024 are now 10.63 million and 1.18 million [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- Series values read from the figure data of web sections 1.1 and 1.2. Incident cases, millions: 2010 11.64; 2015 11.10; 2019 10.44; 2020 10.34; 2021 10.47; 2022 10.71; 2023 10.73; 2024 10.63; 2025 10.56. Rate per 100 000: 2010 166.7; 2015 149.2; 2019 134.2; 2020 131.6; 2021 132.1; 2023 133.1; 2024 130.7; 2025 128.7. Notifications: 2010 5.79; 2015 6.16; 2019 7.12; 2020 5.84; 2021 6.43; 2022 7.49; 2023 8.19; 2024 8.33; 2025 8.38 [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- Notified: 8.4 million, 79% (74–86%) of the estimate; the gap was 2.2 million (2.3 million in 2024; 4.5 million in 2020). Ten countries had about 60% of the gap [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- Regions, share of cases and rate per 100 000: South-East Asia 35%, 197; Western Pacific 27%, 128; Africa 25%, 201; Eastern Mediterranean 8.7%, 110; Americas 3.4%, 34; Europe 1.7%, 20. Shares of HIV-negative deaths: 39%, 30%, 20%, 8%, 2.3%, 1.4% (South-East Asia, Africa, Western Pacific, Eastern Mediterranean, Americas, Europe) [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- Countries: India about a quarter, Indonesia 10%, the Philippines 6.8%, China 6.5%, Pakistan 6.4%, Nigeria 4.9%, DR Congo 3.9%, Bangladesh 3.7%; top five more than 50%; 30 high-burden countries 88%. 50 countries had fewer than 10 per 100 000 [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- Who: 5.7 million adult men (54%), 3.7 million adult women (35%), 1.2 million children under 15 (11%). People with HIV were 5.6% of cases (best estimate 592 000). Notifications were 81% of estimated incidence in adult men and in adult women, 59% at age 0–14 and 52% under 5 [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- Deaths by group: HIV-negative 508 000 men, 332 000 women, 157 000 children; with HIV 71 000 men, 64 000 women, 1800 children. Children under 15: 158 000 in all; 110 000 of the HIV-negative child deaths were under 5. The site's "579,000 men" and "396,000 women" are sums of the two WHO figures, calculated here [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- Case fatality 10.7% in 2025 (11.2% in 2024, 13.8% in 2019). Treatment is estimated to have averted 86 million deaths in 2000–2025 [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- Leading infectious killer: the latest all-cause estimates are for 2023, when TB was the leading cause of death from a single infectious agent and 13th overall [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- Treatment success: drug-susceptible TB 90% (2024 cohort; 81% in 2016; 70% Americas to 94% Eastern Mediterranean); people with HIV 80%; age 0–14 94%; women and girls 91% vs men and boys 88% (119 countries); RR-TB 70% (2023 cohort; 71% in 2022; 50% in 2012) [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- ART for notified people with TB and HIV: 87% (91% in 2024); 59% of all estimated (61% in 2024) [[WHO-GTR2026]].

### resistance, drtb · Drug-resistant TB
- 370 000 people (330 000–400 000) developed RR-TB in 2025 and 140 000 (85 000–190 000) died. RR-TB was 3.1% of new cases (4.4% in 2015) and 15% of previously treated cases (20% in 2015); in new cases 2.0% in the African and Eastern Mediterranean regions to 21% in the European Region. China, India, the Philippines and the Russian Federation had over half [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- 155 333 people were treated (164 960 in 2024), 42% of the estimate; ten countries had 74% of the gap. 85% of bacteriologically confirmed TB was tested for rifampicin resistance and 58% of RR-TB for fluoroquinolone resistance [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- Regimens in 2025: 6-month 42% (18% in 2024, 3% in 2023), 18–20-month 44%, 9-month 11%; 6-month regimens in use in 106 countries (98 at the end of 2024, 60 at the end of 2023) [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- The 2026 report does not restate country shares, the pre-XDR share, isoniazid-resistant TB or second-line drug prices; the site keeps those as 2024 figures [[WHO-GTR2025]].

### diagnose, symptoms, tpt
- A WHO-recommended rapid test was the first test for 59% of people newly diagnosed (54% in 2024). 17% of notified new and recurrent TB was extrapulmonary [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- Preventive treatment reached 5.2 million people (5.3 million in 2024): 3.7 million household contacts (26% coverage) and 1.5 million people with HIV (60%) [[WHO-GTR2026]].

### frontier, bcg, living · Targets, money and pipelines
- By 2025, 99 countries had cut the incidence rate by at least 20% and 89 had cut deaths by at least 35%; 28 had cut incidence by at least 50%, including Namibia and South Africa; Kenya, Tanzania and Zambia were close. Regions, incidence since 2015: Europe −42%, Africa −30%, South-East Asia −18%, Eastern Mediterranean −6.6%, Western Pacific 0, Americas +13% [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- Funding in low- and middle-income countries: US$ 6.6 billion in 2025 (US$ 6.0 billion in 2024; 30% of the US$ 22 billion target); domestic US$ 5.63 billion (+14%); international donors US$ 0.94 billion (−14%). Research funding is still the 2023 figure, US$ 1.2 billion [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- Catastrophic costs: pooled 47% (95% CI 36–57%) in 40 countries; 81% (70–92%) for drug-resistant TB in 37 countries; range 13% (El Salvador) to 92% (Solomon Islands). Social protection covers 52% of people (43% in 2015) [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- Pipelines in August 2026: 31 drugs in phase 1–3 trials (29 in 2025, 8 in 2015); 65 tests in development for TB disease; 17 vaccine candidates, 12 in active development (3 in phase 1, 6 in phase 2, 3 in phase 3) and 5 classed as inactive or not under active development, a category new in 2026 [[WHO-GTR2026]].

### special · Empirical TB treatment in infants with HIV (EMPIRICAL)
- Open-label 2×2 factorial RCT in 19 hospitals in Côte d'Ivoire, Malawi, Mozambique, Uganda, Zambia and Zimbabwe: infants aged 28–365 days with severe HIV-associated pneumonia and no TB diagnosis or contact; 558 analysed (Early evidence) [[DominguezRodriguez2026]].
- Empirical 6-month TB treatment (2HRZE/4HR) for all against treatment guided by tests and the clinician: 132/276 (48%) vs 121/282 (43%) had died at 12 months; adjusted hazard ratio 1.12 (95% CI 0.87–1.43; p=0.3868). At 15 days the odds ratio was 1.24 (0.83–1.84) [[DominguezRodriguez2026]].
- In the comparison groups 130/282 (46%) started TB treatment anyway, 55 on a positive test (53 by urine LAM), which the authors say may have reduced the contrast. Serious adverse events did not differ; no treatment-related deaths [[DominguezRodriguez2026]].

### Exam hooks
- 2025: 10.6 million ill (129 per 100 000), 8.4 million notified (79%), 1.13 million deaths including 137 000 people with HIV [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- End TB by 2025: incidence −14% (milestone −50%), deaths −35% (milestone −75%; the 2020 milestone reached five years late) [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- RR-TB 2025: 370 000 new, 42% treated, 42% of those on 6-month regimens [[WHO-GTR2026]].

### In plain words
- TB is falling slowly. In 2025 about 10.6 million people fell ill and 1.13 million died, about a third fewer deaths than in 2015 [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- Giving TB medicines to every very sick baby with HIV and pneumonia, without waiting for tests, did not save more lives than testing and treating those who needed it [[DominguezRodriguez2026]].

## Open questions and controversies
- India's incidence estimates are "under review" in the 2026 report (Annex 4), so the global series may be revised again [[WHO-GTR2026]].
- EMPIRICAL: did the 46% who were treated in the comparison arm hide a benefit, or does empirical treatment not help? [[DominguezRodriguez2026]] Status: open.
