## Findings

### resistance · How resistance arises: mutation and selection
- In fluctuation tests, M. tuberculosis mutated to drug resistance spontaneously and at random. The average mutation rates per bacterium per generation were 2.56 × 10⁻⁸ for isoniazid, 2.95 × 10⁻⁸ for streptomycin, 10⁻⁷ for ethambutol and 2.25 × 10⁻¹⁰ for rifampin [[David1970]].
- Rifampin had the lowest mutation rate of the four drugs and ethambutol the highest. Both were confirmed by watching mutant proportions rise in a growing bacterial population [[David1970]].
- Because each drug's resistance arises independently, the chance of one bacillus being resistant to two drugs at once is roughly the product of the single-drug rates. That is the logic behind combination therapy (Established) [[David1970,Dheda2024]].
- WHO lists three causes of acquired resistance: incorrect prescribing, poor-quality drugs, and people stopping treatment early. Resistance can also be transmitted directly from person to person [[WHO-GTR2025,Kendall2015]].
- All forms of drug-resistant TB can be as transmissible as rifampicin-susceptible TB. They are harder to diagnose and carry higher mortality, morbidity and post-TB lung damage [[Dheda2024]].

### resistance · Acquired versus transmitted resistance
- A transmission model fitted to national notification data estimated that transmission, not treatment-related acquisition, accounts for a median 95.9% of all incident MDR-TB (95% uncertainty range 68.0–99.6) [[Kendall2015]].
- The same model attributed 61.3% (16.5–95.2) of MDR-TB in previously treated people to transmission. By country, the share ranged from 48% in Bangladesh to 99% in Uzbekistan [[Kendall2015]].
- The model's inputs were global MDR prevalences of 3.5% among new TB notifications and 20.5% among re-treatment notifications [[Kendall2015]].
- The authors concluded that improving drug-susceptible TB treatment alone is unlikely to greatly reduce future MDR-TB. Finding and treating MDR-TB should be prioritised (Established) [[Kendall2015]].
- In KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa (404 people with XDR-TB, 2011–2014), 69% had never been treated for MDR-TB and 77% had HIV [[Shah2017NEJM]].
- In the same study, 84% of genotyped isolates fell into 1 of 31 clusters, including one cluster of 212 people (55%) with a LAM4/KZN strain. Person-to-person or hospital links were found for 30% [[Shah2017NEJM]].
- The authors concluded that most XDR-TB there was probably due to transmission rather than inadequate MDR-TB treatment [[Shah2017NEJM]].
- In Tugela Ferry, South Africa (2005–2006), 52 of 53 people with XDR-TB died. Median survival was 16 days from diagnosis. All 44 tested had HIV, and 85% of isolates were genotypically similar [[Gandhi2006]].
- In that cohort, 55% of people with XDR-TB had never been treated for TB before and 67% had a recent hospital admission. This pointed to transmission, including in health facilities [[Gandhi2006]].

### resistance · WHO definitions (2021 update)
- MDR-TB: resistant to both rifampicin and isoniazid. RR-TB: resistant to rifampicin, with or without isoniazid resistance. The two are reported together as MDR/RR-TB [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- Pre-XDR-TB: rifampicin-resistant (possibly also isoniazid-resistant) and resistant to at least one fluoroquinolone (levofloxacin or moxifloxacin) [[WHO-Mod4-2025,Viney2021]].
- XDR-TB: rifampicin-resistant, resistant to at least one fluoroquinolone, and resistant to at least one other Group A drug (bedaquiline or linezolid) [[WHO-Mod4-2025,Viney2021]].
- The 2021 XDR definition replaced the older one, which was based on fluoroquinolone plus second-line injectable resistance. Trials enrolling before 2021 (Nix-TB, ZeNix) used the old definition [[Viney2021,Conradie2022]].
- Hr-TB: isoniazid-resistant, rifampicin-susceptible TB [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- WHO Group A medicines are levofloxacin or moxifloxacin, bedaquiline and linezolid. Group B is clofazimine and cycloserine/terizidone. Group C includes ethambutol, delamanid, pyrazinamide, imipenem–cilastatin, meropenem and amikacin [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].

### resistance · Global burden of drug-resistant TB (2024 data)
- WHO estimated that 390 000 people developed MDR/RR-TB in 2024 (95% UI 360 000–430 000). The estimated number has been falling since 2015 [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- MDR/RR-TB caused an estimated 150 000 deaths in 2024 (95% UI 93 000–210 000) [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Among people with no previous TB treatment, the share with MDR/RR-TB fell from 4.7% in 2015 to 3.2% in 2024. Among previously treated people it fell from 19% to 16% [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Regional shares in new cases ranged from 2.0% (African and Eastern Mediterranean regions) to 23% (European Region). In previously treated people they ranged from 5.6% (Eastern Mediterranean) to 51% (European) [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Four countries accounted for over half of incident MDR/RR-TB in 2024: India 32%, China 7.1%, the Philippines 7.1% and the Russian Federation 6.7% [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- An estimated 18% (95% UI 15–20%) of MDR/RR-TB cases in 2024 were pre-XDR-TB [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- There were an estimated 1.5 million incident cases of isoniazid-resistant TB in 2024 (95% UI 0.59–2.4 million), counting both rifampicin-susceptible and rifampicin-resistant forms [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- About a quarter of deaths caused by antimicrobial resistance are due to rifampicin-resistant TB [[Dean2022]].
- In 2024, 164 545 people were enrolled on MDR/RR-TB treatment, down from 179 491 in 2022 and 177 017 in 2023 [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Enrolment set against the 390 000 estimated incident cases gives roughly 42% treatment coverage in 2024 (calculated here from the two WHO figures) [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- In 2024, ten countries accounted for 74% of the global gap between incident MDR/RR-TB and people enrolled on treatment. India alone accounted for 33% [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Drug-resistant TB often consumes more than 50% of national TB budgets while making up less than 5–10% of the TB caseload [[Dheda2024]].
- Second-line regimens cost about US$ 200–600 per person at Global Drug Facility prices, against about US$ 50 for first-line treatment [[WHO-GTR2025]].

### drtb · The legacy era: long injectable regimens
- In an individual patient data meta-analysis of 12 030 people with MDR-TB from 25 countries (studies 2009–2016), 61% had treatment success, 8% had failure or relapse, and 14% died [[Ahmad2018Lancet]].
- Treatment success was associated with linezolid (adjusted risk difference 0.15), levofloxacin (0.15), carbapenems (0.14), moxifloxacin (0.11), bedaquiline (0.10) and clofazimine (0.06) [[Ahmad2018Lancet]].
- Lower mortality was associated with linezolid (−0.20), bedaquiline (−0.14), moxifloxacin (−0.07) and levofloxacin (−0.06). Kanamycin and capreomycin were associated with worse outcomes [[Ahmad2018Lancet]].
- In a meta-analysis of 18 studies, the pooled prevalence of aminoglycoside-induced hearing loss in people treated for DR-TB was 40.62%. By drug, it was 49.65% for kanamycin, 38.93% for amikacin and 10.21% for capreomycin [[Dillard2021]].
- The same authors estimated that not using aminoglycosides could prevent about 50 000 cases of hearing loss each year [[Dillard2021]].
- Globally, MDR/RR-TB treatment success rose from 50% in the 2012 cohort to 64% (2020), 68% (2021) and 71% (2022) [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- For comparison, success with first-line treatment for drug-susceptible TB was 88% in the 2023 cohort [[WHO-GTR2025]].

### drtb · Shortening with existing drugs: Bangladesh and STREAM
- In Bangladesh (1997–2007), a regimen of at least 9 months (gatifloxacin, clofazimine, ethambutol and pyrazinamide throughout, with prothionamide, kanamycin and high-dose isoniazid in a 4-month or longer intensive phase) gave 87.9% relapse-free cure (95% CI 82.7–91.6) in 206 people [[VanDeun2010]].
- STREAM stage 1 randomised 424 people. At 132 weeks, favourable status was 78.8% on the 9–11-month regimen and 79.8% on the 20-month 2011 WHO regimen (adjusted difference 1.0 point, 95% CI −7.5 to 9.5), meeting noninferiority [[Nunn2019]].
- In STREAM stage 1, QT or QTcF prolongation to 500 ms occurred in 11.0% on the short regimen and 6.4% on the long regimen. Deaths were 8.5% versus 6.4% [[Nunn2019]].
- In STREAM stage 2, favourable outcome at 76 weeks was 83% (162/196) on a 9-month all-oral bedaquiline regimen and 71% (133/187) on the 9-month injectable control (difference 11.0%, 95% CI 2.9–19.0) [[Goodall2022]].
- In STREAM stage 2, grade 3–4 hearing loss occurred in 9% of the injectable control arm and 2% of the oral bedaquiline arm [[Goodall2022]].
- A 6-month bedaquiline regimen with 8 weeks of injectable gave 91% favourable outcomes, against 69% on the concurrent control (adjusted difference 22.2%) [[Goodall2022]].
- At 132 weeks, unfavourable outcomes were 19.6% (oral) versus 29.3% (control). Death rates were 1.01 per 100 person-years on bedaquiline arms versus 1.52 on control, so earlier bedaquiline mortality concerns were not substantiated [[Goodall2024]].

### drtb · New drugs: bedaquiline and delamanid
- In the C208 phase 2b trial (160 people with MDR-TB), bedaquiline cut median time to culture conversion from 125 to 83 days (HR 2.44) [[Diacon2014]].
- In C208, culture conversion at 24 weeks was 79% versus 58%. Cure at 120 weeks was 58% versus 32% [[Diacon2014]].
- C208 recorded 10 deaths in the bedaquiline group and 2 in the placebo group, with no causal pattern evident [[Diacon2014]].
- The current US bedaquiline label keeps a boxed warning for QTc prolongation. It reports the mortality imbalance from that trial (9/79, 11.4% versus 2/81, 2.5%) in Warnings and Precautions [[FDA-Sirturo]].
- The bedaquiline label's indication covers adults and children aged 2 years and older weighing at least 8 kg with pulmonary TB resistant to at least rifampin and isoniazid. It requires an ECG, liver enzymes and electrolytes before starting [[FDA-Sirturo]].
- In a phase 3 trial (511 adults with MDR-TB), adding delamanid to an optimised background regimen did not significantly shorten time to culture conversion: median 51 versus 57 days, HR 1.17 (95% CI 0.91–1.51) [[VonGroote2019]].
- In ACTG A5343, mean QTcF increases were 12.3 ms with bedaquiline, 8.6 ms with delamanid and 20.7 ms with both. There were no grade 3–4 QTc events, so the combined effect was no more than additive [[Dooley2021LID]].

### drtb · BPaL: Nix-TB and ZeNix
- Nix-TB (single-arm, 109 people with XDR-TB or treatment-intolerant or non-responsive MDR-TB) gave 26 weeks of bedaquiline, pretomanid and linezolid 1200 mg. 90% (95% CI 83–95) had a favourable outcome 6 months after treatment [[Conradie2020]].
- In Nix-TB, the 11 unfavourable outcomes were 7 deaths, 1 withdrawal, 2 relapses and 1 loss to follow-up [[Conradie2020]].
- Linezolid toxicity in Nix-TB was common: peripheral neuropathy in 81% and myelosuppression in 48%. It was manageable with dose changes [[Conradie2020]].
- ZeNix (181 people, 88% with XDR or pre-XDR-TB by the old definitions) randomised four linezolid strategies within BPaL [[Conradie2022]].
  - 1200 mg for 26 weeks: favourable outcome 93%, peripheral neuropathy 38%, myelosuppression 22%, linezolid dose modified in 51%.
  - 1200 mg for 9 weeks: 89%, 24%, 15%, 30%.
  - 600 mg for 26 weeks: 91%, 24%, 2%, 13%.
  - 600 mg for 9 weeks: 84%, 13%, 7%, 13%.
- In ZeNix, optic neuropathy occurred in 4 participants (9%) on 1200 mg for 26 weeks, and all cases resolved. Six of the seven unfavourable microbiological outcomes were in the 9-week linezolid arms [[Conradie2022]].
- The ZeNix authors judged 600 mg for 26 weeks to have the best risk–benefit balance [[Conradie2022]]. WHO recommends linezolid 600 mg once daily in both BPaLM and BPaL, with reduction to 300 mg allowed for toxicity [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- The US pretomanid label limits use to a combination with bedaquiline and linezolid in adults with highly resistant pulmonary TB, or MDR-TB that is treatment-intolerant or non-responsive. It states that approval rests on limited data [[FDA-Pretomanid]].
- The pretomanid label's warnings cover the following, with monitoring of liver tests, complete blood counts, visual function and ECGs [[FDA-Pretomanid]]:
  - hepatotoxicity
  - myelosuppression
  - peripheral and optic neuropathy
  - QT prolongation
  - lactic acidosis
  - testicular toxicity in rats

### drtb · BPaLM: TB-PRACTECAL
- TB-PRACTECAL stage 2 (Belarus, South Africa, Uzbekistan; aged 15 years or older) compared 24 weeks of bedaquiline, pretomanid, linezolid and moxifloxacin (BPaLM) with 9–20-month standard care [[Nyangwa2022]].
- At 72 weeks (modified ITT), unfavourable outcomes occurred in 11% on BPaLM and 48% on standard care (risk difference −37 points, 96.6% CI −53 to −22) [[Nyangwa2022]].
- In the per-protocol population, unfavourable outcomes were 4% versus 12% (risk difference −9 points, 96.6% CI −22 to 4) [[Nyangwa2022]].
- Grade 3 or higher or serious adverse events occurred in 19% on BPaLM versus 59% on standard care [[Nyangwa2022]].
- Recruitment stopped early; 145, 128 and 90 participants were evaluable in the ITT, modified ITT and per-protocol populations [[Nyangwa2022]].
- WHO's evidence summary cites treatment success of 89% with BPaLM versus 52% with standard care in TB-PRACTECAL stage 2 [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].

### drtb · endTB, endTB-Q and BEAT Tuberculosis
- endTB (754 randomised; rifampin-resistant, fluoroquinolone-susceptible TB) compared five 9-month all-oral regimens with standard therapy. Standard therapy gave 80.7% favourable outcomes at week 73 [[Guglielmetti2025NEJM]].
- Risk differences against standard therapy for the regimens that met noninferiority were [[Guglielmetti2025NEJM]]:
  - BCLLfxZ: +9.8 points
  - BLMZ: +8.3
  - BDLLfxZ: +4.6
  - DCMZ: +2.5 (not noninferior per protocol)
- In endTB, grade 3 or higher hepatotoxic events occurred in 11.7% of participants overall and 7.1% of those on standard therapy [[Guglielmetti2025NEJM]].
- endTB-Q (324 people with pre-XDR-TB) tested BDLC (bedaquiline, delamanid, linezolid, clofazimine) for 6 or 9 months against an individualised longer regimen. It reached 87% versus 89% favourable outcomes, but overall noninferiority was not shown [[Guglielmetti2025LRM]].
- endTB-Q's authors suggest that people with fluoroquinolone-resistant TB may need longer, reinforced regimens (Early evidence) [[Guglielmetti2025LRM]].
- BEAT Tuberculosis (South Africa, 403 randomised, aged 6 years or older, including people who were pregnant or breastfeeding and people with fluoroquinolone resistance) tested 6 months of bedaquiline, linezolid and delamanid plus levofloxacin and/or clofazimine [[Conradie2026]].
- In BEAT, success was 86.1% (174/202) versus 86.0% (172/200) on the 9-month standard of care. The adjusted difference was −0.2 points (95% CI −6.9 to 6.5) [[Conradie2026]].
- In BEAT, grade 3 or higher adverse events occurred in 31.2% versus 37.0%, and 10 participants died in each group [[Conradie2026]].

### drtb · WHO 2025 and ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA 2025 recommendations
- WHO suggests the 6-month BPaLM regimen rather than 9-month or longer regimens for MDR/RR-TB (conditional recommendation, very low certainty). BPaL without moxifloxacin is used when fluoroquinolone resistance is documented [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- BPaLM/BPaL eligibility in WHO 2025 [[WHO-Mod4-2025]]:
  - aged 14 years or older, regardless of HIV status
  - pulmonary TB, or extrapulmonary TB except CNS, osteoarticular or disseminated multiorgan disease
  - less than 1 month of previous exposure to bedaquiline, linezolid, pretomanid or delamanid
  - the recommendation does not apply in pregnancy or breastfeeding, because safety data on pretomanid are limited
- WHO 2025 newly suggests the 6-month BDLLfxC regimen for MDR/RR-TB with or without fluoroquinolone resistance. Its eligibility covers people of all ages, including children, people with HIV, and pregnant and breastfeeding women [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- After drug-susceptibility testing, BDLLfxC is trimmed: to BDLLfx when the strain is fluoroquinolone-susceptible, or to BDLC when it is fluoroquinolone-resistant [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- WHO suggests the 9-month all-oral regimens BLMZ, BLLfxCZ and BDLLfxZ (in that order of preference) over longer regimens when fluoroquinolone resistance has been excluded. It suggests against DCLLfxZ and DCMZ [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- Longer regimens are now a last resort [[WHO-Mod4-2025]]:
  - 18–20 months in total for most people
  - kanamycin and capreomycin are not to be used
  - amikacin may be used only in adults with demonstrated susceptibility and adequate monitoring for adverse reactions
- For Hr-TB, WHO recommends rifampicin, ethambutol, pyrazinamide and levofloxacin for 6 months [[WHO-Mod4-2025]]. An individual patient data meta-analysis found that adding a fluoroquinolone to at least 6 months of (H)REZ was associated with greater success (aOR 2.8, 95% CI 1.1–7.3) [[Fregonese2018]].
- WHO bedaquiline dosing options in the 6-month regimens are 200 mg daily for 8 weeks then 100 mg daily, or 400 mg daily for 2 weeks then 200 mg three times weekly, as published [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- Monitoring in WHO guidance [[WHO-Mod4-2025]]:
  - an ECG before starting treatment
  - checking potassium, calcium and magnesium when QTc is prolonged
  - liver tests and a complete blood count
  - watching for peripheral and optic neuropathy
  - audiometry when certain agents are used
- ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA (2025) recommend 6-month BPaLM for rifampin-resistant, fluoroquinolone-susceptible pulmonary TB in people aged 14 years and older, rather than regimens of 15 months or longer (strong recommendation, very low certainty) [[Saukkonen2025]].
- The same guideline recommends 6-month BPaL when there is fluoroquinolone resistance or intolerance and less than 1 month of prior bedaquiline and linezolid exposure (strong recommendation, very low certainty) [[Saukkonen2025]].
- By 2024, 6-month regimens were in use in 97 countries, up from 41 at the end of 2022 [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- 34 256 people started a 6-month regimen in 2024, against 5653 in 2023 and 1744 in 2022 [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Even so, 54% of people with MDR/RR-TB were still on longer (18–20-month) regimens in 2024, 21% on 9-month regimens and 21% on 6-month regimens [[WHO-GTR2025]].

### special · TB and HIV: burden
- In 2024, an estimated 150 000 TB deaths occurred among people with HIV (95% UI 120 000–183 000), out of 1.23 million TB deaths in total [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- WHO classifies these deaths as HIV deaths with TB as a contributory cause [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- In autopsy studies of adults with HIV in resource-limited settings, TB was found in a pooled 39.7% (95% CI 32.4–47.0) of bodies and was the cause of death in 37.2% of adult HIV/AIDS-related deaths [[GuptaRK2015AIDS]].
- In those autopsies, TB was disseminated in 87.9% of adult cases and undiagnosed at death in 45.8% [[GuptaRK2015AIDS]].
- Treatment success for drug-susceptible TB in people with HIV was 79% in the 2023 cohort, against 88% overall [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Only 61% of people with HIV estimated to have developed TB in 2024 received ART [[WHO-GTR2025]].

### special · TB and HIV: when to start ART
- SAPIT (642 people, Durban, CD4 <500): starting ART during TB treatment cut deaths to 5.4 per 100 person-years, against 12.1 when ART waited until after TB treatment (HR 0.44, a 56% relative reduction) [[AbdoolKarim2010]].
- In SAPIT's comparison of earlier and later integrated ART, AIDS or death occurred at 6.9 versus 7.8 per 100 person-years overall [[AbdoolKarim2011]].
- In SAPIT, among people with CD4 below 50, AIDS or death occurred at 8.5 versus 26.3 per 100 person-years. IRIS occurred at 20.1 versus 7.7 per 100 person-years (incidence-rate ratio 2.62) [[AbdoolKarim2011]].
- CAMELIA (661 adults, Cambodia, median CD4 25): starting ART at 2 weeks rather than 8 weeks reduced deaths from 27% to 18% (HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.44–0.86) and raised the risk of TB-IRIS (HR 2.51) [[Blanc2011]].
- STRIDE (ACTG A5221; 809 people, CD4 <250): overall, AIDS or death was 12.9% with earlier ART versus 16.1% with later ART (not significant) [[Havlir2011]].
- In STRIDE, among people with CD4 below 50, the figures were 15.5% versus 26.6%. TB-IRIS occurred in 11% versus 5% [[Havlir2011]].
- TB meningitis is the exception. In a Vietnamese trial of 253 people, immediate ART did not reduce 9-month mortality (HR 1.12) and caused more grade 4 adverse events (102 versus 87) [[Torok2011]].
- WHO recommends starting ART as soon as possible within 2 weeks of starting TB treatment, regardless of CD4 count (strong recommendation), except when signs of meningitis are present, where a delay of 4–8 weeks may be considered [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- In people with drug-resistant TB needing second-line drugs, WHO recommends ART as early as possible, within the first 8 weeks [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].

### special · TB-IRIS, prednisone and co-trimoxazole
- In a meta-analysis of 40 studies, paradoxical TB-IRIS affected a pooled 18% (95% CI 16–21%) of people with HIV-associated TB starting ART [[Namale2015]].
- In the same meta-analysis, 25% of people with TB-IRIS were hospitalised and case fatality was 7%. Deaths attributed to TB-IRIS were 2% [[Namale2015]].
- PredART (240 people, CD4 ≤100) gave prednisone 40 mg/day for 14 days then 20 mg/day for 14 days. TB-IRIS fell from 46.7% to 32.5% (RR 0.70, 95% CI 0.51–0.96), with no excess of severe infections or cancers [[Meintjes2018]].
- In Abidjan, daily co-trimoxazole for people with HIV and smear-positive TB reduced deaths from 25.4 to 13.8 per 100 person-years (a 46% decrease in risk) and reduced hospital admissions by 43% [[Wiktor1999]].

### special · TB and HIV: drug interactions
- In healthy volunteers, dolutegravir 50 mg twice daily with rifampin gave an AUC 1.33 times that of dolutegravir 50 mg once daily without rifampin. This is the basis for doubling to twice daily [[Dooley2013]].
- The US dolutegravir label says to adjust dosing to twice daily with rifampin in INSTI-naive adults, and to use alternatives to rifampin where possible when INSTI resistance is suspected [[FDA-Tivicay]].
- INSPIRING: in adults with HIV on rifampicin-based TB treatment, twice-daily dolutegravir gave a 48-week viral suppression rate of 75% (efavirenz 82%; not powered to compare). TB-IRIS was uncommon (4 per arm) [[Dooley2020CID]].

### special · Children
- In pre-chemotherapy studies, primary infection before age 2 often progressed to serious disease within 12 months, without much warning [[Marais2004]].
- Infection between ages 2 and 10 rarely progressed, and infection after age 10 often led to adult-type disease [[Marais2004]].
- Children with HIV behaved like children under 2 [[Marais2004]].
- Among 130 512 exposed children followed for 429 538 person-years, the 2-year cumulative TB incidence in untreated children under 5 with a positive infection test was 19.0% (95% CI 8.4–37.4) [[Martinez2020Lancet]].
- In that cohort, preventive therapy was 91% effective in children with a positive infection test. 83% of under-5s who developed TB were diagnosed within 90 days of the first visit [[Martinez2020Lancet]].
- Before treatment existed, the pooled case fatality of childhood TB was 21.9%: 43.6% at ages 0–4 and 14.9% at ages 5–14. In the recent era, with treatment, it was 0.9% [[Jenkins2017]].
- An estimated 239 000 children died of TB in 2015, 80% of them under 5. More than 96% of these deaths were in children not receiving TB treatment [[Dodd2017]].
- WHO estimated 174 000 TB deaths in children under 15 in 2024, of which 122 000 were HIV-negative children under 5 [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- In childhood TB meningitis (19 studies, 1636 children), risk of death was 19.3% [[Chiang2014]].
- In those same studies, 53.9% of survivors had neurological sequelae and only 36.7% of children survived without sequelae [[Chiang2014]].
- Up to 15% of childhood TB may present as TB meningitis [[WHO-Mod5-2022]].
- Treatment success for childhood MDR-TB was pooled at 81.67% in 315 children. 5.9% died and 39.1% had an adverse event, including hearing loss [[Ettehad2012]].
- In a later individual participant data meta-analysis (23 369 children and adolescents with MDR/RR-TB), 72.0% were successfully treated and 12.2% died [[GarciaPrats2025]].
- In that analysis, using two or three Group A drugs was associated with better success than none (aOR 1.41 and 2.12) [[GarciaPrats2025]].
- WHO encourages child-friendly dispersible fixed-dose combinations (HRZ 50/75/150 mg and HR 50/75 mg) for children up to 25 kg. Dispersible or small-strength forms of bedaquiline (20 mg), delamanid (25 mg) and ethionamide (125 mg) are described for DR-TB [[WHO-Mod5-2022]].
- Treatment success for drug-susceptible TB in children aged 0–14 was 92% in the 2023 cohort [[WHO-GTR2025]].

### special · Pregnancy and the postpartum period
- An estimated 216 500 pregnant women had active TB worldwide in 2011 (95% uncertainty range 192 100–247 000) [[Sugarman2014]].
- In UK primary-care data, the postpartum TB rate was significantly raised (adjusted IRR 1.95, 95% CI 1.24–3.07). The increase during pregnancy was not significant (IRR 1.29) [[Zenner2012]].
- In Sweden (649 342 women), TB rates were 9 per 100 000 person-years outside pregnancy, 12 during pregnancy (IRR 1.4) and 17 postpartum (IRR 1.9) [[Jonsson2020]].
- In the Swedish cohort, the excess was concentrated in women from countries with TB incidence of 100 per 100 000 or higher. Their rates were 137, 182 and 233 per 100 000 person-years across the same three periods [[Jonsson2020]].
- Active TB in pregnancy was associated with [[Sobhy2017]]:
  - maternal morbidity (OR 2.8)
  - anaemia (OR 3.9)
  - preterm birth (OR 1.7)
  - low birth weight (OR 1.7)
  - birth asphyxia (OR 4.6)
  - perinatal death (OR 4.2)
- TB APPRISE (956 pregnant women with HIV on ART) found isoniazid preventive therapy started in pregnancy noninferior to starting at 12 weeks postpartum for maternal toxicity (15.1% versus 15.2%) [[Gupta2019NEJM]].
- In TB APPRISE, adverse pregnancy outcomes were more common with antepartum isoniazid (23.6% versus 17.0%) [[Gupta2019NEJM]].
- BPaLM is not recommended in pregnancy or breastfeeding [[WHO-Mod4-2025]]. BEAT Tuberculosis enrolled pregnant and breastfeeding people, and WHO's BDLLfxC recommendation covers them [[Conradie2026,WHO-Mod4-2025]].

### special · Diabetes
- Diabetes was associated with a roughly threefold risk of active TB in cohort studies (RR 3.11, 95% CI 2.27–4.26) [[Jeon2008]].
- In people treated for TB, diabetes was associated with a higher risk of failure or death combined (RR 1.69) and of death (RR 1.89 unadjusted; 4.95 in the 4 studies adjusted for age and other factors) [[Baker2011]].
- Diabetes was also associated with a higher risk of relapse (RR 3.89, 95% CI 2.43–6.23) [[Baker2011]].
- Baker et al. found no evidence that diabetes raises the risk of recurrence with drug-resistant strains [[Baker2011]].

### spectrum · Natural history without treatment
- In HIV-negative people with untreated smear-positive pulmonary TB, 10-year case fatality was reported between 53% and 86%, with a weighted mean of 70% [[Tiemersma2011]].
- For untreated culture-positive, smear-negative TB, 10-year case fatality is estimated at about 20%. Untreated disease lasted about 3 years from onset to cure or death [[Tiemersma2011]].
- A Bayesian reanalysis of the same pre-chemotherapy data estimated TB-specific mortality of 0.389 per year for smear-positive and 0.025 per year for smear-negative TB, roughly a 15-fold difference [[Ragonnet2021]].
- The same reanalysis estimated self-recovery rates of 0.231 and 0.130 per year, giving average untreated durations of 1.57 years (smear-positive) and 5.35 years (smear-negative) [[Ragonnet2021]].
- WHO's global case fatality ratio (the share of people who develop TB that die from it) was 11.5% in 2024, down from 13.6% in 2019. This includes people who were never diagnosed or treated [[WHO-GTR2025]].

### spectrum · Recurrence after cure
- Across 145 studies, the pooled recurrence rate after successful treatment was 2.26 per 100 person-years. It was 1.47 in low-incidence settings and 4.10 in high-incidence settings [[Vega2021]].
- Relapse (the same strain) accounted for a pooled 70% of recurrences: 83% in low-incidence settings and 59% in high-incidence settings, where reinfection is more common [[Vega2021]].

### living · Post-TB lung disease and long-term mortality
- An estimated 58 million people survived TB between 2000 and the time of the review [[Allwood2021]].
- Post-TB lung disease spans bronchiectasis, obstructive disease, parenchymal damage, pulmonary vascular disease and pleural disease. Its risk factors include multiple TB episodes, drug-resistant TB and diagnostic delay [[Allwood2021]].
- Expert opinion, not trial evidence, supports pulmonary rehabilitation, stopping smoking, and pneumococcal and influenza vaccination for post-TB lung disease. Repeated empirical courses of TB drugs should be avoided [[Allwood2021]].
- A history of TB was associated with COPD in adults over 40 (pooled OR 3.05, 95% CI 2.42–3.85). The association was strongest in high-incidence countries, in never-smokers and in younger people [[Byrne2015]].
- People treated for TB had higher all-cause mortality after treatment than the general population or controls (pooled SMR 2.91, 95% CI 2.21–3.84) [[Romanowski2019]].
- Among people confirmed cured or who completed treatment, the SMR was 3.76 (95% CI 3.04–4.66). Cardiovascular disease was the most common cause of post-treatment death (20%) [[Romanowski2019]].
- Including post-TB sequelae, incident TB in 2019 caused an estimated 122 million DALYs. Of these, 58 million (47%) were attributed to post-TB sequelae [[Menzies2021LGH]].
- That is 12.1 DALYs per incident case, of which 5.8 come after the disease episode. 28% of the total accrues 15 or more years later [[Menzies2021LGH]].

### living · Costs to households
- In national surveys completed 2015–2025 and reported to WHO (40 countries), a pooled 47% (95% CI 37–58%) of TB-affected households faced catastrophic total costs, defined as more than 20% of annual household income [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- For people with drug-resistant TB, the pooled share facing catastrophic costs was 82% (95% CI 71–93%) [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- Average total cost per TB episode ranged from US$ 78 in the Gambia to US$ 3800 in Mongolia [[WHO-GTR2025]].
- A meta-regression covering 135 low- and middle-income countries estimated a mean cost per episode to patients of US$ 1253, even where treatment is free [[Portnoy2023]].
  - indirect costs: US$ 530
  - direct non-medical costs: US$ 512
  - direct medical costs: US$ 211
- The estimated share of households with catastrophic costs was 54.9% overall: 75.2% in the poorest quintile and 42.5% in the richest [[Portnoy2023]].

### Exam hooks
- Rifampin has the lowest spontaneous mutation rate of the first-line drugs, about 2.25 × 10⁻¹⁰ per bacterium per generation [[David1970]].
- Most MDR-TB in high-burden settings is transmitted, not acquired: a median 95.9% in a global model [[Kendall2015]].
- Pre-XDR-TB means rifampicin plus fluoroquinolone resistance. XDR-TB adds resistance to bedaquiline or linezolid [[WHO-Mod4-2025,Viney2021]].
- BPaLM = bedaquiline, pretomanid, linezolid (600 mg), moxifloxacin, for 6 months, from age 14 [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- TB-PRACTECAL: unfavourable outcomes 11% (BPaLM) versus 48% (standard care) at 72 weeks [[Nyangwa2022]].
- ZeNix: linezolid 600 mg for 26 weeks kept efficacy (91%) and cut myelosuppression to 2% [[Conradie2022]].
- Linezolid's three classic toxicities: peripheral neuropathy, optic neuropathy, myelosuppression [[Conradie2020,FDA-Pretomanid]].
- Bedaquiline's boxed warning is QTc prolongation, so take an ECG before starting [[FDA-Sirturo]].
- Aminoglycosides caused hearing loss in about 40% of people treated for DR-TB. WHO no longer uses kanamycin or capreomycin [[Dillard2021,WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- BDLLfxC (BEAT Tuberculosis) is the 6-month option that also covers children and pregnancy [[Conradie2026,WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- Hr-TB is treated with 6 months of rifampicin, ethambutol, pyrazinamide and levofloxacin [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- HIV and TB: start ART within 2 weeks, except in TB meningitis [[WHO-Mod4-2025,Torok2011]].
- CAMELIA: ART at 2 weeks versus 8 weeks cut mortality from 27% to 18% [[Blanc2011]].
- Early ART about doubles to triples IRIS risk; prophylactic prednisone (PredART) cuts it by about 30% [[AbdoolKarim2011,Meintjes2018]].
- Dolutegravir goes twice daily with rifampicin [[FDA-Tivicay,Dooley2013]].
- Untreated smear-positive TB: about 70% dead at 10 years; smear-negative about 20% [[Tiemersma2011]].
- TB risk rises after delivery: postpartum IRR about 1.9–2 [[Zenner2012,Jonsson2020]].
- Diabetes roughly triples the risk of TB disease and nearly quadruples relapse risk [[Jeon2008,Baker2011]].
- Survivors of TB have about 3 times the mortality of the general population [[Romanowski2019]].
- 82% of households affected by drug-resistant TB face catastrophic costs [[WHO-GTR2025]].

### In plain words
- Drug-resistant TB means some TB medicines no longer work against the germ. It can be cured with other medicines [[WHO-GTR2025,Nyangwa2022]].
- Most people with drug-resistant TB caught it from someone else. Having it does not mean anyone did something wrong [[Kendall2015,Shah2017NEJM]].
- Treatment used to take about two years and daily injections that could damage hearing. Today most people can take tablets only, for 6 to 9 months [[Dillard2021,WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- The new regimens cured about nine in ten people in trials [[Nyangwa2022,Conradie2026]].
- One of the new medicines, linezolid, can cause tingling in the hands and feet, vision changes or low blood counts. Tell your care team early, because the dose can be adjusted [[Conradie2022,FDA-Pretomanid]].
- Heart tracings (ECGs) and blood tests during treatment are routine safety checks, not a sign that something is wrong [[WHO-Mod4-2025,FDA-Sirturo]].
- If you have HIV and TB, HIV medicines usually start within two weeks of TB medicines. This saves lives [[WHO-Mod4-2025,Blanc2011]].
- Babies and young children who live with someone who has TB should be checked quickly, because they can get very sick fast [[Martinez2020Lancet,Marais2004]].
- TB during pregnancy can be treated, and treating it protects both mother and baby [[Sobhy2017,WHO-Mod4-2025]].
- If you have diabetes, keeping blood sugar under control helps TB treatment work [[Baker2011]].
- Some people still have breathing problems after TB is cured. Lung check-ups and not smoking help [[Allwood2021,Byrne2015]].
- The percentages here describe large groups of people, not what will happen to any one person [[Tiemersma2011,Romanowski2019]].
- TB medicines are usually free, but travel and lost work still cost families money. Ask your clinic about support [[WHO-GTR2025,Portnoy2023]].
- TB is a notifiable disease. Anyone with a long cough, fever, night sweats or weight loss should see a clinician [[WHO-GTR2025]].

## Open questions and controversies
- Bedaquiline and mortality: C208 recorded 10 versus 2 deaths and the US label still reports this imbalance [[Diacon2014,FDA-Sirturo]]. Observational data link bedaquiline to lower mortality [[Ahmad2018Lancet]], and STREAM stage 2 at 132 weeks found no excess deaths [[Goodall2024]]. Status: most evidence now points away from harm, but the label wording stands.
- Trial populations versus programme reality: TB-PRACTECAL stopped early and its per-protocol population was small (90), giving a wide CI (−22 to 4) [[Nyangwa2022]]. WHO graded its BPaLM recommendation very low certainty [[WHO-Mod4-2025]]. ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA made a strong recommendation on the same evidence [[Saukkonen2025]]. Status: same direction, different strength.
- Fluoroquinolone-resistant (pre-XDR) TB: BPaL is recommended [[WHO-Mod4-2025,Saukkonen2025]]. A shortened BDLC strategy (endTB-Q) did not show overall noninferiority, and its authors suggest that longer, reinforced regimens may be needed [[Guglielmetti2025LRM]]. Status: unresolved (Early evidence).
- Linezolid dose and duration: 1200 mg gave slightly higher favourable outcomes but more toxicity, and 9-week arms had more microbiological failures [[Conradie2022]]. WHO prefers 600 mg throughout, with reduction to 300 mg allowed [[WHO-Mod4-2025]]. Status: 600 mg for 26 weeks is the consensus.
- Pregnancy and drug-resistant TB: BPaLM excludes pregnancy for lack of pretomanid data [[WHO-Mod4-2025]]. BEAT enrolled pregnant participants, and WHO extends BDLLfxC to them [[Conradie2026,WHO-Mod4-2025]]. Status: the pregnancy safety data are still thin.
- Timing of ART in TB meningitis: immediate ART gave no survival gain and more grade 4 events [[Torok2011]]. WHO allows a 4–8-week delay [[WHO-Mod4-2025]]. Status: the trial is small and from the pre-dolutegravir era.
- Childhood TB meningitis sequelae: Chiang et al. report 53.9% sequelae among survivors and 36.7% survival without sequelae [[Chiang2014]]. The WHO Module 5 operational handbook cites the 36.7% figure as the "risk of neurological sequelae among survivors", which appears to be a transcription slip (noted in Provenance). Status: use the primary paper.
- How much MDR-TB is transmitted: the global model gives a median 95.9% but with a wide range (68.0–99.6) [[Kendall2015]]. Genomic data from KwaZulu-Natal support a transmission-dominant picture for XDR-TB [[Shah2017NEJM]]. Status: transmission dominance is well supported (Established); the exact share varies by setting.
- Post-TB care: a large share of TB burden lies after cure [[Menzies2021LGH,Romanowski2019]], but no trial-based guidance exists for post-TB lung disease [[Allwood2021]]. Status: research gap.

## Provenance
- **How sources were found and checked.** Searches used Europe PMC REST (title, author and year queries). Every PubMed-indexed record was then confirmed with one batched NCBI E-utilities esummary call. Authors (first six), title, journal, year, volume, issue, pages, PMID, PMCID and DOI in the Sources lines were generated from that esummary output. The ERJ article numbers for Viney 2021 and Jonsson 2020 were added from the PubMed citations.
- **What was read.** Abstracts for all 56 PubMed journal articles were fetched with efetch (rettype=abstract) and read. Every number is quoted as the abstract states it.
- **Guidelines.** WHO Module 4 (2025) and Module 5 (2022) were confirmed as PubMed/NCBI Bookshelf records (PMIDs 40163610, 35404556). Their recommendation wording was read from the full-text files WHO publishes in IRIS, because NCBI Bookshelf pages returned a browser check that was not bypassed. ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA 2025 recommendation wording was read from the Europe PMC full text (PMC11755361).
- **US labels.** Labels were pulled from the openFDA drug label endpoint on 2026-10-25 (Sirturo, Pretomanid, Tivicay). The Tivicay record in openFDA is a repackager listing that carries the ViiV label text.
- **WHO Global TB Report 2025 (unverified).** It has no PubMed, Europe PMC or Crossref record. Figures were read from the WHO online report pages on 2026-10-25, and the line is marked unverified. The roughly 42% treatment-coverage figure for MDR/RR-TB is my own division of two WHO numbers and is labelled as calculated.
- **Not quoted.** The age-band disease percentages in Marais 2004 were not quoted, because only the abstract was accessible.
- **Discrepancy found.** The WHO Module 5 operational handbook describes the 36.7% from Chiang 2014 as the "risk of neurological sequelae among survivors". The primary abstract gives 53.9% sequelae among survivors and 36.7% survival without sequelae. This file uses the primary paper.
- **Checked but not cited.** Linh 2021 (WHO outcome definitions, PMID 34413124) was validated but not cited, because its record has no abstract and I took no facts from it.
- **Not used.** No CDC MMWR pretomanid guidance and no catastrophic-cost primary surveys were used. Household costs rest on the WHO report and Portnoy 2023.
- **Dropped.** "TB as leading cause of death in people with HIV, 12-fold risk" figures from the WHO fact sheet were dropped, because the fact sheet is a separate unvalidated document.
- **Reuse check.** No key here duplicates one in another companion's src/sources.json; the tuberculosis sources.json was empty at pass time.
