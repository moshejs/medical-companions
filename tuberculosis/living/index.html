Part 5 · Living with TB

Living with TB

Six months of treatment is a long road, and most of it happens at home, at work and with family. Follow one person from the first cough to cure and beyond, and switch a support package on and off to see what the trials found.

What support did in trials

WHO recommends health education and counselling for everyone on TB treatment (strong recommendation, moderate certainty), and says a package of adherence interventions may be offered with a suitable way of taking treatment (conditional, low certainty). Material support means food or money: meals, food baskets, vouchers, transport subsidies, living allowances or housing incentives, to cover indirect costs and soften lost income. 515

Everyday questions

Work & school

Going back

When you can go back depends on whether you can still pass TB on, and your clinician decides. For health workers, CDC states the published rule: return after three consecutive negative sputum samples (8 to 24 hours apart, one early in the morning), a response to treatment that is probably effective, and a physician's judgement that they are no longer infectious. 249

For people sent home before their sputum turns negative, CDC lists conditions: a follow-up plan with the TB programme, DOT arranged, no children under 4 or immunocompromised people in the home, and a willingness not to go out except for health-care visits. 249 CDC counts disease of the lung, airway or larynx, cough and a positive smear among the signs that someone is infectious. 249 More in Infection control & contacts.

Pregnancy

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

  • There is no absolute contraindication to breastfeeding during TB treatment, and breastmilk is best for newborns in most settings. Parents who do not breastfeed should be supported too. 314
  • Infection control should not usually separate a parent from their newborn: most pregnant people on effective therapy are unlikely to pass TB on. 314
  • The isoniazid label says the low levels in breast milk "do not threaten the neonate"; the ethambutol label says the drug is excreted in breast milk. 159,160
  • Rifampicin can make the pill and other hormonal contraceptives less reliable; the label advises considering other methods. 168
  • The best-practice guidance suggests an emergency supply of TB medicines and a referral letter before the due date. 314 More in TB with HIV, in children & pregnancy.
Alcohol & smoking

They make treatment work less well

Alcohol use roughly doubled the odds of death, treatment failure or loss to follow-up (OR 1.99 in drug-susceptible TB, 2.00 in MDR-TB; 111 studies). 384 Smoking was linked to poorer outcomes (OR 1.51) and slower sputum conversion. 52,506 Smoking plus alcohol misuse raised unfavourable outcomes about 3.6- to 4.7-fold in one Indian cohort. 466

The rifampin label asks people to avoid alcohol and liver-toxic drugs or herbs. 168 WHO counts alcohol-cessation counselling, self-help groups and TB clubs as psychological support. 515 Your clinic can help you cut down or quit.

Family

Talking to family and children

TB is nobody's fault: it is caused by a germ in the air, and it can be cured. Household contacts are checked because they shared the air, not because anyone did something wrong. About half of household contacts in high-burden settings have TB infection and about 3% have TB disease. 185

Children under 5 who live with someone with TB are high priority: they may be given preventive medicine once TB disease is excluded, even before a second test. 355 WHO suggests family-centred, integrated care for children and adolescents. 515 Feeding the whole household can protect it: monthly food rations cut TB in contacts by 39% to 48%. 39

Stigma & rights

Privacy, fairness and work

In a national survey in Ethiopia, fear of losing a job (32.5%) and of isolation (15.3%) shaped whether people disclosed TB; more knowledge went with less stigma. 108 In Lusaka, 82% of people who described their experience reported stigma, and women more often than men. 95

Public-health interviews take place in your own language, with an interpreter if needed, and you are told how confidentiality is protected and where its limits are. 355 Where communicable-disease laws are used, NTCA/CDC states that "the least restrictive measures" come first. 355 Employment and disability protections vary by country: a TB nurse or social worker can point you to local advice.

Help

Where to get help

  • Your TB clinic or programme: in most national programmes treatment is free and effective. Ask what food, transport and cash support exists; WHO lists all three as material support. 515
  • Your nurse, if you feel low: depression affected about 45% of people with TB in a pooled analysis (52% in MDR-TB), and it is treatable. 143
  • Community or home-based treatment support is preferred by WHO over clinic-only or unsupported treatment. 515
  • After treatment: ask about a lung check and rehabilitation if breathing is still hard. 324
  • TB is a notifiable disease. Anyone with a long cough, fever, night sweats or weight loss should see a clinician.
Words matter

Person-first language

Authors from TB programmes on five continents proposed "person with presumptive TB" instead of "TB suspect", "person lost to follow-up" instead of "defaulter", and "prevention and care" instead of "control". They called the old terms coercive and blaming. 529 This guide says "TB infection" (formerly latent TB) and "TB disease".
