Symptoms & where TB strikes

Most TB starts in the lungs, with a cough that will not settle, fever, night sweats and weight loss. About one case in six is outside the lungs. Pick who you are looking at, then press Play: the symptoms appear first, then the places TB can reach.

The whole body

Fever, night sweats, weight loss

Any TB symptom had a sensitivity of 70.6% and specificity of 65.1% in community screening (29 studies). 489 In national prevalence surveys a median 50.4% of people with confirmed TB had no symptoms on screening, while a chest X-ray detected 89%. That is why screening pairs symptoms with an X-ray. 189,489

In plain wordsTB can make the whole body unwell: fever, sweating at night and losing weight without trying. Some people with TB feel completely well.
Exam hookAbout half of TB found in surveys is subclinical: symptom screens miss it, and chest X-ray finds most of it (89%).

If you have these symptoms, see a clinician. A cough for 2 weeks or more, fever, night sweats or weight loss are reasons to get checked for TB, and some people with TB feel well. TB is a notifiable disease; testing and treatment are free in most public programmes, and treatment cures most people. 189,489

Screening tests on 1,000 people

Screening asks a simple question of many people to decide who needs a proper test, such as sputum Xpert. Choose a screen and how common TB is in the group, and watch what happens to 1,000 people. Accuracy figures are pooled estimates from the populations studied, so the same screen behaves very differently where TB is common or rare. 198,489

Of 1,000 people, 50 have TB. The screen flags 39 of them and misses 11; it also flags 479 people without TB. So 518 people would go on to a confirmatory test. Of those flagged, 7.5% have TB; of those cleared, 97.7% truly do not.
Anyone with symptoms should see a clinician, whatever a screen shows.

A teaching tool, never a self-assessment: no screen replaces a clinician. Sources: 98,118,198,216,489

Screen · people with HIV (12 studies)

WHO four-symptom screen

Sensitivity 78.9%, specificity 49.6%. 198 Developed from an individual-participant meta-analysis; adding any chest X-ray abnormality raised sensitivity to 90.6% and lowered specificity to 38.9%.

In plain wordsA screen sorts people into "test further" and "probably not TB". It is never a diagnosis, and its results change with how common TB is in the group.
Exam hookWhere TB is rare, a negative screen is reassuring (high NPV) but most positives are false alarms (low PPV).

Worth knowing

Established

Subclinical TB: disease without symptoms

In national prevalence surveys since 1990, a median 50.4% (range 36.1–79.7%) of people with bacteriologically confirmed TB screened negative on symptoms. A chest X-ray detected 89% of them. An international consensus now separates subclinical from clinical TB by reported symptoms or signs and by likely infectiousness. In 35 adults with HIV and TB infection but no symptoms, PET-CT found lung changes suggesting subclinical disease in 10, who were more likely to progress. 93,155,189

See TB infection vs TB disease.

Children

Hard to confirm, so treated on clinical grounds

Of 4,718 children under 10 assessed in 13 studies, 38.4% were classified as having TB, and only 29.9% of those were bacteriologically confirmed. WHO treatment-decision algorithms score symptoms with or without an X-ray (sensitivity 0.86, specificity 0.37 with X-ray), so primary-care workers can start treatment without a positive test. Consensus case definitions let research studies of childhood TB be compared. 206,211,520

HIV

The four-symptom screen fades on ART

In people taking antiretroviral therapy the screen caught 51.0% of TB, against 89.4% in people not yet on ART; specificity was 70.7% versus 28.1%. Adding any chest X-ray abnormality raised sensitivity on ART from 52.2% to 84.6% (two studies). 216

Early evidence

Which second screen: CRP or X-ray software?

In Ugandan adults with HIV starting ART, finger-prick CRP of 10 mg/L or more had 89% sensitivity and 72% specificity. 531 In people with HIV not on ART, a positive symptom screen followed by CRP of 5 mg/L or more kept sensitivity at 84% and raised specificity to 64%. In a multinational cohort of adults with cough, CAD4TB reached 70.3% specificity at 90% sensitivity against 49.7% for point-of-care CRP, and CRP then CAD4TB reached 75.9%. The best choice depends on the setting. 98,118
