An animated, cited guide to tuberculosis for students, people with TB and their families. Educational only, not medical advice. Your place is saved on this device.

Not medical adviceThe TB Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and for patient and family understanding. It does not replace a doctor, nurse or TB programme. Regimens, doses, test cut-offs and guideline statements are reported as published, not as instructions or a diagnosis for any individual. TB is a notifiable disease, and treatment is effective and free in most public programmes; anyone with symptoms such as a lasting cough should see a clinician.

Glossary

A searchable glossary of TB terms, from acid-fast bacilli and RD1 to 3HP, BPaLM, AII rooms and catastrophic costs. Type to filter; the arrow on each entry jumps to the module where the term matters most.

Entries marked Language note give person-first terms proposed by TB programme authors in place of blaming ones: "person with presumptive TB" rather than "TB suspect", "lost to follow-up" rather than "defaulter" 534. This guide also says TB infection for what used to be called latent TB, and TB disease for illness.

Active case findingScreening people who have not sought care. Community-wide Xpert screening in ACT3 cut pulmonary TB prevalence by 44% 310.
Adherence supportEducation, counselling, material, psychological and digital support to help people finish treatment 518.
Adenosine deaminase (ADA)A pleural-fluid marker of TB pleurisy: pooled sensitivity 0.92 and specificity 0.90 287.
Administrative controlsThe first and most important level of TB infection control: triage, separation and prompt treatment 250,513.
Air changes per hour (ACH)How many times a room's air volume is replaced each hour: exhaust airflow divided by room volume, times 60 250.

AminoglycosideInjectable drugs such as kanamycin and amikacin; they caused hearing loss in about 40% of people treated for DR-TB 127.
Antigen-based skin test (TBST)A skin test using ESAT-6 and CFP-10 instead of PPD, so BCG does not affect it 277,515.
ArabinogalactanThe sugar layer of the cell wall linking peptidoglycan to mycolic acids; ethambutol blocks arabinan synthesis 48,464.
ART (antiretroviral therapy)HIV treatment. It lowers TB incidence (HR 0.35) and should start within 2 weeks of TB treatment 453,518.
BCG (bacille Calmette-Guérin)A live attenuated M. bovis vaccine, first given in 1921, still the only licensed TB vaccine 279,300.
BCG revaccinationA second BCG dose. The 2025 phase 2b trial in adolescents showed no protection against sustained infection 416.
BDLLfxCA 6-month regimen of bedaquiline, delamanid, linezolid, levofloxacin and clofazimine for MDR/RR-TB, covering children and pregnancy 87,518.
BedaquilineA diarylquinoline that inhibits mycobacterial ATP synthase; boxed warning for QTc prolongation 14,170.
BPaLBedaquiline, pretomanid and linezolid for 6 months, used in non-severe pulmonary pre-XDR-TB; WHO 2026 adds clofazimine (BPaLC) when disease is severe 85,519.
BPaLMBPaL plus moxifloxacin, the 6-month regimen for MDR/RR-TB from age 14 359,518.
BronchiectasisDamaged, widened airways: one form of post-TB lung disease 9.
CAD (computer-aided detection)Software that scores a chest X-ray for TB; some products meet WHO triage targets 383.
Caseous necrosisThe soft, cheese-like dead centre of a granuloma, where drugs and oxygen reach poorly 412,497.
Catastrophic costsTB costs above 20% of annual household income or expenditure; faced by about half of TB-affected households 512.
CavityA hole left when a granuloma empties into an airway. Cavities hold huge numbers of bacilli and drive transmission 261,480.
CFP-10A secreted M. tuberculosis protein encoded in RD1, used with ESAT-6 in IGRAs 12,241.
ClofazimineA WHO Group B drug for DR-TB; Rv0678 mutations cause cross-resistance with bedaquiline 222,518.
Close contactA person who shared air with someone with infectious TB, for example in the same household 356.
Contact investigationFinding, assessing and testing people exposed to someone with TB; it raised case notification (RR 2.5) and cut mortality (RR 0.6) 493.
Culture conversionWhen sputum cultures turn negative on treatment. A positive culture at 2 months predicts relapse poorly (sensitivity 40%) 237.
CYP3A4 inductionRifampicin's speeding-up of drug metabolism, which lowers levels of many other drugs 354.
DelamanidA nitroimidazole prodrug that inhibits mycolic acid synthesis 314.
Digital adherence technology (DAT)Phone-based tools, digital pillboxes or ingestible sensors that record doses 451. Pillboxes did not improve outcomes in ASCENT 252.
Directly observed therapy (DOT)A health worker or supporter watches each dose, in person or by video. It is support, not suspicion 262,447.
Disseminated BCG diseaseSpread of the live vaccine through the body, mainly in infants with HIV or other immunodeficiency 231.
Droplet nucleusThe tiny dried residue of a respiratory droplet that floats in air and carries bacilli 175,510.
Drug-induced liver injury (DILI)Liver damage from TB drugs; reported in 2–28% of people treated 474.
Drug-susceptibility testing (DST)Testing which drugs work against a person's strain, by molecular or phenotypic methods 285,516.
Early bactericidal activityThe rapid early kill of growing bacilli, driven mainly by isoniazid 254,329.
End TB StrategyWHO's 2014 strategy: by 2035, 90% lower incidence and 95% fewer deaths than 2015, and no catastrophic costs 511.
Environmental controlsVentilation, upper-room UV and filtration: the second level of the hierarchy 250,513.
ESAT-6A 6 kDa secreted protein of virulent mycobacteria, absent from BCG 219.
ESX-1A type VII secretion system that exports ESAT-6 and CFP-10 and lets the bacillus breach its phagosome 207,482.
Ethambutol (E)A first-line drug that blocks arabinan synthesis; it can cause optic neuritis 160,464.
Extensively drug-resistant TB (XDR-TB)Rifampicin-resistant TB also resistant to a fluoroquinolone and to bedaquiline or linezolid (2021 definition) 499,518.
Extrapulmonary TBTB outside the lungs; 17% of notified TB in 2025 512, most often in lymph nodes 373.
FASTFind cases Actively, Separate safely, Treat effectively 472.
Fit testingChecking that a respirator model and size seals on the wearer's face 250,513.
Fixed-dose combination (FDC)Several TB drugs in one tablet; WHO recommends FDCs over separate drugs (conditional) 288,518.
FluoroquinoloneLevofloxacin or moxifloxacin; WHO Group A drugs. Resistance arises in gyrA 458,518.
Foamy macrophageA lipid-laden macrophage in granulomas that shelters dormant bacilli and feeds cavitation 374,409.
Four-symptom screenCough, fever, night sweats or weight loss: WHO's screen for TB in people with HIV 198.
Gastric aspirateA stomach sample used to find TB in children who cannot cough up sputum 265,285.
GranulomaAn organised ball of macrophages and lymphocytes around bacilli. It contains infection but can also shelter it 365,387.
Group A drugsWHO's most important DR-TB medicines: levofloxacin or moxifloxacin, bedaquiline and linezolid 518.
H37RvThe M. tuberculosis strain whose genome (4,411,529 bp, about 4,000 genes) was published in 1998 81.
HepatotoxicityLiver injury. ATS advises interrupting treatment at ALT over 3 times normal with symptoms, or over 5 times without 413.
Hierarchy of controlsAdministrative, then environmental, then respiratory protection 250,513.
Hr-TBIsoniazid-resistant, rifampicin-susceptible TB 518.
HyperuricaemiaRaised uric acid; pyrazinamide causes it by blocking urate excretion 167.
IGRA (interferon-γ release assay)A blood test for TB infection measuring interferon-γ released in response to ESAT-6 and CFP-10 317,367.
Incipient TBA proposed state between infection and disease, with bacterial activity but no disease yet 143.
Indeterminate IGRAA result that cannot be read, for example when the Nil or mitogen control fails 317.
IndurationThe firm, raised area of a TST reaction; the only part measured 17.
Infection control (IPC)Measures that stop TB spreading in health care and other settings, worked as a package 513.
Infectious periodThe time someone may have spread TB; usually taken to start 3 months before diagnosis 356.
inhAThe gene for a mycolic acid enzyme targeted by isoniazid and ethionamide 25.
Interferon-γA T-cell cytokine that activates macrophages; essential for containing TB 180.
IRIS (immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome)Worsening inflammation after starting ART; paradoxical TB-IRIS affects about 18% 340.
Isoniazid (H)A first-line prodrug activated by KatG, the main early killer 329,539.
katGThe gene for the catalase-peroxidase that activates isoniazid; katG315 is the commonest resistance mutation 420,539.
LAM (lipoarabinomannan)A mycobacterial cell-wall glycolipid detected in urine by the LF-LAM test 42.
LevofloxacinA fluoroquinolone; 6 months is WHO's preventive treatment for contacts of people with MDR/RR-TB 514.
Line probe assayA strip test that reads resistance mutations: 96.7% sensitive for rifampicin resistance 342.
LinezolidAn oxazolidinone in BPaL(M); toxicities are neuropathy and myelosuppression, lower at 600 mg 86,172.
Lost to follow-upLanguage note: the person-first term replacing "defaulter" 534.
M72/AS01EA protein-adjuvant vaccine candidate: 49.7% efficacy in a phase 2b trial; phase 3 began in 2024 347,457.
Mantoux testThe intradermal tuberculin skin test: 0.1 mL of 5 TU PPD, read at 48–72 hours 17.
Material supportFood or financial help, such as food baskets, vouchers and transport money 518.
Mediastinal lymph nodeThe lung-draining node where T cells are first primed against TB 397,528.
MGITMycobacteria Growth Indicator Tube, an automated liquid culture system 73,99.
Miliary TBTB spread widely through the lymph and blood, with nodules throughout the lungs; more common with impaired cell-mediated immunity 338,425.
MoxifloxacinA fluoroquinolone in BPaLM and the 4-month regimen; boxed warnings include tendon rupture 162,518.
MTBC (M. tuberculosis complex)The closely related, clonal group of bacteria that cause TB, including M. bovis and M. africanum 50,192.
MTBVACA live-attenuated M. tuberculosis vaccine candidate in a phase 3 infant trial 344,459.
Multidrug-resistant TB (MDR-TB)TB resistant to at least rifampicin and isoniazid 518.
Mycobacterium bovisThe cattle TB bacillus; causes zoonotic TB in people and is the parent of BCG 300,361.
Mycobacterium tuberculosisThe bacterium that causes TB, named the year after Koch's 1882 discovery 60.
Mycolic acidsLong fatty acids forming the waxy outer layer of the cell wall; isoniazid blocks their synthesis 48,311.
N95 respiratorA fitted respirator that filters respirable particles; worn by health workers caring for people with infectious TB 250.
NAAT (nucleic acid amplification test)A molecular test such as Xpert or Truenat that detects TB DNA 285,516.
Negative pressureAir flowing into a room from the corridor, so contaminated air does not escape 250.
Non-severe TB (children)Peripheral or uncomplicated intrathoracic node TB, uncomplicated pleural effusion, or limited non-cavitary disease: eligible for 4 months 518.
Non-tuberculous mycobacteria (NTM)Over 190 environmental mycobacterial species; some cause TB-like lung disease 102.
Notifiable diseaseTB is notifiable: CDC asks that suspected or confirmed TB be reported to public health at once, so treatment can be tracked and contacts checked 250.
Optic neuritisInflammation of the optic nerve, ethambutol's key toxicity 158,160.
Paradoxical reactionTemporary worsening despite effective treatment, seen in about 20% of TB lymphadenitis 184.
Patient-centred careCare organised around the person's needs, including education, counselling and support 518.
Peripheral neuropathyNerve damage causing tingling or burning; caused by isoniazid and linezolid 86,161.
Person-first languageWords that name the person before the disease and avoid blame 534.
Person with presumptive TBLanguage note: the term replacing "TB suspect" 534.
PhagosomeThe membrane bubble a macrophage forms around a microbe; TB stops it maturing and acidifying 16,450.
PhthisisThe ancient Greek name for TB, which Hippocrates described as a fatal disease of young adults 26.
Post-TB lung diseaseLasting lung damage after cure: bronchiectasis, obstruction, fibrosis and vascular disease 9.
Pott's diseaseTB of the spine; seen in Egyptian remains from about 2400 BC 26.
PPD (purified protein derivative)The tuberculin used in the TST, given as 5 TU 17.
Pre-XDR-TBRifampicin-resistant TB also resistant to at least one fluoroquinolone 518.
PretomanidA nitroimidazooxazine in BPaL(M) that inhibits mycolic acid synthesis 165,448.
Primary TBDisease shortly after infection, commonest in children and immunocompromised people 338.
Pulmonary rehabilitationSupervised exercise and education for people with post-TB lung disease 325,428.
Pyrazinamide (Z)A first-line sterilising prodrug activated by pyrazinamidase in acid conditions 540.
Pyridoxine (vitamin B6)Given with isoniazid to prevent neuropathy 161,414.
QT prolongationA delay in heart repolarisation on the ECG; a warning for bedaquiline and moxifloxacin 162,170.
Quantum (Wells-Riley)One infectious dose of airborne TB, used to model infection risk in a room 151,341.
QuantiFERONAn IGRA read in IU/mL; positive at 0.35 IU/mL or more 317.
RD1 (region of difference 1)A genomic region deleted from all BCG strains, encoding ESAT-6 and CFP-10 302.
ReinfectionA new TB infection after a previous one or after cure; common where transmission is intense 496.
RelapseReturn of TB with the same strain after cure; 70% of recurrences in pooled data 492.
Respiratory hygieneCovering the mouth and nose when coughing or sneezing, then hand hygiene; a strong WHO recommendation 513.
Rifampicin (R, rifampin)The key sterilising first-line drug, which blocks RNA polymerase 62,254.
Rifampicin-resistant TB (RR-TB)TB resistant to rifampicin, with or without isoniazid resistance; reported with MDR as MDR/RR-TB 518.
Rifapentine (P)A long-acting rifamycin taken with food, used in 3HP, 1HP and the 4-month regimen 142,166.
rpoBThe gene for the RNA polymerase β subunit; its RRDR holds over 95% of rifampicin-resistance mutations 420,463.
SanatoriumA rest-and-fresh-air institution, from Brehmer's 1854 idea; the Madras study showed home treatment works as well 26,462.
Smear microscopyStaining sputum for acid-fast bacilli; three smears find about 70% of culture-confirmed TB 285.
Source controlReducing what an infectious person releases, for example with a surgical mask 119.
Sputum inductionInhaling nebulised hypertonic saline to produce sputum 226,250.
Sterilising activityKilling slow and persisting bacilli so TB does not relapse; rifampicin's key role 254,329.
StigmaNegative judgement of people with TB, mostly from fear of infection; it delays care and disrupts treatment 70,92.
StreptomycinThe first anti-TB antibiotic, reported in 1944 415 and tested in the 1948 MRC trial 333.
Subclinical TBTB disease without reported symptoms; about half of confirmed TB in prevalence surveys 93,189.
T-SPOT.TBAn IGRA that counts spots; positive at 8 or more, borderline at 5–7 317.
TB diseaseInfection with macroscopic pathology, with or without symptoms 93.
TB infectionImmune evidence of M. tuberculosis without disease; it is not contagious. Formerly called latent TB 36,143.
TB meningitisTB of the brain lining, the most lethal form; 6-month mortality 57% with HIV and 16% without 441.
TB preventive treatment (TPT)Medicines given for TB infection to stop disease developing 514.
Tuberculin skin test (TST)An intradermal PPD test read as mm of induration 17,285.
TNF (tumour necrosis factor)A cytokine needed to keep granulomas intact; blocking it unmasks TB 181,267.
Trained immunityNon-specific boosting of innate immune cells, reported after BCG (Early evidence) 274.
Treatment successCure or treatment completion: 90% for drug-susceptible and 70% for MDR/RR-TB 512.
TruenatA chip-based molecular TB test conditionally recommended by WHO for initial diagnosis 371,516.
TubercleAnother word for the granuloma the body builds around TB germs 147,387.
Upper-room germicidal UVUltraviolet lamps near the ceiling that kill airborne bacilli; cut guinea-pig infection from 35% to 9.5% 154.
Video-observed therapy (VOT)Watching doses by smartphone video instead of in person 447.
Window prophylaxisPreventive treatment for young contacts after a negative first test, until a retest at 8–10 weeks 356.
World TB Day24 March, marking Koch's 1882 announcement 66.
Xpert MTB/RIFA cartridge PCR that detects TB and rifampicin resistance in under 2 hours 44.
Xpert UltraA more sensitive Xpert cartridge (90.9% vs 84.7%), with slightly lower specificity 542.
