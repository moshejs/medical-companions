Part 1 · The infection

What TB is

Tuberculosis is an airborne infection with the bacterium Mycobacterium tuberculosis. It is curable, and it is still the world's leading cause of death from a single infectious agent. [[WHO-GTR2025]] Scrub through one year of TB worldwide, then see how many people carry TB infection and how few of them ever fall ill.

TB infection and TB disease are not the same

TB infection

The immune system has met the bacterium

What it is
A lasting immune response to M. tuberculosis without TB disease. It used to be called "latent TB infection"; the old split into "latent" and "active" has been replaced by a spectrum. [[Drain2018]]
Contagious?
No. TB infection is not passed on to other people. [[Drain2018,Coussens2024]]
How it is found
A skin test or a blood test (IGRA). Both show that the body has met the germ, not whether live bacteria remain or whether the person is ill. [[Lewinsohn2017,Behr2019]] See TST & IGRA.
TB disease

The bacteria are causing damage

What it is
An international consensus separates disease from infection by visible damage (macroscopic pathology). It can be subclinical, without reported symptoms, or clinical. [[Coussens2024]]
Contagious?
TB disease of the lungs can spread through the air when the person coughs, talks or sings. Effective treatment quickly makes people much less infectious. [[Fennelly2020,Dharmadhikari2014]] See How TB spreads.
When it appears
Most disease in exposed people appears within the first two years after infection. [[Behr2018]] See the spectrum.
Words

Person-first language

This guide says "people with TB" and "people affected by TB". It avoids "TB suspect", "defaulter" or "TB carrier": a person with TB infection is not carrying a disease they can pass on, and stopping treatment usually has reasons that care services can address. TB is a notifiable disease, treatment is free in most national programmes, and anyone with a long cough, fever, night sweats or weight loss should see a clinician.

Estimate

Controversy

How many people really carry TB infection?

The "about a quarter" figure has two sources: a 2014 model of 131 infection surveys (23.0%, about 1.7 billion people) and a meta-analysis of 88 studies (24.8% by IGRA, 21.2% by a 10 mm skin test). [[Houben2016,Cohen2019ERJ]] Both count immune memory, not live bacteria. Early evidence A 2021 model that allows for self-clearance estimates that 73.1% of infected people clear the bacteria over a lifetime, so the population truly at risk could be 35–80% smaller in India, China and Japan, while each truly infected person's lifetime risk is higher: 17.0% rather than 12.6%. [[Emery2021]] No test yet separates live from cleared infection, so the question is open.

Controversy

Is a big hidden reservoir the main problem?

One view: about 1.7 billion people with TB infection are a central obstacle, and even with no new infections after 2015, existing infection alone would cause about 16.5 cases per 100,000 a year in 2035 (Hypothesis, a model projection). [[Houben2016]] The other view: disease follows infection within months to two years, with no late spike, so most TB comes from recent transmission and the reservoir has been overstated. [[Behr2018]] The timing data are consistent; what they mean for strategy is still argued.

Controversy

The "leading infectious killer"?

WHO says yes for 2024: only about 70,000 COVID-19 deaths were officially reported to WHO that year. [[WHO-GTR2025]] But COVID-19 displaced TB in 2020, and on WHO's latest all-cause estimates (2021) TB ranked second to COVID-19 among single infectious agents. [[Chakaya2021,WHO-GTR2025]] The claim depends on the year, and on whether deaths of people with HIV are counted. The independent GBD study estimated 9.40 million cases and 1.35 million deaths in 2021, with different methods. [[GBD2021TB2024]]

Who is affected

Men

54%

Of the 10.7 million people who fell ill in 2024, 5.8 million were adult men, 3.7 million adult women (35%) and 1.2 million children (11%). [[WHO-GTR2025]] In prevalence surveys men are about twice as likely as women to have TB (ratio 2.21) and more often missed by services. [[Horton2016]] The reasons are structural, such as work, income and how services are designed, not individual blame. [[Chikovore2020]]

Children

50%

Only half of children under 5 with TB were notified in 2024, against 79% of adults. [[WHO-GTR2025]] 174,000 children under 15 died of TB; of the 172,000 HIV-negative children who died, 122,000 were under 5. [[WHO-GTR2025]] Children often cannot cough up sputum, which makes TB harder to confirm [[Kay2025,Gunasekera2023]]: see Diagnosing TB disease.

People with HIV

5.8%

People with HIV were 5.8% of new TB cases in 2024 (about 619,000), down from a peak of 17% in 2000; in parts of southern Africa more than half of people with a new TB episode have HIV. TB is the leading killer of people with HIV. [[WHO-GTR2025]] See TB with HIV.

Where

87%

The 30 high TB burden countries had 87% of new cases in 2024. National rates ranged from fewer than 10 to more than 500 per 100,000; 62 countries had a low incidence, under 10 per 100,000. [[WHO-GTR2025]] What drives risk is covered in Who is at risk.

TB in countries where it is rare

In 2023 the United States provisionally reported 9,615 TB cases (2.9 per 100,000), 16% more than in 2022 and the highest count since 2013. Of cases with known birth origin, 76% were in people born outside the US: 15.0 per 100,000 against 0.8 in US-born people. CDC stresses that most US residents are at minimal risk. [[Williams2024]] About one-third of people with TB in the EU/EEA were born in, or are citizens of, another country. [[Vasiliu2025]]

These patterns reflect TB infection acquired where TB is common, and the stresses of migration and barriers to care, often an old infection that becomes disease years later. [[Frieden1995,Domaszewska2025]] The authors call for accessible, free screening and tailored care, not exclusion. [[Domaszewska2025,Vasiliu2025]] TB is about exposure and conditions, not where someone comes from.
