How TB spreads

What the guinea pigs proved

Established

Riley's ward, 1950s

All the air leaving a TB ward was piped over colonies of guinea pigs, which were then skin-tested for infection. The animals shared nothing with the patients but their air. Riley's pilot-ward work measured the infectivity of that air and tested ways to control it; in 1962 the team irradiated the infected air with ultraviolet light and found that infectiousness varies enormously between people. [[Escombe2008,Riley1957,Riley1962]]

Established

Wells and the droplet nucleus

Wells and colleagues worked out the mechanics of droplet-nucleus infection with quantitative airborne TB in rabbits. Riley's later summary: the essential facts about airborne infection are simple, and have been repeatedly ignored in practice. [[Wells1948,Riley2001]]

Established

Lima, 2000s: a few people, most of the spread

Over 505 days on an HIV–TB ward, 118 admissions exposed 292 guinea pigs, and 144 developed TB disease. Molecular fingerprinting showed that 8.5% of admissions caused 98% of the characterised animal cases, and 90% of transmission came from MDR-TB that was not yet on effective treatment. People with HIV and TB averaged 8.2 quanta per hour, against 1.25 for HIV-negative patients in the 1950s studies. [[Escombe2008,Escombe2007CID]]

Established

Effective treatment ends it fast

In five guinea-pig experiments on an MDR-TB ward in South Africa, infection of the animals ranged from 1% to 77%. Transmission was minimal when 27 people had just started effective treatment; the high-transmission runs involved unsuspected XDR-TB that was not being treated effectively. Cough-aerosol cultures fell within the first 3 weeks of effective treatment. [[Dharmadhikari2014,Fennelly2004]]

A Wells–Riley calculator

The Wells–Riley model turns a source's output of infectious doses (quanta per hour), the room's ventilation and the time spent breathing its air into the share of susceptible people expected to be infected: P = 1 − e−I·q·p·t / Q, where Q is fresh air per hour (air changes × room volume). It is a teaching model: it assumes perfectly mixed air and treats every quantum alike, and real outbreaks vary widely. [[Escombe2007,Nardell1991]]

Quanta rates: [[Escombe2007CID,Nardell1991,Escombe2008]]. Air changes per hour: Lima measurements and the CDC isolation-room standard; the closed-room value is derived as 28 ÷ 18. [[Escombe2007,Jensen2005]] Volume and breathing rate are your inputs. For a published check: with the office-outbreak source, 24 hours modelled infection of 39% in mechanically ventilated rooms, 33% in modern naturally ventilated rooms and 11% in pre-1950 rooms with windows open. [[Escombe2007]]

Who is infectious?

Where the TB is

Lungs, airway or larynx

CDC's 2005 guideline usually considers someone not on treatment infectious if they cough, have cavitation on chest X-ray or a positive acid-fast smear, have disease of the lung, airway or larynx, do not cover the mouth and nose when coughing, or undergo cough-inducing procedures. TB elsewhere in the body is not on that list. [[Jensen2005]]

Cavities

A hole that feeds a cough

People with large cavities (over 7 mL) coughed about twice as often during treatment (rate ratio 1.98), and so did those whose cavity lay within 10 mm of an airway (2.44). The cause is anatomical, not behavioural: a cavity open to an airway supplies both the bacteria and the cough. [[Proano2018]]

Smear grade

A crude but useful sort

In San Francisco, smear-negative people transmitted at 0.22 times the rate of smear-positive people, yet still caused at least 17% of secondary cases. A higher smear grade went with culturable cough aerosol. [[Behr1999Lancet,Fennelly2012]]

Cough aerosol

Most people with TB emit little

Only 28 of 101 people with culture-confirmed TB in Kampala (27.7%) produced culturable cough aerosol. In households, 69% of contacts of high-aerosol producers were newly infected, against 25–30% for low-aerosol or aerosol-negative people (adjusted OR 4.81). [[Fennelly2012,JonesLopez2013]]

Before diagnosis

The untreated months

Before drug treatment existed, untreated smear-positive TB lasted about 3 years on average. Prevalence surveys keep finding people with confirmed TB and no symptoms, which points to transmission before anyone seeks care. Finding and treating people early is how the chain is broken. [[Tiemersma2011,Zenner2027]]

TB infection

TB infection does not spread

About a quarter of the world's population has immune evidence of TB infection, and only a small fraction ever develop TB disease. The model above needs a source: someone with TB disease in the lungs or throat who is producing infectious aerosol. See TB infection vs TB disease. [[Houben2016,Zenner2027,Jensen2005]]

Where shared air concentrates risk

SettingWhat was measuredSource
Households and close contactsIn 95 low- and middle-income studies, TB disease in 3.1% of contacts and TB infection in 51.5%; in high-income settings 1.4% and 28.1%. Incidence is greatest in the first year; children under 5 and people with HIV are most at risk.[[Fox2013]]
PrisonsInfection incidence 15.0 per 100 person-years. TB incidence was 12.6 to 26.9 times the surrounding population in four regions. Prisons concentrate transmission, and about 6–9% of TB in the general population was attributed to transmission inside them.[[Cords2021,Baussano2010]]
Health careAcross 86 studies, 7.1% of exposed people were infected, 3.8% in studies after the 2005 ventilation standards. Transmission followed long contact (median 24 hours) without respirators or negative-pressure rooms.[[Manzoor2026]]
An aircraftAfter an 8.75-hour flight, people within two rows of the index passenger were 8.5 times more likely to have a positive skin test (30.8% vs 3.6%).[[Kenyon1996]]
An office27 of 67 workers (40%) converted their skin test after about 4 weeks with one coworker with cavitary TB, estimated at 13 quanta per hour.[[Nardell1991]]

Shared air is a fact about buildings and time, not about blame. People with TB did not choose to be infectious, and in most programmes treatment is free and works. The fastest way to protect a household is to find TB early and start treatment that works, then open the windows. Continue to Infection control & contacts.
