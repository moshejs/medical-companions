Part 1 · The infection

The TB bacterium

Mycobacterium tuberculosis is a slow-growing rod wrapped in a waxy, fatty envelope. That envelope is why it keeps the red stain in a sputum smear, why many drugs cannot get in, and — together with its slowness — why treatment is counted in months and culture results in weeks. Scrub from a stained smear into the cell wall, then watch it grow.

Schematic, not to scale: the layers are drawn in their published order, not at their true thickness. Growth curves are modelled from published mean generation times in controlled culture. [[Brennan2003,Jeeves2015]]

The envelope, layer by layer

The cell-wall core is one massive covalent assembly: peptidoglycan, joined through a linker unit to a linear galactofuran, attached to branched arabinofuran, attached in turn to mycolic acids standing perpendicular to the membrane — described by its chemists as "a truly special lipid barrier". That barrier is responsible for much of the organism's physiology and its capacity to cause disease, and the wall is impermeable to many compounds, which is part of why the bacillus resists so many drugs. [[Brennan2003,Hett2008]]

The envelope is not a fixed shell. It is remodelled during growth, division and stress — "a moving target" that matters for both drug penetration and drug discovery. [[Dulberger2020]]

Why RD1 and ESAT-6 decide what a blood test can tell you

ESAT-6 · CFP-10

Two proteins BCG does not have

ESAT-6 is a 6 kDa protein secreted by M. tuberculosis and by virulent M. bovis, and absent from M. bovis BCG. Comparing BCG with virulent M. bovis identified the deleted stretch of genome responsible, called region of difference 1 (RD1). [[Harboe1996,Mahairas1996]]

Test specificity

That gap is what makes IGRAs specific

Because ESAT-6 and CFP-10 lie inside RD1 and are missing from BCG, an immune test built on these antigens can tell infection with M. tuberculosis apart from BCG vaccination — the design principle of the interferon-gamma release assays. Pooled specificity for QuantiFERON was 99% (95% CI 98–100) in people not vaccinated with BCG and 96% (94–98) in people who were; the tuberculin skin test was 97% (95–99) specific in unvaccinated people. [[Andersen2000,Pai2008]]

Virulence

RD1 is not an incidental loss

Deleting RD1 from virulent M. bovis and from M. tuberculosis attenuated both, and the ΔRD1 mutant protected mice against aerosol challenge much as BCG does. The attenuating mechanism is loss of ESAT-6-mediated lytic activity: mutants failed to lyse pneumocytes. Loss of RD1 also contributed to the attenuation of M. microti, the other historical live vaccine, and putting RD1 back into BCG restored part of the virulent phenotype. [[Hsu2003,Lewis2003,Pym2002]]

Genome

4.4 million letters, read in 1998

The H37Rv genome is 4,411,529 base pairs with around 4,000 genes and a very high guanine-plus-cytosine content. An unusually large share of that coding capacity is given to enzymes that build and break down lipids, plus the glycine-rich PE/PPE protein families that may be a source of antigenic variation. [[Cole1998]]

The M. tuberculosis complex: one family, several members

TB is not caused by a single organism but by a group of closely related bacteria, the M. tuberculosis complex. The group is clonal, with so little DNA sequence diversity that standard bacterial typing methods are uninformative; whole-genome sequencing of large clinical collections is what opened up its ecology and evolution. Coalescent analysis of 259 complete genomes dates the complex to about 70,000 years ago, travelling with out-of-Africa migrations of modern humans and expanding as Neolithic population density rose. [[Gagneux2018,Comas2013]]

Exam hook. ESAT-6 and CFP-10 lie in RD1, deleted from every BCG substrain. That single deletion is why an IGRA stays specific after BCG vaccination, why BCG is safe enough to give to newborns, and why ΔRD1 M. tuberculosis behaves like a vaccine strain in mice. [[Mahairas1996,Hsu2003,Pai2008]]
