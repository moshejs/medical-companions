The research pipeline

As of August 2025, 29 drugs for TB disease were in clinical trials (8 in 2015), and almost 100 diagnostic products were in development. [[WHO-GTR2025]] Reviewers describe the drug pipeline as more advanced than at any time since rifampicin. [[Marais2027Microbe]] Filter the routes by evidence label and by kind of research. Vaccines have their own board in BCG & new vaccines.

Evidence
Route
Early evidenceNew drugNCT03557281

Ganfeborole

A first-in-class benzoxaborole that blocks the bacterium's leucyl-tRNA synthetase. In a 14-day phase 2a trial in 75 men, 5, 15 and 30 mg doses gave numerical falls in sputum bacterial counts, 1 mg did not, and all adverse events were grade 1–2. [[Diacon2024]]

Early evidenceNew drugNCT07209761

Quabodepistat

With delamanid and bedaquiline it lowered sputum log10 CFU/mL by a mean 2.73 over 14 days, similar to standard HRZE (−2.71), in a small study. [[Dawson2025]] A phase 3 trial of 4-month regimens for rifampicin-resistant TB (QUANTUM-TB, planned 532) began recruiting in October 2025. [[NCT07209761]]

Early evidenceNew drug

BTZ-043

A benzothiazinone that blocks DprE1, a cell-wall enzyme. Safe in healthy volunteers at 125–500 mg; in people with TB, 1,000 mg cleared bacteria twofold faster than 250 mg over the first 2 days. [[Lutchmun2025,Koele2025]]

Early evidenceNew drug

Sutezolid

A next-generation oxazolidinone, a cousin of linezolid. Added to bedaquiline, delamanid and moxifloxacin, it steepened the culture time-to-positivity slope by 16.7% at the 1,200 mg exposure, and no neuropathy was reported. [[Heinrich2025]]

Early evidenceNew drugNCT06058299

Sorfequiline (TBAJ-876)

A second-generation diarylquinoline. In a first-in-human study, placebo-corrected QTcF changes ranged from −11.4 to +2.4 ms without dose dependency, suggesting less QT effect than bedaquiline. [[Darpo2026]] NC-009 compares three doses with pretomanid and linezolid against BPaL and HRZE to choose a phase 3 dose. [[Olugbosi2026]]

EstablishedTreatment shorteningNCT03474198

TRUNCATE-TB

A strategy starting with 8 weeks of bedaquiline-linezolid (plus isoniazid, pyrazinamide, ethambutol) was non-inferior to 6 months of standard treatment at week 96 (5.8% vs 3.9%), with a mean 85 days of treatment against 180. The high-dose rifampicin-linezolid strategy did not meet non-inferiority. Established for this strategy only. [[Paton2023]]

Early evidenceTerminatedNCT05971602

PAN-TB

A Gates MRI trial of 2–4-month regimens with bedaquiline, quabodepistat and sutezolid plus delamanid or pretomanid. It was terminated: its data did not support a regimen treating TB in 3 months or less. [[NCT05971602]]

Early evidencePlatform trialNCT06114628

UNITE4TB · PARADIGM4TB

An EU-funded phase 2B/C platform testing 11 initial combinations, including BTZ-043 and ganfeborole, against standard 2HRZE/4HR; planned 2,500 participants, recruiting. [[NCT06114628]]

Early evidence4-month regimensNCT05766267

CRUSH-TB (TBTC Study 38)

Compares two 4-month bedaquiline-moxifloxacin-pyrazinamide regimens (with rifabutin or delamanid) against 6-month HRZE in 288 people aged 12 or over. [[Kurbatova2025]] Related trials: RAD-TB (NCT06192160) tests linezolid, TBI-223 or sutezolid on a bedaquiline-pretomanid backbone [[Harrison2025]], and panTB-HM (NCT05686356) adds N-acetylcysteine to sutezolid regimens, with lung function as an endpoint. [[Mudzengi2026]]

Early evidenceDiagnostics

Tongue swabs instead of sputum

Tongue-swab Xpert Ultra in Kampala: sensitivity 77.8%, specificity 100% against sputum. [[Andama2022]] Across 20 studies, oral-swab sensitivity was 36–91% in adults and 5–42% in children. [[Church2024]] MiniDock MTB reached 85.7% on sputum swabs and 79.6% on tongue swabs, with specificity above 97.5%, meeting WHO near-point-of-care targets (≥85% on sputum, ≥75% on non-sputum samples, ≥98% specificity). [[Yerlikaya2026]]

Early evidenceDiagnostics

Next-generation urine LAM

FujiLAM in people with HIV (13 studies, 5,819 people): pooled sensitivity 63% and specificity 90%. Sensitivity rose to 76% at CD4 ≤100 cells/µL and fell to 42% above 200. [[Taye2025]] Current urine LAM is covered in Diagnosing TB disease.

Early evidenceDiagnostics

Computer-aided chest X-ray reading

Five commercial programs reading 23,954 chest X-rays in Dhaka all outperformed radiologists (AUC 84.9–90.8%); only qXR and CAD4TB met WHO's triage target of ≥90% sensitivity and ≥70% specificity. All halved the Xpert tests needed but did worse in people over 60 and people with previous TB. [[Qin2021]]

Early evidenceDiagnostics

Near-point-of-care tests

In August 2025, 14 near-point-of-care molecular tests were awaiting WHO assessment, and WHO updated its target product profiles for detection (2024) and screening tests (2025). [[WHO-GTR2025]] In India, Uganda and Vietnam, tongue-swab Truenat MTB Ultima (77.9%) and MiniDock MTB (85.7%) were both more sensitive than sputum smear microscopy. [[Steadman2025]]

EstablishedActive case finding

ACT3: screen everyone, every year

In Ca Mau, Vietnam, 3 years of annual sputum Xpert screening of everyone aged 15 or over, regardless of symptoms, lowered pulmonary TB prevalence in year 4 to 126 against 226 per 100,000 (prevalence ratio 0.56). TB infection in children did not differ. Established in that setting. [[Marks2019]] Prevalence surveys keep finding confirmed TB without symptoms. [[Zenner2027]]

EstablishedNutrition

RATIONS: food as prevention

In Jharkhand, India, household contacts given monthly food rations had TB incidence of 0.78 against 1.27 per 100 person-years (adjusted IRR 0.61): about 30 households supported per case prevented. Established in that setting. [[Bhargava2023Lancet]] WHO now lists food assistance for contacts in food-insecure settings among preventive approaches. [[WHO-GTR2025]]

HypothesisModel projection

What RATIONS would cost

Modelling estimated US$289 per DALY averted from the health-care perspective, averting 38% of TB episodes and 58% of TB deaths among contacts over 2 years. [[Sinha2026]]

HypothesisHost-directed

Host-directed therapies

These aim to limit inflammation that damages the lungs. None tested in humans has yet sped up sputum culture conversion, although some reduced biomarkers of lung damage. [[Shaku2026]] In a phase 2 trial of 200 patients, CC-11050 and everolimus improved lung function (FEV1) at day 180 by about 6.3–6.6 points; auranofin and vitamin D did not, and one auranofin recipient had life-threatening thrombocytopenia. [[Wallis2021]]

Money, promises and costs

Funding for services in low- and middle-income countries has stagnated since 2020. [[WHO-GTR2025,Zenner2027]]

UN 2023

High-level meeting targets for 2027

At least 90% of people with TB diagnosed and treated; 100% of people diagnosed tested first with a WHO-recommended rapid test; 90% coverage of preventive treatment (about 45 million people in 2023–2027); and 100% access to a health and social benefits package. [[WHO-GTR2025]] In 2024, 5.3 million people received preventive treatment and rapid-test coverage reached 54%. [[WHO-GTR2025]]

Funding

The gap, and the cuts

Member States committed US$22 billion a year for services and US$5 billion a year for research by 2027. Research funding reached US$1.2 billion in 2023: 35% to drugs, 19% to vaccines, 14% to diagnostics. The US NIH gave 34% of it; cuts announced in 2025 put at least 20 TB trials in South Africa at risk. Hypothesis Modelling cited by WHO warns long-term donor cuts could cause up to 2 million extra deaths in 2025–2035. [[WHO-GTR2025]]

Costs

Free treatment is not free to live through

Even where treatment is free, a TB episode cost households a mean US$1,253, much of it lost income, across 135 countries; an estimated 54.9% of affected households faced catastrophic costs, 75.2% in the poorest fifth. [[Portnoy2023]] Social protection now covers 52% of people, up from 43% in 2015. [[WHO-GTR2025]] See Living with TB.

Treatment

Shorter regimens are already here

People starting 6-month MDR/RR-TB regimens rose from 1,744 in 2022 to 34,256 in 2024. [[WHO-GTR2025]] Treatment success was 88% for drug-susceptible and 71% for drug-resistant TB in the latest cohorts. [[WHO-GTR2025]] See Treating drug-resistant TB and Treating TB disease.

TB is curable, treatment is free in most programmes, and TB is a notifiable disease. Anyone with a cough for more than 2 weeks, fever, night sweats or weight loss should see a clinician.
