Symptoms & where TB strikes

Most TB starts in the lungs, with a cough that will not settle, fever, night sweats and weight loss. About one case in six is outside the lungs. Pick who you are looking at, then press Play: the symptoms appear first, then the places TB can reach.

Schematic. One illustrative person, not a checklist: TB can show any mix of these, or none. Shares of extrapulmonary sites are from US surveillance, 1993–2006, and differ by country. [[Peto2009,WHO-GTR2025]]

If you have these symptoms, see a clinician. A cough for 2 weeks or more, fever, night sweats or weight loss are reasons to get checked for TB, and some people with TB feel well. TB is a notifiable disease; testing and treatment are free in most public programmes, and treatment cures most people. [[VantHoog2022,Frascella2021]]

Screening tests on 1,000 people

Screening asks a simple question of many people to decide who needs a proper test, such as sputum Xpert. Choose a screen and how common TB is in the group, and watch what happens to 1,000 people. Accuracy figures are pooled estimates from the populations studied, so the same screen behaves very differently where TB is common or rare. [[Getahun2011,VantHoog2022]]

Worth knowing

Established

Subclinical TB: disease without symptoms

In national prevalence surveys since 1990, a median 50.4% (range 36.1–79.7%) of people with bacteriologically confirmed TB screened negative on symptoms. A chest X-ray detected 89% of them. An international consensus now separates subclinical from clinical TB by reported symptoms or signs and by likely infectiousness. In 35 adults with HIV and TB infection but no symptoms, PET-CT found lung changes suggesting subclinical disease in 10, who were more likely to progress. [[Frascella2021,Coussens2024,Esmail2016]]

See TB infection vs TB disease.

Children

Hard to confirm, so treated on clinical grounds

Of 4,718 children under 10 assessed in 13 studies, 38.4% were classified as having TB, and only 29.9% of those were bacteriologically confirmed. WHO treatment-decision algorithms score symptoms with or without an X-ray (sensitivity 0.86, specificity 0.37 with X-ray), so primary-care workers can start treatment without a positive test. Consensus case definitions let research studies of childhood TB be compared. [[Gunasekera2023,WHO-Mod5-2022,Graham2015]]

HIV

The four-symptom screen fades on ART

In people taking antiretroviral therapy the screen caught 51.0% of TB, against 89.4% in people not yet on ART; specificity was 70.7% versus 28.1%. Adding any chest X-ray abnormality raised sensitivity on ART from 52.2% to 84.6% (two studies). [[Hamada2018]]

Early evidence

Which second screen: CRP or X-ray software?

In Ugandan adults with HIV starting ART, finger-prick CRP of 10 mg/L or more had 89% sensitivity and 72% specificity. [[Yoon2017]] In people with HIV not on ART, a positive symptom screen followed by CRP of 5 mg/L or more kept sensitivity at 84% and raised specificity to 64%. In a multinational cohort of adults with cough, CAD4TB reached 70.3% specificity at 90% sensitivity against 49.7% for point-of-care CRP, and CRP then CAD4TB reached 75.9%. The best choice depends on the setting. [[Dhana2022,Crowder2026]]
