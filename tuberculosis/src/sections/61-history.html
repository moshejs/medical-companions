What changed the curve?

TB deaths were falling in England and Wales long before any drug existed, and they did not fall the same way everywhere. Pick a lens. [[Davies1999,Vynnycky1999,Hermans2015]]

People

1882 · Berlin

Robert Koch

Presented his isolation of the tubercle bacillus to the Physiological Society in Berlin on 24 March 1882, the date World TB Day now marks. [[Barberis2017,CDC2018WTBD]] The work rested on Villemin's proof that TB is transmissible and on two new methods: a staining technique and a solid, serum-based culture medium. [[Cambau2014]] His tuberculin, offered as a cure in 1890–91, failed in practice but later became a diagnostic tool. Nobel Prize, 1905. [[Gradmann2006,Sakula1982,Barberis2017]]

1908–1921

Albert Calmette and Camille Guérin

From 1908 they subcultured a virulent bovine strain on bile-glycerine-potato medium; after about 230 passages over 11 years it no longer caused progressive TB in animals. On 18 July 1921 Weill-Hallé and Turpin gave the first human dose, by mouth, to a newborn in Paris whose mother had died of TB. [[Luca2013]] BCG is still the only licensed TB vaccine. [[Lange2022]] See BCG & new vaccines.

1944

Albert Schatz, Elizabeth Bugie and Selman Waksman

Reported streptomycin, from a soil actinomycete, in 1944. [[Schatz1944,Crofton2006]] Its first controlled test, the 1948 MRC trial, also showed resistance emerging when it was used alone. [[Crofton2006]]

1946

Jörgen Lehmann

Reported para-aminosalicylic acid (PAS) in The Lancet in January 1946. Given with streptomycin, it greatly reduced drug resistance: the first step to combination therapy. [[Lehmann1946,Murray2015]]

1948

John Crofton

His first-hand account of the 1948 MRC streptomycin trial, "a view from the clinical front line", records the deaths and the resistance that followed single-drug treatment, the lesson that TB must be treated with several drugs together. [[Crofton2006]]

The short-course era

Denis Mitchison

Best known for the idea that TB bacteria live as subpopulations, some growing fast and some barely metabolising, that different drugs reach differently: the basis of multi-drug, two-phase short-course treatment. [[Mitchison2000]] His summary of the trial-proven steps: streptomycin works, combinations prevent resistance, home treatment works, supervised intermittent dosing helps, and rifampicin and pyrazinamide shorten treatment. [[Mitchison2005]]

Former names

What TB used to be called

Phthisis
The ancient Greek name; Hippocrates described a fatal disease, especially of young adults. [[Barberis2017]]
Consumption
The English name, as in Benjamin Marten's 1720 "A new theory of Consumption", the first suggestion of an infectious cause. [[Barberis2017]]
Pott's disease
TB of the spine; its deformity is visible in Egyptian remains from about 2400 BC. [[Barberis2017]]
"Captain of All These Men of Death"
Its title in 18th- and 19th-century Europe and North America, when it caused about one death in four. [[Barberis2017,Daniel2006]]
Tuberculosis
Coined by Johann Lukas Schönlein in the mid-19th century. [[Barberis2017]] Historians also call it "the white death". [[Gradmann2006]]
Controversy

Why did TB fall before drugs?

One model credits a fall in effective contact: each infectious person effectively contacted about 22 people in 1900, 10 by 1950 and about 1 by 1990. [[Vynnycky1999]] Another analysis found that TB mortality fell 1.71% a year from 1853 to 1910 while total mortality, infant mortality and housing density did not fall significantly, and proposed a role for natural selection (Hypothesis). [[Davies1999]] Comparing cities, rates fell in New York and London but stayed high in Cape Town, pointing to transmission tied to social and economic conditions. [[Hermans2015]] The sanatorium movement drew attention to people with TB and helped spur public-health action. [[Murray2015]]

Discrepancies in the sources: Murray and colleagues say PAS was "discovered in 1945", while Lehmann's Lancet report is dated January 1946. [[Murray2015,Lehmann1946]]
