BCG & new vaccines

BCG, first given to a baby in 1921, is still the only licensed TB vaccine. Follow one injection from bleb to scar, then see what the trials say it protects against, and for whom. [[Lange2022]]

The vaccine pipeline

As of August 2025, 18 TB vaccine candidates were in clinical trials, up from 15 in 2024: four in phase 1, eight in phase 2 and six in phase 3. [[WHO-GTR2025]] Several results in 2025–2026 were negative. That is normal in vaccine research, and it is why a phase 3 trial is the test that counts. Evidence
For
EstablishedLicensed · infants

BCG

Live attenuated M. bovis, given to more people than any other vaccine. Strong, lasting protection against miliary and meningeal TB in children; weaker against pulmonary TB, especially in adults. [[Lange2022]] WHO's GTR 2025 states BCG is recommended in national childhood immunisation programmes, in line with a country's TB epidemiology. [[WHO-GTR2025]]

Early evidencePhase 3 · adolescents & adults

M72/AS01E

A protein-adjuvant subunit vaccine. In a phase 2b trial in 3,575 IGRA-positive, HIV-negative adults in Kenya, South Africa and Zambia, 13 of 1,626 vaccinated against 26 of 1,663 placebo recipients developed confirmed pulmonary TB: efficacy 49.7% at 36 months (95% CI 2.1–74.2). [[Tait2019]] A phase 3 trial (NCT06062238) began in March 2024 with a planned 20,080 people in five countries; primary completion is listed for April 2028. [[NCT06062238]] A standalone trial in IGRA-negative people would need about 134,800 participants. [[Dagnew2026]]

Early evidencePhase 3 · infants

MTBVAC

A live-attenuated M. tuberculosis vaccine, described as the leading candidate to replace BCG in infants (expert opinion). [[Vashishtha2026]] In 99 South African infants the chosen dose was less reactogenic and more immunogenic than BCG. [[Tameris2025]] An infant efficacy trial against BCG (NCT04975178, planned 7,120) is recruiting, and an adolescent and adult trial (NCT06272812, planned 5,500) is active. [[NCT04975178,NCT06272812]]

Early evidenceNegative result

VPM1002 (recombinant BCG)

In 6,940 newborns, QFT conversion was 5.3% against 4.3% with BCG (HR 1.23): not non-inferior; the trial stopped early in October 2024. [[Nduba2026]] In PreVenTB (India, 12,717 household contacts), efficacy against all TB was 21.4% (−8.9% to 43.2%), not significant. A post hoc analysis suggested 64.6% in children aged 6–14 (hypothesis-generating only). [[Singh2026BMJ]]

Early evidenceNegative result

Immuvac (MIP)

An inactivated whole-cell vaccine. In PreVenTB, TB developed in 2.09% with Immuvac against 2.13% with placebo; the authors concluded it was not efficacious against microbiologically confirmed TB. [[Singh2026BMJ]]

Early evidenceNot confirmed

BCG revaccination

In 2018, revaccinating 990 QFT-negative adolescents did not prevent initial QFT conversion, but sustained conversion was 6.7% against 11.6% with placebo (efficacy 45.4%). [[Nemes2018]] The larger phase 2b BCG REVAX trial (NCT04152161, 1,836 adolescents) found no effect: HR 1.04, efficacy −3.8%. [[Schmidt2025]] A phase 3 trial in Indian contacts (NCT05330884) is listed as suspended. [[NCT05330884]]

Early evidenceNegative result

H4:IC31

Tested alongside BCG revaccination in the same 2018 trial: efficacy against initial QFT conversion 9.4% (P=0.63) and against sustained conversion 30.5% (P=0.16), neither significant. [[Nemes2018]]

Early evidenceOther platforms

GamTBvac, viral vectors, whole-cell vaccines

GamTBvac is among the six phase 3 candidates. Other platforms include viral vectors such as ChAdOx1.85A and inactivated whole-cell vaccines such as DAR-901 and RUTI. [[daCosta2027]] Investment remains "risky and inadequate", held back by unclear demand and too little committed procurement. [[vanRiet2026]]

Early evidenceBeyond TB

Non-specific effects of BCG

BCG roughly doubled monocyte TNF and IL-1β responses to unrelated pathogens for at least 3 months ("trained immunity"). [[Kleinnijenhuis2012]] In low-birth-weight infants, a meta-analysis of three trials showed 38% lower neonatal mortality (MRR 0.62); the single Guinea-Bissau trial alone was not significant. [[BieringSorensen2017]]

HypothesisModel projection

What a new vaccine could do

For 2028–2050 in low- and middle-income countries, an adolescent/adult vaccine was projected to spare 22.9 million households catastrophic costs, an infant vaccine 3.7 million; the poorest two income quintiles would account for 56% of cases averted. [[Portnoy2023BMJGH]] WHO states the 2035 End TB targets need a breakthrough such as a new vaccine. [[WHO-GTR2025]] See Ending TB: targets & research.

Good to know

Tests

BCG and the skin test

BCG can make a tuberculin skin test positive. After BCG in infancy, 8.5% had a TST of 10 mm or more from BCG, but only 1% when tested 10 or more years later; after BCG given past the first birthday, 41.8% did, and 21.2% still did after 10 years. [[Farhat2006]] IGRAs use ESAT-6 and CFP-10, which are missing from every BCG strain, so they are barely affected (QFT specificity 96% after BCG, 99% without). [[Harboe1996,Pai2008]] More in TB infection tests.

Safety

Adverse effects

In 2,435 French children followed for 12 months, 17.8% had an adverse event, mostly redness or induration; abscess 2.5%, ulceration 0.9%, lymphadenitis 0.1%. Reactions were frequent but rarely severe, and less likely when BCG was given before age 1. [[Dommergues2009]] As a live vaccine, BCG can spread in people with weak immunity. [[Lange2022]] In infants with HIV, surveillance found disseminated BCG disease in 992 per 100,000 vaccinated (95% CI 567–1,495); in one hospital series all 8 children with disseminated disease were immunodeficient and 75% died. [[Hesseling2009BWHO,Hesseling2006CID]]

Policy

WHO position paper, 2018

The February 2018 position paper replaced the 2004 paper and the 2007 guidance for infants at risk of HIV. It revises guidance on immunising children with HIV, re-emphasises the birth dose, and includes recommendations for leprosy prevention. [[WHO2018BCG]] Low-incidence countries such as Sweden have ended general BCG vaccination and offer it to infants at higher risk. [[Kaufmann2026]]

Hypothesis

Intravenous BCG in macaques

Given into a vein instead of the skin, BCG induced far more T cells in the lungs and airways of rhesus macaques; 9 of 10 were highly protected against challenge 6 months later, and 6 had no detectable infection. [[Darrah2020]] In SIV-infected macaques, 9 of 12 vaccinated animals were protected. [[Larson2023]] These are animal data (early evidence in animals); nothing here shows the same in people.

Hypothesis

Why does BCG work less well near the equator?

Latitude and study validity explained 66% of the variation between trials. [[Colditz1994]] Failing to exclude people already sensitised to mycobacteria explained most of the rest. [[Abubakar2013]] A leading idea is that environmental mycobacteria, commoner in warm climates, mask or block BCG's effect, but the mechanism is unproven. [[Fine1995,Mangtani2014]]

History

Calmette and Guérin

From 1908, Albert Calmette and Camille Guérin subcultured a bovine strain about 230 times over 11 years until it no longer caused progressive TB in animals. On 18 July 1921 the first human dose was given by mouth to a newborn in Paris whose mother had died of TB. [[Luca2013]] Read more in History & people.

TB is a notifiable disease. A BCG scar does not rule TB out: anyone with a cough for more than 2 weeks, fever, night sweats or weight loss should see a clinician.
