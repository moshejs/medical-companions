Part 1 · The infection

TB infection vs TB disease

Breathing in M. tuberculosis is not the same as having TB. Most people who become infected never fall ill; many clear the bacteria altogether; and of those who do develop TB disease, most do so within the first two years. Follow 100 people with a positive test over twenty years, then change who they are and watch the curve change with them.

Two boxes, or a spectrum?

The old teaching split everyone into "latent" and "active". The modern reading is a continuum of bacterial activity and immune response, with two further states proposed in between, and an international Delphi consensus that separates disease from infection by the presence of macroscopic pathology, defines two subclinical and two clinical states, and makes viable bacteria plus a host response a prerequisite for every state. [[Drain2018,Coussens2024]]

Which is it? Six short vignettes

Pick an answer for each, then read why. These are teaching examples, not clinical guidance.

How much TB comes from a long-dormant reservoir?

Established

A very large reservoir

Modelling of 131 infection surveys plus WHO prevalence estimates put the global burden of TB infection in 2014 at 23.0% of the world's population (95% UI 20.4–26.4), about 1.7 billion people, with around 80% in the South-East Asia, Western Pacific and Africa regions. Recent infection — within 2 years — affected about 55.5 million people. Even with no new infections after 2015, existing infection alone would still generate around 16.5 cases per 100,000 per year in 2035. [[Houben2016]]

Established

But disease follows infection quickly

Poulsen, who could pinpoint exposure in the Faroe Islands to a two-week period, found the incubation period of TB disease to be typically 3–9 months and almost always under two years. Among untreated tuberculin-positive contacts in Amsterdam, 75% of cases occurred within one year and 97% within two. Molecular fingerprinting gave a median incubation of 1.3 years, with 45%, 62% and 83% of cases by one, two and five years. [[Behr2018]]

Early evidence

And much of it may clear

Inferring self-clearance from post-mortem and tuberculin-reversion studies gave 24.4% (95% UI 17.8–32.6) self-clearing within 10 years of infection and 73.1% (64.6–81.7) over a lifetime. That shrinks the population at risk — by 35–80% in India, China and Japan — while raising the lifetime risk per person who really is infected to 17.0% (10.9–22.5) from 12.6% assuming lifelong infection. [[Emery2021]]

Contested

What is actually disputed

The timing data are consistent across eras and methods. What is contested is the strategic implication: whether most global TB comes from recent transmission, so that the "latent reservoir" has been overstated, or whether the reservoir remains a central obstacle to elimination. Two centuries of shifting definitions explain much of the confusion around the word "latent". [[Behr2018,Houben2016,Behr2021]]

Language. This page says TB infection, not "latent TB", and never "carrier": a person with TB infection is not carrying TB to anyone. A positive test records an encounter with the bacillus; whether live bacteria remain is a separate question, and often the answer is no. [[Behr2019,Behr2021]]
