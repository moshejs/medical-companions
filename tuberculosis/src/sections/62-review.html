Ninety flashcards on tuberculosis, from droplet nuclei and the granuloma to the 5-10-15 mm rule, Xpert, 2HRZE/4HR, BPaLM, BCG, contact tracing and life after treatment. Tap a card or press Space to flip it; arrow keys move through the deck. Filter by part, or shuffle.

Cards tagged well known are mnemonics or acronyms in common use; cards tagged memory aid were written for this guide to hold a published fact in mind. Neither replaces the source, and none is a dosing instruction.

Mnemonic in motion: RIPE and its warning signs

Play the scene to send each first-line drug to the part of the body its best-known side effect touches: rifampicin to the tears and urine, isoniazid to the nerves (and the B6 that protects them), pyrazinamide to the liver and the big toe, ethambutol to the eyes. Then watch the four run together as 2HRZE/4HR. See Side effects & monitoring for the full story.

Schematic, not anatomy. Bars: major adverse events per 100 person-months in one Montreal cohort [[Yee2003]]; isoniazid hepatitis rates from its label [[FDA-Isoniazid]]. Teaching only, not instructions.

Quiz

Single-best-answer questions on tuberculosis with cited explanations, including vignettes on TST and IGRA interpretation, infection versus disease, regimen choice, drug interactions and adverse effects. The mixed quiz draws 20 at random; or choose a part.

The vignettes are exam teaching. They quote guidelines and labels as published and are not advice for any real person; decisions about testing, treatment or isolation belong to a clinician or TB programme.

Glossary

A searchable glossary of TB terms, from acid-fast bacilli and RD1 to 3HP, BPaLM, AII rooms and catastrophic costs. Type to filter; the arrow on each entry jumps to the module where the term matters most.

Entries marked Language note give person-first terms proposed by TB programme authors in place of blaming ones: "person with presumptive TB" rather than "TB suspect", "lost to follow-up" rather than "defaulter" [[Zachariah2012]]. This guide also says TB infection for what used to be called latent TB, and TB disease for illness.

Sources

Every reference behind the TB Companion: journal articles, WHO and CDC guidelines, US drug labels, trial registrations and regulator records. The small numbers throughout the app point here; filter by research area or search.

Eight research passes ran in parallel, one per area: epidemiology, global burden and history; transmission, risk factors and host susceptibility; microbiology, immunology and pathogenesis; clinical presentation and diagnosis; treatment of drug-susceptible TB disease and TB infection; drug-resistant TB, comorbidity and outcomes; living with TB, infection control and nursing; and vaccines, the End TB Strategy and research. Before inclusion each source was checked against PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, ClinicalTrials.gov or openFDA, and the green tag on an entry names the service that confirmed it. Works found by more than one pass appear once, with any extra keys listed beside them.

Entries marked check, if any, are works that could not be confirmed against a database. They are used only for what they state, so treat them with extra care.
