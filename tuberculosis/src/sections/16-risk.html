Part 1 · The infection

Who is at risk

Most people infected with TB never fall ill. What tips the balance is mostly recent exposure and conditions that weaken the immune defence: HIV, undernutrition, diabetes, alcohol, smoking, dust and some medicines. Every bar below is a published population estimate, not a forecast for one person.

View

How to read it. A dot is the published estimate, the line its 95% confidence interval; a hollow dot has no interval in the source abstract. The scale is logarithmic, so 2 and 0.5 sit the same distance from 1. Estimates come from different study types (cohorts, registries, meta-analyses) and comparisons, so they are not a league table. The undernutrition point is derived from the per-unit gradient. Attributable shares overlap and must never be added up.

Conditions, not character

Crowding

Housing is an exposure

In New Zealand, each unit of household crowding raised TB incidence by 5% (IRR 1.05), and by 8% in New Zealand-born people under 40, after adjusting for income, TB burden and migration. The authors treat crowding as a structural exposure that can be changed, not as a trait of the people living there. [[Baker2008Crowding]]

Prisons

Where buildings concentrate TB

People in prison acquired TB infection at 15.0 per 100 person-years. TB incidence was 26.9 times the general population in South America and above 10 times in three other WHO regions. Transmission inside prisons was estimated to cause 6.3–8.5% of TB in the general population outside. [[Cords2021,Baussano2010]]

Migration

Infection acquired elsewhere

In 20 low-incidence European countries, rates in recently arrived migrants appeared up to 11 times those in their countries of origin. The authors read this as infection acquired where TB is common plus the stresses of migration and barriers to care; the response they call for is accessible, free screening and care, not exclusion. [[Domaszewska2025]]

Poverty

Upstream of biology

WHO's review places living and working conditions upstream of the biological risk factors: they raise the chance of exposure and cluster undernutrition, smoking and other vulnerabilities in the same households. Thirty high-burden countries carried 87% of cases in 2024. Language that blames people affected by TB discourages the care-seeking that stops transmission. [[Lonnroth2009,Zenner2027]]

Diabetes

Screening both ways

Diabetes roughly triples TB risk, and in people treated for TB it went with more failure or death (RR 1.69) and more relapse (RR 3.89). Diabetes is growing fastest in the countries that carry the TB burden, so collaborative TB and diabetes services are described as necessary and feasible, though without trial evidence yet. Early evidence [[Jeon2008,Baker2011,Lonnroth2014DM]]

Planned risk

Test before the medicine

Of 70 TB cases reported after infliximab, the median onset was 12 weeks and 40 were outside the lungs; none of 69 cases in the French registry had received correct preventive treatment. Because the exposure is planned, these are the clearest reasons to test for TB infection and offer preventive treatment first. See Treating TB infection. [[Keane2001,Tubach2009,Sterling2020]]

Established

Rare inherited susceptibility

Mendelian susceptibility to mycobacterial disease comes from inborn errors of the interferon-γ circuit; IL-12Rβ1 deficiency, the commonest form, began at a mean age of 2.4 years, usually with BCG. Each such defect is rarer than 1 in 600,000. The common TYK2 P1104A variant matters only with two copies: homozygotes made up 1% of TB patients in UK Biobank vs 0.2% of controls (OR 5.0), about 1% of TB in people of European ancestry, while one copy showed no excess. [[Bustamante2014,deBeaucoudrey2010,BoissonDupuis2018,Kerner2019]]

The practical reading of all these numbers is the same: find TB early in households, offer preventive treatment to those most at risk, treat HIV, and tackle food, housing and tobacco. Coverage, not evidence, is the bottleneck: in 2024 preventive treatment reached 58% of eligible people living with HIV but only 25% of eligible household contacts. [[Fox2013,Suthar2012,Zenner2027]]
