Side effects & monitoring

Most people finish TB treatment without serious problems, but each drug has its own side effects, and many appear in a typical window. Scrub through six months to see which organs to watch and when, and the checks that go with each drug.

Schematic timing. Bars show when each drug is given; darker shading marks a typical window only where a source gives one (isoniazid hepatitis usually in the first 3 months; liver events in SHINE almost all in the first 8 weeks; linezolid neuropathy mainly after 28 days). Frequencies are population figures from trials and cohorts. [[FDA-Isoniazid,Turkova2022,FDA-Zyvox]]

Tell the TB team straight away about yellow eyes or skin, dark urine, stomach pain or loss of appetite; blurred vision or trouble telling red from green; or tingling or burning in the hands or feet. Orange-red urine, sweat and tears from rifampicin are expected and harmless. [[FDA-Rifampin,FDA-Ethambutol,FDA-Isoniazid]]

A raised liver test: how the ATS rule works

A teaching tool, not a decision aid. Move the ALT level and say whether there are symptoms; the tree follows the American Thoracic Society statement as published. Real decisions are made by the treating clinician, who weighs much more than one number.

Rifampicin and other medicines

Rifampicin speeds up the liver and gut enzymes (CYP3A4, and also CYP2C and P-glycoprotein) that clear many other medicines. Induction is complete in about a week and wears off over roughly two weeks after rifampicin stops, so levels of other medicines fall during TB treatment and rise again afterwards. Established

Which first-line drug causes most trouble?

In a Montreal cohort of 430 people, major adverse events per 100 person-months were 1.48 for pyrazinamide, 0.49 for isoniazid, 0.43 for rifampin and 0.07 for ethambutol. Risk was higher over 60 (HR 2.9), in women (2.5), people born in Asia (2.5) and people with HIV (3.8). [[Yee2003]]

Established

Liver injury rises with age

The isoniazid label gives hepatitis rates of under 1 per 1,000 under age 20, 3 at 20–34, 12 at 35–49 and 23 at 50–64. Across studies, drug-induced liver injury during TB treatment ranged from 2% to 28%; risk factors include older age, female sex, slow acetylation, malnutrition, HIV and liver disease. [[FDA-Isoniazid,Tostmann2008]]

Established

Ethambutol and the eyes

In a meta-analysis, visual impairment occurred in 22.5 per 1,000 people treated and was permanent in 4.3 per 1,000; at average doses of 27.5 mg/kg/day or less for 2–9 months, 19.2 and 2.3 per 1,000. Most episodes reversed after about 3 months. [[Ezer2013]]

Established

Linezolid dose matters

In ZeNix, peripheral neuropathy was 38% with 1,200 mg for 26 weeks against 24% with 600 mg for 26 weeks, and myelosuppression 22% against 2%, with favourable outcomes of 93% and 91%. In Nix-TB, at 1,200 mg, neuropathy reached 81%. [[Conradie2022,Conradie2020]]

Early evidence

QT effects add up, but modestly

In ACTG A5343, mean QTcF rose by 12.3 ms with bedaquiline, 8.6 ms with delamanid and 20.7 ms with both, with no grade 3–4 QTc events: no more than additive. [[Dooley2021LID]]

Established

Newer regimens can be gentler

In TB-PRACTECAL, grade 3 or higher or serious adverse events occurred in 19% on 6-month BPaLM against 59% on the older standard care for drug-resistant TB. [[Nyangwa2022]] See Treating drug-resistant TB.
