Drug resistance

Resistance is not caused by the drugs: rare mutants are already in every large population of TB bacteria. Pick a scenario and watch what one drug, four drugs, or stopping early does to them, then how a resistant strain reaches someone new.

Why combinations work: the arithmetic

Each resistance mutation arises independently, so the chance that one bacillus resists two drugs at once is roughly the product of the two single-drug rates [[David1970,Dheda2024]]. Name the resistance: WHO 2021 categories

In 2021 WHO redefined pre-XDR-TB and XDR-TB around the drugs that matter most today. The older XDR definition was based on fluoroquinolone plus second-line injectable resistance, and trials that enrolled before 2021, such as Nix-TB and ZeNix, used it [[Viney2021,Conradie2022]]. Tick what a strain resists.

WHO Group A medicines: levofloxacin or moxifloxacin, bedaquiline and linezolid. Group B: clofazimine and cycloserine/terizidone. Group C includes ethambutol, delamanid, pyrazinamide, imipenem–cilastatin, meropenem and amikacin [[WHO-Mod4-2025]].

Which gene, which test

Most resistance comes from mutations in a handful of genes, so molecular tests can read resistance in hours or days instead of the weeks that growing the bacteria takes. In one implementation study the median time to a resistance result was 20 days by line probe assay against 106 days by conventional drug-susceptibility testing (DST) [[Boehme2011]]. WHO conditionally recommends tNGS on respiratory samples from people with rifampicin-resistant TB, covering isoniazid, fluoroquinolones, bedaquiline, linezolid, clofazimine, pyrazinamide, ethambutol, amikacin and streptomycin [[WHO-Mod3-2024]]. Established The 2021 WHO mutation catalogue graded only two resistance mutations each for bedaquiline, delamanid, clofazimine and linezolid, so genotypic testing for the newest drugs is still incomplete [[Walker2022]]. The global picture

New MDR/RR-TB, 2024390 000

Estimated people who developed MDR/RR-TB in 2024 (95% UI 360 000–430 000), falling since 2015. MDR/RR-TB caused an estimated 150 000 deaths [[WHO-GTR2025]]. About a quarter of deaths caused by antimicrobial resistance are due to rifampicin-resistant TB [[Dean2022]].

Who has it3.2% · 16%

Share of MDR/RR-TB among people never treated before (down from 4.7% in 2015) and among previously treated people (down from 19%). In new cases it ranged from 2.0% (African and Eastern Mediterranean regions) to 23% (European Region) [[WHO-GTR2025]].

Whereover half

Four countries had over half of incident MDR/RR-TB in 2024: India 32%, China 7.1%, the Philippines 7.1% and the Russian Federation 6.7%. An estimated 18% of MDR/RR-TB was pre-XDR, and 1.5 million people developed isoniazid-resistant TB [[WHO-GTR2025]].

The treatment gap≈ 42%

164 545 people were enrolled on MDR/RR-TB treatment in 2024, down from 179 491 in 2022: roughly 42% of the estimated 390 000 (calculated here from the two WHO figures). Ten countries made up 74% of the gap; India alone 33% [[WHO-GTR2025]].

Testing83% · 58%

In 2024, 83% of people with bacteriologically confirmed TB were tested for rifampicin resistance, but only 58% of people with rifampicin-resistant TB were tested for fluoroquinolone resistance [[WHO-GTR2025]].

Cost82%

Share of households affected by drug-resistant TB facing catastrophic costs, against 47% for all TB, even where treatment is free [[WHO-GTR2025]]. Drug-resistant TB often uses more than 50% of national TB budgets while making up less than 5–10% of cases [[Dheda2024]].

Resistance is curable, and it is not a fault. Most people with drug-resistant TB were infected with a strain that was already resistant [[Kendall2015,Shah2017NEJM]]. All-oral regimens of 6 to 9 months now cure most people in trials; see Treating drug-resistant TB. Contacts of people with MDR/RR-TB can be offered preventive treatment: WHO recommends 6 months of daily levofloxacin [[WHO-Mod1-2024]].
