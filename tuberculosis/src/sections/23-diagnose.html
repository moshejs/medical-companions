Diagnosing TB disease

One sample, several tests, very different clocks: a rapid molecular test answers in under 2 hours, a smear the next day, and culture takes weeks. Pick a person, then follow the tests along a time axis that runs from hours to months.

Schematic. Times are medians or ranges from named studies and the ATS/IDSA/CDC guideline; real times depend on the laboratory and how far samples travel. Which tests turn positive is one illustrative case per person, not a prediction. [[Boehme2010,Boehme2011,Chihota2010,Lewinsohn2017]]

How many does each test find?

Sensitivity is the share of people with TB a test finds; specificity is the share of people without TB it correctly calls negative. Pooled estimates from the populations studied, mostly adults with symptoms. [[Zifodya2021,Lewinsohn2017]]

Chest X-ray

What an X-ray can and cannot tell

  • Can: find most TB, with or without symptoms. Any abnormality had a summary sensitivity of 94.7% and specificity of 89.1%; it detected 89% of TB in prevalence surveys. [[VantHoog2022,Frascella2021]]
  • Can: show cavities, which go with poorer outcomes, more transmission and drug resistance, and which feed into decisions such as treatment length. [[Urbanowski2020,Nachiappan2017]]
  • Cannot: prove TB. A flagged person still needs a bacteriological test; old scars look like disease, and only stability over 6 months marks them inactive. [[VantHoog2022,Nachiappan2017]]
  • Cannot: rule TB out in people with HIV, who form fewer cavities, or in miliary TB, whose pattern may appear late. [[Pai2016,Sharma2005]]
Early evidence

X-ray read by software

Computer-aided detection gives each digital film an abnormality score. In 23,954 people screened in Dhaka all five algorithms outperformed radiologists and halved the Xpert tests needed while keeping sensitivity above 90%, but they did worse in people over 60 and after previous TB. In Viet Nam, 6 of 12 products performed on par with an expert reader. A 2019 review found accuracy lower in clinical evaluations (median AUC 0.75) than in development studies (0.88). [[Qin2021,Codlin2021,Harris2019]]

Open questions

Early evidence

Does faster diagnosis save lives?

When nurses ran Xpert in primary care, more people were diagnosed (24% vs 13%) and treated (23% vs 15%) the same day, but TB-related illness at 2 and 6 months did not differ. Pooling five trials, Xpert did not clearly lower 6-month mortality (OR 0.88, 0.68–1.14), with a possible benefit in people with HIV. Gains depend on the whole care cascade. [[Theron2014,DiTanna2019]]

Early evidence

What to do with an Ultra "trace"

"Trace", Ultra's lowest positive category, made up 3.0–30.4% of Ultra positives, and data were too few to judge repeat testing. Ultra specificity is lower in people with previous TB (93% vs 98% for Xpert MTB/RIF in one study). [[Zifodya2021,Dorman2018Ultra]]

HIV

Urine LAM: test everyone admitted, or target?

In 10 African hospitals, LAM-guided treatment cut 8-week mortality from 25% to 21% (RR 0.83). STAMP, which screened all HIV-positive inpatients, found no significant overall benefit (18% vs 21%) but lower mortality with CD4 under 100, severe anaemia or suspected TB. Pooled, LAM strategies likely reduce 8-week mortality (RR 0.85). [[Peter2016,GuptaWright2018,Nathavitharana2021LAM]]

Children

Most childhood TB is never confirmed

In 13 studies, 70.1% of children classified as having pulmonary TB had no bacteriological confirmation. Gastric aspirates on three mornings yield up to 40–50% (up to 90% in infants), nasopharyngeal aspirate or induced sputum 20–30%. Xpert MTB/RIF on children's sputum was 36–44% more sensitive than microscopy. [[Gunasekera2023,Lewinsohn2017,Detjen2015]] See TB with HIV, in children & pregnancy.
