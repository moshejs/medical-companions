TB Companion · animated and cited

Tuberculosis, explained

Tuberculosis is an airborne infection that can be cured, yet in 2024 an estimated 10.7 million people fell ill with it and 1.23 million died, more than from any other single infectious agent. [[WHO-GTR2025]] About a quarter of the world's population is estimated to have TB infection, and only a small share of them ever develop TB disease. [[Houben2016,Cohen2019ERJ]] This guide shows how TB spreads, how it is found and how it is treated, in animations you can scrub, with every claim cited.

Every animation can be driven from the keyboard: Tab to its timeline, then arrow keys to scrub and Enter to play or pause.

Schematic, not to scale; the timing is illustrative.

For students

Every module has an Exam hook box and a citation trail. Finish with flashcards, mnemonics and a quiz. Nursing and infection-control content is in parts 4 and 5.

For people with TB and their families

Look for the In plain words boxes. Bring questions to your own doctor, nurse or TB programme: the app reports what guidelines and trials say, not what you should do. Regimens and doses are quoted as published, never as instructions.

How sure is each claim?

Research claims carry a label: Established Early evidence Hypothesis. Small numbers link to the Sources list.
