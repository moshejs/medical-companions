An animated, cited guide to tuberculosis for students, people with TB and their families. Educational only, not medical advice. Your place is saved on this device.

Not medical adviceThe TB Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and for patient and family understanding. It does not replace a doctor, nurse or TB programme. Regimens, doses, test cut-offs and guideline statements are reported as published, not as instructions or a diagnosis for any individual. TB is a notifiable disease, and treatment is effective and free in most public programmes; anyone with symptoms such as a lasting cough should see a clinician.

Part 6 · Context & review

Sources

Every reference behind the TB Companion: journal articles, WHO and CDC guidelines, US drug labels, trial registrations and regulator records. The small numbers throughout the app point here; filter by research area or search.

Eight research passes ran in parallel, one per area: epidemiology, global burden and history; transmission, risk factors and host susceptibility; microbiology, immunology and pathogenesis; clinical presentation and diagnosis; treatment of drug-susceptible TB disease and TB infection; drug-resistant TB, comorbidity and outcomes; living with TB, infection control and nursing; and vaccines, the End TB Strategy and research. Before inclusion each source was checked against PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, ClinicalTrials.gov or openFDA, and the green tag on an entry names the service that confirmed it. Works found by more than one pass appear once, with any extra keys listed beside them.

Entries marked not matched in a research database, if any, are works that could not be confirmed against a database. They are used only for what they state, so treat them with extra care.
