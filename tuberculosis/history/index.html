History & people

18 July 1921 · Public health

First human BCG

From 1908 Albert Calmette and Camille Guérin subcultured a virulent bovine strain; after about 230 subcultures over 11 years it no longer caused progressive TB in animals. On 18 July 1921 Benjamin Weill-Hallé and Raymond Turpin gave the first human dose by mouth, to a newborn in Paris whose mother had died of TB. From 1924 to 1928, 114,000 infants were vaccinated without serious complications. 299

In plain wordsRobert Koch found the TB germ in 1882. World TB Day, 24 March, marks that day.
Exam hook24 March 1882 = World TB Day. Tuberculin failed as a cure (1890) but became the skin test (von Pirquet 1907). First BCG: 18 July 1921.

What changed the curve?

TB deaths were falling in England and Wales long before any drug existed, and they did not fall the same way everywhere. Pick a lens. 111,226,500

England and Wales, 1853–1910

Falling before any drug

TB mortality in England and Wales fell by an average of 1.71% a year (95% CI 0.77–2.63) from 1853 to 1910, before any effective drug. Over the same period total mortality, infant mortality and housing density did not fall significantly. The authors argue that social conditions alone do not explain the decline and propose a role for natural selection (Hypothesis). 111 Koch's 1882 discovery changed understanding, not treatment.

In plain wordsTB was already becoming less deadly in Victorian England, decades before any medicine. Why is still debated.

People

1882 · Berlin

Robert Koch

Presented his isolation of the tubercle bacillus to the Physiological Society in Berlin on 24 March 1882, the date World TB Day now marks. 26,66 The work rested on Villemin's proof that TB is transmissible and on two new methods: a staining technique and a solid, serum-based culture medium. 60 His tuberculin, offered as a cure in 1890–91, failed in practice but later became a diagnostic tool. Nobel Prize, 1905. 26,204,409

1908–1921

Albert Calmette and Camille Guérin

From 1908 they subcultured a virulent bovine strain on bile-glycerine-potato medium; after about 230 passages over 11 years it no longer caused progressive TB in animals. On 18 July 1921 Weill-Hallé and Turpin gave the first human dose, by mouth, to a newborn in Paris whose mother had died of TB. 299 BCG is still the only licensed TB vaccine. 278 See BCG & new vaccines.

1944

Albert Schatz, Elizabeth Bugie and Selman Waksman

Reported streptomycin, from a soil actinomycete, in 1944. 96,414 Its first controlled test, the 1948 MRC trial, also showed resistance emerging when it was used alone. 96

1946

Jörgen Lehmann

Reported para-aminosalicylic acid (PAS) in The Lancet in January 1946. Given with streptomycin, it greatly reduced drug resistance: the first step to combination therapy. 282,336

1948

John Crofton

His first-hand account of the 1948 MRC streptomycin trial, "a view from the clinical front line", records the deaths and the resistance that followed single-drug treatment, the lesson that TB must be treated with several drugs together. 96

The short-course era

Denis Mitchison

Best known for the idea that TB bacteria live as subpopulations, some growing fast and some barely metabolising, that different drugs reach differently: the basis of multi-drug, two-phase short-course treatment. 328 His summary of the trial-proven steps: streptomycin works, combinations prevent resistance, home treatment works, supervised intermittent dosing helps, and rifampicin and pyrazinamide shorten treatment. 329

Former names

What TB used to be called

Phthisis
The ancient Greek name; Hippocrates described a fatal disease, especially of young adults. 26
Consumption
The English name, as in Benjamin Marten's 1720 "A new theory of Consumption", the first suggestion of an infectious cause. 26
Pott's disease
TB of the spine; its deformity is visible in Egyptian remains from about 2400 BC. 26
"Captain of All These Men of Death"
Its title in 18th- and 19th-century Europe and North America, when it caused about one death in four. 26,103
Tuberculosis
Coined by Johann Lukas Schönlein in the mid-19th century. 26 Historians also call it "the white death". 204
Controversy

Why did TB fall before drugs?

One model credits a fall in effective contact: each infectious person effectively contacted about 22 people in 1900, 10 by 1950 and about 1 by 1990. 500 Another analysis found that TB mortality fell 1.71% a year from 1853 to 1910 while total mortality, infant mortality and housing density did not fall significantly, and proposed a role for natural selection (Hypothesis). 111 Comparing cities, rates fell in New York and London but stayed high in Cape Town, pointing to transmission tied to social and economic conditions. 226 The sanatorium movement drew attention to people with TB and helped spur public-health action. 336

Discrepancies in the sources: Murray and colleagues say PAS was "discovered in 1945", while Lehmann's Lancet report is dated January 1946. 282,336
