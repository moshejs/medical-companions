Part 1 · The infection

TB infection vs TB disease

Breathing in M. tuberculosis is not the same as having TB. Most people who become infected never fall ill; many clear the bacteria altogether; and of those who do develop TB disease, most do so within the first two years. Follow 100 people with a positive test over twenty years, then change who they are and watch the curve change with them.

Year 0

100 people, one positive test

Everyone in this grid has a positive test for TB infection on the day we start. None of them is ill, none is infectious, and the test says only that their immune system has met the bacillus. Globally this group is enormous: modelling of 131 infection surveys put the burden of TB infection in 2014 at 23.0% of the world's population (95% UI 20.4–26.4), about 1.7 billion people, of whom 55.5 million had been infected within the previous two years. 93,239

In plain wordsA positive TB test is not a diagnosis of TB. It means the body has met the germ at some point. Most people with a positive test never become unwell and cannot pass anything on.

Two boxes, or a spectrum?

The old teaching split everyone into "latent" and "active". The modern reading is a continuum of bacterial activity and immune response, with two further states proposed in between, and an international Delphi consensus that separates disease from infection by the presence of macroscopic pathology, defines two subclinical and two clinical states, and makes viable bacteria plus a host response a prerequisite for every state. 93,142

The spectrum

Five states, not two

Cleared. No viable bacteria left. Reactivity can outlast infection by at least nine years, and self-clearance is estimated at 24.4% within 10 years and 73.1% over a lifetime. 35,148

TB infection. Viable bacteria and a host response, but no macroscopic pathology: no symptoms, nothing to find in sputum, not infectious. 93,142

Incipient. Proposed between infection and disease: a blood transcriptomic signature predicted progression with 66.1% sensitivity and 80.6% specificity in the 12 months before diagnosis, and interferon signalling was already raised 18 months out. 142,418,532

Subclinical TB. Disease without reported symptoms. A median 50.4% of prevalent bacteriologically confirmed TB in national surveys was subclinical, and PET-CT found subclinical pulmonary abnormalities in 10 of 35 asymptomatic adults with HIV and TB infection, who were much likelier to progress. 154,188

TB disease. Symptoms or signs, and likely infectiousness. The consensus defines two subclinical and two clinical states, all requiring viable bacteria and a host response. 93

Exam hookThe ICE-TB Delphi consensus (71 participants) distinguishes disease from infection by macroscopic pathology, defines two subclinical and two clinical states differentiated by reported symptoms or signs and by likely infectiousness, and requires viable M. tuberculosis plus a host response for every state. 93

How much TB comes from a long-dormant reservoir?

Established

A very large reservoir

Modelling of 131 infection surveys plus WHO prevalence estimates put the global burden of TB infection in 2014 at 23.0% of the world's population (95% UI 20.4–26.4), about 1.7 billion people, with around 80% in the South-East Asia, Western Pacific and Africa regions. Recent infection — within 2 years — affected about 55.5 million people. Even with no new infections after 2015, existing infection alone would still generate around 16.5 cases per 100,000 per year in 2035. 239

Established

But disease follows infection quickly

Poulsen, who could pinpoint exposure in the Faroe Islands to a two-week period, found the incubation period of TB disease to be typically 3–9 months and almost always under two years. Among untreated tuberculin-positive contacts in Amsterdam, 75% of cases occurred within one year and 97% within two. Molecular fingerprinting gave a median incubation of 1.3 years, with 45%, 62% and 83% of cases by one, two and five years. 34

Early evidence

And much of it may clear

Inferring self-clearance from post-mortem and tuberculin-reversion studies gave 24.4% (95% UI 17.8–32.6) self-clearing within 10 years of infection and 73.1% (64.6–81.7) over a lifetime. That shrinks the population at risk — by 35–80% in India, China and Japan — while raising the lifetime risk per person who really is infected to 17.0% (10.9–22.5) from 12.6% assuming lifelong infection. 148

Contested

What is actually disputed

The timing data are consistent across eras and methods. What is contested is the strategic implication: whether most global TB comes from recent transmission, so that the "latent reservoir" has been overstated, or whether the reservoir remains a central obstacle to elimination. Two centuries of shifting definitions explain much of the confusion around the word "latent". 34,36,239

Language. This page says TB infection, not "latent TB", and never "carrier": a person with TB infection is not carrying TB to anyone. A positive test records an encounter with the bacillus; whether live bacteria remain is a separate question, and often the answer is no. 35,36
