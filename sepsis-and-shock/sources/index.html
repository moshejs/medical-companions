An animated, cited guide for students, people who survive sepsis, and their families and caregivers. Educational only, not medical advice. Your place is saved on this device.

Not medical adviceThe Sepsis Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. It does not replace a doctor, nurse, pharmacist, paramedic or other clinician. The qSOFA, SOFA, NEWS2, Phoenix and shock-index calculators are teaching aids, never for self-triage or for deciding whether to seek care, and antibiotic and vasopressor doses, fluid volumes, MAP and lactate targets and guideline statements are quoted as published, never as instructions. Mortality figures are population statistics, not predictions for one person. Sepsis is a medical emergency. If someone with an infection becomes very unwell (confused or very drowsy, breathing very fast, cold, mottled or bluish skin, passing little or no urine), call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department now and ask: could this be sepsis?

Sources

Every reference behind the Sepsis Companion: journal articles, randomised trials, systematic reviews and meta-analyses, Surviving Sepsis Campaign and other guidance, drug labels and trial registrations. The small numbers throughout the app point here; filter by research area or search.

Eight research passes ran in parallel, one per area: epidemiology, risk factors and history; immunology, the endothelium, clotting, organ dysfunction and the research frontier; haemodynamics and the shock types; recognition, early warning scores and definitions; tests, biomarkers, the first hour and maternal sepsis; fluids, vasopressors, steroids and blood-pressure targets; source control, stewardship, intensive care and nursing; and life after sepsis, families and prevention. Before inclusion each source was checked, where possible, against PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, ClinicalTrials.gov or openFDA, and the green tag on an entry names the service that confirmed it. Works found by more than one pass appear once, with any extra keys listed beside them.

Sepsis Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on sepsis (the body's harmful response to an infection), septic shock and the other types of shock, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people who survive sepsis, their families and caregivers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine or a care plan without your own care team. The qSOFA, SOFA, NEWS2, Phoenix and shock-index calculators are for learning, never for self-triage or for deciding whether to seek care. Antibiotic and vasopressor doses, fluid volumes, MAP and lactate targets and guideline statements are quoted as published in guidelines and labels (Surviving Sepsis Campaign, NICE, Sepsis-3), never as instructions, and mortality figures are population statistics. Sepsis is a medical emergency. If someone with an infection becomes very unwell (confused or very drowsy, breathing very fast, cold, mottled or bluish skin, passing little or no urine), call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department now and ask: could this be sepsis? Please check the original sources, which are listed in full in the Sources module.
