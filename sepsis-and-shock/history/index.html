The qSOFA, SOFA, NEWS2, Phoenix and shock-index calculators are teaching aids, never for self-triage or for deciding whether to seek care, and antibiotic and vasopressor doses, fluid volumes, MAP and lactate targets and guideline statements are quoted as published, never as instructions. Mortality figures are population statistics, not predictions for one person. Sepsis is a medical emergency. If someone with an infection becomes very unwell (confused or very drowsy, breathing very fast, cold, mottled or bluish skin, passing little or no urine), call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department now and ask: could this be sepsis?

History & people

1847–1870s

Hands, antisepsis, germs

Semmelweis's chlorine handwashing (May 1847) cut Clinic 1 deaths to 2.0%; Lister published the antiseptic principle in 1867; Pasteur then grew Streptococcus pyogenes from a woman with puerperal sepsis. 265,458

In plain wordsWashing hands between patients saved many mothers' lives. It is still one of the best ways to prevent the infections that lead to sepsis. 458

Washing hands in Vienna, 1847

At the Vienna General Hospital, Clinic 1 was staffed by medical students and doctors who also did autopsies, and Clinic 2 by midwives. Ignaz Semmelweis blamed "cadaverous particles" carried on hands, and in mid-May 1847 brought in chlorine handwashing. 458 Choose a period to see how many of every 100 women who gave birth in Clinic 1 died of puerperal (childbed) sepsis. Alexander Gordon in Aberdeen (1795) and Oliver Wendell Holmes in Boston (1843) had already suspected that birth attendants carried the disease. 458

Mortality ratios as reported from the clinic's records; historical figures for one hospital, not a prediction for anyone. 458 Semmelweis delayed publishing his main work until 1861, and his ideas were poorly received in Vienna. 458

When a promising result did not hold

Two stories from the 2000s shaped how sepsis research is done. A single-centre trial of early goal-directed therapy (EGDT) reported a large fall in deaths, and three later multicentre trials did not confirm it. 367,395 Drotrecogin alfa (activated) was approved after one trial, PROWESS, that was stopped early for efficacy; the manufacturer withdrew it worldwide in 2011 after PROWESS-SHOCK failed. 20,288 More than 100 randomised trials have tested ways to modulate the septic response; "with one short-lived exception" (drotrecogin alfa), none produced a new treatment. 277

How the definitions changed

YearNameWhat it said
1991 · 1992Sepsis-1 (ACCP/SCCM)Defined SIRS, sepsis, severe sepsis, septic shock and multiple organ dysfunction syndrome. SIRS meant 2 or more of 4 simple signs: temperature, heart rate, breathing rate and white cell count. 60 Because most infected patients meet them, sepsis and infection became confused. 501
2001 · 2003Sepsis-2An international conference of 29 participants expanded the list of signs and symptoms but found no evidence to change the 1992 definitions. 258
2005Paediatric consensusAdapted the adult SIRS-based definitions for children. 172
2016Sepsis-3Sepsis became "life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection", shown by a SOFA rise of 2 points or more; "severe sepsis" was dropped and qSOFA introduced. 447

People behind the ideas

Antiquity

Homer, Hippocrates, Aristotle, Galen

"Sepsis" comes from the Greek word for putrefaction. Homer used it more than 2,500 years ago, and Hippocrates, Aristotle, Plutarch and Galen used it to mean decay. 501

1847 · 1861

Ignaz Semmelweis

Brought in chlorine handwashing in Vienna's Clinic 1 in May 1847; deaths from childbed fever fell to 2.0% over the next 20 months. He published his main work only in 1861. 458

1867

Joseph Lister

Published "On the Antiseptic Principle in the Practice of Surgery" in the BMJ. 265 Historical reviews trace sepsis thinking through Lister, Semmelweis and Fleming. 159

1870s

Louis Pasteur

About 30 years after Semmelweis, he grew Streptococcus pyogenes from the blood of a woman with puerperal sepsis: germ theory linking a named microbe to sepsis. 458

1929 · 1940

Alexander Fleming, Ernst Chain, Howard Florey

Fleming reported the antibacterial action of a Penicillium mould; Chain, Florey and colleagues showed penicillin working as a medicine, opening the antibiotic era. 87,154

Late 1980s · 1992

Roger Bone

Introduced the term "sepsis syndrome", then led the consensus conference that defined SIRS and Sepsis-1. 60,501

1996

Jean-Louis Vincent and the ESICM group

Published the SOFA (Sepsis-related Organ Failure Assessment) score, which scores six organ systems. 494 It became the yardstick for organ dysfunction in Sepsis-3. 447

2001

Emanuel Rivers

His emergency-department trial of early goal-directed therapy shaped the "early, protocolised" era; later trials found no benefit over usual care. 367,395

2016

Mervyn Singer, Christopher Seymour and the Sepsis-3 task force

Redefined sepsis around organ dysfunction. Seymour's analysis showed qSOFA predicting death outside the ICU (AUROC 0.81) and SOFA doing better inside it. 434,447

2017

Konrad Reinhart and colleagues

Described the World Health Assembly resolution that made sepsis a global health priority, covering prevention, diagnosis, management and survivors' access to rehabilitation. 387

2024

Luregn Schlapbach and the Phoenix task force

Replaced the 2005 SIRS-based criteria for children with the data-driven Phoenix score. In-hospital mortality with Phoenix sepsis was 7.1% in higher-resource and 28.5% in lower-resource settings (population figures). 418 See Sepsis in babies and children.

Sepsis Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on sepsis (the body's harmful response to an infection), septic shock and the other types of shock, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people who survive sepsis, their families and caregivers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine or a care plan without your own care team. The qSOFA, SOFA, NEWS2, Phoenix and shock-index calculators are for learning, never for self-triage or for deciding whether to seek care. Antibiotic and vasopressor doses, fluid volumes, MAP and lactate targets and guideline statements are quoted as published in guidelines and labels (Surviving Sepsis Campaign, NICE, Sepsis-3), never as instructions, and mortality figures are population statistics. Sepsis is a medical emergency. If someone with an infection becomes very unwell (confused or very drowsy, breathing very fast, cold, mottled or bluish skin, passing little or no urine), call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department now and ask: could this be sepsis?
