Part 5 · Special situations & recovery

Cardiogenic, hypovolaemic, obstructive & anaphylactic shock

Not all shock is septic. A failing heart, heavy bleeding, a blocked circulation or a severe allergic reaction can each starve the organs of oxygen, and each is treated differently. Pick a type and scrub from cause to treatment, with the trials and guideline wording behind each step.

Call nowShock of any kind is an emergency. If someone has signs of a severe allergic reaction, heavy bleeding or collapse, or an infection with confusion, very fast breathing, cold, mottled or bluish skin or little or no urine, call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department now. An adrenaline auto-injector is used straight away in a severe allergic reaction, and emergency help is still called 135,372.

Cardiogenic · 1

A coronary artery blocks

Cardiogenic shock occurs in up to 10% of people immediately after an acute myocardial infarction (heart attack) and is the leading cause of in-hospital death after one 409. In the 2019 SCAI scheme, stage A is "at risk" 41.

In plain wordsA heart attack happens when a blood vessel feeding the heart muscle is blocked. Sometimes so much muscle is affected that the heart cannot pump enough blood: that is cardiogenic shock 409.
Exam hookCardiogenic shock complicates up to 10% of acute MI; 30-day mortality is about 40% 409.

Cardiogenic shock: stages and trials

Cardiogenic shock affects 40,000–50,000 people a year in the US and is the leading cause of in-hospital death after a heart attack. Thirty-day mortality is about 40% and 1-year mortality approaches 50% 409. These are population figures and do not predict what will happen to any one person. The main US consensus document is the 2017 American Heart Association scientific statement 489.

The 2019 SCAI scheme grades cardiogenic shock from A to E: "at risk", "beginning", "classic", "deteriorating" and "extremis". Hypoperfusion separates stage B from C, and stage D means the first treatments have not restored stability after at least 30 minutes 41. In 10,004 cardiac intensive care patients at the Mayo Clinic, unadjusted hospital mortality rose with each stage 215.

Jentzer 2019, Mayo Clinic cardiac ICU, unadjusted hospital mortality by SCAI stage 41,215.

Cardiogenic shock trials, as 100 people

SHOCK (1999; 302 patients with left-ventricular failure shock after a heart attack): 30-day mortality was 46.7% with emergency revascularisation vs 56.0% with initial medical stabilisation (P=0.11); at 6 months it was 50.3% vs 63.1% (P=0.027) 194.

Hypovolaemic and haemorrhagic shock

ATLS teaches four classes of hypovolaemic shock by estimated blood loss, with matching heart rate, blood pressure and mental status 313,314. Real patients rarely fit them: in 36,504 trauma patients only 9.3% could be placed in a class using heart rate, systolic pressure and the Glasgow Coma Scale together, and no relevant tachycardia was seen in any blood-pressure group 311. In a survey of 383 European ATLS instructors, only 10.9% called the classes a "good guide" for fluids and blood products 314.

The same group proposed classes by base deficit (16,305 patients): from class I (≤2 mmol/L) to class IV (>10 mmol/L), mortality rose from 7.4% to 51.5% and massive transfusion from 5% to 52% 312. Severe burns bring an inflammatory response and a distributive shock that can lead to multiple organ failure 216.

Obstructive shock

ESC 2015

Cardiac tamponade

ESC 2015 defines tamponade as "a life-threatening, slow or rapid compression of the heart due to the pericardial accumulation of fluid". Listed signs include tachycardia, hypotension, pulsus paradoxus (an inspiratory fall in systolic pressure of more than 10 mmHg), raised jugular venous pressure and muffled heart sounds 9. Echocardiography is "the single most useful diagnostic tool", and drainage, "preferably by needle pericardiocentesis", should not be delayed in unstable patients 9.

Not the textbook picture

Beck's triad is uncommon

The classic triad of low pressure, raised neck veins and muffled heart sounds "does not appear commonly"; breathlessness is the most frequently reported symptom. Diastolic right-ventricular collapse on echo is specific, and a plethoric, non-collapsing inferior vena cava is sensitive. Positive-pressure ventilation and sedation can lower cardiac output in tamponade 13.

Ventilation

Tension pneumothorax

In a review of 183 case reports and 5 cohorts, hypoxia was reported in 50.0% of people breathing on their own and 91.8% of ventilated people. With assisted ventilation, the adjusted odds of hypotension were 12.6 times and of cardiac arrest 17.7 times higher, and 70.4% of ventilated patients who became hypotensive or arrested did so within minutes 396.

ESC 2019

High-risk pulmonary embolism

ESC 2019 defines high-risk PE as cardiac arrest, obstructive shock (systolic pressure below 90 mmHg, or vasopressors needed to reach 90, with end-organ hypoperfusion) or persistent hypotension 233. In ICOPER, the 4.5% with massive PE had 90-day mortality of 52.4% vs 14.7% 238. ESC 2019: "Primary reperfusion treatment, in most cases systemic thrombolysis, is the treatment of choice", and its Table 10 lists rtPA as 100 mg over 2 h (quoted as published) 233.

Controversy

Thrombolysis below high risk

In 16 trials (2115 patients), thrombolysis lowered all-cause mortality (2.17% vs 3.89%) but raised major bleeding (9.24% vs 3.42%) and intracranial haemorrhage (1.46% vs 0.19%) 91. In PEITHO's normotensive intermediate-risk patients, tenecteplase cut death or collapse at 7 days (2.6% vs 5.6%) but raised stroke (2.4% vs 0.2%) 293. ESC 2019 does not recommend routine full-dose thrombolysis here 233.

Anaphylaxis: adrenaline as the guidelines and label print it

Anaphylactic shock is massive histamine-mediated vasodilation and maldistribution, a distributive shock 457. The World Allergy Organization (WAO) 2020 criteria call anaphylaxis highly likely with acute skin or mucosal involvement plus breathing trouble, low blood pressure, organ dysfunction or severe gut symptoms; or with acute low blood pressure, bronchospasm or throat involvement after exposure to a known or probable allergen, even without skin features 82. Criteria like these are "an aid to diagnosis", not a definition 82,408.

Minutes count

How fast it can turn

In UK fatal reactions, the median time to respiratory or cardiac arrest was 5 min for medicines, 15 min for venom and 30 min for food, and adrenaline had been given before the arrest in only 14% 372. In England and Wales (1992–2012), anaphylaxis admissions rose 615% while the fatality rate stayed at 0.047 per 100,000 a year 483.

A second wave

Biphasic reactions

Biphasic reactions come back after the first has resolved, without new exposure. In a meta-analysis (4114 patients) their median onset was 11 h (range 0.2–72 h), and they were more likely with low blood pressure at the start (OR 2.18) or an unknown trigger (OR 1.72) 253. RCUK 2021 puts them at about 5% of cases and links giving adrenaline more than 30 min after onset with more of them (OR 3.39) 120. Antihistamines and glucocorticoids do not reliably prevent them 437.

RCUK 2021

How long to watch

RCUK 2021 suggests risk-stratified observation: fast-track discharge 2 h after resolution in selected low-risk patients, at least 6 h for others, and at least 12 h after higher-risk reactions. One third of biphasic reactions in the European registry came more than 12 h after the first symptoms 120.

In plain wordsA severe allergic reaction can become life-threatening within minutes. An adrenaline auto-injector is used straight away, and then you still call for emergency help 135,372. Symptoms can come back hours later, so people are usually watched in hospital for a while after treatment 120,253.

Other shock states

Toxin-mediated

Toxic shock syndrome

Toxic shock syndrome is an acute, toxin-mediated illness caused by toxin-producing Staphylococcus aureus or group A streptococcus, whose toxins act as superantigens and set off a "cytokine avalanche" 250. Specific treatment includes source control and antibiotics that suppress toxin production, such as clindamycin or linezolid 250. Standl 2018 quotes an incidence of about 0.5 per 100,000 and mortality of 2–11% 457.

Early evidence

Anti-toxin add-ons

In 1079 invasive group A streptococcal infections, adding clindamycin went with in-hospital mortality of 6.5% vs 11.0% (aOR 0.44; observational) 35. Linezolid appeared non-inferior to clindamycin in a target-trial emulation (1095 patients) 36. In streptococcal toxic shock treated with clindamycin, IVIG went with mortality falling from 33.7% to 15.7% (1 RCT and 4 non-randomised studies) 344.

Necrotising infection

LRINEC cannot rule it out

The LRINEC score combines white count, haemoglobin, sodium, glucose, creatinine and CRP; a cut-off of 6 gave a PPV of 92.0% in derivation 522. In a meta-analysis (5982 patients), LRINEC ≥6 had a sensitivity of only 68.2%, and the authors conclude it "should not be used to rule-out" necrotising soft-tissue infection. Fever (46.0%) and hypotension (21.0%) were also insensitive; CT reached 88.5% sensitivity 141.

Spinal cord injury

Neurogenic shock

A distributive shock after spinal cord injury from an imbalance of sympathetic and parasympathetic control, classically low pressure with a slow heart rate 400,457. In TARN data, a systolic pressure below 100 mmHg with a heart rate below 80/min at arrival was seen in 19.3% of cervical, 7% of thoracic and 3% of lumbar cord injuries 179. Reported incidence varies widely with the definition, 29% after cervical injury in one series 400.

Adrenal crisis

When cortisol runs out

In 423 educated patients with chronic adrenal insufficiency followed for 2 years, there were 8.3 crises and 0.5 crisis-related deaths per 100 patient-years. Gastrointestinal infection, fever and emotional stress each triggered about 20% 182.

Hypothesis

Septic cardiomyopathy and mixed shock

Shock can combine processes, for example tension pneumothorax plus bleeding after trauma 85,457. In septic shock, global left-ventricular hypokinesia was found in 60% of 67 ventilated patients, usually reversible and not linked to a worse prognosis 493. Definitions compete and its effect on prognosis "remains to be demonstrated" 49; whether it is an adaptive response is a hypothesis 196.

Survival figures here describe large groups of people, not any one person. In SOAP II, dopamine went with higher 28-day mortality than noradrenaline (norepinephrine) in the 280 patients with cardiogenic shock (P=0.03) 112. Sepsis Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on sepsis (the body's harmful response to an infection), septic shock and the other types of shock, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people who survive sepsis, their families and caregivers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine or a care plan without your own care team. The qSOFA, SOFA, NEWS2, Phoenix and shock-index calculators are for learning, never for self-triage or for deciding whether to seek care.
