Part 1 · Understanding sepsis & shock

Infection to dysregulated host response

In sepsis the harm comes from the body's own response, not only from the germ. 447 Scrub through the cascade: sensors spot the invader, cytokines and complement sound the alarm, neutrophils throw out sticky traps, the response spreads through the body, and then, often at the same time, the immune system wears out.

Phase 1 · invasion

Germs multiply and shed fragments

Bacteria grow in the tissue and shed pieces of themselves. Gram-negative bacteria carry lipopolysaccharide (LPS, endotoxin) in their outer membrane; Gram-positive bacteria carry lipoteichoic acid and peptidoglycan. These are PAMPs, pathogen-associated molecular patterns. 200,359,425 Injured host cells release their own alarm molecules, DAMPs, such as mitochondrial DNA, histones and HMGB1. 505,525,534

In plain wordsGerms growing in the body shed tiny pieces of themselves. Damaged body cells also leak "danger" signals.
Exam hookPAMPs come from microbes; DAMPs from injured host cells. Both trigger the same sensors. 200

Current view. Pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory (suppressive) responses start together and vary from person to person. In survivors suppression resolves; in others it persists. Mouse and human data show both arms rising together, with the worst outcome when both are high. Early evidence 224,334,487

Sensors and signals

Pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs, molecules found on microbes) and damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs, molecules released from injured host cells) are detected by many sensors working in parallel: complement, toll-like receptors (TLRs), NOD-like and RIG-like receptors, mannose-binding lectin and scavenger receptors. At least ten human TLRs are known, and they often work in pairs. 200

The cytokine storm is real, but brief

Volunteers

A pulse of TNF

In 13 healthy men given intravenous E. coli endotoxin (4 ng/kg), plasma TNF peaked 90 to 180 minutes later at 240 ± 70 pg/mL, against 35 ± 5 pg/mL after saline: "a brief pulse". Ibuprofen did not stop the TNF rise but greatly reduced symptoms. 296

Animal model

Timing is everything

Baboons given anti-TNF antibody 2 hours before a lethal E. coli infusion were completely protected against shock, organ dysfunction and death. Given 1 hour before, it protected against shock but not organ failure. 482

Pneumonia cohort

Both arms of the response, together

In 1,886 people admitted with pneumonia (GenIMS), TNF, IL-6 and IL-10 were raised in 82%, peaked at presentation and stayed raised for over a week. The highest risk of death came with high pro-inflammatory IL-6 and high anti-inflammatory IL-10 (hazard ratio 20.5). 224

Early evidence

Not SIRS then CARS

In mouse sepsis, pro-inflammatory (IL-6, TNF, IL-1β) and anti-inflammatory mediators (soluble TNF receptors, IL-10, IL-1 receptor antagonist) rose at the same time in animals that died early. This challenges the older model of an inflammatory phase followed by an anti-inflammatory phase. 334

Neutrophils, NETs and immunothrombosis

Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) are fibres of chromatin and granule proteins released by activated neutrophils. They bind Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, degrade virulence factors and kill bacteria. 71 Platelet TLR4 senses TLR4 ligands in the blood and makes platelets bind to neutrophils, triggering NETs; plasma from people with severe sepsis did the same, and the NETs trapped bacteria mainly in liver sinusoids and lung capillaries. 97

The same defence harms the host. NETs bind TLRs and drive inflammation, and activated monocytes express tissue factor and phosphatidylserine, which start the clotting cascades: "thromboinflammation". 207 Sepsis produces a hypercoagulable state with microvascular clots, fibrin deposits, NETs and endothelial injury. 200 What this does to vessels and organs: Vessels, clotting & failing organs.

The second half: an exhausted immune system

Sepsis is now described as sustained excessive inflammation together with immune suppression that fails to return to balance. 487 As more patients survive the early phase, sepsis has become more of a chronic critical illness, with prolonged inflammation, immune suppression, organ injury and muscle wasting. 200

Cell death

Lymphocytes disappear

In 20 patients who died of sepsis, at least focal apoptosis was seen in 56.3% of spleens and 47.1% of colons, and 15 of 19 had marked lymphocytopenia; active caspase-3 staining was markedly higher than in non-septic patients. 198 Lymphocyte apoptosis is directly immunosuppressive, and persistent lymphopenia predicts death. 200 Severe persistent lymphopenia on day 4 was linked to more secondary infections. 124

Antigen presentation

HLA-DR falls

Antigen-presenting cells lose HLA-DR, the molecule they need to show antigens to T cells, and failure of monocytes to recover it predicts poor outcome. 200 In septic shock, monocyte HLA-DR below 30% on days 3–4 independently predicted death (adjusted OR 6.48). 303

Checkpoints

T cells switched off

T cells raise PD-1 and stromal cells raise PD-L1, expanding regulatory and anergic (unresponsive) T cells; myeloid-derived suppressor cells also increase. 200 Septic monocytes make less TNF, IL-1α, IL-6 and IL-12 when stimulated but keep releasing IL-10, resembling "endotoxin tolerance". 199

Reactivation

Old viruses wake up

Decreased interferon-γ production by natural killer cells often comes before cytomegalovirus reactivation in protracted sepsis. 199 Spleens from people who died of sepsis secreted generally less than 10% of control cytokine levels and showed extensive loss of CD4, CD8 and HLA-DR cells. 63

Hypothesis

PICS

Persistent inflammation, immunosuppression and catabolism syndrome is a later state with high CRP, neutrophilia and immature myeloid cells, thought to be driven by DAMPs from injured tissue such as mitochondrial DNA, histones and HMGB1. 200

Early evidence

Treat the phase, not the label

Biomarker-guided immunotherapy timed to the immune phase has been proposed. 199 Trials of precision immunotherapy are covered in Research frontier.

Why calming the storm did not work

More than 100 randomised trials have tested whether modulating the septic response improves survival. "With one short-lived exception" (drotrecogin alfa), none produced a new treatment. 277 Pick a trial to see what happened. Lessons

Why the trials failed

The response is complex and redundant, the same trigger can do good and harm, and trials enrolled unselected patients. Proposed fixes are large epidemiological studies and stratifying patients to target those likely to benefit. 20,277

Lessons

Animals are not patients

Animals in classic models die of shock within 72 hours, while many patients die later, with opportunistic infections and low monocyte HLA-DR. 119

Guideline

Endotoxin removal

The Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2021 "suggest[s] against using polymyxin B haemoperfusion" (weak recommendation, low-quality evidence). 133 Early evidence Tigris (2026), in 157 people with a narrower band of endotoxin activity, reported 28-day mortality of 39% vs 45% and a posterior probability of benefit of 95.3% in an open-label Bayesian design; the guideline predates it. 325

In plain wordsImmune cells carry sensors that spot pieces of germs, like a smoke alarm spotting smoke. In sepsis the alarm rings far too loud and spreads through the whole body, harming the body's own tissues. Strangely, the immune system can be overactive and worn out at the same time, and later some people's defences are so tired that new infections take hold more easily. Many medicines that calmed inflammation in animals did not help people with sepsis, which is why researchers now test treatments matched to each person's immune state. 63,199,200,277
