Organ support in intensive care

While antibiotics and source control deal with the infection, intensive care keeps failing organs going until they recover. Step through the organ support panel: lungs, oxygen, kidneys, blood, gut, brain and family, each with the trial that shaped today's practice.

Call nowSepsis is a medical emergency. If someone with an infection becomes very unwell (confused or very drowsy, breathing very fast, cold, mottled or bluish skin, passing little or no urine), call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department now and ask: could this be sepsis?

Sepsis is common in intensive care. In the ICON audit of 10,069 ICU patients, 29.5% had sepsis during their stay; ICU mortality among them was 25.8% and hospital mortality 35.3%, varying from 19.3% in Oceania to 47.2% in Africa (hospital). [[Sakr2018ICON]] On one day in 2017 (EPIC III, 15,202 patients in 88 countries), 54% of ICU patients had suspected or proven infection and 70% were on an antibiotic. [[Vincent2020EPICIII]] These are population figures: they cannot predict what will happen to one person.

Established

Oxygen: enough, not more

SSC 2021 found insufficient evidence to recommend conservative oxygen targets (generally PaO2 55–70 mmHg or SpO2 88–92%). [[Evans2021SSC]] Three trials since then found no mortality difference: ICU-ROX (180-day 35.7% vs 34.5%), HOT-ICU (PaO2 60 vs 90 mmHg, 42.9% vs 42.4%) and UK-ROX, in 16,500 ventilated adults, where an SpO2 target of 90% cut oxygen exposure by 29% with 90-day mortality 35.4% vs 34.9%. [[Mackle2020ICUROX,Schjorring2021HOTICU,Martin2025UKROX]]

Early evidence

ECMO: a heart-lung machine

SSC 2021 suggests veno-venous ECMO for sepsis-induced severe ARDS when conventional ventilation fails, in experienced centres (weak, low quality). [[Evans2021SSC]] In EOLIA (249 patients), 60-day mortality was 35% with early ECMO vs 46% with conventional care (RR 0.76, 95% CI 0.55–1.04; P = 0.09), and 28% of controls crossed over to ECMO. [[Combes2018EOLIA]] For septic cardiomyopathy, a retrospective cohort reported 90-day survival of 60% with VA-ECMO vs 25% in controls; unmeasured confounding cannot be excluded. [[Brechot2020VAECMO]]

Kidney support: when to start dialysis

KDIGO defines acute kidney injury (AKI) as a creatinine rise of ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48 h, creatinine ≥1.5 times baseline within 7 days, or urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/hour for 6 hours. [[Kellum2013KDIGO]] Starting kidney replacement therapy (dialysis or haemofiltration) early, before there is an urgent reason, has not saved lives in three trials. Established SSC 2021 suggests against it for AKI without a definitive indication, and accepts continuous or intermittent methods when it is needed (weak). [[Evans2021SSC]] See the hourly urine chart in nursing care.

Sedation, delirium and getting moving

Established

Wake up and breathe

In the ABC trial (336 ventilated patients), pairing a daily sedation interruption with a spontaneous breathing trial gave more days breathing unassisted (14.7 vs 11.6), a shorter ICU stay (median 9.1 vs 12.9 days) and lower one-year mortality (HR 0.68; number needed to treat 7.4). [[Girard2008ABC]]

Sedatives

Which sedative?

SPICE III (4,000): early dexmedetomidine gave the same 90-day mortality as usual care (29.1% vs 29.1%), with more slow heart rate and low blood pressure. [[Shehabi2019SPICE3]] MENDS2 (422 with sepsis): dexmedetomidine vs propofol, the same days alive without delirium or coma (10.7 vs 10.8) and 90-day mortality (38% vs 39%). [[Hughes2021MENDS2]]

Early evidence

The ABCDEF bundle

Early physical and occupational therapy during sedation breaks raised return to independent function at discharge (59% vs 35%) and shortened delirium (median 2 vs 4 days). [[Schweickert2009Mobility]] In the ICU Liberation Collaborative (15,226 adults, observational), complete ABCDEF bundle performance went with lower hospital death within 7 days (aHR 0.32), less delirium, fewer restraints and fewer ICU readmissions. [[Pun2019ABCDEF]] The SCCM PADIS guideline (pain, agitation, delirium, immobility, sleep) issued 37 recommendations, with ICU survivors on the panel. [[Devlin2018PADIS]]

Goals of care and families

SSC 2021 recommends discussing goals of care and prognosis with patients and families (Best Practice Statement), early (within 72 h) rather than late (weak recommendation). It recommends integrating palliative care principles where appropriate, but suggests against routine formal palliative care consultation for everyone, in favour of clinician judgement. [[Evans2021SSC]] For families of patients dying in 22 French ICUs, a proactive end-of-life conference plus a bereavement brochure lowered PTSD-related symptoms at 90 days (45% vs 69%), anxiety and depression; families spent more of the longer conference talking (14 vs 5 minutes). [[Lautrette2007Brochure]] SSC 2021 also suggests written and verbal sepsis education before discharge, noting that almost 40% of survivors are rehospitalised within 3 months. [[Evans2021SSC]] Recovery is covered in after sepsis.

In plain words

For patients and familiesIn intensive care, a breathing machine gives small, gentle breaths to protect the lungs, and some people are turned onto their front to help oxygen get in. [[ARDSNet2000,Guerin2013PROSEVA]] Kidneys often recover on their own, so dialysis is not started early unless there is a clear need. [[Gaudry2016AKIKI,Bagshaw2020STARRT]] Staff keep sedation light, wake people each day and get them moving as early as they can, because this helps people walk and look after themselves sooner. [[Girard2008ABC,Schweickert2009Mobility]] Expect regular, honest talks with the team about what is happening and what matters most to your loved one. [[Evans2021SSC,Lautrette2007Brochure]] The numbers here come from studies of large groups and cannot tell anyone what will happen to one person. [[Sakr2018ICON]]
