Definitions & scores: SIRS to Sepsis-3

What counts as sepsis has been redefined three times in 25 years. The 1991 definition looked for inflammation (SIRS); the 2016 definition, Sepsis-3, looks for organ damage, measured with the SOFA score. This module walks through the timeline and lets you try the qSOFA and SOFA scores as teaching tools.

"Sepsis should be defined as life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection." Sepsis-3, 2016 [[Singer2016Sepsis3]]

SIRS, Sepsis-1 and Sepsis-2

The ACCP/SCCM Consensus Conference met in Northbrook in August 1991 and published in 1992. It introduced the systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) and definitions of sepsis, severe sepsis, septic shock and multiple organ dysfunction syndrome. Sepsis meant infection plus 2 or more SIRS criteria; severe sepsis added organ dysfunction; septic shock was sepsis-induced hypotension despite fluids. [[Bone1992SIRS,Kaukonen2015]] The 2001 International Sepsis Definitions Conference (29 participants, published 2003) found that "apart from expanding the list of signs and symptoms of sepsis… no evidence exists to support a change to the definitions". It proposed PIRO (predisposition, infection, response, organ dysfunction) as "a hypothesis-generating model for future research". Hypothesis [[Levy2003Sepsis2]]

The weakness of SIRS showed up in a 2015 study of 1,171,797 patients in 172 Australian and New Zealand ICUs. Among 109,663 people with infection and organ failure, 12.1% were SIRS-negative: "one in eight". Each additional SIRS criterion raised the odds of death steadily (OR 1.13 per criterion), with no step at 2 criteria, the cut-off the definition relied on. [[Kaukonen2015]]

A 19-member SCCM/ESICM task force wrote Sepsis-3, and 31 societies endorsed it. It criticised the earlier definitions for "an excessive focus on inflammation", the "misleading model" of a continuum through severe sepsis to shock, and the "inadequate specificity and sensitivity" of SIRS. [[Singer2016Sepsis3]] The septic shock criteria came from a systematic review (44 studies, 166,479 patients, crude mortality 46.5%) and from the SSC database (28,150 patients), where needing vasopressors for a MAP of 65 mmHg or more with a lactate above 2 mmol/L after fluids carried the highest mortality of six combinations, 42.3%. These are population statistics. [[ShankarHari2016Shock]] Sepsis-3 is an adult definition; the paediatric task force noted that it "excluded children". [[Schlapbach2024Phoenix]]

Try it: qSOFA

A teaching tool, not for self-triageqSOFA is shown as published in 2016 so you can see how it works. It is not a test for sepsis and must never be used to decide whether someone needs medical care. The Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2021 recommends against using it as a single screening tool. Anyone who seems very unwell with an infection needs urgent medical help, whatever any score says. [[Evans2021SSC]]

SIRS or qSOFA? Sensitivity against specificity

A screening tool has to catch the people who will become very ill (sensitivity) without flagging everyone else (specificity). SIRS and qSOFA sit at opposite ends. Pick a tool to see the pooled results of a 38-study meta-analysis (385,333 patients) for predicting death. [[Fernando2018]]

Derivation

Why qSOFA was chosen

In 148,907 encounters with suspected infection at 12 Pennsylvania hospitals, outside the ICU qSOFA predicted in-hospital death with AUROC 0.81, against 0.79 for SOFA and 0.76 for SIRS. A qSOFA of 2 or more meant a 3- to 14-fold increase in mortality across baseline-risk deciles. Inside the ICU, SOFA did better (0.74 vs 0.66). [[Seymour2016qSOFA]]

Emergency departments

Freund 2017

In 879 patients in 30 emergency departments, in-hospital mortality was 3% with qSOFA below 2 and 24% with qSOFA 2 or more. qSOFA's AUROC was 0.80, against 0.65 for SIRS. [[Freund2017]]

Intensive care

Raith 2017

In 184,875 adults in 182 ICUs, crude AUROC for in-hospital death was 0.753 for a SOFA rise of 2 or more, 0.607 for qSOFA and 0.589 for SIRS. 90.1% met the SOFA criterion; only 54.4% had qSOFA 2 or more. [[Raith2017]]

Meta-analyses

Low sensitivity outside the ICU

Song 2018 (146,551 patients outside the ICU) found pooled sensitivity 0.51 for qSOFA against 0.86 for SIRS, and specificity 0.83 against 0.29. Serafim 2018 found SIRS better for diagnosing sepsis and qSOFA slightly better for predicting death. [[Song2018qSOFA,Serafim2018]]

Established

Where the guideline landed

SSC 2021: "We recommend against using qSOFA compared to SIRS, NEWS, or MEWS as a single screening tool for sepsis or septic shock" (strong recommendation, moderate-quality evidence). A positive qSOFA "should alert the clinician to the possibility of sepsis in all resource settings". [[Evans2021SSC]]

Try it: the SOFA score

SOFA (the Sepsis-related Organ Failure Assessment, as first named) was proposed in 1996 by an ESICM working group to describe organ dysfunction. It scores six organ systems from 0 to 4, so the total runs from 0 to 24. Conventions: the worst value of each sub-score in each 24-hour period is used, parameters that match no row score zero, and delta SOFA is the change from a defined baseline. [[Vincent1996SOFA,Lambden2019SOFA]]

A teaching tool, not for clinical decisionsThe rows below are the published SOFA points, so you can see which findings move the score. Real SOFA scoring uses the worst values over 24 hours, measured by a clinical team. This page must never be used to judge how ill someone is, their prognosis or their treatment. [[Lambden2019SOFA]]

SOFA over time

Trend

Direction matters

In 1,449 patients in 40 ICUs, among those who stayed at least a week, total SOFA rose in 44% of non-survivors but only 20% of survivors. [[Vincent1998SOFA]]

Serial scores

Ferreira 2001

In 352 ICU patients, over the first 96 hours mortality was at least 50% when SOFA increased, 27–35% when it was unchanged and below 27% when it decreased. An initial or highest score above 11 corresponded to mortality above 80%. These are population statistics. [[Ferreira2001SOFA]]

Early evidence

SOFA-2 (2025)

Built by modified Delphi and 3.34 million ICU encounters in 9 countries, SOFA-2 keeps six systems (brain, respiratory, cardiovascular, liver, kidney, haemostasis) with revised thresholds. AUROC 0.79 against 0.77 for the original. It leaves out gut and immune dysfunction for "insufficient data and lack of content validity". [[Ranzani2025SOFA2]]

Early evidence

What changed in SOFA-2

SpO₂/FiO₂ as an alternative to PaO₂/FiO₂; high-flow oxygen and CPAP count as respiratory support and ECMO is included; more vasopressors with equivalent doses plus mechanical circulatory support; kidney replacement therapy in the kidney score; delirium treatment in the brain score. Sepsis-3 is still defined with the original SOFA, and a "phased dual-scoring approach" has been suggested. [[Lobo2025SOFA2]]
