Part 2 · Who is at risk & recognising it

Recognising sepsis

Sepsis often starts with signs that look like any other infection. The clues are a person who is suddenly much more unwell than expected, and observations (breathing, pulse, blood pressure, temperature, alertness) that keep getting worse. This module shows the warning signs in plain words, then how hospitals turn the numbers into a score such as NEWS2.

Call nowSepsis is a medical emergency. If someone with an infection becomes very unwell (confused or very drowsy, breathing very fast, cold, mottled or bluish skin, passing little or no urine), call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department now and ask: could this be sepsis?

The warning signs, one at a time

One public message spells the signs as SEPSIS. It was written by the World Sepsis Day committee to raise awareness and, as its own evaluators point out, it "has not been validated" as a test. Why sepsis is easy to miss

Vague at first

No single sign is reliable

In the Thai cohort, 14.9% of people with sepsis had none of the SEPSIS signs, so a "negative" mnemonic does not rule sepsis out. Vague presenting symptoms were independently linked with in-hospital death (adjusted OR 2.17). [[Wattanapaiboon2020SEPSIS]]

Who it happens to

Often people already in contact with care

A CDC review of 246 adults and 79 children with severe sepsis or septic shock in four New York hospitals found that 72% had a health-care factor in the previous 30 days, or a chronic condition needing frequent care. Pneumonia was the commonest infection behind it. [[Novosad2016MMWR]]

Children

Early signs come before the rash

In 448 UK children with meningococcal disease, leg pain, cold hands and feet and abnormal skin colour first appeared at a median of 8 hours, well before the classic rash and the median hospital admission at 19 hours. [[Thompson2006Meningococcal]]

Awareness

Many parents would not spot it

In an Australian poll of 3,352 parents (2023), 61.6% had heard the word sepsis. Only 27.8% of those parents thought they could recognise it in their child. [[Peters2023Awareness]]

Watching the numbers: how a score rises

In hospital, nurses record six observations again and again: breathing rate, oxygen saturation (and whether oxygen is being given), blood pressure, pulse, level of consciousness and temperature. NEWS2, the National Early Warning Score used across the UK NHS, gives each one 0 to 3 points and adds them up. How the scores compare

Several bedside tools compete for the same job: spotting the infected patient who is about to get much worse. qSOFA (3 items) and SIRS (4 items) come from the sepsis definitions, explained in . The Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2021 guideline puts it plainly: "We recommend against using qSOFA compared to SIRS, NEWS, or MEWS as a single screening tool for sepsis or septic shock" (strong recommendation, moderate-quality evidence). It adds that "neither SIRS nor qSOFA are ideal screening tools". [[Evans2021SSC]]

Screening systems, alerts and the evidence

Established

Screening as part of a programme

SSC 2021 recommends that hospitals and health systems use a performance improvement programme including "sepsis screening for acutely ill, high-risk patients" (strong recommendation). A meta-analysis of 50 observational studies linked such programmes with lower mortality (OR 0.66); a pooled analysis of three randomised trials of active screening showed no clear mortality benefit (RR 0.90, 95% CI 0.51–1.58). [[Evans2021SSC]]

Early evidence

NEWS across a whole region

In the West of England (2015–2019), using NEWS across community, primary and secondary care was followed by falling suspected-sepsis mortality not seen in the rest of England. This was a quality-improvement study, not a randomised trial. [[Pullyblank2020]]

Established

Not every alert works

A widely used proprietary sepsis model, validated externally in 38,455 hospitalisations, had an AUROC of 0.63. It missed 67% of sepsis cases while alerting on 18% of all patients. [[Wong2021Epic]]

Early evidence

Alerts that are acted on

With the TREWS machine-learning alert (590,736 patients, 5 hospitals), confirmation of the alert by a clinician within 3 hours was associated with an 18.7% relative reduction in in-hospital mortality. The study was a prospective cohort, not randomised. [[Adams2022TREWS]]

Early evidence

NEWS2 plus a blood test

The PRONTO trial added procalcitonin to NEWS2 in emergency departments in England and Wales. It did not change intravenous antibiotic starts at 3 hours (48.4% vs 48.2%); 28-day mortality was lower with the procalcitonin-guided algorithm (13.6% vs 16.6%), an effect the authors say needs further research. [[Todd2026PRONTO]]

Shock index

Pulse divided by blood pressure

Shock index is heart rate ÷ systolic blood pressure. In 2,524 emergency patients with suspected infection, those with a shock index of 0.7 or more were about three times as likely to have a lactate of 4.0 mmol/L or more (15.8% vs 4.9%); 1.0 or more was the most specific cut-off. A single-centre pilot. [[Berger2013SI]]

In hospital: NEWS2 in the UK guidance

NICE's 2024 update of its sepsis guidance brought NEWS2 in for assessing people with suspected sepsis who are aged 16 or over, are not pregnant or recently pregnant, and are in an acute hospital, an acute mental health setting or an ambulance. [[Gildea2024NICE]] The score is a trend tool: Churpek and colleagues found that patients who went on to die or need intensive care first met 2 or more SIRS criteria a median of 17 hours before that outcome, and 2 or more qSOFA criteria only 5 hours before. Repeated observations are what reveal the slope. [[Churpek2017]] A lactate blood test often sits beside the score: SSC 2021 suggests measuring blood lactate in adults suspected of having sepsis (weak recommendation, low-quality evidence), while noting that lactate "alone is neither sensitive nor specific enough to rule-in or rule-out the diagnosis". Trust your sense that something is wrongYou know the person best. Say clearly that you think they are much more unwell than usual, and ask: could this be sepsis? A person can have sepsis without any one of these signs, and the mnemonic is an awareness message, not a test.
