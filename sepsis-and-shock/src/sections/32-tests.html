Part 3 · Definitions & tests

Lactate, cultures, procalcitonin & tests

No single blood test diagnoses sepsis. Lactate shows how stressed the body's cells are, blood cultures try to catch the germ, and procalcitonin helps decide when antibiotics can stop. Follow why lactate rises, how cultures are taken and read, and what the trials found, all quoted as published.

Call nowSepsis is a medical emergency. If someone with an infection becomes very unwell (confused or very drowsy, breathing very fast, cold, mottled or bluish skin, passing little or no urine), call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department now and ask: could this be sepsis?

Try it: what a lactate number means, as published

A teaching tool, not a diagnosisA lactate result is read by the clinical team together with the person, their history and their other results. SSC 2021 says lactate "should be interpreted considering the clinical context and other causes of elevated lactate". Never use this to decide whether to seek care: if someone with an infection seems very unwell, get emergency help now. [[Evans2021SSC]]

Steering treatment by lactate or by capillary refill

SSC 2021 suggests guiding resuscitation to decrease serum lactate when it is raised, rather than not using lactate (weak recommendation, low-quality evidence). It also suggests using capillary refill time (how fast colour returns after pressing a fingertip) to guide resuscitation in septic shock "as an adjunct to other measures of perfusion" (weak, low quality). [[Evans2021SSC]]

Blood cultures: catching the germ

Schematic bottles and dots. Percentages, likelihood ratios and hours as published. [[Lee2007Cultures,Cheng2019Cultures,Sautter2024Contamination,Coburn2012Bacteremia,Banerjee2015rmPCR]]

Guideline

Cultures first, if quick

SSC 2021 restates its 2016 best-practice statement: routine cultures, including blood, are taken before antimicrobials "if it results in no substantial delay in the start of antimicrobials (i.e. < 45 min)". SMFM 2023 says the same for pregnancy, as long as antibiotics are not substantially delayed. [[Evans2021SSC,SMFM2023Sepsis]]

Source

Cultures from the likely source

The IDSA/ASM 2024 microbiology guide lists the best specimens, volumes and transport for blood, respiratory, urine, cerebrospinal fluid, intra-abdominal and skin samples. After antibiotics, adding other cultures found a pathogen in 67.6% of people whose earlier blood cultures were positive. [[Miller2024IDSAMicro,Cheng2019Cultures]]

Imaging

Looking for a source to drain

SSC 2021 recommends "rapidly identifying or excluding a specific anatomical diagnosis of infection that requires emergent source control", such as an abscess, a perforated or ischaemic bowel, cholangitis, an obstructed kidney or necrotising soft tissue infection. In practice this usually means imaging. [[Evans2021SSC]]

False alarms

Why contamination matters

A false-positive culture can lead to unnecessary antibiotics, higher costs and a delay in finding the true cause. Chlorhexidine skin preparation, diversion devices, sterile technique, phlebotomy teams and education together cut contamination by about 40–60%. [[Sautter2024Contamination,Doern2019Contamination]]

Procalcitonin: when can antibiotics stop?

Procalcitonin (PCT) is a blood protein that SSC 2021 describes as "undetectable in healthy states, but rises rapidly in response to pro-inflammatory stimuli, especially bacterial infections". In a meta-analysis of 30 studies (3,244 critically ill patients) it separated sepsis from non-infectious inflammation with sensitivity 0.77, specificity 0.79 and AUROC 0.85, though the studies varied a great deal (I² = 96%). Against CRP in hospitalised patients, PCT had higher sensitivity (88% vs 75%) and specificity (81% vs 67%). [[Evans2021SSC,Wacker2013PCT,Simon2004PCTCRP]]

SSC 2021 "suggest[s] against using procalcitonin plus clinical evaluation to decide when to start antimicrobials" (weak, very low quality). Where source control is adequate and the right duration is unclear, it suggests "using procalcitonin AND clinical evaluation to decide when to discontinue antimicrobials" (weak, low quality). [[Evans2021SSC]]

An illustrative patient, not trial data. The stop rule is SAPS's as published: a fall of 80% or more from peak, or a level of 0.5 μg/L or lower. [[deJong2016SAPS]]

CRP, white count and fever

White count

A weak test on its own

Isolated leukocytosis (a raised white cell count) has a likelihood ratio below 1.7 for bacteraemia, so on its own it says little about whether germs are in the blood. [[Coburn2012Bacteremia]]

Fever

Chills say more than a temperature

A temperature of 38 °C or more had a likelihood ratio of only 1.9 for bacteraemia. Shaking chills were more informative (likelihood ratio 4.7). [[Coburn2012Bacteremia]]

CRP

Less specific than PCT

CRP (C-reactive protein) was less sensitive and less specific than PCT for bacterial infection in hospitalised patients, and in ADAPT-Sepsis a CRP-guided stopping protocol did not shorten antibiotic courses (difference 0.09 days, −0.60 to 0.79). [[Simon2004PCTCRP,Dark2025ADAPT]]

Rapid molecular tests

Established

Hours, not a day

In a randomised trial of 617 patients with positive blood culture bottles, rapid multiplex PCR named the organism 1.3 h after the Gram stain, against 22.3 h with standard processing. Fewer contaminants were treated (25% control, 11% PCR, 8% PCR with stewardship), but mortality, length of stay and cost did not differ. [[Banerjee2015rmPCR]]

Early evidence

Faster helps with stewardship

Across 31 studies (5,920 patients with bloodstream infection), molecular rapid testing was linked to lower mortality (OR 0.66, 0.54–0.80; NNT 20). The benefit was significant only with an antimicrobial stewardship programme (OR 0.64), not without one (OR 0.72, 0.46–1.12). Time to effective therapy fell by 5.03 h. [[Timbrook2017mRDT]]

Early evidence

Testing whole blood for yeast

T2 magnetic resonance testing for candidaemia directly from whole blood (1,801 patients) was 91.1% sensitive and 99.4% specific per assay, with species named in a mean 4.4 h against 2–5 days for culture. [[Mylonakis2015T2]]

Open questions

Debated

Lactate or capillary refill?

ANDROMEDA-SHOCK did not reach significance on mortality, and ANDROMEDA-SHOCK-2's win-ratio benefit for a personalised capillary refill protocol came from less vital support rather than fewer deaths. Early evidence favours capillary refill as an adjunct. [[Hernandez2019ANDROMEDA,Hernandez2025ANDROMEDA2]]

Debated

PCT: safety or survival?

SAPS and the ICU meta-analysis reported lower mortality; PRORATA and ADAPT-Sepsis showed noninferior mortality with shorter courses; ProACT showed no fall in antibiotic use in US EDs. PCT safely shortens ICU courses; a mortality benefit is uncertain. [[deJong2016SAPS,Wirz2018PCTICU,Bouadma2010PRORATA,Dark2025ADAPT,Huang2018ProACT]]

Hypothesis

Is high lactate adaptive?

A 2014 review suggested sepsis-associated hyperlactataemia may even be an adaptive survival response, while remaining a strong independent predictor of death. [[GarciaAlvarez2014]]
