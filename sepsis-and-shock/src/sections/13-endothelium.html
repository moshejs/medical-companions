Part 1 · Understanding sepsis & shock

Vessels, clotting & failing organs

Sepsis affects almost every job the inner lining of the blood vessels does: vessel tone, the barrier, inflammation and clotting. Endothelial dysfunction is thought to be the key step from sepsis to organ failure. [[Ince2016Endothelium]] Scrub from a healthy lining to leak, microclots, stalled capillaries and failing organs, and back to recovery.

Schematic. The capillary bed shows the published direction of change (perfused small vessels 48% in severe sepsis vs 90% in volunteers) with five capillaries, not to scale; the lactate tube carries no numbers. [[DeBacker2002]] The order of organs is drawn, not measured.

The glycocalyx and the leak

Structure

A gel on the inside of every vessel

The glycocalyx is about 0.5–5.0 μm thick in humans, made of proteoglycans (syndecan-1 is the most studied), glycosaminoglycans (over half heparan sulfate) and plasma proteins. It controls permeability and tone, prevents microvascular thrombosis and regulates white-cell adhesion. [[Uchimido2019]]

Shedding

Enzymes strip it off

Metalloproteinases, heparanase and hyaluronidase, activated by reactive oxygen species, TNF-α and IL-1β, shed the glycocalyx, causing leaky vessels, unregulated vasodilation, microthrombi and more leukocyte adhesion. In volunteers given low-dose endotoxin, sublingual glycocalyx thickness fell from 0.60 to 0.30 μm. [[Uchimido2019]]

Marker

Syndecan-1 in the blood

In a meta-analysis of 2,318 patients, baseline syndecan-1 (a shed glycocalyx protein) was higher in people who died (SMD 0.53), in septic shock (SMD 0.81) and in acute kidney injury (SMD 0.48). [[Sun2022SDC1]]

Hypothesis

Can fluids make it worse?

Over-aggressive fluid therapy may worsen glycocalyx degradation, while plasma and albumin may lessen it. These effects are not yet well understood. [[Uchimido2019]] The fluid trials are in Fluids.

Pro-inflammatory proteases make endothelial cells internalise VE-cadherin and lose their tight junctions. Leaky capillaries lose large amounts of protein and plasma into the tissues, causing oedema. [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]] At the same time, diffuse vasodilation throughout the microcirculation causes distributive shock, in which blood pressure may not recover with fluids alone. [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]]

Vasodilatory shock (vasoplegia, failure of the vessels to constrict) is classically attributed to nitric oxide, activation of ATP-sensitive potassium channels in vascular smooth muscle and relative vasopressin deficiency. Established [[Landry2001]] Upregulation of inducible nitric oxide synthase may link inflammation to cardiovascular collapse. [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]] Yet blocking nitric oxide does not help: the non-selective inhibitor 546C88 raised blood pressure but increased mortality (59% vs 49%). [[Lopez2004NOS]] How pressure, flow and tone fit together: Shock and haemodynamics.

When the smallest vessels stop flowing

Under the tongue

Half the small vessels

Filming the tiny vessels under the tongue in 50 people with severe sepsis, perfused small vessels (< 20 μm) were 48% vs 90% in healthy volunteers, and changes were worse in people who died. Topical acetylcholine fully reversed them. [[DeBacker2002]]

Over time

Survivors recover their flow

In 46 people with septic shock filmed daily, perfused small vessels were similar at onset (65.0% vs 58.4%) and improved only in survivors. People who later died of multiple organ failure still had fewer perfused vessels after shock resolved (57.4% vs 79.3%), despite similar blood pressure. [[Sakr2004]]

Hypothesis

Cells that cannot use oxygen

Cytopathic hypoxia is reduced oxygen use from an acquired defect in cellular respiration rather than poor delivery, perhaps through nitric oxide, peroxynitrite and NAD+ depletion. [[Fink2002]] In muscle biopsies from 28 people with sepsis, ATP was lower in those who died, and complex I activity fell as noradrenaline needs rose. [[Brealey2002]]

Hypothesis

Kidney injury without low flow

A "unified theory" proposes that inflammation, microvascular dysfunction and a mostly adaptive, mitochondria-driven tubular-cell response explain septic acute kidney injury, which can occur with normal or even increased renal blood flow. [[Gomez2014]]

Lactate is not only a sign of low oxygen. Lactate above 2 mmol/L is part of the Sepsis-3 septic-shock criteria. [[Singer2016Sepsis3]] But in 14 people with septic shock, muscle lactate exceeded arterial lactate by a mean 1.98 mmol/L, and ouabain, which blocks the Na⁺/K⁺ pump, stopped that excess production; the lactate-to-pyruvate ratio was unchanged. [[Levy2005Lactate]] This fits the idea that adrenaline-driven aerobic glycolysis in muscle, not only hypoperfusion, raises lactate. [[James1999Lactate]] A 2014 review concluded that sepsis-associated hyperlactataemia is better explained by stress-driven glycolysis than by tissue hypoxia alone, and may even be adaptive, yet it remains a strong independent predictor of death. [[GarciaAlvarez2014]] The lactate test itself: Lactate, cultures, procalcitonin & tests.

Clotting: from sepsis-induced coagulopathy to DIC

Severe sepsis is almost always accompanied by systemic activation of coagulation. Tissue factor on activated monocytes and endothelial cells starts thrombin generation and tissue factor pathway inhibitor does not keep up; the protein C system is impaired by cytokines; and clot breakdown is blocked, mainly by raised plasminogen activator inhibitor-1. More fibrin and less clearance means microvascular clots, tissue ischaemia and organ dysfunction. [[Levi2017Coag]] Antithrombin, activated protein C and endothelial heparan sulfate all fall. [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]] Because clotting factors are used up, the same patient can have clots and bleeding. [[Levi2017Coag]]

Two published scores describe this. The ISTH overt DIC score (2001) captures late, decompensated disease; sepsis-induced coagulopathy (SIC), adopted by the ISTH in 2019, aims to detect an earlier, compensated stage. [[Taylor2001DIC,Iba2017SIC,Iba2019SIC,Iba2023DIC]] Antithrombin activity is reduced in SIC and DIC but usually maintained in thrombotic microangiopathies (TTP, aHUS), which helps tell them apart. [[Iba2023DIC]]

Rare, severe

Purpura fulminans

Haemorrhagic skin infarction from DIC and clots in the small skin vessels, linked to depletion of protein C. Meningococcal sepsis is the commonest cause, then pneumococcal sepsis in adults. Mortality is over 50%, and amputation or skin grafting is often needed. [[Betrosian2006]]

Trials

Anticoagulants have not helped

High-dose antithrombin (38.9% vs 38.7% mortality, more bleeding with heparin), recombinant thrombomodulin (26.8% vs 29.4%, P = .32) and activated protein C all failed in broad sepsis populations, partly, reviewers suggest, because many patients were not coagulopathic. No anticoagulant is established for sepsis-associated coagulopathy outside trials. [[Warren2001KyberSept,Vincent2019SCARLET,Ranieri2012PROWESSSHOCK,Iba2023DIC]]

Platelets

Low platelets, more bleeding

In 329 ICU patients, 41.3% had platelets below 150 × 10⁹/L (crude OR 5.0 for ICU death). Bleeding occurred in 4.1% without thrombocytopenia, 21.4% at 101–149 and 52.6% below 100 × 10⁹/L; a fall to half the admission count or less carried OR 6.0 for death. [[Vanderschueren2000]]

Organ by organ

SOFA scores six organ systems, each from 0 (no dysfunction) to 4 (failure), for a total of 0–24. [[Vincent1996SOFA,GiamarellosBourboulis2026ImmunoSep]] In the first multicentre SOFA study, total SOFA rose during the stay in 44% of non-survivors vs 20% of survivors. [[Vincent1998SOFA]] Sepsis-3 counts an acute rise of 2 points or more as organ dysfunction. [[Singer2016Sepsis3]] Mechanisms include immune suppression, mitochondrial dysfunction, altered cell death, organ-to-organ crosstalk and microcirculatory failure. [[Lelubre2018]]

In plain wordsThe inside of blood vessels has a thin, gel-like lining. Sepsis strips it away, so vessels leak fluid into the tissues and swelling builds up. Blood can clot in small vessels while clotting factors are used up, which can cause both clots and bleeding. The tiniest vessels can stop flowing well even when blood pressure looks normal, and organs such as the lungs, kidneys and brain start to struggle. In people who recover, flow improves and the heart usually recovers within about 10 days. Confusion or drowsiness during an infection needs urgent medical attention. [[Uchimido2019,Levi2017Coag,Sakr2004,Parker1984,Gofton2012]]

Call nowSepsis is a medical emergency. If someone with an infection becomes very unwell (confused or very drowsy, breathing very fast, cold, mottled or bluish skin, passing little or no urine), call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department now and ask: could this be sepsis?
