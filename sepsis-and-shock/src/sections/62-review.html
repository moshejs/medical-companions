Cards tagged memory aid are short phrases written for this guide to hold a published fact in mind. They are not official groupings from the Surviving Sepsis Campaign, Sepsis-3 or any guideline, and none replaces the source. The card tagged public message is the World Sepsis Day "SEPSIS" mnemonic, which its source says has not been validated. Doses, volumes, targets and timings are quoted as published, never as instructions.

Mnemonic in motion: the Hour-1 bundle

The Surviving Sepsis Campaign's 2018 update introduced an "Hour-1 bundle" of five elements meant to be started within the first hour [[Levy2018Bundle,Wang2021Hour1]]. As a memory aid, think measure, culture, treat, fill, squeeze: lactate, blood cultures, broad-spectrum antibiotics, crystalloid and vasopressors. Play the animation to watch the clock run while each element starts, with the evidence behind it. More in .

The elements are quoted as published in the 2018 bundle; the order and the times on the clock are a teaching sketch, not a protocol. A teaching tool, never instructions for care. [[Levy2018Bundle,Wang2021Hour1,Evans2021SSC]]

These cards are for learning, not for deciding whether someone is ill. Sepsis is a medical emergency. If someone with an infection becomes very unwell (confused or very drowsy, breathing very fast, cold, mottled or bluish skin, passing little or no urine), call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department now and ask: could this be sepsis?

Sources

Every reference behind the Sepsis Companion: journal articles, randomised trials, systematic reviews and meta-analyses, Surviving Sepsis Campaign and other guidance, drug labels and trial registrations. The small numbers throughout the app point here; filter by research area or search.

Eight research passes ran in parallel, one per area: epidemiology, risk factors and history; immunology, the endothelium, clotting, organ dysfunction and the research frontier; haemodynamics and the shock types; recognition, early warning scores and definitions; tests, biomarkers, the first hour and maternal sepsis; fluids, vasopressors, steroids and blood-pressure targets; source control, stewardship, intensive care and nursing; and life after sepsis, families and prevention. Before inclusion each source was checked, where possible, against PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, ClinicalTrials.gov or openFDA, and the green tag on an entry names the service that confirmed it. Works found by more than one pass appear once, with any extra keys listed beside them.

Entries marked %t:sources.check% are pages with no PMID or DOI, or works that could not be confirmed. They are not cited for any fact in this guide.
