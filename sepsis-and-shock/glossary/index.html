An animated, cited guide for students, people who survive sepsis, and their families and caregivers. Educational only, not medical advice. Your place is saved on this device.

Not medical adviceThe Sepsis Companion is an educational synthesis of published literature for study and understanding. It does not replace a doctor, nurse, pharmacist, paramedic or other clinician. The qSOFA, SOFA, NEWS2, Phoenix and shock-index calculators are teaching aids, never for self-triage or for deciding whether to seek care, and antibiotic and vasopressor doses, fluid volumes, MAP and lactate targets and guideline statements are quoted as published, never as instructions. Mortality figures are population statistics, not predictions for one person. Sepsis is a medical emergency. If someone with an infection becomes very unwell (confused or very drowsy, breathing very fast, cold, mottled or bluish skin, passing little or no urine), call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department now and ask: could this be sepsis?

98 of 98 terms

Acute kidney injury (AKI)A sudden fall in kidney function. By KDIGO, urine below 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 hours is one criterion 225. Septic shock was the commonest contributor to ICU acute renal failure (47.5%) 485.
Adrenaline (epinephrine)A hormone and medicine that tightens vessels and stimulates the heart. SSC 2021 suggests adding it when MAP stays low despite noradrenaline and vasopressin 133. Intramuscular adrenaline is first-line for anaphylaxis 120.
AlbuminA blood protein also given as a fluid. In SAFE, 4% albumin matched saline for 28-day mortality (RR 0.99) 144; SSC 2026 suggests crystalloids alone over adding albumin 366.
AnaphylaxisA severe allergic reaction that can become life-threatening within minutes. Median time to arrest was 5 minutes for medicines, 15 for venom and 30 for food in fatal cases 372.
Angiotensin IIA vasopressor hormone. In ATHOS-3 it raised MAP response (69.9% vs 23.4%) 226; its label warns of thrombosis (12.9% vs 5.1%) 136.
Antimicrobial resistance (AMR)When germs survive the medicines meant to kill them. In 2019, 1.27 million deaths were attributable to bacterial AMR 310.
Antimicrobial stewardshipUsing the right antibiotic, at the right dose, for the shortest effective time. SSC 2021 suggests shorter courses with adequate source control 133.
ARDS (acute respiratory distress syndrome)Sudden, widespread lung inflammation with low oxygen. Berlin grades by PaO2/FiO2: mild 200–300, moderate 100–200, severe 100 or less mm Hg 27.
AspleniaHaving no working spleen. After splenectomy, infection incidence was 3.2% over a median 6.9 years 58.
Asymptomatic bacteriuriaBacteria in the urine without symptoms of infection; guidelines advise not treating it in older adults 301,329.
Balanced crystalloidA salt solution closer to plasma than 0.9% saline. In SMART, major kidney events were 14.3% vs 15.4% with saline 430; BaSICS and PLUS found no mortality difference 147,531.
Blood cultureBlood placed in bottles to grow germs. Two sets detect about 90% of bloodstream infections and three about 98% 252; an accepted contamination rate has been 3% or less 412.
BundleA small set of steps done together. The Hour-1 bundle has five elements meant to be started within the first hour 261,507.
CAM-ICUA 2-minute bedside test for delirium in ventilated patients; nurses using it had 93–100% sensitivity 129.
Capillary refill timeHow fast colour returns after pressing a fingertip. SSC 2021 suggests it as an adjunct to guide resuscitation 133, tested in ANDROMEDA-SHOCK 190.
Cardiac outputThe blood the heart pumps each minute: heart rate × stroke volume, set by heart rate, contractility, preload and afterload 497.
Cardiogenic shockShock from the heart failing as a pump. In infarct-related cardiogenic shock, an intra-aortic balloon pump did not lower 30-day mortality (39.7% vs 41.3%) 469.
Central venous pressure (CVP)Pressure in the large veins near the heart. It does not predict fluid responsiveness (AUC 0.56) 274.
CLABSICentral line-associated bloodstream infection. A checklist bundle cut it from 7.7 to 1.4 per 1,000 catheter-days 369.
Clostridioides difficileA gut bacterium that can cause severe diarrhoea after antibiotics; clindamycin carried the highest odds of community infection (OR 16.8) 73.
Community-onset sepsisSepsis present on, or within 3 days of, admission: 90.3% of adult cases in a CDC review 134.
Complement (C5a)A cascade of blood proteins that tags germs and calls in immune cells; C5a is among the most active inflammatory peptides in sepsis 200.
CorticosteroidsHormone medicines such as hydrocortisone. SSC 2026 still suggests IV corticosteroids for septic shock 366; the SSC threshold is noradrenaline or adrenaline 0.25 µg/kg/min or more for at least 4 hours, as published 133.
Culture-negative sepsisSepsis where no germ is grown. It was 41.5% of severe sepsis in a Singapore ICU and 47.1% in US national data 180,355.
CytokinesSignalling proteins that immune cells release. In pneumonia sepsis, high IL-6 together with high IL-10 carried the highest risk (HR 20.5) 224.
DAMPsDamage-associated molecular patterns: molecules released from injured host cells that the immune system senses as danger 200.
DeliriumAcute confusion that comes and goes. It affected 83.3% of evaluable ventilated patients in the CAM-ICU study 129.
DIC (disseminated intravascular coagulation)Widespread clotting in small vessels that uses up clotting factors, causing clots and bleeding 257. ISTH overt DIC is 5 points or more 467.
Distributive shockShock where vessels relax and leak, so pressure falls even with a high cardiac output; septic shock is the main example 85.
DopamineA vasopressor no longer first-line: in SOAP II it caused more arrhythmias than noradrenaline (24.1% vs 12.4%) 112.
Drotrecogin alfa (activated)Recombinant activated protein C, the one sepsis-specific drug approved and later withdrawn (2011) 55,288,382.
Dysregulated host responseThe body's own reaction to infection, out of proportion and harmful; the core of the Sepsis-3 definition 447.
Early goal-directed therapy (EGDT)A 6-hour protocol aimed at pressure and oxygen targets. Rivers 2001 reported benefit; PRISM found none over usual care 367,395.
Early-onset neonatal sepsisSepsis within 72 hours of birth, mainly from E. coli and group B streptococcus 462. The Kaiser calculator roughly halved empirical antibiotics 4.
EndotypeA subgroup of patients sharing an underlying biology, such as the SRS1 and SRS2 gene-expression groups (Early evidence) 109.
EndotheliumThe single layer of cells lining blood vessels, covered by the glycocalyx; in sepsis it leaks 484.
ExtravasationLeaking of an infused medicine out of the vein into tissue. The noradrenaline label advises phentolamine in saline as soon as possible 137; rates were about 2–3.4% with peripheral vasopressors in cohorts 81,474.
Febrile neutropeniaFever with very low neutrophils, often after chemotherapy. In-hospital mortality was 9.5% overall 239; a MASCC score of 21 or more marks low risk 228.
FludrocortisoneA mineralocorticoid given with hydrocortisone in APROCCHSS, where 90-day mortality was 43.0% vs 49.1% 24.
Fluid responsivenessWhether more fluid would raise cardiac output. Passive leg raising with cardiac output predicts it (AUC 0.95) 304.
FrailtyReduced strength and reserve. In REGARDS it raised sepsis risk (aHR 1.44) 271.
GlycocalyxA gel-like layer, about 0.5–5.0 μm thick, lining the inside of blood vessels 484.
Group A streptococcus (GAS)Streptococcus pyogenes. Postpartum women have about 20-fold higher incidence of invasive GAS 116, and it drove UK maternal sepsis deaths in 2006–08 80.
Group B streptococcus (GBS)A bacterium that can pass to babies at birth. Intrapartum prophylaxis cut early-onset GBS from 1.7 to 0.6 per 1,000 live births 421.
Hand hygieneCleaning hands between patients and procedures. Semmelweis's chlorine handwashing cut childbed deaths in 1847 458, and hand hygiene stops germs spreading in hospital and at home 357.
HLA-DR (monocyte)A molecule monocytes use to show antigens. Low levels mark immunoparalysis: below 30% on days 3–4 predicted death (OR 6.48) 303.
Hospital-onset sepsisSepsis that starts in hospital: 12.1% of cases, with twice the adjusted mortality of community-onset sepsis 390.
Hour-1 bundleFive steps started within the first hour: lactate, blood cultures, broad-spectrum antibiotics, crystalloid and vasopressors, as published 261,507.
Hypovolaemic shockShock from loss of blood or fluid. In trauma bleeding, tranexamic acid works best early; after 3 hours it was linked with more bleeding deaths 104.
ICU diaryA diary of the intensive care stay written by staff and family; it reduced new PTSD from 13% to 5% 217.
ImmunoparalysisA worn-out immune state later in sepsis. Post-mortem spleens produced under 10% of normal cytokines 63.
Inflammasome (NLRP3)A sensor complex that activates caspase-1 to make IL-1β and IL-18 and triggers pyroptosis 324.
LactateA blood chemical that rises in shock and severe illness, typically above 2 mmol/L in shock 85. It does not always mean oxygen shortage 163.
Lung-protective ventilationSmall breaths of about 6 mL/kg predicted body weight with plateau pressure 30 cmH2O or less; ARDSNet mortality fell from 39.8% to 31.0% 28.
MAP (mean arterial pressure)The average pressure driving blood to organs, estimated as diastolic plus one third of pulse pressure 338. SSC 2021 recommends an initial target of 65 mm Hg on vasopressors 133.
Maternal sepsisOrgan dysfunction from infection during pregnancy, childbirth, after abortion or after birth (WHO 2017) 61. It caused 10.7% of maternal deaths worldwide in 2003–2009 415.
Meningococcal diseaseInfection with Neisseria meningitidis. Leg pain, cold hands and feet and abnormal skin colour appear before the rash 473.
MicrocirculationThe smallest vessels. In severe sepsis only 48% of small vessels were perfused, against 90% in volunteers 111.
MODS (multiple organ dysfunction syndrome)Failure of more than one organ system, defined at the 1992 consensus 60.
MottlingBlotchy, violet-patterned skin, a sign that the small vessels are not perfusing well 85.
Necrotising soft-tissue infectionA fast-spreading infection that kills tissue under the skin. Surgery within 6 hours: mortality 19% vs 32% 320. The LRINEC score cannot rule it out 141.
NETs (neutrophil extracellular traps)Webs of DNA and granule proteins thrown out by neutrophils to trap and kill bacteria 71.
NEWS2A UK early warning score used in hospitals to spot deterioration; its SpO2 scale 2 is only for confirmed hypercapnic respiratory failure 492. A tool for trained staff.
Nitric oxideA gas made by vessel walls that relaxes them. Blocking its production raised pressure but increased mortality (59% vs 49%) 268.
Noradrenaline (norepinephrine)The first-line vasopressor in septic shock (SSC 2021, 2026) 133,366.
Obstructive shockShock from a blockage to flow, such as tamponade or tension pneumothorax. Pulsus paradoxus is an inspiratory fall in systolic pressure of more than 10 mm Hg 9.
PAMPsPathogen-associated molecular patterns: pieces of microbes, such as LPS, that immune sensors recognise 200.
Phoenix Sepsis ScoreThe 2024 paediatric score over respiratory, cardiovascular, coagulation and neurological systems; 2 or more with suspected infection is sepsis 418.
PICS (post-intensive care syndrome)New or worse physical, cognitive or mental health problems after critical illness that persist beyond hospital 323.
PICS-FAnxiety, stress, depression or complicated grief in relatives after a loved one's critical illness; it may last more than 4 years 110.
Pneumococcal vaccineVaccines against Streptococcus pneumoniae. PCV7 in infants cut invasive disease in under-2s by 69% 516; PCV13 protected adults 65 and over against vaccine-type disease 62.
Post-sepsis syndromeThe lasting physical, mental and cognitive after-effects of sepsis; having them is not a sign of weakness 308.
Procalcitonin (PCT)A blood protein that rises mainly in bacterial infection. SSC 2021 suggests it, with clinical judgement, to help decide when to stop antibiotics, not to start them 133.
Prone positioningTurning a ventilated patient onto the front. In PROSEVA, 28-day mortality was 16.0% vs 32.8% in severe ARDS 178.
Puerperal sepsisSepsis after childbirth ("childbed fever"), the disease Semmelweis fought in Vienna 458.
Purpura fulminansRapid bleeding into the skin from clots in its small vessels, linked to protein C depletion and most often meningococcal sepsis; mortality is over 50% 56.
qSOFAQuick SOFA: breathing rate 22/min or more, altered mentation, systolic BP 100 mm Hg or less 434. Not recommended as a single screening tool 133.
ReadmissionGoing back into hospital. About 40% of sepsis survivors are readmitted within 90 days 361.
Renal replacement therapyDialysis or filtration that does the kidneys' work. Starting early without urgent indications did not save lives 38,42,165.
SepsisLife-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection 447. Any infection can lead to it.
Sepsis-induced coagulopathy (SIC)An early clotting disorder of sepsis scored from PT-INR, platelets and SOFA; 4 points or more 205.
Septic cardiomyopathyReversible weakening of the heart in septic shock; in survivors ejection fraction recovered in about 10 days 342.
Septic shockSepsis with vasopressors needed for MAP of 65 mm Hg or more and lactate above 2 mmol/L despite adequate fluids 447.
Severe sepsisA pre-2016 category of sepsis with organ dysfunction, dropped by Sepsis-3 as redundant 447.
ShockA state in which cells cannot get or use enough oxygen; low blood pressure is common but not required 85.
Shock indexHeart rate divided by systolic blood pressure; values near 1.0 suggest shock 232. A teaching tool, not for self-triage.
SIRSSystemic inflammatory response syndrome: 2 or more of temperature, heart rate, breathing rate and white count criteria 60. One in eight ICU patients with infection and organ failure is SIRS-negative 223.
SOFA scoreSepsis-related Organ Failure Assessment (1996): six systems scored 0–4, total 0–24 246,494.
SOFA-2The 2025 revision of SOFA, built from 3.34 million ICU encounters; it left out gastrointestinal and immune dysfunction 383.
Source controlDraining, removing or debriding the source of infection as soon as medically and logistically practical (SSC 2021) 133.
Starches (HES)Synthetic colloid fluids. In 6S they caused more deaths and kidney replacement; SSC recommends against them 133,353.
Surviving Sepsis Campaign (SSC)An international effort launched by the 2002 Barcelona Declaration, publishing guidelines since 2004 114,450.
Toll-like receptors (TLRs)Sensors on immune cells that recognise pieces of germs, such as TLR4 for LPS 359.
Toxic shock syndromeA toxin-driven illness from S. aureus or group A streptococcus, whose superantigens trigger a "cytokine avalanche" 250.
Transfusion thresholdThe haemoglobin level at which blood is given. In TRISS, 7 g/dL was as safe as 9 g/dL in septic shock 197.
VasopressinA hormone used as the second vasopressor; SSC 2021 says it is usually started at noradrenaline 0.25–0.5 µg/kg/min, as published 133.
VasopressorA medicine that tightens blood vessels to raise blood pressure. SSC suggests starting peripherally rather than waiting for a central line 133.
Vitamin C (high-dose)Tested as a sepsis treatment; in LOVIT it increased death or persistent organ dysfunction (RR 1.21) 248.

Sepsis Companion is an educational synthesis of the published literature on sepsis (the body's harmful response to an infection), septic shock and the other types of shock, made for health-science, medical and nursing courses and for people who survive sepsis, their families and caregivers.

It is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, and nothing here should start, stop or change a medicine or a care plan without your own care team. The qSOFA, SOFA, NEWS2, Phoenix and shock-index calculators are for learning, never for self-triage or for deciding whether to seek care. Antibiotic and vasopressor doses, fluid volumes, MAP and lactate targets and guideline statements are quoted as published in guidelines and labels (Surviving Sepsis Campaign, NICE, Sepsis-3), never as instructions, and mortality figures are population statistics. Sepsis is a medical emergency. If someone with an infection becomes very unwell (confused or very drowsy, breathing very fast, cold, mottled or bluish skin, passing little or no urine), call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department now and ask: could this be sepsis?
