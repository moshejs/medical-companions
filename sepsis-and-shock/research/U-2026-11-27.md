## Findings

### fluids · Ringer's lactate versus saline in septic shock (FISSH)
- FISSH was a multicentre, blinded, parallel-group randomised trial in 30 academic and community hospitals in Canada and Saudi Arabia. Adults (16 or over) with septic shock (systolic pressure below 90 mm Hg or MAP below 65 mm Hg after a 1 L bolus, or lactate above 4 mmol/L, with suspected infection, within 6 h of presentation) received Ringer's lactate or 0.9% saline for all bolus and maintenance fluid during their ICU stay up to day 30. NCT03677102. [[Rochwerg2026FISSH]]
- 1113 were randomised between October 2018 and February 2026 and 1103 analysed (557 Ringer's lactate, 546 saline). Mean APACHE II 24.7; mean baseline lactate 4.8 mmol/L; mean study fluid 5844 mL. [[Rochwerg2026FISSH]]
- 30-day in-hospital mortality: 33.6% (187) with Ringer's lactate vs 33.0% (180) with saline; RR 1.02 (95% CI 0.86 to 1.21; P = 0.80). No serious adverse events. (Established) [[Rochwerg2026FISSH]]
- The confidence interval allows a relative difference of about 14% lower to 21% higher mortality, so a small effect either way is not excluded. SSC 2026 still suggests balanced crystalloids over 0.9% saline [[Prescott2026SSC]]; the site's guideline rows are unchanged.

### fluids · Liberal or usual fluids in the ambulance (PITSTOP-Fluids)
- PITSTOP was a 2 × 2 factorial randomised trial run by 4 paramedic services and their destination hospitals in Ontario, Canada, enrolling March 2020 to August 2025. Adults with suspected sepsis (suspected infection, fever of 38.0 °C or more, systolic pressure below 100 mm Hg) received liberal fluids (up to 2 L of normal saline regardless of later blood pressure) or usual care (fluid only when systolic pressure was below 90 mm Hg, stopped at 100 mm Hg or more) until handover in the emergency department. NCT03068741. [[Scales2026PITSTOPfluid]]
- 1686 randomised, 1669 eligible, 1646 in the primary analysis (830 liberal, 816 usual). Median age 76. A bolus was given to 97.4% vs 85.3%; median volume 1500 mL (IQR 1000–2000) vs 500 mL (IQR 175–800). [[Scales2026PITSTOPfluid]]
- Hospital mortality (death before discharge, to day 90): 18.0% (149/830) vs 18.3% (149/816); RR 0.98 (95% CI 0.80 to 1.20; P = 0.82). All 17 secondary outcomes were similar; adverse events, including pulmonary oedema during transport, were infrequent in both groups. (Established) [[Scales2026PITSTOPfluid]]

### firsthour · Antibiotics in the ambulance (PITSTOP-Antibiotics)
- Same trial, placebo-controlled factor: ceftriaxone 1 g intramuscularly or placebo in the ambulance; all patients could receive open-label antibiotics in the emergency department. [[Scales2026PITSTOPabx]]
- 2091 randomised, 2048 eligible for the primary analysis (1026 ceftriaxone, 1022 placebo). Median age 75. Mean time from paramedic arrival to study drug: 37 min (SD 36) vs 39 min (SD 55). Antibiotics were given in hospital to 92.5% vs 92.2%, a mean 247.3 (SD 207.7) vs 252.8 (SD 218.0) min after paramedic arrival. [[Scales2026PITSTOPabx]]
- Hospital mortality: 18.0% (182/1009) vs 18.4% (186/1011); RR 0.97 (95% CI 0.81 to 1.17; P = 0.77). All 17 secondary outcomes were similar. (Established) [[Scales2026PITSTOPabx]]
- This is the second randomised trial of prehospital antibiotics with no mortality difference, after PHANTASi [[Alam2018PHANTASi]], and the first that was placebo-controlled.

### Exam hooks
- FISSH: Ringer's lactate vs saline in septic shock, 30-day in-hospital mortality 33.6% vs 33.0% (RR 1.02). [[Rochwerg2026FISSH]]
- PITSTOP: neither ceftriaxone in the ambulance (18.0% vs 18.4%) nor liberal prehospital fluid (18.0% vs 18.3%) changed hospital mortality. [[Scales2026PITSTOPabx,Scales2026PITSTOPfluid]]

### In plain words
- A large trial found that people with septic shock did equally well whether their drip fluid was salt water (saline) or Ringer's lactate.
- Giving an antibiotic injection or extra drip fluid in the ambulance, instead of waiting until hospital, did not save more lives in a Canadian trial.

## Open questions and controversies
- Balanced versus saline: FISSH adds a neutral, blinded trial confined to septic shock [[Rochwerg2026FISSH]] to the neutral BaSICS and PLUS [[Zampieri2021BaSICSfluid,Finfer2022PLUS]]; pooled analyses still give a probable small benefit [[Hammond2022NEJMEvid,Zampieri2024BESTLiving]]. Status: open; no updated pooled analysis including FISSH was found in the window.
- Is each hour of antibiotic delay causal? Two randomised trials of antibiotics in the ambulance found no mortality difference [[Alam2018PHANTASi,Scales2026PITSTOPabx]]. PITSTOP used one intramuscular dose of ceftriaxone in people with fever and hypotension, not confirmed septic shock. Status: association consistent in cohorts, causal size uncertain.
