## Findings

### frontier · Ulinastatin did not reduce mortality (ADJUST)
- ADJUST was a multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in adult ICU patients with sepsis (Sepsis-3) within 48 hours of admission. Patients received intravenous ulinastatin (urinary trypsin inhibitor) 400,000 IU every 8 hours or placebo for up to 10 days, on top of standard care. NCT02647554. (Established) [[Chen2026ADJUST]]
- 347 were randomised, 340 received the intervention and 335 were in the intention-to-treat analysis (165 ulinastatin, 170 placebo). [[Chen2026ADJUST]]
- 28-day all-cause mortality: 23.6% (39/165) vs 22.9% (39/170); absolute difference 0.7% (95% CI −8.4% to 9.7%); hazard ratio 1.03 (95% CI 0.66 to 1.61). ICU, hospital and 90-day mortality and lengths of stay were similar. (Established) [[Chen2026ADJUST]]
- Adverse events: 56.8% (96/169) vs 42.7% (73/171); absolute difference 14.1% (95% CI 3.6% to 24.6%; P = 0.009). [[Chen2026ADJUST]]
- The authors conclude that routine use of ulinastatin for sepsis is not supported. The confidence interval is wide, so a moderate effect in either direction is not excluded.

### Exam hooks
- ADJUST: ulinastatin vs placebo in sepsis, 28-day mortality 23.6% vs 22.9% (HR 1.03); more adverse events with ulinastatin. [[Chen2026ADJUST]]

### In plain words
- Ulinastatin, an anti-inflammatory medicine used for sepsis in some countries, did not save more lives than a dummy treatment in a careful trial.

## Open questions and controversies
- Ulinastatin: earlier small trials and meta-analyses suggested a survival benefit; ADJUST found none in 335 people, with a wide confidence interval [[Chen2026ADJUST]]. Status: no benefit shown; a larger trial would be needed to exclude a small effect.
