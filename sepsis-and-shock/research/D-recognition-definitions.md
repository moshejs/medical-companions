# D · Recognising sepsis, early warning scores & definitions

Pass date: 2026-11-04. Modules served: recognise, definitions, children.

This pass covers how sepsis (the body's harmful response to an infection) is recognised and defined. It includes the symptoms and red flags in the NICE risk tables, NEWS2, qSOFA and the evidence that qSOFA screens poorly, SIRS and Sepsis-2, Sepsis-3 and the SOFA score (with SOFA-2, 2025), shock index, electronic sepsis alerts, and children: IPSCC 2005, the Phoenix criteria 2024, pSOFA, PEWS, the NICE under-16 tables, neonatal early- and late-onset sepsis, group B streptococcus (GBS) and the Kaiser early-onset sepsis calculator.

> **Call-now box (every page on symptoms, recognition or treatment):** Sepsis is a medical emergency. If someone with an infection becomes very unwell (confused or very drowsy, breathing very fast, cold, mottled or bluish skin, passing little or no urine), call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department now and ask: could this be sepsis?
>
> **Calculator caveat:** qSOFA, SOFA, NEWS2, Phoenix and shock-index widgets are teaching tools. They are never for self-triage or for deciding whether to seek care. Thresholds and targets are quoted as published, not as instructions. All mortality figures are population statistics, not a prediction for one person.

## Findings

### recognise · When to think "could this be sepsis?" (NICE 2025)
- NICE NG253 (16 and over) and NG254 (under 16s) both open: "Think 'could this be sepsis?' if a person presents with symptoms or signs that indicate possible infection." [2016 wording] [[NICE-NG253,NICE-NG254]]
- NICE: "people with sepsis may have non-specific, non-localised presentations, for example feeling very unwell, and may not have a high temperature." [[NICE-NG253,NICE-NG254]]
- NICE: "Pay particular attention to concerns expressed by the person and their family or carers, for example changes from usual behaviour." [[NICE-NG253,NICE-NG254]]
- NICE asks for extra care when history-taking is difficult, for example English as a second language or communication challenges (neurodiversity, cognitive impairment, learning disabilities, severe mental health conditions or brain injury in NG253). [[NICE-NG253]]
- In any suspected infection, NICE asks clinicians to identify the possible source, factors that raise sepsis risk, and "new-onset abnormalities of behaviour, circulation or respiration". [[NICE-NG253,NICE-NG254]]
- NG253 1.2.1 [2025] lists factors that may increase the risk of sepsis, or of sepsis not being identified promptly:
  - age 75 or over
  - being from an ethnic minority background
  - frailty, multimorbidity or severe chronic conditions
  - impaired immune function
  - surgery or invasive procedures in the past 6 weeks
  - indwelling catheters, repeated antibiotic use, breach of skin integrity
  - communication challenges (learning disabilities, cognitive impairment, needing an interpreter)
  - drug or alcohol misuse
  - homelessness, living in deprived areas [[NICE-NG253]]
- NG254 uses the same list for under 16s, but the age factor is "being under 1 year". [[NICE-NG254]]
- NICE rated the evidence on sepsis risk factors as "very low certainty". The committee still made strong recommendations "because, in a life-threatening situation… the potential benefit may save lives." [[NICE-NG253]]
- Neutropenic sepsis: NICE says to suspect it in people who become unwell and are having, or have had, systemic anticancer treatment within the last 30 days, or immunosuppressant treatment. Refer them immediately. [[NICE-NG253,NICE-NG254]]
- A CDC chart review of 246 adults and 79 children with severe sepsis or septic shock in four New York hospitals found that 72% had a health-care factor in the previous 30 days, or a chronic condition needing frequent care. Pneumonia was the most common infection behind sepsis, 25% of patients died, and CDC launched its sepsis awareness campaign in response. [[Novosad2016]]

### recognise · NICE risk criteria, people aged 16 or over, community and custodial settings (NG253 table 1)
- Labels as published (2026 wording: high risk / medium risk). Anyone meeting no high- or medium-risk criterion is "low risk". [[NICE-NG253]]
- **History.**
  - High risk: "Objective evidence of new altered mental state".
  - Medium risk: a history of new altered behaviour or mental state; acute deterioration of functional ability; impaired immune system (illness or drugs including oral steroids); trauma, surgery or invasive procedures in the last 6 weeks. [[NICE-NG253]]
- **Respiratory.**
  - High risk: respiratory rate 25/min or more, or a new need for oxygen (40% FiO2 or more) to keep saturation above 92% (above 88% in chronic hypercapnic respiratory failure).
  - Medium risk: respiratory rate 21–24/min. [[NICE-NG253]]
- **Blood pressure.**
  - High risk: systolic 90 mmHg or less, or more than 40 mmHg below normal.
  - Medium risk: systolic 91–100 mmHg. [[NICE-NG253]]
- **Circulation and hydration.**
  - High risk: heart rate more than 130/min; no urine passed in the previous 18 hours; catheterised output below 0.5 ml/kg/h.
  - Medium risk: heart rate 91–130/min (100–130 in pregnancy) or new arrhythmia; no urine for 12–18 hours; catheterised output 0.5–1 ml/kg/h. [[NICE-NG253]]
- **Temperature.** Medium risk only: tympanic temperature below 36°C. There is no high-risk temperature criterion, and fever is not a criterion at all. [[NICE-NG253]]
- **Skin.**
  - High risk: mottled or ashen appearance; cyanosis of skin, lips or tongue; non-blanching petechial or purpuric rash.
  - Medium risk: signs of potential infection at a surgical site or wound breakdown. [[NICE-NG253]]
- NICE notes that mottled or ashen skin, a non-blanching rash and cyanosis can also be signs of meningococcal disease. [[NICE-NG253]]

### recognise · NEWS2 in acute hospitals, mental health units and ambulances (NICE 2024)
- NICE 1.1.8 [2024]: "Use the national early warning score (NEWS2) to assess people with suspected sepsis who are aged 16 or over, are not and have not recently been pregnant, and are in an acute hospital setting, acute mental health setting or ambulance." [[NICE-NG253,Gildea2024NICE]]
- NICE 1.6.2 [2024] NEWS2 bands for risk of severe illness or death from sepsis:
  - 7 or more: high
  - 5 or 6: medium
  - 1 to 4: low
  - 0: very low [[NICE-NG253]]
- NICE 1.6.2: if a single parameter contributes 3 points, "request a high-priority review by a clinician with core competencies in the care of acutely ill patients (FY2 or above)". [[NICE-NG253]]
- NICE 1.6.3 says to consider risk higher than the NEWS2 score suggests if there is mottled or ashen appearance, a non-blanching petechial or purpuric rash, or cyanosis of skin, lips or tongue. The same applies (1.6.4) when the person is deteriorating or not improving since a previous score or intervention. [[NICE-NG253]]
- NICE 1.6.5 recalculation intervals, following the AoMRC 2022 statement:
  - high risk: every 30 minutes
  - medium risk: every hour
  - low risk: every 4–6 hours
  - very low risk: routine observations [[NICE-NG253]]
- NICE committee rationale:
  - Evidence showed higher ICU admission and mortality in suspected sepsis at NEWS2 5 or more.
  - NEWS2 "should be used as a tool to support clinical decision making, not to replace clinical judgement".
  - A score of 0 does not mean no risk.
  - NEWS2 "can be less accurate" in spinal injury and in heart or lung disease. [[NICE-NG253]]
- NICE lists, as a research priority, the association between NEWS2 bands (0, 1–4, 5–6, 7 or above) and risk of severe illness or death in suspected sepsis. It states that evidence on NEWS2 in this setting "was not found". [[NICE-NG253]]
- In community or custodial settings, NICE says only to "consider using an early warning score" [2016, amended 2024]. [[NICE-NG253]]

### recognise · NEWS2 chart (RCP 2017), for the calculator
- NEWS2 was first produced (as NEWS) in 2012 and updated in December 2017. [[RCP2017NEWS2]]
- Points per parameter (values as reproduced from the RCP chart): [[Verma2023NEWS2,RCP2017NEWS2]]

  | Parameter | 3 points | 2 points | 1 point | 0 points | 1 point | 2 points | 3 points |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Respiration rate (/min) | ≤8 | – | 9–11 | 12–20 | – | 21–24 | ≥25 |
  | SpO2 scale 1 (%) | ≤91 | 92–93 | 94–95 | ≥96 | – | – | – |
  | Air or oxygen | – | oxygen | – | air | – | – | – |
  | Systolic BP (mmHg) | ≤90 | 91–100 | 101–110 | 111–219 | – | – | ≥220 |
  | Pulse (/min) | ≤40 | – | 41–50 | 51–90 | 91–110 | 111–130 | ≥131 |
  | Consciousness | – | – | – | Alert | – | – | new Confusion, V, P or U |
  | Temperature (°C) | ≤35.0 | – | 35.1–36.0 | 36.1–38.0 | 38.1–39.0 | ≥39.1 | – |

- SpO2 scale 2 points: ≤83% = 3; 84–85% = 2; 86–87% = 1; 88–92%, or ≥93% on air = 0; 93–94% on oxygen = 1; 95–96% on oxygen = 2; ≥97% on oxygen = 3. [[Verma2023NEWS2,RCP2017NEWS2]]
- RCP interpretation: aggregate 0–4 low clinical risk, 5–6 medium, 7–20 high (maximum 20). A score of 3 in any single parameter is treated as "low-medium" risk. [[Verma2023NEWS2,NICE-NG253]]
- Scale 2 "should only be used in patients confirmed to have a hypercapnic respiratory failure on blood gas analysis" (RCP additional guidance, as quoted). [[Verma2023NEWS2]]
- RCP definition of new confusion: new disorientation or agitation where mental state was previously normal, which "may be subtle". It scores 3 on NEWS2 (equivalent to GCS verbal 3–4). [[RCP2017NEWS2]]
- In 198,755 observation sets (35,585 acute medical admissions), the original NEWS predicted death within 24 h with AUROC 0.894, and any of cardiac arrest, unplanned ICU admission or death with 0.873. This was better than all 33 other early warning scores tested (death: 0.813–0.858). [[Smith2013NEWS]]
- In the West of England (2015–2019), using NEWS across community, primary and secondary care was associated with falling mortality in suspected sepsis that was not seen in the rest of England. By March 2019 the region had the lowest suspected-sepsis mortality in England (quality-improvement design, not randomised). [[Pullyblank2020]]

### recognise · qSOFA: derivation (Sepsis-3)
- qSOFA scores 0–3, with 1 point each for respiratory rate ≥22/min, altered mentation, and systolic BP ≤100 mmHg. A score of 2 or more is positive. [[Seymour2016qSOFA,Singer2016Sepsis3]]
- In the SSC 2021 wording, altered mentation is "a Glasgow Coma Score < 15". [[Evans2021SSC]]
- Derivation cohort: 1.3 million EHR encounters at 12 Pennsylvania hospitals (2010–2012), including 148,907 with suspected infection, of whom 6,347 (4%) died. Confirmatory analysis used 706,399 encounters at 165 hospitals. [[Seymour2016qSOFA]]
- Outside the ICU (66,522 validation encounters, 3% died), AUROC for in-hospital mortality was qSOFA 0.81 (95% CI 0.80–0.82), SOFA 0.79 (0.78–0.80) and SIRS 0.76 (0.75–0.77). [[Seymour2016qSOFA]]
- Inside the ICU (7,932 encounters, 16% died), AUROC was SOFA 0.74, LODS 0.75, qSOFA 0.66 and SIRS 0.64. This is why SOFA, not qSOFA, anchors the definition. [[Seymour2016qSOFA]]
- Compared with qSOFA below 2, a qSOFA of 2 or more meant a 3- to 14-fold increase in hospital mortality across deciles of baseline risk. [[Seymour2016qSOFA]]
- Sepsis-3 framed qSOFA as a prompt "to consider possible sepsis", not as a diagnostic criterion. [[Seymour2016qSOFA,Singer2016Sepsis3]]
- In the derivation data, only 24% of infected patients had qSOFA 2 or 3, but they accounted for 70% of poor outcomes (as summarised by SSC 2021). [[Evans2021SSC]]

### recognise · qSOFA: later evidence and SSC 2021
- Churpek 2017 studied 30,677 ward and ED patients with suspected infection (5.4% died). AUROC for in-hospital mortality: NEWS 0.77, MEWS 0.73, qSOFA 0.69, SIRS 0.65 (all pairwise P < 0.01). [[Churpek2017]]
- Churpek 2017 also found, for death or ICU transfer:
  - Sensitivity and specificity: SIRS ≥2 91% and 13%; qSOFA ≥2 54% and 67%; MEWS ≥5 59% and 70%; NEWS ≥8 67% and 66%.
  - Timing: most patients met ≥2 SIRS criteria 17 h before the outcome, against 5 h for qSOFA ≥2. [[Churpek2017]]
- Fernando 2018 meta-analysis (38 studies, n = 385,333), mortality prediction:
  - qSOFA pooled sensitivity 60.8% (95% CI 51.4–69.4%) and specificity 72.0% (63.4–79.2%).
  - SIRS sensitivity 88.1% (82.3–92.1%) and specificity 25.8% (17.1–36.9%).
  - Outside the ICU, qSOFA sensitivity was 51.2% and specificity 79.6%. [[Fernando2018]]
- Song 2018 meta-analysis (23 studies, 146,551 patients outside the ICU), in-hospital mortality:
  - Pooled sensitivity: qSOFA 0.51 vs SIRS 0.86. Specificity: 0.83 vs 0.29. AUC 0.74 vs 0.71.
  - For acute organ dysfunction, qSOFA sensitivity was only 0.47. [[Song2018qSOFA]]
- Serafim 2018 meta-analysis (10 studies, 229,480 patients outside the ICU): SIRS was better than qSOFA for diagnosing sepsis, and qSOFA was slightly better for predicting in-hospital mortality. [[Serafim2018]]
- Freund 2017 prospective ED cohort (30 EDs, 879 patients):
  - In-hospital mortality was 3% with qSOFA <2 against 24% with qSOFA ≥2 (absolute difference 21%, 95% CI 15–26%).
  - qSOFA AUROC was 0.80 against 0.65 for SIRS. [[Freund2017]]
- Raith 2017 (184,875 adults in 182 Australian and New Zealand ICUs) used in-hospital mortality as the outcome.
  - Crude AUROC: SOFA increase ≥2 0.753, qSOFA 0.607, SIRS 0.589.
  - 90.1% met the SOFA ≥2 criterion, 86.7% had ≥2 SIRS and 54.4% had qSOFA ≥2. [[Raith2017]]
- Goulden 2018 (1,818 UK emergency admissions treated as sepsis), in-hospital mortality:
  - Sensitivity: NEWS ≥5 74%, SIRS ≥2 80%, qSOFA ≥2 37%.
  - Specificity: 43%, 21% and 79%.
  - AUROC: NEWS 0.65, qSOFA 0.62. [[Goulden2018]]
- Usman 2019 (130,595 ED visits, triage detection of severe sepsis or septic shock):
  - AUROC: NEWS 0.91, SIRS 0.88, qSOFA 0.81.
  - Sensitivity: NEWS ≥4 84.2%, SIRS ≥2 86.1%, qSOFA ≥2 28.5%. [[Usman2019]]
- Verma 2023 (373 ED sepsis patients in India, 35.12% mortality): NEWS2 AUC 0.781 against 0.729 for qSOFA. [[Verma2023NEWS2]]
- SSC 2021 Recommendation 2: "We recommend against using qSOFA compared to SIRS, NEWS, or MEWS as a single screening tool for sepsis or septic shock" (strong recommendation, moderate-quality evidence). [[Evans2021SSC]]
- SSC 2021 adds: "Neither SIRS nor qSOFA are ideal screening tools for sepsis". A positive qSOFA "should alert the clinician to the possibility of sepsis in all resource settings". [[Evans2021SSC]]
- SSC 2021 Recommendation 1: hospitals and health systems should use a performance improvement programme including "sepsis screening for acutely ill, high-risk patients" (strong recommendation; moderate-quality evidence for screening). [[Evans2021SSC]]
- SSC 2021 evidence behind Recommendation 1:
  - A meta-analysis of 50 observational studies linked such programmes with lower mortality (OR 0.66, 95% CI 0.61–0.72).
  - A pooled analysis of three RCTs of active screening showed no mortality benefit (RR 0.90, 95% CI 0.51–1.58). [[Evans2021SSC]]
- SSC 2021 Recommendation 3: "For adults suspected of having sepsis, we suggest measuring blood lactate" (weak recommendation, low-quality evidence). Lactate "alone is neither sensitive nor specific enough to rule-in or rule-out the diagnosis". Detail belongs to pass E. [[Evans2021SSC]]

### recognise · Shock index and electronic alerts
- Shock index (SI) = heart rate ÷ systolic blood pressure. Berger 2013 studied 2,524 ED patients with suspected infection (Established as a definition):
  - Patients with SI ≥0.7 were about three times as likely to have lactate ≥4.0 mmol/L (15.8% vs 4.9%).
  - SI ≥0.7 had a negative predictive value of 95%, the same as SIRS.
  - SI ≥1.0 was the most specific predictor (pilot, single centre). [[Berger2013ShockIndex]]
- Wong 2021 externally validated the proprietary Epic Sepsis Model (38,455 hospitalisations, 7% sepsis):
  - Hospitalisation-level AUROC was 0.63.
  - It missed 1,709 of 2,552 sepsis cases (67%), while alerting on 18% of all patients. (Established: poor external performance of this model.) [[Wong2021ESM]]
- TREWS machine-learning alert (590,736 patients monitored across 5 hospitals): among 6,877 people with sepsis flagged before antibiotics, provider confirmation within 3 h was associated with an adjusted absolute in-hospital mortality reduction of 3.3% (95% CI 1.7–5.1%), or 18.7% relative. Prospective cohort, not randomised. (Early evidence) [[Adams2022TREWS]]
- SSC 2021 cites a meta-analysis of 42,623 patients in which machine-learning tools for hospital-acquired sepsis had pooled AUROC 0.89, against SIRS 0.70, MEWS 0.50 and SOFA 0.78. (Early evidence) [[Evans2021SSC]]

### definitions · SIRS and Sepsis-1 (1991–1992)
- The ACCP/SCCM Consensus Conference (Northbrook, August 1991; published 1992) introduced:
  - the systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS)
  - definitions of sepsis, severe sepsis, septic shock, hypotension and multiple organ dysfunction syndrome [[Bone1992SIRS]]
- SIRS criteria, positive with 2 or more:
  - temperature >38°C or <36°C
  - heart rate >90/min
  - respiratory rate >20/min or PaCO2 <32 mmHg
  - white cell count >12,000/mm³, <4,000/mm³ or >10% bands [[Bone1992SIRS,Song2018qSOFA]]
- Sepsis-1/2 logic: sepsis = infection + ≥2 SIRS; severe sepsis = sepsis + organ dysfunction; septic shock = sepsis-induced hypotension despite fluids. [[Bone1992SIRS,Kaukonen2015]]
- Kaukonen 2015 (1,171,797 patients in 172 Australian and New Zealand ICUs, 2000–2013), among 109,663 with infection and organ failure:
  - 12.1% (13,278) were SIRS-negative: "one in eight".
  - Mortality fell from 36.1% to 18.3% (SIRS-positive) and from 27.7% to 9.3% (SIRS-negative).
  - Each additional SIRS criterion raised mortality odds (OR 1.13, 95% CI 1.11–1.15), with no step at 2 criteria. [[Kaukonen2015]]

### definitions · Sepsis-2 (2001 conference, published 2003)
- The 2001 SCCM/ESICM/ACCP/ATS/SIS International Sepsis Definitions Conference (29 participants) found that "apart from expanding the list of signs and symptoms of sepsis… no evidence exists to support a change to the definitions". [[Levy2003Sepsis2]]
- Sepsis-2 introduced PIRO (predisposition, infection, response, organ dysfunction) as "a hypothesis-generating model for future research". (Hypothesis) [[Levy2003Sepsis2]]

### definitions · Sepsis-3 (2016)
- Sepsis-3 was written by a 19-member SCCM/ESICM task force and endorsed by 31 societies. [[Singer2016Sepsis3]]
- Definition: "Sepsis should be defined as life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection." [[Singer2016Sepsis3]]
- Organ dysfunction is operationalised as an increase in SOFA score of 2 points or more, "associated with an in-hospital mortality greater than 10%". [[Singer2016Sepsis3]]
- Sepsis-3 found the term "severe sepsis" redundant and criticised the earlier definitions for:
  - "an excessive focus on inflammation"
  - the "misleading model" of a continuum through severe sepsis to shock
  - "inadequate specificity and sensitivity" of SIRS [[Singer2016Sepsis3]]
- Septic shock definition: "a subset of sepsis in which particularly profound circulatory, cellular, and metabolic abnormalities are associated with a greater risk of mortality than with sepsis alone." [[Singer2016Sepsis3]]
- Septic shock clinical criteria: "a vasopressor requirement to maintain a mean arterial pressure of 65 mm Hg or greater and serum lactate level greater than 2 mmol/L (>18 mg/dL) in the absence of hypovolemia". This is associated with hospital mortality above 40%. [[Singer2016Sepsis3,ShankarHari2016Shock]]
- Shankar-Hari 2016 systematic review (44 studies, 166,479 patients): crude septic-shock mortality was 46.5% (95% CI 42.7–50.3%), with I² = 99.5%. [[ShankarHari2016Shock]]
- In the SSC database (n = 28,150), the group needing vasopressors for MAP ≥65 mmHg with lactate >2 mmol/L after fluids had mortality of 42.3% (95% CI 41.2–43.3%). This was higher than the 5 other combinations of hypotension, vasopressors and lactate, and was validated in the UPMC (1.3 million) and KPNC (1.8 million) datasets. [[ShankarHari2016Shock]]
- Sepsis-3 applies to adults. The paediatric task force noted that Sepsis-3 "excluded children". [[Schlapbach2024Phoenix]]

### definitions · SOFA score (Vincent 1996), for the calculator
- SOFA was proposed in 1996 by the ESICM Working Group on Sepsis-Related Problems to describe organ dysfunction/failure. [[Vincent1996SOFA]]
- It scores six systems from 0 to 4, for a total of 0–24. [[Lambden2019SOFA,Ferreira2001SOFA]]
- **Respiratory** (PaO2/FiO2, mmHg): >400 = 0; <400 = 1; <300 = 2; <200 with respiratory support = 3; <100 with respiratory support = 4. [[Lambden2019SOFA]]
- **Nervous system** (GCS): 15 = 0; 13–14 = 1; 10–12 = 2; 6–9 = 3; <6 = 4. [[Lambden2019SOFA]]
- **Cardiovascular:** [[Lambden2019SOFA]]
  - MAP >70 mmHg = 0; MAP <70 mmHg = 1
  - dopamine ≤5 µg/kg/min or dobutamine (any dose) = 2
  - dopamine >5, or epinephrine ≤0.1, or norepinephrine ≤0.1 µg/kg/min = 3
  - dopamine >15, or epinephrine >0.1, or norepinephrine >0.1 µg/kg/min = 4
- **Liver** (bilirubin, mg/dL [µmol/L]): <1.2 [<20] = 0; 1.2–1.9 [20–32] = 1; 2.0–5.9 [33–101] = 2; 6.0–11.9 [102–204] = 3; >12.0 [>204] = 4. [[Lambden2019SOFA]]
- **Coagulation** (platelets ×10³/µL): >150 = 0; <150 = 1; <100 = 2; <50 = 3; <20 = 4. [[Lambden2019SOFA]]
- **Kidneys** (creatinine, mg/dL [µmol/L]; urine output): <1.2 [<110] = 0; 1.2–1.9 [110–170] = 1; 2.0–3.4 [171–299] = 2; 3.5–4.9 [300–440] or urine <500 ml/day = 3; >5.0 [>440] or urine <200 ml/day = 4. [[Lambden2019SOFA]]
- Scoring conventions: [[Lambden2019SOFA]]
  - Use the worst value of each sub-score in each 24-h period.
  - Parameters that match no row score zero.
  - The admission SOFA uses the worst values in the 24 h before ICU admission.
  - Delta SOFA is the change from a defined baseline.
- Vincent 1998 (1,449 patients, 40 ICUs in 16 countries; ICU mortality 22%), among the 544 patients who stayed at least 1 week: total SOFA rose in 44% of non-survivors but only 20% of survivors, and fell in 33% of survivors against 21% of non-survivors. [[Vincent1998SOFA]]
- Ferreira 2001 (352 ICU patients):
  - Initial or highest SOFA >11, or mean SOFA >5, corresponded to mortality above 80%.
  - Over the first 96 h, mortality was at least 50% when the score increased, 27–35% when unchanged, and below 27% when it decreased. [[Ferreira2001SOFA]]
- Ferreira 2001 also found that an increase in SOFA during the first 48 h predicted mortality of at least 50%, whatever the initial score. The highest score had AUROC 0.90. [[Ferreira2001SOFA]]

### definitions · SOFA-2 (2025)
- SOFA-2 was developed by modified Delphi plus data from 3.34 million ICU encounters (1,319 ICUs, 9 countries, 2014–2023).
  - It keeps 6 systems (brain, respiratory, cardiovascular, liver, kidney, haemostasis) with revised thresholds.
  - AUROC: SOFA-2 0.79 (95% CI 0.76–0.81) vs original SOFA 0.77 (0.74–0.81). [[Ranzani2025SOFA2]]
- SOFA-2 left out gastrointestinal and immune dysfunction because of "insufficient data and lack of content validity". [[Ranzani2025SOFA2]]
- Key changes in SOFA-2: [[Lobo2025SOFA2]]
  - SpO2/FiO2 as an alternative to PaO2/FiO2
  - non-invasive support (high-flow nasal oxygen, CPAP) counts as respiratory support, and ECMO is included
  - expanded vasopressors and inotropes with equivalent dosing, plus mechanical circulatory support
  - renal replacement therapy, or eligibility for it, in the kidney score
  - delirium treatment counts in the brain score
- Sepsis-3 is still defined with the original SOFA. A "phased dual-scoring approach" has been suggested while studies move to SOFA-2. (Early evidence) [[Lobo2025SOFA2]]

### children · IPSCC 2005 (Goldstein)
- The 2005 International Pediatric Sepsis Consensus Conference (20 experts from 5 countries) adapted the adult SIRS, sepsis, severe sepsis, septic shock and organ-dysfunction definitions for children.
  - It uses age-specific vital-sign and laboratory cut-offs across newborn, neonate, infant, child and adolescent groups.
  - Paediatric SIRS requires that either the temperature or the white-count criterion be met. [[Goldstein2005IPSCC]]
- These were "first-generation" definitions, based on expert opinion. [[Goldstein2005IPSCC,Schlapbach2024Phoenix]]
- The Phoenix task force describes IPSCC-era SIRS criteria as having "poor predictive properties". [[Schlapbach2024Phoenix]]
- In 2,594 Australian and New Zealand PICU admissions with infection (5.8% died), a rise of 2 points or more meant crude mortality going from 3.1% to 6.8% for SIRS, from 1.9% to 7.6% for age-adapted SOFA and from 3.9% to 8.1% for qSOFA. Adjusted AUROC: SOFA 0.829, PELOD-2 0.816, qSOFA 0.739, SIRS 0.710. [[Schlapbach2018SOFAchildren]]
- pSOFA (Matics 2017; 8,711 PICU encounters, 2.6% mortality):
  - Maximum pSOFA AUC was 0.94.
  - Sepsis-3-style sepsis carried 12.1% mortality and septic shock 32.3%.
  - Sepsis vs infection without sepsis: OR for death 18 (95% CI 11–28). [[Matics2017pSOFA]]

### children · Phoenix Sepsis Criteria (2024)
- The SCCM Pediatric Sepsis Definition Task Force (35 experts, 6 continents) based the criteria on an international survey, a systematic review and meta-analysis, a modified Delphi process, and a new score derived from more than 3 million EHR encounters. [[Schlapbach2024Phoenix]]
- Sepsis in children (<18 years) = suspected infection + Phoenix Sepsis Score ≥2. This "indicates potentially life-threatening dysfunction of the respiratory, cardiovascular, coagulation, and/or neurological systems." [[Schlapbach2024Phoenix]]
- Septic shock = sepsis with ≥1 cardiovascular point: severe hypotension for age, blood lactate >5 mmol/L, or need for vasoactive medication. [[Schlapbach2024Phoenix]]
- In-hospital mortality (population statistics): [[Schlapbach2024Phoenix]]
  - Phoenix sepsis: 7.1% in higher-resource and 28.5% in lower-resource settings, more than 8 times that of children with suspected infection who did not meet the criteria.
  - Septic shock: 10.8% and 33.5% respectively.
- Derivation: 10 health systems (USA, Colombia, Bangladesh, China, Kenya). 3,049,699 encounters were in the development set and 581,317 in external validation.
  - Among 172,984 children with suspected infection, mortality was 1.2%.
  - AUPRC was 0.23–0.38 and AUROC 0.71–0.92.
  - Positive predictive value was higher, and sensitivity higher or similar, compared with IPSCC. [[SanchezPinto2024Phoenix]]
- **Respiratory, 0–3 points:** [[DeWitt2024phoenix]]
  - 0: PaO2:FiO2 ≥400 or SpO2:FiO2 ≥292
  - 1: PaO2:FiO2 <400 or SpO2:FiO2 <292 on any respiratory support
  - 2: PaO2:FiO2 <200 or SpO2:FiO2 <220 with invasive mechanical ventilation
  - 3: PaO2:FiO2 <100 or SpO2:FiO2 <148 with invasive mechanical ventilation
  - SpO2:FiO2 is valid only when SpO2 ≤97%.
- **Cardiovascular, 0–6 points** (the sum of three parts): [[DeWitt2024phoenix]]
  - vasoactive medications: 1 medication = 1, 2 or more = 2
  - lactate: 5–10.9 mmol/L = 1, ≥11 = 2
  - age-adjusted MAP: 1 or 2 points
- **Age-adjusted MAP thresholds** (mmHg; 1 point / 2 points): [[DeWitt2024phoenix]]
  - age <1 month: 17–30 / <17
  - 1–11 months: 25–38 / <25
  - 1 to <2 years: 31–43 / <31
  - 2 to <5 years: 32–44 / <32
  - 5 to <12 years: 36–48 / <36
  - 12–17 years: 38–51 / <38
  - Age in months, not adjusted for prematurity.
- **Coagulation, 0–2 points** (1 for each, maximum 2): platelets <100 ×10³/µL; INR >1.3; D-dimer >2 mg/L FEU; fibrinogen <100 mg/dL. [[DeWitt2024phoenix]]
- **Neurological, 0–2 points:** GCS ≤10 = 1; bilaterally fixed pupils = 2. [[DeWitt2024phoenix]]
- In the score, missing data map to zero. Vasoactive medications counted: any systemic dose of dobutamine, dopamine, epinephrine, milrinone, norepinephrine or vasopressin. [[DeWitt2024phoenix]]
- The research-only Phoenix-8 adds four systems: [[DeWitt2024phoenix]]
  - endocrine: glucose <50 or >150 mg/dL
  - immunologic: ANC <500 or ALC <1,000 cells/mm³
  - renal: age-adjusted creatinine
  - hepatic: bilirubin ≥4 mg/dL or ALT >102 IU/L
- US nine-centre validation (25,680 PICU encounters with infection, 4.6% mortality):
  - 43% met Phoenix criteria, with 9% in-hospital mortality.
  - 28% of Phoenix-positive children were IPSCC-negative. They had higher mortality than IPSCC-positive/Phoenix-negative children (4.7% vs 1.7%). [[SanchezPinto2025Validation]]

### children · Paediatric Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2020
- The panel had 49 experts from 12 organisations and issued 77 statements: 6 strong recommendations, 52 weak and 9 best-practice statements. [[Weiss2020SSCchildren]]
- Recommendation 1: "In children who present as acutely unwell, we suggest implementing systematic screening for timely recognition of septic shock and other sepsis-associated organ dysfunction" (weak recommendation, very low quality of evidence). [[Weiss2020SSCchildren]]
- On lactate, the panel "were unable to issue a recommendation about using blood lactate values to stratify children". "In our practice", lactate is often measured if it can be rapidly obtained. [[Weiss2020SSCchildren]]
- Working definitions (pre-Phoenix): septic shock = "severe infection leading to cardiovascular dysfunction (including hypotension, need for treatment with a vasoactive medication, or impaired perfusion)". Scope: from 37 weeks' gestation at birth to 18 years. [[Weiss2020SSCchildren]]
- Antimicrobial timing statements (quoted as published; detail in passes E/G):
  - septic shock: "within 1 h of recognition" (strong recommendation)
  - sepsis-associated organ dysfunction without shock: "within 3 h of recognition" (weak recommendation) [[Weiss2020SSCchildren]]

### children · NICE risk tables for under 16s (NG254, 2025; wording [2016])
- NG254 tables use "high risk" and "moderate to high risk". Anyone meeting neither is low risk. [[NICE-NG254]]
- **Under 5s, behaviour.**
  - High risk: no response to social cues; appears ill to a healthcare professional; does not wake, or does not stay awake if roused; weak, high-pitched or continuous cry.
  - Moderate to high risk: not responding normally to social cues; no smile; wakes only with prolonged stimulation; decreased activity; parent or carer concern that the child is behaving differently. [[NICE-NG254]]
- **Under 5s, respiratory.**
  - High risk: grunting; apnoea; SpO2 <90% in air or increased oxygen requirement.
  - Moderate to high risk: SpO2 <92% in air; nasal flaring.
  - Respiratory rate (high / moderate to high):
    - under 1 year: ≥60 / 50–59
    - 1–2 years: ≥50 / 40–49
    - 3–4 years: ≥40 / 35–39 [[NICE-NG254]]
- **Under 5s, circulation.**
  - High risk: heart rate <60/min.
  - Moderate to high risk: capillary refill ≥3 seconds; reduced urine output; catheterised output <1 ml/kg/h.
  - Heart rate (high / moderate to high):
    - under 1 year: ≥160 / 150–159
    - 1–2 years: ≥150 / 140–149
    - 3–4 years: ≥140 / 130–139 [[NICE-NG254]]
- **Under 5s, skin and temperature.**
  - High-risk skin: mottled or ashen; cyanosis; non-blanching petechial or purpuric rash. Moderate-to-high-risk skin: pallor.
  - Temperature: below 36°C at any age is high risk; 38°C or more under 3 months is high risk; 39°C or more at 3–6 months is moderate to high risk.
  - Moderate to high risk also includes leg pain and cold hands or feet. [[NICE-NG254]]
- **Children aged 5–11.**
  - Respiratory rate (high / moderate to high): age 5: ≥29 / 24–28; age 6–7: ≥27 / 24–26; age 8–11: ≥25 / 22–24.
  - Heart rate (high / moderate to high): age 5: ≥130 / 120–129; age 6–7: ≥120 / 110–119; age 8–11: ≥115 / 105–114.
  - High risk also: SpO2 <90% in air; heart rate <60/min. [[NICE-NG254]]
- **Young people aged 12–15:** same thresholds as the adult community table (respiratory rate ≥25 high, 21–24 moderate to high; systolic ≤90 high, 91–100 moderate to high; heart rate >130 high, 91–130 moderate to high). [[NICE-NG254]]
- NG254 1.1.8: "Consider using an early warning score to assess people under 16 with suspected sepsis, in any setting." [[NICE-NG254]]
- NG254 neonatal risk factors for early-onset infection: [[NICE-NG254]]
  - major: suspected or confirmed infection in another baby in a multiple pregnancy
  - other: invasive GBS in a previous baby, or maternal GBS colonisation, bacteriuria or infection; preterm birth after spontaneous labour before 37 weeks; confirmed rupture of membranes for more than 18 hours before a preterm birth

### children · PEWS
- EPOCH cluster RCT (21 hospitals in 7 countries; 144,539 discharges):
  - All-cause mortality: 1.93 per 1,000 discharges with Bedside PEWS against 1.56 with usual care (adjusted OR 1.01, 95% CI 0.61–1.69; P = .96).
  - Significant clinical deterioration events fell: 0.50 vs 0.84 per 1,000 patient-days (adjusted rate ratio 0.77, 95% CI 0.61–0.97). [[Parshuram2018EPOCH]]
- PEWS have "a limited evidence base", and "it is uncertain whether a unified system does deliver benefits in terms of outcomes". (Early evidence) [[Chapman2019PEWS]]

### children · Burden in children
- Fleischmann-Struzek 2018 meta-analysis (15 studies with complete data; high- and middle-income countries):
  - Children: 48 (95% CI 27–86) sepsis cases and 22 (14–33) severe sepsis cases per 100,000 person-years.
  - Mortality: 1–5% for sepsis and 9–20% for severe sepsis. [[FleischmannStruzek2018]]
- The same review estimated neonatal sepsis at 2,202 per 100,000 live births (95% CI 1,099–4,360), with mortality of 11–19%. Extrapolated worldwide, that is about 3.0 million neonatal and 1.2 million paediatric sepsis cases a year. [[FleischmannStruzek2018]]
- Fleischmann 2021 (26 studies, 2,797,879 live births, 14 countries) found:
  - Neonatal sepsis incidence of 2,824 per 100,000 live births (95% CI 1,892–4,194), with 17.6% dying.
  - 3,930 per 100,000 in 2009–2018 (four LMIC studies).
  - Higher incidence and mortality in early-onset than late-onset sepsis. [[Fleischmann2021Neonatal]]
- "Half of the 50 million people affected by sepsis globally every year are neonates and children younger than 19 years." [[Watson2024Burden]]
- GBD 2017 estimated 48.9 million (95% UI 38.9–62.9) incident sepsis cases and 11.0 million sepsis-related deaths, 19.7% of all global deaths. Detail belongs to pass A. [[Rudd2020GBD]]

### children · Neonatal sepsis: early vs late onset, GBS
- Neonatal sepsis may come from in-utero infection, maternal flora, or hospital or community acquisition. Its expression depends on timing of exposure, inoculum, immune status and pathogen virulence. [[Shane2017Neonatal]]
- Early-onset sepsis (EOS) is defined in NICHD surveillance as a pathogen in blood or CSF within 72 hours of birth. [[Stoll2020EOS]]
- In 217,480 newborns in 18 NICHD centres (2015–2017):
  - EOS incidence was 1.08 (95% CI 0.95–1.23) per 1,000 live births, and 18.47 per 1,000 at 22–28 weeks.
  - Pathogens: E. coli 36.6%, GBS 30.2%. [[Stoll2020EOS]]
- In the same cohort, 93.6% of newborns with EOS showed signs of illness within 72 h. Death occurred in 29.0% of infected preterm infants, and no term infants died. [[Stoll2020EOS]]
- In the same cohort, 53.3% of GBS cases (24 of 45) were in infants whose mothers had negative GBS screening results. [[Stoll2020EOS]]
- US population surveillance 2005–2014 (1,484 cases): [[Schrag2016EOS]]
  - All-cause EOS incidence 0.79 → 0.77 per 1,000 live births.
  - GBS fell from 0.27 to 0.22 per 1,000. E. coli was stable (0.21 → 0.18).
  - 11% of cases died.
- Late-onset sepsis is sepsis "occurring after 3 days of age". Among 6,215 VLBW infants surviving beyond 3 days: [[Stoll2002LOS]]
  - 21% had culture-proven late-onset sepsis, 48% of it coagulase-negative staphylococci.
  - Mortality was 18% in infected infants vs 7% in uninfected.
- ACOG 2020 on GBS: [[ACOG2020GBS]]
  - About 50% of colonised women transmit GBS to their newborns.
  - Without intrapartum prophylaxis, 1–2% of those newborns develop early-onset GBS disease.
  - Universal GBS screening is now recommended at 36 0/7–37 6/7 weeks.
- AAP 2019: intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis is "the only currently available effective strategy" against early-onset GBS disease, and there is no effective prevention of late-onset disease. [[Puopolo2019GBS]]
- AAP 2018: laboratory tests "alone are neither sensitive nor specific enough to guide EOS management decisions" in infants born at ≥35 weeks. [[Puopolo2018AAP]]

### children · Kaiser early-onset sepsis calculator
- Puopolo 2011 used a nested case–control design (350 cases from 608,014 births at ≥34 weeks).
  - Risk rose rapidly above a maternal intrapartum temperature of 100.5°F and increased steadily with duration of membrane rupture.
  - Intrapartum antibiotics given more than 4 h before delivery lowered risk.
  - Model c-statistic was 0.800. [[Puopolo2011EOS]]
- Escobar 2014 combined the maternal prior with the newborn's clinical status, creating three groups:
  - "treat empirically": 4.1% of births, 60.8% of EOS cases, 8.4 per 1,000
  - "observe and evaluate": 11.1% of births, 23.4% of cases, 1.2 per 1,000
  - "continued observation": 84.8% of births, 15.7% of cases, 0.11 per 1,000 [[Escobar2014EOS]]
- Kuzniewicz 2017 (204,485 infants ≥35 weeks at Kaiser Permanente Northern California), baseline vs calculator period:
  - Blood cultures fell from 14.5% to 4.9%.
  - Empirical antibiotics in the first 24 h fell from 5.0% to 2.6%.
  - Culture-confirmed EOS stayed at 0.02–0.03%, and adverse outcomes were unchanged. [[Kuzniewicz2017EOS]]
- Achten 2019 meta-analysis (13 studies, 175,752 newborns):
  - Relative risk of antibiotic use with the calculator was 56% (95% CI 53–59%) in before–after studies.
  - Missed EOS cases: 28% vs 29% (pooled OR 0.96, 95% CI 0.26–3.52).
  - Safety evidence was "limited". [[Achten2019EOS]]

### Exam hooks
- Sepsis-3 definition: life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection. [[Singer2016Sepsis3]]
- Organ dysfunction = acute SOFA rise ≥2 points, which carries in-hospital mortality >10%. [[Singer2016Sepsis3]]
- Septic shock (Sepsis-3) = vasopressors to keep MAP ≥65 mmHg plus lactate >2 mmol/L despite adequate fluids, with hospital mortality >40%. [[Singer2016Sepsis3,ShankarHari2016Shock]]
- "Severe sepsis" was dropped in 2016 as redundant. [[Singer2016Sepsis3]]
- qSOFA = RR ≥22, altered mentation, SBP ≤100. Positive is ≥2 of 3. [[Seymour2016qSOFA]]
- Outside the ICU in derivation: qSOFA AUROC 0.81 > SOFA 0.79 > SIRS 0.76. Inside the ICU, SOFA was best (0.74). [[Seymour2016qSOFA]]
- SSC 2021 recommends against qSOFA as a single screening tool, compared with SIRS, NEWS or MEWS (strong, moderate). [[Evans2021SSC]]
- qSOFA: high specificity, low sensitivity. SIRS: high sensitivity, low specificity (pooled 60.8%/72.0% vs 88.1%/25.8%). [[Fernando2018]]
- SIRS = temperature >38 or <36°C; HR >90; RR >20 or PaCO2 <32; WBC >12, <4 ×10⁹/L or >10% bands. ≥2 positive. [[Bone1992SIRS,Song2018qSOFA]]
- One in eight ICU patients with infection and organ failure is SIRS-negative. [[Kaukonen2015]]
- NEWS2 bands for sepsis risk (NICE): ≥7 high, 5–6 medium, 1–4 low, 0 very low. A single 3 needs high-priority review. [[NICE-NG253]]
- NEWS2 SpO2 scale 2 is only for confirmed hypercapnic respiratory failure. [[Verma2023NEWS2]]
- SOFA has 6 systems scored 0–4 each (maximum 24): respiratory, coagulation, liver, cardiovascular, CNS and renal. [[Lambden2019SOFA]]
- Phoenix: sepsis = suspected infection + score ≥2. Septic shock = sepsis + ≥1 cardiovascular point. The four systems are respiratory, cardiovascular, coagulation and neurological. [[Schlapbach2024Phoenix]]
- Phoenix septic-shock cardiovascular triggers: hypotension for age, lactate >5 mmol/L, or any vasoactive medication. [[Schlapbach2024Phoenix]]
- Neonatal EOS ≤72 h after birth; late-onset after 3 days. Main EOS pathogens are E. coli and GBS. [[Stoll2020EOS,Stoll2002LOS]]
- The Kaiser EOS calculator roughly halves empirical antibiotic use (RR 56%). [[Achten2019EOS]]
- NICE: absence of fever does not exclude sepsis, and temperature <36°C is itself a risk criterion. [[NICE-NG253]]
- Shock index = HR ÷ SBP. ≥0.7 is sensitive, and ≥1.0 is more specific, for severity in ED sepsis screening. [[Berger2013ShockIndex]]

### In plain words
- Sepsis is when the body's fight against an infection starts harming its own organs. [[Singer2016Sepsis3]]
- Septic shock is the most serious form: blood pressure stays very low even after fluids, and the body's cells are struggling. [[Singer2016Sepsis3]]
- People with sepsis do not always have a fever. Some feel cold, and a temperature below 36°C can be a warning sign. [[NICE-NG253]]
- Warning signs that need urgent help include: new confusion or being very hard to wake; breathing very fast; a very fast heartbeat; skin that is mottled, grey or bluish; a rash that does not fade when pressed; or passing no urine for many hours. [[NICE-NG253]]
- In babies and young children, worrying signs include not responding normally, a weak or high-pitched cry, grunting, pauses in breathing, very pale or blotchy skin, and cold hands and feet. [[NICE-NG254]]
- A baby under 3 months with a temperature of 38°C or more needs to be seen urgently. [[NICE-NG254]]
- If you are worried that someone is behaving differently from usual, say so. Doctors are told to take family and carer concerns seriously. [[NICE-NG253,NICE-NG254]]
- Hospitals use scores like NEWS2 to spot people who are getting sicker. These scores help staff decide how often to check someone. [[NICE-NG253]]
- No single score can rule sepsis in or out. Scores are tools for trained staff, not a home test. [[Evans2021SSC]]
- Older people, very young babies, people with weak immune systems and people who have just had surgery are more likely to get sepsis. [[NICE-NG253,NICE-NG254]]
- Children can get sepsis too. About half of all people with sepsis worldwide are babies and children. [[Watson2024Burden]]
- The death rates in this guide describe large groups of people. They cannot tell what will happen to any one person. [[Schlapbach2024Phoenix,Singer2016Sepsis3]]
- Sepsis is a medical emergency. If someone with an infection becomes very unwell, call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department now and ask: could this be sepsis?

## Open questions and controversies
- **qSOFA's role.**
  - Sepsis-3 promoted qSOFA as a prompt outside the ICU (AUROC 0.81) [[Seymour2016qSOFA]].
  - Later cohorts and meta-analyses found it insensitive (pooled sensitivity about 51–61%) and worse than NEWS [[Churpek2017,Fernando2018,Song2018qSOFA,Usman2019]].
  - SSC 2021 recommends against it as a single screening tool [[Evans2021SSC]].
  - Status: settled against screening use. It is still used for prognosis.
- **SIRS: discarded or still useful?**
  - Sepsis-3 removed SIRS for poor specificity [[Singer2016Sepsis3]], and one in eight patients with organ failure is SIRS-negative [[Kaukonen2015]].
  - Meta-analyses favour SIRS for sensitivity when screening [[Fernando2018,Serafim2018]].
  - Status: SIRS persists in screening tools and some quality measures.
- **NEWS2 for sepsis.**
  - NICE adopted NEWS2 bands for sepsis risk in 2024 [[NICE-NG253]], supported by ED cohorts showing NEWS beat qSOFA [[Goulden2018,Usman2019,Verma2023NEWS2]].
  - NICE itself says NEWS2 evidence in suspected sepsis "was not found" and lists it as a research priority, including the single-parameter-3 rule [[NICE-NG253]].
  - Status: widely used in the UK, with limited sepsis-specific validation.
- **NEWS2 plus procalcitonin.**
  - The PRONTO RCT (7,667 randomised; 5,453 analysed) found no change in intravenous antibiotic initiation at 3 h (48.4% vs 48.2%).
  - 28-day mortality was lower with the procalcitonin-guided algorithm (13.6% vs 16.6%), an effect the authors say needs further research.
  - NICE updated NG253 in September 2026 after reviewing procalcitonin evidence [[Todd2026PRONTO,NICE-NG253]].
  - Status: open. Biomarker detail belongs to pass E.
- **SOFA vs SOFA-2.**
  - SOFA-2 (2025) modernises thresholds and support modalities [[Ranzani2025SOFA2]], but Sepsis-3 is still defined with SOFA-1.
  - Dual scoring has been suggested [[Lobo2025SOFA2]].
  - Status: open. Whether a future Sepsis-4 adopts SOFA-2 is unknown.
- **Electronic alerts.**
  - A widely used proprietary model performed poorly in external validation [[Wong2021ESM]].
  - Another system was associated with lower mortality when alerts were acted on quickly [[Adams2022TREWS]].
  - Status: Early evidence, with no large RCTs.
- **Phoenix criteria.**
  - They outperform IPSCC in derivation and US validation [[SanchezPinto2024Phoenix,SanchezPinto2025Validation]], but were built on retrospective EHR data and need lab tests that are not always available.
  - Phoenix was designed to identify sepsis, not to screen for early sepsis [[Schlapbach2024Phoenix]].
  - Status: being adopted. Prospective validation is ongoing.
- **PEWS.**
  - It reduced deterioration events but not mortality in EPOCH [[Parshuram2018EPOCH]], and the evidence base remains limited [[Chapman2019PEWS]].
- **Kaiser EOS calculator.**
  - It roughly halves antibiotic exposure, but safety data are limited (only 46 missed-case events pooled) [[Achten2019EOS]].
  - GBS cases still occur after negative maternal screens [[Stoll2020EOS]].

  - Lambden2019SOFA (SOFA table)
  - DeWitt2024phoenix (Phoenix table)

- **Records found without abstracts.** No abstract exists in PubMed for Vincent1996SOFA, Bone1992SIRS (abstract without thresholds) or Gildea2024NICE. They are cited only for content confirmed elsewhere (SOFA table via Lambden; SIRS thresholds via Song) or for their existence and date.

  - CDC "Get Ahead of Sepsis" signs page: Access Denied.
  - UK Sepsis Trust: no response.
  - JAMA full texts for Singer, Seymour, Shankar-Hari and the Phoenix consensus paper: only abstracts were in PMC XML.
