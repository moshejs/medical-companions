## Findings

### immune · Recognising the invader: PAMPs, DAMPs and receptors
- Sepsis-3 defines sepsis as "life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection". In other words, the harm comes from the body's own response, not only from the germ (Established) [[Singer2016Sepsis3]].
- Sepsis was long defined as infection with fever or low temperature, fast heart rate, fast breathing and white-cell changes. It is now seen as a dysregulated inflammatory and immune response that injures organs [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]].
- Pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs, molecules found on microbes) and damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs, molecules released from injured host cells) are detected by many sensors working in parallel and overlapping. These include complement, Toll-like receptors (TLRs), NOD-like receptors, RIG-like receptors, mannose-binding lectin and scavenger receptors [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]].
- At least ten different human TLRs are known. They often work as pairs of the same or different TLRs [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]].
- TLR4 is the receptor for lipopolysaccharide (LPS, endotoxin, from the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria). Mice with Tlr4 mutations do not respond to endotoxin but are highly susceptible to Gram-negative infection (Established) [[Poltorak1998]].
- TLR2 senses lipoteichoic acid and peptidoglycan, the main stimulating parts of Gram-positive bacteria. It then activates NF-κB; TLR1 and TLR4 did not respond to these molecules in the same experiment [[Schwandner1999]].
- Sensor signalling leads to MAP kinase, JAK and STAT activation and to NF-κB moving into the nucleus. NF-κB then switches on "early activation genes", including TNF, IL-1, IL-12, IL-18 and type I interferons [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]].
- The NLRP3 inflammasome is a protein complex inside the cell that reacts to pathogen and damage signals. Its activation uses caspase-1 to mature IL-1β, IL-18 and gasdermin D, which leads to pyroptosis (inflammatory cell death) [[Neumann2026NLRP3]].
- Too much inflammasome activity is linked to hyperinflammation, endothelial dysfunction, immunothrombosis and multi-organ failure. Too little, or an exhausted response, may weaken microbial clearance and invite secondary infections (Early evidence) [[Neumann2026NLRP3]].
- Mitochondria descend from bacteria. When injury releases mitochondrial DAMPs (formyl peptides and mitochondrial DNA), they activate human neutrophils through formyl peptide receptor-1 and TLR9 and cause a sepsis-like state [[Zhang2010mtDAMPs]].
- HMGB1 (then called HMG-1) is a late mediator. Macrophages released it more than 8 hours after stimulation, and serum levels in mice rose from 8 to 32 hours after endotoxin. Giving anti-HMGB1 antibodies late still reduced deaths in mice, and septic patients who died had higher serum HMGB1 [[Wang1999HMGB1]].
- Extracellular histones damage endothelium and are lethal in mice. Activated protein C cleaves them, and anti-histone antibody reduced mortality in LPS, TNF and caecal ligation-and-puncture models [[Xu2009Histones]].

### immune · Cytokines: the early storm and why it is short
- In 13 healthy men given IV E. coli endotoxin (4 ng/kg), plasma TNF rose 90 to 180 minutes later to a mean peak of 240 ± 70 pg/mL, against 35 ± 5 pg/mL after saline. IL-1β and interferon-γ did not change, so the authors described "a brief pulse" of TNF [[Michie1988]].
- In the same study, ibuprofen pretreatment did not stop the TNF rise (peak 170 ± 70 pg/mL) but greatly reduced symptoms. This points to the cyclooxygenase pathway as the route for many of TNF's effects [[Michie1988]].
- Baboons immunised against TNF (cachectin) 2 hours before a lethal E. coli infusion were completely protected against shock, organ dysfunction and death. Antibody given only 1 hour before protected against shock but not organ failure [[Tracey1987]].
- GenIMS followed 1886 people admitted with community-acquired pneumonia. 583 (31%) developed severe sepsis and 149 of those (26%) died [[Kellum2007GenIMS]].
- In GenIMS, systemic cytokines (TNF, IL-6, IL-10) were raised in 82% of people with pneumonia. They peaked at presentation and stayed raised for over a week [[Kellum2007GenIMS]].
- In GenIMS, the highest risk of death came with high pro-inflammatory IL-6 together with high anti-inflammatory IL-10 (hazard ratio 20.5; 95% CI 10.8–39.0). "Unbalanced" patterns (one high, one low) were uncommon (4.6%) [[Kellum2007GenIMS]].
- In mouse sepsis, pro-inflammatory (IL-6, TNF, IL-1β) and anti-inflammatory (soluble TNF receptors, IL-10, IL-1 receptor antagonist) mediators rose at the same time in animals that died early (days 1–5). This challenges the older model of a pro-inflammatory phase (SIRS) followed by an anti-inflammatory phase (CARS) (Early evidence) [[Osuchowski2006]].
- Complement activation is a hallmark of sepsis and starts as soon as PAMPs and DAMPs appear. C5a is among the most active inflammatory peptides produced in sepsis and is a potent chemoattractant for neutrophils, monocytes and macrophages [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]].
- Excessive C5a can paralyse innate immune functions of phagocytes. This is described as "the dark side of C5a" (Early evidence, mostly animal data) [[Ward2004C5a]].

### immune · Neutrophils, NETs and immunothrombosis
- Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) are fibres of chromatin and granule proteins released by activated neutrophils. They bind Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, degrade virulence factors and kill bacteria [[Brinkmann2004]].
- Platelet TLR4 senses TLR4 ligands in blood and makes platelets bind to neutrophils, triggering NETs. Plasma from people with severe sepsis did the same. NETs trapped bacteria mainly in liver sinusoids and lung capillaries [[Clark2007NETs]].
- Sepsis causes a hypercoagulable state with microvascular clots, fibrin deposits, NET formation and endothelial injury [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]].
- Activated neutrophils expel NETs that bind TLRs and drive inflammation. Activated monocytes express tissue factor and phosphatidylserine, which start the clotting cascades: "thromboinflammation" [[Iba2023DIC]].

### immune · Hyperinflammation and immunosuppression at the same time
- After the early hyperinflammation, many patients enter a state of immunosuppression ("immunoparalysis"), and biomarker-guided immunotherapy timed to the immune phase has been proposed (Early evidence) [[Hotchkiss2013NRI]].
- Sepsis is now described as sustained excessive inflammation together with immune suppression that fails to return to balance [[vanderPoll2017]].
- Persistent inflammation, immunosuppression and catabolism syndrome (PICS) is a later state with high CRP, neutrophilia and immature myeloid cells. It is thought to be driven by DAMPs from injured tissues, such as mitochondrial DNA, histones and HMGB1 (Hypothesis) [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]].
- Monocytes from septic patients typically make less TNF, IL-1α, IL-6 and IL-12 when stimulated, while IL-1 receptor antagonist and IL-10 release is preserved. This resembles "endotoxin tolerance" [[Hotchkiss2013NRI]].
- Decreased natural killer cell interferon-γ production often comes before cytomegalovirus reactivation in patients with protracted sepsis [[Hotchkiss2013NRI]].
- Boomer et al. took spleen and lung tissue at the bedside right after death from 40 patients who died with active severe sepsis (2009–2011). Their stimulated splenocytes secreted far less cytokine than controls at 5 hours (all P < .001):
  - TNF 418 vs 5361 pg/mL
  - interferon-γ 37.5 vs 1374 pg/mL
  - IL-6 365 vs 3691 pg/mL
  - IL-10 58 vs 633 pg/mL [[Boomer2011]]
- In that post-mortem series, cytokine secretion in sepsis was generally less than 10% of controls, whatever the age, duration of sepsis, steroid use or nutrition. Spleens showed extensive loss of CD4, CD8 and HLA-DR cells [[Boomer2011]].
- In 20 patients who died of sepsis and multiple organ dysfunction, at least focal apoptosis was seen in 56.3% of spleens, 47.1% of colons and 27.7% of ileums. Marked lymphocytopenia was present in 15 of 19 [[Hotchkiss1999]].
- Active caspase-3 staining was markedly higher in septic than non-septic patients (p < .01). Over 25–50% of cells were positive focally in the splenic white pulp of 6 septic patients and none of the non-septic ones [[Hotchkiss1999]].
- Lymphocyte apoptosis is directly immunosuppressive, and persistent lymphopenia predicts death in sepsis [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]].
- Drewry et al. studied 335 adults with bacteraemia and sepsis; 22.7% died within 28 days. At sepsis onset, lymphocyte counts were similar (median 0.7 vs 0.6 × 10³/μL). By day 4, survivors' counts had risen to 1.1 vs 0.7 × 10³/μL in non-survivors (P < 0.0001) [[Drewry2014]].
- A higher day-4 lymphocyte count was independently linked to better survival: adjusted OR 0.68 (95% CI 0.51–0.91) for 28-day survival and 0.74 (0.59–0.93) for 1-year survival. Severe persistent lymphopenia (≤ 0.6 × 10³/μL on day 4) was linked to more secondary infections [[Drewry2014]].
- Antigen-presenting cells lose HLA-DR (an MHC class II molecule needed to show antigens to T cells) in sepsis. Failure of monocytes to recover HLA-DR predicts poor outcome [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]].
- Monneret et al. studied 93 people with septic shock. Monocyte HLA-DR (mHLA-DR) did not differ on days 1–2. By days 3–4, survivors had recovered (43% HLA-DR-positive monocytes) but non-survivors had not (18%; p < 0.001) [[Monneret2006]].
- In that study, mHLA-DR below 30% on days 3–4 independently predicted death: adjusted OR 6.48 (95% CI 1.62–25.93) [[Monneret2006]].
- T cells raise PD-1 and stromal cells raise PD-L1 in sepsis, which expands regulatory T cells and anergic (unresponsive) T cells. Myeloid-derived suppressor cells also increase [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]].
- Patients now survive the early phase more often and sepsis has become more of a chronic critical illness. It often brings prolonged inflammation, immune suppression, organ injury and muscle wasting [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]].

### immune · Why anti-inflammatory and anti-endotoxin trials failed
- More than 100 randomised trials have tested whether modulating the septic response improves survival. "With one short-lived exception" (drotrecogin alfa), none produced a new treatment [[Marshall2014]].
- Anti-TNF, TNFR:Fc (etanercept-type fusion protein; 141 people with septic shock): 28-day mortality rose with dose: placebo 30%, low dose 30%, middle dose 48%, high dose 53% (P = 0.02 for dose-response) [[Fisher1996TNFR]].
- Anti-TNF, p55-IgG (498 people): no overall mortality benefit (P = .30). In a subgroup with severe sepsis or early shock (n = 247), mortality was 23% with the higher dose vs 36% with placebo (P = .07), a subgroup signal that was not confirmed [[Abraham1997p55]].
- IL-1 receptor antagonist (anakinra-type rhIL-1ra; 696 people): 28-day mortality was 33.1% vs 36.4% with placebo (p = .36). The trial was stopped at interim analysis [[Opal1997IL1ra]].
- HA-1A (anti-endotoxin IgM), 1991: in 200 people with proven Gram-negative bacteraemia, mortality was 30% vs 49% with placebo (P = 0.014). In all 543 people treated, it was 39% vs 43% (P = 0.24) [[Ziegler1991HA1A]].
- HA-1A confirmatory trial (CHESS, 2199 enrolled): no benefit in Gram-negative bacteraemia and shock (14-day mortality 33% vs 32%). Without Gram-negative bacteraemia it trended worse: 41% vs 37% with placebo (one-tailed P = 0.073) [[McCloskey1994CHESS]].
- Eritoran (a TLR4–MD2 antagonist that blocks LPS) in ACCESS (1961 people with severe sepsis, 197 ICUs): 28-day mortality was 28.1% vs 26.9% with placebo (HR 1.05; 95% CI 0.88–1.26). 1-year mortality was 44.1% vs 43.3% [[Opal2013ACCESS]].
- Polymyxin B haemoperfusion (endotoxin removal) in EUPHRATES (450 people with septic shock and endotoxin activity ≥ 0.60): 28-day mortality was 37.7% vs 34.5% with sham (RR 1.09; 95% CI 0.85–1.39) [[Dellinger2018EUPHRATES]].
- The Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2021 "suggest[s] against using polymyxin B haemoperfusion" (weak recommendation, low-quality evidence). It found insufficient evidence to recommend other blood purification techniques [[Evans2021SSC]].
- Tigris (2026) tested polymyxin B in a narrower group with endotoxin activity 0.60–0.89 and multi-organ dysfunction (157 enrolled, 2:1 randomisation, open label, Bayesian design using a EUPHRATES-subgroup prior). 28-day mortality was 39% vs 45%, a posterior probability of benefit of 95.3% (adjusted OR 0.67; 95% credible interval 0.39–1.08). For 90-day mortality the posterior probability of benefit was 99.4% (Early evidence) [[Neyra2026Tigris,NCT03901807]].
- Non-selective nitric oxide synthase inhibitor 546C88 (797 people with septic shock): day-28 mortality was 59% vs 49% with placebo (p < .001), with excess cardiovascular events. The trial was stopped early [[Lopez2004NOS]].
- Drotrecogin alfa (activated), PROWESS 2001 (1690 people): 28-day mortality was 24.7% vs 30.8% with placebo, a 6.1% absolute reduction (P = 0.005). Serious bleeding was 3.5% vs 2.0% [[Bernard2001PROWESS]].
- Drotrecogin alfa, PROWESS-SHOCK 2012 (1697 people with septic shock): 28-day mortality was 26.4% vs 24.2% (RR 1.09; 95% CI 0.92–1.28). 90-day mortality was 34.1% vs 32.7% [[Ranieri2012PROWESSSHOCK]].
- Drotrecogin alfa (Xigris) was withdrawn in 2011. It had first been approved after a single trial, PROWESS, that was stopped early for efficacy, and the manufacturer withdrew it worldwide after PROWESS-SHOCK failed [[MedLett2011Xigris,Angus2012Drotrecogin]].
- High-dose antithrombin (KyberSept; 2314 people): 28-day mortality was 38.9% vs 38.7% (P = .94). Bleeding with concomitant heparin was 23.8% vs 13.5% (P < .001) [[Warren2001KyberSept]].
- Recombinant thrombomodulin (SCARLET; 800 people with sepsis-associated coagulopathy): 28-day mortality was 26.8% vs 29.4% (P = .32) [[Vincent2019SCARLET]].
- Thymosin α1 (TESTS; 1089 people analysed): 28-day mortality was 23.4% vs 24.1% (HR 0.99; 95% CI 0.77–1.27) [[Wu2025TESTS]].
- Explanations offered: the response is complex and redundant, the same trigger can do good and harm, and trials enrolled unselected patients. Proposed fixes are large epidemiological studies and stratifying patients to target those likely to benefit [[Marshall2014,Angus2012Drotrecogin]].
- Animal models may mislead. Animals in classic models die of shock within 72 hours, while many patients die later with opportunistic infections and low monocyte HLA-DR [[Docke1997]].

### endothelium · Glycocalyx, capillary leak and vasodilation
- Sepsis affects almost every endothelial function (vessel tone, barrier, inflammation, clotting). Endothelial dysfunction is thought to be the key step from sepsis to organ failure [[Ince2016Endothelium]].
- The glycocalyx is a gel-like layer lining the inside of blood vessels, about 0.5–5.0 μm thick in humans. It is made of proteoglycans (syndecan-1 is the most studied), glycosaminoglycans (heparan sulfate is over 50% of them) and plasma proteins [[Uchimido2019]].
- The glycocalyx controls vessel permeability and tone, prevents microvascular thrombosis and regulates white-cell adhesion [[Uchimido2019]].
- In sepsis, enzymes shed the glycocalyx: metalloproteinases, heparanase and hyaluronidase, activated by reactive oxygen species, TNF-α and IL-1β. Shedding causes leaky vessels, unregulated vasodilation, microthrombi and more leukocyte adhesion [[Uchimido2019]].
- In 13 healthy volunteers given low-dose IV endotoxin, sublingual glycocalyx thickness fell from 0.60 ± 0.1 μm to 0.30 ± 0.1 μm (p < 0.01) as plasma hyaluronan rose. This is the Nieuwdorp study as summarised in the review [[Uchimido2019]].
- Over-aggressive fluid therapy may worsen glycocalyx degradation, while plasma and albumin may lessen it. These effects are not yet well understood (Hypothesis) [[Uchimido2019]].
- In a meta-analysis (11 studies, 2318 patients), baseline syndecan-1 was higher in people who died (SMD 0.53; 95% CI 0.40–0.67), in septic shock (SMD 0.81) and in acute kidney injury (SMD 0.48) [[Sun2022SDC1]].
- Pro-inflammatory proteases make endothelial cells internalise VE-cadherin and lose tight junctions, raising vascular permeability. Leaky capillaries lose large amounts of protein and plasma into the tissues, causing oedema [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]].
- Diffuse vasodilation throughout the microcirculation causes distributive shock, in which blood pressure may not recover with fluids alone [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]].
- Upregulation of inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) may link inflammation to cardiovascular collapse. Nitrosative stress is a major part of sepsis pathophysiology [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]].
- Vasodilatory shock (vasoplegia, failure of vessels to constrict) is classically attributed to nitric oxide, activation of ATP-sensitive potassium channels in vascular smooth muscle and relative vasopressin deficiency (Established; review framing) [[Landry2001]].
- Blocking nitric oxide production does not help: the non-selective NOS inhibitor 546C88 raised blood pressure but increased mortality (59% vs 49%) [[Lopez2004NOS]].

### endothelium · Microcirculation and cellular energy
- De Backer et al. (2002) used sublingual orthogonal polarisation spectral imaging, filming the tiny vessels under the tongue. In 50 people with severe sepsis, perfused small vessels (< 20 μm) were 48% vs 90% in healthy volunteers (p < 0.001), and vessel density was lower. Topical acetylcholine fully reversed the changes [[DeBacker2002]].
- In that study, microvascular changes were more severe in people who died, while ICU patients without sepsis and pre-cardiac-surgery patients looked like volunteers [[DeBacker2002]].
- Sakr et al. filmed 46 people with septic shock daily. At shock onset, perfused small vessels were similar in survivors and non-survivors (65.0% vs 58.4%). They improved over time only in survivors [[Sakr2004]].
- Patients who later died of multiple organ failure after shock had resolved still had fewer perfused small vessels than survivors (57.4% vs 79.3%; p = .02), despite similar blood pressure and vasopressor use [[Sakr2004]].
- Cytopathic hypoxia is reduced oxygen use caused by an acquired defect in cellular respiration rather than by poor oxygen delivery. Proposed causes include nitric oxide inhibiting cytochrome a,a3, peroxynitrite damage to respiratory complexes and NAD+ depletion via PARP-1 (Hypothesis) [[Fink2002]].
- Brealey et al. took muscle biopsies within 24 hours of ICU admission in 28 people with sepsis. ATP was significantly lower in the 12 who died than in the 16 survivors (p = 0.0003) [[Brealey2002]].
- In the same biopsies, mitochondrial complex I activity fell as noradrenaline requirement and nitrite/nitrate (nitric oxide markers) rose. Over 90% of the body's oxygen use goes to mitochondrial ATP production [[Brealey2002]].
- A "unified theory" of septic acute kidney injury proposes that inflammation, microvascular dysfunction and a mostly adaptive, mitochondria-driven tubular-cell response explain it. Septic AKI can occur with normal or even increased renal blood flow (Hypothesis) [[Gomez2014]].

### endothelium · Lactate is not only a sign of low oxygen
- Sepsis-3 identifies septic shock by vasopressors needed to keep mean arterial pressure ≥ 65 mm Hg plus lactate > 2 mmol/L (> 18 mg/dL) without hypovolaemia. That combination carried hospital mortality > 40% [[Singer2016Sepsis3]].
- James et al. (1999) proposed that high lactate in injury or sepsis often comes from aerobic glycolysis in skeletal muscle. In this account, adrenaline drives the Na⁺/K⁺ pump, rather than hypoperfusion causing anaerobic glycolysis (Hypothesis, since supported) [[James1999Lactate]].
- Levy et al. placed microdialysis probes in thigh muscle in 14 people with septic shock (mean blood lactate 4.0 mmol/L). Muscle lactate exceeded arterial lactate by a mean 1.98 mmol/L. Ouabain, a Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase blocker, stopped the excess muscle lactate and pyruvate production (p = 0.0001) [[Levy2005Lactate]].
- In that study, the lactate-to-pyruvate ratio stayed unchanged. The authors argue that hyperlactataemia in resuscitated septic patients should not be taken as proof of oxygen debt [[Levy2005Lactate]].
- A 2014 review concluded that sepsis-associated hyperlactataemia is better explained by stress-driven (adrenergic) aerobic glycolysis than by tissue hypoxia alone. It may even be an adaptive survival response, yet it remains a strong independent predictor of death (Established as a marker; mechanism debated) [[GarciaAlvarez2014]].

### endothelium · Coagulation: tissue factor, consumed anticoagulants, DIC
- Severe sepsis is almost always accompanied by systemic activation of coagulation. Tissue factor on activated monocytes and endothelial cells starts thrombin generation, and tissue factor pathway inhibitor does not keep up [[Levi2017Coag]].
- At the same time, the protein C system is impaired by cytokines, and fibrinolysis (clot breakdown) is blocked mainly by raised plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1). More fibrin plus less clearance means microvascular clots, tissue ischaemia and organ dysfunction [[Levi2017Coag]].
- Natural anticoagulants fall: antithrombin and activated protein C decrease, as does endothelial heparan sulfate. Reduced thrombomodulin and endothelial protein C receptor lower activated protein C further [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]].
- Antithrombin activity is significantly reduced in SIC and DIC but is usually maintained in thrombotic microangiopathies (TTP, aHUS), which helps tell them apart [[Iba2023DIC]].
- The ISTH overt DIC score (2001), as tabulated in Iba 2023, gives points for four items. A total ≥ 5 is compatible with overt DIC [[Taylor2001DIC,Iba2023DIC]]:
  - platelet count: < 50 × 10⁹/L = 2; 50 to < 100 = 1
  - fibrin-related marker (e.g. D-dimer, FDP): strong increase = 3; moderate increase = 2
  - PT prolongation: ≥ 6 s = 2; 3 to < 6 s = 1
  - fibrinogen: < 1 g/L (100 mg/dL) = 1
- Sepsis-induced coagulopathy (SIC) was derived from 1498 Japanese patients with sepsis and coagulopathy. It scores three items, each 0, 1 or 2 points [[Iba2017SIC]]:
  - PT-INR: ≤ 1.2 = 0; > 1.2 = 1; > 1.4 = 2
  - platelets: ≥ 150 × 10⁹/L = 0; < 150 = 1; < 100 = 2
  - "total SOFA" (sum of respiratory, cardiovascular, hepatic and renal SOFA, capped at 2): 0, 1 or ≥ 2
- SIC is diagnosed when the total is ≥ 4 and the PT plus platelet points exceed 2 [[Iba2017SIC]].
- In the derivation cohort, 28-day mortality was 38.4% with SIC vs 26.5% without. The ISTH adopted SIC criteria in 2019 to detect an earlier, compensated stage before overt DIC [[Iba2017SIC,Iba2019SIC,Iba2023DIC]].
- How common DIC looks depends on the definition. In one Sepsis-3 cohort of 296 people summarised by Iba 2023 (prevalence; 28-day mortality):
  - overt DIC: 22.6%; 55.2%
  - modified JAAM DIC: 43.2%; 47.7%
  - SIC: 56.1%; 44.0% [[Iba2023DIC]]
- Purpura fulminans is rare but life-threatening: haemorrhagic skin infarction from DIC and clots in the small skin vessels. It is linked to depletion of protein C. Meningococcal sepsis is the commonest cause, then pneumococcal sepsis in adults. Mortality is over 50%, and amputation or skin grafting is often needed [[Betrosian2006]].
- Anticoagulant trials in unselected sepsis have failed (antithrombin, thrombomodulin, activated protein C). Reviewers attribute this partly to enrolling patients who were not coagulopathic [[Warren2001KyberSept,Vincent2019SCARLET,Iba2023DIC]].

### immune · Organ dysfunction system by system (matching SOFA)
- SOFA scores 6 organ systems, each from 0 (no dysfunction) to 4 (failure), for a total of 0–24 [[Vincent1996SOFA,GiamarellosBourboulis2026ImmunoSep]].
- In the first multicentre SOFA study (1449 ICU patients in 40 ICUs), total SOFA rose in 44% of non-survivors vs 20% of survivors staying at least a week. It fell in 33% of survivors vs 21% of non-survivors (p < .001) [[Vincent1998SOFA]].
- Sepsis-3 operationalises organ dysfunction as an acute rise in SOFA of ≥ 2 points, linked to in-hospital mortality > 10% [[Singer2016Sepsis3]].
- Six organ systems can be monitored in practice: cardiovascular (including the microcirculation), respiratory, renal, neurological, haematological and hepatic. Others, such as the gut, are harder to assess [[Lelubre2018]].
- Lungs (ARDS, Berlin 2012), graded by PaO2/FiO2 [[ARDS2012Berlin]]:
  - mild: 200 < PaO2/FiO2 ≤ 300 mm Hg; mortality 27%
  - moderate: 100 < PaO2/FiO2 ≤ 200; mortality 32%
  - severe: ≤ 100; mortality 45%
- In LUNG SAFE (459 ICUs, 50 countries), ARDS made up 10.4% of ICU admissions and 23.4% of ventilated patients. Clinicians recognised it in only 51.3% of mild cases and 78.5% of severe cases [[Bellani2016LUNGSAFE]].
- ARDS can be split into a hyperinflammatory subphenotype (more sepsis, vasopressors and acidosis, higher mortality) and a hypoinflammatory one. In the ALVEOLI trial the effect of PEEP strategy differed by subphenotype (Early evidence) [[Calfee2014]].
- Kidney: in 29,269 critically ill patients across 23 countries, 5.7% had acute renal failure. Septic shock was the most common contributing factor (47.5%), and hospital mortality was 60.3% [[Uchino2005]].
- Sepsis-associated acute kidney injury is one of the commonest organ injuries in sepsis and adds to both short- and long-term harm [[Poston2019]].
- Brain: sepsis-associated encephalopathy is diffuse brain dysfunction from infection elsewhere in the body, ranging from mild delirium to deep coma. It occurs in up to 70% of people with severe systemic infection, and mortality is almost 70% in severe cases, nearly always from multi-organ failure [[Gofton2012]].
- In 50 people with severe sepsis, mortality rose as Glasgow Coma Score fell [[Eidelman1996]]:
  - GCS 15: 16%
  - GCS 13–14: 20%
  - GCS 9–12: 50%
  - GCS 3–8: 63%
- Heart: in 20 people with septic shock, all had normal or high cardiac index, yet 10 had ejection fraction below 0.40. Paradoxically, 10 of 13 survivors but none of 7 non-survivors had an initial EF < 0.40 [[Parker1984]].
- Survivors' mean initial EF was 0.32 with dilated ventricles and normal stroke volume. EF and volumes returned to normal by about 10 days after shock began ("profound but reversible myocardial depression") [[Parker1984]].
- Liver: in 38,036 ICU admissions, early hepatic dysfunction (bilirubin > 2 mg/dL within 48 h) occurred in 10.9%. Hospital mortality was 30.4% vs 16.4%, and the independent mortality risk (OR 1.86) exceeded that of all other organ dysfunctions [[Kramer2007]].
- The liver both defends against sepsis (bacterial clearance, acute-phase proteins) and is injured by it (hypoxic hepatitis, cholestasis, drug toxicity, secondary sclerosing cholangitis) [[Strnad2017]].
- Platelets and bleeding: in 329 ICU patients, 41.3% had a platelet count < 150 × 10⁹/L, with ICU mortality crude OR 5.0 [[Vanderschueren2000]].
- Bleeding rose with lower platelet counts: 4.1% without thrombocytopenia, 21.4% at 101–149 × 10⁹/L and 52.6% below 100 × 10⁹/L. A fall to ≤ 50% of the admission count carried OR 6.0 for death [[Vanderschueren2000]].
- Mechanisms behind organ failure include immune suppression, mitochondrial dysfunction, altered cell death, organ–organ crosstalk (for example kidney and gut) and microcirculatory failure [[Lelubre2018]].

### frontier · Heterogeneity: phenotypes and endotypes
- Clinical phenotypes (Seymour 2019): k-means clustering of 29 routine variables in 20,189 people with Sepsis-3 sepsis gave four groups (Early evidence) [[Seymour2019Phenotypes]]:
  - α (33%): least vasopressor use
  - β (27%): older, more chronic illness and kidney dysfunction
  - γ (27%): more inflammation and lung dysfunction
  - δ (13%): more liver dysfunction and septic shock
- Mortality at 28 days differed across the phenotypes: α 5%, β 13%, γ 24%, δ 40% [[Seymour2019Phenotypes]].
- In simulations, changing the phenotype mix of an early goal-directed therapy trial swung results from a > 33% chance of benefit to a > 60% chance of harm [[Seymour2019Phenotypes]].
- Transcriptomic endotypes (Davenport 2016): white-cell gene expression in community-acquired pneumonia sepsis defined SRS1 (41% of the discovery cohort; immunosuppressed, with endotoxin tolerance, T-cell exhaustion and HLA class II loss) and SRS2. SRS1 had higher 14-day mortality (HR 2.4; validation HR 2.8), and a 7-gene set classifies patients (Early evidence) [[Davenport2016]].
- In a post-hoc analysis of the VANISH trial (176 people), hydrocortisone was linked to higher mortality in the immunocompetent SRS2 endotype (OR 7.9; 95% CI 1.6–39.9; interaction P = 0.02) (Early evidence; post hoc) [[Antcliffe2019]].
- MARS endotypes (Scicluna 2017) were Mars1–4. Mars1 had the worst outcome (39% 28-day mortality; HR 1.86) and was the only endotype consistently linked to death across cohorts (Early evidence) [[Scicluna2017]].
- Macrophage activation-like syndrome (MALS) was found in 3.7–4.3% of more than 5000 people with infection and SIRS. Ferritin > 4420 ng/mL carried about 66–67% 28-day mortality [[Kyriazopoulou2017MALS]].

### frontier · Precision immunotherapy
- Anakinra reanalysis (Shakoory 2016) of the earlier IL-1ra trial (763 adults): 43 people (5.6%) had both hepatobiliary dysfunction and DIC, as features of macrophage activation syndrome. In them, 28-day survival was 65.4% with anakinra vs 35.3% with placebo (HR for death 0.28; 95% CI 0.11–0.71) (Early evidence; post hoc subgroup) [[Shakoory2016]].
- Interferon-γ given to septic patients with low monocyte HLA-DR restored HLA-DR and TNF production, with clearance of sepsis in 8 of 9 patients (Early evidence; uncontrolled) [[Docke1997]].
- GM-CSF guided by mHLA-DR (38 people with mHLA-DR < 8000 antibodies per cell): mHLA-DR normalised in 19 of 19 treated vs 3 of 19 controls. Mechanical ventilation was shorter (148 vs 207 h; P = 0.04) (Early evidence) [[Meisel2009]].
- PROVIDE (240 people) classified patients by ferritin > 4420 ng/mL and monocyte HLA-DR < 5000 receptors per cell: MALS 20.0%, immunoparalysis 42.9%, intermediate 37.1%. Survival with a falling SOFA score at day 7 was 42.9% with matched immunotherapy vs 10.0% with placebo (p = 0.042) (Early evidence) [[Leventogiannis2022PROVIDE]].
- ImmunoSep (JAMA 2026; 276 analysed; 6 countries): IV anakinra for MALS, or subcutaneous interferon-γ for immunoparalysis, vs placebo. The primary end point (SOFA fall of ≥ 1.4 points by day 9) was met by 35.1% vs 17.9% (difference 17.2%; 95% CI 6.8–27.2; P = .002) (Early evidence; phase 2) [[GiamarellosBourboulis2026ImmunoSep,Kotsaki2022ImmunoSep,NCT04990232]].
- In ImmunoSep, 28-day mortality did not differ significantly. Anaemia was more frequent with anakinra and haemorrhage with interferon-γ [[GiamarellosBourboulis2026ImmunoSep]].
- Anti-PD-1 (nivolumab) phase 1b (31 people with lymphopenia ≤ 1.1 × 10³/μL): no "cytokine storm" or unexpected safety findings, and receptor occupancy stayed > 90% for ≥ 28 days. Twelve deaths occurred (40% and 37.5% in the two dose arms) (Early evidence; safety study) [[Hotchkiss2019Nivolumab]].
- IL-7 (CYT107) in IRIS-7 (27 people with septic shock and severe lymphopenia) caused a 3- to 4-fold rise in lymphocyte, CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ counts lasting weeks, without worsening inflammation (Early evidence) [[Francois2018IRIS7]].
- The Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2021 suggests against IV immunoglobulins for adults with sepsis or septic shock (weak recommendation, low-quality evidence) [[Evans2021SSC]].

### frontier · Metabolic and haemodynamic adjuncts
- CITRIS-ALI (167 people with sepsis and ARDS; IV vitamin C 50 mg/kg every 6 h for 96 h): no difference in the change in modified SOFA (3 vs 3.5 points; difference −0.10; 95% CI −1.23 to 1.03), CRP or thrombomodulin [[Fowler2019CITRISALI]].
- VITAMINS (216 people with septic shock): vitamin C, hydrocortisone and thiamine vs hydrocortisone alone gave the same time alive and vasopressor-free to day 7 (122.1 vs 124.6 h). 90-day mortality was 28.6% vs 24.5% (HR 1.18; 95% CI 0.69–2.00) [[Fujii2020VITAMINS]].
- VICTAS (501 people; stopped early for administrative reasons): ventilator- and vasopressor-free days were a median of 25 vs 26. 30-day mortality was 22% vs 24% [[Sevransky2021VICTAS]].
- LOVIT (872 people on vasopressors; vitamin C 50 mg/kg every 6 h up to 96 h): death or persistent organ dysfunction at day 28 was 44.5% vs 38.5% with placebo (RR 1.21; 95% CI 1.04–1.40; P = 0.01). This is a harm signal [[Lamontagne2022LOVIT]].
- The Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2021: "we suggest against using IV vitamin C" for adults with sepsis or septic shock (weak recommendation, low-quality evidence) [[Evans2021SSC]].
- Angiotensin II (ATHOS-3; 321 people with vasodilatory shock on > 0.2 μg/kg/min noradrenaline-equivalent): MAP response at 3 h was 69.9% vs 23.4% (OR 7.95). Day-28 mortality was 46% vs 54% (HR 0.78; 95% CI 0.57–1.07; P = 0.12, not significant) [[Khanna2017ATHOS3]].
- Esmolol (Morelli 2013; single-centre, open-label, 154 people with septic shock and heart rate ≥ 95/min): heart rate held at 80–94/min. 28-day mortality was 49.4% vs 80.5% (adjusted HR 0.39). The authors called for further investigation (Early evidence) [[Morelli2013Esmolol]].
- Landiolol (STRESS-L 2023; 126 of a planned 340 enrolled, stopped for possible harm): mean SOFA 8.8 vs 8.1. Mortality was 37.1% vs 25.4% at day 28 and 43.5% vs 28.6% at day 90, neither statistically significant [[Whitehouse2023STRESSL]].

### frontier · Rapid diagnostics and AI early warning
- Molecular rapid diagnostic tests for bloodstream infection (31 studies, 5920 patients) were linked to lower mortality (OR 0.66; 95% CI 0.54–0.80; NNT 20). Time to effective therapy was 5.03 h shorter. The mortality benefit was seen with antimicrobial stewardship programmes (OR 0.64), but not without them (OR 0.72; 0.46–1.12) [[Timbrook2017mRDT]].
- The Epic Sepsis Model was validated externally in 38,455 hospitalisations, 7% with sepsis. Its AUC was 0.63; it missed 67% of sepsis cases while alerting on 18% of all hospitalised patients (score ≥ 6) [[Wong2021Epic]].
- TREWS (5 hospitals, 590,736 patients monitored) identified 82% of retrospectively found sepsis cases. 89% of alerts were evaluated by a clinician, and confirming an alert within 3 h was linked to a 1.85-h shorter median time to first antibiotic order [[Henry2022TREWS]].
- In 6877 people with sepsis flagged before antibiotics, alert confirmation within 3 h was linked to an adjusted absolute in-hospital mortality reduction of 3.3% (relative 18.7%) in an observational, non-randomised comparison (Early evidence) [[Adams2022TREWS]].
- The Sepsis ImmunoScore, described as the first FDA-authorised AI sepsis tool, had an external-validation AUC of 0.81 [[Bhargava2024]]:
  - likelihood ratios by risk band: low 0.1, medium 0.5, high 2.1, very high 8.3
  - in-hospital mortality by band: 0.0%, 1.9%, 8.7%, 18.2%

### Exam hooks
- Sepsis-3: "life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection", operationalised as SOFA rise ≥ 2 [[Singer2016Sepsis3]].
- LPS (Gram-negative) → TLR4; lipoteichoic acid/peptidoglycan (Gram-positive) → TLR2; both → NF-κB [[Poltorak1998,Schwandner1999]].
- NLRP3 inflammasome → caspase-1 → mature IL-1β and IL-18, and gasdermin D → pyroptosis [[Neumann2026NLRP3]].
- Highest-risk cytokine pattern in pneumonia sepsis: both IL-6 and IL-10 high (HR 20.5) [[Kellum2007GenIMS]].
- Septic immunoparalysis marker: low monocyte HLA-DR; < 30% on days 3–4 predicted death (OR 6.48) [[Monneret2006]].
- Post-mortem spleens in sepsis: cytokine secretion < 10% of controls, CD4/CD8/HLA-DR cell depletion [[Boomer2011]].
- Lymphocyte apoptosis in sepsis is caspase-3-mediated [[Hotchkiss1999]].
- TNFR:Fc increased mortality dose-dependently (30% → 53%) [[Fisher1996TNFR]].
- The one sepsis-specific drug approved and later withdrawn: drotrecogin alfa (PROWESS 2001 positive; PROWESS-SHOCK 2012 negative; withdrawn 2011) [[Bernard2001PROWESS,Ranieri2012PROWESSSHOCK,MedLett2011Xigris]].
- Non-selective NOS inhibition raised blood pressure but increased mortality (59% vs 49%) [[Lopez2004NOS]].
- Lactate in sepsis can come from adrenaline-driven Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase aerobic glycolysis in muscle (ouabain abolishes it) [[Levy2005Lactate]].
- Sublingual microcirculation: perfused small vessels 48% in severe sepsis vs 90% in volunteers [[DeBacker2002]].
- ISTH overt DIC ≥ 5 points; SIC ≥ 4 points (PT-INR, platelets, 4-item SOFA) [[Taylor2001DIC,Iba2023DIC,Iba2017SIC]].
- Septic cardiomyopathy: survivors had low EF with dilated LV that recovered in about 10 days [[Parker1984]].
- Septic shock was the commonest contributor to ICU acute renal failure (47.5%) [[Uchino2005]].
- Berlin ARDS: mild 200–300, moderate 100–200, severe ≤ 100 mm Hg PaO2/FiO2 [[ARDS2012Berlin]].
- Seymour phenotypes: δ (13%) had the highest 28-day mortality (40%) [[Seymour2019Phenotypes]].
- LOVIT: IV vitamin C increased death or persistent organ dysfunction (RR 1.21) [[Lamontagne2022LOVIT]].
- Epic Sepsis Model external AUC 0.63, missed 67% of sepsis [[Wong2021Epic]].
- MALS cut-off in Greek trials: ferritin > 4420 ng/mL; immunoparalysis: HLA-DR < 5000 receptors per monocyte [[Leventogiannis2022PROVIDE,GiamarellosBourboulis2026ImmunoSep]].

### In plain words
- Sepsis is the body's harmful, out-of-control response to an infection. The infection starts it, but the body's own reaction is what hurts the organs [[Singer2016Sepsis3]].
- Immune cells carry "sensors" that spot pieces of germs, like a smoke alarm spotting smoke. In sepsis, the alarm keeps ringing far too loud [[Hotchkiss2016NRDP]].
- Strangely, the immune system can be overactive and worn out at the same time. Later in sepsis, some people's defences are so tired that new infections take hold more easily [[Hotchkiss2013NRI,Boomer2011]].
- The inside of blood vessels has a thin, gel-like lining. Sepsis strips it away, so vessels leak fluid into the tissues and swelling builds up [[Uchimido2019]].
- The tiniest blood vessels can stop flowing well even when blood pressure looks normal. In people who recover, this flow improves [[Sakr2004]].
- Sepsis can make blood clot in small vessels while also using up clotting factors. This can cause both clots and bleeding [[Levi2017Coag]].
- A high lactate level is a warning sign that someone is very ill. It does not always mean the body is short of oxygen [[GarciaAlvarez2014]].
- The heart can weaken during septic shock. In people who survive, it usually recovers within about 10 days [[Parker1984]].
- Confusion or drowsiness during an infection can be a sign that sepsis is affecting the brain. It needs urgent medical attention [[Gofton2012]].
- Many medicines that calmed inflammation in animals did not help people with sepsis, and some caused harm. That is why researchers now test treatments matched to each person's immune state [[Marshall2014,GiamarellosBourboulis2026ImmunoSep]].
- High-dose vitamin C did not help in large trials, and in one large trial it was linked to worse outcomes [[Lamontagne2022LOVIT]].
- Computer alert systems can help staff spot sepsis sooner, but some widely used ones miss many cases [[Wong2021Epic,Adams2022TREWS]].
- The numbers in this section describe large groups of people in studies. They cannot predict what will happen to one person.
- Call-now box (to carry on every page about symptoms, recognition or treatment): "Sepsis is a medical emergency. If someone with an infection becomes very unwell (confused or very drowsy, breathing very fast, cold, mottled or bluish skin, passing little or no urine), call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department now and ask: could this be sepsis?"

## Open questions and controversies
- Sequential or simultaneous? The classic model is a SIRS-then-CARS sequence (hyperinflammation followed by immunosuppression) [[Hotchkiss2013NRI]]. Mouse and human data show pro- and anti-inflammatory mediators rising together, with the worst outcome when both are high [[Osuchowski2006,Kellum2007GenIMS]]. Status: the field has largely moved to "concurrent and individually variable" (Early evidence).
- Is immunosuppression treatable? Biomarker-guided GM-CSF, IFN-γ, IL-7 and anti-PD-1 restore immune markers [[Meisel2009,Docke1997,Francois2018IRIS7,Hotchkiss2019Nivolumab]]. ImmunoSep improved SOFA response but not 28-day mortality [[GiamarellosBourboulis2026ImmunoSep]]. Status: no immunostimulant has shown a mortality benefit in a phase 3 trial.
- Endotoxin removal: EUPHRATES showed no benefit overall [[Dellinger2018EUPHRATES]], and the SSC 2021 suggests against polymyxin B haemoperfusion [[Evans2021SSC]]. Tigris, in a narrower endotoxin-activity band with a Bayesian prior drawn from a EUPHRATES subgroup, reported a high posterior probability of benefit in an open-label trial of 157 people [[Neyra2026Tigris]]. Status: unresolved; guideline position predates Tigris.
- Is lactate a hypoxia signal or a stress signal? Lactate is a marker of shock in Sepsis-3 [[Singer2016Sepsis3]], but much of it may come from adrenergic aerobic glycolysis rather than oxygen debt [[Levy2005Lactate,GarciaAlvarez2014]]. Status: both views agree it predicts risk; they disagree on whether chasing lactate clearance always means more oxygen delivery is needed.
- Is mitochondrial shutdown harmful or protective? Low muscle ATP and complex I activity track with death [[Brealey2002]]. Others frame tubular-cell and mitochondrial changes as an adaptive hibernation-like response [[Gomez2014]]. Status: Hypothesis.
- Beta-blockade in septic shock: a single-centre open-label trial found a large mortality signal favouring esmolol [[Morelli2013Esmolol]]. The multicentre STRESS-L trial with landiolol was stopped for possible harm [[Whitehouse2023STRESSL]]. Status: not supported outside research.
- Vitamin C: early enthusiasm was followed by neutral trials [[Fowler2019CITRISALI,Fujii2020VITAMINS,Sevransky2021VICTAS]], then harm in LOVIT [[Lamontagne2022LOVIT]]. The SSC suggests against it [[Evans2021SSC]]. Status: largely settled against routine use.
- Steroids by endotype: hydrocortisone may harm the SRS2 endotype [[Antcliffe2019]]. This is post hoc and needs prospective testing.
- Which coagulopathy definition should trigger anticoagulants? Overt DIC captures late, decompensated disease, while SIC captures earlier disease [[Iba2023DIC,Iba2017SIC]]. Anticoagulant trials in broad populations were negative [[Warren2001KyberSept,Vincent2019SCARLET]]. Status: no anticoagulant is established for sepsis-associated coagulopathy outside trials.
- AI alerts: a proprietary model performed poorly externally [[Wong2021Epic]], while TREWS was linked to better outcomes in a non-randomised analysis [[Adams2022TREWS]]. Status: randomised evidence on AI sepsis alerts is still limited.
