Health topics people read most on Wikipedia

210 English Wikipedia articles about health topics, ranked by how often people opened them in an average month from November 2025 to October 2026. How much people read about a topic is one of the things we weigh when choosing which guide to write next.

This measures reading, not searching, and not patients. Wikipedia does not record who its readers are or why they opened an article: they may be students, clinicians, carers, the curious or people with the condition. A high place says nothing about how common or how serious a condition is.

What the numbers are

  • Source: the Wikimedia Analytics pageviews API, per article, for English Wikipedia, all devices, counting only views classed as by people: crawlers that identify themselves as bots and traffic the Wikimedia Foundation flags as automated are left out.
  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. The figure is the average of the 12 monthly totals, rounded to the nearest hundred.
  • Fetched: 24 November 2026.
  • Licence: Wikimedia Analytics datasets are released under the CC0 public domain dedication (Wikimedia's statement).

What they leave out

  • One article per topic. Readers of a broader or related article are not counted. The figure for Type 2 diabetes, for example, does not include anyone who read the general Diabetes article.
  • Views through a redirect are not counted. Wikipedia counts a view under the title the reader opened, so someone who follows an alternative name to the article is counted under that name, not here.
  • Not every health topic. The list is the set of topics we looked at as possible guides in October 2026, plus the two the site began with (Alzheimer's disease and Familial Mediterranean fever). A topic missing from it may well be read more than the ones shown.
  • English only. Readers in other languages are not counted.
